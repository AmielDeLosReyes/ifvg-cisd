## 4. Before the open (preparation)

1. **Mark liquidity:** *"every morning before the market opens, I mark swing highs and lows on the 1-hour chart"*
   (#15, #115, #249); session levels **Asia high/low, London high/low, previous-day high/low** (#86); **data highs/lows**
   = the swing high/low printed by a news candle (*"It has to be a swing high or swing low"* 13 Mar, 00:32); equal
   highs/lows; new-week opening gap; all-time highs. His indicator draws these automatically (frames: labels AH, AL, LH,
   LL, PDL, "$$$", "External Liquidity").
2. **Read the higher-time-frame story** (his word: *narrative*): where the draws are, which HTF FVGs sit above/below,
   whether HTF CISDs (4H / 1H / 15m) formed and got retested, where price is in the big range (50% = equilibrium).
3. **Write scenarios:** *"Either we sweep London high and then get a play down or we come down, sweep this liquidity
   ... and then look for a play to London high"* (6 Feb, 00:30).
4. **Path of least resistance / "grandma staircase":** trade toward liquidity that was built in a clean staircase
   (his "market maker buy/sell model": *"You build, you build ... and then you run it in one fell swoop"* 12 Mar,
   00:00), not through big opposing FVGs or after a vertical move — *"If you can imagine your grandmother making it
   down a staircase of liquidity easily, then there's a good chance price wants to run to the bottom"* (#243).

## 5. The entry model — "The Confirmation Model"

### 5.1 The four confirmations (must happen in this order — *"Four checks, that order"*, #100)

**1. Liquidity sweep.** *"Price must take out a major swing high or swing low"* (#22). Liquidity = a swing high/low
where stops sit (#64). Swept levels are the ones marked in §4.
- **Internal sweep (bonus):** after the main (external) sweep, price makes a small counter-move and then takes that
  new little high/low, *"knocking out the traders who jumped in too soon"* (#13, #21, #69, #112). This is what
  turns his ~1:1 entries into 1:3 (#112).

**2. Delivering from a higher-time-frame FVG.** Price has tapped a 15m/30m/1H/4H fair value gap and the gap is
holding — *"we've tapped into it ... it seems to be holding. We have not inverted it. It is being
respected"* (9 Dec, 02:12). Respect = *"price taps in and bounces"*; chopping through it = trap (#70, #92).
- One version: *"I wait for price to tap and bounce at least once before I trust it"* (#9, #65, #179).
- Another: prefers a **fresh** gap; a gap *"already tapped into four times"* is a red flag (#110, #244);
  *"not delivering out of any kind of fresh higher-timeframe fair value gap"* = no trade (24 Jul, 01:01).

**3. Lower-time-frame FVG inversion (iFVG).** On the 1-minute (1–5m), a gap made by the move into the sweep gets
**closed through by a candle body**: *"not a wick through it, a full candle close on the other side"* (#74);
*"A close at the boundary is not an inversion"* (indicator description; shown on chart 25 Mar, 03:37).
- **Stacked gaps:** *"connect all of the fair value gaps and make it one big fair value gap because you're going to
  need to inverse all of them"* (9 Dec, 06:28); *"Not until all three fair value gaps are closed below do I consider
  making my move"* (#31). Includes gaps from other lower timeframes: *"we also have a 3-minute here that we need to
  invert ... because you don't want to enter a change in the state if there's still order flow going against you"*
  (9 Feb, 02:00; also 18 Jun, 02:30; 22 Jun, 04:04). Frame (24 Jun): he draws one box labelled "iFVG" over the
  stacked 1m/3m/4m gaps.

**4. CISD — change in the state of delivery = the entry trigger.** *"A close below the last series of up close candles
or opposing candles that led into the liquidity sweep"* (24 Jun, 05:04); *"the last series of down close or up close
candles that led into the sweep. We want to see a close above this point"* (24 Aug, 10:40).
- He also words it *"close through the first opposing candle in the last push"* (#126, #154).
- **Frame-confirmed on two of his charts:** the CISD is a horizontal line drawn from the **open of the first candle of
  the last unbroken run of opposite-coloured candles that made the sweep extreme** — a one-candle run on the 24 Aug
  1-minute teaching replay, a three-candle run on the 4 Jun 5-minute chart (line at the first candle's open). The
  trigger is a candle that **closes** beyond that line.
- *"No CISD, no trade"* (#35, #95). Entering at the inversion close without CISD = *"a pattern trade"* (13 Mar, 01:02).
- A CISD stays alive until price closes back beyond the origin: *"this change in the state doesn't fail until we get
  a close back above this candle"* (#215, #226).

### 5.2 The original (Dec 2025) long checklist
*"A liquidity sweep that happens inside of OTE. Delivering from a higher time frame fair value gap. Put in an SMT.
Get an internal liquidity sweep. Inverse a fair value gap. See a change in the state of delivery with a clear draw
targeting opposing liquidity"* (9 Dec, 00:03). From mid-Dec the reels shrink it to the four items above and move
SMT / internal sweep / OTE to "bonus".

### 5.3 Bonus confluences
- **SMT divergence (NQ vs ES):** NQ takes a swing high and ES does not (bearish), or NQ takes a swing low and ES does
  not (bullish) (#134). Only at swing highs/lows — mid-range divergence is *"noise"* (#131); marginal divergence
  ignored (#170). If both sweep together, *"the breakout has more conviction"* → he passes (#42, #103).
- **OTE (optimal trade entry):** a fib over the leg being retraced — *"low to high in a move up, high to low in a move
  down. The area between 61.8 and 79 is your OTE. The 50 is equilibrium"* (#158). **Frame-confirmed:** his fib shows
  only 0.5, 0.62 and 0.79 (9 Dec 1H example; 24 Jun; 19 Aug). Range examples: London low to the price at the open
  (22 Jun, 03:03); the 1H swing high to low (9 Dec). *"OTE tells you where, the confirmations tell you when"* (#36).
- **Clear opposing liquidity** as the target (required, see §8).

### 5.4 Grades (and size)
Stated several ways:
- A+ = four confirmations + SMT + clean structure; A = four + clear opposing liquidity; **B = three — "I skip these
  now"**; C = two or fewer (#34, 9 Jan).
- *"A setup with only three confirmations can still get taken, but I'm cutting my size in half"* (#80, 8 Mar);
  *"Three confirmations is a B. I can take a B, but ... I size down"*; A+ = four + *"SMT divergence, internal sweep,
  or an OTE zone confluence"* (#124, 28 Apr).
- *"Only three, that's a B and I tend to skip it"* (#185, 21 Jul); *"Profitable traders only take As"* (#199, 2 Aug).
- *"The grade decides everything, including how much I risk"* (#155).

## 6. How he enters (execution)

- **Aggressive:** at the close of the CISD candle — for *"an A+ setup with strong momentum"* (#33, #143).
- **Pullback (his usual when risk is big):** wait for price to come back to the CISD line or the inverted FVG — *"Most
  of the time, price does retrace to CISD"* (#33). Used when the first entry's risk/reward is bad (19 Feb, 26 Mar,
  18 May, 21 May).
- **Scale in:** 1 contract at the CISD close, more at the CISD retest (*"now my risk is balanced"* 22 Jun, 04:34);
  adds at a 50% retest (24 Feb) or at a second CISD (18 Mar "triple CISD").
- **Order block — only inside a model that already triggered:** *"we don't trade order blocks ... inside of a vacuum
  ... when you get something like an order block inside of that narrative, you can enter"* (#205). Definition:
  *"the last bearish candle or cluster of bearish candles before a strong bullish move"*, confirmed by *"a candle
  close below [above] the open of the initial pullback candle"*; valid if 1–3 candles, trending context, displacement
  close (14 Sep, 04:04–06:37). Used to enter late or add, with a tighter stop beyond the order block (25 Jun,
  24 Jul, 31 Aug). He also says *"Order blocks are theoretical ... I put less weight in them"* (#113).
- **"Trust fund trade":** everything except the FVG inversion → taken small (10 Feb, 03:33; #212b 13 Aug).

## 7. Stop loss

- **Default: beyond the swept high/low.** *"Your stop goes above the high that was swept for shorts or below the low
  that was swept for longs. Not one tick above ... above the actual sweep"* (#25, #250); *"placing our stop above the
  candles that caused the change in the state"* (#2, #166, #186…); *"stop above the SMT high"* (#1, #12).
  **Frame (24 Aug replay):** stop 29,565.75 under a ~29,566 sweep wick; entry 29,652 = close of the CISD candle.
  **Frame (24 Jun):** short stop 29,904.25 above the Asia-high sweep.
- **When that stop is too wide he moves it to:**
  - just past the **inverted FVG** — *"I put my stop below the fair value gap, not below the swing low because my
    risk-to-reward isn't going to be what I need it to be ... if we're going to come back and invert this, market
    doesn't want to go higher"* (14 Jan, 02:03; also 9 Dec 07:28, 19 Feb 06:08, 22 Apr 04:09);
  - past an **order block** — *"traditionally, your stop would be all the way up here. That's a 106 point stop. That's
    way too much for me. So I'm going to put mine right at this order block"* (31 Aug, 04:34);
  - below *"the first gap that's created after open ... tends to get respected"* (10 Jun, 05:08).
  - For a pure FVG entry (before CISD): stop beyond the FVG, *"not at the swing low because this is a fair value gap
    entry ... the probabilities are less"* (9 Jul, 05:10).
- **Maximum risk (NQ points):** doesn't like >100 (12 Feb); 120–150 = small size (9 Feb, 18 May); 205 / 209 / 232 /
  ~250 = no trade — *"You can't risk 232 points even if you are trying to target 449 ... I'll blow the account"*
  (21 May, 03:09).
- **Position size:** 0.5% of the account per trade (#18, #43, #107): *"$50,000 ... $250. If my stop is 25 points away
  on MNQ and ... each point is worth $2 per contract ... five contracts"* (#68).

## 8. Targets and trade management

- **Target = opposing liquidity**, chosen before entry: *"If I enter after a sweep of a high, my target is the next major
  low"* (#78); equal lows, swing low, unmitigated FVG (#117); London/Asia highs/lows, PDH/PDL, data highs/lows.
  *"If you can't see the opposing liquidity, there's no entry"* (#78).
- **Minimum risk/reward — three versions:** at least 1:2, *"If the math doesn't work, I skip the trade, even if all
  four confirmations are there"* (#23, #10, #16); *"If it doesn't give me better than one to one, I skip it"* (#117);
  and in recaps he takes ~1:1 or 0.9:1 toward a strong draw (13 Mar, 01:33; 10 Jun, 05:08).
- **TP1 / break-even point = the first obstacle:** the first liquidity, an opposing HTF FVG, an order block formed on
  the way, internal liquidity. There he takes partials (*"sell three [of four] ... move our stop to break even or even
  a little bit into profit"* 9 Dec, 03:45; *"half"* 26 Mar; *"90% out"* 24 Aug) and runs the rest.
  **Frame (24 Aug):** TP1 = next swing high at 1.97R, final TP = the higher swing high (~2.5R).
- **"Standard deviation" targets (frame-confirmed 19 Aug):** a fib drawn from the **sweep extreme** to the **extreme of
  the CISD candle** — *"the low that was put in by the sweep and the high of the candle that caused the change in the
  state"* (19 Aug, 11:49) — with levels −1, −2, −2.5 (and −4). −1 = first partials + break-even (#121); −2 to −2.5 =
  *"the algorithmic area where change in the states tend to play out to"*; −4 = extended (#191, 9 Jul 02:35).
  Used as the target when there is no liquidity (all-time highs, 22 Apr 05:12). Two other anchors appear once each:
  *"I mark one standard deviation from my entry"* (#121) and *"a standard deviation of the order block"* (25 Jun, 05:11).
- **Break-even triggers he uses:** first liquidity taken; an order block forms in his favour (4 Feb, 22 Apr); price
  chops / "barcodes" (15 Jan, 22 Jun); ES takes a level (31 Aug, #231); red-folder news; 20–30 min sideways (#212).
  "Suffocating the play" = trailing the stop into profit (24 Jul, 05:37).
- **Take the money:** *"Take your profits early"*, one-and-done; exits before the full target in choppy months.

## 9. Daily rules

- **Max two trades a day; two losses = done.** *"if I take more than two losses in a day, I'm done. Also, I can't take
  more than two trades in a day"* (9 Apr, 07:13); *"After two losses, I close my charts"* (#82, #201).
- **One and done** after a clean win (19 Feb, 4 Jun, 8 Sep).
- After a loss, don't flip and take the opposite direction right away (21 Jan, 03:04); after a break-even he often
  stops for the day (4 Feb, 05:39).
- No shorts at all-time highs (12 May, #146). No trade when timeframes disagree (*"If the 15-minute is telling me we are
  bullish, but the 1-minute is giving me a bearish entry, I'm not interested"* 17 Jun, 06:07; #197, #210).
- Don't catch a falling knife: after a big flush the **first** CISD is skipped; wait for an internal sweep
  (24 Feb, 02:03; 19 Aug; 1 Sep; 17 Sep).
- Hides P&L while in a trade (10 Jun, 08:46).

## 10. Red flags (skip even with four confirmations)

No clean sweep · HTF FVG chopped through · iFVG but no CISD · iFVG + CISD with no HTF FVG · choppy price ·
no clear target · RR under 1:1 · high-impact news in the next 10–30 min · *"parabolic aggressive move before the sweep"*
(#225) / *"already moved 100 points before sweeping"* (#29) · level already swept 3–4 times (#163) · HTF gap tapped
4 times (#110) · opposing FVGs right in front of the entry (6 Feb, 4 Mar, 21 May, 24 Jul) · Friday afternoon.

## 11. Where he contradicts himself or bends the rules (as observed)

- B setups: "I skip these" vs "take at half size" (§5.4). Minimum RR: 1:2 vs 1:1 vs 0.9 accepted (§8).
- OTE zone: 61.8–79 / 62–79 vs 50–79; stated as required (*"If price never gets there, I don't take the trade"* #36,
  #105) but graded as a bonus elsewhere (#124).
- HTF gap: "tap and bounce at least once first" vs "fresh gap" preferred.
- News buffer 10 / 15 / 30 minutes.
- Standard-deviation anchor: sweep extreme → CISD candle (19 Aug, frame) vs "from my entry" (#121) vs "of the order
  block" (25 Jun).
- Trades taken outside the model, flagged by him: trust-fund trades (no iFVG), an order block with no HTF FVG
  (#200 — skipped) vs with no full model (#223 — taken), a 30-second order block without CISD (#193 — lost),
  a pre-market trade (12 Mar), trades after 11:00 ET (24 Jun, 17 Sep), a 3-minute "FVG entry" on one eval (#229).

## 14. Can it be built as a Pine Script entry model?

**Yes for the mechanical core; no for his discretion.**

Codeable as he defines it: the NY 9:30–11:00 ET window; session / previous-day / 1H-swing levels and their sweeps;
15m/30m/1H/4H FVGs via `request.security` and "price inside one"; lower-timeframe FVGs closed through by a body, all
