=== [2022 Ep5 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 5 (ID N29ZJ-o31xs)] — Index futures contract months, expiration and rollover  (aliases ICT uses: "front month", "nearby contract", "next month out")

=== [2022 Ep41 — 2022 ICT Mentorship - Episode 41 & Final (ID 2XhDi5GoNUI)] — Position sizing (risk per trade) — worked example
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Use free lot-size calculators (search for them): enter account size, risk %, and stop size.
  - Worked example:
    1. $10,000 account at 1% risk means $100.
    2. Stop is 9 points, so $100 / 9 is about $11 per point.
    3. One ES mini is $50/point, which is too big, so you cannot trade a mini at 1%.
    4. One micro (MES) is $5/point. $11 / $5 means 2 micros ($10/point).
    5. Actual risk: 9 × $10 = $90, which is under 1%.
    6. Target 25.5 points × $10 = $255. He calls this "basically 2¼%" (the arithmetic gives 2.55%).
  - He does not factor commissions, fees, or spread into the sizing. "Costs are like taxes, you just eat it." Beginners who want to be strict about risk can include them.

=== [2022 Ep41 — 2022 ICT Mentorship - Episode 41 & Final (ID 2XhDi5GoNUI)] — Drawdown control: halve risk after each loss
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a loss at 1% risk, the next trade risks 0.5%. Reasons: emotions, revenge, the urge to over-leverage, and impatience (the "loser's cycle, gambler's mentality").
  - You must make back 50% of what you lost before returning to full 1% risk. For example, lose about $100 (with commission), then earn $50 back.
  - If the 0.5% trade also loses, the next is 0.25%, the lowest you go.
  - Three straight losses then total only 1.75% drawdown (1 + 0.5 + 0.25). Compare doubling up: 1% then 2% is already 3%, and spiralling to 3–4% risk, removing or widening stops, leads easily to 10% drawdown and shame.
  - Same logic at 2% risk: after a full 2% loss, the next trade can risk only 1%. For example, 4 micros ($20/point) at 2% become 2 micros at 1% on the same 9-point stop.
  - Stops are not static. This trade needed 9 points, while others may need 5, 10, or 12. Recalculate size each time. Deep drawdown can leave you unable to trade even one micro.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:07](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1867) — after a loss risk half; [0:31:54](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1914) — make back 50%, then 0.25% floor; [0:34:17](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2057) — 1.75% vs doubling-up spiral; [0:41:25](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2485) — 2% loss means the next is 1% (4 micros to 2).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Professionals "are not allowed to touch their account when they're highly charged or emotional." Risk management is a psychological game as well as numbers.

=== [2026 #10 — How New Students Should Start Part 5 \ Back Testing Properly (ID xnbtp_j81lI)] — Backtesting properly (ICT's method): recorded price, hypothetical trades, no market replay
- **Kind:** rule | process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Do NOT use market replay. It shows you a candle's open and then its close; you miss the "breath and lifespan" of each candle: how it opens, the flurry, whether it makes a higher high or lower low while still active, where it settles. The logic applies at any timeframe (1m to weekly); "1-minute chart's not moving any faster than the weekly chart." Replay-based mentors pause and describe, which he calls shameful.
  - Instead, watch recorded price action (live or delayed data both work, because you are studying price that has already happened). On Windows: hold Alt + Windows key and tap R to start a screen recording (microphone on) and record observations in your own voice; avoid a screensaver coming on. Free TradingView real-time data is not necessary; he says real-time data is cheap ("$7 on TradingView") and he will not pay for students' data feeds.
  - Backtesting = pattern recognition and characteristic observations; "all setups are not equal ... they rhyme" and don't have an identical fingerprint but share key characteristics. Record small deviations from what you expect.
  - Minimum: 6 weeks of back data and then 6 weeks forward (live/recorded) — "I promise you your ability to see price action will be so far ahead." Repetition conditions the subconscious so you can read the tape live.
  - Define "backtesting" as: a hypothetical entry, a hypothetical stop, and how price delivered. Don't just take a picture after the fact; trace over every individual candlestick (e.g., a candle that opened and traded right back up to the previous candle's low = "immediate rebalance"; a retrace that stops "shy of the close" is only respecting the wick). Use empty chart space with A/B/C legend markers for longer explanations.
  - Add price notes (TradingView "price note") to anchor exact prices of key highs/lows/opens/closes; increase font size. Be precise: his analogy is a cash business, where missing $2.25 of a 562.25 vs "560-ish" matters because "it's your business." Precision in logging is a precondition for precision in trading.
  - Log charts as annotated static pictures or electronically; don't worry about a naked chart if you annotate. Review everything on the weekend.
- **How to use:** Record each day's 7–9 pre-market range, opening range, 10:00 hour, annotate hypothetical entries, stops and targets as if real; compute risk, drawdown and heat.
- **Example in this video:** NQ (September 2026 delivery contract per the caption) 1-minute day, logged with pre-market range, RTH gap and 10:00 hour annotations (below).
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:37](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=157) — market replay is useless; [0:03:25](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=205) — lifespan of each candle; Alt+Win+R recording; [0:05:49](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=349) — 6 weeks back, 6 weeks forward; [0:21:37](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1297) — what backtesting is: hypothetical entry, stop, delivery, trace each candle; [0:43:52](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2632) — avoid replay "like the plague."
- **Nuance / caveats:** Strong claims: AI/software "can't see the subtle nuances"; reading a candle while it is forming (rather than waiting for the close) is the skill. The rest of the video's rhetoric about complainers and begging for funded challenges is skipped.

- **How to identify / use:** (1) weekly continuous chart, (2) mark the previous month's high/low and previous week's high/low, (3) check them on the front-month contract, (4) go to daily, then 15-minute.

=== [2026 #75 — Futures Market Commentary \ June 18, 2026 (ID bUN_f8U_1Eg)] — Futures Contract Months (aliases: contract symbols, H, M, U, Z)
- **Kind:** reference information
- **What ICT says:** Index futures have four main contract months: March (H), June (M), September (U), December (Z). Always note which contract you're analyzing; the continuous contract (rolling between months) can show different behavior than individual month contracts. For NQ in mid-June, June contract (M) is expiring soon; September (U) becomes the primary contract.
- **How to identify:** Check the futures ticker; note the symbol (ZNU for Sept NQ, ZNM for June NQ, etc.).

- **How to use:** During rollover weeks, let volume be very light; synchronize with the new contract rather than trying to build equity.
