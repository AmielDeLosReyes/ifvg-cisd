=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — Inversion FVG — the highest-precision form  (aliases: iFVG, role reversal)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - **All** of his PD arrays have an inversion aspect: every one can reverse its role. He introduced iFVGs on Twitter Spaces "last year" and will detail them in his book.
  - The highest-precision, most trustworthy iFVGs depend on this context: after 7:00 there's a smooth area (relative equal highs), price runs it (a stop hunt), and an FVG **inside the range that creates the bearish breaker** fails.
  - In the bearish case, the inverting gap is the **BISI** in the up-leg that ran the relative equal highs. Without the time and narrative context, a beginner would treat that BISI as a buy and get collapsed on.
  - Required behavior: price runs above the gap, comes back below it, then redelivers up into it (it acts as resistance) before a deeper protraction lower. That's what his old tweets meant by "want to see this FVG act as an inversion FVG".
  - Once it inverts, the BISI is the only buy-side gap of interest. After a valid return it "sells off", and your focus shifts to SIBIs.
- **How to use:** Sell the return into the iFVG. Respect its CE. For stops, see the breaker section.
- **Example:** Aug 6, 1m, just after 7:00: relative equal highs form and are run. The up-leg's BISI then fails. Price breaks down, returns into it, respects CE, and breaks lower. Later another return gives an OTE as well.

=== [2024 #3 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #3   August 7, 2024 (ID ZkLc4-adodg)] — Sell-side / buy-side delivery vs. liquidity; inefficiency vs. BPR  (aliases: one-sided delivery, "liquidity void" critique)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Liquidity** means real resting orders, the buy stops above highs and sell stops below lows. He calls it the "lifeblood"/"heartbeat" of the market.
  - An **inefficiency** (FVG, gap) has had **one-sided delivery**. A down move is *sell-side delivery*, and what it lacks is *buy-side delivery*, and vice versa. So "sell-side delivery" and "sell-side liquidity" are not the same thing.
  - Chris Lori's "liquidity void" is a misnomer, ICT says: there was buying (or selling) in it, and what's missing is the opposite side of delivery.
  - Price merely trading back into an FVG does not balance it. It must trade there **and leave**, and then it becomes a **balanced price range**.
  - **Hierarchy of draws:** NDOG/NWOG and relative equal highs/lows rank above inefficiencies (FVGs). Inefficiencies are "subordinate and less important" because the algorithm places more weight on the former. Real opening gaps outrank everything because no trades took place there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:10](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1330) what the gap lacks (sell-side delivery) · [1:53:37](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6817) hierarchy of draws · [1:55:15](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6915) delivery vs liquidity · [1:57:39](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=7059) BPR and the Lori critique

=== [2024 #4 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #4    August 8, 2024 (ID hdSscxZgE58)] — Balanced Price Range (BPR)  (aliases: balanced price range, balanced price delivery)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:** When price has been offered both up and down through the same range (e.g. between one candle's low and another's high), it is balanced. Analogy: painting a wall with a roller — once an area is painted (balanced), you don't need to go deep into it again; price only returns to the edge. Use quadrants of the BPR to study reactions.
- **Example in this video:** 15-sec chart: a BPR formed from a low that was also Wednesday's NDOG CE up to a high; price later traded back to the up-close closing price/open and rallied. On the 1-second chart, price dropped into a BPR around Wednesday's NDOG CE and didn't go deeper.

=== [2024 #8 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #8   August 14, 2024 (ID zU7iAqg4sSA)] — Balanced Price Range (BPR) as the real range of an FVG and the "Mohawk"  (aliases: "mohawk", "coloring outside the lines")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement (ICT says it is the first time he is introducing this PD array)
- **What ICT says:**
  - When one candle's wick overlaps another candle's body (or a candle opens and trades down a little into the previous candle's range) inside a gap, the overlap is a balanced price range, and the BPR is the real range of the inefficiency, which you have to encapsulate rather than just drawing the FVG on the standard three candles. He says this is a PD array students "have never learned before".
  - Example logic: a candle runs up and is all body at the top, the next candle opens and makes a flush tap down, overlapping a little portion with no wick. That overlap is the BPR, not the plain FVG. Price then reacts at that BPR boundary instead of filling the FVG fully ("you will not pass").
  - A "mohawk" is the tiny spike outside a PD array boundary (a candle poking a few ticks beyond a level before turning). He says about 90% of mohawks outside a PD array are predictable but he won't teach that.
  - Do not confuse with volume imbalance: a volume imbalance is a separation between two candle bodies inside an FVG; he notes that is absent here.
- **How to identify:** inside an FVG, look for overlap between one candle's wick/body and the next candle's range.
- **How to use:** draw the BPR as the entry/stop boundary: "we never want to see price go back through the whole BPR to the top".
- **Timestamps:** [2:02:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7373) — BPR from the overlap of a wick and a body, "mohawk" · [2:03:41](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7421) — 90% of mohawks predictable; won't teach · [2:30:01](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9001) — BPR introduced as a new PD array

=== [2024 #10 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #10  August 16, 2024 (ID qoNQfVzVXZQ)] — Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) mechanics  (aliases: "reclaim", "treat it as an inversion gap")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A bearish FVG becomes an inversion FVG when a candle **closes above** it; then a **new candle** must come back down and touch it (the high, CE, or low of the gap); a single candle cannot do both. A wick spiking above does not count as the close. Once touched, price can use it as support and rally ("treat it as an inversion FVG as it did here, then sent it higher"). Reverse for bearish. He wants it to "stay open", i.e., not be completely closed.
- **How to identify:** Close beyond the gap, then a retest by a following candle; the touch can be as deep as the low of the gap.
- **How to use:** In a bearish context he lists a very nice confluence: iFVG + a bearish FVG + bearish order block, where the order block is the candle with the highest up-close inside the FVG and the iFVG (not the more obvious-looking one). Test the wick: if price trades up but does not touch the midpoint of the gap/wick, that is weak (bearish); if it trades up and prints a volume imbalance, that could be an entry.
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: an orange FVG: "see it go above it, close above it, open a new candle" then touch it; a second gap with a body inside and a tail down was acceptable; a wick-up into a gap that fails to reach the midpoint was called weak.
- **Timestamps:** [1:17:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4622) iFVG: gap taken, comes back, touches · [1:20:29](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4829) bearish order block inside both gaps; wick must not touch the midpoint · [2:24:44](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8684) must close a candle beyond, start a new candle, then touch · [2:38:00](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9480) close above, new candle, then touch

=== [2024 #16 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #16  August 24, 2024 (ID kuURDD1ajdQ)] — Balanced Price Range (BPR) and high resistance liquidity  (aliases: balanced price range, "guarded by the algorithm", manual intervention)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement (his usage differs from the common overlapping-FVG BPR definition)
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 1-minute chart he labels a segment a balanced price range: a small SIBI exists between one candle's low and another's high; price trades up into it (a small wick above is fine), breaks lower to a low, comes back up and overlaps the whole prior run, breaks lower again, retraces to the "propulsion block" again, then finally leaves the range below the low.
  - Why "balanced": price delivered both ways, back and forth (down, up, down, up), offering efficient delivery both higher and lower inside a clear trading range, and then leaves it. It is not enough to go up, down and leave.

  - Therefore if price does return to the BPR low and reaches into it, nothing is wrong with the logic; but if price reaches the high it signals manual intervention; he would not chase long and would "cut the interest in the day and be done."
  - Always trade after those news drivers; you do not know what they will do at the time of delivery.
  - Extend the BPR through the day. He kept the levels on a notepad (the chart gets busy); he recommends putting them on the chart while learning.
- **How to identify:** (1) a mapped range with a small SIBI inside; (2) price trades both directions inside it; (3) price leaves it with displacement; (4) the range's high holds as resistance.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 23 before the open: the range sits above, price displaces below its low; ICT says he is not interested in longs "because of the factors on the daily chart." The shaded BPR high-low extended through 9:30.

=== [2024 #42 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ October NFP NQ \ October 04, 2024 (ID qcYYm1iNCIM)] — Efficient delivery, rebalancing an inefficiency, and why liquidity still matters more  (aliases: "SIBI", "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency", "efficient delivery", "balanced price range")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A single candle with a sell-side delivery leaves an inefficiency on the buy side (an "island" between the previous candle's low and the next candle's high). The interval of time between those two prices has not been balanced with price in both directions, so price tends to return to overlap that range. The algorithm "reprices to engage liquidity" and also rebalances inefficiencies. It does not know how many stops are above an old high or below an old low.
  - Once price has traded back up over the single candle and touched its low, the inefficiency has been "efficiently delivered". That is not a balanced price range (BPR).
  - But reaching the top of an inefficiency is not enough as a target. The large pools of orders are at old highs (buy stops) and old lows (sell stops), not at the top of the inefficiency. Price is "offered" there for efficient delivery, and that is not the same as a stop pool.
  - He answers a viewer question: there are no larger pools of liquidity than those above old highs and below old lows. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling a new theory.
  - Time symmetry: the time it took to build the inefficiency is the time you should expect for the return delivery. Here it took two candles instead of one, which is fine.
  - Speed and magnitude: if the inefficiency was created by a quick sudden move, expect the buy-side repricing to be quick and made of large candles. If price instead sputters up and back inside the gap, it is "high resistance" and manual intervention.
  - Management when price sputters up inside an inefficiency (and it is not an NFP day): do not chase or trail the stop up. Lock in cover costs and let it go. Trades like that have many retracements.
- **How to identify:** One candle, or a short run, that traded away leaving a gap with no overlap. Check whether price has since traded back over the whole range.
- **How to use:** Use the gap as context for a draw. Target the higher-liquidity pool above or below and look for speed as confirmation. Slow grinding means do not trust it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/5m: a sell-side delivery candle formed on Tuesday (red shaded area). From 8:30, price repaid it (overlapping candles on top of it, then through it). ICT said it was now "efficiently delivered" but not a BPR, so a further run to the relative equal highs above was possible. He also recalled that people had thought price would go up just to fill the inefficiency, and he had said that it would go to where the positions are protected.

  - First expectation: price trades THROUGH it. A body gets laid over it.
  - How it gains meaning: the imbalance is created between two candle bodies (an up candle's close and the next candle's open). If an up-closed candle goes through that range (buy-side delivery) and later a down-closed candle lays a body over the same range (sell-side delivery), then both sides have delivered and it is "efficiently delivered." He then treats it as a (small) Balanced Price Range, and the imbalance becomes more meaningful. He says he does not hang much on tiny, insignificant ones.
  - Paint roller analogy: roll paint onto a wall; the first foot delivers ample paint, then pockets appear as paint runs out; you roll back over to fill the pockets. Price does the same inside a volume imbalance — delivers one direction first, then returns to deliver the other side.
  - After both sides have delivered and price leaves it, the balanced price range acts as a DISCOUNT array (support) if price returns to it from above; as a premium array if the reverse. If long, you can roll your stop loss to just below that volume imbalance, because "it's done its work in framing out a balanced price range."
  - Contrast: a volume imbalance with NO body ever laid over it — only wicks — behaves like a breakaway gap: price traverses it with a wick and rips away in the same candle; this is not the same as being delivered on both sides. "Two different dynamics."
  - It is not "liquidity" in itself; the buy-side/sell-side delivery means the market has been offered on the up and the down, not that stops are resting there.
  - Practical: annotate the volume imbalances inside the 8:30 delivery range, extend them to the right, and keep referring to them while price is inside that range. One example is not enough; collect many instances in your charts.
  - Management: he often uses volume imbalances to start positioning (as in London); then when time (a macro) kicks in and price displaces and "spools" toward the target, he can use the volume imbalance again as a new entry or as a way to manage the stop. It needs management ("a little bit of management is required").
  - He criticizes people who teach volume imbalances improperly or monetize his material without fully understanding it.
- **How to identify:** (1) Find the gap between two candles' bodies with overlapping wicks; (2) check whether later candles have laid bodies over it from above (sell-side) and below/through it (buy-side); (3) both delivered then left behind = BPR; wicks only = breakaway-gap-like.
