=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — Volume imbalance inside an FVG: draw to the body, ignore the wick
- **Kind:** refinement (he calls it new and his own)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the separation between one candle's **body** and the next candle's body. If the bodies don't overlap or at least touch, there's a volume imbalance.
  - When an FVG has a volume imbalance in it, the FVG's boundary should incorporate the volume imbalance, which means taking it to the **body** rather than the wick. "The wicks are not the true narrative." A wick is like a child coloring outside the lines.
  - Wicks inside such an FVG that reach only up to the CE (halfway) or less are disregarded. Use the candle body.
- **How to identify:** Zoom in. Check whether adjacent candle bodies leave a gap. If they do, re-draw the FVG edge at the body.
- **Example:** Aug 6, 1m: he corrects one of his own SIBI drawings because its edge should include a volume imbalance between bodies.
- **Timestamps:** [1:06:28](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3988) "look closely, what's wrong". [1:07:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4036) VI definition. [1:08:53](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4133) VI means ignore the wick. [1:09:40](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4180) wicks up to CE or less are ignored; take it to the body.
- **Nuance:** He says you won't find this in books or among "five-minute trainer" copies. The exact wording on which wicks are ignored is a little loose in the captions.

=== [2024 #8 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #8   August 14, 2024 (ID zU7iAqg4sSA)] — Wick as an inefficiency: measure it and use its consequent encroachment (CE)  (aliases: "wick CE", "midpoint of the wick", "quadrants of the wick", "the tell")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement (ICT says mentorship students never got this; he claims nobody else has ever taught it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Every time there is a wick, especially on a swing high or swing low that matters, measure it and mark its midpoint. Measure from the very highest high / lowest low (the extreme), using the real wick tip, not the body. He keeps candle borders switched on so the wick tips plot accurately (without borders the levels are not technically exact).
  - He treats the wick as a gap even though it printed inside a single candle. Because it is a gap, its midpoint is called consequent encroachment, not mean threshold (mean threshold is an order-block term). How long the candle took to print the wick does not matter, only the visual range from the candle's open to the extreme.
  - Long wicks: get the exact number (he draws a Fibonacci on the candle and uses the 50% level, then deletes the Fib; he scolds students who leave Fibs cluttering the chart). Short wicks: eyeball it.
  - The wick range is inefficient (the 8:30 news candle fell "over 100 handles" and printed back up inside the same one-minute candle). Price tends to return to that wick's CE, as a magnet.
  - A wick above price is a premium array, a wick below price is a discount array. If a wick sits above price and you are bearish, price should trade up into the wick's CE and the bodies should stop at or below it.
  - Wicks have quadrants too: CE (50%) and the lower/upper quadrant. In the first wick example he also points to a "3/4 level" as an entry-pattern / partial / Terminus level (caption unclear: it may just be the wick CE level he had marked). (He insists this is not "quarters theory", which he calls nonsense and asks people to stop asking about.)
  - Works on every time frame, down to sub-1-minute charts. "Price is price"; a 1m candle's open/high/low/close behaves like a daily candle's.
- **How to identify:** a candle with a visible wick at an important swing high/low or a big news candle. Draw from open to extreme, mark 50% and the quadrants.
- **How to use:**
  - Anticipation: after a wick forms, expect price to return to its CE, and to explore its lower quadrant if the bodies fail to hold.
  - Tell: when price returns to the wick, watch the bodies. If bodies do not close above the wick CE (bearish case) and the next candle makes a lower high, that is the algorithm "tipping its hand" and it is an entry (see Order block section for stop placement).
  - Failure: if a PD array (FVG, wick CE) cannot even be retraced to while you are directionally biased, the market is "decidedly weak" in that direction.
  - Confluence stacking: wick CE + FVG CE + 6 PM opening price + prior candle high are layered levels, and the more of them agree the stronger the expectation.
  - Even if no other level existed, a candle that runs up and stops short of the wick CE tells him the next candle will want to trade into that candle's body, which then acts as a bearish order block (trade it before others see it).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 14, 2024. The 8:30 news candle opened, dropped over 100 handles in seconds and came back to close up in its body (long lower wick). ICT marked the wick's CE and the lower quadrant. Price (after running up into the July 28 NWOG and reversing) traded down to the wick CE, held there with the bodies, and later explored toward the lower quadrant/low. A wick's CE (captioned 19,965, unreliable) coincided "to the tick" with the high of a candle on the 15m chart, which he used as a retracement ceiling. Price pushed into an FVG whose CE it failed to reach (stopped about a tick short), and the bodies did not close above a wick CE, then it dropped.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:18](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=318) — wick midpoint, "FVG that can't trade to its CE" · [0:15:23](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=923) — first rule: annotate CE on any swing-high/low wick · [0:22:44](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1364) — confluence of wick CE + FVG + prior high · [0:26:02](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1562) — drawing the 50% level, removing the Fib afterwards · [0:51:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=3068) — measure every wick from highest high/lowest low, all time frames · [1:16:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4565) — why it is CE not mean threshold
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:**
  - ICT says explicitly he has never seen anyone else break a wick into quadrants for reversals, short-term trading or targets, and that he is teaching it live for the first time (0:21:06, 1:46:53). Mentorship students did not get it.
  - He refuses to teach everything about wicks ("90% of the mohawks I can anticipate, I'm not teaching it", 2:03:41).
  - Price can wick through the CE; what matters is where the bodies stop.

- **What ICT says:** Wicks are gaps, just like full gaps. They can be graded (subdivided) using fibonacci levels. Discount wicks form when price trades down to a lower level and leaves a gap on the way back up; premium wicks form when price trades up and leaves a gap on the way back down.

=== [2026 #138 — ICT 2026 Smart Money Concepts Lecture \ January 12, 2026 (ID dt9mk4n5g_o)] — Relative equal highs/lows — anchor point and wick grading
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Anchor point = the leftmost swing, the one that starts the definition. For relative equal HIGHS the anchor is the higher of the pair; for relative equal LOWS the anchor is the lower of the pair. The one closest to price must be lower (highs) / higher (lows) than the anchor.
  - If the anchor swing has a wick, you must grade the wick (lower quadrant, consequent encroachment, upper quadrant). Price may only trade into the wick (to its upper quadrant, CE, or lower quadrant) and then reject, instead of taking out the extreme.
  - Not every relative equal high/low is equal in quality.
  - Always (he stresses "never ever not do this") take a partial when running to such a level: he likes a partial anywhere between the wick's lower quadrant and its consequent encroachment, i.e., split it in half. You do not need the high taken out.
  - Stopping short of the wick's CE (not even touching it) signals weakness; expect reversal.
- **How to identify:** a candle with a wick forming the anchor; mark quadrants/CE on the wick.
- **How to use:** target the wick (not necessarily the extreme); partial near lower-quadrant-to-CE area of the wick when running up (mirror for lows); read failure to reach CE as a warning.
