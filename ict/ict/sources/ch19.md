=== [2022 Ep2 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 2 (ID tmeCWULSTHc)] — Targets: low-hanging fruit  (aliases ICT uses: "closest target", "opposing high or low")

- **What ICT says:** Aim for the closest target: an old low or an imbalance (when short). Don't get fancy targeting the lowest low; the market can deny it. === [2022 Ep25 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 25 (ID CnTXwAuDi9Y)] — Stop placement and trailing
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Initial stop: 1–2 ticks above the high of the candle that creates the FVG. Wider alternative: above the swing high (Judas high) — if that is too much risk, trade micros (ES micro $5/point vs mini $50/handle). If you can't weather that on a micro, you're underfunded (e.g., a $100 account is asking to fail). Don't trail tight right away — positions need room to breathe; move the stop only after a larger shift in structure (a lower low taken out), then to above the corresponding lower swing high. If it then stops you, you were probably wrong or price is consolidating (ugly conditions anyway).
- **Example in this video:** 2m FVG short: stop above the FVG-creating candle was not hit, though price retraced after moving in favor.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:04](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2524) — stop above FVG candle; [0:43:39](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2619) — 1–2 ticks, swing high, micros; [0:44:25](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2665) — when to move stop.

=== [2022 Ep41 — 2022 ICT Mentorship - Episode 41 & Final (ID 2XhDi5GoNUI)] — Compounding and realistic return targets
- **Kind:** rule / numbers
- **What ICT says:**
  - 1% a day, Monday–Friday, over about four weeks compounds to about 22% a month, not 20%.
  - Fund managers consider 12–15% a year stellar and 20%+ a blockbuster year.
  - 1% every single day is unrealistic for a new trader. For a seasoned trader, a 1% average per day is "really easy". It is an average: one 5% trade in a week equals 1% a day for that week.
  - He does not sit at the charts every day for 1%.
  - With 2% risk you can use two orders on the same setup: order 1 at the same limit and stop, targeting 3780.25 (about +2.25%); order 2 targeting the short-term high or 3805.50 (about +4.5% more). That is about 6.75% in one day from one trade idea.

=== [2022 Ep41 — 2022 ICT Mentorship - Episode 41 & Final (ID 2XhDi5GoNUI)] — Hard stop is mandatory
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you do not put in a hard stop, you are not respecting the risk. You are saying "I'll figure out where I'm wrong when it gets there."
  - "I don't really use a hard stop but I know when I want to get out" tells him that trader does not have it together.
  - With this model the stop location is defined, so the hard stop must be placed, period. If it gets hit, it has done you a favor. Then your job is to manage the next trade with less risk to come out of drawdown incrementally.
  - Focus on whether price keeps showing the expected institutional order flow, not on fear of the stop being tripped. "You pay it well" to do its job.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:21](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=861) — stop does its job; watch order flow instead; [0:15:08](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=908) — no hard stop means not respecting risk; [0:40:34](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2434) — "I know when I want to get out" criticised; hard stop period.

=== [2022 Ep41 — 2022 ICT Mentorship - Episode 41 & Final (ID 2XhDi5GoNUI)] — Systematic stop management (25% at 50% of the target range; breakeven at 75%)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Define the expected range from entry to target. Example: 3754.75 to 3780.25, 25.5 points.
  - When price has moved 50% of that range in your favor (about 12.75, rounded to 12.5 points), the stop can be moved up by 25% of the stop-to-entry distance, trimming risk a little. This is not jamming it to breakeven. (Caption is ambiguous between "trim by 25%" and "to 25%"; he describes it as "moving a little bit".)
  - When price has moved 75% of the entry-to-target range, move the stop to breakeven, "period".
  - Meanwhile, judge whether you are still on the right side:
    - Is price still reaching for the objective?
    - Do down-closed candles keep supporting price?
    - Does it run short-term lows (sell stops) and rally with energy?
    - Does it drop into FVGs, re-accumulate, and send another leg higher?
    
    If those hold, together or individually, keep holding for the target.
  - For multiple positions at "competition level leverage" (3%–4.5% risk), use a static partial rule, for example always take something off at +15 or +20 points.
  - These are foundations, not ironclad rules. Adapt them to your personality and model.
- **Timestamps:** [0:45:21](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2721) — stop management: define entry-to-target range; [0:46:10](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2770) — at 50% of range, trim stop 25%; [0:46:57](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2817) — at 75%, breakeven; what to watch; [0:47:46](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2866) — competition leverage 3–4.5%, static partials at 15–20 points.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says that in many shown trades he takes partials without rushing his stop up. This rule is meant to build trust in partials and reduce fear of being stopped out.

=== [2024 #6 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #6   August 12, 2024 Begins 9:15am ET (ID woAQ1XJeD7Y)] — Partials, stop trailing and "pay yourself"  (aliases: Terminus, trade management)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take a partial before the main objective ("Terminus"); once the first partial is taken and the stop is moved to cover costs "you cannot lose", barring a gap, and the need to be right disappears. "Partials always pay you."
  - Do not punish yourself if it does not run. In volatile markets price can return many times to the entry area; if you lack a plan, take the win.
  - He shows a limit order, expand it beyond the initial level when price really moves, and take single partials at levels he likes.
  - Once the stop has been reduced (moved to a gain), do not widen it back. "If I moved it too prematurely I'm going to eat it."
  - If a trade can be closed prematurely by a failing narrative, move the stop to an advantageous location so a reversal still leaves you with something. He asks you to know why the trade is still viable, why hold, and what lets you re-enter after a stop.
  - If you realize you made a mistake entry (accidental fill), close the trade and see how you would manage it; this removes the "it has to be right" weight. Do not partially pyramid into a mistake.

=== [2024 #7 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #7   August 13, 2024 (ID -WItxLARGpE)] — Three-drives pattern (his measured-move target)  (aliases: three Indians — Street Smarts, Connors/Raschke)
- **Kind:** example only / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price pierced the weekly objective three times, which he calls a "three drives" pattern, from the book *Street Smarts* ("three Indians"). He likes classic patterns that pre-date him.
  - Classic use: he used to look for it, usually with a stochastic bearish divergence on the third high, as a reversal setup.
  - His own measured-move twist, which he says is not in the book: measure from the **low before the first high to the third swing high**. First target is **half of that range**, then the low itself.
  - He does not rely on it. Three higher highs do not guarantee a reversal; on a manipulated day the market can "grind you to dust". Some people see it as a long-term top; he has seen it also mark just an intermediate retracement before continuation.
  - He prefers to wait for the logic he teaches (next easy draw on liquidity) over predicting where the weekly closes.
- **Also:** he likes selling inside a bull flag when probabilities favor lower, i.e. he likes seeing "obvious" patterns when he is on the opposite side. He credits Nick Van Nice (not George Lane) with the trend-following stochastic divergence, which he calls a type-two divergence.

=== [2024 #10 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #10  August 16, 2024 (ID qoNQfVzVXZQ)] — Terminus, partials and the 50% rule  (aliases: "running down equity" (caption unclear), "first threshold")

- **What ICT says:** **Terminus** is where you think the trade will draw to; frame the start and the Terminus before taking anything. Inside a range, look for short-term lows/highs (single or relative equal) and any inefficiency (as in market maker buy/sell models); choose the easiest to get to (top, CE, or low of a gap) and don't demand the halfway point or the low. - **How to use:** Log the time at entry (candle 1), partial (e.g., minute 5), stopped on the balance (minute 9), and the distance (about 10 handles including spread in and out).

=== [2024 #19 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #19  August 28, 2024 (ID i3olkndPmEE)] — Dynamic trailed partial (trail stop on part of the position)  (aliases: dynamic trailing stop, trail buy stop on one contract, runner management)
- **Kind:** entry/exit model (trade management) — new emphasis
- **What ICT says:** Instead of a limit exit at a guessed level, let the market decide your partial. Example: short 2 contracts. After price breaks below the target low and builds a short-term low, every down-closed candle that creates an imbalance gets a buy stop for ONE contract placed just above that candle's high; as lower candles print, lower the stop to the previous candle's high. When it triggers you take that partial and keep the other contract (core position) running. It adds no risk, no extra leverage, and no new position; you are only managing a portion of the size.
- **How to identify / conditions:** Use it only on the 1-minute chart — "never never" on sub-one-minute charts, which are spiky/fuzzy with overlapping candles. Start trailing only after price has broken below the target low and is "spooling" (running). Stop goes above the previous 1m candle's high, ultra tight.
- **How to use:** Works for a partial or for the entire position (e.g. two contracts, trail all of it). Alternative of trailing from larger/structural candle highs gives back too much range intraday; he considers it better for swing trades held over a week (reading slightly uncertain from captions). Accept that you will be stopped out very close to the low; do not berate yourself for exits that are "not good enough". Keep trailing the remainder lower after each new lower low; do not be involved in retracements back into the old low. Averaging matters if you pyramided: your average entry moves as you add, and adding too much early can put you below the waterline (unrealised red) for a while.
- **Example in this video:** On the 1m NQ chart, price breaks Tuesday's low, makes a short-term low, retraces, then drops again; trailing the one-contract buy stop above each successive 1m down-candle high gets stopped out only a candle or two off the swing low. He cites a move quoted as "278 and a half" to "302 and 3/4" (digits of the full NQ price are unclear in the caption), implying a large capture compared with exiting beneath the old low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:22](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1222) — mechanism for 1 of 2 contracts; [0:52:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3123) — 1-minute only, never sub-minute; [0:53:39](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3219) — ultra tight trail, accept being knocked out near the low; [0:56:50](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3410) — objective, mechanical, "all you're doing is dropping your stop to the previous candle's high".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Answers repeated viewer questions about how to take partials and hold runs. He also says the same dynamic trail can be used on the whole trade. His recorded trade videos show stops far from entry because he is "not rushing to strangle the position."

=== [2026 #30 — Trade Management & Removing The Need To Be Right (ID F8s_vfK9HaM)] — Trade management: fund the trade, partial early, trail the rest  (aliases: "trade management 101", "pay myself a hassle/harassment fee", "take the risk out of the trade")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The target (here NWOG high, 30,169.75) is only the best-case scenario. If you have no way of getting paid before price reaches the terminus (the last bit of what you expect to see), it is very hard to be profitable early in a career.
  - Once price has moved in your favor, take a partial so the position is "funded" and the trade cannot become a net loser in theory. The exception he gives is a gap against you that stops the rest out above entry.
