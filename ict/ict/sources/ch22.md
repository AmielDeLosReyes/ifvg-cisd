=== [2026 #20 — NQ Trade Review Jackson Hole Symposium Day #2 (ID iSWZOe3aUFE)] — Trader Independence & Mentality
- **Kind:** rule (psychology)
- **What ICT says:** As a trader, you owe no one your results or schedule. Don't feel obligated to trade every day (even Mondays or Fridays) just because content creators, live streamers, or social media expect it. The strongest traders close profitable weeks early (take 3-day weekends) rather than chasing more money. Trading every day when you're already profitable is greed, not discipline. Conversely, don't skip trading just to please followers. Make decisions based on YOUR risk/reward, not external pressure.
- **How to identify:** Ask yourself: "Am I trading this setup because I genuinely see it, or because I feel pressured to be active?"
- **How to use:** Build decision-making independence. Close winners early if risk isn't worth it. Skip days if necessary. This communicates maturity and self-control, which is more valuable than constant activity.
- **Example in this video:** ICT stated he wouldn't trade Friday (Jackson Hole vol); when a student's sincere question prompted execution, he did it but with caution and early partials. He didn't feel obligated to "perform" for audience.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:18](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=858) "every single day... every report day," [0:15:06](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=906) "if you've been very profitable, take a 3-day weekend," [0:17:27](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=1047) "you're not the influencer, you're the influenced."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Discipline isn't trading more; it's having the courage to do less.

=== [2026 #30 — Trade Management & Removing The Need To Be Right (ID F8s_vfK9HaM)] — Right is not required for profitability  (aliases: "removing the need to be right", "correct is not the game")
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:**
  - Profitability means getting in while price moves in one direction and finding ways to get out in that direction, without needing the exact terminus you called.
  - Critics focus on "he said it would go there and it didn't" and ignore the entry logic, wick support and partials.
  - A trade can be "fruitful" even if the target is never reached. Seeing price reach the target proves you were right, but it is not what pays you.
  - Every trade is not guaranteed, and you should not demand that price reaches your target.
- **How to use:** build the exit plan so that profit is banked before the last leg, which is the purpose of the partials above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:07](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2107), [0:37:33](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2253), [0:41:27](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2487)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says nobody else teaches "all or nothing is wrong"; he positions partials as a core of his method.

=== [2026 #52 — Reviewing High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions - July 21, 2026 (ID e6XwTK4hVqI)] — Participation Discipline: When NOT to Trade
- **Kind:** rule + philosophy
- **What ICT says:** In choppy, HRLRC conditions with small ranges and multiple false breakouts, it's better to NOT participate. Close the charts, step away, let the condition resolve. The few handles you might capture aren't worth the stress and whipsaws. This is a key risk-management principle: knowing when to sit out is as important as knowing when to enter.
- **Example in this video:** ICT trades in and out multiple times, each with small profits or stops. By mid-day, he decides to close the charts and spend time with family. He explicitly says this type of choppy action isn't worth participating in.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:57](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=717) — "Leave it alone"; [0:12:43](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=763) — "Wait-and-see"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is advice many traders ignore. FOMO drives them to trade choppy conditions and lose money.

=== [2026 #61 — Post CPI NQ Futures Commentary \ Monday Analysis Delivered - 07/15/2026 (ID IF7dEkgWoO0)] — Sniper Mentality (Waiting vs. Chasing)
- **Kind:** rule · psychology · execution approach
- **What ICT says:** Don't chase price or actively pursue it. Instead, set up your entry zone (your "hide"), wait for price to "walk right into your crosshairs," and then execute. This is the opposite of chasing. "We're sitting still. We're trying to get to a vantage point where we can see the mark walk right into our crosshairs and we're not chasing it. We're not pursuing it... we're not reacting. We're anticipating." (0:16:02)
- **How to use:** Before each session, identify your target entry zones based on your model. Don't enter until price reaches those zones. If price runs past them without touching, let it go—don't chase. Wait for the next setup.
- **Example in this video:** ICT had setup zones marked in advance; price eventually walked into them. He didn't adjust or chase; he waited. (0:16:02)
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:16:02](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=962) — "We're sitting still... the mark walk right into our crosshairs... we're not chasing it... we're anticipating."

=== [2026 #101 — ICT 2026 Entries & Drills \ April 15, 2026 (ID KASpfAd1MnI)] — Timeframe indifference ("price is price")  (aliases: fractal reading)

=== [2026 #104 — ICT 2026 Futures Opening Range Tape-Reading \ April 13, 2026 (ID Il_DM4T7nzg)] — Anticipatory Price Reading (Sniper vs. Reactive)  (aliases: prediction, foresight, not reacting)
- **Kind:** skill + mindset
- **What ICT says:** Most traders react: they wait for price to move, then jump in. ICT teaches anticipation: you set your "crosshairs" ahead of where price is going. Like a sniper accounting for wind, distance, target speed—you're shooting ahead, not at the bird. This requires knowing what "should" happen, then watching to see if price does it. When it doesn't (canary dies), you know to expect the opposite.
- **How to identify:** Recognizing patterns before they complete. Expecting a wick rejection before the wick appears. Knowing "if it takes out that low, I'm expecting a run to that target."
- **How to use:** Build a model (FVG sequence, body positions, gap rules). Before price moves, say "if bodies stay above this CE, I expect acceleration to that minor buy side." Watch. If price does what you predicted, confidence rises. If it doesn't (canary warning), expect reversal. This is tape reading in the moment.
- **Example in this video:** ICT saying, "If we get above this level, this won't act as an inversion; it'll act as a bullish FVG" before the body even closed. Then watching price confirm exactly that. Or: "This reluctance tells me they're not done; they're going higher" before the reversal happened. Pre-announcing what should happen, then proving it (or proving it won't).
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:13](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2413) sniper analogy: anticipate, don't react. [0:44:58](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2698) warning signs (canary) when price doesn't do what should happen. [0:52:35](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=3155) anticipate before they form; don't wait for chart to show it after the fact.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Anticipation requires you to get wrong sometimes. The skill is improving prediction rate over time (70%+), not achieving 100%.

=== [2026 #104 — ICT 2026 Futures Opening Range Tape-Reading \ April 13, 2026 (ID Il_DM4T7nzg)] — Reticular Activating System (RAS) / Pattern Recognition  (aliases: seeing repeating signatures, confidence building)
- **Kind:** psychology
- **What ICT says:** When you buy a car, you suddenly see that car everywhere—not because it's more common, but because you're now tuned to notice it (RAS activated). Same with price action: once you activate your RAS for a FVG sequence or body rejection pattern, you start seeing it everywhere, and your confidence grows. You begin to **anticipate** it before it forms.
- **How to identify:** Exposing yourself to price action repeatedly. After 10 times seeing a body close above a CE and then rally, your brain automatically expects the 11th time.
- **How to use:** This is why tape reading (not trading) is valuable: repeated exposure builds RAS. Once RAS is activated, you recognize setups in real time, and entries become obvious (not guessing).
- **Example in this video:** ICT's students who've watched streams develop the ability to spot FVG sequences and body rejections immediately, without thinking. His own prediction of "reluctance here tells me they want higher" happens because his RAS has seen it 1,000 times.
- **Timestamps:** [0:37:49](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2269) car analogy: your eye jumps to it subconsciously; same for patterns in price.
- **Nuance / caveats:** This is not mystical; it's neurobiology. Repeated exposure + conscious study = pattern recognition.

=== [2026 #108 — ICT 2026 OR Tape-reading \ April 08, 2026 (ID yqBvOeOKcBM)] — Scar Tissue (aliases: trauma from losses, loss aversion, emotional residue)
- **Kind:** psychology
- **What ICT says:** When a trader takes a big loss, they develop "scar tissue." Every subsequent trade feels like it might be another big loss. They flinch at normal market moves, expecting them to be catastrophic. The scar tissue makes you tentative and prevents you from entering trades at all. The remedy: avoid big losses through position sizing and stops. If you do take a loss, don't rush back in; give yourself time to process it. Eventually, the scar tissue fades.
- **How to identify:** Do you feel like every candle is going to hurt you? Do you avoid setups that look similar to ones that lost you money? That's scar tissue talking.
- **How to use:** Acknowledge it's there. Then deliberately place small trades to prove to yourself that not every trade is a disaster. Build winning trades to replace the emotional imprint of the losing one.
- **Example in this video:** ICT says if you get hit by a 400-handle candle (which can happen in volatile markets), the scar tissue will make you scared of every subsequent 1-minute candle. You'll avoid trading altogether.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:46](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2446) discusses scar tissue from large losses; [0:55:09](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=3309) emphasizes that scar tissue is common and manageable.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is emotional/psychological, not technical. ICT acknowledges it as a real barrier to success.
