=== [2022 Ep6 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 6 (ID Bkt8B3kLATQ)] — Market efficiency paradigm (smart money vs uninformed retail)  (aliases ICT uses: "market efficiency paradigm", "informed/smart money", "speculative uninformed money")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Two camps: a small circle of informed/smart money and a large collective of speculative uninformed money, with constant inflow of new money and outflow as accounts blow up. Smart money uses no secret indicators (no stochastics/RSI); it looks at time and price, and time is the most crucial element (time of day builds the likelihood of volatility and of short-term reversals). Smart money "cannibalizes" retail, which is typically wrong on bias and on stop placement. Smart money's question is the narrative: is the daily range going higher, and how — e.g., lower first to sucker shorts and run sell stops so they can acquire longs, then rally into the afternoon/close. Using retail liquidity this way is the market efficiency paradigm.
- **How to identify:** n/a.
- **How to use:** "We look to enter longs where retail sells; shorts where retail buys." "We anticipate price seeking opposing liquidity." Don't trade patterns for their own sake, indicator readings, or momentum.
- **Example in this video:** He notes the e-mini markets' 4:00 PM algorithmic run "today" was relentless (one of the times he gave earlier).
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:02](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2) — enter longs where retail sells; [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=100) — price seeks opposing liquidity; two camps; [0:03:16](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=196) — time and price, time most important; [0:06:24](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=384) — narrative: lower first to sucker shorts, then rally.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Trend is your friend" fails at the end of a trend; every rule has an exception — that's experience.

=== [2022 Ep12 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 (ID 8GkQfdAXZP0)] — Invalidation rule: an ITH/ITL must not be violated
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If bearish and an imbalance is rebalanced, the next short-term high should be lower than that ITH. If price trades higher than the ITH, "your trade idea's probably flawed".
  - Don't force the trade again. Wait for market structure to get back in sync with your expectation. That's how you avoid blowing the account or forcing your will on the market.
  - If the ITH or ITL breaks after your idea became possible, go to the sidelines and don't re-enter. The next setup may be the same session, the same day, or the following week.
  - While the key high holds, trust the move and keep holding.

=== [2022 Ep19 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 19 (ID IEa1N0rTtbc)] — Smart-money order pairing  (aliases: offset, pairing orders, counterparty)
- **Kind:** rule (narrative logic)
- **What ICT says:** When price purges sell stops below old lows, smart money pairs their buy orders with those sell stops and is now net long. To get out they must sell to willing buyers at higher prices: the buy stops resting above old highs. So after sell-side is taken, the draw is to buy-side. Continuum: smart-money buys ↔ retail sells, smart-money sells ↔ retail buys. If you don't think about how orders are paired before you click "enter," you are gambling.

=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — How far can it go: ranges & draw
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**

=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — Algorithmic delivery vs. buying/selling pressure
- **Kind:** definition (worldview)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price is scripted by time and price, not buying and selling pressure. The algorithm refers to salient prior price points at given times.
  - HFT-style algos don't buy while price expands up. They wait for price to reach the inefficiency threshold, then fire long entries on the **first downtick** after it's met.

  - Limit-up analogy: one contract can open a market limit up.

=== [2024 #18 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #18  August 27, 2024 (ID yOZHawAE8QI)] — ES short review: sequence of order flow before the previous-day-low run
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says (step by step):**
  1. Price is dropping toward Monday's (8/26) low. Every candle returning to the order block fails to take half, so it is a propulsion block.
  2. An indecisive candle (open equals close) builds a volume imbalance; the next candle comes up and fills the void with no bodies ("patchwork", his term for the algorithm filling inefficiencies).
  3. Price displaces lower. It comes back and digs into the original OB (reclaimed bearish OB), responds immediately, retraces into an FVG, hits it, displaces lower, takes out the low, retraces to match the high of the inefficiency with no willingness to enter, and then breaks lower.
  4. He sells in the lower part of the large down-close candle as the next candle opens and digs into it, then adds in the volume imbalance, expecting the upper half never to trade.
  5. The market "rolls over energetically" and runs just below Monday's low; his fill was one tick from the candle's low.
- **How to use:** The "heaviness" signature is repeated refusal to trade the upper half of arrays on the way down. He said the market was "indicating in every shape, facet or form that it's heavy".

=== [2024 #20 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #20  August 29, 2024 (ID NMCy1H2_Wr8)] — Cutting a fizzling trade in half  (aliases: scale out when trade quality fades)
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** If the technicals that justified the trade fade, take half off immediately if you have a profit (or close the whole position if it is one contract), even if commissions are a "hundred bucks". - **Kind:** core concept / execution rule
- **What ICT says:** When looking at an inefficiency (gap/SIBI/BISI) in the direction of your bias, price can *wick* into the opposite half (e.g., wick above midpoint while bearish), but it *cannot lay down a body* in that opposite half if the inefficiency is to remain valid. - **How to identify:**

  2. Determine your bias (bearish = expect bodies in lower half; bullish = expect bodies in upper half).
  3. Watch each candle: is the body in the correct half, or has it crossed into the "do not trespass" zone?
  4. If the body never lays in the wrong half, the inefficiency is valid.
  5. If a body lays in the wrong half, that's a violation of the inefficiency and suggests a change in direction.
