=== [2022 Ep2 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 2 (ID tmeCWULSTHc)] — Fair Value Gap (FVG) / imbalance  (aliases ICT uses: "imbalance", "buy-side imbalance", "fair value gap")
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** An imbalance is where one candle moves in one direction only and the candle before's high and candle after's low don't overlap (for an up candle); price only went one way there and nothing offset it, so it has to be "efficiently delivered" on the opposite side (the algorithm must deliver equal price in the other direction to book it). He introduced "fair value gap" in 2016. Mark the first candle's low and third candle's high (for a bearish gap) as the FVG.
- **How to identify:** Three candles; the middle candle's range is not overlapped by candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick. Bearish FVG: candle 1 low above candle 3 high.
- **How to use:** After the MSS, when price trades back up into the FVG, sell short. If you can't get it on that timeframe, drop to a lower one (e.g., from 3m to 1m, or from 5m to 1m) and find an FVG there. A bullish (buy-side) imbalance below in discount is also a target, because price wants to rebalance it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 2m, Friday: the big displacement candle breaking the short-term low leaves an FVG between that candle sequence; the next candle opens, trades higher and stops right in the gap: sell entry. The downside target was a buy-side imbalance (one up candle, prior candle high vs next candle low) located just below the 50% of the day's range near old lows; price traded down into it and "completely closed" it with down-closing price.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:56](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1076) — imbalance defined; [0:29:18](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1758) — price goes up into the area: sell; scale down timeframes; [0:30:04](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1804) — "fair value gap", introduced 2016; [0:38:04](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2284) — buy-side imbalance as target, closed in.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says 1m, 2m, 3m charts are best for finding imbalances in indices because HFT algorithms operate at timeframes up to about 3 minutes (often 15s/30s/45s/60s intervals).

=== [2022 Ep2 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 2 (ID tmeCWULSTHc)] — Displacement entry, stop placement, and chasing  (aliases ICT uses: "sell into the gap", "don't chase")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Enter as price trades up into the FVG; it feels scary to sell while price is rising, but that is what you want (retail wants "confirmation" and comes in late). If price has started moving lower you can still get in near the FVG, but once a short-term low is taken out you are chasing; market orders there risk slippage and larger risk. Stop: above the swing high (the stop-run high) or above the FVG-forming candle's high, whichever fits your risk parameters. Alternative: a sell stop order in the gap candle to trip you in, with stop above that candle's high (may be too wide).
- **How to identify:** n/a.
- **How to use:** Use micros while learning: full contract leverage on NQ can "burn you" if price moves 100 points quickly.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 2m short "at 14,800" (he abbreviates "798"), covered at "675" (about 14,675) as price hit the top of the target imbalance range, about 120-130 handles.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:26](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1946) — entry and stop options; [0:33:13](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1993) — can still get in near the area; [0:34:02](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2042) — chasing, slippage; sell when the candle is going up; [0:43:47](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2627) — sell stop entry alternative.

=== [2022 Ep3 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 3 (ID nQfHZ2DEJ8c)] — Fair Value Gap (FVG) as MSS entry; stacked FVGs rule
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:** FVG = a candle with one single pass up, and the next candle's low doesn't fully overlap the prior candle's high (gap remains). The candle after the MSS that forms an FVG is "at the earliest" where you'd trade. When there are two FVGs, one above the other (for a long), he lets price trade down into the lower one, sacrificing that better entry; if it trades into the lower one and comes back up into the higher (first) FVG, he enters there and expects the lower one won't be retraded. If price runs up into the target zone first, before retracing to the entry FVG, he nixes the trade and sits out.
- **How to identify:** After an MSS, the gap left by the displacement candle.
- **How to use:** Enter on the retrace into the FVG (limit or market). During the trade, a dip back into the lower FVG is permissible; if price overlaps the entire lower FVG and goes lower, close the trade (save the stop). Watch whether candle bodies stay inside the FVG (sign of accumulation).
- **Example in this video:** Live NQ 1m, Jan 25, 2022: bullish MSS, green-box FVG with a smaller FVG just below it. Price dips into the lower FVG, leaving wicks, comes back into the green FVG; he buys at market. Price stabs down once more into the wicks (he says they took it down to accumulate more longs, not selling pressure); max drawdown about $455. Then rallies to the bottom of the pink rectangle (an imbalance in premium) where he flattens: +$1,190, account $27,915.11.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:45](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=825) — FVG after MSS, earliest entry; [0:41:38](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2498) — two FVGs rule; [0:43:12](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2592) — nix trade if target hit first; [0:45:36](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2736) — permissible dip into lower FVG; close if it overlaps fully.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says he only draws these on the chart for teaching; trades a naked chart.

=== [2022 Ep6 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 6 (ID Bkt8B3kLATQ)] — Bearish ICT Fair Value Gap (SIBI) — exact definition  (aliases ICT uses: "bearish ICT fair value gap", "institutional order flow pattern")
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** Three-candle formation, preferably after a run above an old high. Candle 1's low is traded below by candle 2; candle 3 makes an extended low below candle 2 but its high does NOT trade back up to candle 1's low. The space between candle 1's low and candle 3's high was traded only by candle 2 — only sell side was offered there, buy side was not efficiently offered. Paint roller analogy: rolling down leaves porous pockets; you roll back up over the same area to fix it — the algorithm returns to the area for efficient delivery. When price trades back into it and you're bearish, that's a short signal. Optimal formations come after a run into buy-side liquidity (above a single high or multiple highs such as a double top); nothing before that candle matters except that an old high was run. The FVG is smart money's "saving grace" if they missed selling above the high.
- **How to identify:** Label candles 1, 2, 3. Gap resides in candle 2. Upper boundary = low of candle 1. Lower boundary = high of candle 3.
- **How to use:** Easiest entry: sell limit just above candle 3's high (one tick, or one to two ticks/handle, "whatever you believe is ideal"; one tick removes guesswork). Stop above candle 1's high or above candle 2's high (whichever you can afford); while learning, use the wider stop. If the risk is too much, don't take the trade.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3 2022 1m (see worked example).
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=672) — three-candle rules; [0:12:47](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=767) — paint roller analogy; [0:15:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=912) — optimal after run into BSL; [0:16:46](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1006) — boundaries, limit entry, stop.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Experienced students know how to reduce the stop; he deliberately teaches the wide stop to protect beginners. He insists it not be renamed or repackaged.

=== [2022 Ep10 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 10 (ID S9ORTYmXwdE)] — Paint-roller analogy for imbalance and rebalancing (FVG logic)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The chart background is the wall and each candle is a paint roller. A big, long candle leaves "porous" places where price wasn't efficiently offered to the other side (not enough time or back-and-forth for buyers to participate). There's a strong (not universal) tendency for price to go back and overlap the whole range, repainting it evenly. Once a range is "completely balanced" there's no reason for price to hang around there.

- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** If price spends too much time (more than 1–2 candles) inside a FVG, the gap has lost validity. A strong gap is entered and exited in 1–2 candles *maximum*. Time inside = hesitation = weakness. Prolonged candles in a gap means the market is not in a hurry to move, which contradicts the premise that the gap should be filled/rejected urgently.
- **How to identify:** Count how many candles price spends with bodies inside the gap. If >2, flag it as weak.
- **How to use:** 
  - If you're short and price lingers in a gap for 3+ candles, consider taking off a position.

  - Use time distortion as a stop or pivot signal: if price *should* be moving but isn't, it's a warning.

=== [2026 #139 — ICT 2026 Smart Money Concepts In Action \ January 06, 2026 (ID Jxl5UJk_ylc)] — Premium Gap-Up Entry & The "Non-Participation" Test
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** When a market gaps up (premium opening), ICT tests whether the gap will fill (bearish) or be rejected (bullish). "So, what I'm looking for is it to kind of like shun that, not want to do it at all. And then if it does negate that and not go back down into this orange shaded area, then this was a draw." If price refuses to fill the gap down to the previous RTH settlement, it proves the gap is a legitimate bullish move.
- **How to identify:** Gap up opens. Draw a line at the previous RTH settlement (the fill point). Watch if price wants to test it or rejects it.
- **How to use:** If price shuns the gap-fill and rallies instead, the gap is real; stay long. If price fills the gap and closes below it, the gap was a trap (what's sometimes called "trap and run" or rejection).
- **Example in this video:** NQ gapped up on Jan 6; ICT was watching to see if it would pull down to RTH settlement (blue line) or immediately reject that level and rally. His longs were predicated on price NOT wanting to fill the gap.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:38](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=278) (setting up the test: "if it's going to go back down here...I think it's going to be bearish")
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a live trading decision point. Many trades fail because price DOES fill the gap; only reject participation when price truly shuns the fill level.
