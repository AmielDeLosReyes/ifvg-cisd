=== [2024 #10 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #10  August 16, 2024 (ID qoNQfVzVXZQ)] — Clean vs. unorganized ("sloppy", "fickle/thickle", "picket fence") price action  (aliases: "choppy day", "one-sided train day")
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:** A day or session is *clean* if you see impulsive legs leaving a singular FVG, a singular short-term low, a run to an obvious level, a retracement with a single FVG, and a quick move to another obvious level. If not, don't push hard and demand more conviction. *Unorganized/sloppy*: overlapping candles ("picket fence"), balanced price range with no speed, many little gaps and highs/lows but no obvious side; it's usually the day when people "get chopped up". Wait for displacement; once an inefficiency exists, watch how price reacts when it returns into it. A **one-sided "train"** day (like Thursday and Tuesday) is the other bad environment: don't chase; get in before it starts or stay out; hold and be out of the way. Explanatory apple analogy: with one green apple among reds your eye finds it; with many reds you get distracted — clean = only one obvious draw.
- **How to use:** Screenshot examples of both and label them; avoid 8:30 news for beginners (he told Caleb to wait until after). On a choppy Friday: do your one setup and be done.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:51](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1791) one-sided train day (Thursday) · [0:46:11](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2771) apples analogy · [1:38:31](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5911) criteria for a clean day · [1:54:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6842) picket fence · [1:44:12](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6252) balanced price range, no speed
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Fickle = overlapping, sputtering; occurs after the weekly 20% retracement.

=== [2024 #20 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #20  August 29, 2024 (ID NMCy1H2_Wr8)] — High vs low resistance liquidity runs and when a market is tradable  (aliases: low-resistance conditions, 50/50 market)
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:** A low-resistance run is easy, one-sided, gives immediate positive feedback; analysis is not conflicted. If the market can be framed both ways, it is 50/50, not high probability, and not worth putting money behind; just study it ("tape it") with no weight on the outcome. If you must be 100% nimble with zero forgiveness for being wrong on either side, that is a high-probability losing day. When references conflict and "it could be this or that," accept it is not time to trade. If the first FVG has not even been traded to and nothing qualifies, close the charts and review that night.
- **How to use:** Frame the scenario in both directions; if both are plausible, skip. Sitting out is a "position that wins every single time" and you never lose money sitting still. If price later does the bullish thing, sitting still was still the right call, not a regret.
- **Example in this video:** NQ stayed below the 19,694 high for most of the session, chopping around the layered gaps: one side said run to 19,694, the other said drop into the first presentation. He wrote it off as 50/50 and said a student following his criteria would be flat.

=== [2024 #33 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ How To Avoid Seek & Destroy \ Tape Reading \ September 23, 2024 (ID Qg3O1GkJDeQ)] — Time distortion — recognising a held / strangled market  (aliases ICT uses: time distortion, "held", "strangled", muddy, dirty, holding pattern)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price spending too much time inside inefficiencies (any gap, FVG, wick, the unfilled part of an opening range gap) is the signature that the market is being held — it is not permitted to run yet.
  - He wants to see price spend time ABOVE old highs (accumulating new longs) or BELOW old lows (accumulating new shorts), but not inside inefficiencies.
  - Buying and selling pressure exists but price barely moves; it is kept "artificially" in a tight range; when the range is released the move is cleaner and more sustainable, so the afternoon session should be good.
  - Trading ahead of the release gets you beat up ("chopped up"); "the better you recognise when the market is being held, the better your trading".
  - Signs listed: too many wicks that are not adjacent, so he cannot draw a clean gray (precision) level; a PD array that is only touched with no displacement; the market that "looks like a technical day" turning into a holding pattern; a medium-impact news driver at 9:45 (Flash Manufacturing PMI) causing a flurry but no meaningful run to highs or lows followed by a rejection.
  - With muddy morning probabilities he elects to wait until 10:00 (Silver Bullet starts) and use the first presented FVG then.
- **How to identify:** price repeatedly re-entering the same gap; overlapping candles; small ranges; many equal-ish wicks; he says it can look like consolidation inside a range between two obvious highs/lows.
- **How to use:** do not press; stand aside; use 10:00 Silver Bullet; revisit in the afternoon. He tells viewers to screenshot the chart and label it "time distortion and very difficult morning session conditions".
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/15s Monday Sept 23 9:30–10:35 ET: price chops inside last week's gaps and the unfilled lower part of a premium opening range gap; it clears the early highs, then consolidates under and around them; at 10:31 it is still ranging between the same high and low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898) muddy = time distortion, wait till 10:00 · [0:40:04](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2404) "the time distortion is real" · [0:41:14](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2474) why he isn't pressing the button · [0:42:00](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2520) above old highs / below old lows vs inside inefficiencies · [1:02:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3757) held artificially, release gives a nice afternoon

=== [2024 #43 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ NQ High Resistance Liquidity Conditions Tape Reading \ October 07, 2024 (ID DzJpkxI057g)] — High / Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases: "high resistance liquidity run", "low resistance liquidity run")
- **Kind:** definition | rule (market-condition filter)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ask yourself early in the session which condition delivery is in. He asks the viewers explicitly "are we in low resistance liquidity run conditions or high resistance," and answers: high resistance, "that's why it's [so] deliberate" (slow/choppy).
  - Diagnostic he gives: "are you seeing fair value gaps spending a lot of time inside them?" If price keeps spending time inside FVGs / inefficiencies, you are in high resistance.
  - High resistance means: lack of precision; moves are deferred; retracements are deeper; price runs pull back "real deep each time"; targets (PD arrays) are not delivered to as precisely. It is described as uncomfortable, especially when building larger positions.
  - Low resistance means: easy to time, easy to frame low risk, easy to find low-hanging objectives for partials and ultimate targets; price "goes where you want it to go"; smooth run to the target, then you go to the sidelines and wait for the next setup. It rewards patience and goes to targets "almost near effortlessly."
  - You can trade high resistance conditions, but expect many more stop-outs, frustration and anxiety in and during the trade.
  - Just because you see a setup does not make it high probability; in high resistance "nothing in here is high probability." The danger is looking back afterwards and thinking "I wish I'd done this."
  - He refers to this as a concept "I shared with you last week" (prior video in the series), and notes that knowing it lets you sit on your hands.
  - Observation that carries forward: if the morning session is high resistance and unclear, the lunch macro and afternoon session should be cleaner (setups easier to identify).
- **How to identify:** (1) FVGs/inefficiencies are being traded inside for long stretches rather than displaced away from; (2) price is range-bound and "disorganized" after the open; (3) retracements are deep and not precise; (4) no clean displacement leaving fresh FVGs.
- **How to use:** If high resistance: be slow to act, do not force the A+ model, expect the textbook return-to-PD-array entry NOT to come, and consider framing a different reference (here, an inefficiency that price was likely to be drawn to). If low resistance: take the clean model (see below) and target quickly.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday 7 Oct 2024, 9:30–10:30 ET. Price chops around the opening range gap area and the first presented FVG without displacing; the mid-gap is not reached within the first 30 minutes. ICT labels it high resistance at [0:51:04] and says that is why nothing "perfect" developed.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:51:04](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3064) — asks viewers to classify delivery; high resistance diagnosed ("FVGs spending a lot of time inside them").
  - [0:52:42](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3162) — contrast with low resistance: easy to time, easy low-risk framing, fewer stop-outs.
  - [0:56:55](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3415) — high resistance pulls back deep each time; "not a fun market"; low resistance feels effortless.
  - [1:00:09](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3609) — absence of precision means PD arrays are not delivered to as perfectly.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is the practical filter that sits above the entry model: the same setup definition is valid in both conditions, but its probability and precision are not. He frames it as what he teaches "my son" and addresses a student, "Caleb", throughout (a teaching device, not a separate rule).

=== [2024 #48 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ How ICT Picks Winning FVG's & Orderblocks \ October 25, 2024 (ID svYZKOrWPRo)] — Large-range day anticipation: 3-day average range (and 5-day alternative)  (aliases: "large range day", "range expansion day", "small range day / compression")
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Large range" is measured against the average daily range of the last 3 days. If the current day's range is larger than that average, it is outside the normal 3-day range.
  - A day that closes with a range smaller than the 3-day average is a "small range day". It is "mathematically defined" and very sensitive.
  - Stricter or more sensitive version: use a 5-day average. If daily ranges start printing smaller than the last 5 days, you are close to a large-range expansion day.
  - Compression precedes expansion. He frames it as twisting a metal spring tighter and tighter until it releases (a borrowed image from Larry Williams). The smaller the ranges get, the bigger the likely expansion.
  - The large day usually comes the very next day, or worst case the second day after. The compression gives no direction on its own; you still need higher-timeframe order flow or the draw on liquidity.
  - He says large-range days are about 90% low-resistance liquidity runs (he first misspoke "high"). If the day is not a large range day, you will probably meet high-resistance runs instead, "not all the time but 90%".
- **How to identify:** compute the average range of the last 3 days (optionally 5). If yesterday's range is below that average, or an inside day, flag the next day as a large-range candidate.
- **How to use:** as a volatility filter that says WHEN an expansion is likely. Direction comes from the DOL (see next concept). On such a day he is willing to trade pre-market and hold, expects little retracement, and expects the day's open to be near the low (bullish case).
- **Example in this video:** Wed 10/23 NQ large down day. Thu 10/24 range sat inside Wed's range, with a higher low and lower high. Fri 10/25 was expected to be the large bullish range day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443) 3-day average and what counts as "large"; [0:17:05](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1025) spring analogy; [1:37:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5857) restated as the math for anticipating a large range day; [1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906) 5-day version and the 90% low-resistance figure.
- **Nuance / caveats:** he admits an expansion day inside the last 3 days can skew the average. He notes the smallest-range-in-7-days idea (Toby Crabel, from the book "Street Smarts" by Raschke & Connors, 1995) and Larry Williams' inside-day precursor as influences, and says the 3-day average is his own repeating theme.

=== [2024 #48 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ How ICT Picks Winning FVG's & Orderblocks \ October 25, 2024 (ID svYZKOrWPRo)] — Low-resistance vs high-resistance liquidity runs  (aliases: "high/low resistance liquidity run conditions", "fuzzy price action")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Low-resistance run:** little give and take. Price goes straight to a PD array and "gone in 30 seconds". Down-close candles (in a bullish run) support price higher when price returns to them, and inefficiencies (FVGs) are left partly open instead of being fully closed. Retracements are shallow. Large-range days are about 90% low-resistance.
  - **High-resistance run:** lots of back-and-forth, overlapping ranges, every fair value gap gets closed in, and price spends a lot of time inside inefficiencies. That is the tell that you are in high resistance. Thursday was an example, visible on the 15m, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 minute and sub-minute charts. It can blow accounts (combines) or tilt you.
  - Write the two characteristic lists in your daily journal and have them in front of you at the start of each day.
- **How to use:**
  - **Low resistance:** pay at or below the PD arrays that will hold. Keep stops very tight (he was comfortable adding as price neared his stop). Do not rush partials. Manage with the stop and not with early partials.
  - **High resistance:** don't run the stop to breakeven quickly (let it move) or don't trade. It is the environment where a fast-trailed stop gets hit, and he treats the days as ones to avoid or trade lightly.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday 10/24 as high resistance and Friday 10/25 as low resistance. On Friday, the OB at the lower quadrant held, the FVG that formed left its lower portion open (a breakaway gap), and price ran in a straight line to the NWOG and the 15m smooth highs.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:58](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=2578) large range days deliver as low-resistance runs; [0:51:44](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3104) Thursday as high resistance; [1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744) low-resistance characteristics; [1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793) high-resistance characteristics; [1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128) "signatures of a low-resistance run".
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says nobody else teaches this and he did not know he needed it earlier in his career. The stop advice in the high-resistance context is loosely worded in the captions (lines around 1:19:53 and 1:42:29). See the stop-management concept below.

=== [2024 #49 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ High Resistance & Low Resistance Conditions \ October 28, 2024 (ID 3YfetuXVhOE)] — High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases ICT uses: high resistance conditions, "harder delivery", fuzzy / scribbling back-and-forth price action)

=== [2026 #20 — NQ Trade Review Jackson Hole Symposium Day #2 (ID iSWZOe3aUFE)] — Seek and Destroy Model
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Market maker pattern of taking out buy-side and sell-side liquidity in alternating runs. Price runs up to take out buy-side stops (above prior highs), then reverses to take out sell-side stops (below prior lows), then reverses again upward. This back-and-forth pattern — low, higher high, lower low, higher high, etc. — is the "seek and destroy" where institutions systematically collect both long and short stop-losses. Occurs frequently on event-driven weeks (Jackson Hole, FOMC, NFP).
- **How to identify:** Mark the highs and lows on a 1-minute chart; look for alternating pattern where each swing extends beyond the prior swing. Example: Low → Higher high → Lower low (below first low) → Higher high (above second high).
- **How to use:** Anticipate the pattern; expect multiple "second runs" (reversals to take out the other side's stops). Don't panic on reversals; they're part of the pattern. Hold through reversals if your original thesis (direction) is intact on larger timeframe.
- **Example in this video:** Price ran low (took out sell-side), then higher high (took out buy-side), then lower low (took out new sell-side), then higher high (took out new buy-side) — classic seek and destroy on an event-driven day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664) "second runs," [0:11:56](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=716) "that is the seek and destroy model. That's it."

  - Signature to look for when bearish: stretched-out down-close candles ("black limousines" on his color scheme), expanding range size, contracting counter-direction, and unfilled gaps left in the back part of the run (breakaway/continuation/measuring gaps), meaning the market is weak (captions garbled).
- **How to identify:** Overlapping candle ranges with no clean inefficiency to trade from = high resistance. Visible FVGs/volume imbalances that price returns to and then expands from = low resistance.
- **How to use:**
  - In high resistance do not trade the "scale in, add if it pokes outside the bottom of the gap" approach; the odds fall because volatility and chop are high. In low resistance he does that "90% of the time" (scaling in, adding, adding).
  - Use the 15-minute chart as the bellwether to recognise the condition, then go to the 1-minute/sub-1-minute only if conditions are low resistance.
- **Seasonal rhythm he gives (opinion, from experience):** early-year months are the accumulation phase / high resistance; he deliberately measures risk by not participating the first month or two, then dabbles around the first/second Tuesday of February to gauge the tone (do orders come easily? do I feel in sync? are targets met easily?). Participation normally picks up in the second week of February; March should look clearly different. Summer: mostly high resistance with maybe one or two low-resistance days; fall (from the last two weeks of August or early September through the first/second week of December): low resistance; holiday period back to about 50/50.

=== [2026 #129 — ICT 2026 Smart Money Concepts Lecture \ February 23, 2026 (ID NxwfXE6Zyns)] — High Resistance vs Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Low resistance conditions show volume imbalances and FVGs, big one-sided range candles in one direction, small-body short-lived retracements against it, and longer protractions with more candles in the run's direction. High resistance conditions are the opposite: price stays inside the previous candle's range and inside inefficiencies longer than it should, "not in a hurry to go anywhere". Typical at the start of the year; he expects the shift toward more freely running conditions toward spring (last couple of weeks of March, April, May).
- **How to identify:** Price keeps overlapping the prior candle's range, wicks poke outside and come back, inefficiencies take a long time to fill.
- **How to use:** In high resistance, be highly selective and cherry-pick days; precision is worse (a wick-only deviation outside a range is not a clean signal).

=== [2026 #136 — ICT 2026 Smart Money Concepts Lecture \ January 29, 2026 (ID 8wHewi6N6dg)] — Low-resistance delivery vs "fuzzy" delivery  (aliases ICT uses: low resistance liquidity runs, porous wake, fuzzy, intervention)
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:**
  - He wants low-resistance liquidity runs where price leaves pools of liquidity and inefficiencies in its wake ("a porous wake").
  - If price spends a lot of time back and forth over previous candles, sharing the same space as previous ranges, he calls that "fuzzy": it is not algorithmic delivery, it is intervention ("them working previous ranges, constantly going after orders"). In that case he will not bet his heaviest hand.
- **How to identify:** Overlapping candle bodies, repeated re-trading of old ranges; no clean inefficiencies left behind.
- **How to use:** Reduce size or stand aside when delivery is fuzzy; save size for clean runs.
