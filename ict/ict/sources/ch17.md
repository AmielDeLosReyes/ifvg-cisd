=== [2022 Ep7 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 7 (ID G8-z91acgG4)] — 2022 Mentorship entry model (raid, MSS, FVG), timed on a correlated index
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sequence: price trades below an old low (SSL taken), then displacement and a bullish market structure shift (breaks a swing high). Look back through the up-leg for a FVG and enter on the retrace into it.
  - If price instead keeps going lower after your entry, "that's what your stop is for."
  - For homework, check the 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute charts. He used the 2-minute chart that day.
- **How to identify:** old low, then a run below it, then a displacement up through a swing high, then the FVG in that leg.
- **How to use:** Stop just below the low candle of the move (about 1 tick below). Target the short-term high or BSL.
- **Example in this video:**

    - He exited at 14,622.75, above the 14,620 high: about 117 handles on 1 NQ mini.
  - ES 2m:
    - Price traded below the old low (SSL taken) with displacement down, then displacement up.
    - It broke a swing high (bullish MSS) and left a FVG.
    - The 10:36 candle traded down into that FVG, which was the long signal in ES.
    - ES then ran to the short-term high around 4495/4496 ("44.95 level"), swept it, accumulated more, and ran higher through the longer-term old high.
  - He used the timing of the ES FVG entry (10:36) to buy NQ: "trading what's not in the chart."

=== [2022 Ep19 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 19 (ID IEa1N0rTtbc)] — Fair Value Gap — fractal "signature" of the 2022 model  (aliases: imbalance, fair value gap, "the gap")
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:** The repeating signature he wants students to look for is: price creates a FVG, breaks a low (or high), comes back and fills the gap. It may partially fill, go halfway, or completely close it; you do not need pinpoint precision, an "average" entry with sound money management is enough. The same pattern he teaches on 1m–5m exists on the hourly chart; it applies to forex, bonds and futures, not just index futures.
- **How to identify:** Break in structure, then a FVG made by one candle (the gap between the candle before's low and candle after's high); look at candle *bodies*, allow wicks/tails to poke through ("the volume of the move is inside the bodies").
- **How to use:** The retrace into the gap typically lands near 50% of the "parent" price swing (the whole swing from the low to the high that produced the gap).
- **Example in this video:** DXY hourly at the 101 level: market drops, takes a swing low, leaves a FVG; price comes back up and the candle bodies respect the gap while wicks go slightly above it. Then it "breaks aggressively," runs stops below several lows, fills the gap and goes right below the low, which was ~50% (equilibrium) of the parent swing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=389) — the signature described; [0:10:27](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=627) — DXY hourly, bodies respecting the FVG = algorithmic delivery; [0:13:38](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=818) — hourly fractal of the YouTube model, 50% of parent swing; [0:15:14](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=914) — single-candle imbalance, bodies vs wicks.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He stresses this is "algorithm delivering price," not supply/demand, harmonics or indicators.

=== [2022 Ep24 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 24 (ID nv0ey5UyOmY)] — 2022 Mentorship model — where setups form (bearish version via relative equal highs)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:** Setup forms after price runs above an old high or relative equal highs. As soon as price trades above the relative equal highs, scan back through the run-up and find the **nearest short-term low** — that is the trigger. It is not enough that price goes above the highs and simply drifts back down below them; price must go below that short-term low **with displacement** ("energetic move"). That displacement leg is "the foundation of the setup." Only then go into that leg and look for a fair value gap to short into.
- **How to identify:** (1) relative equal highs / old high level; (2) price runs through it; (3) identify the nearest short-term low formed during the run-up; (4) displacement takes out that short-term low = market structure shift; (5) FVG inside the displacement leg.
- **How to use:** Sell when price retraces up into the FVG in the displacement leg. Until the short-term low is taken out, "there's no trade there, there's no setup there." Reverse everything for longs (run below relative equal lows/old low, displacement above nearest short-term high, buy the FVG).
- **Example in this video:** Diagram drawn: green line = relative equal highs; price approaches, small correction, approaches again, then runs through; trigger = nearest short-term low in that run; displacement through it; FVG in the leg.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:41](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1901) — "where do setups form" journal subtitle; [0:33:16](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1996) — nearest short-term low is the trigger; [0:34:03](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2043) — no displacement through the low = no setup.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He calls this the filter that removes low-probability trades — the displacement requirement is what separates a real shift from a simple dip back below the highs.

=== [2022 Ep24 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 24 (ID nv0ey5UyOmY)] — 2022 Mentorship model — second entry framing: raid into an old FVG
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Same logic as relative equal highs, but the "level" being raided is an old fair value gap above price (bearish case). Price trades up into the old FVG; it does **not** have to completely close the gap — trading anywhere between its low and high is good. Simplify it: treat the **low of the fair value gap** exactly like the relative equal highs. Once price trades above the FVG's low into it, go back through that up-leg, find the trigger (short-term low); when it is taken out = the market structure shift; the move down is the displacement leg; then look for a (new) FVG in that leg to sell.
- **How to identify:** old opposing FVG overhead; price trades into it (partial fill OK); nearest short-term low of the run-up taken out with displacement; new FVG formed.
- **How to use:** Short the new FVG in the displacement leg. For longs: old FVG below price, price trades down into it, treat the FVG's high as the "relative equal lows," wait for displacement above the nearest short-term high, buy the new FVG.
- **Example in this video:** The bullish version is what he hoped for at the 9:30 open: drop into the small gap he marked with a blue arrow, then displacement up, then buy an FVG targeting a run to 4303 (level quoted; likely ES — caption context unclear which index).
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:37](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2137) — old FVG version, doesn't have to fully close; [0:36:24](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2184) — FVG low treated as the relative-equal-highs line; "those are the two entry strategies for this model".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He states these are "the two entry strategies for this model" — i.e., the model's sweep can be of liquidity (equal highs/lows) or of an opposing PD array (old FVG).

=== [2022 Ep38 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 38 (ID 36184dDAqtM)] — Entry variations on the same bullish setup  (aliases: OTE, turtle soup, FVG entry, buy stop above swing high)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** In answer to "which FVG do I use", he gives several ways to enter the same idea:
  1. **OTE:** fib from the low to the high and buy the 62–79% retracement.
  2. **Turtle soup:** a low, then a lower low violating it, with the OTE on top of that.
  3. **FVG in discount:** low to high, find the FVG below the 50% level, and buy when price trades to the top of it. Stop below the lower low.
  4. **Buy stop above the swing high:** wait for price to trade into the FVG, then put a buy stop above the swing high formed just before price entered the FVG. Stop just below the lowest low after it hits. This is "buying strength". It is not his preferred strategy.

=== [2024 #8 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #8   August 14, 2024 (ID zU7iAqg4sSA)] — FVG entry model for a short (and the mirror for longs): entry, scale-in, stop, and "permissible" fills  (aliases: "lowest barrier to entry", "lowest threshold of entry", "pyramiding", turtle soup entry)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Entry: the lowest barrier to entry is the edge of the FVG closest to current price. For a bearish FVG that is the old candle high at the bottom of the gap (the high of candle 3, inference); put the limit order at that high + 1 tick. On the morning's first retracement price came within a tick of the FVG and turned, so even a limit one tick above the old candle high was not filled (he says so at 2:16:31; the caption at 0:11:16 says "would have been filled" but the context, "punishment for trying to be too perfect", points to not filled). His lesson: demanding perfection costs fills, so have fallback entries (wick CE, next array, price delivery continuum) and know that missing a fill still means you read direction correctly.
  - Long version (mirror): buy the high of the bullish FVG or one tick below its highest part, with the largest portion there. Add at the midpoint (CE), and add again if price leaves and retests the CE. Fills at/below the CE are rare, though they are the best entries when they occur.

Reasoning: candle 2 opens and trades down through candle 1's low, so the overlap forms a balanced price range; price should not trade back through the whole range to the top, and if it did it would probably go higher and he would not want to be short anyway. So he can put the stop just above the wick's CE.

- **How to use:** limit short at the old high + 1 tick, add at CE, stop at candle-1 high (or wick CE + 2 ticks); scale partials at the PD arrays below; see targets section.

=== [2024 #11 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #11  August 19, 2024 Begins 9:15am ET (ID aONkyy-HTqE)] — Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: "institutional order flow entry drill", "entry drill", Caleb's entry mechanism)
- **Kind:** entry model (this lecture's core)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A fair value gap is formed during a displacement move. While the displacement candle is still forming you anticipate the FVG. Once the next candle opens, you drop a buy limit at the gap's high edge (the top of a bullish FVG, i.e. the low of the third candle) plus ONE tick. That assures a fill. You do not try to buy the low end of the gap or its midpoint.
  - The expectation: price retraces into the gap and goes a tick or so below the reference candle's low, "leaving a portion of the gap open" (does not close the whole FVG), then displaces higher. Each FVG used "as a propellant" should stay at least partly open; the failure to fill completely is the signature of strength ("cracks in order flow").

=== [2026 #16 — Trading NFP Thursday High Resistance Liquidity Runs (ID xpH0wDANtmM)] — Market Maker Sell Model (Three Stages)
- **Kind:** definition (advanced)
- **What ICT says:** A market maker sell event typically unfolds in three distinct stages:
  1. **First Stage Distribution** — Institutional selling begins; price moves down steadily.
  2. **Second Stage Redistribution** — Main sell-off; most animated, fastest price movement; longest duration.
  3. **Third Stage Accumulation** (implied) — Sellers have left; price consolidates near lows before resuming or reversing.
