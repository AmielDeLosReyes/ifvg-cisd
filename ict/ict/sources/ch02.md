=== [2022 Ep2 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 2 (ID tmeCWULSTHc)] — Market Structure Shift / break in market structure  (aliases ICT uses: "break in market structure", "market structure break", "short-term low being broken")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** After the buy stops are run, look for a short-term swing low to be broken. A swing low = a candle with a higher low on its left and a higher low on its right (three-candle swing low). A break below it is "key" and is the specific signature that tips the market's hand. Says it forms almost every day, many times per week, in one direction or the other.
- **How to identify:** On the lower timeframe (1–3m for indices), after the stop run, mark the short-term low that formed on the way up; wait for price to break below it.
- **How to use:** Only after the MSS do you look for the imbalance (FVG) to sell into.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 2m, Friday: after running the old/relative equal highs, price creates short-term lows then breaks below them with a large down candle.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:43](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1663) — break in market structure; [0:28:31](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1711) — don't rush in after the high is taken, wait for the signature; [0:42:59](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2579) — swing low definition.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He points to other YouTube lessons on market structure breaks.

=== [2022 Ep3 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 3 (ID nQfHZ2DEJ8c)] — Market Structure Shift (MSS) vs market structure break  (aliases ICT uses: "intraday market structure shift", "shift in market structure", "break in market structure")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** For intraday use he says "shift", not "break". An intraday MSS signals a likely intraday draw up or down; it may produce just an intraday leg and the opposing high can still be taken the same day. "Market structure break" means more in context (can lead to prolonged multi-day movement). You do not force or anticipate ahead of the shift; you wait for the evidence. He says this is the complete, algorithmic definition — nothing else is needed.
- **How to identify:** (1) Price runs liquidity first: trades below an old low (sell stops under retail "support": a double bottom or single low) or above old highs/relative equal highs. (2) The short-term swing high (for bullish) formed right before the low is taken out — a swing high = a candle with lower highs on both sides. (3) The candle that trades through that short-term high: it does NOT need to close above it. The break is significant ONLY if liquidity was taken first.
- **How to use:** After the MSS candle closes, watch the next candle: when it closes, has an FVG been created? That FVG is the earliest place to trade. Bearish mirror: after buy stops are taken, a short-term swing low is broken and an FVG forms; sell the retrace into it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March 2022 15m (homework chart): old low ~14,600 with sell stops below it; relative equal highs ~14,820–14,860 (caption gives both) with buy stops above. 2m chart same day: price dives under ~14,600 into sell stops, then rallies through the short-term swing high formed before that low ("light bulb" candle) — bullish MSS; the next candles leave an FVG, price trades down into it and "takes off" to run buy stops above the highs and through the relative equal highs. Then above the equal highs, a swing low forms and is broken — bearish MSS (because buy stops were taken); rally into the FVG = short, targeting sell stops below lows and an imbalance below the 50% of the range.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:50](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=110) — anticipate MSS at the liquidity points, don't force it; [0:02:39](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=159) — shift vs break; [0:06:35](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=395) — short-term high taken is significant only if price ran sell stops; [0:13:45](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=825) — need not close above; watch next candle for FVG.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He notes there are ways to sell right above the highs (or buy at the low) without waiting for the MSS, but says students won't have that skill yet. HFT algorithms use market structure on 3m/2m/1m and sub-minute (45s/30s/15s) charts.

=== [2022 Ep5 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 5 (ID N29ZJ-o31xs)] — Three drives pattern into an old high  (aliases ICT uses: "three drives", "three little Indians" from Raschke & Connors' Street Smarts — caption reads "three little endings")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A swing high, then a higher swing high, then a higher swing high — price pressing up three times — into an old high, on any timeframe. You don't need the third high to take out the old high: each swing high that fails gets sold by bears who put buy stops above the previous high, which keep getting taken, so liquidity is already being run and smart money is establishing shorts. He credits Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' book (Street Smarts) for helping him understand stop hunts.
- **How to identify:** Three ascending swing highs approaching or around an old high/relative equal highs.
- **How to use:** Treat as liquidity being run; then wait for displacement lower and an FVG to sell.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m: old high at about 4514.50 (caption unclear: "45145 / 4514 A5") matched by a later candle's high exactly (relative equal highs) with multiple successively higher short-term highs = three drives.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=942) — relative equal highs and three drives; [0:16:31](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=991) — three little Indians pattern; [0:17:19](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1039) — third high need not take the old high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** If there are no stair-stepping higher highs and just one steady run up, anticipate the old high to be taken out (doesn't need to be by much), then look for the energetic move lower.

=== [2022 Ep5 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 5 (ID N29ZJ-o31xs)] — Displacement  (aliases ICT uses: "elephant in a children's swimming pool", "energetic/animated move", "obvious displacement")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** After price goes above an old high and trades back down, you want an obvious displacement — like an elephant falling into a kiddie pool — not a lethargic or anemic move. Only when displacement occurs do you look for the FVG (the low and high) and sell the retrace into it. Used to answer viewer criticism of a prior example where a swing low "looked violated" but he didn't use it: the break lacked displacement.
- **How to identify:** A pronounced, energetic candle (or candles) breaking the swing low, leaving an FVG; weak drifting breaks don't count.
- **How to use:** Displacement + FVG = trade; anemic break without FVG = wait.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m morning: first poke above the swing high with a weak anemic move lower (no); a wick that snapped back without an FVG (no); a matching high, a slight anemic dip below (no); then it trades up and "smashes down", next candle closes leaving an FVG = short; target the sell-side liquidity below the pre-8:30 swing low; price hits it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:06](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1086) — displacement analogy; [0:18:52](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1132) — then FVG and short; [0:20:25](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1225) — anemic vs energetic; [0:21:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1275) — smash down, FVG, target SSL.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Patterns sometimes fail (news events, market rolls over); hence the stop loss and money management.

=== [2022 Ep6 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 6 (ID Bkt8B3kLATQ)] — Market Structure Shift with displacement; displacement high/low range  (aliases ICT uses: "displacement high", "displacement low", "trade idea being birthed")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Bearish MSS: price trades higher, retraces a little, runs above an old high (or the initial short-term high), then breaks below the short-term low — the "new trade idea is born" (still no entry). "Quick" = displacement: energetic, a real willingness to go lower, preferably a big beefy candle that closes below the low; a wick through and back is not convincing (it might go higher and make another high). The high that ran liquidity = displacement high; the low/close beyond the short-term low = displacement low. The FVG to trade must form in between these. No FVG in that range → no trade: wait or check another market (one of them will have it every trading day). Applies on all timeframes. Bullish is reversed.
- **How to identify:** Liquidity run → energetic close beyond short-term swing → look for FVG within displacement high-to-low range.
- **How to use:** Entry at the FVG per the rules above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3 1m (below).
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:21](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1101) — bearish MSS concept; [0:19:08](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1148) — quick = displacement, prefer close below; [0:19:55](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1195) — displacement high/low; FVG hunted between; [0:21:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1289) — no FVG = no trade; forms every trading day.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Refines Ep. 3 (where the MSS candle didn't need to close beyond the high): here a close beyond with displacement is preferred/more significant.

=== [2022 Ep11 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 11 (ID Sqw2bww93Zo)] — Short-, intermediate- and long-term highs/lows (market structure nesting)  (aliases ICT uses: halos, long-term intraday high)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Swing high: a candle with a lower-high candle on its left and a lower-high candle on its right (3 candles).
  - Short-term high (STH): any swing high.
  - Intermediate-term high (ITH): a swing high with lower short-term swing highs on both sides of it.
  - Long-term high (LTH): a high with lower intermediate-term highs on both sides. On a 3m chart this is a "long-term intraday high", which he admits sounds like an oxymoron.
  - Lows are mirrored.
  - Old chart-book habit: one circle (halo) over a short-term high, two for an intermediate-term high, three for a long-term high. This classifies "the nesting effect of the highs and lows".
  - It's "far more than just lower lows and lower highs", and it's not supply and demand or support/resistance.
  - Source: Larry Williams, from his early-90s VHS course (*Future Millionaires Confidential*, title approximate) and the market-structure chapters of *How I Made One Million Dollars Last Year Trading Commodities*. ICT says it's still essential reading (minus the "moon phases" fluff), and he rereads it yearly.
- **How to identify:** Mark every 3-candle swing high. Then promote a swing high to intermediate-term when the swing highs on either side are lower. Then promote to long-term when the intermediate-term highs on either side are lower.
- **How to use:** In a bearish leg with the draw below, short close to the current intermediate-term high. It's a lower high relative to the long-term high, so it's unlikely to be exceeded and is "likely to break" lower. Backtest by classifying swing highs and lows to build trust in the model.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 3m, 21 Feb 2022. An earlier long-term intraday high had lower swing highs on either side, making the next high an intermediate-term high.
  - At 10:33, one candle back showed a high with a lower high on either side: a swing high lower than the ITH, which was lower than the LTH.
  - That nested structure (with the SSL draw below acting as a "magnet") is why he trusted the short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:28](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=628) — long-term, intermediate and short-term highs on a 3m chart · [0:11:17](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=677) — the draw below as a magnet; short near the ITH · [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=822) — swing high definition; chart-book halos (1/2/3) · [0:14:29](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=869) — nesting; Larry Williams book
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Expanded in the next episode's market structure lesson.

=== [2022 Ep12 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 (ID 8GkQfdAXZP0)] — Short-, intermediate- and long-term swing classification (ICT's version)  (aliases ICT uses: STH/STL, ITH/ITL, LTH/LTL)
- **Kind:** definition / new coinage (extends Larry Williams)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Base classification (from Larry Williams; the source book shown is *Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading*): an ITH is the highest high between two short-term highs, i.e. a STH with a lower STH on its left and a lower STH on its right. Lows mirror this.
  - **ICT's addition, "the main one":** any time price trades back to **rebalance an imbalance** (fill a FVG), the swing high formed at that rebalance is **immediately** an **intermediate-term high**. If price drops back down to fill a bullish imbalance, that low is an **intermediate-term low**.
  - So there are two ways to classify an ITH: (a) a STH with lower STHs either side, or (b) a high formed by rebalancing an imbalance.
  - He labels these in his mind, not on the chart, to avoid distraction. Annotations are "lipstick" for teaching only.
  - Don't mark every swing on the lower timeframes; that's overkill. Establish structure on the framing timeframe (1h here).
- **How to identify:** Find FVGs (single-candle "one-pass" moves). When price returns and fills one, the turning point at that fill is an ITH or ITL.
- **How to use:** In a bearish idea, the ITH formed by rebalancing must not be exceeded. The next short-term high should be lower than it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March 2022, 1h, inside the daily FVG. A down candle left an imbalance; price rallied back up to fill it. The swing high formed there was labeled an ITH. On the way down, a fill of a bullish imbalance made an ITL.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:00](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=780) — every rebalance of an old imbalance becomes an ITH or ITL · [0:16:56](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1016) — ITH between two STHs; "main takeaway for your notes" · [0:41:08](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2468) — the two classifications of an ITH stated explicitly
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** It extends the Ep. 11 halo hierarchy. ICT stresses he doesn't "teach Larry Williams market structure". Williams was only the starting point.

=== [2022 Ep12 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 (ID 8GkQfdAXZP0)] — Weak intermediate-term high (ITH below its neighbouring short-term highs)
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ideally an ITH should be above both STHs beside it. When the ITH formed by rebalancing is **not higher** than the STH to its left or right, it's "only rebalancing". The market is **exceedingly weak**, and "the algorithm is tipping its hand".
  - ICT says he pioneered this idea; no one taught it to him.
  - This answers the retail question of which way a pennant or triangle will break. Retail sees low, high, higher low, failed higher high: a pennant. The weak ITH says it breaks down.
  - Retail traders see the consolidation as a bull flag. ICT likes to fade those ("false bull flag").
- **How to use:** With a bearish HTF premise, a weak ITH justifies anticipating the failure. Treat the up-close candles forming there as a bearish order block and look for an aggressive short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:14](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=734) — false bull flag; fade it · [0:14:35](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=875) — pennant: which side breaks? · [0:22:31](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1351) — ITH not above the STHs either side; "it's going lower" · [0:37:56](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2276) — not higher than two STHs means very weak; algorithm tipping its hand

=== [2022 Ep12 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 (ID 8GkQfdAXZP0)] — Institutional order flow: up-close candles are resistance in bearish swings, down-close candles are support in bullish swings
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When bearish, all up-close candles should keep price from going higher than them. Price may come back and touch them (acting as a bearish OB, a "speed bump") but shouldn't trade above them. If price doesn't overtake the nearest up-close candles, it doesn't need to reach the ones above. Price "remains heavy".
  - When bullish, the swing has few down-close candles. Those should support price, without being overlapped below. It's permissible only if there's a short-term low in close proximity whose sell stops get taken before reaccumulation and continuation higher. If there's no nearby swing low (no SSL), there's nothing to worry about.
  - "Bullish swings: down-close candles are support. Bearish swings: up-close candles are resistance."
  - Use this to know the move is still good (hold without fear) and when it's likely flawed.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 4m/5m. After the OB short, rallies traded back into up-close candles but never overtook them. Later up-close clusters were never re-reached, and price kept falling.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:22](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2902) — institutional order flow defined · [0:49:10](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2950) — doesn't overtake the up-close candle; real resistance · [0:51:35](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3095) — speed bumps; bullish mirror · [0:53:13](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3193) — down-close overlap only OK if a short-term low with SSL is nearby

=== [2022 Ep12 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 (ID 8GkQfdAXZP0)] — Significant break in market structure and targeting projections

=== [2022 Ep13 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 13 (ID tpPtItWqmlg)] — Judas swing / 9:30 opening retracement
- **Kind:** example / rule
