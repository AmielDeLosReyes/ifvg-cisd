- **Kind:** rule (study method)
- **What ICT says:** Backtesting = going back and marking up old moves (bar replay or hindsight). Screenshot/print into your journal and reread weekly. This "tricks" the subconscious into forming pseudo-memories and activates the reticular activating system (like noticing your new car everywhere), so live setups feel familiar. Time to full understanding varies — weeks to a year+.

- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** Lectures 1–3 covered back-testing; this lecture and Friday's are about walking forward. === [2024 #10 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #10  August 16, 2024 (ID qoNQfVzVXZQ)] — Observations / price-delivery measurements (tape reading journal)  (aliases: "baseline", "tally", "heat on the move", "study not trade")
- **Kind:** process / refinement
- **What ICT says:** While watching price live, state what you expect it to do, then write down: the time you observed it, the time it took to reach the level or to close over (count 1m candles), how much heat (adverse movement) there was, and whether it ever came back to take the low/high before delivering. Do it after the fact on recordings too if you can't watch live. Keep a daily tally of how many opportunities you saw in real time with no help; early on it will be near zero, weeks later 3–4 a session, by year-end many; the ratio of correct to incorrect will drift to about half and half, and after that the failures diminish. Record failures honestly and study them; do not "pretend it never happened" because no button was pushed. Do not attempt this on live money.
- **How to identify:** An observation = an FVG/PD array that, if it holds, suggests a draw to a specific level (e.g., a new day opening gap), plus an explicit target.
- **How to use:** Minimum thresholds: **ES 5 handles**; **NQ at least 10 handles, he prefers 15**; and in framing a trade he wants a **range of at least 20 handles** from where it could begin to where it could draw (a "minimum range for targeting purposes"); if 20 handles are available, 10–15 is reasonable to capture. Don't demand a trade pan out to the full target.
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: an FVG formed 10:22, price moved in favor for 9 minutes (until 10:31), could not get above the high, and a stop would have been hit at 10:37; he logs this as a failed first data point. Second example: a gap that became an inversion FVG and rallied toward the NDOG; count the candles on the 1m chart and note whether heat occurred, and whether price returned to take the low before the run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:57:01](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3421) record the time observed and time to deliver · [0:58:37](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3517) daily tally and baseline · [1:09:04](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4144) worked example: 10:22 FVG, 9 minutes, stopped 10:37 · [1:14:36](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4476) ES 5 handles, NQ 10 to 15 · [2:26:41](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8801) 20-handle minimum NQ range
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as what a student should do for weeks before pressing a demo button; the numbers are thresholds for study, not signals.

=== [2024 #21 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #21  August 30, 2024 (ID eYYyonGwTRs)] — Naked chart with raw price levels (notepad method)  (aliases: levels in raw form, naked chart)
- **Kind:** process / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He keeps his chart naked and keeps the levels he is watching in a notepad in raw price form, so he gets a clean measurement between what price is doing and what it can do by reading the price axis against his notes. He anticipates price running to those levels; they only appear on the chart when price presents and "books" there. He draws levels on charts in these lectures only for teaching. Having all levels drawn without an outlined narrative is confusing and lets trolls say "it will hit one of them".
- **How to use:** For each level ask: if it is a premium level, does it repel price lower or stop it going higher? If a discount level, does it hold price up or is it smashed through easily? Couple that with an easy narrative (time of day, draw on liquidity); let setups that go against your bias pass.

- **Kind:** rule (psychology/journaling)

=== [2026 #19 — How New Students Should Start Part 1 (ID BWEPtHZ1Y2Y)] — Chart layouts / workspaces and labeling conventions
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Keep a "naked" working layout (the one he teaches and executes from) plus separate saved layouts for specific lists: new week opening gaps, new day opening gaps, first-presented FVGs, and a layout with only RTH opening range gaps and a running tally. Toggle between layouts instead of putting everything on one chart (clutter and overload).
  - Anything you only need once, write down the numbers on a notepad.
  - Right-justify labels for higher-timeframe levels. If a label is left-justified on a daily chart, on a 15-minute chart it sits far off-screen and you see a price on the axis but do not know what it is.
  - Lines anchored to the right and extended to the right; otherwise they disappear as new data prints. Save the layout after editing.
  - For intraday or minor levels, place the label just above where it is anchored (not right-justified) so you can find it by compressing the chart (drag the time axis).
  - Colors: buy-side liquidity in blue, sell-side liquidity in deep red, always the same so viewers know what he is highlighting even without understanding his language. Tiny liquidity levels can be drawn as short trend lines.
  - Hierarchy of line thickness: higher-timeframe-anchored lines should be thicker, but not the very boldest (daily-anchored: one notch below the thickest; weekly: same logic) so they stand out among lower-timeframe noise.
  - Add the context to the label (e.g., "daily SIBI") so you keep things framed.
  - Do not show the high/low or midpoint tool lines he doesn't use; each extra line is something to babysit or explain.
