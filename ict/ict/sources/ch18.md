=== [2022 Ep13 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 13 (ID tpPtItWqmlg)] — Entries & stops — trailing stops below down-close candles
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Initial stop below the middle (mean threshold) of the bullish order block candle.
  - When price trades above a new down-close candle's high, your stop must remain below that candle's low — not inside it — because price can "dig into" that candle (it acts as an order block). Putting the stop inside it gets you stopped prematurely.
  - Once price rallies and takes out (goes above) a cluster of down-close candles, the stop can be raised to below that down-close candle (cluster), treating the consecutive down-close candles as one order block.
  - If price breaks the low of those down-close candles, being stopped out was correct — the move may be failing.
- **How to identify / use:** Each new bullish leg has its supporting down-close candle(s); trail the stop below their lows.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: two consecutive down-close candles; price got very close to them on a retracement but did not eat through their range; he held.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:49](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1489) — stop below mean threshold; stop must stay below the candle's low. [0:25:37](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1537) — raise stop once the down-close candles are taken out.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Getting stopped early often leaves beginners afraid to re-enter — he calls that "infancy."

=== [2022 Ep13 — 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 13 (ID tpPtItWqmlg)] — Risk management — pyramiding (largest position first)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Build the biggest position at the initial entry, then each add is smaller (3 micros, then 2, then 1) — a stable pyramid. Buying 1, then 2, then 3 is an "inverted pyramid" — unstable. The earlier, larger position's equity supports the adds so you can weather a deeper retracement. Adds only while price is still below the objective. He counts the whole thing as one trade.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ demo: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 micro contracts; $10,000 hypothetical account → $12,111 (≈21% on one trade). 6 micros required about $1,200 margin (hypothetical AMP Futures discount broker). MNQ = $2 per point (4 ticks).
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:59](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1919) — pyramid: 3 then 2. [0:32:47](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1967) — inverted pyramid unstable. [0:33:34](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2014) — one trade, 3 scalings, $10,000 → $12,111. [0:34:21](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2061) — 6 micros ≈ $1,200 margin.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Says this gearing was "optimal" for $10k; more would have been too much leverage. "Stop thinking you need a lot to make a lot."

=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — 15-second chart & Price Delivery Continuum Theory
- **Kind:** refinement ("extra credit", explicitly not for beginners)
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Price Delivery Continuum Theory:** with an expected draw, the fractal market offers continuous participation. Once a timeframe has used up its entry opportunities, go to a lower timeframe (down to 15s or 5s) instead of chasing.
  - The 15s chart is "very forgiving". It lets you get into a move you missed on the 1m/5m/15m, or cut stop size (handles or pips). It is **not** a shortcut. If you can't read 1m/5m/15m price, you'll get chopped up on 15s.
  - **Limit orders:** don't demand perfection. Place the limit **just above** the level (for longs), e.g. just above a wick's midpoint, not at the exact extreme. That's the "low-hanging fruit" where volume is. Expect occasional non-fills even when price touches or slightly exceeds your price: "don't be a dick for a tick". Constant extreme fills invite B-book broker slippage and requotes.
  - **Adding:** when the run is already "long in the tooth", an add-on partial can't be larger than one contract. Pyramiding is hard to teach. Don't start yet; be consistent with small size first.
  - Tighter stops via 15s are for later, once you're managing real risk.
- **Example:** Aug 6 rally leg on 15s: the 1m immediate rebalance shows up as price returning to rebalance the "quick sudden run from this close to that close". Then relative equal highs, an FVG, price trading outside it, and back in with a 1m immediate rebalance to a deeper discount. That's the first return after taking buy side and a 15s long or add-on. Later, a down-close candle inside an FVG that returned into an old inefficiency is another buy, with the limit just above the wick's midpoint.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1959) why 15s; unfilled limits. [0:37:28](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2248) re-entry and stop reduction, not a golden ticket. [2:38:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9493) PDCT defined. [2:48:36](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10116) 15s long, add of at most one contract, limit just above the level. === [2024 #7 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #7   August 13, 2024 (ID -WItxLARGpE)] — Institutional Orderflow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: institutional order flow entry drill)
- **Kind:** entry model (re-taught; the detail is the valuable part)
- **What ICT says:**
  - IOFED is a **partial entry into a fair value gap that you believe will not fill completely**. Use it only when he (or you) says a gap should stay open/unfilled. "Unless I say that this gap should stay open … you're never looking at IOFED" — it is not the go-to for every FVG. You can still adopt it as a personal entry model.
  - **Where to fill:** the upper half of the gap. In the bullish case the gap spans candle-1 high (bottom) to candle-3 low (top). The best fills are from the **CE to the top of the gap**. The lower half is the least probable fill. If you are wrong and price trades down to the low, it usually still matters not, because the CE-to-top fill has already happened.
  - **Limit order placement:** put the limit at the candle-3 low (top of the gap) — it only takes one tick below that to fill a long — or even one tick above it. Never aim for the low end: he has had real limit orders where price traded 2 ticks past and did not fill (spread, queue), then ran without him. He asks the viewer to choose between the top of the gap and one tick above, and to accept the extra drawdown. (Exact wording on the tick offset is garbled in captions.)
  - **Stop:** below the candle-1 high (bottom of the FVG). In his example the span was about nine ticks (~2.25 NQ handles; 4 ticks = 1 handle, as captioned).
  - **Philosophy:** "low resistance barriers to entry" and "low-hanging fruit" for targets. He is "never trying to get a fill at the low end"; he does not demand precision; the spread must not be a factor.
  - **Scaling variant (his own):** first entry 6 contracts at the top of a wider gap; add 4 more when price goes into the CE/upper quadrant; add 5 more one tick below CE if it trades there (same assumption: the lower portion stays open). In his timed example: 10:47 first entry, 10:49 into CE, 10:51 one tick below CE. That is averaging into a losing position, which at least one funded-account firm (he names Topstep, Caleb's choice) does not allow. Workaround: use IOFED as the only entry and pyramid with later, higher PD arrays (order blocks and FVGs above), which that firm allows.
  - **Mirror:** he says the same applies when the array is a premium array (price trading up into it), but the sensitive area flips accordingly (mirror by implication; he does not walk it through).
- **How to identify:** a gap you expect to remain open (breakaway gap, left after a consolidation/stop hunt), a higher-timeframe target above, and price returning toward the gap.
- **How to use:** place a limit at/near the top of the gap, stop below the gap's candle-1 high, target the next array/liquidity (here the 18951.50 candle high ~20 handles, then the daily volume imbalance).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Tue 13 Aug 2024, call made around 10:47–10:50 ET: "I want this gap to stay open". 10:52 ET (inside the 10:50–11:10 macro): price dips into the top of the gap — a drop of roughly one tick — without touching the lower portion, then displaces up. Target: the high at 18951.50 (about 20 handles away) and then the daily volume imbalance whose low he ballparked near 18956 with CE near 18960 (about 30 handles total from the 18931 entry). ICT said he would only expect Caleb to aim at the CE of that daily volume imbalance. He did not trade it himself.
- **Timestamps:** [2:02:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7352) — "screenshot this": any trade one tick below that low is an IOFED · [2:18:35](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8315) — CE-to-high is the sensitive zone · [2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457) — limit placement and the unfilled-limit experience · [2:29:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8949) — 6 + 4 + 5 scaling and funded-account averaging rule · [2:43:41](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9821) — targets: ~20 handles, then daily VI CE ~30 handles
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** this restates the older IOFED as a "gap should stay open" tool rather than a general OTE-style entry into any FVG. Prices as captioned are noisy. All figures in the example are approximate.

=== [2024 #8 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #8   August 14, 2024 (ID zU7iAqg4sSA)] — Price Delivery Continuum Theory  (aliases: price delivery continuum, following the delivery chain, joining a run you missed)
- **Kind:** new coinage (ICT says it is "brand new stuff" and mentorship students did not learn it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you place a precise limit order (e.g., at the FVG edge) and price runs away without filling, do not abandon the idea. The delivery is underway and a finite target exists, so keep looking for the next specific price level, a new PD array formed by the unfolding move (a new short-term high, a new up-closed candle/OB, a new FVG), and enter there.
  - He says he developed it because as a young trader he would miss his single setup, get angry and stop looking, though a big move was still available.
  - It requires a finite price (entry, stop, partial, terminus); if he does not know the specific price he does not trade.
- **How to use:** after the initial miss, wait for retracements into the newly-formed arrays; use the lowest-barrier edge entry; keep the same stop logic.
- **Example in this video:** after the missed FVG fill, price came back up into the body of an up-closed candle and traded to the CE of the 8:30 wick without taking out that candle's high, which he says could have been the entry.
- **Timestamps:** [0:45:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2728) — missed fills led him to develop it · [0:47:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2873) — defined: anticipate a move to a finite target · [1:59:41](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7181) — "do you say forget it? no" (continuing to look)

=== [2024 #13 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #13  August 20, 2024 Begins 9:15am ET (ID LMi_mO3AP08)] — Price Delivery Continuum Theory (reading real order flow from price)  (aliases: "price delivery continuing theory", "my way of reading real order flow", the algorithm)

- **What ICT says:**

Resting orders can be pulled (spoofing); you cannot spoof the OHLC of a candle. Volume booked is seen with time and sales only after price engages the level.
  - Frame it as a question: does price support the algorithm repricing back to these reference points? Can price reach the next level, how fast, and what does it do on arrival (stop short or go through)? Then did it show willingness to trade to the next CE?
  - Because price is algorithmic it follows "scripts"; contract counts have no bearing. The market is drawn to inefficiency ("a big magnet"), it needs only to touch the low end of an inefficiency first, then you watch whether it aggressively runs to the midpoint.
  - He says he does not use Bookmap-type tools, and that students who understand his method do not need them; he admits it might have helped him early on.
- **How to use:** screenshot your own chart and watch price hit levels; do not rely on his charts.
