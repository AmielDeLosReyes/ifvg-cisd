=== [2024 #2 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #2   August 6, 2024 (ID pkI4AgC4OPI)] — Immediate rebalance (+ breakaway gap, order block)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Immediate rebalance:** an extended-range candle goes up. Price drops back and **just touches that candle's high**, rather than filling a gap, then runs to an objective (liquidity or an inefficiency).
  - He also wanted a nearby FVG to **stay open** (not traded fully down into the candle high). An FVG that stays open acts as a **breakaway gap**.
  - These "drills and exercises" teach anticipatory price reading and lead later to entries, stops and management.
- **Example:** Aug 6, after the AM selloff: there's a disruption at the lows (sell side taken), and relative equal highs sit above. He expects a poke above the day's high, which is the initial high near 7:00 (the "blue level"). Price does an immediate rebalance (touches the candle), rallies, trades back down into a down-close candle (an **order block**), closes the inefficiency once more, then expands up through the 7:00 high. That's a 40+ handle run. After touching the high of day he'd be done: no afternoon trading.
- **Timestamps:** [2:27:41](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=8861) definition and the breakaway gap wish. [2:34:55](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9295) it played out: immediate rebalance, then OB. [2:35:43](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9343) breakaway gap and OB detail; done for the day.

=== [2024 #5 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #5    August 9, 2024 (ID ul6x6BwOg6M)] — Asia long — order block entry, stop, management (his live trade)  (aliases: bullish order block, breakaway gap, BISI)
- **Kind:** entry model (worked example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the displacement above the NDOG leaves a BISI, he does not want price to return into it — that makes it a **breakaway gap**. Then he wants momentum/speed through the relative equal highs and initial BSL ("nitrous oxide"), not a push above and right back in.
  - Order block entry: buy in the discount of the down-close candle — **below its body** (here its close), anywhere in the wick. People who just "buy down-close candles" only heard his first introduction to order blocks.
  - Stop: below the consequent encroachment of the NDOG.
  - Once price takes out the short-term high, move the stop to break-even, or at minimum to the top of the NDOG. Don't rush the stop.
  - Additional entries: a gap-up retraced on the next candle, and a classic volume-imbalance retrace.
  - "Every displacement does not have to see it trade back down into" — it's better when the BISI is never revisited; a return into it means the move is deferred, with uncomfortable waiting, drawdown or chop.
  - Two qualifiers that build confidence: (1) displacement ahead of 7 PM after working above and below the NDOG, keeping the initial SSL intact; (2) taking out the short-term high.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 8 ~7 PM ET: down-close candle closed at 18,560; his entry 18,559.75; price rallied through relative equal highs, consolidated around the level, then ran into the initial BSL and beyond. Exit 18,607.5 (limit order).
- **Timestamps:** [0:50:46](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3046) — breakaway gap, relative equal high run; [0:53:07](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3187) — buy below the order block body; [0:53:57](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3237) — entry 59.75, stop below NDOG CE; [0:55:33](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3333) — break-even / top of NDOG; displacement shouldn't be revisited.

=== [2024 #7 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #7   August 13, 2024 (ID -WItxLARGpE)] — Breakaway gap: FVG left after a consolidation + stop hunt  (aliases: breakaway gap; "this gap should stay open")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price leaves a **consolidation after a stop hunt** (the stops below the range are taken, then price breaks above the range high) and leaves an FVG on the way, that gap is a **breakaway gap**. They "tend to stay open" — not always but most of the time, so it acts like real support (or resistance for the mirror case).
  - It acts as support even though price may not return to it for a long time. In this video price came back down to it only one tick short in places, and later dipped just into it.
  - **Why expect that:** the gap formed leaving the consolidation. It is not "support and resistance" (price did not need to retest a wick) and not "supply and demand" (he calls S&D a myth outside commodities); it is algorithmic delivery.
  - Contrast: other times he **wants** a gap to close, i.e. wants it to fail so it can invert (see inversion expectation). Say which you expect before the trade.
- **How to identify:** (1) a multi-bar consolidation, (2) a sweep of its low (for a long idea), (3) displacement above the consolidation high leaving an FVG, (4) a higher-timeframe target above.
- **How to use:** it is the setup for the IOFED entry (next concept). Go back through old charts: look for gaps left open after leaving a consolidation after a stop hunt and see how often they hold.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, ~10:47–10:52 ET. A 9:30-onward consolidation with a sweep of the stops below it, then a break above the range high. That left an FVG whose candle-3 low (top of the gap) was 18931.00 (as captioned). Price dropped back into the top of the gap at 10:52 without filling the lower portion, then rallied to the daily volume imbalance.
- **Timestamps:** [2:04:55](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7495) — why he expects it to stay open · [2:08:10](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7690) — left the consolidation after a stop hunt, gap tends to stay open · [2:25:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8758) — "breakaway gap … breaking away from this consolidation" · [2:50:54](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10254) — not classic support/resistance because price never touched the wick
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** an old idea made explicit. He says whenever he says "I want to see this one stay open / remain unfilled" in streams, this is the logic. He calls the stream remarks repeatable proof it is not cherry-picked.

=== [2024 #9 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #9  August 15, 2024 (ID pg5XBNkpotM)] — Exhaustion gap (gap that has been traded up into fully and left behind)
- **Kind:** definition (brief) / new usage
- **What ICT says:** If price trades up into a gap and moves away aggressively without leaving part of it open, that gap is "exhausting" — it has done its job and loses relevance as resistance. For the key gap, he wants it traded into only partially; the upper half open means "really heavy" and he would have strong conviction for a return to the 9:30 opening price.
- **How to identify:** Gap that is completely filled with bodies, not just wicks. He notes a tiny "Mohawk" (a small wick) outside the range is fine as long as bodies stop inside.
- **How to use:** Exhaustion = that gap no longer supports the short idea; look at the next higher inefficiency or stand aside.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m ~10:18 ET: "this entire gap closed in here, the bodies are stopping, that one little Mohawk outside... I'm viewing that as an exhaustion; press press press press press, high, trade below the gap I have interest".
- **Timestamps:** [0:43:05](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2585) don't want it to trade up to there, exhaustion gap · [0:44:42](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2682) one gap exhaustion, other stays open in upper half · [0:54:08](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3248) entire gap closed with bodies, viewed as exhaustion

=== [2024 #17 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ Lecture #17  August 26, 2024  Impeding Barriers (ID iLe_4m84_yw)] — Immediate Rebalance  (aliases ICT uses: "one directional and an immediate rebalance")
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - It is a single candle with a large range lower and a lower close, clearly bigger than the surrounding ranges, followed by a candle that opens and trades all the way back up to touch the prior candle's low. That low is the level that would have bounded a bearish FVG. The down candle's whole range is re-delivered with buy side, so the gap never forms.
  - The rebalance must happen on the candle immediately after the big down candle. If the first candle does not do it, the next one still counts. After that it is not valid.
  - It works on all timeframes.
  - In a bearish context it is a strong continuation tell, "tipping its hand" like a poker player flashing a full house. It promotes speed and large range, and it is not a reversal.
  - He describes only the bearish case, and says "it matters not if it goes higher or lower" (caption unclear, probably meaning the mirror image is equivalent).
- **How to identify (checklist):**
  1. A low forms.
  2. One candle prints a large down-close range, larger than recent candles.
  3. The next candle (at most the following one) opens and trades back up to the prior candle's low.
- **How to use:** if you are short from higher, or want to short or add (pyramid), anticipate this when the large down candle closes. Watch where the next candle opens and expect it to run back to the prior candle's low, then expect speed to the downside. He says it "might be your model" in itself. Retail chat reads it as a reversal (candlestick logic, "Steve Nison") and gets it backwards.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart just before and around 10:00 ET (video about 0:26–0:31). Price made a low, printed a big down-close candle below the prior candle's low, and the next candle opened and ran back up to that low. Price then took out the low, "boom". He used it as the example of an observation to screenshot and annotate.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:27:38](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1658) — walk-through of the candle sequence.
  - [0:28:25](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1705) — "fair value gaps are not a renaming of imbalance": the superior form is the gap that does not fill in.
  - [0:32:30](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1950) — definition: single big candle, lower close, must be larger than prior ranges.
  - [0:33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998) — one-or-two-candle window and how to use it.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** the candle-count window is stated slightly inconsistently. At 0:31:40 it is "valid if it does it on the next candle, but after that it's not", and at 0:33:18 he allows the candle after the first. Treat it as one or two candles. The one example is only partly clear in the captions, so the diagram should be redrawn from the description above.

=== [2024 #27 — ICT 2024 Mentorship \ September 12, 2024 (ID DRuEMX4RhMw)] — Immediate rebalance, vacuum block and order block sequence (worked example)  (aliases: immediate rebalance, vacuum block, order block theory, opening price, change in state of delivery)
- **Kind:** definition + example (several new/applied terms)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price in the lower half of the gap: when price trades to the CE and returns up into the old low without reaching the midpoint of that smaller imbalance, it is weak (the most sensitive points are in the lower half), so it should go on to the next level, the lower quadrant of the 1H BISI.
  - Immediate rebalance: a candle opens and trades straight to the prior candle's low (here a discount wick that rebalances the gap formed by the open); annotate it. He counts three things at once: immediate rebalance + discount wick CE + nearness to the BISI's CE, then price hits the lower quadrant and runs slightly past it.
  - After the lower quadrant is swept, price trades higher: the candle's opening price plus a volume imbalance are two PD arrays. The series of down-closed candles is an order block ("order block theory, there's a chapter coming out"); the opening price of the order block is the change in the state of delivery.
  - Vacuum block (as used here): a separation between one candle's close and the next candle's open; there is no price between the two except the wick. The wick trades down to the prior close — that's the immediate rebalance, because the wick is the gap.
  - Price then delivers up to the first (highest) of the down-closed candles, the high of the whole order block; the market opens, trades into the order block and sends price higher to a swing high at the high end of the opening range gap, taking buy side.
  - Rule on timing: because the lowest desired level (BISI's lower quadrant) had already been traded to and slightly breached, the bounce from there is the valid reaction.
- **How to identify:** Series of down-closed candles at the low; the first up move's opening price; a close-to-open separation (vacuum); wick filling the separation.
- **How to use:** Entry is the order block / opening price after the sweep of the lower quadrant, with the stop under the sweep low (his stop guidance above); target the ORG high / swing high above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ ~10:35–10:45 ET (video 1:05–1:09): price hits the lower quadrant of the 1H BISI, spikes slightly past it, rallies; a candle opens with a gap above the prior close, wicks back down to that close (immediate rebalance), then rallies to the top of the down-closed series (order block high) and on to the opening range gap high.
