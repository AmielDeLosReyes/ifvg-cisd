## Summary
Live-streamed on PPI day (Tuesday 13 Aug 2024), with NQ (September contract) as the vehicle. ICT teaches how to behave on a high-impact-news day. He gives no pre-8:30 liquidity framing and takes no trade in the post-news run, because he will not buy at the tail end of a long one-sided run. He frames the day from the weekly and daily draw on liquidity first. He then uses a tiny 10:52 ET setup to teach three things. (1) The **Institutional Orderflow Entry Drill** (IOFED): a limit order inside a fair value gap you expect to stay open. (2) A **new order-block definition** for runs of consecutive down-closed candles, where the open of the series is the "change in the state of delivery". (3) The **macro** concept: the 20-minute window around every hour in which price "spools" to a gap, order block or liquidity. About half the video is mindset: stand aside on grinding, jagged or muddy days, find your own "Kryptonite", draw your setup on a napkin, journal, and do not trade every day. He also previews the next lecture (#8), which is on turtle soup.

## Concepts

### News-day protocol (8:30 ET reports: NFP, CPI, PPI)  (aliases: post-news framing; "don't trade the storm")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On days with an 8:30 ET high-impact driver (NFP, PPI, CPI, and by implication FOMC) you "shouldn't be trading that day". Wait for the 9:30 opening bell and, if you insist on engaging for practice, usually trade the **10:00 Silver Bullet**. For NFP specifically, waiting for the Silver Bullet is "the easiest way in", though it does not guarantee a win.
  - Do not mark an "initial high / initial low" for liquidity before these reports. The release is heavily manipulated, usually produces one-sided runs, and any directional bias or target given before it is more likely wrong than right. Let it run "like a hurricane" and look for setups only after the storm: what did the run leave behind?
  - Frame the day from the opening-bell range. Building the framework inside roughly 7:00–9:30 ET is fine; do not look at anything before 7:00, because that pulls in London-session price action and makes the daily profile more complex. Relative equal highs/lows that form after the open count as the initial BSL/SSL.
  - For a new trader these are days to watch and log, not to trade. He is explicitly telling his son not to feel he has to be successful navigating them yet.
  - He believes CPI/PPI are especially manipulated in an election year, and that the Fed is "in the market". These are his stated opinions.
- **How to identify:** a scheduled 8:30 ET report. Price before and during it is a one-sided run.
- **How to use:** stand aside until 9:30. Look for relative equal highs/lows after the open, then a 10:00 hour (Silver Bullet / 9:50–10:10 macro) idea. Study the 8:30 run's origin afterwards (see "Higher-timeframe draw on liquidity").
- **Example in this video:** PPI 8:30. NQ rallied off the 6 pm new-day opening gap, challenged the London high, and pushed through it. By 9:30 it had delivered straight to the weekly/daily objective he gave at the start of the stream. He called the 9:30 open "one-sided, one-shot delivery". He took no trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:47](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=407) — no trading news days; Silver Bullet for NFP · [0:14:06](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=846) — no initial high/low before CPI/PPI-type reports · [0:30:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1858) — frame from 7:00–9:30, nothing before 7:00
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** consistent with earlier "avoid the news candle, trade the aftermath" teaching. The new detail is "no initial BSL/SSL before the report" and "do not use pre-7:00 price".

### Higher-timeframe draw on liquidity first (weekly → daily → intraday)  (aliases: where is price going to be drawn; "the first thing you're trying to do")
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first and only initial job is to get a feel for where the market could genuinely end up without much back-and-forth, i.e. the draw on liquidity. You are not trying to find patterns, the best stop location or where to move your stop. "If you don't have [the draw], everything else is going to put you offside."
  - He builds it from the weekly chart down to the daily, then the intraday charts. If the market is going to move, it moves to something on the daily chart.
  - A tradable idea needs range. He wants at least ~20 NQ handles of simple, timeable run, because from 20 handles he can take 10–15.
- **How to identify:** weekly and daily imbalances (SIBI/BISI), candle lows/highs, consequent encroachment of wicks and old highs/lows that price has not yet reached.
- **How to use:** mark the weekly and daily levels on your chart or notepad before the session. Then watch price either reach them or fail. Do not chase once price is already there.
- **Example in this video:**
  - Weekly: the low of the weekly candle of 22 July 2024 at 18883.25 (as captioned). A weekly sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency (SIBI) sits there, and he also notes the consequent encroachment (CE) of that candle's wick. He asks for stiff opposition at the level ("I like to see some kind of stiff opposition to any forward run higher at the 18883¼ level"), meaning he does not expect a clean run through it.
  - Daily: the butt end of a daily candle low about 10 handles away, ~18854 (as captioned).
  - Result: NQ ran about 50 handles up off the Monday-evening/overnight gap area and hit both levels. Weekly and daily objectives were met early, so he saw nothing left to buy.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:34](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=454) — weekly low 18883.25 and its CE · [0:10:03](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=603) — daily ~854 and weekly ~883/884 written on the notepad · [0:33:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2012) — the draw is the first thing, "almost like meditation" · [3:09:24](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=11364) — if you do not know where the market is likely to draw to, everything else puts you offside
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** prices are as auto-captioned and may be off (e.g. thousands digits). He says "forget the PPI label" and just study the whole run from its origin (the new-day opening gap) to the target to see the PD arrays along the way.

### New Day Opening Gap (NDOG): CE rule and as a level  (aliases: new day opening gap; 5 pm close → 6 pm reopen)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The NDOG is the difference between the 5:00 pm ET close and the 6:00 pm ET reopen. "Sticking with the rules", if it is **over 20 handles** (NQ) he annotates its consequent encroachment. Price traded back to the CE level, then rallied.
  - Friday's NDOG and the new week opening gap are also downside targets: if price goes below the NWOG it can run to Friday's NDOG.
- **How to identify:** 5 pm close vs 6 pm open gap on NQ, larger than 20 handles → draw high, low and CE.
- **How to use:** CE of a large NDOG is a retracement/support level on the next session (here, the 8:30 run's origin). The NDOG high was also noted as a level that was formed "last evening".
- **Example in this video:** The NDOG high formed 5→6 pm Monday. Early Tuesday, price traded back down to the NDOG CE, then rallied aggressively (an order block and a breaker block with the CE and a sell-side imbalance/buy-side inefficiency converging). Price then pushed above the London high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:38](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=698) — NDOG high, over-20-handle CE rule · [0:18:59](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1139) — walk-through of the rally from the NDOG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** the explicit "over 20 handles → mark CE" threshold is the number to keep. NDOG origin was used as the start of the measuring leg for the whole day's study.

### TGIF / Monday weekly-range retracement (20–30%)  (aliases: TGIF (Thank God It's Friday); weekly range retracement)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He expects a **20–30% retracement of the present or previous week's range**. If that retracement does not happen by Friday (he dubbed it "TGIF"; the candle is one-sided), it can instead show up on Monday.
  - To use it: if the last week was predominantly one-sided and Friday did not retrace, use the **highest high Monday forms**. Measure a fib from last week's low to that high and anticipate the retracement.
  - Look for confluence between the 20% level and gaps. On Monday 12 Aug, the 20% level of (last week's low → Monday's high) sat on **Friday's NDOG**. His call was "if it goes below the new week opening gap it can go down to Friday's NDOG", and it did.
- **How to identify:** weekly fib (low → high including Monday's high) plus Friday's NDOG and the NWOG.
- **How to use:** a Monday/Tuesday retracement target for longs after a one-sided up week. He also says early-week action is where you do it "early" on days like this.
- **Example in this video:** Monday 12 Aug: price traded below the NWOG to Friday's NDOG, which coincided with the 20% fib. ICT says he was stopped out on his own Monday trade ("I did not lose on purpose") before it ran to all the levels he outlined.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:31](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1471) — the 20–30% / TGIF explanation · [0:25:17](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1517) — fib from week low to Monday high + Friday NDOG confluence · [0:26:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1617) — what happened to his stop on Monday
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** refers to Monday's live stream (12 Aug) and Friday's stream (9 Aug). The 20–30% weekly retracement idea is an expectation, not a certainty.

### Relative equal highs/lows (initial BSL/SSL) — smooth vs jagged  (aliases: initial relative equal lows/highs)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He is teaching Caleb to focus on **relative equal lows and highs** as the initial liquidity, and to wait for a relative equal high to form rather than predicting it.
  - He prefers the **smooth** level (clean equal lows or highs) to a jagged one. "I don't like this one because it went slightly lower than that one."
  - Pre-8:30 lows had been run on the news, so he did not use them. He marked the post-open relative equal lows (SSL) and later equal highs (BSL) instead.
  - Sequence he observed: post-open relative equal highs ran (BSL taken), and no SSL run yet. Later the sell-side run came, then price was too far up to want to buy.
- **How to identify:** two or more highs/lows at nearly identical price, formed after the opening bell (7:00–9:30 window), with smooth, not overlapping, price action.
- **How to use:** mark them and see which side the market wants. A higher high after a sweep of relative equal lows means he has no interest in shorting; he is "done for the morning".
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/5m before and after 9:30. Equal lows were swept at the 8:30 news, then equal highs formed post-open and were raided at ~9:50 into the 10:00 hour.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:44](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=944) — initial relative equal lows annotated · [0:23:44](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1424) — rejects a slightly lower low · [0:30:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1809) — relative equal highs form after the open · [1:24:25](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=5065) — "where is it smooth here"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he repeatedly contrasts smooth levels (good) with jagged action (muddy, avoid).

### Inversion expectation (shading a gap to fail)  (aliases: iFVG expectation, orange shading)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When he wants a gap to **fail**, he colors it differently (orange rather than his usual color) to signal "I expect inversion characteristics". A gap that trades down through becomes a buy-side imbalance/sell-side inefficiency (BISI) that now acts as resistance.
  - He wanted to see price trade below a gap, come back up, and find stiff resistance on it, then retrace into the daily range lower. Price did not oblige, and that kept him out.
  - He says he actually wants to see PD arrays fail, because it gives confidence that the directional expectation is still on.
  - Small wicks beyond an array do not invalidate it ("coloring outside the lines"); but too much of that is what makes a day muddy.
- **Example in this video:** the small 9:30-area gap on the 1-minute chart. Plan: price breaks below it, returns, rejects, retraces to discount. Instead price pushed up, and he said anything above the short-term high would "kill it" for the morning.
- **Timestamps:** [0:59:27](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=3567) — shading the gap orange · [1:00:17](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=3617) — inversion color convention · [1:20:27](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=4827) — he wants to see PD arrays fail
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** only a statement of expectation and annotation practice; no rule numbers.

### Invalidation by candle close (exit before the stop)  (aliases: save the stop)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If he were short with the stop placed at a given area and a candle **closes** above it, he would kill the trade rather than wait for the stop to be hit ("save the stop"). A close up there means price will likely come back and treat the area as support.
- **How to use:** define the invalidation area for a short idea at a gap/high. A closed candle (not just a wick) beyond it ends the idea.
- **Timestamps:** [1:52:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=6778)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he did this on a hypothetical short idea; wicks are allowed, bodies/closes are what count (same logic as "wicks do the damage, bodies tell the narrative").

### Three-drives pattern (his measured-move target)  (aliases: three Indians — Street Smarts, Connors/Raschke)
- **Kind:** example only / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price pierced the weekly objective three times, which he calls a "three drives" pattern, from the book *Street Smarts* ("three Indians"). He likes classic patterns that pre-date him.
  - Classic use: he used to look for it, usually with a stochastic bearish divergence on the third high, as a reversal setup.
  - His own measured-move twist, which he says is not in the book: measure from the **low before the first high to the third swing high**. First target is **half of that range**, then the low itself.
  - He does not rely on it. Three higher highs do not guarantee a reversal; on a manipulated day the market can "grind you to dust". Some people see it as a long-term top; he has seen it also mark just an intermediate retracement before continuation.
  - He prefers to wait for the logic he teaches (next easy draw on liquidity) over predicting where the weekly closes.
- **Also:** he likes selling inside a bull flag when probabilities favor lower, i.e. he likes seeing "obvious" patterns when he is on the opposite side. He credits Nick Van Nice (not George Lane) with the trend-following stochastic divergence, which he calls a type-two divergence.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2458) — three drives / Street Smarts · [0:45:01](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2701) — half-range measured-move target · [0:46:36](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2796) — prefers the "next easy thing" logic
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** an aside, not a model he is teaching in this mentorship.

### Day-type filter: when to stand aside (grinding / choppy / jagged days)  (aliases: environment filter; "fish out of water"; "swim upstream")
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:**
  - He skips days where price just **grinds one way** with no meaningful retracement, or is **muddy, lethargic and jagged**: small candles, back-and-forth, coloring outside the lines, constantly overlapping. On such days PD arrays are not cleanly respected and he cannot reliably say where it will go.
  - He wants an environment with **gyration**: clean runs, obvious gaps, pullbacks into those gaps, and room to be a buyer and a seller in the same day or even hour ("Spider-Man web swinging"). He calls a day where he can buy and sell both sides a "playground".
  - Conditions that make him back off: right after a high-impact news driver, one-sided from the open, and any session where he cannot paint a simple 20-handle run.
  - Pre-set kill criteria: for the morning he said if price made a **higher high above the short-term high after sweeping the relative equal lows**, he was done and would close the stream. Otherwise by about 10:20 ET with no displacement below the low, he was done.
  - If he is in a trade on such a day he takes many more partials, because he cannot hold comfortably. On unpleasant days he may take one contract off and see how he feels, then another.
  - "High probability" is defined here as: it is very difficult to frame the other side of the market going against the trade idea. If both scenarios can be painted, it is not high probability.
- **How to identify:** one-sided grind with no real retracements; overlapping candles and tiny ranges; long consolidation; many small wick violations of arrays; heavy-news day.
- **How to use:** write down the conditions that make you not trade, and a stop-for-the-day trigger, before the session. Log these days and study them afterwards.
- **Timestamps:** [0:49:03](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2943) — grinding days · [0:55:26](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=3326) — coping by scaling out one contract at a time · [1:25:13](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=5113) — definition of high probability · [1:29:14](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=5354) — lethargic, "swim upstream" days · [3:19:05](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=11945) — wants intraday swing-trader environment
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he stresses this is personal fit. Some students may thrive on these days. The line he adds: "you have to know your own Kryptonite."

### Breakaway gap: FVG left after a consolidation + stop hunt  (aliases: breakaway gap; "this gap should stay open")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price leaves a **consolidation after a stop hunt** (the stops below the range are taken, then price breaks above the range high) and leaves an FVG on the way, that gap is a **breakaway gap**. They "tend to stay open" — not always but most of the time, so it acts like real support (or resistance for the mirror case).
  - It acts as support even though price may not return to it for a long time. In this video price came back down to it only one tick short in places, and later dipped just into it.
  - **Why expect that:** the gap formed leaving the consolidation. It is not "support and resistance" (price did not need to retest a wick) and not "supply and demand" (he calls S&D a myth outside commodities); it is algorithmic delivery.
  - Contrast: other times he **wants** a gap to close, i.e. wants it to fail so it can invert (see inversion expectation). Say which you expect before the trade.
- **How to identify:** (1) a multi-bar consolidation, (2) a sweep of its low (for a long idea), (3) displacement above the consolidation high leaving an FVG, (4) a higher-timeframe target above.
- **How to use:** it is the setup for the IOFED entry (next concept). Go back through old charts: look for gaps left open after leaving a consolidation after a stop hunt and see how often they hold.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, ~10:47–10:52 ET. A 9:30-onward consolidation with a sweep of the stops below it, then a break above the range high. That left an FVG whose candle-3 low (top of the gap) was 18931.00 (as captioned). Price dropped back into the top of the gap at 10:52 without filling the lower portion, then rallied to the daily volume imbalance.
- **Timestamps:** [2:04:55](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7495) — why he expects it to stay open · [2:08:10](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7690) — left the consolidation after a stop hunt, gap tends to stay open · [2:25:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8758) — "breakaway gap … breaking away from this consolidation" · [2:50:54](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10254) — not classic support/resistance because price never touched the wick
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** an old idea made explicit. He says whenever he says "I want to see this one stay open / remain unfilled" in streams, this is the logic. He calls the stream remarks repeatable proof it is not cherry-picked.

### Institutional Orderflow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: institutional order flow entry drill)
- **Kind:** entry model (re-taught; the detail is the valuable part)
- **What ICT says:**
  - IOFED is a **partial entry into a fair value gap that you believe will not fill completely**. Use it only when he (or you) says a gap should stay open/unfilled. "Unless I say that this gap should stay open … you're never looking at IOFED" — it is not the go-to for every FVG. You can still adopt it as a personal entry model.
  - **Where to fill:** the upper half of the gap. In the bullish case the gap spans candle-1 high (bottom) to candle-3 low (top). The best fills are from the **CE to the top of the gap**. The lower half is the least probable fill. If you are wrong and price trades down to the low, it usually still matters not, because the CE-to-top fill has already happened.
  - **Limit order placement:** put the limit at the candle-3 low (top of the gap) — it only takes one tick below that to fill a long — or even one tick above it. Never aim for the low end: he has had real limit orders where price traded 2 ticks past and did not fill (spread, queue), then ran without him. He asks the viewer to choose between the top of the gap and one tick above, and to accept the extra drawdown. (Exact wording on the tick offset is garbled in captions.)
  - **Stop:** below the candle-1 high (bottom of the FVG). In his example the span was about nine ticks (~2.25 NQ handles; 4 ticks = 1 handle, as captioned).
  - **Philosophy:** "low resistance barriers to entry" and "low-hanging fruit" for targets. He is "never trying to get a fill at the low end"; he does not demand precision; the spread must not be a factor.
  - **Scaling variant (his own):** first entry 6 contracts at the top of a wider gap; add 4 more when price goes into the CE/upper quadrant; add 5 more one tick below CE if it trades there (same assumption: the lower portion stays open). In his timed example: 10:47 first entry, 10:49 into CE, 10:51 one tick below CE. That is averaging into a losing position, which at least one funded-account firm (he names Topstep, Caleb's choice) does not allow. Workaround: use IOFED as the only entry and pyramid with later, higher PD arrays (order blocks and FVGs above), which that firm allows.
  - **Mirror:** he says the same applies when the array is a premium array (price trading up into it), but the sensitive area flips accordingly (mirror by implication; he does not walk it through).
- **How to identify:** a gap you expect to remain open (breakaway gap, left after a consolidation/stop hunt), a higher-timeframe target above, and price returning toward the gap.
- **How to use:** place a limit at/near the top of the gap, stop below the gap's candle-1 high, target the next array/liquidity (here the 18951.50 candle high ~20 handles, then the daily volume imbalance).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Tue 13 Aug 2024, call made around 10:47–10:50 ET: "I want this gap to stay open". 10:52 ET (inside the 10:50–11:10 macro): price dips into the top of the gap — a drop of roughly one tick — without touching the lower portion, then displaces up. Target: the high at 18951.50 (about 20 handles away) and then the daily volume imbalance whose low he ballparked near 18956 with CE near 18960 (about 30 handles total from the 18931 entry). ICT said he would only expect Caleb to aim at the CE of that daily volume imbalance. He did not trade it himself.
- **Timestamps:** [2:02:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7352) — "screenshot this": any trade one tick below that low is an IOFED · [2:18:35](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8315) — CE-to-high is the sensitive zone · [2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457) — limit placement and the unfilled-limit experience · [2:29:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8949) — 6 + 4 + 5 scaling and funded-account averaging rule · [2:43:41](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9821) — targets: ~20 handles, then daily VI CE ~30 handles
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** this restates the older IOFED as a "gap should stay open" tool rather than a general OTE-style entry into any FVG. Prices as captioned are noisy. All figures in the example are approximate.

### Order block with consecutive down-closed candles; the open = change in the state of delivery  (aliases: bullish order block; CISD; "first time I taught this")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement / new teaching (he states his private-mentorship students never saw it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - An order block is **not only the last down-closed candle** before the run. When several down-closed candles are consecutive, the series is **one order block**. Count down-closed candles leftward: is there one immediately to the left? One before that? One before that? Stop when there is not.
  - The key price is the **opening price of the first (leftmost, highest) candle in the series**. That open is the **change in the state of delivery**: "the algorithm sees these three candles and goes right to that opening price". The algorithm is called back to it.
  - **Validation:** the block is validated when price trades **above that candle's open** (for a bullish block).
  - **Support:** it needs a supporting PD array: here a fair value gap below ("momentum shifted higher; this should stay open"). "An order block is never an island unto itself" and is not supply and demand.
  - **Body range:** the range is the open of the series down to the close of the lowest down-closed candle. The narrative is in the **bodies**: they stay in the **upper half** (open down to the mean threshold). Wicks are allowed to do the damage (they can spike below, even through the mean threshold) but the wicks never pierce "the body's low" (wording unclear in captions; read as: bodies do not trade through the low of the range). Any pullback into that range should have bodies in the upper half.
  - "You don't have to sweat picking the best entry" if you know the range: any return to the open price and/or into the upper half of the consecutive down-closed candles is the entry.
  - It is the same logic as his entry the previous day (the 6-contract short on NQ in Monday's stream), mirrored.
- **How to identify:** (1) a run of consecutive down-closed candles before the up move; (2) a supporting FVG/PD array below; (3) price displaces above the open of the series; (4) mark the open, the mean threshold and the lowest close.

### At least one of three: short-term low, order block, fair value gap
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - In a macro, price needs a reason to go to liquidity. Check for (1) a short-term low (or high) to be run, (2) an order block, or (3) an FVG/inefficiency. You will have **at least one of the three, usually one, at best two, never all three** at the same price and time.
  - If there is **no gap**, wait for the down-closed candle that forms in the retracement: it has to run away and then come back (the order block logic). In his case there was no short-term low until it formed, but there was a gap.
- **Timestamps:** [2:49:18](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10158) — "either a gap or an order block or a short-term low" · [2:50:06](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10206) — never all three
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** only a pointer on what to look for inside a macro. He says paid-mentorship students always asked about this.

### Macros (algorithmic time windows) — 10:50–11:10 ET and every hour  (aliases: macro; the "spool")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A macro is a **20-minute window every hour: 10 minutes before the top of the hour to 10 minutes after** (e.g. 9:50–10:10, 10:50–11:10, 11:50–12:10). Label it on your chart "10:50 to 11:10 a.m. Eastern Time".
  - Always use **New York time** regardless of where you live. If you do not use an alarm (e.g. 10:45 for the 10:50 macro), draw vertical lines framing each macro (but be careful they do not hide candle colors).
  - Inside the macro the market "spools" (like a fishing line leaving a reel). It will: (a) run to a **short-term low** (SSL / sell stops), (b) run to a **short-term high** (BSL / buy stops), or (c) trade to an **inefficiency** (FVG) to give smart money an entry **just before** it spools to the liquidity.
  - The macro is "like a supercharger" for entries: tighter fills, immediate response (price moving away and never near your stop is how you know you are on side).
  - Not every macro is equally good; he leaves "which one and when" for the book. He also mentions a **midnight–1:00 am** macro and a **final-hour-of-the-day** macro.
  - Entries outside macros are fine ("I'm not saying … ICT only teaches the first 10 minutes before and after the hour"), but you demand more of yourself.
  - **90-minute cycles:** he says "there is no 90-minute cycle" to copy; 90 minutes is just the smallest window you should give yourself, because it guarantees at least one macro.
  - Timing is "the most critical function in price delivery". The macro tells you when to look; the higher-timeframe draw tells you which direction.
- **How to identify:** clock window (ET). Then ask which of the three the market needs: SSL, BSL, or an FVG/OB entry first.
- **How to use:** be positioned in the first 10 minutes (before the hour) for the entry into the inefficiency, then expect the spool in the last half. At 10:52 (mid-macro) he saw the entry FVG, then the run up to the daily imbalance, then at 11:10 a second entry in the order block open.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Tue 13 Aug.
  - 10:50–11:10 macro: a bullish FVG (breakaway gap) trades down into at 10:52 → rally to the 18951.50 high and the daily volume imbalance. The OB open is revisited at 11:10 and delivers higher.
  - 11:50–12:10 macro: price dug down at about 11:50 into a sell-side run into the daily inefficiency at discount (mid-macro, ~12:00 per his narration), then rallied to a smaller volume imbalance above and the liquidity above the high ("rock-climbing footholds"). The real runs start inside the window; after 10:50–11:10 "look how shitty it gets".
- **Timestamps:** [2:40:28](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9628) — 10:50–11:10, every 60 minutes · [2:41:16](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9676) — the three things price does in a macro · [2:59:00](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10740) — New York time always, alarms or vertical lines · [3:07:03](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=11223) — spooling analogy and the 11:50 run · [3:02:12](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10932) — 90-minute cycle comment
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he calls this his "clinic on macros" and says most other macros circulating are not macros. In this stream he says the 10:00 macro begins at 9:50 (10 minutes before the hour); this lecture makes the "every hour, ±10 minutes" statement explicit.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- Order block of consecutive down-closed candles: key price = open of the leftmost candle; valid when price trades above that open; bodies stay in the upper half; wicks may violate.
- Order block must have a supporting PD array (not an island).
- Macro = 10 minutes before to 10 minutes after each hour; labeled in ET; always NY time. Inside it price goes to SSL, BSL or an FVG entry first.
- At least one (usually one, at most two) of: short-term low/high, OB, FVG.

- A smooth equal-high/low pool is preferred over a jagged one.
- Higher-timeframe draw first; if none is clear, no trade.
- Chart hygiene: scale the chart so the whole move from origin to target fills the screen with room to annotate; elongate vertically to see daily volume imbalances.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Do not chase.** Going long in the premium at the tail end of a run (e.g. 50 handles at the end of a 200-handle move) is "chasing price". He teaches buying in discounts and waiting for opposing liquidity and a failed swing, or a clear indication after a retracement into a pre-set PD array or liquidity pool [0:35:15](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2115), [0:36:04](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2164).
- **Missing moves is fine.** You will not see every move at the beginning; do not punish yourself. "Tomorrow" and "this afternoon" always have trades. You are not required to trade every day, and "every day is a trading day" is gambler talk. Study price daily but do not expect a trade daily [1:34:05](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=5645), [3:29:34](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=12574).
- **Find your Kryptonite.** Identify your weak conditions (for him: grinding, one-sided, jagged days), say them openly, and avoid them. Weaknesses are how you prevent failure. - **Pick one thing.** He does not use everything he teaches. ## Glossary candidates
`IOFED (Institutional Orderflow Entry Drill)` — limit-order entry in the upper half of a FVG expected to stay open; best fills from CE to top, stop below the gap's candle-1 high.
`Breakaway gap` — FVG formed leaving a consolidation after a stop hunt; expected to remain at least partly open.
`Change in the state of delivery` — here: the opening price of the first (highest) candle in a run of consecutive down-closed candles (bullish block); price is "called" back to it.
`Macro` — 20-minute algorithmic window, 10 minutes either side of each hour (ET), in which price spools to a short-term low, high or an inefficiency.
`Spool` — the fast run away from the start of a macro, compared to fishing line leaving a reel.
`Manual intervention` — abrupt, forceful non-algorithmic move (FOMC 2:00/2:30 ET); not tradable in real time.
`TGIF` — expectation of a 20–30% retracement of the weekly range by Friday; if it does not happen, use Monday's high.
`Three drives` — three successive higher highs; ICT's measured move targets half the range from the pre-first-high low to the third high, then that low.
`Kryptonite` — a trader's personal weak conditions, to be identified and avoided.
`Mean threshold` — midpoint of an order block's body range (used for the upper-half-bodies test).
`Volume imbalance (daily)` — small gap between consecutive daily candle bodies with overlapping wicks; used as a target, with its CE as the practical objective.
`Low-hanging fruit / low-resistance entry` — entries and targets chosen where the spread and precision do not matter.
