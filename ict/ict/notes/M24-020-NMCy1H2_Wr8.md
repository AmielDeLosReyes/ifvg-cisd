## Concepts

### Re-entering after being stopped out of a winning trade  (aliases: trail-stop re-entry, getting back in)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** If you trail your stop and get stopped out with a profit, the trade idea may still be valid; you can go back in at the next valid PD array. He demonstrates his own trade from the early morning (shown on a live account, replayed with labels).
- **How to use:** In the example he was short from a bearish order block / breaker, added on a wick back up into the area, took a small partial on a sell-side liquidity run, got stopped when he brought the stop down "aggressively", then re-sold a return into the inversion FVG and added more once he knew he was "on side". He says tightening the stop that fast "would never be done" if you understand the market maker sell model: you must let the second stage of redistribution deliver before trailing hard. Later he was stopped out on a trail while away from the screen and price reached Tuesday's low (the draw on liquidity of the MMSM).
- **Example in this video:** Overnight NQ opened Wed 6 PM with a huge gap, rallied to Tuesday's daily low area, formed a consolidation of relative equal lows, rallied, then took out relative equal highs (turtle soup/smart money reversal). He faded that high: the entry was off a bearish order block (a small breaker within a larger one), then later the iFVG. Final target Tuesday's (Aug 27) low.

### Bearish order block and CISD as he defines them  (aliases: change in the state of delivery, order block, breaker)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A bearish order block is built from a run of consecutive up-closed candles; the change in the state of delivery (CISD) is the opening price of the lowest-opening candle in that run of up-closed candles (when price trades below it). In the example two adjacent candles together form the bearish order block. He says not every up-closed candle is a bearish order block and not every three-candle pattern with a gap-like separation is an FVG — he is correcting people who mis-call these.
- **Example in this video:** The early-morning rally of up-closed candles into the high; he waited for the change in state of delivery (price trading down through the lowest open of the up candles) before selling, and noted a smaller breaker inside the larger one.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:27](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=507) — definition and mis-calls; [0:11:39](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=699) — "every up-closed candle is not an OB, not every candle separation is an FVG".

Taught in the 2017 mentorship core content and subsequent lectures. Applied here to the huge 6 PM gap and the prior afternoon's FVG, whose extension projected the next target.

### Opening range gap (premium gap) and "too expensive" — not chasing  (aliases: ORG, RTH gap, premium gap, don't chase)
- **Kind:** rule / application
- **What ICT says:** NQ opened Thursday with a large gap above the previous settlement (premium opening range gap) and ran straight up for the first minutes without any retracement. When price is "really, really elevated" from the ORG low (the previous settlement), it is expensive: he elects to look for shorts rather than chase longs. The gap can stay open all day, but eventually price tends to trade back to it, giving a better discount. Do not try to catch a run just because it is running ("mass transit": if you miss the bus, wait 20 minutes for the next one, like another macro). If price takes off and you are not already positioned, sit on your hands and let it come back for a low-risk entry (he referenced a Tuesday two weeks earlier).
- **How to use:** Halfway of the ORG is marked as a reference; the ORG low is the prior settle.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 29: opens with a huge premium gap, runs to the Aug 27 NDOG and the Aug 18 NWOG area and through them in about two 1m candles, toward quadruple-high buy-side at 19,694.25. He does not buy; plan is short on the first valid presentation if it breaks down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:34](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1114) — don't chase, let it come back; [0:19:22](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1162) — bus analogy; [0:29:05](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1745) — very expensive vs ORG low, ORG may stay open all day.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Continuation of the ORG discussion from lecture 19, now with a premium gap instead of a discount gap.

### Layered NDOG/NWOG levels as a high-resistance liquidity run  (aliases: high resistance liquidity run, spider web, opening gaps layered)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** When many new day and new week opening gaps (here the Aug 27 NDOG, the Aug 23 gap he had called a huge magnet / "black hole", and the Aug 18 NWOG) are stacked around price, a run through them is held back "like a cobweb tangled around its legs". It is not an absence of buying/selling pressure; the algorithm references all those levels and offers back-and-forth price. If price blasts through them in one or two candles it should have no problem reaching the target; if it keeps petering out above them it is a high-resistance run. Traders who do not plot these gaps never notice why. A strong "fly" can still escape the web and run to "the sugar" (the buy-side).
- **How to identify:** Plot NWOG/NDOG levels; look for price stalling and chopping between them instead of expanding.
- **How to use:** If price drives through them fast it implies the buy-side at 19,694 gets targeted and then likely a puke-out and drop into the ORG; if it chops, stay out. Judge strength above the high: does it accelerate through the new week opening gap high and keep a bullish body without wicks retracing?
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 29 morning: runs through the gap levels within two candles, gets close to the 19,694 quadruple high and peters out (twice), then hangs around inside the gap cluster for most of the session.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:25](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=805) — NDOG Aug 27 and NWOG above; [0:30:41](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1841) — aggressively slicing through them implies a run to buy-side; [1:49:05](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6545) — high-resistance explanation; [1:52:16](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6736) — spider web analogy.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He explains that the paid mentorship streams were built on identifying high- vs low-resistance liquidity run signatures day by day.

### High vs low resistance liquidity runs and when a market is tradable  (aliases: low-resistance conditions, 50/50 market)
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:** A low-resistance run is easy, one-sided, gives immediate positive feedback; analysis is not conflicted. If the market can be framed both ways, it is 50/50, not high probability, and not worth putting money behind; just study it ("tape it") with no weight on the outcome. If you must be 100% nimble with zero forgiveness for being wrong on either side, that is a high-probability losing day. When references conflict and "it could be this or that," accept it is not time to trade. If the first FVG has not even been traded to and nothing qualifies, close the charts and review that night.
- **How to use:** Frame the scenario in both directions; if both are plausible, skip. Sitting out is a "position that wins every single time" and you never lose money sitting still. If price later does the bullish thing, sitting still was still the right call, not a regret.
- **Example in this video:** NQ stayed below the 19,694 high for most of the session, chopping around the layered gaps: one side said run to 19,694, the other said drop into the first presentation. He wrote it off as 50/50 and said a student following his criteria would be flat.
- **Timestamps:** [0:52:22](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3142) — framing both directions = low probability; [1:02:52](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3772) — conflicting references, sit out; [1:05:45](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3945) — the student would be flat; [1:13:12](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4392) — sitting still always wins; [1:17:38](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4658) — high vs low resistance framework.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a restatement of the "high resistance / low resistance liquidity run" ideas from earlier lectures, now applied as a participation filter. He also says rule-less trading is gambling.

### Partials, covering costs and 5:1 gearing  (aliases: first partial, move stop to cover costs, risk to reward 1:5, trade management)
- **Kind:** rule / trade management
- **What ICT says:** Partials are always profit: no partial has ever lost money. Know your first partial before entering. When price has moved halfway to the first partial, move the stop to cover commissions (not just break even, since commission means true break even is above entry) and sit still. Place the first partial at the short-term high that forms at a logical premium level. Because a partial is realised profit, it "scratches the itch" to move a stop prematurely. Aim for roughly 5:1 gearing (risk 1 to make 5): then you can be wrong a lot — at about a 38% win rate (he says he thinks it is 38%) you still make money. Move stops as the position moves your way. Do not overleverage (e.g. 15 contracts on a Topstep-style account is the wrong answer; reduce size so the risk is measured).
- **How to use:** Entry on the first bullish FVG model (devil's advocate long in the video): fill around ...588 (digits prefix unclear), stop under the imbalance (middle) candle's low minus one tick (or first candle's low minus one tick if conservative); he prefers the imbalance candle to keep risk manageable. His arithmetic: entry about 588, stop about 570.5, so risk about 18 handles; with five micro NQ contracts and a first partial 60–100 handles away, that is roughly 5:1. First partial at the high end of the inefficiency above (an FVG formed by a down-closed candle), second partial higher if affordable.
- **Example in this video:** After price dropped into the entry FVG and rallied, the high end of the upper inefficiency (an FVG made by a down-closed candle) was his first partial, because lower levels were too close to partial at and the move could fail; later he watched the consequent encroachment of the premium wick near 19,694.5 (price then traded one tick above it; caption unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [1:20:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4804) — partials are always profit; [1:26:34](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5194) — first partial at FVG high; [1:39:29](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5969) — entry/stop math; [1:41:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6064) — 5:1 gearing, win rate; [1:42:42](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6162) — cover costs after halfway to first partial.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Contradicts the "stop or target" mindset (all-or-nothing). Slippage can still hurt if a big news gap runs over your stop.

### Cutting a fizzling trade in half  (aliases: scale out when trade quality fades)
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** If the technicals that justified the trade fade, take half off immediately if you have a profit (or close the whole position if it is one contract), even if commissions are a "hundred bucks". As a developing trader this reduces anxiety, then tape-read the remainder. You are in control of your actions; nobody sucked you in. If you feel anxious while only tape reading with no trade on, that signals harsh self-criticism.
- **Timestamps:** [1:20:55](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4855) — trade fizzles, cut in half; [1:21:42](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4902) — close it if one contract; [1:24:05](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5045) — anxiety while tape reading.

### Institutional order entry drill and breakaway gap, restated  (aliases: institutional orderflow entry)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** In the afternoon-of-this-session example, a gap forms; price trades to the high of one candle and the low of another (into the gap) but leaves it open, "too strong for entry": that is the entry drill, and the unfilled gap is a breakaway gap. For a bullish scenario, the entry is on that candle; a stop beneath the imbalance candle's low. Without the gap closing fully, momentum continues.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Do not chase a run that opens with a big gap; wait for price to come back; look for shorts when price is far above the ORG low (previous settle).
- First FVG after the open forms at the earliest on the 9:31 candle; pre-open candles do not count.
- Quadruple high / buy-side referenced at 19,694.25; highs repeated at that level.
- Premium opening: price should enter only the lower half of the layered premium array, not the midpoint, before reversing.
- If a setup can be drawn both ways, treat it as 50/50: do not trade.
- First partial known before entry; move stop to cover costs when price is halfway to the first partial.
- Target about 5:1 reward to risk; you can win about 38% and be profitable.
- Stop for FVG entry: imbalance (middle) candle's low minus one tick; more conservative: first candle's low minus one tick.
- Demo/test any rule set: backtest, forward test, tape read, then demo for at least 3 to 6 months.
- Partial profit is always profit; when a trade deteriorates, cut half off.
- Remainder of this week's lectures focus on stops and re-entry; the last two weeks of the mentorship will be mostly executions/management.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Do not trust gut feelings; use procedures you have studied by backtesting, forward testing, tape reading, then demoing for 3–6 months ([0:49:53](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2993)).
- "Move slow when making money, move fast when protecting it" (from Larry Williams) ([0:50:42](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3042)).
- FOMO is conquered by knowing what you're doing and giving yourself permission not to trade every fluctuation ([0:39:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2344)).
- Be rule-based: have procedure in advance; "I demand price come to me and behave a certain way" before engaging.
- Social media pressure to prove you were right is a character flaw; making money must be the goal, not other people's opinions ([1:02:52](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3772)).
- Trading that "works" on a 50/50 call is gambling even if profitable.
- Learn to wait for your setup (restaurant/lobster analogy); do not settle for a grilled cheese ([1:33:41](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5621)).
- Waiting in a discount with a limit order while price moves away takes courage; chasing a rallied price with no stop is fake bravery ([1:46:41](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6401)).
- Mistakes are useful: highlight what you need to improve; journaling as a self-supportive habit; do not copy his trades with real or funded money ([1:28:08](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5288)).
- He does not take other people's money and is not paid by advertisers; the swearing is a character for marketing.

## Glossary candidates
`Grading a price swing` — applying fib quadrants to an inefficiency/swing; not "quarters theory".
`High-resistance liquidity run` — a move held back by layered opening gaps/PD arrays; vs low-resistance, which runs one-sided and fast.
`Trend line phantom` — a retail diagonal trendline that is a fake target for stop runs.
`Cover costs` — moving the stop to entry plus commissions rather than raw break even.
`Gearing` — reward-to-risk ratio (here 5:1).
`Devil's advocate trade` — a hypothetical trade used to show how a setup would be managed.
`Inversion aspect (of a PD array)` — each PD array flips function after violation (breaker, iFVG).
