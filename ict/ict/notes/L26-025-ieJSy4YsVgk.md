## Concepts

### Inversion Fair Value Gap and Utilization Shift
- **Kind:** refinement + example
- **What ICT says:** A fair value gap has multiple utilizations. First utilization = original function (bearish FVG = down-close, bullish = up-close). When price closes above/outside a bearish FVG, it becomes an inversion FVG on the way back up. When price then closes back below it, it returns to its first (bearish) utilization. Each closure changes the gap's "character"; the shift is confirmed by body placement and close location.
- **How to identify:** (1) Mark the FVG, (2) note the close of the candle that created it, (3) watch if price closes outside (above/below) it—that's the shift to inversion, (4) once price closes back inside it from the inversion direction, it reclaims original utilization.
- **How to use:** Entry zones. On the shift to inversion, do not short immediately. On the return to first utilization (close outside it again), that's the true breakout and short entry. Wait for body placement confirmation (lower half) before committing.
- **Example in this video:** NQ pre-market trades down through a FVG (bearish, first utilization). Price bounces back up into it—this is now the inversion FVG phase. ICT doesn't short here. When price closes back below the FVG, it returns to original (bearish) character. Now short is valid with bodies showing lower half.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:19](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=559) — explaining utilization concept; [0:14:41](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=881) — visual diagram of shift; [0:15:30](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=930) — drawing utilization layers
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The shift is mechanical but requires patience. New traders want to short on inversion phase, but that's premature. Wait for the close-back-through and body placement confirmation.

### Obsidian PD Array (aliases: Obsidian high/low, two-opposing-wicks midpoint)
- **Kind:** new coinage + example
- **What ICT says:** When two wicks point in opposite directions, find the midpoint (consequent encroachment) of each wick. That level is the "Obsidian" and has strong support/resistance. Price tends to find levels there and use them as mini swing points within a range. Specific to consolidation periods.
- **How to identify:** Look for two wicks (one up, one down) in a consolidation range; measure each wick's midpoint (0.5 Fib level); if they align or are very close, you have Obsidian. Draw horizontal line(s) at these levels.
- **How to use:** Intraday range trading target. If shorting into a consolidation, aim for Obsidian to take a partial or place a stop. Price respects Obsidian more reliably in choppy ranges than in trends.
- **Example in this video:** NQ pre-market 7–9 AM consolidation: two opposing wicks, their midpoints form Obsidian levels. ICT watches price bounce between Obsidian high and a lower level. When price closes below Obsidian and stops holding, it signals willingness to drop further.

### Consolidation vs. Trending Sessions and Session Phases
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** After London trends (2–5 AM ET), the 7–9 AM pre-market will likely consolidate (boxy, choppy, rangy), not trend straight. Once 9:30 RTH opens, trending can resume. Each session type requires different tactics: consolidation = take partials quick, tight stops, nimble exits; trending = hold longer, let winners run, wider stops.
- **How to identify:** (1) Look at the previous session (London) for trend direction, (2) the 7–9 AM high/low forms a range, (3) price bounces within that range instead of breaking out, (4) rarely exceeds the prior high/low without quick rejection.
- **How to use:** Plan exits before entering. In consolidation, scale out 25–50% at minor levels (Obsidian, octants); don't wait for the full move. In trending, only take first partial, hold runner(s).
- **Example in this video:** London trended up overnight. 7–9 AM (pre-market) traded in a range, bouncing between highs and Obsidian. ICT planned to take partials here, noted his regret at not taking one at the first Obsidian touch. No trending until 9:30.

### Partial Taking Strategy in Consolidation
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** In a consolidation, don't hold the full position to the ultimate target. Take 25–75% at minor levels (Obsidian, consequent encroachment of key wicks, octants), lock in some profit, then let smaller position run if it does. ICT regrets not taking a partial at his first identified level; this costs him swing-to-swing scalps. By taking partials, you convert risk-free or near-risk-free situations.
- **How to identify:** Mark the minor levels (Obsidian, wick CE, quadrants) before trade; plan exits at each. Execute them on close confirmation.
- **How to use:** (1) Enter full position, (2) first target = take 50%, (3) second target = take 25% or half of remaining, (4) last 25% = runner or hold per plan. Adjust position size accordingly.
- **Example in this video:** ICT enters short 6 contracts into NWOG. First planned target = Obsidian low. Second = sell-side level lower. Meant to take 4 contracts off at Obsidian but didn't (regret noted). Later takes 6 contracts off entirely instead of scaling, then re-enters with 4. Says he should have split positions: 2–2–2 (three separate exits) instead of 6 then 4.

### Wick Behavior and Rejection Signals in Consolidation
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** In consolidation, watch the wicks as they form. If a wick points up but the body stays in lower half and closes below the wick's midpoint (CE of the wick), that's rejection of the upside and a signal to continue lower. Conversely, if a wick points down but body closes in upper half, it's rejection of downside. The body's half-placement is the order flow truth; the wick is bait/test.
- **How to identify:** Watch each candle: wick direction, body close location (upper/lower half of prior PDA or range midpoint). Non-alignment = rejection.
- **How to use:** Confirmation for staying in trade or adding. If you're short and see upwick + lower-half body, hold/add. If upwick + upper-half body, consider exit.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple candles form in pre-market range; upwicks followed by bodies in lower half = continuation of short. When a body finally shows up in upper half of a wick, ICT considers taking a partial or tightening stop.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:45](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=405) — wick behavior; [0:12:41](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=761) — body placement rules; [0:19:52](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=1192) — watching for upper-half body as warning
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Mohawk (wick touch + quick close back down) is permissible but weakest form of order flow.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- **Session phases:** London 2–5 AM trends → 7–9 AM consolidates → 9:30 RTH can trend again (rarely 3 trending sessions back-to-back).
- **Consolidation protocol:** Take partials at every minor level (Obsidian, octants, prior swing low); don't hold full position to final target in a range.
- **FVG utilization shift:** Close above/outside gap = inversion phase (don't short yet). Close back below/outside = returns to original (short valid on body placement confirmation).
- **Obsidian:** Two opposing wicks' midpoints; strong mini-swing point in consolidation.
- **Body placement in lower half = bearish; upper half = bullish.** Wicks can exceed either zone; bodies determine order flow.
- **Wick + lower body = continuation signal; wick + upper body = warning/rejection of move.
- **Jackson Hole Symposium:** Summer doldrums + event = choppier, rangy markets; be extra nimble.

## Glossary candidates
`Obsidian` — (ICT coinage) the midpoint level of two opposing wicks in a consolidation range; acts as mini-swing support/resistance where price bounces repeatedly within a box. Specific to ranging markets.

`Utilization` — the functional role of a PD array in price action. First utilization = original character (e.g., bearish down-close FVG). When price closes outside, it becomes an inversion. When price closes back through the inversion direction, it returns to first utilization. Each shift is confirmed by body placement and close.

`Session type` — the behavioral character of a market period: trending (one directional move), consolidation (rangy, choppy), reversal, or expansion. Each requires different tactics (partial-taking, stop placement, position sizing).

`Mohawk` — (from prior teachings) a wick that pokes into a PD array followed immediately by a body close back below the wick; permissible but weak order flow signal. Lowest-confidence formation.
