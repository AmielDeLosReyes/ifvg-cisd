## Concepts

### Draw on liquidity first ("where is it going?")  (aliases ICT uses: "where is it drawing to", "the secret ingredient", "reach for", cookie-cutter run)
- **Kind:** rule / philosophy (central lesson of the video)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first and most important skill is knowing where the market is likely to go next. Until you know that, entries, stop loss and targets are "not even important yet".
  - Knowing the draw lets you frame risk (where your stop goes), which he calls the first real measure of understanding what you are doing.
  - Price is always doing one of two things: running to liquidity or running to an inefficiency, above or below the market. It is not "buying and selling pressure", indicators, or bookmap bubbles. He calls the repeating pieces "signatures", not patterns.
  - If you know the draw correctly you can trade "any old thing" (his "flip a quarter" line) with a realistic stop and still win.
  - Beginners should not try to pick entries at all at first. Only pick the direction/draw; afterwards, in hindsight, look back to see what PD arrays (FVG, breaker, Silver Bullet, market maker model) formed in the delivery.
  - The "cookie cutter" moves he lists, in rising difficulty: (1) run from an inefficiency to a liquidity pool; (2) run from a liquidity pool to an inefficiency; (3) run from one liquidity pool to another, e.g. a false breakout above an old high into a false breakout below an old low (he calls this the highest form of what he teaches, "not all that complex" but needs experience).
- **How to identify:** On the chart ask: where is the nearest premium (or discount) liquidity, and where is the nearest inefficiency? Price in a discount reaches up for the nearest short-term high / minor BSL or an unfilled gap.
- **How to use:**
  - Pick the smallest, easiest objective. Short-term high (minor BSL) is easier to find than an inefficiency because shorts will have trailed their buy-stop protection just above it.
  - Two-stage exit with multiple micros: partial at the minor BSL / short-term high, final exit (his word "terminus") at the low of the inefficiency above.
  - If the expected draw is not clearly there, do nothing; be content with it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, pre-8:30 to 9:00 ET. Price had sold off overnight leaving lows jagged (no equal lows). A minor BSL sat at the lower of two relative equal highs. Above it was an upper-quadrant inefficiency. After the 8:30 news price ran to the short-term high and then up to the bottom (low) of the inefficiency, then sold hard. He calls that "low-hanging fruit" and says "you could be done".

### Dealing range from the pre-session high and low (and use the smaller/lower reference high)  (aliases ICT uses: dealing range, "the canvas", range between two reference points, pre-session range)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Before 9:30 ET, define the range as the pre-session low and a pre-session high. Everything that matters for the first idea is inside that range. You may never need price to trade above the high or below the low.
  - When there are two candidate highs (here a lower high and a higher relative-equal-high), use the lower/smaller reference as the range high "to make things very simple" and to reduce the need to be right. The lower high is also the more reactionary one (the sell-off from it was more energetic than from the other).
  - Why not assume the higher equal high is the draw: when the two highs sit close together and are obvious, price may bump the lower one, stay inside the range and never clear the higher one. He explicitly says this is what he watches for between 9:30 and 10:00.
  - Using the larger high instead would still have been "fine, not grossly out of spec"; the inefficiency below it was still the focal point.
  - Time scope: for a 9:30 plan he cares about the previous 30 minutes, hour or hour and a half; he says you can do this all day on sub-1-minute charts.
  - Beginner benefit: trading inside a defined range removes the need to know where the next big run is. Big runs "color outside the lines" and a new trader is not equipped to find those yet; aim for "base hits".
- **How to identify:** Pre-9:30 low to pre-9:30 high. Within it, locate where price rejected (bodies and wicks show it has no interest in going lower). Look for relative equal highs/lows (smooth) vs. a jagged run.
- **How to use:**
  - Three quarters of the range (upper quadrant for a rally) is the OTE zone. An inefficiency near that area is a valid target; he says a run to the OTE-area inefficiency is enough and price need not reach the equal highs.
  - Frame stop and target from the range edges; use the range as the canvas for choosing a draw.
- **Example in this video:** NQ pre-session: range low at the pre-8:30 low (sell-side taken, failing to reach a lower eighth), range high at the lower of the two relative-equal highs (a minor BSL). Inefficiency sat roughly at three quarters of the low-to-high range.

### Jagged vs smooth levels (relative equal highs/lows as the draw)  (aliases ICT uses: made jagged, smooth levels, relative equal highs, relative equal lows)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A side of the market where price keeps making lower low after lower low with no relative equal lows is "jagged". There is nothing smooth below to draw to.
  - After a jagged run, look for where the market left smooth levels (relative equal highs or lows). The market gravitates to the smooth level. He said he has taught this "this year and previous years".
  - A shift in market structure after the jagged drop, keyed off a sell-side-imbalance gap that then inverts, signals the turn toward the smooth level.
- **How to identify:** Count the lows in the leg down: if each is lower and none matches another, it is jagged. The relative equal highs above, where bodies converge (here near the .125 level), are smooth.
- **How to use:** Use the smooth level (or the nearest minor pool before it) as the draw after a jagged run; wait for 8:30 or 9:30 as the starting gun (see time section).
- **Example in this video:** NQ overnight: lows kept dropping with no equal lows (jagged). Relative equal highs sat above with bodies converging on the .125 line from yesterday's RTH opening range gap. He used this as the "I like that as a draw, staying inside the range, needing it not to trade there" idea.

### Minor buy-side liquidity / where short stops sit (short-term high as the draw)  (aliases ICT uses: minor buy side, short-term high, buy stop above an old high)
- **Kind:** definition / reasoning
- **What ICT says:**
  - A short seller protects the position with a buy stop above a high. After a big energetic sell-off that makes a new lower low, shorts trail their stops down to just above the most recent short-term high; that clustered buy stop is the minor BSL.
  - Traders that kept stops above the relative equal highs (thinking the short-term high was "too close, give it some room") leave the higher pool intact, which is why there are two levels to consider.
  - He says he needs no heat map, book map or "other maps": he knows where liquidity pools because he knows what traders do when they are short.
- **How to identify:** Short-term high just before a sharp sell-off leg (energetic drop from it).
- **How to use:** Treat as an objective for a long from discount; the short-term high is easier to find than an inefficiency (look at how energetic the drop from it was).
- **Example in this video:** NQ: the high at which the sell-off began (lower of the two relative equal highs) = minor BSL; price ran into it after 8:30 and rejected.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:45:47](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2747) — why a short-term high attracts price (shorts trail buy stops there)
  - [0:46:33](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2793) — no heat map needed
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Fair Value Gap as a "two SIBIs plus a volume imbalance" array; how to draw it and test it  (aliases ICT uses: SIBI, sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency, volume imbalance, inefficiency, PD array, "split")
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A candlestick low, then a sell-off creating a volume imbalance, then a continuing sell-side imbalance (SIBI), is another PD array: two SIBIs connected by a volume imbalance. He explicitly calls it "another PD array".
  - Draw it from top to bottom to include the volume imbalance ("encapsulate the entire thing") to get the full range and precision. Drawing the two parts separately is "not technically wrong", but you lose the precision of the full range.
  - Split it in half (classic mid-range / consequent encroachment) and watch how price trades into it.
  - Tests when price rallies into an overhead gap (before 9:30): does it treat it defensively (reject at the low, "sensitivity")? Or does it trade into the upper half, go completely through without coming back to the midpoint, and treat it as an inversion (he would change the colour to orange)?
  - Upper-quadrant rule: if the array sits in the OTE/upper quadrant of the range, you need to see price trade into it and then "stay outside the lower half" (bodies); only then expect a run higher. If it only touches the low of the array and sells off, that shows "premium sensitivity" and weakness.
  - Gap respect rule for an overhead gap price has traded down through (post-9:30): wicks to the halfway point are acceptable; bodies in the lower half or below are best; bodies laid in the upper half make it much less likely price reaches for the lower liquidity before a deeper retracement.
- **How to identify:** Candle low, one sell-off candle that leaves a volume imbalance, then another down-closed candle leaving a SIBI; combine them into one box.
- **How to use:** Use as a target (final exit at the low of the array), or as a resistance/support zone watched for how it is treated (respect vs inversion).
- **Example in this video:** NQ after 8:30: price ran up and touched exactly the low of the array and sold off immediately, "giving up the ghost", so the array was not explored. After 9:30, a different overhead gap (the "blue box") was traded down through; he wanted bodies to stay in its lower half.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:09:45](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=585) — two SIBIs with a volume imbalance explained
  - [0:10:33](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=633) — "you have to encapsulate the entire thing"
  - [0:39:28](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2368) — in the upper quadrant it must trade up and stay outside the lower half
  - [1:13:27](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=4407) — respect rule for the overhead blue box (upper half vs lower half bodies)

### Inversion FVG as part of a bullish breaker (A+ pre-news setup) and the FVG-qualification wick comment  (aliases ICT uses: inversion fair value gap, iFVG, breaker, "fulcrum point", discount array)
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He labels the pre-8:30 pattern an A+ setup. Ingredients: (1) liquidity left in the market (lows made jagged); (2) price comes down ahead of news and takes that liquidity; (3) it does not reach a lower eighth level (a blue line carried from yesterday's RTH opening range gap) - if it were weak it should have come down to it, but it did not; (4) it rallies back above the lowest portion of a bullish breaker; (5) it comes back down into an inversion FVG that is part of that breaker and holds there; (6) it then rallies to the minor BSL and to the low of the inefficiency above.
  - The lows create the liquidity pool and the "fulcrum point"; drive down, take that liquidity, then the inversion FVG forms.
  - Body behaviour: price may wick through the inversion gap, "not a big deal"; what matters is where bodies stop. Bodies holding there show the market is being held there, not that buyers and sellers changed; "the algorithm is going to keep offering higher prices whether there's buyers or sellers".
  - The iFVG sat "laser at top of an eighth" on the RTH opening range gap lines.
  - Qualification comment (caption confusing, treat with caution): he says the gap area "is not an inefficiency" in the plain sense because a later green candle's wick clears the previous candle's high; if that wick had instead stayed inside the lowest candle's low (26,161.25) he would have treated that area as "a different type of PD array", a discount array, which he declined to teach, adding that he has never taught all of them and never will ("82 of them", and he never promised that).
  - That run from the iFVG to the minor BSL (as a partial) and to the inefficiency's low "is a model in and of itself".
- **How to identify:** Sweep of lows (sell-side taken) into a bullish breaker whose lowest portion is reclaimed; a bearish FVG inside the breaker that price trades up through (inverted) and then retests with bodies holding; also confirm it sits on a reference level (eighth line).
- **How to use:** Buy the retest of the iFVG; first target the short-term high (minor BSL), final target the low of the inefficiency above (two-stage exit). Stop beyond the sweep / below the array (he does not state an exact stop here).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m (approx.): low of the lowest down-closed candle 26,161.25; price takes lows pre-8:30 and fails to reach a blue eighth line below; rallies above the bullish breaker low; returns into the iFVG, holds, then runs to the short-term high at 8:30 news and the low of the inefficiency.

### Today's RTH opening range gap behaviour (9:30 open, partial fill, abandon rule)  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap, gap closure, discount gap opening, opening price, "magnet", first presented fair value gap)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The gap is from yesterday's RTH settlement (close of the regular session candle) to today's 9:30 open. Here it was a discount gap opening (open below settlement).
  - Price rallied about three quarters of the way up the gap (he says "almost like a 3/4 of a closure") and was rejected, then ran lower.
  - Rule: once price breaks outside the gap past the opening price and starts to run, respect that it is probably going to leave the open. It does not need 15 or 30 minutes to be clear. After a partial fill and a rip lower, anticipate deeper discounts; look at the old regular trading hours price action for references, not only the electronic trading hours chart.
  - Abandon rule (stated live): if price slices through the lower gap level on a closing basis, abandon the idea of gap closure for the morning session; it "would remain heavy".
  - The opening price is very sensitive, like the midnight New York opening price: "many times a magnet", and it also helps with daily directional bias. At 9:30 he saw the open, a rally above (Judas), a break down that hit the open and then lower.
  - First presented FVG after 9:30: he chose the gap formed when price displaced into the previous-session (RTH) inefficiency over a small earlier gap, because the small one would be viewed as a "breakaway" gap (caption: "order or break away coming gap", unclear) and "doesn't have that much of a range". He expects price to gravitate back to that first-presented FVG eventually before the week closes: they act as magnets "like new week opening gaps, new day opening gaps".
  - If price goes right through the downside with no interest, he would skip the morning and wait until about 1:30 PM, check for smooth sell-side/buy-side left intact and use it for the PM session.
  - In the 1990s he taught students on day 1 to mark settlement, opening price, range, half, three quarters, lower quadrant, and watch whether the gap closes fully; day 2 to ask where it goes next if not; day 3 to add an entry mechanism (an order block, selling short on a stop, buying on a stop).

### Market Maker Sell Model: retracement to 3/4 of the range without returning to the consolidation
- **Kind:** refinement (brief mention)

### Seasonal participation: sitting out January, starting the first Tuesday of February  (aliases ICT uses: first month of the year, sentiment build-up)
- **Kind:** rule / personal process (seasonal tendency)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He does not actively trade the first month of the year. He starts the first Tuesday of February (a self-imposed parameter: not a Monday, a Tuesday, in February). He has done this for about three decades.
  - Reason: over the holidays traders build opinions and sentiment; at the start of a new year they have the most energy and are impatient and greedy, putting down heavy bets. Market makers know there is a shift in sentiment and big bets are coming.
  - Observation: January more often fails to deliver efficiently; it is "sloppy, choppy, indecisive, weird, wild opening range gaps" far from the prior settlement. Some Januaries in recent years delivered well, but that has never made him change what he does.
  - This year adds geopolitics, making lower-timeframe precision harder.
- **How to use:** Be a light/no-risk participant in January; observe and study rather than size up.

### Low-resistance delivery vs "fuzzy" delivery  (aliases ICT uses: low resistance liquidity runs, porous wake, fuzzy, intervention)
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:**
  - He wants low-resistance liquidity runs where price leaves pools of liquidity and inefficiencies in its wake ("a porous wake").

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Define the range first: pre-session (7:00 to 9:30 ET) low and high; use the smaller/lower reference high; you do not need price to leave the range.
- Know the draw before entries, stops or targets; with the draw you frame risk.
- Cookie-cutter run types: inefficiency to liquidity, liquidity to inefficiency, liquidity to liquidity (false breakout above old high to false breakout below old low).
- After a jagged leg, look for the smooth side (relative equal highs/lows).
- Upper-quadrant inefficiency: price must trade into it and keep bodies out of the lower half before a higher run is expected; if it only taps the low and sells, treat as weakness.
- Overhead gap after a downside run: wicks to midpoint OK, bodies in lower half or below preferred; bodies in the upper half reduce the odds of a run to lower liquidity before a deeper retracement.
- Eighths (.125 increments) of the previous RTH opening range gap stay relevant as levels after the gap closes (examples 26,161.25, 26,144.25, 26,122.50).
- 8:30 ET is always an algorithmic time (news or not); 9:30 likewise; last hour 3:00 to 4:00 has a flurry.
- Opening gap: once price breaks outside the gap and runs it probably leaves the open; abandon gap-closure if price slices through on a closing basis.
- If the morning gives no interest, wait until about 1:30 PM for the PM session and look for smooth sell-side/buy-side left intact.
- Review at about noon: go back over 7:00 to 9:30 and 9:30 to noon and log how price reached for inefficiency/liquidity, how it expanded the daily range, which side it worked toward and how fast it gave up.
- Sit out January; start the first Tuesday of February.
- Trade micros rather than minis in current volatility; he says one micro is enough to learn everything.
- OTE is the upper quadrant (about three quarters) of the dealing range in this teaching.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Base hits, not grand slams: "base hits make millionaires" (0:32:22). Buying the iFVG and exiting at the short-term high is a base hit.
- Do not start by trading demo, prop or real money; start by watching price and doing the same daily exercise for weeks to a couple of months; wanting instant gratification is the "Western mindset" he warns about (0:43:24).
- Trade micros: three micros with a two-stage exit; "one micro will teach you everything", and he claims one micro can produce a surgeon's salary a year (0:50:33). Not trading a mini because it "is not worth your time" is doing it wrong.
- Learn one video a day (two on a weekend) and work through the playlists in about a year (0:33:56).
- Be teachable: lower expectations, defer gratification, accept being wrong in the beginning (1:23:39).
- Self-talk: avoid negative self-talk (the average person has 300 negative thoughts a day); find something positive in every day even if it went against expectations and record it in the journal (1:29:58).
- Responsibility: do not blame ICT's system when you switch PD arrays and miss the one you left; the operator is the problem (1:41:53).
- Process for beginners: show up every day, do the same thing every day; do not pick entries yet, pick the direction; then study the PD arrays in hindsight (1:26:00).
- The first thing to fix is how you think; patience; not caring about being right in the beginning frees you to learn.
- Journal the review of the morning session (noon) and the afternoon.
- Avoid assuming markets are always efficient: January tends to be messy; do not bet the heaviest hand in fuzzy conditions.

## Glossary candidates
`JAGGED` — a leg with successive lower lows (or higher highs) and no relative equal lows/highs, leaving nothing smooth to draw to.
`SMOOTH` — a side with relative equal highs/lows; where price tends to gravitate after a jagged run.
`COOKIE CUTTER` — ICT's term for a very simple repeatable small run (inefficiency to liquidity or the reverse).
`FUZZY` — price delivery that overlaps previous candles/ranges (intervention) rather than leaving a clean wake.
`SIGNATURE` — a repeating price behaviour (run to liquidity or inefficiency), as opposed to a "pattern".
`LOW-HANGING FRUIT` — the smallest, easiest draw in a defined range (short-term high or nearby inefficiency).
`DEALING RANGE (CANVAS)` — the two reference points (high and low) within which price is expected to trade for the idea.
`FULCRUM POINT` — the lows that form the liquidity pool from which the drive down and iFVG formed (caption: "fulcrum point right here").
`DISCOUNT GAP OPENING` — a 9:30 open below the previous RTH settlement.
`FIRST PRESENTED FVG` — the first fair value gap after the 9:30 open that forms with displacement; treated as a magnet for the week.
`EIGHTHS` — .125 increments of an opening range gap, carried forward as levels.
