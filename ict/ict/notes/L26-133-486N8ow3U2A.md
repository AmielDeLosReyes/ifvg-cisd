## Concepts

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** definition · rule
- **What ICT says:** A consolidation pattern with volume bounce at both the high and the low. On the daily chart shown (NQ March 2026), this created a "positive balance outside efficiency" which made it a suspension block. The upper and lower edges are the extremes where volume was transacted; the interior is the range of equilibrium.
- **How to identify:** Two clear volume bounces (one at the top, one at the bottom) with balanced or positive volume profile. Must see volume confirmation at both extremes.
- **How to use:** The midpoint of the suspension block is the fulcrum. If price trades above it and closes above it in the upper half, it becomes bullish. If price whips into the upper half but fails to close above it, that turns the suspension block into a bearish inversion fair value gap (iFVG). Price action inside the range is extremely difficult; traders new to this should wait for cleaner market conditions (post-spring, after early-year manipulation).
- **Example in this video:** NQ March daily chart: suspension block formed with lows around 18,390 area and highs in upper range; the midpoint served as the trigger level. Price went up, only whipped into the upper half with a wick, then reversed—turning bullish into bearish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=53) (suspension block defined) · [0:02:29](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=149) (testing relative equal highs) · [0:06:30](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=390) (suspension block as fulcrum, upper half wick action) · [0:04:04](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=244) (advice to wait until spring if inexperienced in ranges)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The suspension block midpoint can act as a reversal trigger *before* price reaches it. Merely touching the upper half without closing above it creates a bearish bias. Not every consolidation with volume at extremes is a suspension block; you need the "positive balance outside efficiency" structure.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE)
- **Kind:** definition · refinement
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint of the suspension block, used as the key trigger or fulcrum level. "As long as we remain below that we were going to be bearish." If unable to trade above it and establish above it, the bias stays bearish.
- **How to identify:** Calculate the midpoint of the suspension block (high + low) / 2. This is the consequent encroachment level.
- **How to use:** Use it as a pivot level: if price closes above it and stays above, switch bias to bullish. If price fails to close above it or only wicks into the upper region, the bearish bias remains. It's a decision point, not an entry point itself.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March: the daily suspension block midpoint was approximately in the zone of the previous day's close. Price rallied up to it on 1-minute chart, hesitated, and reversed—exactly what ICT was watching for to confirm bearish bias continuation.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=53) (setup) · [0:04:50](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=290) (trigger point on 1-minute, rally up to CE of daily suspension block) · [0:06:30](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=390) (inversion FVG at the midpoint showing bearish turn)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** CE is specific to the suspension block's midpoint, not a generic midpoint level. It's a qualifier and confirmation tool, not a selection or timing tool by itself.

### Gradient Levels / Quadrant Levels / Eighths
- **Kind:** refinement · entry model
- **What ICT says:** Fibonacci subdivisions (eighths, sixteenths) applied to create more granular price levels for entries and draws on liquidity. "If you put these levels in there, you'll get levels that would be pertinent for more highly specific quadrant levels or gradient levels." Originally used "gradients" terminology to avoid confusion when discussing quadrants and eighths.
- **How to identify:** Apply Fibonacci tools set to display eighths and sixteenths in addition to the standard quarters (or quadrants). These subdivisions create "gradient" levels within the larger framework.
- **How to use:** Use gradient levels to fine-tune entries and to identify micro draws on liquidity within larger structures. Discount and premium wicks can be graded (subdivided) using these levels to show where specific inefficiencies occur.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily chart: the discount wick was graded into gradient levels; on the 1-minute, price traded down into these gradient levels, identifying precise support/resistance zones. The "lower of the two" discount wicks' gradient subdivisions acted as an intermediate-term draw on liquidity.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:41](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=101) (introduction to fib settings for eighths and quadrants) · [0:05:39](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=339) (two wicks graded with gradients, lower wick as DOL)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Gradient/quadrant/eighths terminology can be confusing; the principle is the same—subdivision of price ranges into smaller imbalances to identify precise liquidity.

### Discount Wick & Premium Wick
- **Kind:** definition · entry model
- **What ICT says:** Wicks are gaps, just like full gaps. They can be graded (subdivided) using fibonacci levels. Discount wicks form when price trades down to a lower level and leaves a gap on the way back up; premium wicks form when price trades up and leaves a gap on the way back down.
- **How to identify:** Look for wicks on daily charts; they appear as small gaps between the close and the next open or between candles. These can be graded and subdivided for precision.
- **How to use:** Graded discount/premium wicks become draws on liquidity. Price will often gravitate back to these levels. On the 1-minute chart, price can then target the gradient levels within those wicks.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily: there were two discount wicks graded. The lower of the two became the intermediate-term draw. On the 1-minute, price traded down into the gradient levels of this lower discount wick (the red line ~18,225 area) after breaking down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:41](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=101) (wicks as gaps, not new concept) · [0:02:29](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=149) (discount wick on daily chart) · [0:05:39](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=339) (two graded discount wicks) · [0:08:55](https://youtu.ie/486N8ow3U2A?t=535) (low of daily discount wick as intermediate-term DOL)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not every wick is tradeable; context (daily bias, market structure) matters. The grading (gradient subdivision) is what makes precision entry/exit points.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)
- **Kind:** definition · rule
- **What ICT says:** The gap formed at the opening of the new week (Sunday evening into Monday morning in electronic trading). Treated as a liquidity pool. When price breaks below it and then returns to its halfway point or full range, this becomes a draw on liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Find the Sunday open (first tick of the week) and Monday's first candle; the gap between them is the NWOG. Identify the high and low of this gap, and the midpoint.
- **How to use:** NWOG acts as a liquidity pool. Once broken, traders hunt for equilibrium (halfway point) or full closure. Use it as a target after initial moves away from it. It's a reference structure for intraday traders.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-minute chart: market opened (RTH 9:30) above the NWOG, then gravitated down to the gap's low, broke below it, traded to the low, found premium sensitivity, and broke further into the halfway point of the lower discount wick on the daily chart.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:04](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=244) (trading hours opening range gap, specific to ICT's framework, not a breakout strategy) · [0:08:07](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=487) (NWOG as liquidity reference, "just like a dog whistle") · [0:10:30](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=630) (new week opening gap in the larger price sequence, next area of interest when trading down from higher levels)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT's NWOG framework is distinct from "opening range breakout" strategies—he's not chasing breakouts. He's identifying NWOG as a liquidity structure to run through or draw back to, depending on larger bias.

### SMT Divergence
- **Kind:** definition · qualifier
- **What ICT says:** A comparison of relative price highs between NQ and ES at the same time of day. If NQ makes new highs but ES does not (or vice versa), there's a divergence. "It is not a selection tool. It is not a timing tool." It's a qualifier—a warning that momentum is not synchronized across the indices.
- **How to identify:** Plot the highs (or cumulative high prices) of both NQ and ES on the same timeframe for the same session. Draw a line showing cumulative highs. If one index rises while the other flatlines or falls, you have divergence.
- **How to use:** Divergence indicates weakness or lack of follow-through. If NQ rallies into inefficiencies and ES diverges, shorts can be taken because the move lacks institutional conviction across the complex.
- **Example in this video:** ES chart shown plotting only the high price (cumulative) against NQ's highs. NQ rallied and made inefficiency highs, but ES failed to keep pace—divergence confirmed weakness. When SMT broke the divergence and moved lower, shorts could be taken as the breakdown accelerated and took out SSL.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:19](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=439) (SMT divergence setup, comparing NQ highs with ES highs) · [0:08:07](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=487) (divergence breaks aggressively lower, shorts taken)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** SMT divergence is a confirmation and risk qualifier, not a standalone trade signal. It works best when you already have a bias and are looking for confirmation of reversal.

### Opening Range Gap Theory vs. Opening Range Breakout
- **Kind:** rule · refinement
- **What ICT says:** ICT does NOT use opening range breakout strategy. His approach is specifically about the opening range *gap* structure and drawing on liquidity within it. "I don't use a opening range breakout strategy. You know, that's all chasing price. I'm not interested in that. I want to know what I'm looking for and then key off of a very specific price level and I'm trading against the direction of where the market's going to go."
- **How to identify:** Mark the RTH (regular trading hours) open time (9:30 ET) and the previous day's close or electronic session settlement. The gap and the range created by these constitute the opening range.
- **How to use:** Instead of chasing a breakout above or below, use the opening range's midpoint, edges, and draws on liquidity as targets. If bearish, scale into shorts on rallies into inefficiencies within or near the opening range, not on breakouts.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opened gapped up from the previous close, but instead of chasing the gap higher, ICT showed how to use the gap's internal structure (halfway point, full range) as targets on down moves. The market opened at the gap high, traded to the midpoint, then broke out of the range low—all of this price action was driven by the gap structure, not by breakout chasing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:04](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=244) (explicit statement: "my trading hours opening range gap theory, which is not what everybody else talks about... I don't use a opening range breakout strategy")
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a critical distinction. Breakout chasers buy highs and sell lows; ICT traders use the gap structure to predict reversals and trade against the initial direction.

### Tape Reading & Inefficiencies
- **Kind:** rule · entry model
- **What ICT says:** "I want to sell short when the market's going up into some kind of inefficiency, some kind of a premium array or take out an old high." Reading the tape means watching for resistance (old highs, premium zones) being taken out, then shorting as the market pushes into these inefficiencies.
- **How to identify:** Identify old highs, premium areas (previous resistance), and inefficiencies (gaps, imbalances). Watch price rally into these zones.
- **How to use:** On rallies into inefficiencies or old highs, place shorts. Combine this with time-of-day context (pre-market, early RTH) for timing. The tape shows where resistance exists; price moving through it is the trigger.
- **Example in this video:** NQ rallied up to take out the previous Friday's high and old resistance levels on the 1-minute chart—these were inefficiencies. Shorts could be taken as price moved through these zones, with targets below at draws on liquidity.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:19](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=679) (tape reading definition and entry approach)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Tape reading requires experience. New traders should practice by observing price action and marking where entries *would have been* before risking real capital.

### Market Difficulty & Volatility During Geopolitical Periods
- **Kind:** psychology · risk management
- **What ICT says:** Early-year trading is extremely difficult due to geopolitical manipulation and metals market volatility. If you're brand new and not experienced in range trading, take 2-3 months off and come back in spring (end of March into April) when price action is cleaner. "The markets are stuck in these large ranges. Unless you know how to trade inside these ranges, it's going to be very problematic for you." Current high volatility means "everyone's methodology" has been questioned; it's not the trader or the method that's failing—it's the environment.
- **How to identify:** Watch for wide ranges, choppy price action, and inability to establish new daily trends. Geopolitical news and metals market moves (FX, commodities) drive this.
- **How to use:** New traders should reduce or pause activity. Experienced traders should reduce position size (micros instead of minis, 1-3 contracts instead of 30) and only take high-confidence setups. Avoid revenge trading and over-sizing.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Suspension block midpoint is a binary trigger:** if price closes above it and establishes above, switch to bullish bias. If unable to close above it, remain bearish.
- **Discount wicks are targets:** once a lower discount wick is identified on the daily, it becomes the intermediate-term draw on liquidity.
- **New week opening gap is a liquidity pool:** once broken, expect price to return to its halfway point or full range closure before trending further.
- **SMT divergence requires institutional follow-through:** if one index (NQ or ES) fails to confirm the other's highs, assume weakness and prepare for reversal.
- **Opening range gap theory:** use the gap's structure (high, low, midpoint) as target zones, not as a breakout strategy.
- **Time-of-day context matters:** pre-market, RTH open (9:30 ET), and session transitions affect how price moves relative to these structures.
- **Position sizing in high-volatility periods:** use micros (1-3 contracts) instead of minis (10+) to survive incorrect executions and preserve capital during uncertain environments.
- **Tape reading entry:** sell short when price rallies into inefficiencies, old highs, or premium zones—do not chase breakouts.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Accepting difficult market conditions:** New traders should accept that early-year (Jan–Mar) trading is difficult and either wait until April or reduce activity. Blaming yourself or your method during choppy, manipulated markets is a mistake.
- **Position sizing as risk management:** Reduce size during high volatility, not to be "safe," but to allow room for error. "Live to weather an incorrect execution." Gamblers get "their head cleanly removed from their shoulders" in these conditions; professionals reduce and wait.
- **Patience with structure:** Don't force trades. If a setup doesn't deliver (e.g., price doesn't reach an expected target), acknowledge the misread and move on. Revenge trading in range-bound markets is a capital killer.
- **Tape reading as experience:** The more you watch and practice marking inefficiencies before trading them, the faster you develop the skill to enter correctly on rallies into resistance.
- **Demonstrating influence responsibly:** ICT is intentionally using micro contracts and smaller position sizes on his broadcast to show viewers that big size is not necessary and can be dangerous in uncertain markets. This is an ethical stance.

## Glossary candidates

`Suspension block` — A consolidation pattern with volume bounces at both the high and low, forming a balanced-or-positive-volume range. The midpoint (consequent encroachment) is the fulcrum for bullish/bearish bias.

`Consequent encroachment (CE)` — The midpoint of a suspension block, used as a trigger level to determine if bullish (close above) or bearish (fail to break above) bias will continue.

`Gradient level` — A fibonacci subdivision (eighths, sixteenths) of a larger price range, used to identify precise support/resistance and micro draws on liquidity.

`Discount wick` — A gap formed when price trades down and leaves a gap on the way back up. Wicks can be graded into gradient levels to identify inefficiencies.

`New week opening gap (NWOG)` — The gap between the previous week's close and the new week's open (Sunday into Monday electronic trading). Treated as a liquidity pool and draw on liquidity.

`SMT divergence` — Lack of synchronized movement between indices (NQ vs. ES), indicating weakness and potential reversal. A qualifier, not a timing or selection tool.

`Inefficiency` — A zone where price has been taken out (old high, premium area) without a corresponding candle body; typically a wick. A trade location when price rallies or drops into it.

`Tape reading` — Observing and marking where resistance/support exists (old highs, premium zones, inefficiencies) and entering trades as price moves through these zones.

`Opening range gap theory` — ICT's framework for using the daily opening range's internal structure (high, low, midpoint) as target zones for drawing on liquidity, distinct from opening range breakout strategies.
