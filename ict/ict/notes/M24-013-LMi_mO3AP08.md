## Summary
ICT trades the first hour of Tuesday Aug 20, 2024 live on NQ (prices in the high 19,800s), narrating it as a practice framework for his son Caleb. The model on display is "gaps as magnets": a new day opening gap (NDOG), new week opening gap (NWOG) and opening range gap (ORG) are the draws, a daily volume imbalance from the Aug 13 stream is the higher-timeframe target, and he measures whether price "wants" the next quadrant or consequent encroachment of each PD array. He teaches how far a wick may overshoot a fair value gap (use the consequent encroachment of the wick on the lower timeframe), shows a 15-second FVG nested inside a 1-minute FVG, and an institutional order entry drill off the NDOG. The biggest transferable rules are to take a partial at every broken short-term high (before touching the stop), to treat the opening range gap midpoint as a very high-probability draw, and to define success by a framed move (e.g. 15-20 handles), not by the target being hit. He ends with how a trader graduates from a one-bias framework to trading both ways by time of day.

## Concepts

### New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)  (aliases: new day opening gap, "the gap carried forward")
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NDOG = the difference between the 5:00 PM ET settlement and the 6:00 PM ET reopen (one-hour pause in trading). It is labeled for the next trading day (Aug 19 settle to 6 PM open is "the 20th"), and you carry it forward into the day you are watching.
  - Once price trades through it, do not discard it. He says the common error is to disregard a gap as soon as it fills ("throwing the baby out with the bathwater"); the algorithm refers back to the high, low and quadrant levels again and again whenever price trades through and back to them.
  - Bodies of candles tell you the gap is respected; wicks are allowed to do the damage.
  - Caleb's model: the NDOG or NWOG is the draw. Lookback he gives for Caleb: NDOGs up to about 5 days back, NWOGs up to about 5 weeks back. He adds that older NWOGs do not get stale for sentiment and can still produce reactions.
- **How to identify:** compare the 5:00 PM settlement candle with the 6:00 PM open on a one-minute chart, mark the high and low, optionally add quadrants.
- **How to use:** (bullish case) look for relative equal highs just below or at the NDOG/NWOG so there is liquidity to run on the way to the magnet; (bearish case) relative equal lows just above the NDOG/NWOG. Treat the gap as support after price goes through it and returns (like an old FVG).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 20 open. Price opens below the NDOG; ICT watches the NDOG high as the draw. A small 15-second gap forms, the wick stops it and price rallies into the NDOG. He highlights a 15-second BISI overlapped with the NDOG as "a very very easy bread and butter setup." Later price drops back to the NDOG low, the candle bodies hold it, and price rallies (see the entry drill concept).
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:49](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=49) opens below the NDOG, watching it as a draw · [0:05:48](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=348) "that is my son's model," the draw is the NDOG/NWOG · [0:06:34](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=394) REH/REL placement and 5-day / 5-week lookback · [0:09:53](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=593) how the NDOG is built and labeled · [0:10:43](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=643) don't discard it once filled
- **Nuance / caveats:** the opening range and the NDOG are different things; he reminds viewers of that when he mixes terms. "Five days / five weeks" is the criteria he gives Caleb, not a hard rule for everyone.

### Opening Range Gap (RTH opening gap) and its midpoint  (aliases: opening range gap, ORG, "the gap from yesterday's settlement to today's 9:30")
- **Kind:** definition + rule (with a high-probability tendency)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening range gap is the difference between the previous session's settlement (the RTH close candle at 4:15 PM ET per his description) and the 9:30 AM open. Use the candle close to open as the edges, break it into quadrants (he stresses this is not "quarters theory"), keep it on the chart and extend it through the day.
  - Price will use the high, low and quadrants repeatedly whenever it trades through it and back.
  - Gap lower is a lure to get traders short; you then expect a gap fill. At the very minimum, expect price to go to the 50% (midpoint, consequent encroachment) of the gap; he calls this "one of the strongest things in trading." Use a Fibonacci to mark the midpoint.
  - The gap does not always close completely: it can go halfway and then rocket in the direction of the gap. If it will go lower it goes halfway (or a little more) and fails to close, then drops.
  - You do not even need to be right on bias for this retracement trade.
  - He also uses the "opening range" as 9:30 to 10:00 (30 minutes) for the beginning of the session; later he corrects himself for using "opening range" for the 9:31 moment.
- **How to use:** if the open gaps lower, drop the fib on the first tick; if there is range between current price and the gap midpoint, he will go long right there (he says he has done it many times) and target the midpoint. If bullish with a higher-timeframe target, expect price to go above the gap and then use the gap as support.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 20. Price gapped lower from Monday's settlement, took the ORG, went above it, came back and used it as support. A consequent encroachment of about "834" (caption; likely 19,834) is cited as the midpoint; the ORG high is around 19,850.25 (he places it "on the close of that candle").
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:14](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1034) ORG on the 1m chart · [0:37:21](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2241) ORG high at 19,850.25, extend through the day · [0:38:09](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2289) gap lower, mark 50%, no bias needed · [0:49:29](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2969) go long on the first tick if there is range to the midpoint
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says people disregard gaps because "everybody knows about gaps," which is why the setup is ignored; the half-fill is the minimum expectation, not a guarantee.

### Consequent Encroachment, Mean Threshold and quadrants  (aliases: CE, MT, quadrants, "lower/upper quadrant")
- **Kind:** definition (clarification)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Consequent encroachment (CE) = the midpoint of a gap: FVG (up- or down-close), BISI/SIBI, volume imbalance, opening gap, even a wick treated as a gap.
  - Mean threshold (MT) is different: it is the equilibrium or midpoint of an order block.
  - Wicks are classified like gaps ("I internalize them as the same thing as a gap"), so a wick can be divided into quadrants and its CE used as a limit for how far price may overshoot.
  - Lower quadrant, midpoint (CE) and upper quadrant are the levels he uses to measure what is still unfilled in a gap.
- **How to use:** mark quadrants on every gap you track; measure whether price reaches the next quadrant, how fast, and how it behaves there (stalls or slices through "like a hot knife through butter").
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:45](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=885) CE vs MT, wicks as gaps · [0:15:35](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=935) quadrants, not quarters theory
- **Nuance / caveats:** corrects a common mix-up between MT (order block) and CE (gap).

### Daily volume imbalance as a higher-timeframe draw  (aliases: volume imbalance, "the yellow area", daily SIBI target)
- **Kind:** definition + example (target given on the Aug 13 stream)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Volume imbalance: the space between one candle's close and the next candle's open where the bodies do not overlap and only a wick bridges the two candles. He teaches to look at the wick as a gap and measure between the bodies.
  - Divide it into quadrants; the portion not yet traded is the objective. On the daily chart, price had already traded into the daily SIBI (the blue line is that candle's low at 19,868.25; the pink area is the SIBI); the yellow shaded volume imbalance sits just above and is the remaining target.
  - He wants to see whether price can reach the lower quadrant, then the midpoint (CE), of the yellow area. "Not saying it has to go right there right now; technicals have to support the idea."
  - He says his interest for the rest of the week is whether price trades into that area; it need not reach it today.
- **Example in this video:** Daily NQ, levels flagged Aug 13. In Monday's last hour and London on Aug 20 price rallied into the SIBI up to roughly the upper quadrant of its first part (caption unclear on exactly which quadrant), then reversed down into the NDOG. On Aug 20 price reaches the lower quadrant of the yellow area and then its CE (perfect delivery, he says), pulls back, then makes a short-term high after the target.

### Price Delivery Continuum Theory (reading real order flow from price)  (aliases: "price delivery continuing theory", "my way of reading real order flow", the algorithm)
- **Kind:** new coinage / method
- **What ICT says:**
  - Read order flow from what has actually traded: the open, high, low and close of each interval, not from depth of market, ladders, level-2 or Bookmap. Resting orders can be pulled (spoofing); you cannot spoof the OHLC of a candle. Volume booked is seen with time and sales only after price engages the level.
  - Frame it as a question: does price support the algorithm repricing back to these reference points? Can price reach the next level, how fast, and what does it do on arrival (stop short or go through)? Then did it show willingness to trade to the next CE?
  - Because price is algorithmic it follows "scripts"; contract counts have no bearing. The market is drawn to inefficiency ("a big magnet"), it needs only to touch the low end of an inefficiency first, then you watch whether it aggressively runs to the midpoint.
  - He says he does not use Bookmap-type tools, and that students who understand his method do not need them; he admits it might have helped him early on.
- **How to use:** screenshot your own chart and watch price hit levels; do not rely on his charts.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1364) no DOM or ladders, just price · [0:25:09](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1509) price delivery continuum theory, spoofing · [0:25:58](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1558) lowest quadrant yes, CE yes · [0:29:10](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1750) inefficiency, delivery of price vs contracts
- **Nuance / caveats:** this restates his recurring argument against order-book tools, now under a named theory; the name in captions is "price delivery continuum / continuing theory."

### Fair Value Gap overshoot and nested timeframes  (aliases: coloring outside the lines, "mohawk", wick CE)
- **Kind:** refinement + example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ideal FVG behavior (bullish): price only goes into the upper half of the gap, not the bottom half. If it leaves the gap open it is "extremely strong" and should keep pressing higher. It can still overlap the gap completely and remain valid ("completely permissible").
  - How far can a wick overshoot? Take the highest wick to the left and use its consequent encroachment; that is where the overshoot should stop. On Aug 20, the 1-minute FVG was measured from one candle's high to another candle's low, and the wick ran to the CE of that wick ("perfect delivery").
  - Nested timeframes: on the 15-second chart the candle bodies stay inside the FVG and respect it; only the wick reaches as low as the 1-minute FVG's low / the CE of the wick.
  - "Mohawk" (caption unclear; spelling as heard): a bearish candle that goes just outside an FVG. It looks scary but is permissible price action; knowing it can be measured is what keeps you in the trade.
- **How to use:** measure the allowable overshoot beforehand so a scary bearish candle does not shake you out; keep the lower-timeframe levels alongside the 1-minute levels.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:05](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1265) upper half of an FVG ideal, leaving it open is strong · [0:22:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1364) wick CE perfect delivery · [0:24:20](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1460) 15s bodies respect the 1m FVG · [0:40:33](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2433) "mohawk" and measuring overshoot
- **Nuance / caveats:** this builds on the "how far can it color outside the lines" idea from earlier lectures (he references yesterday's session).

### Partial profits at short-term highs ("running down equity")  (aliases: "run down equity", scaling out, "dollar menu men")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When long in a buy program, every short-term high that gets broken is a candidate to take a partial. He says this is important and to put it in your notes. Do it above the broken short-term high because that is premium-level pricing.
  - Take a partial rather than move the stop. In the early stages of development, tightening the stop to "lock in profit" only increases uncertainty and anxiety, and you end up watching the stop rather than whether price still supports the idea.
  - After a partial is banked, moving the stop to cover cost means "theoretically you don't have any chance of losing money," a huge paradigm shift for a developing trader.
  - The purpose: you do not know if it is going to continue, so distribute; even if your target does not get hit, the trade has paid. If you skip the partial and price turns, you watch a winner become a loser.
  - The initial stop is not meant to remain; he has protocols for moving it. When trading back and forth all day he is more aggressive moving the stop because he will take other trades.
  - He says partials are "a professional mindset," not weakness; claims that you must be right or hit your target are social-media nonsense. What matters is constantly making money.
- **How to use:** (1) define minor targets in advance; (2) at each broken short-term high, take something off; (3) only then think about the stop (cover cost after a profitable partial); (4) do not require the final target.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 20: after reaching the daily volume-imbalance lower quadrant and CE, price made another short-term high; he says if you did not take a partial there you should be taking something off. Price then slipped lower, and he points out the long trader already distributed near the highs and is comfortable if price drops.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1844) partial above a short-term high · [0:32:19](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1939) partial before moving the stop; don't move early · [0:46:18](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2778) distributing at premium levels · [0:52:37](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3157) partials as professional mindset · [0:55:01](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3301) partial, then cover cost
- **Nuance / caveats:** this seems to sit alongside last lecture's "let one contract go" advice (M24-012): partial profits plus a runner. It is aimed at early development; he says discretion on stops loosens with experience.

### Framework and "bias-less" progression  (aliases: graduating, trading without a bias, "liquid" trader)
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:**
  - Beginners need a framework or structure (one bias, a named model with a draw) in order to measure progress; he says Caleb's model is gaps plus the higher-timeframe target.
  - Eventually you trade price by time of day and what it is reaching for, with no fixed bias: buy and sell back and forth for 10-20 handles each way, 100-200 handles in a day, and survive two or three stopped trades, breakevens or commission-only losses.
  - He says this takes a decade or more, not six months or two years; anyone claiming faster is using his vocabulary without having been taught.
  - The market must have enough volatility for that; on aimless days take one trade (or one morning, one afternoon, or a lunch-macro reversal) and be done.
  - Different traders can trade long and short on the same day with different models and both be profitable, because the tools are meant to be made your own.
  - Success is measured by being able to frame a setup from a beginning point to a "terminus" worth 15-20 handles; the draw on liquidity need not be reached. A 15-20 handle frame is, in his definition, a high-probability trade that Caleb may take, but it is a green light, not a guarantee.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- NDOG: 5:00 PM ET settlement vs 6:00 PM ET reopen (labeled for the next day); lookback for Caleb: about 5 days (NDOG), about 5 weeks (NWOG); older ones still useful for sentiment.
- ORG: previous day's settlement (4:15 PM ET RTH candle per his words) vs 9:30 open; use quadrants; mark 50% with a fib; at minimum price tends to retrace to the midpoint; the full gap may not close.
- Opening range = 9:30-10:00 (30 minutes).
- CE = gap midpoint (incl. wicks); MT = order-block midpoint.
- Bullish FVG ideal: price only enters the upper half; staying out is strength; overshoot limit = CE of the highest wick to the left.
- Entry drill fill: candle + one tick.
- At every broken short-term high while long, take a partial; partial first, stop later; after a profitable partial, stop at cost.
- Do not move your stop early in development just to "lock in profit."
- Levels quoted: daily candle low 19,868.25 (blue line); ORG high about 19,850.25; ORG CE "834" (caption).
- Keep levels on a notepad rather than clutter the chart.
- Stream ends 10:30 ET.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Framework for reading price in the first minutes: you do not need to know right away what it will do; he states that "I want to see..." is a framework, not a bias ([0:02:31](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=151), [0:04:08](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=248)).
- Screenshot setups and your own charts; log the reference points while price meanders without you risking anything; repetition builds recognition ([0:47:54](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2874)).
- Relaxed observation, no urgency: "same boring stuff"; no panic or fear when learning ([0:27:34](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1654), [0:47:54](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2874)).
- Notepad method: write levels down and refer back to them; he used graph paper in his early days, dotting the open and recording the highest high and lowest low every 15 minutes with rough times; this made relative equal highs/lows visible and trained him to demand aggressive runs through them ([0:34:49](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2089), [0:36:27](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2187)).
- A profitable exit does not require the target to be hit; that is "a huge paradigm shift" ([0:47:06](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2826)).
- Being right vs making money: prefer constantly making money; criticizes the "must hit target" mentality ([0:54:12](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3252)).
- Your eye will see different opportunities than his; that is fine ([0:48:42](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2922)).
- Weekly plan: next week he moves into holding trades better and more weekly/daily context ([1:01:21](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3681)).

## Glossary candidates
`NDOG` — new day opening gap: 5 PM ET settlement to 6 PM ET reopen.
`ORG` — opening range gap: previous day's settlement (RTH close) to the 9:30 open.
`Quadrants` — splitting a gap into quarters (low, 25%, CE/50%, 75%, high); he says it is not "quarters theory."
`CE` — consequent encroachment: midpoint of a gap or wick.
`MT` — mean threshold: midpoint of an order block.
`Running down equity` — his term for scaling out of a long at each broken short-term high.
`Price delivery continuum theory` — his name for reading real order flow from price delivery to PD arrays instead of DOM.
`Mohawk` (caption unclear) — a bearish candle that spikes just outside an FVG but is still permissible.
`Color outside the lines` — wicks overshooting a PD array without invalidating it.
`Terminus` — end point of a framed move.
`Draw` / `magnet` — the level price is expected to be pulled toward.
