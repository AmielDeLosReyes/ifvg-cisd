# L26-087 — Turning Loss Into Gain - Market Alchemy
ID: d8du4fMvOQU · URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d8du4fMvOQU · Duration: 1:34:34
Content type: live trade (real-time chart, NQ-like prices around 29,2xx–29,3xx; he stresses it is "not market replay") with long mindset/psychology digressions
Teaching density: high   (a full trade narrated candle by candle: stop-out, re-entry, scaling plan, event horizon; plus a lot of psychology; roughly the last 30 minutes are rambling and personal)

## Summary
ICT trades a "high-resistance" morning live: price is chopping between two PD arrays after an 8:30 news-driven sell-off that he reads as manipulation to build a bearish narrative. He fades it, buying the lower half of a bullish inversion FVG with a stop below the signal candle's low, targeting the 8:30 candle's buy-side high (his "Terminus") via a minor buy-side first partial. The first long gets stopped out; he re-enters on the same narrative, using a new inversion FVG, a stop under the CE of a wick, and a "fewer than seven candles" speed expectation as a quality filter. He then manages the position explicitly in layers (14 off at the first buy-side, 10 off, 4 kept, partials on every new high, "event horizon" partial), is eventually stopped on the remainder having booked a mitigated-loss day, and spends the rest of the video on why taking losses calmly, journaling with screenshots, and gaining screen time (not shortcuts) is what turns a loss into a gain.

## Concepts

### 8:30 news run as the mark: fade the news-driven move toward the opposite-side 8:30 liquidity  (aliases ICT uses: "the mark", "8:30 buy side", "economic calendar", "news embargo")
- **Kind:** rule / narrative
- **What ICT says:**
  - The 8:30 news candle was used "to send price lower to build in a bearish narrative"; he is fading that. The high of the 8:30 candle (the buy-side sitting right before the news release) is the target.
  - "You have to identify the economic calendar and the liquidity off of a price run that originates at the 8:30. That's the mark... where the sniper has his crosshairs trained." Everything between is distraction and "wind gusts".
  - Ask "who made money this morning?" (shorts) and "who are they going after?": longs were stopped out, breakout sellers were baited short; he expects both sides to have been delivered before the move up.
  - "I don't have to be leading the pack. I just got to be part of the meal."
  - Why price may be held below: if they take the buy-side above 8:30 they can go short, ride it down and buy back at a low price from sellers, keeping the low intact first.
- **How to identify:** 8:30 (news) candle; a price run that originates from it; the 8:30 high is the resting buy-side pool when the news drove price down.
- **How to use:** go long once the sell-side below has been taken and a bullish PD array holds; target the 8:30 candle high (Terminus), with minor buy-side levels along the way as first partials.
- **Example in this video:** NQ-like chart (levels about 29,2xx–29,3xx). News at 8:30 drove price lower; ICT buys inversion FVGs in the morning chop, targets the minor buy-side highs (the "29,315s" area), then the 8:30 high (around the 29,360 level, caption).
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:43](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=103) — minor buy-side vs the 8:30 high; [0:02:31](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=151) — the 8:30 move was implemented to build a bearish narrative; [0:13:08](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=788) — "the mark"; [1:18:53](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4733) — why they may keep lows intact first.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** minor buy-side is called minor "because it's below the low that will be targeted" (i.e. lower than the real draw). The 8:30 buy-side is the final target ("Terminus").

### Terminus (the final draw on liquidity) with minor buy-side first partials  (aliases ICT uses: "Terminus", "first partial", "stepping stones")
- **Kind:** definition (as used here) / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - "The draw overall is here. So, this is Terminus": the overall target (the 8:30 high). Before it there is a logical first objective (a minor buy-side, an old relative-equal-high pool and SIBI) used for the first partial.
  - "I don't care if it goes up to 30,000 today" — the 8:30 high is the terminus; as a developing trader "look for the market to go to specific locations and be content with enough".
  - Knowing the draw on liquidity makes the stepping stones easy to find; it is not support/resistance.
  - After the stop-out he still thinks the 8:30 high will be reached ("daily range"), but he can't stay with the trade.
- **How to identify:** the 8:30 candle's high on a day where news sent price down; minor buy-side = nearer highs ("smooth highs") below it.
- **How to use:** first partial at the minor buy-side (29,315s area), second at event horizon, final at Terminus.
- **Example in this video:** minor buy-side ~29,312–29,315 first partial (filled ~59:32), next objective ~29,360, final = 8:30 high (not reached).
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:08](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2528) — "this is Terminus", first partial vs overall draw; [1:22:09](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4929) — "I don't care if it goes up to 30,000"; [1:20:32](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4832) — draw on liquidity and handholds.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Terminus" here is a target/destination concept (the title of an ICT model in other videos; not defined separately here).

### Inversion FVG long: lower-half entry, bodies in the upper half, no bodies in the lower half  (aliases: "reclaimed bullish fair value gap", "SIBI that failed its first utilization")
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He uses a sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (SIBI) that did not do its job as resistance as an inversion FVG to go long: "I'll buy in the lower half of that. Just wait for the candlestick to put market price inside that."
  - "First presented utilization": a SIBI is first expected to offer bearish prices when price rises into it. If price goes up into it and it does not hold price down ("these two have failed in their initial role of first utilization"), it becomes an inversion FVG that is bullish. "We want this to offer zero resistance."
  - Bodies should stay in the upper half of the iFVG. Once price leaves the gap upward (a wick back in is fine), no candle bodies may be permitted in its lower half "at all". Candle bodies leave and then "bookings" on a closing basis with range expansion above the high confirm.
  - Missed entry: if price is "reluctant" to drop into the entry zone, that is strength (he skips rather than chasing).
  - Stop loss goes below the signal candle's low: "there's no need for it to trade back down there. If it does, I'm wrong."
  - Re-entry after a stop-out: when price takes out that low he looks for a new inversion FVG ("the good long one") with the same idea.
- **How to identify:** SIBI above a low, price trades up into and through it without being rejected; then retests it from above; confirm with bodies holding the upper half.
- **How to use:** buy lower half on retest, stop below the candle low/wick midpoint, partial at first buy-side, trail the stop below the wick CE or the iFVG.
- **Example in this video:** morning chop, first long in the lower half of an iFVG, stop below the signal candle low; stopped out around 14:42 into the video; second long at ~15:34 after a "bear trap" with a stop beneath the CE of a wick.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:01](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1) — "I'm going to use this as inversion to go long"; [0:05:40](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=340) — no bodies once price leaves; [0:16:22](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=982) — SIBIs failing first utilization; [0:24:40](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1480) — once outside, lower half should see no bodies.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** this is an elaboration of the standard iFVG (first utilization → inverted) with explicit upper/lower-half body rules.

### Consequent Encroachment of a wick and the "mohawk" (stop placement)  (aliases: "consequent cushion/coercion" (caption), "midpoint of this wick", "mohawk")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Stop is placed beneath the midpoint (CE) of the signal wick "allowing for that little Mohawk" (a small poke below the gap then recovery). Then the stop is "brought up just below that low" once price proves itself.
  - A candle that touches the iFVG again is acceptable but "it's better if it didn't"; a mohawk is fine.
  - After a pull-back, bodies should return above the CE of the wick, "at or above, preferably above".
  - Reading: a candle's low "says you can't pass, you can't go below that" — a body below it negates the long.
- **How to use:** stop below the wick CE (or the candle low for the first entry), and move it up to just above the CE of the wick for the balance once partials are done ([1:01:11](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3671)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:34](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=934) — stop under the midpoint of the wick; [0:39:35](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2375) — bodies must return above the wick CE; [1:01:11](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3671) — balance stop just above CE of the wick.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** same wick-CE logic as in the Reaper FVG video L26-077.

### Reading manipulation / chop between two PD arrays ("they're holding it")  (aliases ICT uses: "manual intervention", "porcupine", "Phil" (his joking name for the manipulator), "bear trap", "fake bear flag")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Way too many candles" between two PD arrays means price is being held; "this isn't making sense algorithmically". Wicks on both sides ("porcupine needles") signal manual manipulation rather than smooth higher-timeframe (5- or 15-minute) order-flow delivery through PD arrays.
  - A sloppy stretch between two SIBI/BISI zones becomes a balanced price range (wicks both sides).
  - What looks like a bear flag for retail is a trap: "validated that bear flag for retail traders to be short, they're caught offside". He wants the fake bear flag to act as a real bull flag. His criticism is that the classic patterns are "implemented incorrectly" by many.
  - Do not go short here: baiting; strong rejection and abrupt turn expected.
  - If you are less than a year in a live/funded account, hold fewer contracts (e.g. scale off half) because the tug of war shifts your attention from process to whether you'll be stopped.
- **How to use:** stay with the narrative if bodies keep respecting the levels, and size so that being stopped is tolerable.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:12](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=492) — "this is all manipulation"; [0:13:08](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=788) — "between two PD arrays, this doesn't make any sense"; [0:26:17](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1577) — wicks like a porcupine = manual; [0:15:34](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=934) — bear trap.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Phil" is his joke name for the manipulator.

### Speed filter: fewer than seven candles to the target after the confirming close  (aliases ICT uses: "less than seven candlesticks", "range expansion speed")
- **Kind:** refinement (explicitly "not a requirement")
- **What ICT says:**
  - Count: it took seven candles to travel from the earlier low to the current area; after a close above the CE of the wick (candle one) he wants the run to the buy-side to take fewer than seven candles.
  - Reason: run fast so that "those individuals can't pull their orders" and counterparties can hand longs to buyers at higher prices; speed and magnitude indicate liquidity being swept (while he adds that "they don't go for your stop loss, it's just buying and selling pressure").
  - He insists: "ICT counts candles and he only buys at a specific number" is not what he is saying; it is just what he'd like to see here.
  - Failure to meet the speed ("unwilling to do that less than seven candlestick run") is why he brought the stop up and scaled out.
  - One single displacement candle through the candle highs makes it "all but a done deal".
  - Another timing rule: after the "tiny short-term high", he wants price to start moving "inside the next three candles", because three candles would allow a swing low to form; the swing low then formed.
- **How to use:** if price takes longer than expected, reduce risk or exit, do not hold hoping.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:26](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2186) — fewer candles, sharper speed; [0:38:02](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2282) — "less than seven candlesticks"; [0:43:56](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2636) — candles one, two, three; [0:56:54](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3414) — next three candles.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he warns viewers not to turn it into a rule; treat as a situational quality check.

### Position management: layered partials, stop to wick CE, "take a partial on every new high", event horizon  (aliases: "chasing down equity", "event horizon in action", "midpoint between two pools of liquidity")
- **Kind:** entry model / management
- **What ICT says:**
  - Plan stated in advance ("explained ahead of time") so viewers see what and why. The size implied (14 + 10 + 4) is about 28 contracts (inference). Beginners with a funded account should consider something like 24 micros and scale off half.
  - At ~41:20: take 14 contracts off at the buy-side (first partial), the rest at the next level.
  - When price pulled down too far: take 10 off, set a stop such that if the wick low is taken the 10 more come off, and keep four contracts with a stop just below the CE of the iFVG ("not about being perfectly right about everything").
  - Re-checks the orders ("glad I checked that") so he doesn't cancel the wrong ones.
  - Once cleared to the 29,315s area, first partial filled; balance stop moved just above the CE of the wick.
  - "Every time it takes a new high and I'm in a long, as soon as it takes the high out, I'm going to take a partial off there" (taught a couple of years ago); not caring about the full-size final target is for beginners.
  - Event horizon: draw from the relative equal highs to the high of the 8:30 candle (a fib between two pools); the 50% is the midpoint between two pools of liquidity. Place a limit just below it (allow for spread), take a partial there, and raise the stop beneath the consolidation low, because price should not come back through the midpoint of the consolidation after a stop hunt.
  - Two contracts left at event horizon; no desire to hold through lunch. The last point of reference for sell-side liquidity; trading down there would imply a deeper retracement. He took three partials at the high of the session and another extra one.
  - He ended stopped out on the balance but with the day's drawdown mitigated ("20 2850" / "2800 on a mitigated loss" — caption unclear).
  - After a stop-out when price is far along the curve, he would not re-enter or add.
  - Later he tightens the stop above the wick and takes additional contracts off "if it stops me out" so that the exit still pays, and uses positive self-talk instead of "target or bust".
- **How to identify:** decide in advance (a) the first partial level, (b) the event-horizon level, (c) the stop moves tied to specific candle lows/CEs.
- **How to use:** reduce risk at each pool; plan stop moves as functions of structure; avoid babysitting by taking more off when conditions are high resistance.
- **Example in this video:** NQ-like levels; long off the second iFVG, 14 off at the first buy-side, event horizon between the old relative equal highs and the 8:30 candle high; remaining stopped before the 8:30 high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:20](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2480) — 14 contracts off at the buy side; [0:46:22](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2782) — 10 off, four kept; [0:52:57](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3177) — full contingency plan explained; [1:03:39](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3819) — event horizon drawn; [1:06:48](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4008) — partial on every new high; [1:08:22](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4102) — two left, don't want the market to roll on him.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** compare with the L26-074 and L26-086 event-horizon definitions: here the fib spans from a relative-equal-high pool to the 8:30 high (two buy-side pools).

### High-resistance vs. low-resistance liquidity run conditions
- **Kind:** refinement / context
- **What ICT says:**
  - Beginners want "low resistance liquidity run conditions" ("so easy"); they occur, but days like this (lots of overlap, wicks, many candles) are common: he says "98% of the time" conditions are like this one.
  - In high-resistance conditions the trade needs experience, patience and babysitting: "I try not to participate that much in conditions like this".
  - In low-resistance conditions the stop can be moved aggressively because retracement is not a concern; in high resistance you must be more forgiving with the stop until the first partial is reached.
  - You can make money on good days and give it all back on days like this if you cannot navigate.
- **Timestamps:** [0:51:17](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3077) — the 98% statement; [1:00:24](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3624) — tradeable but requires babysitting; [1:21:20](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4880) — stops more forgiving in high-resistance conditions.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ties to the canonical "high/low resistance liquidity run" concept.

### Journaling by screenshot with execution box, then annotate with live dialogue  (aliases: "priming your subconscious")
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:** take a screenshot with the execution window visible (so it shows the fill, proving it is not painted on); put it in your journal and fill the annotations with the dialogue you had during the trade (if you did not do it live, fill it in as if you did). Reviewing these at week/day end builds "pseudo experience" and primes the subconscious to notice PD arrays like the car-you-just-bought effect or the woman in the red dress. After execution is complete, take a reflective debrief: what did you do that could be improved, how well did price behave with the logic.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Entry: lower half of the iFVG; stop below the signal candle low; second entry stop below the wick's CE.
- Bodies must stay in the upper half of the iFVG; once price leaves, no bodies in the lower half.
- Speed filter: after the first close above the wick CE, want fewer than seven candles to the buy-side; three candles to form a swing low during consolidation.
- Partials: 14 off at first buy-side, 10 off on weakness, four kept; extra partials on each new high; event horizon partial just below the 50% line.
- Prices mentioned (caption, NQ-like): 29,312–29,315 first partial area, ~29,360 next level, the 8:30 candle high as Terminus; "264 level" close-above reference.
- Beginners: under a year, scale off half (e.g. 24 micros example); in the first year do not take another trade after a stop-out.
- Do not hold through lunch if the setup is high resistance and the remaining target is far.
- The 8:30 high is the target; the lunch macro starts at 11:30 (he says "12 minutes or so" before the lunch macro starts at about 11:18 on the video clock).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Don't be afraid of losing trades; you never know which trade will win; "if you can't overcome that fear, you can't be a trader" [0:10:39](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=639).
- After a stop-out, do not throw everything out; if the chart still supports the narrative, re-enter, but don't take 15 trades or overleverage [0:21:21](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1281).
- A loss is just a transaction: "I'm running a business and my business is my business"; first year: no re-entry after a stop-out, break that rule and you tilt [1:16:24](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4584).
- Experience factor: like the haunted house, repeated exposure desensitises; no shortcuts via "five-minute mentors" or Cliff-notes versions of his work [0:28:08](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1688).
- Each new candle: weigh whether it still supports the trade idea; the skill is knowing if you are on side or offside [0:40:23](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2423).
- Give each trade a plan beforehand (what you want to see and shouldn't see); indifference to the result improves management; avoid strangling the position when irritated [1:30:10](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=5410).
- Your edge is the market, not secrecy; individual personality shapes execution; learning from someone does not make you a copy [1:10:00](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4200).
- Learn the draw on liquidity first; stepping stones and be content with enough [1:22:09](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4929).
- Aim for modest, consistent income ("make your grocery bill") rather than lifestyle fantasy [1:13:11](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4391).
- Watching long recordings of price action is required: you can't hold a trade for 90 minutes but not sit through a lecture of equal length [1:29:21](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=5361).

## Glossary candidates
`TERMINUS` — the overall target of the trade (here the 8:30 candle high), as opposed to minor partial levels.
`FIRST PRESENTED UTILIZATION` — a SIBI/BISI's first expected role (resistance/support); failing it makes it an inversion FVG.
`MOHAWK` — small wick poke beyond a level that recovers; stops allow for it.
`HIGH/LOW RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN CONDITIONS` — how clean (fast, few candles) vs. choppy the run to a pool is.
`MINOR BUY-SIDE` — nearby high below the real target used for the first partial.
`EVENT HORIZON` — midpoint (50%) between two liquidity pools; second scale-out zone.
`CHASING DOWN EQUITY` — taking partials to run the account's equity down to a safe level (caption: "running down equity") as the trade works.
`PHIL` — his joke name for the manipulating counterparty ("our proverbial friend").
