## Concepts

### First Hour's Dealing Range (FHDR)  (aliases: "first hour's range", "first hour's dealing range")
- **Kind:** new coinage / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - The algorithm's reference range is the first *hour* of the regular session, 9:30 to 10:30 ET, not the 5-, 15- or 30-minute "opening range breakout" ranges that influencers trade.
  - Mechanics on the chart: mark 9:30 and 10:30 ET, then snap the range box to the highest high and lowest low printed between them.
  - He says he writes the FHDR levels on his notepad the moment 10:30 passes, then watches time and price against them for the rest of the day.
  - He is explicitly not a breakout trader (does not buy broken resistance or sell broken support). He uses the range to judge where manipulation vs. real delivery happens.
  - He offers it as a side study: collect data, see if it adds to what you already do. He does not demand you replace a working model.
- **How to identify:** 9:30–10:30 ET high and low (use Eastern time on the chart regardless of where you live). The first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00) is the opening range and sits inside it. Equilibrium = 50% of the FHDR.
- **How to use:**
  - If price is one-sided from the open (example: gapped lower, never retraced into the gap, made the low at 10:00) the FHDR is one-sided and shorts are the profitable side. That is what entices retail to chase the "breakout", which sets up the shallow stop run and turtle soup below the low (see Turtle Soup entry).
  - The equilibrium of the FHDR is a valid retracement target for the lunch macro (see Lunch macro).
  - Project the range (see Projections entry).
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1-minute, Friday Mar 27, 2026 (video recorded Mar 28). Price gapped below the RTH open (9:30 ET open), never traded into the gap ("gap and go"), made the FHDR low around 10:00, retraced to the FHDR 50% / 10:00-high area in the 10:30–11:30 window, was rejected, broke the low shallowly, and trended lower into the PM session.

### RTH Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG) and its consequent encroachment
- **Kind:** definition / rule of thumb
- **What ICT says:**
  - The gap is between the prior RTH settlement price (the 4:14 PM ET candle close on Thursday, in this example; he calls it "4:14's regular trading hours settlement price") and the 9:30 ET open.
  - The side above is labelled RTH ORG **high**, the side below is RTH ORG **low**. In the example the 9:30 open was below the prior close, so the prior close is the ORG high and the open is the ORG low (sell-side delivery with the opening price gap).
  - Putting a fib across the two points gives 50%, the consequent encroachment of the gap.
  - Rule: in the first 30 minutes (9:31–10:00 ET) always allow for price to try to reach the midpoint of the opening range gap; he states a 70% likelihood.
  - The gap only exists on the RTH chart. On an electronic trading hours (ETH) chart overnight price action fills the space, so "make sure you're looking at the right chart".
- **How to identify:** compare 4:14/4:15 PM ET prior-day RTH close vs 9:30 AM open on a futures chart (use RTH session settings).
- **How to use:** expect an attempt at the gap CE between 9:31–10:00; but he was reluctant to short the retracement from the gap on Friday (holiday-type conditions, TGIF) and wanted more evidence.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ Friday: gap down at 9:30; price did not reach the gap CE ("gap and go"); the first retracement up to the gap he had marked before it happened but did not trade.

### Wick consequent encroachment as a dominant PD array ("bodies tell the narrative, wicks do the damage")  (aliases: wick CE, wicks are the dominant PDA)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A wick that sits next to (just left of) a fair value gap can be the dominant PD array: price can poke a wick outside it but the bodies stay inside, and that defines the boundary. EURUSD example: two consecutive SIBIs plus a wick; bodies stayed inside the wick while wicks poked out.
  - Put a 50% fib across a candle (open to low for a down candle) to get its CE. A body *closing below* that CE (EURUSD, the 23rd candle) is indicative of continuation, i.e. more probable that it goes lower.
  - On the NQ session: when a candle closes below the CE (midpoint) of a specific wick inside the first hour's range, "in many ways a confirmation" that the PM session continues lower. He said live that he would not return for the PM session but the afternoon should be bearish.
  - "Chihuahua on the porch": price breaks out of the range shallowly then runs back inside; if it keeps going it will continue, but if it returns inside it was a false run.
  - Wicks can "color outside the lines" of a bearish FVG as long as the bodies stay in the lower half; he asks students to be forgiving and flexible because markets are not perfect.
- **How to identify:** Mark CE of the wick (50% of the wick candle range). Judge by where *bodies close*, not where wicks poke.
- **How to use:** A close below the wick CE in the first hour is the confirmation for a PM-session continuation. If price instead returns into the range and keeps bodies above, the bias is in doubt.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD daily; MNQ 1-minute Friday first-hour wick CE closing break; wicks outside the FVG but bodies inside the lower half (reclaimed bearish FVG, below).

### Reclaimed Bearish Fair Value Gap  (aliases: reclaimed bearish FVG, inversion FVG, bearish FVG that was reclaimed)
- **Kind:** refinement of iFVG
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price trades up through a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency (a SIBI), then closes back below it, the gap becomes an inversion / "reclaimed" bearish FVG. Any retracement into it afterward is a premium array to sell from.
  - Order flow check: for a bearish FVG the bodies should stay in the lower half of the gap (below CE). Wicks may poke up through the upper half; bodies must not.
  - Sequence he walked: price reaches a PD array, fails to reach even the CE, then drops. A failure to reach CE shows weakness, "if you can't even meet me halfway, why bother".
  - After buy-side is run and bodies close back below the gap, the bodies confirm the run was a stop hunt; then look for the next premium PD array to short.
- **How to identify:** Candle closes through the gap then returns to retest it; bodies' location in the gap halves tells order flow.
- **How to use:** Sell the retracement into the reclaimed gap (or the first gap after the range break, see below). Be flexible with wicks.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ Friday PM session: several runs up into reclaimed bearish FVGs failed (sometimes wicks through, bodies below) and each preceded more downside, sell-side cleared; a wick through the last gap was labelled a stop run.

### Turtle Soup logic (retail breakout flipped)  (aliases: turtle soup, stop run below range low)
- **Kind:** rule / explanation
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the first hour's range has been established and has been one-sided (down from the open), the market makes money for shorts and entices breakout sellers to chase support breaks. The algorithm uses that: a shallow run below the low, then back inside the range.
  - Quote worth keeping: "we're flipping retail logic upside down and attacking them with it."
  - "This is how you find wonderful turtle soup setups."
  - Expect the sweep below to be shallow in this climate ("not likely to break the range"), not an expansion.
- **How to identify:** One-sided first-hour range; a run that takes out the low by a small margin and does not hold; closes back inside.
- **How to use:** Do not chase the breakout; instead trade the reversal or, in a trending day, the continuation after the tested range (see next entry).
- **Example in this video:** Friday MNQ: low broken by a shallow run then back into range; he said live in advance it would just go below a little bit and return.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Ties turtle soup to the FHDR specifically.

### First FVG presented after the FHDR is broken (trending-day entry)  (aliases: first presented fair value gap after first hour's range is broken)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a trending day, after the first hour's dealing range is broken, the first fair value gap presented after that is the one to sell (in a bearish day). Do not expect price to go back up to older gaps.
  - The run just below the low (the "test") is not the break; the real break happens after the test and retrace. After a test and retrace the first gap after blowing through is the trade.
  - If the retracement into that gap cannot reach CE, that is the signature of weakness and the run continues.
  - He also warns against others "teaching" that he never said (e.g., entering the wrong gap).
  - "In a trending day, how do we know it's going to be a trending day?" — by the earlier criterion: a closing basis break below the wick CE inside the first hour's range sets up lower prices in the afternoon.
- **How to identify:** After the FHDR low is taken, find the first FVG formed in the down move.
- **How to use:** Short the retracement into that FVG (bodies below CE). Stops above the gap's high. Trail with the three-candle rule (below).
- **Example in this video:** Friday MNQ: sell-side run through low, first gap after blowing through (with a volume imbalance, close 35 3/4 vs open 35 1/2), retrace could not even reach CE, dropped lower repeatedly.

### FHDR Projection: standard deviation, 75%/25% and octants/quadrants  (aliases: dealing range projection, octants, quadrants)
- **Kind:** refinement / new coinage
- **What ICT says:**
  - Project the first hour's range downward by its own height: drag the fib so the dotted 0 (or 100) line sits on the range low, giving a one-range-down projection ("one full replication or standard deviation of the first hour's dealing range"). Think of it like Asian range projections he taught in 2017.
  - Perfect scenario = full 100% projection; in current climate it is unlikely. So use the 75% level (and 25% if the fib is drawn high-to-low) as the realistic area to take a close or aggressively trail the stop.
  - Add all the octants (fib settings with eight divisions) to the range placed so the 50% level is at the range low: price used the upper octant/quadrant, middle, lower quadrant on the day. Price that runs but does not trade to an octant/quadrant is manipulation.
  - He anchored to the visual low of the range rectangle; levels exist before price gets there, so you can write them down at 10:30.
- **How to identify:** Draw the range 9:30–10:30; extend; set fib levels (including 25/75 and octants).
- **How to use:** Targets/partials on projections; unexplained runs off these levels are manipulation; the levels often agree with liquidity pools he calls out ("watch this level"), and alignment with a liquidity pool strengthens it.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ Friday: after 10:30 the range was projected down; price did not reach the full projection; it respected the upper octant, mid, lower quadrant; the high of one candle rode along a level; bodies stayed on the lower quadrant ("proof").

### Three-Candle Trailing Stop  (aliases: trail every three candles, strangle the unrealized profit)
- **Kind:** rule (trade management)
- **What ICT says:** In poor/difficult market conditions, trail the stop above the highest high of the last three candles on a 1-minute chart (or whichever timeframe you use); each new set of three candles lower, drop the stop to just above the last three candles' high. No swing highs required. If stopped out, walk away. Do not use this on every trade, only when conditions make a reversal likely and targets are not being reached. The partials he takes on up-close candles use the same logic.
- **How to use:** Also helpful to avoid overnight/weekend gap risk (Sunday gap opening can go against you).
- **Example in this video:** Illustrated on the Friday MNQ PM-session drop: stop kept above the last three candles' high as price made lower lows; he says you would be stopped out somewhere in the drop, not at the exact low, but would keep most of the run.
- **Timestamps:** [1:16:55](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4615); [1:17:41](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4661); [1:18:27](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4707) — partials explained

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Charts must be in Eastern time, wherever you live.
- RTH close reference: 4:14 PM ET candle (prior session) vs 9:30 AM ET open for the RTH opening range gap.
- 9:31–10:00 ET: 70% likelihood price attempts the CE of the RTH opening range gap.
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET (30 min). First hour's dealing range = 9:30–10:30 ET.
- Lunch macro: officially 11:30–1:30 ET; early start from 10:30–11:30 ET; target is the high/low after 10:00 and FHDR equilibrium.
- PM session: post 1:00 ET.
- Body above/below CE of a wick: a closing basis break below it in the first hour sets lower prices for the PM session.
- Bearish FVG: bodies in the lower half; bullish: upper half. Wicks may extend.
- Fib: 50% = CE; 75% / 25% for realistic projection exits; octants available.
- Trailing stop: highest high of last three candles; reset each new set of three candles.
- He is not a breakout trader; never chase support/resistance breaks.

## Glossary candidates
`FHDR / First Hour's Dealing Range` — the 9:30–10:30 ET high-low range used as the algorithm's reference for the day.
`RTH ORG high/low` — upper/lower edge of the regular-trading-hours opening range gap (prior 4:14 PM ET settlement vs 9:30 open).
