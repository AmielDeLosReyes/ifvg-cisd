## Concepts

### News drivers: NFP / FOMC / CPI / PPI handling  (aliases ICT uses: "manual intervention", high/medium-impact news driver)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NFP is "not algorithmic": like FOMC (and CPI, PPI) it is a manual intervention, where "they literally pick where they want it to go abruptly". The same logic (run to liquidity / inefficiency) still applies, it is just delivered abruptly and farther outside the normal range.
  - Do not try to position ahead of it (he would have terrible stats; he does not know where they will run it first). Do not trade the release itself. Earliest anyone should trade NFP is about 15 minutes after the 8:30 release (8:45 ET); 20-30 minutes after is better. If already in a trade you can leave a small runner.
  - After the initial shock, look at what is "left behind" (smooth edges / inefficiencies / untouched pools); that is what price goes for next.
  - Why you cannot trade the first minutes: slippage (his example: aiming to buy 18,845 and getting filled 18,862 or worse, which blows up your risk), requotes, or no fill at all. Brokers "pull their engagement" because retail over-leverages funded accounts into these events; this is the illusion of "illiquidity" (liquidity exists, brokers withhold execution). A limit at the opening price of the run +1 tick can fill about 20 minutes after, not in the first ~3 minutes.
  - Use NFP as a clue only: framing the 15-min pools beforehand tells you which pool is more likely to be run (see next concept). He is "not always right"; on CPI/PPI he says his anticipated direction of the initial delivery is right only ~2% of the time (98% wrong) (as stated).
  - Medium/high impact only: he uses Econoday (since the 1990s) and Forex Factory; ignores low-impact; cares about US data, Fed-related events (caption unclear) and bond auctions; speeches matter less. Fundamentals/raw numbers (jobs created or lost) are irrelevant ("the chart's going to tell you everything"); the data is "meant to stimulate" investors into risking money.
- **How to use:** Pre-frame buy-side/sell-side on the 15-min before 8:30, note which is favored, do NOT enter, wait for the shock, then re-read.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Fri Sept 6, 2024. Before 8:30 price had already traded down to the Aug 13 daily FVG low (~18,765, his target; he had covered his short there). NFP then ran up: first to a minor 5-min buy-side level, jammed down taking a short-term low without breaking the main 15-min low, then ripped through the buy side (a single 5m candle; wicking around on the 1m) and into the New Day Opening Gap before 9:30.

### 15-minute "bellwether" liquidity framing  (aliases: primary vs minor buy-side/sell-side, "dealing range" for the day)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The 15-minute chart is the bellwether time frame for where the obvious, clear pools of liquidity the algorithm refers to for that day's range are resting. Frame 15-minute relative equal highs (buy side) and relative equal lows / key lows (sell side) as the primary buy-side and sell-side pools. These give the first parameters for a narrative for the session or daily range.
  - Drop to the 5-minute: levels there are "minor" buy-side/sell-side pools inside the 15-min range. The 5m breaks the primary dealing range into smaller pools.
  - Do this every day, and put the 15-minute pools annotated at the start of 9:30 in every journal entry. No order-book/heatmap tool needed ("I've never been ambiguous about where it is").
  - He told his son Caleb to expect to mark the wrong pools at first and that is fine; do it on your own chart before watching the review.
- **How to identify:** 15m relative equal highs above / relative equal lows below; the pool of the 5m shows as a smaller equal-high/low inside that range.
- **How to use:** After framing, ask: stay inside the range, run just outside the low, just outside the high, or run both sides (and which first)? That frames the morning narrative.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15m on Sept 6: relative equal highs above (buy side) and lows below; the sell-side low had been taken slightly lower than a second adjacent low, which he said "tipped its hand" (see next concept). ### Wicks as PD arrays  (aliases: discount wick / premium wick, wick quadrants, midpoint of a wick)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He treats wicks with quadrants and the halfway point. If price is above a wick, he wants to see it trade down into the upper half of the wick but not below its midpoint (consequent encroachment of the wick). If a leg passes through easily and spends time below it, that was accumulation.
  - Wicks below price are "discount wicks" worth annotating and writing on the notepad as key levels; a wick that price trades below becomes a "premium wick" (resistance from above).
  - A volume imbalance sitting under the wick range is the next draw below the wick's CE.
- **How to use:** Mark discount wicks and their 50% as levels; expect support at the upper half; a break of the midpoint with time spent = accumulation/transition.
- **Example in this video:** Daily NQ: he marked the discount wicks below the Aug 13 FVG and the volume imbalance beneath them as "next draw" levels if price kept going lower; on 1m Thursday, a discount wick became a premium wick once price was below it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:57](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2457) wick halfway point; [1:28:24](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5304) wick CE and volume imbalance below; [3:08:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11297) discount wick becomes premium wick

### Fair Value Gap (FVG; BISI / SIBI) — definition, mechanics and tolerance
- **Kind:** definition / rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Definition: the range between candle 1 and candle 3 not overlapped by any other candle's range. A bullish one (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency) means the market has not offered efficient sell-side delivery through that range; for every range that goes up, efficient delivery means price must be offered back down through the same range. "Anybody tells you anything other than that... making stuff up." (He mis-says SIBI as "CBI" in the captions: "sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency" = bearish gap.)
  - Draw only the real gap: low of one candle to the high of another, shade only that. Every small separation between three candles is not a tradeable FVG; you need other PD-array context. His example of a bearish FVG that is not tradeable is the intraday inefficiencies inside the larger bearish structure above the CISD price.
  - Preferred behavior: you do NOT want a bearish FVG to fully close in; you want price to go into it a little and reverse. Trading just because "an imbalance completely closes in" is "the lowest form of trading with imbalances".
  - Tolerance: wicks may color outside the FVG, but only about one "standard deviation" of the FVG, meaning roughly its own width, and generally less; bodies should not close beyond it. Small stray pokes are "mohawks". It will not go "five times" the width.
  - Paint-roller analogy: the daily is the roller, the 15m a 2-inch brush, the 5m a 1-inch brush, the 1m a fine brush; price runs out of "paint" in porous pockets which an FVG marks.
- **How to identify:** 3-candle pattern; gap between candle 1's high (or low) and candle 3's low (or high); bodies of lower-TF candles respecting it.
- **How to use:** Premium/discount and CE rules; for shorts enter inside the gap (see Order Block entry); stops beyond the OB; FVGs from the HTF are projected to the LTF as boxes.
- **Example in this video:** Daily NQ Aug 12 (Mon) high to Aug 14 (Wed) low, with Aug 13 (Tue) the displacement candle in the middle, is the bullish FVG he used as his weekly downside objective (low of the gap about 18,765.25, hit Thursday into Friday).
- **Timestamps:** [1:26:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5209) textbook definition; [2:12:08](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=7928) do not want gaps to fill; [2:30:41](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9041) draw only the real gap; [2:47:31](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10051) one-standard-deviation wick tolerance
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The wick tolerance rule (~one FVG width) is new; he says it was "supposed to be in the book". He also says there is "no one-trick-pony explanation" of an FVG: other PD arrays must be recognized even if you do not trade them.

### What moves price: only two mechanisms  (aliases: draw on liquidity)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** Price only goes up to (a) go into inefficiencies above market price or (b) take buy-side liquidity (buy stops: either breakout longs' entries or stops protecting shorts). Price only goes down to (a) go into inefficiencies below (FVG, opening range gap) or (b) take sell stops below old lows (breakout shorts or stops protecting longs). "Nothing else"; manual interventions use the same levels, just farther and quicker. It does not need many contracts (he claims one contract can move price a tick when the next price is offered), so "buying and selling pressure" is not the driver; price skips creating small gaps when it is delivered to a level.
- **How to use:** Always ask which inefficiency or which pool price is reaching for.
- **Timestamps:** [1:29:59](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5399) the two mechanisms; [1:31:33](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5493) volume/contracts study; [3:12:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11537) house-price analogy
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Stated as an absolute; he is demystifying the "algorithm" language.

### One Shot One Kill (sniper model)  (aliases: OSOK, sniper mentality)
- **Kind:** entry model / mindset
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take very few, surgical trades: "one or two, maybe three a week", not an everyday setup. Do not spray a magazine (machine gun) hoping to hit something.
  - The PD array (FVG) is the "scope": the target is the moment price steps into the FVG; as price hits its consequent encroachment or approaches it, it is in the crosshairs. Then "exhale", enter as price enters the FVG. Stop placed beyond the imbalance candle: below it for a bullish FVG, above its high for a bearish FVG (which candle he means by "imbalance candle" is caption unclear; in the OB entry below he uses the order block high +1 tick). After entering, do nothing extra and sit still; do not announce trades on social media.
  - Aim at the largest magnitude: sell the highest premium array available and target the extreme (his example: ~360 NQ handles from the Thursday short to the Friday FVG low). Be prepared to absorb fluctuations between entry and the target.
  - Start with humble goals: consistently 5 handles in ES, 10-15 handles in NQ.
- **How to use:** Define target first (HTF FVG low), then find the highest-timeframe premium array to enter from, place the stop once with logical price points, never chase it.

### Change in State of Delivery (CISD) and the bearish Order Block
- **Kind:** definition / rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price was offered up (buy-side delivery) through a run of up-close daily candles; then a displacement lower (the Tuesday Sept 3 drop) overtakes that candle's low. The reference point is the opening price of the (lowest/first) up-close candle in that run; that open is the change in the state of delivery and the algorithm refers back to it. Here: the opening price of Thursday Aug 29's daily candle (exact candles are pointed at, caption unclear).
  - The up-close candle(s) before the displacement form "all one singular bearish order block". He says it is not simply "the last up-close candle" as he first taught it; the rule definitions will be in his book. Because the displacement overtakes the low, you cannot use just that candle's high and low; use the truest beginning of the run-up as the order-block reference (the opening price).
  - Validation: the order block is only confirmed when price crosses below that opening price. It does not need to close below; no engulfing candle or "FU candle" requirement. On the 15-minute chart price had wicked above the open/high a couple of times and then a candle traded below the opening price, which validates it. Lower-TF bodies respecting the level adds confidence.
  - Mean threshold: for the up-close bearish OB, shorts belong from the candle's open up to the halfway point (mean threshold); the upper half should ideally never be traded through. Staying away from it signaled strength; he added a pyramided entry once and let it ride.
  - Entry/stop (the 15m example): after validation the next candle created a bearish FVG (the "first presentation", not limited to 9:30-10:00). Entry = the FVG's candle-3 high (lower edge, which is the lowest threshold entry) minus 1 tick to ensure a fill (a sell limit placed one tick below the edge; the price quoted is about 19,183.25, caption unclear). Stop = high of the bearish order block candle plus 1 tick. Risk is defined, "it never goes there". You can also add on the open of that candle or deeper into the FVG.
  - FVGs that sit above the CISD price inside the bearish structure should stay open and are not to be traded up into ("real heavy").
- **How to use:** Mark the open of the run-up candle on the HTF chart; track when LTF price wicks above and bodies respect it; once price trades below the open, the OB is valid; short retests between the open and the 50% (mean threshold); stop = OB high +1 tick; target = next draw (HTF FVG low/old low).
- **Example in this video:** Daily NQ (Aug 29 open as reference), Tue Sept 3 displacement lower, Wed Sept 4 candle high (he describes the low-of-one-candle to Wednesday's-high range as the "real" daily bearish FVG; my reading is Wednesday's high is its lower edge), then Thursday Sept 5 the 15m/5m/1m trade (below). Exact candles are pointed at on screen, so treat the candle identification as approximate.
- **Timestamps:** [2:05:43](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=7543) bearish OB from two up-close candles; [2:12:56](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=7976) CISD is the opening price; [2:25:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=8749) validation on lower time frame; [2:28:15](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=8895) entry minus 1 tick, stop OB high +1 tick; [3:00:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10817) mean threshold

### Price Delivery Continuum Theory (top-down PD array transposition)  (aliases: higher time frame PD array respected by lower time frame)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Whatever PD arrays exist on a higher-timeframe chart, the lower time frames should respect them: draw the HTF FVG as a rectangle and carry it to lower charts. Wicks may color outside the lines; judge whether the bodies go beyond the array. If bodies respect it, you are getting closer to the entry.
  - Standard ladder only: monthly, weekly, daily, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, 1m (nothing in between like a 6m or 7m); sub-1m is excessive.
  - Time is the most important factor: "if it ain't the right time, price ain't going nowhere".

### Market Maker Sell Model (1-minute read) and second-stage distribution
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 1m: look for the original consolidation (a clean box of price, not the impulsive sharp stretches); a stop run below its lows (relative equal lows, sell side) then rally back to touch the original consolidation high (first stage accumulation/reaccumulation), then rally and take a high (the "smart money reversal") inside the HTF premium. You do not need the smart money reversal itself; wait for the break of market structure at the low, then trade the retrace back up into the order block (CISD price).
  - First stage distribution then second-stage distribution: the second stage is the "unicorn", the fastest, most powerful and easiest hold. If you can spot it, sell the second-stage redistribution (or buy second-stage reaccumulation for the buy model).
  - He says this is a market truism (markup then sell-off), nothing to do with Wyckoff or ICT. Targets are the pools: sell side below, then below the original consolidation.
  - "Second mouse gets the cheese": do not sell the first touch of the premium just because price is high; wait for the run and the re-test after the opening range.

Break of structure at the low, retrace up into the bearish OB (opening price), pyramided adds in the lower half of the OB candle, then expansion down through first stage distribution then second stage distribution into the sell-side pools.

## Glossary candidates
`Bellwether time frame` — the 15-minute chart used to mark primary buy-side/sell-side pools for the day.
`Primary vs minor liquidity pool` — primary = 15m relative equals; minor = 5m levels inside the range.
`SIBI / "CBI" (caption)` — sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency: a bearish FVG.
`BISI` — buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency: a bullish FVG (e.g., the Aug 13 daily FVG).
`Mohawk` — a small stray wick poking beyond an FVG edge, within tolerance.
`Mean threshold` — halfway point of an order-block candle; entries should stay on the far side of it.
`Change in state of delivery` — price crossing back through the open of the run-up candle(s), making that open the reference for the order block.
`Price Delivery Continuum Theory` — lower-timeframe price must respect higher-timeframe PD arrays.
`Terminus` — the final target of a trade (here the Aug 13 daily FVG low).
`One Shot One Kill (OSOK)` — sniper-style single entry with a defined target and a stop that is not chased.
`Unicorn (second stage distribution)` — the fast second leg of a Market Maker Sell Model.
`Time distortion` — a holding pattern in which price drifts while positions are built.
`Engineering liquidity` — building resting stops (e.g., sell stops under fake bull flags) before delivering to the target.
`Lunch macro` — 11:30–1:30 ET retracement window against the morning move.
`Moral victory` — logging a precise level touch in your journal while executing exits with margin for fills.
`Marrying the vein` — staying in a trade that has already yielded its gold.
`Watering hole` — a crowd (chat, stream, forum) whose stated bias creates resting liquidity.
