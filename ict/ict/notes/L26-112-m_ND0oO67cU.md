## Concepts

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** definition | rule
- **What ICT says:** A suspension block is a range-bound level where price has consolidated but not broken (yet). As long as bodies trade above the suspension block, it remains supportive (bullish). If price closes below it, the block breaks and downside likely follows. The suspension block is a "line in the sand"—a clear structural boundary. On the daily dollar chart, a suspension block was in play; ICT was watching to see if it would hold.
- **How to identify:** Look for a multi-candle consolidation range (daily or weekly) where price has stopped a move. Note the low of that range as the suspension block threshold.
- **How to use:** Use the block low as a stop-loss level. If holding a bullish position (e.g., long dollars), place stop just below the block. If price breaks the block on a body close, exit or reverse.
- **Example in this video:** Dollar index: a suspension block was in place; as long as no body traded below it, ICT maintained a bullish dollar bias. If the block broke (close below the low), he would shift to bearish.

### Wick Precedence Over Adjacent FVGs
- **Kind:** refinement | rule
- **What ICT says:** When a wick is in close proximity to an FVG, the wick takes precedence in price's attention hierarchy. Price will often work inside the wick range before confirming a move through the FVG. This is especially important for stop placement: if shorting into a FVG and a wick is nearby, set stop above the wick, not just above the FVG. The wick's high becomes the more sensitive level.
- **How to identify:** Mark wicks separately from bodies. Note when a wick sits on the edge of or overlaps with an FVG.
- **How to use:** Set stops above wicks, not FVGs. When price approaches a FVG with a nearby wick, expect price to test the wick range first. Don't assume a direct break through the FVG.
- **Example in this video:** MES: "This wick here. And that's what's being the challenging factor there. Not that swing low. Okay, not that liquidity below that low. It's that wick. It's It's It's staring you in your face, but you don't see it because you don't see an inefficiency."

### Buy-Side / Sell-Side Imbalance with Sell-Side Inefficiency
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A PD array can be defined as "buy side and balance sell side inefficiency." This means: the buy-side imbalance is a gap where buyers eagerly bid price up with no sellers (inefficient); the sell-side inefficiency is the lower level where sellers have concentrated orders. When price trades into this structure, it first "takes" the buy-side, then reverses to "take" the sell-side. Both sides must be tagged for the move to complete.
- **How to identify:** Mark an area of chop or consolidation. Identify where the last rally peaked (buy-side) and where the last dip bottomed (sell-side). The range between is the imbalance.
- **How to use:** Trade into the sell-side after the buy-side has been "taken" (hit). Set targets at the sell-side and below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "This big buy side and balance sell side inefficiency... That midpoint here. Or consequent encroachment. Uh I want to see does it use this then to make a run higher?"

### Opening Range Gap (ORG) Application on Premium Gap Openings
- **Kind:** rule | entry model | refinement
- **What ICT says:** On a premium gap opening (open above prior close), the first protocol is to watch if price can make it back to the opening price (9:30) within the first 30 minutes. This is the minimum expectation. If bulls want to run higher, they need to at least defend the 9:30 level and start sending it higher from there. Conversely, if they can't hold 9:30, expect downside toward consequent encouragement (the midpoint of the ORG).
- **How to identify:** Compare prior-day close to 9:30 open. If open is higher (premium gap), mark the 9:30 price level.
- **How to use:** On premium gap days, watch the 9:30 level closely. If price closes above it, bulls are defending; if it closes below, bears are winning. Use 9:30 as a pivot for intraday entries.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "This is a 9:30 opening price. We went below it here. We came right back to it now. So bulls, if they really want to send it higher, this would be the minimum expectation here."

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) as Potential Reversal
- **Kind:** definition | rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:** After a swing move up or down, if a prior FVG sits at the exact level of a new rally/dip, that prior FVG becomes an "inversion fair value gap"—a potential reversal zone. Price reaching an iFVG and failing to close above/below it (depending on direction) signals weakness in the trend. Entry: short into an iFVG rally if bodies refuse to touch the iFVG midpoint/high.
- **How to identify:** Mark a prior FVG. Note where price rallies into it later. If that prior FVG aligns perfectly with the new high of the rally, it's an iFVG.
- **How to use:** Use iFVG levels as reversal targets. Short into iFVG rallies; cover at the iFVG low or prior support.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "This could potentially become a inversion fair value gap, which is basically laying right top of that same level there, okay?"

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### Turtle Soup (False Breakout)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Turtle soup is a false breakout; price breaks above a key level (relative equal high, resistance) but fails to sustain it, reversing back into the range. Entry: short the breakout at the high; cover at the range midpoint or prior lows. This is a reversal setup.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a candle that closes above a resistance level. If the next few candles close back inside the range, it's likely a turtle soup.
- **How to use:** Short the candle that breaks out above resistance. Set stop above the breakout high. Target: midpoint of the range or prior support.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "Using this as turtle soup, it's a false breakout, okay? And then expect it to pull back down into the range."
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:26](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=1586)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Works best in consolidating, non-trend markets; less reliable in strong trends.

### Octant Levels & Fibonacci Subdivisions
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Subdivide an ORG or any range into eighths (octants). Mark and watch for price to react at these levels, especially the 37.5% and 62.5% (3rd and 5th octants). Octant levels often hold as micro-support/resistance and can be screenshotted and shared as teaching points.
- **How to identify:** Measure the ORG (high minus low). Divide by 8. Mark the octant levels.
- **How to use:** Use octant levels for stops and micro-targets. Expect bodies to hesitate or reverse at these levels.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "There's your octants. You can see we traded down into the 37.5 octant level there."

### Low-Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions
- **Kind:** definition | rule
- **What ICT says:** Low-resistance liquidity run conditions are price moves where there is minimal supply/demand interference—smooth, fast breakouts with no pullbacks or consolidation. These are ideal for momentum trading and large directional moves. Conversely, choppy, sideways moves with lots of wicks are high-resistance (ugly) conditions and should be avoided or only scalped lightly.
- **How to identify:** Watch candlestick patterns: smooth bodies, minimal wicks, consistent direction = low resistance. Lots of wicks, small bodies, multiple reversals = high resistance.
- **How to use:** Trade only in low-resistance conditions. If market is ugly and choppy, reduce size, avoid leverage, or sit out.
- **Example in this video:** MES: "I don't like markets like this. This is not clean price action...I don't like it. I think it's ugly. I think it shouldn't be taken in terms of using real leverage against it with money and equity."

### "Phil" and Algorithmic Manipulation
- **Kind:** observation | definition
- **What ICT says:** "Phil" is the market maker or algorithm making sudden, localized moves that don't align with order flow logic. These moves trap breakout traders and stop hunters. When ICT sees Phil at work, he notes it but doesn't trade it (or fades it). Phil can't run the market all day (too obvious), but at strategic times, you'll see sudden spikes or drops with no conviction. The signal: wicks extend far, bodies fail to follow through.

### Swing High / Swing Low Definition
- **Kind:** definition | refinement
- **What ICT says:** A swing high is NOT just the wick high; it's the highest body close in a series of bodies (or the highest open if the open is higher than the close). A swing low is the lowest body element. When looking for a prior FVG to create an iFVG, you must identify the swing high by its body, not its wick. The wick's damage may have extended beyond, but the swing high for structural purposes is the body.
- **How to identify:** Look at a series of candles. Find the highest close or open (whichever is higher) among the bodies. That's the swing high. Do the same for lows.
- **How to use:** Use swing highs/lows (based on bodies) to find prior FVGs for iFVG setups. Use them as entry/exit levels.
- **Example in this video:** MES: "Swing highs and swing lows are not limited just to the wick high and the wick low of it, okay? So, if we're comparing swings you want to look at the bodies in the rotation that creates a swing high."
- **Timestamps:** [0:46:45](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2805), [0:47:31](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2851)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a clarification: many traders incorrectly use wick highs/lows for swing identification.

### Reclaimed Fair Value Gap (Bearish or Bullish)
- **Kind:** definition | rule
- **What ICT says:** When price revisits a FVG and closes back inside it (after leaving it earlier), it "reclaims" the gap. A reclaimed bearish FVG occurs after a strong downmove when price pulls back up into the FVG but fails to close above the top (bodies stay in lower half). This reclamation is a continuation pattern: expect downside to resume. Conversely, a reclaimed bullish FVG (after a pullback into it from below) forecasts upside continuation.
- **How to identify:** After price leaves a FVG and moves away, watch if it comes back to touch the FVG. If it closes inside the gap (not above/below it), it's being reclaimed.
- **How to use:** After reclamation, expect the move to continue in the original direction (bearish reclaim = more downside; bullish reclaim = more upside).
- **Example in this video:** MES: "Let's see if this can become a reclaimed bearish fair value gap."
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:18](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=1398), [0:48:19](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2899)

### Volume Imbalance in Commodities & Precious Metals
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Volume imbalances (gaps with no overlap in range) are common in commodities (crude oil, gold, silver) and futures. They work like FVGs—price will often revisit them as targets. Mark them on daily and intraday charts; they're reliable S/R levels.
- **How to identify:** Look for large single-candle gaps (or multi-candle gaps) where no candlestick overlaps the preceding one. Mark the gap as a volume imbalance.
- **How to use:** Volume imbalances are targets when price moves away from them. Use them for profit-taking or to set additional targets.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Re-acclimation protocol:** After time away, tape-read only. Don't force trades. Spend a few days getting in sync before resuming live trading.
- **Event-driven risk management:** When major geopolitical/political events are expected, reduce leverage, reduce size, or sit out. The probability of violent moves and account blowup is elevated.
- **Patience and observation:** Observation (tape reading) is free learning; trading capital is at risk. Choose observation first.
- **Market manipulation acknowledgment:** Government, politicians, and market makers actively manipulate markets to extract retail capital. Be aware of this; it changes risk calculus.
- **Emotional discipline:** Don't chase Phil moves. Don't force trades in ugly conditions. Let the market come to you.
- **Macro awareness:** Geopolitical events (wars, military threats, political upheaval) cascade into price volatility. Awareness of these events is part of risk management.
- **Seasonal perspective:** Summer 2026 is expected to be chaotic. Planning to trade it is unwise if inexperienced.

## Glossary candidates
`Suspension block` — A support/resistance level formed by a consolidation range; breaks indicate a reversal of bias
`ORG (Opening Range Gap)` — The range between prior-day close and 9:30 AM open
`Consequent encouragement` — The midpoint of an ORG; a ~70% probability target in the first 30 minutes
`iFVG (Inversion FVG)` — A prior FVG that becomes a reversal zone when price rallies into it later
`Turtle soup` — A false breakout; price breaks above resistance but reverses back inside
`Volume imbalance` — A gap between candles with no overlap; works like an FVG as a price target
`Octant` — 1/8th of a range (Fibonacci subdivision); 37.5% and 62.5% octants are often significant
`Swing high / Swing low` — Identified by the highest/lowest body (open/close), NOT the wick
`Reclaimed FVG` — A gap that price revisits and closes back inside; signals continuation of the original move
`Phil` — Algorithmic or manual manipulation; sudden moves with no conviction
`Tape reading` — Observation of price action without taking real trades; learning without capital risk
`Suspension block low` — The floor of a consolidation range; close below it = block broken = bearish
`Event-driven` — Market reacting to geopolitical, political, or economic catalysts; high volatility, manipulation likely
`Low-resistance liquidity run` — Smooth, fast price moves with minimal wicks; ideal for trading
`High-resistance conditions` — Choppy, sideways moves with lots of wicks; avoid or scale down
