## Summary
Episode 12, "Market structure for precision technicians", is ICT's advanced structure lesson. He says even his paid group hadn't seen it. It goes beyond higher-high/higher-low. ICT classifies swings as short-, intermediate- and long-term, nested under the daily chart as "parent". He adds his own rule: **whenever an imbalance (FVG) is rebalanced, the swing formed at that moment is an intermediate-term high or low.** That ITH must not be violated while the idea is valid. An ITH that fails to exceed its neighboring short-term highs signals an exceedingly weak market. He redefines the order block as the whole consecutive series of up-close (or down-close) candles, not "the last candle". He shows an aggressive FVG entry inside the order block that doesn't need a short-term low broken, and a classic return-to-OB entry. He gives two targeting methods: the long-term range projection, and a Fib from the ITH to the LT low at −1.5 SD. He lays out institutional order flow rules (up-close candles are resistance in bearish swings; down-close candles are support in bullish swings). He ends with a neutral, no-trade stance and his thesis that price only does two things: run liquidity or rebalance imbalance.

## Concepts

### The two narrative questions (the only things price does)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Before sitting down at the charts, ask: is price likely to go **up for buy stops (BSL) or to rebalance an imbalance (FVG) above**? Or **down for sell stops (SSL) or to rebalance an imbalance below**? That's the current market narrative.
  - He ignores patterns, harmonics, Elliott waves, ratio ideas and anything "retail".
  - The market only books and prints candles on two principles: **liquidity** (above old highs for buy stops, below old lows for sell stops) and **imbalance** (creating or rebalancing it). "Imbalance, rebalance, run for liquidity. That's it."
  - The riddle: if harmonics, Elliott, supply/demand, volume profile, Wyckoff, Ichimoku and the rest were real, how would the market choose which conflicting discipline to follow on a given day? Believing them is "faith-based".
  - Bank traders only look at imbalance, rebalance and liquidity (in Forex they add interest-rate differentials). They don't care about S/R, trend lines or MA crosses.

### Higher-timeframe parent / lower-timeframe child structure
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The daily chart is the parent. All minor lower-timeframe swings are subordinate to it. The daily has the bulk of the volume and order flow; institutions and banks work off it. It "makes or breaks your trend continuation."

  - A retail "trend change" (a failed higher high, then a break of the swing low before it) is often just a deep discount buy for ICT when the HTF is bullish. You need the HTF hierarchy to know which.

If it breaks, his daily analysis is probably wrong. Then he demands more information and sits on his hands, or, if in a trade, takes the stop as a cost of business (not a flawed model, not a failed trader).
  - Developing students should keep forecasts to about a 5-day horizon and not worry about forecasting the whole weekly range. It took ICT six years.

### Short-, intermediate- and long-term swing classification (ICT's version)  (aliases ICT uses: STH/STL, ITH/ITL, LTH/LTL)
- **Kind:** definition / new coinage (extends Larry Williams)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Base classification (from Larry Williams; the source book shown is *Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading*): an ITH is the highest high between two short-term highs, i.e. a STH with a lower STH on its left and a lower STH on its right. Lows mirror this.
  - **ICT's addition, "the main one":** any time price trades back to **rebalance an imbalance** (fill a FVG), the swing high formed at that rebalance is **immediately** an **intermediate-term high**. If price drops back down to fill a bullish imbalance, that low is an **intermediate-term low**.
  - So there are two ways to classify an ITH: (a) a STH with lower STHs either side, or (b) a high formed by rebalancing an imbalance.
  - He labels these in his mind, not on the chart, to avoid distraction. Annotations are "lipstick" for teaching only.
  - Don't mark every swing on the lower timeframes; that's overkill. Establish structure on the framing timeframe (1h here).
- **How to identify:** Find FVGs (single-candle "one-pass" moves). When price returns and fills one, the turning point at that fill is an ITH or ITL.
- **How to use:** In a bearish idea, the ITH formed by rebalancing must not be exceeded. The next short-term high should be lower than it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March 2022, 1h, inside the daily FVG. A down candle left an imbalance; price rallied back up to fill it. The swing high formed there was labeled an ITH. On the way down, a fill of a bullish imbalance made an ITL.

### Weak intermediate-term high (ITH below its neighbouring short-term highs)
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ideally an ITH should be above both STHs beside it. When the ITH formed by rebalancing is **not higher** than the STH to its left or right, it's "only rebalancing". The market is **exceedingly weak**, and "the algorithm is tipping its hand".
  - ICT says he pioneered this idea; no one taught it to him.
  - This answers the retail question of which way a pennant or triangle will break. Retail sees low, high, higher low, failed higher high: a pennant. The weak ITH says it breaks down.
  - Retail traders see the consolidation as a bull flag. ICT likes to fade those ("false bull flag").
- **How to use:** With a bearish HTF premise, a weak ITH justifies anticipating the failure. Treat the up-close candles forming there as a bearish order block and look for an aggressive short.

### Invalidation rule: an ITH/ITL must not be violated
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If bearish and an imbalance is rebalanced, the next short-term high should be lower than that ITH. If price trades higher than the ITH, "your trade idea's probably flawed".
  - Don't force the trade again. Wait for market structure to get back in sync with your expectation. That's how you avoid blowing the account or forcing your will on the market.
  - If the ITH or ITL breaks after your idea became possible, go to the sidelines and don't re-enter. The next setup may be the same session, the same day, or the following week.
  - While the key high holds, trust the move and keep holding.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:55](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2515) — next STH should be lower; if broken, the idea is flawed, don't force it · [0:50:47](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3047) — key high/low; violated means cancel the idea · [0:52:26](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3146) — move to the sidelines; can't force it

### Order block, ICT's actual definition (consecutive candles, not "the last candle")
- **Kind:** definition (correction of common usage)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A bearish order block is the **consecutive series of up-close candles** before the down move, not "the last up-close candle before the down move". "That is not my order block... stop teaching that... call it something else."
  - On the 1h it was the last two up-close candles. On the 15m the same zone was five up-close candles.
  - Inside the order block's range you can hunt FVGs. You **don't need** price to trade below the last up-close candle first.
  - The "classic ICT bearish order block" entry: price trades down below the OB, then comes back up and bumps the bottom of the OB rectangle.
  - The "advanced interpretation" uses market structure: you're already expecting the sell-off at the weak ITH, so you see the imbalances forming inside the OB while it's still building.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1h, two up-close candles at the weak ITH make the bearish OB (shaded pink). On the lower timeframe, the last two candles inside the OB with a gap delivered "exactly to the quarter point" of a candle's high (check it on your own chart).

### Aggressive short inside the order block (no short-term low break required)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Standard model: a short-term low is broken (MSS), then price returns into the FVG to short.
  - Aggressive variant: with the HTF premise and a weak ITH, if a FVG forms with a breakdown inside the up-close OB candles, short the return into it. "Notice I don't need a swing low broken."
  - Stop right above the high.
  - It's a fractal: a small single-candle imbalance on the 15m mirrors the hourly imbalance. If price trades back to that candle's high, that's the aggressive entry.
  - The 15m is the bellwether for the actual in-and-out. If you don't like the risk parameters there, drop lower.
- **How to identify:** Daily premise (bearish), a 1h weak ITH at the rebalanced imbalance, 1h up-close candles (OB), then on 15m (or lower) a breakdown leaving a FVG inside the OB.
- **How to use:** Short the FVG or the OB bottom. Stop above the high. Target the HTF objective (daily low/SSL), often many times the risk.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15m. Five up-close candles inside the hourly OB, then a breakdown with a FVG. Price rebalanced it and the aggressive short followed. Heat was limited to one candle, the stop above the high, and the downside objective many times the risk.

### Institutional order flow: up-close candles are resistance in bearish swings, down-close candles are support in bullish swings
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When bearish, all up-close candles should keep price from going higher than them. Price may come back and touch them (acting as a bearish OB, a "speed bump") but shouldn't trade above them. If price doesn't overtake the nearest up-close candles, it doesn't need to reach the ones above. Price "remains heavy".
  - When bullish, the swing has few down-close candles. Those should support price, without being overlapped below. It's permissible only if there's a short-term low in close proximity whose sell stops get taken before reaccumulation and continuation higher. If there's no nearby swing low (no SSL), there's nothing to worry about.
  - "Bullish swings: down-close candles are support. Bearish swings: up-close candles are resistance."
  - Use this to know the move is still good (hold without fear) and when it's likely flawed.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 4m/5m. After the OB short, rallies traded back into up-close candles but never overtook them. Later up-close clusters were never re-reached, and price kept falling.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:22](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2902) — institutional order flow defined · [0:49:10](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2950) — doesn't overtake the up-close candle; real resistance · [0:51:35](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3095) — speed bumps; bullish mirror · [0:53:13](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3193) — down-close overlap only OK if a short-term low with SSL is nearby

### Significant break in market structure and targeting projections
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If price breaks a short-term low **and** an intermediate-term low (or just the ITL), that's a **significant break in market structure**, more meaningful than merely taking out a short-term low.
  - The long-term high to long-term low range is then used for targeting. Measure it and replicate it downward for generic price targets.
  - Alternative: anchor the Fibonacci from the **ITH** (the failed retracement where the decline begins) down to the long-term low that breaks. The **−1.5 standard deviation** projection marked the eventual low.
  - Why the ITH and not the earlier high? Because that's where the swing (the decline) actually begins; it's the framework. This answers years of "why did you anchor your Fib to that swing?" questions.
  - The long-term high/low range is generally linked to a higher-timeframe (daily) range, here the daily FVG framing.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1h. The ITH at the rebalanced imbalance, then a decline below the STL and ITL. Fib from the ITH to the LT low: −1.5 SD = the low of the move.

### Why a daily FVG gets revisited (efficiency)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A one-sided single-candle (one-day) drop leaves too little time for buyers to participate. The algorithm allows efficient price delivery by going back up to offer those prices again, with an up candle balancing the move. It doesn't matter whether those buyers are right. Once rebalanced, price spends a little time, then displaces again toward the objective. This is "likely", not "must", and not necessarily immediate.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Pre-chart questions: up for BSL or to rebalance above? Down for SSL or to rebalance below?
- Daily = parent. LTF swings are subordinate. Every trade idea must link to the daily.
- The high at the daily PD array (FVG high) = LTH. If it breaks, the analysis is wrong: stand aside or accept the stop.
- ITH = (a) a STH with lower STHs either side, or (b) the high formed when price rebalances an imbalance. ITL is mirrored.
- Weak ITH (not above the neighbouring STHs) = exceedingly weak market; expect the breakdown.
- Bearish: the next STH must be lower than the ITH. Above it means the idea is flawed. Don't force it; wait for resync.
- Order block = the whole consecutive series of up-close (bearish OB) or down-close (bullish OB) candles, not the last one.
- Aggressive short: FVG inside the OB, no short-term low break needed. Stop above the high.
- Classic short: price trades below the OB, returns to its bottom. Stop above the candle high.
- Bearish swings: up-close candles must hold as resistance. Bullish swings: down-close candles must hold as support (overlap only OK to take a nearby STL's sell stops).
- Significant MSS = STL and ITL (or just ITL) broken. Then project the LT range.
- Fib target: anchor at the ITH where the decline starts down to the LT low. −1.5 SD.
- Frame on 1h. Execute on 15m (bellwether), then 5/4/3/2/1m if needed for risk. Don't label every LTF swing.
- Forecast horizon for students: about 5 days. Don't worry about the full weekly range.
- If a trade "could go either way", don't take it.

## Glossary candidates
`Intermediate-term high (rebalance definition)` — the swing high formed the moment price rebalances (fills) an imbalance; a key high that must not be violated while bearish.
`Weak ITH` — an intermediate-term high that fails to exceed the short-term highs on either side; signals an exceedingly weak market.
`Parent/child price swings` — HTF (daily) swings govern and subordinate LTF swings.
`Significant break in market structure` — taking out an intermediate-term low (with or without the short-term low), not just a short-term low.
`Institutional order flow` — in bearish swings up-close candles act as resistance; in bullish swings down-close candles act as support.
`Speed bumps` — prior up-close (bearish) or down-close (bullish) candles that price may touch but shouldn't overtake.
`Imbalance / rebalance` — a one-sided move leaving inefficiency (FVG), and the later return that offers those prices again.
`Lipstick` — chart annotations used for teaching; not kept on live trading charts.
`Technical science` — ICT's term for measurable, logic-based price analysis vs subjective "technical analysis".
`False bull flag` — a bearish consolidation that retail reads as bullish continuation; ICT fades it.
