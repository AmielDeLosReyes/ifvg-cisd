## Concepts

### Consequent Encroachment
- **Kind:** refinement / definition
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint between two price levels (e.g., a low and a lower low, or a high and a higher high). Used to grade discount wicks and measure premium/discount relative to range resets. When price trades below consequent encroachment on the daily dollar index, he grades the wick to see if bodies are below it—body placement below consequent encroachment indicates bearish strength.
- **How to identify:** Draw a fib from high to low (or old low to new low); the midpoint is consequent encroachment.
- **How to use:** As a reference for body placement; if bodies close below consequent encroachment, it signals continuation lower (bearish). If bodies stay above it, it may reject.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: 10:13 suspension block at consequent encroachment of Wednesday RTH opening range gap; bodies held below halfway point, validating sell signal targeting 24,809.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:49](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=529), [0:14:28](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=868), [1:01:28](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=3688)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Distinct from support/resistance; it is a mathematical midpoint that resets the range when broken. Price often revisits it as equilibrium before continuation.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) | Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:** An FVG is the gap between a candlestick's close and the next candlestick's open. An inversion FVG (iFVG) is when price trades below a buy-side imbalance (sell-side inefficiency), then re-enters that level from below. For iFVG to be valid: (1) price must close below the midpoint of the original imbalance on the low side, (2) price must be allowed to trade back into the level, (3) when it does, bodies must close below the midpoint to confirm, (4) the first touch of that level on the next break lower is an entry.
- **How to identify:** Look for a buy-side imbalance (open = close of previous candle, creating no volume imbalance); then price fails to get above consequent encroachment; it closes below consequent encroachment. Later, price retraces back up into the imbalance level, bodies close below midpoint—now it's an iFVG.
- **How to use:** Once confirmed, the first re-touch of the imbalance on a break lower is a demo/paper entry. It is "superior to supply and demand" because there are specific rule-based conditions; not just a rectangle drawn on a chart.
- **Example in this video:** GBP/USD daily: buy-side imbalance (sell-side inefficiency) from the original consolidation; waiting for lower prices if it breaks down and validates as iFVG with proper body placement below midpoint.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:15](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=915), [0:23:17](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=1397), [0:39:50](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2390)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Price can wick above the imbalance but if the body doesn't close above consequent encroachment, it doesn't invalidate the bearish structure. iFVG is superior to simpler "support/resistance" because the rule set is precise and repeatable.

### Event Horizon (Midpoint Targeting Strategy)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:** When you have two sell-side liquidity pools (two lows), you can draw a fib between them; the midpoint is event horizon. Use it as a partial exit point when you're bearish and expecting continuation lower. Take 25% off at event horizon on a long downtrend; let the balance run to the lower target. You can also use it as an adding-in point if you're adding to a short.
- **How to identify:** Identify two lows (or one old low and one new lower low); draw fib or measure midpoint.
- **How to use:** As a partial exit (especially if you're nearing the end of a move), or as a point to add to the short if you believe lower prices will follow. On a long-established downtrend, take 25% minimum at event horizon to bank some profit.
- **Example in this video:** Bitcoin daily (hypothetically short): two sell-side pools; event horizon is midpoint. If short Bitcoin, take 25% off at event horizon, then let the runner go to the lower target around 49,577 or lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:14](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=1634), [0:34:22](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2062), [0:42:30](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2550)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Event horizon changes in meaning once the range resets; if you break the lower low and treat that as a new range, the old event horizon no longer applies—you measure from the new lows.

### Range Reset & Premium/Discount
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price takes out an old low (part of a larger run lower), the range resets. What was previously "discount" becomes "premium" if price retraces back to the midpoint of the new range. This is a critical lesson—don't be fearful of taking setups in perceived "deep discount" if the range has reset and you're actually at equilibrium or premium relative to the new lows. Many traders misunderstand discount/premium because they look at a limited snapshot of price action instead of recognizing when a range has been invalidated.
- **How to identify:** When price breaks below a key low that was part of a prior range, that low no longer defines the bottom of the new range. The new low becomes the reference point.
- **How to use:** When you're bearish and shorting, take partials at equilibrium (consequent encroachment of new range) even if it looks like you're "buying at a premium"—you're actually at equilibrium of the new, lower range.
- **Example in this video:** Bitcoin: once price broke the prior established lows and moved lower for months, the range reset. What appeared to be a "premium" retracement was actually equilibrium of the new range, validating further shorts.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:09](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2109), [0:37:30](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2250), [0:38:17](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2297)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a common source of confusion; ICT addresses traders who wrongly avoid "deep discount" setups. The discount/premium labels reset when the structural low is broken.

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick that shows clear bearish close (bodies in lower half, wick above) at a key level like consequent encroachment. It acts as a suspension of bullish momentum and signals continuation lower. On NQ 1m, the 10:13 candlestick was a suspension block—bearish close, bodies below halfway, validating the short setup into the next target.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candlestick with: strong wick up (showing rejections of buyers), body closed in lower half (especially below consequent encroachment), at a key resistance or consequent encroachment level.
- **How to use:** As confirmation to enter short (or add to short) with conviction.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, 10:13 ET: suspension block at the RTH gap consequent encroachment; bodies held in lower half; validated the short targeting 24,809.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:41](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=3641), [1:07:48](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=3868)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Related to rejection blocks; suspension is the specific moment where momentum stalls.

### Rejection Block
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick showing strong rejection of price trying to move in the wrong direction (e.g., rejecting upside near a sell-side liquidity level on a bearish day). Bodies hold below a key level, wick extends above, closing below. Multiple rejection blocks in sequence reinforce bearish bias.
- **How to identify:** Strong wick above a key level, body closes back below that level; bodies stay compressed/small.
- **How to use:** As confirmation of directional bias; multiple rejection blocks reinforce conviction.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: rejection block patterns seen in the morning session near Wednesday RTH gap levels.
- **Timestamps:** [1:01:28](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=3688)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Often confused with other wicking patterns; rejection block specifically shows rejection of a direction at a key level.

### Order Flow & Body Placement
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** You don't need level-2 data or volume profile to read order flow. You can see it visually in the candlestick bodies. When bodies stay below consequent encroachment, that is the footprint of smart money. When bodies are above, it shows different intent. This visual reading is as valid as any proprietary tool—don't overcomplicate it. "These are footprints. You want to talk about footprints and level two and you want to go inside the candlestick... none of that stuff is valuable because it's already happened."
- **How to identify:** Look at where candle bodies close relative to consequent encroachment or a key level.
- **How to use:** Validate or invalidate your bias based on body placement. Bearish validation = bodies below consequent encroachment.
- **Example in this video:** EUR/USD: bodies staying below the consequent encroachment wick indicated continuation lower; no need for volume profile or level-2 data.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:15](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=915), [0:16:02](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=962)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT dismisses volume profile complexity; argues visual body placement is sufficient and often misused by traders.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Macro times:** Do not trade ahead of FOMC days; 8:55 ET is a macro time. CPI at 8:30 ET.
- **Range resets:** When a prior low is broken as part of a larger run, that becomes the new range definition; old midpoints and reference levels reset.
- **Body placement rule:** In a bearish move, bodies must stay below consequent encroachment to validate continuation. If bodies close above it, weakness signature is lost.
- **Inversion FVG rules:** (1) Close below midpoint of imbalance on low side, (2) Permission to trade back up into imbalance, (3) Bodies close below midpoint on retest = confirmation, (4) First touch on next break lower = entry.
- **Event horizon partials:** On long downtrends, take 25% minimum off at event horizon (midpoint between two lows).
- **Risk management:** "Managing risk is superior to seeking profit." Interpret when prices are near intermediate or long-term highs/lows; this is more valuable than any entry tool.

- **Bitcoin:** ICT does not trade Bitcoin; his calls are intermediate-term, not intraday. He expects lower prices, targets around 49,577 (intermediate) and potentially much lower long-term; states he expects it to eventually go to zero.

## Glossary candidates

`CONSEQUENT ENCROACHMENT` — The midpoint between two price levels (e.g., a low and a lower low). Used to assess premium/discount and validate body placement in order flow.

`INVERSION FVG` — A fair value gap that forms when price trades below a buy-side imbalance and then retraces back into it with proper body placement, creating a high-probability short entry on the next break lower.

`EVENT HORIZON` — Midpoint between two lows (or two highs); used as a partial exit point or adding-in point in trending markets.

`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — A candlestick with strong wick and body closed below consequent encroachment, showing momentum stall and validating bearish continuation.

`ORDER FLOW` — The directional intent of price, visible in candlestick body placement relative to key levels (especially consequent encroachment). Dismisses the need for level-2 data or volume profile.

`FIRM (DOLLAR)` — The dollar index consolidating or refusing to drop significantly; this allows foreign currencies to weaken without dollar support.

`RANGE RESET` — When price breaks below (or above) a prior structural level, the range redefines and old midpoints/reference levels no longer apply to the new range.

`MACRO TIME` — Specific times when algorithmic activity or news releases (e.g., 8:30 ET for CPI, 8:55 ET) create volatility; ICT advises against trading ahead of FOMC.
