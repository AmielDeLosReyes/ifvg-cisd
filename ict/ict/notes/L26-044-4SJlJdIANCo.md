## Summary
ICT explains one session with a single frame: price opened at 9:30 in the empty space between two daily-chart suspension blocks (a lower gray one and an upper blue one), after a weekly low he believed was in. Because the open is in "open space" between two inefficiencies, he anticipated a fast run into the lowest octant of the upper blue BISI, and shows each step: open above a short-term high, flush into relative equal lows, a wick that cannot reach its own midpoint, order blocks and FVGs that must be anchored to octant/quadrant levels, then a 9:41 ten-candle, ~200-handle run. The new idea is the "time and price grid": horizontal lines are the octants/quadrants of a graded range and vertical lines are macro start/end times (e.g., 9:50–10:10, 10:50–11:10); every time price hits a grid level you look for a PD array to form, and turning points tend to print at macro closes. He then flips to shorts as an inversion FVG fails, then takes the afternoon buys off wicks and iFVGs again. Everything is framed as anticipating, not reacting, with validity tests based on where bodies sit relative to the midpoint.

## Concepts

### Open space between two daily suspension blocks (the "white space" narrative)  (aliases: daily BISI suspension blocks, narrative building)
- **Kind:** rule / bias framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the daily chart there are two BISIs, a blue one above and a gray one below; both are suspension blocks, and between them is a region where price has not overlapped (the "space between two inefficiencies").
  - When price is in a space between two inefficiencies that have been traded back and forth (here price went down into the lower gray one at FOMC, then came back up all night), it is "real easy to forecast and predict" the next move. Bias that morning: weekly low in, and draw up to the lowest octant of the upper blue BISI.
  - He says to anticipate, not react to price; everyone else says the market is random and you must react.
- **How to identify:** two daily BISIs with a gap between them; the day's open sits inside that space (here in its lower portion, between the low of the upper blue block and the high of the lower gray block).
- **How to use:** the nearer daily PD array is the draw; the lowest octant of the blue block was the call. Daily levels continue to anchor the 1m PD arrays (see below).
- **Example in this video:** that day's session on the 1m chart: 9:30 open above a short-term high, up a little, then a 1-minute drop into relative equal lows, a sell-side run at about 9:33, then reversal and rally to the upper block.

### 9:30 open narrative: open above a short-term high, run the relative equal lows, buy the reversal  (aliases: stop hunt, trapping buyers, Power of 3 / Judas)
- **Kind:** entry model (worked example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Opening price at 9:30 was above a short-term high (above the buy-side liquidity) with relative equal lows below. First the buy side is taken above, then price comes back for the relative equal lows. Traders who bought above to catch continuation have stops just under the equal lows, "where the books tell you it's support"; the market dives, knocks them out, then the real direction begins.
  - After taking sell-side (about 9:33), grade the resulting wick (see next concept): price cannot even come down to that wick's midpoint, which signals bullish order flow, then it trades above the previous candle (retail calls that an "engulfing candle"; he does not care because he identified the turn at the wick earlier).
  - He ties this to the origin of his Power of 3 idea: Larry Williams said he wished he knew how people buying near the low of the day got in; ICT took that as a challenge.
- **How to identify:** open above a short-term high; flush through relative equal lows within minutes; wick at the low with no body below the midpoint.
- **How to use:** buy after the sell-side run when bodies hold the upper half of the reversal wick; target is the pre-stated PD array (here the lowest octant of the daily BISI).

### Grading a wick: bodies must hold the upper half when bullish
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Measure a wick with the fib: the high is the candle's close (body top), the low is the wick's extreme. When price is bullish, the upper half of the wick supports the narrative: price can wick down through the midpoint but the body should not breach it, and preferably price does not even touch the midpoint (that is "indicative of bullishness").
  - For a series of wicks, the longest wick sets the tone for where bodies should not trade below; a later wick that goes below an earlier wick's CE and also pierces its low is "the lowest one".
  - A big prominent wick: measure it as soon as the candle closes, wait for price to come back to half, and if bullish the body must not breach it; a small wick through it is permissible.
  - Wick after a large drop: once price trades below the graded wick, any move back up toward it should turn into a wick, not a body (short setup, see iFVG below).
- **How to identify:** fib from body-close extreme to wick extreme; CE at 50%.
- **How to use:** buy when a candle opens and trades down inside the upper half of the graded wick; stop below the consequent encroachment of that wick.
- **Example in this video:** the 9:33 reversal wick at the relative equal lows; later a big wick at about the 10:10 macro; later afternoon wicks used as buys ("you shall not pass" = bodies do not breach the wick CE).
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:20](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=440) grading the first wick · [0:08:56](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=536) upper half supports bullishness · [0:21:48](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1308) big wick after the macro · [0:25:46](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1546) buy setup in the upper half of the wick · [1:03:08](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=3788) afternoon wick CE

### Order block as "opening price to the body"; mean threshold; anchoring to an octant  (aliases: bullish OB, mean threshold)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The order block here is the body of the candle: the algorithm is "reaching into" the opening price down to the body. He draws it from the open to the body.
  - A valid bullish order block inside a bullish FVG: bodies do not violate the halfway point (mean threshold) of the OB, and the block is anchored to the lower octant of the FVG.
  - A candle laying on an octant counts as an order block ("laying on an octant", wick high and open as the sweet spot in a later example).
  - The opening price of the move is marked as the change in state of delivery (CISD) level (caption: he says "that's the change in state of delivery"; he does not elaborate).
- **How to identify:** up-close candle sitting on an octant/quadrant level, bodies in the area stay out of the lower half.
- **How to use:** buy retraces into it; during the macro "price drifts into the order block and sends it higher" with multiple attempts to accumulate.
- **Example in this video:** after the 9:41 run into the upper BISI, price trades back to a lower-octant bullish FVG, forms an OB, bodies stay in the upper half; later, retracement into the same OB again.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:32](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=632) the body is the order block · [0:19:22](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1162) valid bullish OB within a bullish FVG, CISD at the opening price · [1:06:24](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=3984) OB laying on an octant

### PD array validity test: it must anchor to an octant or quadrant level  (aliases: "anchored", "tethered")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - "That's how you know an ICT PD array is valid": the PD array must be anchored to one of the octant or quadrant levels of the range you are grading. Just because something looks like a gap or order block does not make it valid.
  - FVG anchoring detail: candle 1, candle 2 (the inefficiency), candle 3, and the gap is the area between 1 and 3. The gap is valid because candle number 2 touches an octant, or touches a higher low of an inefficiency or whatever range you are measuring and grading.
  - A small volume imbalance BISI in the form of a suspension block is valid because it touches the lower octant.
  - Homework: grade all turns, order blocks, breakers, FVGs and iFVGs this way. Adding market profile, footprint or other overlays dilutes what he teaches ("the algorithm has no respect for all that stuff").
- **How to use:** a daily chart mapped with key PD arrays gives the octants/quadrants; the 1m PD arrays that sit on them are the ones to trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:07](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=967) homework: grade everything · [0:48:08](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2888) candle 2 anchoring rule · [0:48:55](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2935) suspension block anchored to the lower octant
- **Nuance / caveats:** parallels the "tethering rule" in L26-042 (PD arrays must touch a graded level of the time-based range). Here the graded range is a daily BISI/suspension block.

### Low resistance liquidity run conditions vs "efficient buy program"  (aliases: LRLR conditions)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Low resistance liquidity run conditions: when price opens inside open space between two daily inefficiencies it can run quickly with big candles and speed; here it screamed up 10 one-minute candles in a row from 9:41 (about 200 handles in 10 minutes). The narrative (open space) predicts both that it should go up and how it should behave on the way.
  - The overnight move was a different thing: a slow, systematic grind with very short candle ranges building higher and higher ("buy program"), "death by a thousand cuts" for shorts. He labels that efficient price delivery, not an LRLR, and not something to short.
  - The daily suspension block was "controlling" the efficiency of the overnight grind; once the 9:30 open gets outside that control, the run is the LRLR kind.
- **How to identify:** big-bodied candles moving through nothing vs small candle ranges ratcheting.
- **How to use:** do not fade either; use pullbacks into PD arrays inside the run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:32](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=872) low-resistance conditions · [0:15:19](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=919) buy program overnight · [0:18:31](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1111) 9:41 ten-candle run, ~200 handles

### Algorithmic time and price grid  (aliases: "time & price grid", multiplication-table analogy)
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - Horizontal axis of the grid = price levels from grading a range: octants and quadrants of a gap or inefficiency (examples he names: an opening range gap, new week opening gap, new day opening gap, a daily BISI/suspension block). You must know what you are measuring and grading.
  - Vertical axis = time: the start of a macro (e.g., xx:50) and the top of the hour, ending at the macro close (e.g., xx:10). He draws these grid cells (the "multiplication table") and carries them forward to the next macro.
  - Two primary functions: (1) a specific level based on an octant/quadrant; (2) a time window. Every time price gets to an octant or quadrant level, look for a PD array to form; where level meets macro time, turning points are expected.
  - The low of the move formed at the close of the macro; he argues that is not random.
  - He states you can strip any hour down to time and price this way ("I can trade every hour").
  - You still need a bias first: know whether you expect a draw above the high or a retracement to the low (here the low of the daily suspension block), then look for things to time it on the grid.
  - You do not need to trade only inside macro windows; the macro shows when time influences price (a short just ahead of a macro is fine).
- **How to identify:** a graded range, its octant/quadrant lines extended right, plus vertical lines at macro start/end.
- **How to use:** (1) set bias from the daily range, (2) pick the octant/quadrant where you expect the reaction, (3) wait for PD array (FVG, iFVG, OB) on it, (4) trade inside or just before the macro, stops from the array.
- **Example in this video:** 9:50–10:10 macro: lowest candle at 10:10, order block and BISI both anchored to the lowest octant. The "short fractal of the grid": market leaves the macro at 10:10, trades back into the lower-octant BISI and OB. Later the 10:50–11:10 grid: his last candle at 11:10, the macro close, is where the iFVG-led sell-off runs out.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:25](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1645) the 10:50 grid drawn · [0:28:13](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1693) horizontal vs vertical axis · [0:29:04](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1744) two primary functions · [0:29:57](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1797) lowest candle at the macro close · [0:31:31](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1891) know your bias, then time it · [0:42:38](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2558) 11:10 close of the macro

### Inversion FVG: failure of a BISI turns it into a bearish iFVG; short rules  (aliases: first utilization = buy-side delivery)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - A BISI's first utilization is buy-side delivery; in a bullish market it supports price. If price trades down through it and closes outside it (a breakdown close), that validates it as an inversion FVG: bearish.
  - Once a bearish iFVG: bodies need to stay out of the upper half; wicks into it are fine; if price wicks into it you can short it just ahead of the macro (here ahead of the 10:50 macro).
  - If price cannot trade to the next octant or the midpoint of the whole blue area, expect failure; retail sees a bull flag, ICT sees the failure to reach CE with a macro approaching.
  - Below the iFVG, a second iFVG short: price trades down, closes below it, opens and trades up into it; short. Confidence from grading the wick below: once price is beneath it, rallies need to be wicks not bodies.
  - After an iFVG forms, the target was the low of the daily suspension block; price went "right down to the low".
  - Mirror for a bullish iFVG (afternoon): body stays in the lower half of an iFVG when bullish; wicks can go to and past its high but bodies are not allowed in the upper half. Bodies that violate that mean the story is not intact.
  - Blending: a former bearish FVG that price crosses over becomes an iFVG that changes its first utilization to supportive; combined with a bullish FVG, both anchored to the lower quadrant of the daily blue suspension block, price stops dead and rallies.
- **How to use:** short the retest into the iFVG ahead of the macro; stop above the iFVG/wick (not specified numerically); cover at the daily block's low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:34:45](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2085) breakdown close validates the iFVG · [0:35:31](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2131) upper half rule, short before the macro · [0:36:20](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2180) short at the iFVG after the new macro · [0:40:17](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2417) second short, graded wick · [0:55:13](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=3313) bullish iFVG lower-half rule · [1:00:01](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=3601) FVG + iFVG blended

### Learning path: one PD array first, then all of them
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** learn with a single PD array as your entry model, then take time learning the rest; do not take advice from anyone (even someone claiming to be his student) who says to skip the others. At the top tier you know all the public PD arrays and where they sit in price, which gives extra criteria for why price should or should not do something. Backtest and look at hindsight data until you can recognise things; then tape-read live and anticipate "not even taking a trade, not even demo". This conditions you to anticipate rather than react.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Bullish wick/gap: upper half holds; bodies should not touch or breach the midpoint (wicks through are allowed).
- Bearish iFVG: bodies stay out of the upper half; short wicks into it, ideally just ahead of a macro.
- Bullish OB inside a bullish FVG: bodies do not violate the OB's halfway (mean threshold); anchored to the lower octant.
- PD array valid only if it anchors to an octant/quadrant (candle 2 of the FVG touches the level).
- Time grid: macros 9:50–10:10 and 10:50–11:10; horizontal lines = octants/quadrants; look for PD arrays whenever price meets a line; the low of a move printed at the macro close.
- Buy stop: below the CE of the graded wick (for a candle opening into the upper half of that wick).
- 9:33 sell-side run, 9:41 start of a 10-candle, ~200-handle move.
- Example precision: a candle low at 28,431.75 stopped one tick above its level (the level's printed value is garbled).
- Do not fade a "buy program" grind or an LRLR run.

## Glossary candidates
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — a BISI/SIBI carrying volume imbalance(s); here used on the daily chart as a key PD array.
`OPEN SPACE BETWEEN TWO INEFFICIENCIES` — gap with no overlap between two daily BISIs; predicts a fast run in the nearer direction.
`ANCHORED` — a PD array whose middle candle (or body/wick) touches an octant/quadrant level.
`MEAN THRESHOLD` — the midpoint of an order block; bodies must not violate it.
`TIME AND PRICE GRID` — octant/quadrant lines (price) crossed with macro start/end times.
`BUY PROGRAM` — slow ratcheting grind of tiny candles, dangerous to fade.
`LOW RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN` — big-candle run through open space (contrasted here with a buy program).
`INSTITUTIONAL ORDER FLOW ENTRY DRILL` — entry into an FVG created off a key daily level.
`FIRST UTILIZATION` — whether a gap was first used as buy-side or sell-side delivery, which determines how it inverts.
