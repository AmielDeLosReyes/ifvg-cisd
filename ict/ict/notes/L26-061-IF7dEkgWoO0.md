## Concepts

### First Instance of Fair Value Gap (First PD Array in a Series)
- **Kind:** definition · price delivery concept
- **What ICT says:** When a new inefficiency (FVG, suspension block, etc.) forms, it's the "first instance" of a fair value gap in a potential series of FVG breaches. Price will use this first instance as a target/magnet, and if it fails to respect it (closes outside of it), it becomes the first confirmation that price is going further in that direction. "First instance of fair value gap from Tuesday and it's the suspension block... we had a close outside of it right there... there's no gap prior to this one forming." (0:02:23–0:03:10)
- **How to identify:** Identify the most recent inefficiency on the chart. This is your "first instance." Mark it prominently.
- **How to use:** Use the first instance as your initial target. If price respects it (body stays inside), it's neutral. If price closes outside of it decisively, it's confirmation that price will continue past it.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's suspension block is the first instance. On Monday, price trades back into it and closes inside it, confirming continued downward direction. (0:02:23–0:03:10, 0:08:47)

### Close Outside of a PD Array as Confirmation
- **Kind:** rule · order flow signal
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick body that closes outside (below for bearish, above for bullish) a PD array is a confirmation signal. Wicks alone don't count. The body must close fully beyond the array boundary for confirmation. "Make it a little bit bigger. See that candlestick closed below first instance of fair value gap. Okay, there's no gap prior to this one forming." (0:02:23–0:03:10) This is one of the most important order flow signals.
- **How to identify:** Mark the boundary of a key PD array. Observe the candlestick body (not the wick). If the body closes completely outside the boundary, it's confirmation.
- **How to use:** Once the body closes outside, assume the direction and trade with conviction in that direction. Don't wait for more confirmation; the close-outside is your signal.
- **Example in this video:** Bodies close below key FVGs, confirming downward continuation; later, bodies close above other FVGs, confirming bullish reclamation. (0:02:23–0:12:02)
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:04:45](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=285) — "We couldn't close below half of this wick... bodies are... indicative of a change in the direction."

### Chain of Custody (Price Moving from One PD Array to the Next)
- **Kind:** rule · price delivery concept
- **What ICT says:** In a directional run, price moves from one PD array (inefficiency) to the next. Each array is a waypoint. As long as each array fails to reverse price (i.e., no close-outside in the opposite direction), price continues to the next array. This is the "chain"—a linked series of targets. "When we were dropping down we were using it for the purpose of the chain of custody where price moves lower from one PD array to the next and every PD array that would fail and indicate an opposite direction in price... lower prices are in order." (0:05:33)
- **How to identify:** Mark all key inefficiencies (FVGs, order blocks, suspension blocks) in your analysis. These are the links in the chain.
- **How to use:** Use the chain as a roadmap. Trade from one target to the next. If price respects the array (close inside), continue to the next target. If price closes outside (opposite direction), the chain is broken and you must recalibrate.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's inefficiencies link to Monday's targets link to additional targets. Price runs through each in sequence. (0:05:33–0:06:22)
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:05:33](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=333) — "Chain of custody where price moves lower from one PD array to the next."

### Bullish Reclaimed Fair Value Gap (Reconfirmation of First Utilization)
- **Kind:** definition · price delivery concept
- **What ICT says:** When a bullish (or bearish) fair value gap is initially invalidated (price closes the opposite way), then later closes back in the original direction, the original bias is "reclaimed" or "reconfirmed." The array is now a "bullish reclaimed FVG" and goes back to its first utilization. "It's now become a bullish reclaimed fair value gap. And it's picked up its first utilization there and we'll change this to dark green." (0:08:47)
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify an array with a bullish first utilization. (2) Price closes bearish (below it), invalidating it. (3) Price later rallies and closes bullish (above it) again, reclaiming it. That reclamation is the signal.
- **How to use:** Once an array is reclaimed, resume trading it in its original direction. It's now a higher-probability setup because it's been tested and confirmed twice.
- **Example in this video:** The suspension block was initially bullish, got invalidated (close below), then was reclaimed (close above) confirming it goes back to being bullish. (0:08:47–0:09:38)
- **Timestamps:**

### Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill
- **Kind:** definition · entry pattern
- **What ICT says:** A partial entry into a PD array, typically around the consequent encroachment level or before it, that shows smart money accumulating into a level. When this happens without touching consequent encroachment, it signals that smart money is entering early (before the full move) and expecting significant continuation. "Institutional order flow entry drill. A partial entry into... not even consequent encroachment." (0:08:47–0:09:38)
- **How to identify:** Look for a price level where a candle begins to bounce/enter an FVG but doesn't go all the way into it. This is a partial entry zone—a hint that smart money is entering.
- **How to use:** When you see an institutional order flow entry drill, expect price to soon accelerate to the full inefficiency (consequent encroachment or beyond). Use it as confirmation to add to positions.
- **Example in this video:** Marked around 0:08:47–0:09:38 where price enters a range but doesn't reach the full level.
- **Timestamps:**

### Buy-Side and Sell-Side Imbalances/Inefficiencies (Detailed Reference)
- **Kind:** definition (revisited)
- **What ICT says:** Buy-side imbalance (unfilled buy orders) and sell-side inefficiency (the opposite; zone where buyers were swept/stopped) form together as a single composite array. When price moves past one, traders in that direction get invalidated. "We have buy side and balance sell side inefficiency after creating a close above that wick." (0:04:45) The terminology is sometimes used interchangeably with FVG but refers to the specific order flow structure.
- **How to use:** Treat as a composite PD array. The wick high or low of this structure is the key level to respect.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple references to "buy side and balance sell side inefficiency" structures. (0:04:45–0:06:22)

### Sniper Mentality (Waiting vs. Chasing)
- **Kind:** rule · psychology · execution approach
- **What ICT says:** Don't chase price or actively pursue it. Instead, set up your entry zone (your "hide"), wait for price to "walk right into your crosshairs," and then execute. This is the opposite of chasing. "We're sitting still. We're trying to get to a vantage point where we can see the mark walk right into our crosshairs and we're not chasing it. We're not pursuing it... we're not reacting. We're anticipating." (0:16:02)
- **How to use:** Before each session, identify your target entry zones based on your model. Don't enter until price reaches those zones. If price runs past them without touching, let it go—don't chase. Wait for the next setup.

### Toothpick Support (Weak Support)
- **Kind:** analogy · technical observation
- **What ICT says:** When a large amount of price action is supported by a single thin wick or small candlestick body, that support is weak—like a building held up by a toothpick. This is a warning that price may reverse or fall through. "See all this? We have sell side resting right below here, and we have this single toothpick one little chopstick holding up all this price action." (0:13:36) It's unstable.
- **How to identify:** Look for a large range of price bars above a very thin/small body or wick. That's the toothpick.
- **How to use:** Recognize toothpick support as fragile. When you see it, be prepared for a sudden breakdown or reversal. Don't go long into toothpick-supported moves; instead, short below them.
- **Example in this video:** Marked around 0:13:36. Large block of price action supported by one thin structure.
- **Timestamps:**

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- First instance of FVG must be respected initially; if price closes outside of it decisively (body, not wick), the opposite direction is confirmed.
- Use close-outside as your primary confirmation signal; don't wait for additional signals.
- Price runs from one PD array to the next (chain of custody); respect each waypoint.
- Reclaimed arrays (invalidated then reconfirmed) have higher probability because they're double-tested.
- Institutional order flow entry drills (partial entries without reaching consequent encroachment) signal imminent acceleration.
- Toothpick support (large price action on thin candles) is unstable; expect breakdown.
- On news days (CPI, PPI, NFP, FOMC), use sniper mentality: set zones in advance and wait for price to enter them; don't chase.
- Don't ask "what's next?"; forecast it yourself and compare with ICT's analysis to self-grade.

## Glossary Candidates

`First Instance of Fair Value Gap` — The most recent/initial inefficiency in a series of potential breaches. Used as a waypoint to confirm if price is continuing in a direction (close outside = confirmation) or reversing.

`Close Outside of PD Array` — A candlestick body that closes fully beyond the boundary of a PD array. The most important order flow confirmation signal; wicks don't count.

`Chain of Custody` — A series of linked PD arrays that price walks through sequentially. Each array is a waypoint; if price respects it (close inside), the chain continues to the next array.

`Bullish Reclaimed Fair Value Gap` — An array that was initially bullish, got invalidated (close bearish), then is reconfirmed (close bullish). Double-tested and now higher-probability.

`Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill` — A partial entry into a PD array before reaching its full level (consequent encroachment), signaling smart money accumulation.

`Toothpick Support` — A large block of price action supported by a single thin wick or small candle body; unstable and likely to break.

`Sniper Mentality` — Pre-planned entry zones set in advance; wait for price to "walk into your crosshairs" rather than chasing it. Anticipate, don't react.

`Reclaimed Array / Reconfirmation` — When a bearish-invalidated bullish array closes bullish again, the original bias is reclaimed and reasserts as high-probability.

`Gap Closure (New Week Opening Gap)` — Closing the gap between Friday's close and the following week's open; a target that price eventually reaches.
