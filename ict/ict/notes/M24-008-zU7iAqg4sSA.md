Lecture #8 was streamed late after an OBS failure. It is taught live on NQ (1m, with 15m and 15s) on Wednesday Aug 14, 2024, which was a high-impact 8:30 news day. ICT teaches that every wick (especially on a swing high/low or a news candle) is an inefficiency: measure it from the candle's open to its extreme, mark its consequent encroachment (50%) and quadrants, and treat price's behaviour at that level (does it reach it? do the bodies close beyond it?) as a "tell". He then defines the change in the state of delivery (CISD) as price trading through the opening price of an order block built from ALL the consecutive up-closed (or down-closed) candles, and says there are only two forms of "turtle soup": a reversal that changes the direction of delivery, and an entry technique inside an existing buy/sell program (every FVG entry is one). ## Concepts

### Wick as an inefficiency: measure it and use its consequent encroachment (CE)  (aliases: "wick CE", "midpoint of the wick", "quadrants of the wick", "the tell")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement (ICT says mentorship students never got this; he claims nobody else has ever taught it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Every time there is a wick, especially on a swing high or swing low that matters, measure it and mark its midpoint. Measure from the very highest high / lowest low (the extreme), using the real wick tip, not the body. He keeps candle borders switched on so the wick tips plot accurately (without borders the levels are not technically exact).
  - He treats the wick as a gap even though it printed inside a single candle. Because it is a gap, its midpoint is called consequent encroachment, not mean threshold (mean threshold is an order-block term). How long the candle took to print the wick does not matter, only the visual range from the candle's open to the extreme.
  - Long wicks: get the exact number (he draws a Fibonacci on the candle and uses the 50% level, then deletes the Fib; he scolds students who leave Fibs cluttering the chart). Short wicks: eyeball it.
  - The wick range is inefficient (the 8:30 news candle fell "over 100 handles" and printed back up inside the same one-minute candle). Price tends to return to that wick's CE, as a magnet.
  - A wick above price is a premium array, a wick below price is a discount array. If a wick sits above price and you are bearish, price should trade up into the wick's CE and the bodies should stop at or below it.
  - Wicks have quadrants too: CE (50%) and the lower/upper quadrant. In the first wick example he also points to a "3/4 level" as an entry-pattern / partial / Terminus level (caption unclear: it may just be the wick CE level he had marked). (He insists this is not "quarters theory", which he calls nonsense and asks people to stop asking about.)
  - Works on every time frame, down to sub-1-minute charts. "Price is price"; a 1m candle's open/high/low/close behaves like a daily candle's.
- **How to identify:** a candle with a visible wick at an important swing high/low or a big news candle. Draw from open to extreme, mark 50% and the quadrants.
- **How to use:**
  - Anticipation: after a wick forms, expect price to return to its CE, and to explore its lower quadrant if the bodies fail to hold.
  - Tell: when price returns to the wick, watch the bodies. If bodies do not close above the wick CE (bearish case) and the next candle makes a lower high, that is the algorithm "tipping its hand" and it is an entry (see Order block section for stop placement).
  - Failure: if a PD array (FVG, wick CE) cannot even be retraced to while you are directionally biased, the market is "decidedly weak" in that direction.
  - Confluence stacking: wick CE + FVG CE + 6 PM opening price + prior candle high are layered levels, and the more of them agree the stronger the expectation.
  - Even if no other level existed, a candle that runs up and stops short of the wick CE tells him the next candle will want to trade into that candle's body, which then acts as a bearish order block (trade it before others see it).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 14, 2024. The 8:30 news candle opened, dropped over 100 handles in seconds and came back to close up in its body (long lower wick). ICT marked the wick's CE and the lower quadrant. Price (after running up into the July 28 NWOG and reversing) traded down to the wick CE, held there with the bodies, and later explored toward the lower quadrant/low. A wick's CE (captioned 19,965, unreliable) coincided "to the tick" with the high of a candle on the 15m chart, which he used as a retracement ceiling. Price pushed into an FVG whose CE it failed to reach (stopped about a tick short), and the bodies did not close above a wick CE, then it dropped.

  - Price can wick through the CE; what matters is where the bodies stop.

### Buy-side delivery vs. sell-side delivery (algorithmic programs) — not buy/sell-side liquidity  (aliases: "sell program / buy program", "buy side of the curve / sell side of the curve")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sell-side delivery is the algorithm delivering lower prices ("a sell program"), buy-side delivery is delivering higher prices. It is NOT sell-side liquidity (resting sell stops). Do not think buying/selling pressure; it spools in a direction "for the express purpose of delivering" prices.
  - Sell-side delivery means the market is going lower to reprice inefficiencies (FVGs, wick CEs) and then the liquidity below; it matters not how much buying/selling pressure there is.
  - While in a sell program, retracements are short-lived, controlled, "stunted", never protracted to the upside.
  - He ties this to the Market Maker Buy/Sell Models (2017 mentorship): the "buy side of the curve" is the run up; the "sell side of the curve" is the run down. He says everything he trades is part of an MMSM or MMBM even when he does not name it.
- **How to identify:** which way the algorithm is currently offering prices (a sequence of up-closed displacement and old highs being taken = buy-side delivery). See CISD for the switch.
- **How to use:** in a sell program, look for repricing to inefficiencies: bearish FVGs (sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency), wick CEs, order blocks and breakers on the way down, and go to the sell-side liquidity below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:17](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2537) — sell-side delivery is not sell-side liquidity · [1:38:03](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5883) — sell program reprices to inefficiencies · [2:21:17](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8477) — MM buy/sell models, buy side of the curve
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In his words the algorithm "doesn't care how many people are buying or selling". This is his core argument against order flow / depth-of-market.

### Change in the State of Delivery (CISD) at the opening price of the order block  (aliases: "change in state of delivery", "the opening price", order block validation)
- **Kind:** definition + entry trigger
- **What ICT says:**
  - The order block is not just the last up-closed candle. For a bearish order block, take ALL the consecutive up-closed candles that make up the run into the high (two candles in this example). The opening price of that up-closed series (the open of its first candle) is the change in the state of delivery level.
  - When price trades below that opening price (he does not require a close below), the delivery flips from buy-side to sell-side and the order block is validated. "If it's multiple candles, just find the opening price of the up-close candles."
  - Using only the last up-closed candle is called a trap ("white candle / buy demand" thinking).
  - The wicks that overshoot are "colouring outside the lines", ignore them and focus on bodies. Measure the mean threshold of the bodies of all those candles for the order block's midpoint. Price can poke a little above the OB range and it still counts.
  - He separates it from MSS: trading below the short-term low and closing there gives a shift in market structure, but before that the order block is validated by trading below its open, which is the CISD. He also says he does not need to see a close below the OB's open for the flip (caption somewhat unclear, 1:26:38).
  - Once flipped, every premium array that forms in the new sell program matters (new short-term highs, new bearish up-closed candles, new FVGs).
- **How to identify:** (1) buy-side delivery has been running; (2) liquidity above (highs) is taken, ideally into a known objective; (3) a series of up-closed candles forms the top; (4) price trades through the open of that series.
- **How to use:** after the CISD, expect sell-side delivery. Look for entries on retracements into the new premium arrays (OB, FVG, wick CE), and targets at inefficiencies/discount arrays below, then sell-side liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 14, 2024 (after the 9:30 open; exact time not stated). The day before he had pointed to the Jul 28, 2024 NWOG (plus the daily short-term high and daily volume imbalance) as an upside objective. Price created relative equal highs, failed to reach the NWOG on the 8:30 news run, then after the open ran into the NWOG, swept the highs, formed a bearish order block of two up-closed candles (a breaker-type high/low/higher-high structure), then traded below that OB's open. Price then fell to the CE of the 8:30 wick, retraced up into an FVG and stopped about one tick under its midpoint (caption unclear), and kept going down.
- **Timestamps:** [1:18:32](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4712) — CISD explained at the order block · [1:24:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5056) — buy-side delivery to sell-side delivery · [1:27:26](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5246) — opening price = CISD, mean threshold · [1:29:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5393) — all consecutive up-closed candles form the OB · [2:18:54](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8334) — "just find the opening price"

### Order block (bearish) — range, trading it early, and stop placement  (aliases: bearish order block, "mile marker", "exit ramp")
- **Kind:** definition + entry/stop rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The OB range = the bodies of the consecutive up-closed candles before the sell-side delivery. If an FVG sits inside the OB's range, label and trade the OB's range, not the small FVG (1:56:33).
  - He says the algorithm "gives a mile marker" at the OB: it is where your eye should go if you are not yet in the trade.
  - Early entry: if price runs up and stops short of a wick CE, he says the next candle that opens and trades into the body of that candle can be entered before anyone calls it an order block ("I'm trading inside the candle that will later be obvious in hindsight").
  - Stop: above the order block, AND encapsulating the premium wick above it: the stop goes just above the highest point of the wick + 1 tick. If a spike goes through and wicks up toward half of that wick, or falls short of it, and you are stopped out, he re-enters short there.
  - He warns order blocks are not always protected; price sometimes colours just outside the lines and the narrative still stands.
- **How to identify:** consecutive up-closed candle bodies at the top of a buy-side run that ended with a CISD.
- **How to use:** short on retracement into the OB range (bodies/mean threshold); stop above the OB and its premium wick +1 tick; target the inefficiencies and lows below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 14, 2024: price retraced up to a bearish OB (below the NWOG high) after the CISD, with the 8:30 wick CE as the retracement low; stop would be one tick above the premium wick above the OB.
- **Timestamps:** [3:08:42](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11322) — "where does your stop go?" quiz on the bearish OB · [3:11:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11468) — stop above the OB's premium wick +1 tick, re-enter if stopped · [2:34:43](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9283) — trading inside the candle before it is "an order block"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Clarifies the usual "last up-closed candle" teaching: he says to include ALL consecutive up-closed candles and warns that people copy only the last candle.

### Turtle soup — only two forms: reversal (change in direction) and in-trend entry technique  (aliases: "Turtle Soup", "run on liquidity", "stop run")
- **Kind:** definition / new refinement (explicitly distinguished from the Street Smarts pattern)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Turtle soup = a run on liquidity that is not always above an old high or below an old low (he says the name was borrowed because he liked it from Connors/Raschke's Street Smarts, which taught him the stop-run idea; he has used the concept since 1995).
  - There are only two forms: buying below old lows or selling above old highs, either (1) as a reversal that changes the direction of delivery (CISD, sustained for a session/day or longer), or (2) as an entry mechanism inside an existing, still-unfolding sell or buy program.
  - Form 1: take out the buy stops above old highs (bodies as well as wicks), hit an objective (e.g., a NWOG or NDOG as a magnet), then CISD. The order block / breaker is part of that reversal ("breakers tend to be turtle soups in changing directional sustained price runs").
  - Form 2: every time you enter a short in a bearish FVG you are selling above an old high (the candle high marking the FVG's edge); every long in a bullish FVG is buying below an old low. It is not support and resistance but an absence of buyers who ever were afforded the chance above that candle's high.
  - Street Smarts version (for contrast): fade a break of the 20-day high/low (and "plus one" = one more day), with a very short profit objective; he calls it infantile, says he does not trade it, and recalls the turtle-trader style strike rate as under about 38%.
  - Harder variant: selling into a rally, a bold-faced bullish candle above short-term highs into a premium array. Takes much more experience and complementary PD arrays; do not start here.
- **How to identify:** (reversal) high swept + objective reached + CISD; (entry) retracement into an FVG in a program that already started.
- **How to use:** form 1: sell above the highs only after the objective and after delivery flips; form 2: put the limit order at the old high + 1 tick (see FVG entry section).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 14, 2024: running above short-term highs / relative equal highs into the Jul 28 NWOG, then CISD at the OB open (form 1); after that, shorting into the FVG that formed in the sell leg (form 2). He adds that if price had reached the NWOG on the 8:30 first leg, then poked above again at 9:30 and failed, that second poke would have been the turtle soup.
- **Timestamps:** [1:10:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4228) — turtle soup is anticipating turning points, not always above old highs · [2:22:04](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8524) — reversal vs. entry mechanism · [2:24:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8667) — Street Smarts 20-day version contrasted · [2:46:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9965) — trading an FVG = trading a turtle soup · [3:33:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=12807) — "only two forms of turtle soup"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Narrows/clarifies earlier use of the term. He calls the Street Smarts version the "wish version"/"street-smarts version" and says Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' book helped only to introduce the idea of stop runs.

### FVG entry model for a short (and the mirror for longs): entry, scale-in, stop, and "permissible" fills  (aliases: "lowest barrier to entry", "lowest threshold of entry", "pyramiding", turtle soup entry)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Entry: the lowest barrier to entry is the edge of the FVG closest to current price. For a bearish FVG that is the old candle high at the bottom of the gap (the high of candle 3, inference); put the limit order at that high + 1 tick. On the morning's first retracement price came within a tick of the FVG and turned, so even a limit one tick above the old candle high was not filled (he says so at 2:16:31; the caption at 0:11:16 says "would have been filled" but the context, "punishment for trying to be too perfect", points to not filled). His lesson: demanding perfection costs fills, so have fallback entries (wick CE, next array, price delivery continuum) and know that missing a fill still means you read direction correctly.
  - Long version (mirror): buy the high of the bullish FVG or one tick below its highest part, with the largest portion there. Add at the midpoint (CE), and add again if price leaves and retests the CE. Fills at/below the CE are rare, though they are the best entries when they occur.
  - His own scale (as a sample, not a recommendation for beginners): first 6 contracts at the edge (+1 tick), then 4 more at the midpoint, 10 total. He explicitly says Caleb (the beginner) should not trade 10 contracts and should only watch and observe.
  - Stop: above the 3-candle FVG pattern, specifically too far if price trades above candle 1's high (the first candle of the pattern). Caption unclear on the exact candle; context says the entire FVG plus the premium wick is "too far".
  - Tight stop (advanced): if candle 1 has a wick (a premium array above the gap), take the CE of that wick + 2 ticks as the stop. Reasoning: candle 2 opens and trades down through candle 1's low, so the overlap forms a balanced price range; price should not trade back through the whole range to the top, and if it did it would probably go higher and he would not want to be short anyway. So he can put the stop just above the wick's CE.
  - FVG naming: the inefficiency is always candle 2; a down-closed candle 2 is a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency (SIBI), an up-closed candle 2 is buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (BISI).
- **How to identify:** FVG that formed during the sell program (the most important ones) and that converges with arrays from the buy side of the curve (see Inversion).
- **How to use:** limit short at the old high + 1 tick, add at CE, stop at candle-1 high (or wick CE + 2 ticks); scale partials at the PD arrays below; see targets section.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m after the CISD: an FVG formed in the drop and was later retraced. First retracement: price stopped about a tick short of the FVG and fell away, so the limit at the edge + 1 tick was not filled (ICT, 2:16:31). Later price did trade up into another FVG that formed in the drop and the bodies stopped there. The tight stop is illustrated with the wick of candle 1.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=676) — "punishment for trying to be too perfect" · [2:11:46](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7906) — buy at the FVG high, add at CE · [2:50:57](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10257) — 6 then 4 contracts at the edge and midpoint · [3:00:37](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10837) — entry/stop drawn · [3:04:43](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11083) — wick CE + 2 ticks stop

### How the FVG should behave: upper half stays open, speed and distance
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you are short and price retraces into a bearish FVG, it is "permissible" (allowed, does not change direction) for it to fill entirely, but it is better and signals strong weakness if price reaches only the midpoint or less and leaves the upper half open (bullish mirror: lower half). Failing to fill means the market is "weak" and lower targets are coming.
  - Two circumstances make large-range candles: (a) the gap leaves a portion open, then the market must accelerate away; (b) it fills the gap entirely, then it must immediately show speed and distance, large-range candles away from it. Absent that, do not stay.
  - Same logic for arrays above price while you are short and still in a trade: keep some FVGs above the target open, which shows the market can't even retrace up to them and fill them in.
  - After price takes out the low (new range low), take a partial there even for one contract, then watch how price behaves around the order block and range.
- **How to use:** demand an immediate response, once price touches the FVG's low. If it leaves part of the gap open you can anticipate fast speed and big range going lower. Observation drill: do this for months as tape reading, with no demo orders ("start with the price level + 1 tick; if it trades there, hypothetically you are in").
- **Timestamps:** [1:52:31](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=6751) — want FVGs above target to stay open when short · [2:51:48](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10308) — "permissible" vs. better if only half filled · [3:02:13](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10933) — demand immediate response, speed and distance · [3:06:20](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11180) — the only two circumstances for large-range candles

### Balanced Price Range (BPR) as the real range of an FVG and the "Mohawk"  (aliases: "mohawk", "coloring outside the lines")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement (ICT says it is the first time he is introducing this PD array)
- **What ICT says:**
  - When one candle's wick overlaps another candle's body (or a candle opens and trades down a little into the previous candle's range) inside a gap, the overlap is a balanced price range, and the BPR is the real range of the inefficiency, which you have to encapsulate rather than just drawing the FVG on the standard three candles. He says this is a PD array students "have never learned before".
  - Example logic: a candle runs up and is all body at the top, the next candle opens and makes a flush tap down, overlapping a little portion with no wick. That overlap is the BPR, not the plain FVG. Price then reacts at that BPR boundary instead of filling the FVG fully ("you will not pass").
  - A "mohawk" is the tiny spike outside a PD array boundary (a candle poking a few ticks beyond a level before turning). He says about 90% of mohawks outside a PD array are predictable but he won't teach that.
  - Do not confuse with volume imbalance: a volume imbalance is a separation between two candle bodies inside an FVG; he notes that is absent here.
- **How to identify:** inside an FVG, look for overlap between one candle's wick/body and the next candle's range.
- **How to use:** draw the BPR as the entry/stop boundary: "we never want to see price go back through the whole BPR to the top".
- **Timestamps:** [2:02:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7373) — BPR from the overlap of a wick and a body, "mohawk" · [2:03:41](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7421) — 90% of mohawks predictable; won't teach · [2:30:01](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9001) — BPR introduced as a new PD array
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Differs from the 2022 BPR definition (overlap of a SIBI and a BISI). Here it is the overlap of wick/body of adjacent candles around an FVG. Caption/description is thin and chart-dependent; treat as "(caption unclear)".

### Inversion logic: arrays used as support on the buy side flip to resistance in the sell program; iFVG caveat  (aliases: inversion, inversion FVG, "reverse its role")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the CISD, go back through the whole run from the high to the low and see which arrays acted as discount/support on the way up (gaps, down-closed candles/order blocks, FVGs). They reverse role and become inversion levels: resistance for the retracements in the sell program. "That is how I know which PD array to use as an inversion level."
  - An order block that was used as a buy extends to the right as resistance once price has gone below it. An FVG extended to the right, with its CE marked, is an inversion FVG, and the same logic applies: you want it not to fill entirely (upper half stays open when short).
  - Not every FVG becomes an inversion FVG (a jab at an over-eager young trader who implies he invented the idea).
  - Every new array formed in the sell program is salient, and weight builds when it converges with an array from the buy side of the curve; expect price to stay heavy and ask for speed and distance.
  - This is how he applies the Market Maker Sell/Buy Models: extend arrays back through the range.
- **How to use:** list the buy-side arrays after the CISD, extend them forward, treat them as the first resistance levels, enter shorts there with the standard FVG/OB entry rules.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Consistent with his 2023 iFVG teaching; adds the "converge with the buy-side arrays" weighting.

### Price Delivery Continuum Theory  (aliases: price delivery continuum, following the delivery chain, joining a run you missed)
- **Kind:** new coinage (ICT says it is "brand new stuff" and mentorship students did not learn it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you place a precise limit order (e.g., at the FVG edge) and price runs away without filling, do not abandon the idea. The delivery is underway and a finite target exists, so keep looking for the next specific price level, a new PD array formed by the unfolding move (a new short-term high, a new up-closed candle/OB, a new FVG), and enter there.
  - He says he developed it because as a young trader he would miss his single setup, get angry and stop looking, though a big move was still available.
  - It requires a finite price (entry, stop, partial, terminus); if he does not know the specific price he does not trade.
- **How to use:** after the initial miss, wait for retracements into the newly-formed arrays; use the lowest-barrier edge entry; keep the same stop logic.
- **Example in this video:** after the missed FVG fill, price came back up into the body of an up-closed candle and traded to the CE of the 8:30 wick without taking out that candle's high, which he says could have been the entry.
- **Timestamps:** [0:45:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2728) — missed fills led him to develop it · [0:47:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2873) — defined: anticipate a move to a finite target · [1:59:41](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7181) — "do you say forget it? no" (continuing to look)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Also relates to the IOFED label below. He stresses entries are easy and finite; the hard part is knowing where the market is going.

### 6 PM opening price, NDOG and NWOG as reference levels  (aliases: new session opening price, new day opening gap, new week opening gap)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - If there is no difference between the 5:00 PM close/settlement and the 6:00 PM first print, there is no gap, so you draw one single line at the 6 PM opening price. Use the 6 PM price (the first print), not the 5 PM. It is a single price level, not a range or inefficiency. It can be swept and revisited, and works like a new day opening gap.
  - If there is a difference, annotate the NDOG (the 5 PM-to-6 PM gap).
  - Baseline for charts: last five weeks of NWOGs and last five days of NDOGs. More if you can keep the chart tidy.
  - Old/filled gaps still act as magnets: a NWOG from Jul 28, 2024 that had been traded through was used as the upside objective on Aug 14 ("filled, cast it out, no, it's one and done: no it's not").

- **How to use:** mark them before the session, use as objectives/retracement targets, and as the first target of a retracement after a CISD.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 14: price went back up to the 6 PM opening line during the retracement, the same area as an FVG and the CE of a wick.

### Targets and partials: PD arrays on the way, then the quarter-range projection beyond the low  (aliases: "negative .25", "one quarter of the range projection", rejection block partial, Terminus)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Partials/terminus can be taken at any PD array below: a rejection block (a candle's close level below the bodies), the CE of the wick, the lower quadrant, the old low. You do not need to hold for the whole run, and not holding to the lowest low does not mean you are bad at trading.
  - If you want to go for a target below an old low, a "low-hanging fruit" projection: take the range (the wick range, or the range from the high down to the low) and project one quarter of that range below the low (a Fibonacci extension of negative .25). That is a decent ballpark, not the exact low.
  - For tighter precision he points to his standard-deviation lessons in the 2017 mentorship playlists (not recalled exactly).
  - Targets are finite: entries and targets are easy, knowing where price is going is the hard part.
  - Whichever PD array "makes the most sense to you" first is your starting point; do nothing when it is not there until you learn more.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Aug 14: first target was the CE of the 8:30 wick, then the lower quadrant and the low, using the closing price of the 8:30 candle as a rejection-block partial; the quarter-range projection was drawn from the high-to-low range and wick (about a quarter below the low).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Mark the wick range from the open to the extreme, put CE at 50%, and also the quadrants. Never leave Fibs on the chart.
- Wick above price = premium array; below = discount array. If it cannot reach the CE in the direction of your bias, expect weakness.
- CISD = price trades through the opening price of the consecutive up-closed (or down-closed) candle series forming the order block; no close required.
- Order block = all the consecutive candles, mean threshold of bodies; if an FVG is inside the OB range, use the OB range.
- Stop for a short at an OB: above the OB and its wick, +1 tick; if stopped but the thesis holds, re-enter.
- Only two forms of turtle soup: reversal (direction change) and in-trend FVG entry.
- Short limit order: old candle high (FVG's lower edge) + 1 tick; add at the FVG midpoint; buy-side mirror: FVG high or 1 tick below.
- Sample sizing: 6 contracts at the edge + 4 at CE = 10 (ICT's own, not for beginners).
- FVG stop: not above candle 1's high; tight stop = CE of candle 1's wick + 2 ticks.
- FVG fill behavior: upper half (short) / lower half (long) staying open = weak; full fill is allowed but needs immediate speed and distance.
- Projection target: one quarter of the range beyond the low (-0.25).
- 6 PM line if no gap; NDOG for 5 days, NWOG for 5 weeks as baseline.
- Do not trade at the time of high-impact news (8:30 CPI/PPI/FOMC/NFP); on NFP wait 15-30 minutes. Opening range = 9:30-10:00 ET.
- Max risk 1% per execution when Caleb starts trading; one contract.
- Do not use zones; use specific prices for entry, stop, partial, terminus.
- Expect to be less than 50% accurate in the first couple of years and still profitable (a "huge Olympic win").

## Glossary candidates
`CISD / change in the state of delivery` — price trading through the opening price of the order-block candle series, flipping buy-side delivery to sell-side delivery (or vice versa).
`Buy-side / sell-side delivery` — the algorithm's direction of price delivery (a "program"), distinct from buy/sell-side liquidity.
`Wick CE` — the 50% level of a wick, measured from the candle's open to its extreme; acts as a premium/discount array.
`Mohawk` — a tiny spike just outside a PD array boundary before price turns.
`Balanced price range (this lecture)` — the overlap of one candle's wick/body with the next candle's range inside an FVG; the real range of the inefficiency.
`Price Delivery Continuum Theory` — keep taking successive new PD arrays in a delivery already underway if a first entry was missed.
`Ring high / ring low` — old name for swing high/low from hand-drawn charts.
`Lowest barrier to entry` — entering at the nearest edge of the FVG (old candle high +1 tick for shorts) rather than the CE.
`Permissible` — an allowed event (e.g., an FVG filling fully) that doesn't change direction.
`Turtle soup (ICT)` — a liquidity run used either as a reversal (CISD) or as an in-trend entry (FVG).
`Premium wick` — a wick above price (or above an OB/FVG) that is a premium array and drives the stop.
`Buy side of the curve / sell side of the curve` — MMBM/MMSM legs of the run up and the run down.
