## Summary
Part 4 of the "How New Students Should Start" week. ICT gives new students three daily logging "pillars" to study on one market (NASDAQ / NQ) instead of trying to consume his whole library. Pillar 1 is the pre-market 7:00–9:00 a.m. ET window: its highest high, lowest low, relative equal highs/lows, and whether it trended or consolidated. Pillar 2 is the RTH opening range gap (4:14 p.m. close to 9:30 open) and how fast price delivers to its consequent encroachment. Pillar 3 is the Silver Bullet: the first fair value gap after 10:00 a.m. ET on a 1-minute chart. Introduce one pillar per two weeks (all three by weeks 5–6), take three screenshots a day with notes, review them on weekends, go back six weeks and walk forward six weeks for 12 weeks of data. The point is pattern recognition by repetition, not backtesting yet (that is the next video).

## Concepts
### The three-pillar daily logging routine  (aliases ICT uses: "logging key phenomenon daily", "three primary pillars", "daily log entries")
- **Kind:** rule / study method
- **What ICT says:** Do not try to learn everything fast; new students who rush learn nothing. Start with three things, but do not look for all three at once. Weeks 1–2: pick one pillar. Weeks 3–4: add a second. Weeks 5–6: add the third. After that make it a steady diet, logged every single day. A more advanced student who has never done this can do all three from the start. Without this, he says you will not be consistent and will hop from one thing to the next. He says people who call his material over-complicated are trying to discourage you.
- **How to use:** Three screenshots per day (one per pillar), chart centered and zoomed so that the highest high to lowest low fill the screen, leaving empty space to write observations. Takes about 20 minutes a day for all three. Review all screenshots on Saturday or Sunday when the market is not moving, because you will see details you missed live. Do it on one market only, NASDAQ, even if you will trade something else (so that his NASDAQ references will resonate). Go back six weeks of old data and do the same, then walk forward six weeks: 12 weeks of data in total. Start day 1 "next Tuesday" after the US Labor Day Monday. This is logging only, not backtesting; the next video covers backtesting.

### Pillar 1 — Pre-market session (7:00–9:00 a.m. ET) range and character  (aliases: "pre-market hours phase", "relative equal lows / relative equal highs", "highest high and lowest low")
- **Kind:** rule / study method
- **What ICT says:** Every trading day except Sunday has a pre-market window of 7:00 a.m. to 9:00 a.m. Eastern. Draw a vertical line at 7:00 and one at 9:00, and mark the highest high and lowest low between them (draw a box around the range). Also mark any relative equal highs/lows inside it. These are the specific pools of liquidity for the day; it removes the ambiguity of "which high or low matters". Then note whether price between 7 and 9 is trending (small range vs one-way movement; could be up or down) or consolidating (sideways). For a brand-new student, reduce it to trending or consolidating. A more advanced alternative is the four phases/conditions of the market (expansion, reversal, consolidation, etc.) from the first video of the 2016 paid mentorship playlist, not necessary here.
- **How to identify:** (1) lines at 7:00 and 9:00; (2) highest high and lowest low in between; (3) relative equal highs/lows; (4) character: trending or consolidating.
- **How to use:** The 7–9 character is a precursor ("crystal ball") for how the market tends to book after 9:30 ET: expansion/trend or chop. He says "it's not a panacea" and will not work every day. The stated example is a trend between 7 and 9 that was followed by a small expansion at 9:30 and then consolidation the rest of the day. (The rule for what a consolidating pre-market implies is cut off in the captions: caption unclear.) The simplest trade shown: after price trades below the relative equal lows (a sweep), aim for the opposite end of the 7–9 range (the high); hitting it is a "bread-and-butter" setup and "you're done for the day".
- **How to log:** The screenshot, plus notes on: the phase/condition, the raw numbers (highest high, lowest low, relative equal levels), how those levels were traded to (if at all), and the time elapsed from 9:30 until price reacted off a key liquidity level. ICT himself logs these by hand in leather journals (not electronically) and says he has done this every day for about 34 years.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart. Price trended up in the 7–9 window from a low to a high. A pair of relative equal lows formed in the window. After 9:30 price swept below the equal lows and then rallied to the 7–9 high ("hit it right here"). A little expansion at 9:30, then consolidation for the rest of the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:12](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=312) — pillar 1 introduced; [0:06:03](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=363) — 7:00 and 9:00 vertical lines, highest high/lowest low; [0:07:44](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=464) — trending vs consolidating (and 2016 four conditions); [0:10:08](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=608) — "crystal ball" for post-9:30; [0:10:55](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=655) — sweep of equal lows then run to opposite end of 7–9 range; [0:11:44](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=704) — how ICT journals it by hand.

### Pillar 2 — RTH opening range gap and consequent encroachment delivery  (aliases: "regular trading hours opening range gap", "RTH ORG", "CE", "middle of the inefficiency")
- **Kind:** rule / study method
- **What ICT says:** On TradingView, use a 1-minute chart and switch the lower-right toggle from ETH (electronic trading hours) to RTH. The gap is between the previous day's 4:14 p.m. ET final print (the close of that 1-minute candle) and the 9:30 a.m. ET opening price. Draw a rectangle across the gap with the "mid level" option turned on (set it in the favorites) so the middle (consequent encroachment, CE) is shown. Log how price pulls back to half the gap and how quickly. In the example it does it almost immediately (the 9:31 candle open trades right down to it, "second minute after 9:30"). He estimates roughly a 70% likelihood it reaches the CE by 10:00 a.m. but says "don't hold me to that": keep daily records and calculate your own statistics at year-end.
- **How to identify:** RTH-only view; previous 4:14 p.m. close; 9:30 open; rectangle with mid-line.
- **How to use:** Anchor a target at the CE. If looking for lower prices, take any old fair value gap or bearish order block (once you learn them) and target the CE; no need to expect the whole gap to close. Annotate the screenshot with observations such as "third time this week it went to the RTH ORG CE by 10 o'clock", and the entry idea (e.g. an inversion fair value gap higher up selling down to the CE).
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart; the gap lies below the open (a gap-down); price opens at 9:30 and by 9:31 trades down to the CE; short idea from an inversion FVG up top to the CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:33](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=753) — pillar 2 introduced; [0:13:22](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=802) — ETH/RTH toggle, 4:14 p.m. final print; [0:14:13](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=853) — rectangle with mid level; [0:15:50](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=950) — pullback to half the gap, 70% by 10:00 claim; [0:16:40](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1000) — anchor a target, annotate observations.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The 4:14 p.m. time and the "70%" are his stated figures; the 70% is explicitly not to be trusted without your own stats.

### Pillar 3 — Silver Bullet first presented FVG after 10:00 a.m. ET  (aliases: "Silver Bullet", "first presented fair value gap in the 10:00 hour")
- **Kind:** entry model (study version) / rule
- **What ICT says:** On a 1-minute chart, look at 10:00 a.m. ET. The 10:00 candle itself can be part of an FVG, or the next candles. The first FVG that forms inside the 10:00 hour is the one to log. Include volume imbalance: when candle bodies don't meet in a potential FVG, include that gap. Draw a rectangle for the FVG and extend it to the right at first; when annotating, turn extending off and drag it to the first time price returns into it. The pattern: price creates the FVG, drops lower after 10:00 and forms a short-term low, rallies back up into the FVG, then from the FVG goes down below that low where sell-side liquidity sits. It tells you what time it forms, how it forms, and you only need to determine where the high/low is that it aims for.
- **How to identify:** (1) 10:00 ET start on a 1-minute chart; (2) the first FVG (incl. volume imbalance) in the 10:00 hour; (3) price returns into it; (4) price takes the short-term low (or high) created after the FVG formed.
- **How to use / log:** In the first four to six weeks, do not try to predict direction; record it after the fact. Record the time the FVG formed (example: 10:01 ET), how many minutes before price came back into it, and the range in handles from the return high to the low it took (highest high to lowest low). Over time you will see the formations, and where it goes (sell side or buy side). Keep an electronic log if possible with keywords.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart: 10:00 candle then 10:01 candle leaves a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency gap (SIBI) with a volume imbalance; price drops lower after 10:00 to a short-term low, rallies back up into the FVG, then sells down through that low (sell side). Earlier in the day price came far from a high, rallied, broke the lows, and traded back up.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:17](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1097) — pillar 3 introduced; [0:19:06](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1146) — first FVG in the 10:00 hour, 10:01 SIBI example; [0:19:54](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1194) — volume imbalance, rectangle extension tip, sequence of the setup; [0:23:09](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1389) — what to record (time, minutes, handles).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This pins the Silver Bullet to the 10:00 hour first FVG only, as a "streamlined" version; the entry/stop/target rules are not covered here.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Pre-market window: 7:00–9:00 a.m. ET, every trading day except Sundays. Mark vertical lines at both times; log highest high, lowest low and relative equal highs/lows.
- RTH opening range gap: previous day's 4:14 p.m. ET final 1-minute print to the 9:30 a.m. ET open; rectangle with mid level on; CE target; claimed about 70% chance CE is reached by 10:00 a.m. (his own figure, not to be relied on).
- Silver Bullet log: 1-minute chart, first FVG formed from the 10:00 a.m. ET candle onward within the 10:00 hour; include volume imbalance; log formation time, minutes to return, handle range.
- Rollout: weeks 1–2 one pillar, weeks 3–4 add second, weeks 5–6 add third. Advanced students may do all three at once.
- One market only (NASDAQ). 6 weeks back plus 6 weeks forward = 12 weeks of data. About 20 minutes a day for all three. Review on weekends.
- Brand-new students can simplify the 7–9 read to trending vs consolidating.
- Logging is not backtesting; backtesting is the next video.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Do not rush; trying to learn everything fast means learning nothing ([0:01:58](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=118)).
- Predict, do not react: recording price action daily builds the ability to anticipate it ([0:06:53](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=413)); if you cannot stick to a three-screenshot routine, you cannot follow a trading model.
- Pattern recognition through repetition: seeing the same thing daily in slightly different forms builds experience he cannot give you, and removes the urge to chase or system-hop ([0:21:33](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1293), [0:22:22](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1342)).
- Everything here is time-based, so it transposes to any session or time frame, even if you plan to trade London, Asia or the afternoon ([0:23:09](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1389)).
- Do not "Netflix and chill" through videos; the work of logging is where the benefit is ([0:25:34](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1534)).
- Journal habit: he logs by hand, writing numbers and times; write observations on the chart itself, treat as your business ([0:27:09](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1629)).

## Glossary candidates
`Pre-market session (7–9 a.m. ET)` — the two-hour window whose range and character he logs daily as a precursor to the 9:30 behavior.
`Relative equal lows/highs` — nearby matching lows/highs forming a liquidity pool; logged in the pre-market window.
`RTH opening range gap` — gap between the previous 4:14 p.m. ET RTH close and the 9:30 a.m. open.
`Consequent encroachment (CE)` — midpoint of a gap/inefficiency; the log target for the opening range gap.
`Silver Bullet (10:00 hour)` — first FVG formed from 10:00 a.m. ET onward, returned to, then a run to the short-term low/high.
`Volume imbalance` — candle bodies that do not meet, counted as part of a potential FVG.
`Phenomenon / pillar` — his word for each of the three repeating daily patterns to log.
