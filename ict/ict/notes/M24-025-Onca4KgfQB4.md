## Concepts

### RTH Opening Range Gap (ORG), its midpoint ("mid gap") and quadrants  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap, opening gap, "the Gap", half gap, upper/lower quadrant)
- **Kind:** definition + rule (with a stated statistic)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening range is 9:30 to 10:00 ET.
  - The opening range gap is previous day's settlement (the 4:15 PM ET close for index futures) to the very first print at the 9:30 open. It must be viewed with RTH (regular trading hours) in mind.
  - He divides the gap into halves and quadrants: mid gap (half gap, consequent-encroachment-style level), upper quadrant and lower quadrant levels. He puts these on both the 15-second and 1-minute charts (matching colors, e.g. the blue line = upper quadrant on both).
  - Statistic: "70% of the time" the half-gap price level gets traded to / repriced to in the first 30 minutes of trading. He stresses 70% is not 100%.
  - Gaps have a magnetic effect on price in the opening minutes "but not all the time"; a gap can stay open for days, so a gap alone is not a 100% probability fill.
  - How the market opens relative to the previous day's settlement is what frames the morning-session setup. The afternoon is judged by different things (lunch/afternoon he looks at other features).
- **How to identify:** Mark Friday 4:15 PM ET settlement and the 9:30 first print; draw the midpoint (50%), the upper quadrant (the level between the midpoint and the opening print, i.e. the upper-quarter line of the gap) and the lower quadrant (the level between the midpoint and the settlement). Exact quadrant arithmetic is not spelled out in this video.
- **How to use:** A large gap opening higher builds an "expensive" premium. He will not buy it. Any move down from the premium sets the stage for a trade toward half the gap. His selling zone this day was between a broken short-term low and the upper quadrant of the gap, with the mid gap as target and the lower quadrant as a further level to watch.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Mon 2024-09-09. Large gap up from Friday's settlement (a big Friday drop preceded it). Price opens higher into the bottom of a weekly inversion FVG, builds a series of small short-term highs, then breaks a short-term low. ICT wants a PD array to form between that low and the upper quadrant of the gap; around 10:30 to 10:36 ET (video ≈1:05–1:10, clock time inferred as video time + ~9:30) an institutional order flow entry drill forms, price rolls over and trades down to the mid gap. He notes it did NOT reach half gap within the first 30 minutes, but it did get there, so the 70% rule was not "wrong".

### Premium gap opening — do not buy it, wait for price to "price in" the premium  (aliases: expensive price, "buy discount")
- **Kind:** rule / mindset applied to entries
- **What ICT says:**
  - A large gap opening is already an expensive price (the difference between Friday settlement and the 9:30 first print is "already built in"). He will not pay it for NQ, especially on a day with no news until Wednesday.
  - "If it's too expensive, why buy it; if it's too cheap, why sell short." If a price does not make sense, he is not willing to pay it because he would not be comfortable holding the position; core principle: buy at a discount (and by symmetry sell at a premium).
  - He also will not short aggressively into the highs: price can keep building premium ("inching higher") and the urge to "fill the gap" has hurt him (he did this as a 20-year-old in bonds).
  - Instead let it "prove" it has done enough: wait for displacement and a break lower, then sell from a premium array down to a discount.
- **How to identify:** Opening gap above prior settlement into a higher-timeframe resistance (here, a weekly inversion FVG), with price creeping up in small increments rather than running aggressively.
- **How to use:** Sit out the creeping highs. Only engage when price breaks a short-term low and leaves a PD array to sell from (see IOFED / breaker below). Target the mid gap.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opened with a premium gap; he refused to buy even if NQ went to 18,900 ("I don't care"). He cites the previous Friday (Sept 6) as an example of what he does wait for: shorting the 1-minute high of the day and riding it to a deep discount, which he says made $100,000 (his claim).
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:22:10](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1330) — frames what he is looking for: a down move, a fair value gap to trade up into, then a run to mid gap; not chasing higher
  - [0:44:55](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2695) — "I'm not willing to pay that price"; 18,900 comment; selling to fill the gap is a trap
  - [0:45:43](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2743) — "I have to buy discount"; expensive premium in an environment being manipulated by two news drivers
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says not to just sell because there is a gap: he first reads whether the highs being taken are followed by quick speed away (rejection) or not.

### Weekly Inversion Fair Value Gap as the higher-timeframe objective  (aliases: weekly iFVG, "the orange shaded box")
- **Kind:** example only / context application of iFVG
- **What ICT says:**
  - A weekly inversion FVG sat above price after Friday's big drop. He expects a Monday retracement up into it: "reasonable to anticipate" a premium gap opening that tries to reach it, then a turn back lower ("taking it back to a weekly objective").
  - The bottom of the weekly iFVG on the 15-minute chart coincides with a tiny separation between one candle's low and another candle's high (he calls it a volume-imbalance-type gap; caption reads "siy"/SIBI, caption unclear). He wants that annotated on the chart.
  - He would "very much prefer" price tags the weekly iFVG first before selling; otherwise he'd give it another chance to run.
  - It is a large range just to reach the midpoint of that iFVG, so price could go ~100 points further up (he cites "791" as the midpoint-ish level; caption unclear), which is why he won't short early.
  - He expects the bigger picture to have price lower later in the week; he expects an election-year volatility increase in the second half of October, but does not expect price to retake all of Friday's range.
- **How to identify:** Weekly chart: a prior FVG that was closed through (inverted). Mark it with a shaded box, project it down to 15m/1m/15s.
- **How to use:** Use as the "where might the retracement end" zone; look for bodies stalling at its bottom (indecisive bodies, wicks into it) then displacement lower. If price runs below the reaction low it becomes meaningful.
- **Example in this video:** Price gaps up, trades into the bottom of the weekly iFVG, bodies bud up against the underside of it with indecisive bodies, then price drops, showing bodies respecting the lower portion of the iFVG on the 15-second chart.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:05:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=322) — 15m view; tiny separation at its bottom to annotate
  - [0:36:09](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2169) — reasonable to anticipate Monday retracement; price needs a large range to reach the iFVG midpoint

### Short-term highs as engineered liquidity (buy-stop absorption)  (aliases: liquidity engineering, "taking the other side", building a premium)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price keeps forming small short-term highs that are taken out only just enough ("just enough to take out the previous highs"), it is smart money engineering liquidity and building premium, not a genuine run.
  - Each time buy stops above a high are taken, smart money is selling into them (and retail is buying the breakout, being the other side of smart money). When the highs stop being taken (buy stops absorbed), the sell is set up.
  - After the most recent swing high is taken, expect speed away from it (a strong drop). If price does not show speed, it will probably keep pressing higher, so do not short.
  - Do not sell directly into these highs; wait for the strong drop.
- **How to identify:** A sequence of 3–4 small short-term highs inside a premium, each marginally above the last, without large expansion candles; then a failed high and break of the prior short-term low.
- **How to use:** Treat the failure of the last high plus a break lower (bodies closing below the breaker) as the cue to look for a PD array to sell.
- **Example in this video:** Four short-term highs formed in a row on the gap-up (0:32:03 recap). A high is taken, then the next high fails, price breaks lower below the breaker/down-close candle, and bodies on the 15-second chart respect the lower portion of the weekly iFVG.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:32:03](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1923) — counts the short-term highs; "engineering liquidity"
  - [0:33:43](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2023) — should show speed after the swing high is taken; don't sell into the highs
  - [1:30:52](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=5452) — recap: every new high someone takes the other side; buy stops above the high are absorbed
  - [1:31:41](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=5501) — smart money sells into buy stops, retail buys the breakout
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Repeats his earlier teaching that buy stops are fuel for smart money, applied on a 15s/1m scale.

### Small bearish breaker on 1m / 15s (down-close candle at the short-term high)  (aliases: the breaker, "shoulder block" (caption unclear))
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 1-minute chart he marks a breaker: the down-close candle at a short-term high that was then exceeded by a higher high. He watches how price behaves when it trades back around it: bodies closing below it with real displacement matter; just wicks poking below, with two wicks and no displacement, mean it will probably take one more run up first.
  - A body closing below the breaker, with bodies stacking at the bottom of the weekly iFVG, is a more meaningful sign that price wants to drop.
  - He watches a specific candle's low as a point of interest: price trades to that low, and a gap sits just above it; he wants price to get below that gap and show "signatures of inversion" on it.
- **How to identify:** Down-closed candle before a push to a higher high (short-term high that exceeds it) inside the premium.
- **How to use:** Don't sell the first wicky poke below it. Wait for displacement through and a body close below; then work from the PD array formed (FVG/OB/small breaker).
- **Example in this video:** 0:24:33–0:25:21: two wicks reach slightly below the breaker with no displacement, so he predicts one more drive up into the weekly iFVG. Price then delivers a body down, leaving wicks inside the iFVG.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:21:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1282) — "I'd like to see it displaced lower"; body closed below the breaker
  - [0:24:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1473) — two wicks, no displacement => one more run up expected
  - [0:25:21](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1521) — candle-low point of interest and inversion signature

### Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOFED) as the sell trigger  (aliases: "institutional overflow entry drill", "entry drill", partial fill, PD array)
- **Kind:** entry model (applied example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Once price breaks the low, he is "certain" it will give him a fair value gap, a bearish order block, a small bearish breaker or an institutional order flow entry drill, which he can key off on the 1m or 15s chart.
  - The ideal trade: price breaks lower, then retraces up into a premium PD array between the broken low and the upper quadrant of the opening range gap (an order-flow partial fill into that array), then drops to mid gap.
  - The zone he sits comfortably waiting in is the difference between that candle's low and 3/4 or half the distance between the opening-range-gap quadrants (as captioned; caption unclear on exact fractions).
  - At 1:17:13 he says he'd like to see price "create an institutional order flow (entry drill), which is a partial fill" of the little inefficiency, then dump to mid gap.
- **How to identify:** Break below the reference short-term low, small retrace into a bearish FVG/OB/breaker sitting between that low and the gap's upper quadrant.
- **How to use:** Enter short from that premium array (1m or 15s), target mid gap, then lower quadrant. No fixed stop given in this video.
- **Example in this video:** After the break lower (about 10:30 to 10:36 ET), price returns into the little array (a volume imbalance halfway point is noted), then rolls over. He annotates "institutional order flow entry drill" on the 1m at 1:05:12 and on the 15s at 1:09:57, then price trades to mid gap.
- **Timestamps:**

### Economic-calendar filter: no news = demand clean price; news = "smoke screen"  (aliases: injection of volatility, "watering hole")
- **Kind:** rule (filter)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Check forexfactory.com or any calendar. This week: PPI and CPI on Wednesday/Thursday; Monday and Tuesday empty. If you want to avoid the news volatility, trade Monday/Tuesday (he will trade after PPI/CPI too).
  - With no news driver, a price-action trader must demand clean price. With a news release at 8:30 or 10:00, he can tolerate sloppier price because the report is a "smoke screen", an injection of volatility that invites people to the "watering hole".
  - A high/medium impact release does not guarantee clean price action; if it is wild back-and-forth, don't trade that either. Much of the skill is knowing when not to trade.
  - Journal this: absence of news = you must absolutely see clean price action.
  - Do not trade PPI/CPI in advance: the repricing is immediate and aggressive. Wait for the report, give it 15–20 minutes, then look at what liquidity or inefficiency is left.
  - Monday/Tuesday are building a big premium ahead of PPI/CPI to run against.
- **How to identify:** Check the calendar before the week starts.
- **How to use:** On empty-calendar days, wait for clean, surgical setups and skip marginal ones; on news days, wait 15–20 minutes after the release.
- **Example in this video:** Whole session (empty Monday): he stays flat for the first hour+ and only frames the sell after the low breaks.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:11:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=682) — calendar check; trade Monday/Tuesday to avoid news
  - [0:28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713) — "smoke screen" and demanding clean price with no news
  - [0:34:29](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2069) — wait 15–20 minutes after PPI/CPI

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30 to 10:00 ET. Opening range gap = previous settlement (4:15 PM ET) to the 9:30 first print.
- 70% of the time the half-gap level is traded in the first 30 minutes (not 100%).
- Do not trade (buy or sell) a premium gap opening just because it is there; wait for price to give a PD array after a break of structure.
- No news on the calendar means demand cleaner price action. If a news day gives wild price, skip that too.
- Don't trade PPI/CPI in advance; wait 15–20 minutes after release.
- If by 11:00–11:15 ET nothing makes sense, he pulls the plug (stops looking for trades).
- Sit still for the first 30 minutes, even if you identify an ideal setup, and engage only after 10:00; do this for a few months as an exercise.
- Best practice for learners: use the lower timeframes (15s/1m) to find repeatable repeating setups; a model that forms 3–4 times a week is wonderful.
- Videos are being kept under 90 minutes (YouTube captions break on very long streams).

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Sit still / do less:** "You make more money doing little" and keep profits longer; don't rush the first setup in the first 10–20 minutes, especially on a Monday. Wasting ammo and mental capital on sub-optimal conditions makes it harder when the market gives easy delivery. [0:15:37](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=937), [0:37:47](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2267)
- **FOMO is a model problem:** without a model, a trader reacts to candles flicking like a dog hearing a food bowl. Exercise: sit still the first 30 minutes for months and observe whether you feel impulsive, reckless or like gambling. [0:18:06](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1086), [0:18:56](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1136)
- **Anxiety journal:** when you feel impulsive or anxious, screenshot the chart and annotate it ("anxious", "feeling impulsive"); at session end (noon NY for index trading) check what price did; often what you feared missing was never on the chart. This desensitizes the "I must catch something" urge. [0:19:46](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1186)
- **Annotation / journaling:** end-of-day annotated charts build a library of completed moves; he disputes people who say markups are tedious.
- **Record live price, not market replay:** TradingView-style replay shows one settled tick at a time, which gives a false sense of how candles form; record live price (he uses Camtasia since 2012) to log your own charts with every fluctuation. If you can't afford the resources, you shouldn't be risking money; don't scrape together funded-account challenge fees as a Hail Mary. [0:40:58](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2458), [0:42:35](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2555)
- **Body-reset techniques:** a yawn (or a mimicked yawn) plus stretching resets the nervous system (vagus nerve); slow breathing with the exhale at least twice as long as the inhale; his version: 4–5 second slow inhale, one extra "sniff" at full capacity, hold 4 seconds, long exhale, repeat about three times; box breathing may not suit everyone. Adrenaline then cortisol: cortisol takes 20–30 minutes to burn off. [0:49:44](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2984), [0:52:58](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=3178), [1:01:53](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=3713) (health anecdotes in the lecture are personal, not trading advice, and are skipped).
- **Have a model in writing and in a diagram:** know what it looks like in price, when it forms, how it behaves; if you cannot draw it you can't recognise it. Anxiety is proportional to the expectations you hold against not having a model. [1:00:15](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=3615), [1:07:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4053)
- **Don't make the target a report card:** don't live ahead of delivery or hold the trade up against the take-profit as proof you're right. Instead keep asking: is the market still doing everything that lets me trust this trade, even if slower than I like? Be indifferent to the outcome. [1:10:48](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4248), [1:12:24](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4344)
- **Novice distractions:** worrying about "what if it goes more / goes against me" instead of watching whether price still supports the narrative that put you in the trade. [1:13:59](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4439)
- **Gambler's numbness:** after heavy losses you click the button with no feeling; a sign of having no model and no data. [1:14:48](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4488)
- **Build the data:** annotate completed moves, backtest, forward-walk test, tape-read without pushing buttons, then paper trade (he used graph paper: note a hypothetical entry, then the high/low to compute drawdown, profit target and time taken). [1:18:49](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4729)
- **Skepticism:** don't blindly follow ICT or anyone; test and backtest the claims. If there is nothing to take, he takes no trade. [1:23:36](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=5016)
- **Don't trade the sake of trading:** "I can call every major move but not every move is a trade I want to put money on." [1:22:02](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=4922)
- Teaching tip: when he says "I'm watching this" on a live stream, pause and screenshot it; ask why he's interested and what it should do next. [1:29:15](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=5355)

## Glossary candidates
`Opening range gap (ORG)` — gap between previous day's settlement (4:15 PM ET) and the first print at 9:30 ET; opening range itself is 9:30–10:00.
`Mid gap / half gap` — 50% of the opening range gap; traded to in the first 30 minutes about 70% of the time.
`Upper / lower quadrant (of the gap)` — the 25% and 75% levels of the opening range gap, used as zone boundaries.
`Weekly inversion FVG` — a weekly fair value gap that price has closed through (inverted); used here as the weekly objective.
`IOFED / institutional order flow entry drill` — retrace into a PD array (partial fill) after the structure break, used for entry.
`Smoke screen` — ICT's term for a news release, an injection of volatility that lets sloppy price action work.
`Engineering liquidity` — creating small short-term highs/lows to attract stops, then selling/buying into them.
`Premium / expensive price` — a price (e.g., gap-up open) he won't buy; "buy discount, sell premium".
`Gambler's numbness` — emotional shutdown after big losses; clicking without caring about outcome.
