## Summary
Recorded Tuesday May 10, 2022 on E-mini S&P (ES) June 2022. ICT shows how his earlier daily-chart call (bearish order block, May seasonal weakness, draw to relative equal lows) played out, then builds a complete top-down short: Monday's large down day is "rebalanced" on Tuesday by a run up to Friday's low; the 8:30 news-embargo lift starts the algorithm, the 9:30 open is the Judas swing, and the rally takes buy-side liquidity (relative equal highs) *first*, which is what makes it bearish. He then demonstrates the core execution procedure of the 2022 model: shade the displacement leg, put a Fibonacci on it, only sell above equilibrium (premium), and step down the timeframes 5→4→3→2→1 until a fair value gap appears inside the leg. He closes with stop placement (FVG candle high or swing high), micros vs minis, when to trail, session notes (London yes, Asia rarely), and a 6‑month study plan.

## Concepts

### Bearish order block — high is the "most unlikely" level
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** On the ES daily, the bearish order block he flagged earlier was a catalyst for a future move down; the high of that candle "shouldn't be pierced" — it is the most unlikely level to be traded to and through. Order blocks were introduced in his free "Scout Sniper" series; the OB is "not the secret sauce" of this model — the FVG is the focus.
- **Example in this video:** ES June 2022 daily: relative equal highs were run (buy stops), price broke down, traded into the bearish order block region without exceeding its high, then fell toward relative equal lows (sell-side liquidity) and lower targets he had outlined.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:15](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=435) — daily chart recap; [0:08:02](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=482) — OB high most unlikely to be traded through.

### Seasonal tendencies (May weakness in indices)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Seasonal tendencies are times of year when markets or asset classes historically produce price swings in a certain direction — usually, not always (analogy: in Maryland you expect heat, not snow, on July 4th). From about the last week of April into May, S&P, Nasdaq, Dow and Russell tend to be weak. By itself it means little; combined with his model it's "a road map to consistency." Study it every year in old data, then forward-test it.
- **How to use:** As HTF bias filter — bearish seasonal + BSL run at highs = framework for shorts.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=672) — definition/analogy; [0:11:58](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=718) — late April into May weakness.

### Smart money liquidity cycle (sell to buy stops, buy back from sell stops)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Smart money sells short to buy stops resting above old/relative equal highs, leaving them net short; to get out they must buy back cheaper, from sell stops resting below old lows (sell-side liquidity). Large fund traders place stops in similar places to retail — the difference is only size. This is the "market efficiency paradigm": liquidity resting or engineered where it's wanted — not support/resistance, not supply/demand. The algorithm goes to the liquidity "whether there is sufficient volume or not" — not because of buying pressure.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:19](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=859) — net short, buy back from sell stops; [0:15:06](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=906) — funds and retail stops in same areas; [0:49:58](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2998) — algorithm goes there regardless of volume.

### Rebalancing a large daily range (previous-day low as retracement target)
- **Kind:** rule / new framing
- **What ICT says:** After a large range down day (Monday, closed near its low), go back to the *previous day's* low (Friday's low). The next day's retracement up to that level "rebalances" Monday's entire range/sell-off. It tricks people into thinking the low is in, when price has only gone up to a logical level on the daily that rebalances the drop. On the 15m the move doesn't look like an imbalance, but on the daily it's a large distribution candle.
- **How to identify:** Large down day closing near low → mark prior day's low → expect the next session's Judas rally to reach it (when bias is bearish).
- **How to use:** Use the prior day's low as the "how far can it go" upper limit for the Judas swing; look for the short setup there.
- **Example in this video:** ES, Tue May 10 2022: price ran up from 8:30 and hit Friday's (May 6) low right at the 9:30 candle, rebalancing Monday (May 9)'s drop, then sold off.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:24](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1284) — go back to previous day's low; [0:31:48](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1908) — rebalances Monday's range; [0:32:36](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1956) — "how far can it go — that's the Friday low."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Links to his 2010 BabyPips advice: study the open, high, low, close of the last three days — that's where he first found the FVG (no one else discovered it from that prompt).

### Order of liquidity runs defines bias (buy-side first = bearish)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** With a bearish bias, the market goes up to a premium for one of two reasons: run old high(s) for buy-side liquidity so smart money can sell to them, then seek sell-side liquidity below to offset. Running buy-side (relative equal highs) at 9:30 *without* first taking the previous low is bearish. If price had first taken the previous low and then rallied, "that to me is not bearish."
- **Example in this video:** ES 1h/15m: relative equal highs above, old low (Friday's low) above that; at 9:30 price runs the relative equal highs and hits the old low (red line), purging buy-side; then sells to take partials below a short-term low and run Monday's low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1621) — BSL purged at 9:30; [0:28:38](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1718) — buy side run first is the bearish tell.

### 8:30 news embargo lift and the 8:30–9:30 Judas swing window
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** At 8:30 ET the news embargo is lifted and the algorithm starts seeking liquidity (even on light-news days); it may wait until 9:30. When bearish, the 8:30–9:30 hour is expected to produce a run higher to set up shorts. The short may form inside that hour, or the hour may just provide the leg that frames the later setup. 9:30 equity open = manipulation time; it looks like a breakout to retail, who buy and get run.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m/5m: run starts at 8:30 from relative equal highs, 9:30 candle tags Friday's low; the shaded rally is the Judas swing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1861) — 8:30 embargo lifted; [0:34:11](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2051) — the hour-long interval; [0:34:57](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2097) — 9:30 manipulation, retail chases.

### Power of Three inside an "indecisive" daily candle
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** A daily candle with a big upper wick and small body looks indecisive, but through power of three: accumulation around the 8:30 opening price (draw it out in time), the rally above it (manipulation), then the mentorship pattern (FVG, market structure shift), displacement and distribution down into a discount array. Lower-timeframe framing with time and price removes the confusion.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:36](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1956) — power of three with 8:30 open; [0:33:23](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2003) — open, rally, high, trade down.

### Displacement leg + Fibonacci equilibrium filter (sell only in premium)
- **Kind:** rule (he calls it a "hard line rule")
- **What ICT says:** Shade the displacement price swing (on the 5m here). Put a Fib on its high and low; 50% = equilibrium. For a short, the FVG entry must be at equilibrium or higher (premium) — "we don't want to sell in a discount." Following this alone keeps you out of many ill-fated trades (not 100%).
- **How to identify:** Displacement leg = the drop from the Judas high that breaks short-term lows (here it broke several short-term lows, so the leg extends to the final low).
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m leg from the 9:30 high down; equilibrium = 4044.50. An FVG visible on the 4m sat below 4044.50 (discount) → not valid for a short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:29](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2189) — fib, equilibrium 4044.50; [0:38:02](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2282) — FVG in discount rejected; [0:38:52](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2332) — hard-line rule.

### Stepping down timeframes to find the FVG (5→4→3→2→1)
- **Kind:** rule / entry procedure
- **What ICT says:** Keep the shaded displacement leg and step down 5m → 4m → 3m → 2m → 1m until you find a fair value gap inside it (in premium).
- **Example in this video:** ES: 5m leg shaded. 4m — one FVG but in discount (invalid). 3m — no FVG in the leg. 2m — a small FVG ("the unicorn," jokingly) formed with a short-term low taken out → the entry.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:43](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2143) — strip down 5,4,3,2,1; [0:40:26](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2426) — 3m none; [0:41:15](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2475) — 2m FVG found.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Unicorn" here is a joke about rarity, not the later named Unicorn model.

### Stop placement and trailing
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Initial stop: 1–2 ticks above the high of the candle that creates the FVG. Wider alternative: above the swing high (Judas high) — if that is too much risk, trade micros (ES micro $5/point vs mini $50/handle). If you can't weather that on a micro, you're underfunded (e.g., a $100 account is asking to fail). Don't trail tight right away — positions need room to breathe; move the stop only after a larger shift in structure (a lower low taken out), then to above the corresponding lower swing high. If it then stops you, you were probably wrong or price is consolidating (ugly conditions anyway).
- **Example in this video:** 2m FVG short: stop above the FVG-creating candle was not hit, though price retraced after moving in favor.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:04](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2524) — stop above FVG candle; [0:43:39](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2619) — 1–2 ticks, swing high, micros; [0:44:25](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2665) — when to move stop.

### Targets: short-term low partial, then sell-side (previous day low); lunch-hour delivery
- **Kind:** example / rule
- **What ICT says:** Offset shorts at a nearby short-term low (partial) and then the sell-side below the previous day's low. Smart money "sells short here and then adds to it." A limit order would have filled during the lunch hour. He expected the final punch through the low just ahead of or right at 12:00; it came ~27 minutes later — the only thing that didn't line up.

### Don't pick tops/bottoms; trade the middle of the move
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** In a bearish market, don't try to call the long-term low (he has lost more money doing that than anything else). Oversold indicators/bullish divergence tempt bottom-picking. "There's a lot of meat in between the major turning points"; you can afford to be wrong at the end, it'll be obvious, then switch.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:13](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1333); [0:23:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1381).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Bearish OB: its high should not be pierced (most unlikely level).
- Seasonal: last week of April into May, US indices tend to be weak.
- Day starts at midnight New York time.
- 8:30 ET news embargo lift starts the liquidity search; 9:30 = manipulation; 8:30–9:30 = expected Judas run (when bearish, up).
- Bearish day: buy-side must be run first (before previous low).
- After a large down day, the previous day's low is the logical rebalance level for the next day's rally.
- Short entries only at/above 50% of the displacement leg (premium); never in discount.
- Drop timeframes 5→4→3→2→1 to find the FVG inside the shaded displacement leg.
- Stop: 1–2 ticks above the FVG-creating candle's high, or above swing high (use micros: $5/pt vs $50/pt).
- Move stop only after a larger structure shift (new lower low).
- Delivery to the low expected around 12:00 lunch; came ~27 min later.
- Study plan: 3 months back-testing + 2–3 months forward-testing (~6 months) with only this model.

## Glossary candidates
`Rebalance (a daily range)` — retracement into the prior day's low/high that offsets a large one-day range; acts as the Judas swing's limit.
`News embargo (8:30)` — the 8:30 ET release time after which the algorithm begins seeking liquidity.
`Displacement leg (shaded)` — the decisive swing after the liquidity run in which the entry FVG is sought, measured with a Fib for premium/discount.
`Fractal` — ICT's term for a small segment of price action under study.
`Composite man` — (Wyckoff term he borrows) smart money; he says they look at liquidity, not Wyckoff schematics.
`Scout Sniper series` — his earlier free YouTube series where order blocks were introduced.
