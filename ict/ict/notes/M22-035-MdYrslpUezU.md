## Concepts

### Order block sensitive levels and the Mean Threshold
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** For a candle used as an order block, the three sensitive areas are its low, its open, and the middle of its range. The midpoint (50%, Fib across the candle) is what he calls the **mean threshold**. When price trades up into the mean threshold of a daily bearish order block (up-close candle), that "bodes well for a continuation" to take out the short-term high beyond it.
- **How to identify:** Fib the OB candle high-to-low; 50% = mean threshold.
- **How to use:** Watch price reaction at low → open → mean threshold; reaching the mean threshold without rejection signals continuation through.
- **Example in this video:** ES daily: price consolidated right below the low of the up-close candle forming the daily bearish OB, then dug up into it during the session, reaching its mean threshold.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:05](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=5) — low, open, middle of the range; [0:00:54](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=54) — mean threshold defined; [0:08:47](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=527) — watching for mean threshold.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Same idea as CE for FVGs, but applied to an order block candle's full range.

### Choosing the dealing range: use the most recent stage of the leg
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** A 5m price leg can deliver in stages. If one leg had a single pass and another has two stages (low → high → small retracement → second run), use the most recent stage — short-term low to short-term high — as the dealing range. Fib it; 50% is equilibrium; wait for a drop into discount, ideally into an FVG.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m: most recent stage measured; discount FVG below 50% traded into; long (also showed on Twitter).
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:14](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=194) — two-stage delivery, use most recent; [0:04:00](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=240) — equilibrium, discount FVG.

### SMT divergence — setup and timing
- **Kind:** definition / rule / how-to
- **What ICT says:** On TradingView, click the "+" compare button, add NQM2022 (NQ June 2022); it plots a line on closes by default — change the settings to plot on the **low** when comparing lows (highs for highs). If NQ makes a lower low while ES fails to, that's SMT (smart money technique): ES is accumulating while NQ takes liquidity — a "crack in correlation." They usually move in tandem; divergence tends to appear at 8:30, 9:30 and news releases. Without a bias you'll often see an apparent SMT that then disappears as they re-sync — so know what price is reaching for first.
- **How to use:** With bullish bias, a higher low in ES vs. a lower low in NQ (especially vs. the 8:30 low) is the "telltale sign" price goes higher; buy the stronger index's FVG/OB.
- **Example in this video:** ES drop back into the FVG made a higher low than the 8:30 low; NQ made a lower low → stop-out/accumulation; ES "compressed like a spring," then exploded at 9:30. He missed the ideal entry and used a close-proximity entry on a 15m order block.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:26](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=386) — TradingView compare setup, plot on low; [0:07:13](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=433) — times of divergence; need bias; [0:07:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=479) — ES higher low vs 8:30, NQ lower low.

### Early run before 7:00 AM → deep retracement → SMT near 9:30
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** If there's a nice early run before (or at) 7:00 AM, expect a deep retracement, and look for the ideal scenario with SMT divergence around 9:30 — it will probably need a "cracking correlation" to set up the next leg (if there is one). Seeing an early run before 7 AM gives insight into what the 9:30 open will be like.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:34](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=814); [0:15:08](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=908).

### Staircase delivery (repeated discount FVG retracements) & not picking tops
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** On the 1h: dealing range low → high → retrace into discount FVG → rally; repeat (discount FVG, order block, rally, consolidate) — the algorithm picks up accumulated longs at each discount, pressing toward buy-side. With a bullish bias, don't try to sell/pick tops.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:34](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=574) — hourly structure; [0:10:21](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=621) — the repeated sequence; [0:11:09](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=669) — did you try to short?

### High resistance liquidity run (why not short here)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** With many supporting ranges below (down-close candles supporting price, underlying bullish order flow), a reversal would have to pierce and break all those ranges — a high resistance liquidity run that's "likely to not form or pan out" (not impossible). So price is likely to "go up just to keep going higher, not go up to go down." Know narrative and bias, and what would constitute a short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=719).

## Glossary candidates
`Mean threshold` — 50% of an order block candle's range.
`Cracking correlation` — when correlated indices (ES/NQ) diverge at a swing — the basis of SMT.
`High resistance liquidity run` — a move that must break through many supporting/opposing ranges; low odds.
`Spring / compression` — price held at a higher low (vs. a correlated market's lower low) before expansion.
