## Summary
Detailed technical review introducing three related but distinct PD arrays: First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG), First Presented FVG with Displacement (FPFVG-D), and First Presented Reflection Fair Value Gap (mirror image SIBI). ICT explains classification hierarchy (FPFVG-D gets more emphasis), uses daily live execution (post-Labor Day, one contract only due to holiday volume caution) to demonstrate wick consequent encroachment as reversal signal, and shows how displacement, macro timing, and body placement relative to gaps confirm bias. Addresses that complexity is necessary for superior trading; simplicity is insufficient.

## Concepts

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The very first candlestick gap that forms after 9:30 AM ET on any trading day. Identified by the candlestick close and next candlestick open not aligning (either up-close gap or down-close gap). Crucial marker for intraday structure. Does NOT need displacement (breaking prior swing highs/lows) to be valid, but FPFVG with displacement is emphasized more heavily.
- **How to identify:** Start observing candles at 9:30 AM ET; mark the first candlestick that creates a gap (body doesn't fill the space between prior close and current open).
- **How to use:** If FPFVG is up-close (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency), look for the first opposing down-close gap (SIBI) formed afterward — this becomes the Reflection FVG, equally important for understanding the market's structure.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's FPFVG was an up-close gap at open (29,681 close to 29,681 next open); Monday's action used this to define targets for the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:52](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=52) "first presented fair value gap," [0:02:26](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=146) "buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not every gap after 9:30 is the FPFVG; only the very first one counts. Confusion arises when FPFVG-D exists (see below).

### First Presented Fair Value Gap with Displacement (FPFVG-D)
- **Kind:** definition (refinement)
- **What ICT says:** When the FPFVG is part of a move that ALSO breaks the prior swing high or low (displacement), it becomes "FPFVG with displacement" and receives significantly more emphasis in future trading. This gap is more significant because it represents a true breakout, not just an opening gap. If both a plain FPFVG and an FPFVG-D exist in the same session, extend the FPFVG-D forward for higher probability targeting.
- **How to identify:** Identify the FPFVG; check if the candlestick that created it (or subsequent candles in that run) broke above or below the prior day's swing high/low. If yes = FPFVG-D.
- **How to use:** Prioritize FPFVG-D for targeting and support/resistance; it's more likely to be referenced intraday by price action. Plain FPFVG still has value but secondary emphasis.
- **Example in this video:** Friday had a plain FPFVG (up-close, no displacement); later a FPFVG-D formed (up-close gap WITH breakout of prior day's highs). The FPFVG-D is carried forward with higher conviction.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:39](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=99) "first presented fair value gap with displacement," [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=195) "much more significant... much more emphasis."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Distinguish between plain FPFVG and FPFVG-D in your chart annotations; don't confuse them.

### First Presented Reflection Fair Value Gap (Reflection FVG)
- **Kind:** definition (derived concept)
- **What ICT says:** After identifying the FPFVG (especially if it's an up-close gap), search for the very first down-close gap (SIBI: sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency) that forms after it. This SIBI is the "reflection" of the FPFVG — it's the mirror-image, opposite characteristic. Named "reflection" because it's the reverse of the FPFVG, just as a mirror reverses an image. CRITICAL: If the FPFVG is NOT an up-close (buy-side) gap, the reflection concept doesn't apply; there's no significance to the first SIBI.
- **How to identify:** (1) Confirm FPFVG is an up-close gap. (2) Scan forward through candlesticks for the first down-close gap (SIBI). (3) That SIBI becomes the Reflection FVG.
- **How to use:** The Reflection FVG acts as a secondary reference level for support/resistance intraday; price often retraces into it or respects its high/low/midpoint boundaries.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's FPFVG was up-close; the first SIBI that formed (down-close gap at 10:01 AM) became the Reflection FVG. Monday and Tuesday, price traded into this Reflection FVG multiple times.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389) "first presented reflection fair value gap," [0:06:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389) "mirror image of the very first presented fair value gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Only applies if FPFVG is up-close (buy-side); if FPFVG is down-close, no Reflection FVG concept applies.

### Displacement (Gap Definition)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When a candlestick gap is part of a run that also breaks the prior swing high or swing low, it has "displacement." Displacement signifies a true breakout or breakdown, not just a routine opening gap. Gaps with displacement are much more significant for future use.
- **How to identify:** Identify a gap; check if the candle(s) forming that gap also clear the prior session's swing high (for upside displacement) or swing low (for downside displacement).
- **How to use:** Prioritize gaps with displacement; they're more likely to serve as reference levels and liquidity magnets throughout the day.
- **Example in this video:** A gap on Friday had displacement because price broke above prior highs; this makes it an FPFVG-D (more significant) vs. plain FPFVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:39](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=99) "displacement is where it's part of a run that takes out the high or low," [0:18:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1109) "displacement here."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Displacement adds conviction to a gap's validity and future use.

### Wick Consequent Encroachment (Midpoint as Support/Resistance)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint of a wick (high + low)/2 acts as a critical support/resistance level. When a candlestick wicks above or below a level but the body closes far from it (not touching or closing in upper/lower half of the wick), the wick's midpoint becomes the next friction point. Price often respects the midpoint for one or two candles before breaking further.
- **How to identify:** Identify a wick; calculate its midpoint; observe candlestick bodies relative to that midpoint on subsequent candles.
- **How to use:** Use wick midpoint as short-term support/resistance for exits and reversal prediction. Bodies failing to close above/below wick midpoint = weakness/strength depending on direction.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's Reflection FVG wick midpoint was monitored; Monday's open wicked above it but bodies stayed below it, signaling weakness (bearish). ICT used this as a signal to go short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:08](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=728) "I'm watching this wick's consequent encroachment, its middle point," [0:12:56](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=776) "trading just to consequent encroachment."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** More granular than full wick support; used for precise short-term reversals.

### Half-Wick Run (50% of Prior Wick Retracement)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price retraces from an extreme (high or low) and reaches exactly 50% of the prior wick's range, that 50% point often acts as a stall point or signal of weakness/strength. If price goes to half-wick and reverses, that's a strong reversal signal. If price reaches half-wick and continues through, the move has strength.
- **How to identify:** Identify a wick (high to low); calculate 50% of its range; mark that level; observe price interaction at that point.
- **How to use:** Half-wick as a micro-target or reversal zone; if price stalls at 50%, expect pullback; if price pierces 50%, expect acceleration.
- **Example in this video:** Post-open, price wicked up through wick midpoint but only reached half of that wick's range upward before reversing, signaling weakness.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:54](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1014) "half of that wick's run up here," [0:16:54](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1014) "that's just one of the sweetest little things."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Micro-level precision; best used in conjunction with macro directional bias.

### Gradient Levels (Pre-Market Session Structure)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The 7:00–9:00 AM ET pre-market session has its own high and low; these act as "gradient levels." When divided into octants and quarters (using Fibonacci), these pre-market levels become support/resistance anchors for the RTH session. Price often gravitats back to these levels intraday.
- **How to identify:** Mark the 7:00 AM and 9:00 AM ET boundaries on a 1-minute chart; identify the high and low between those times; divide using Fibonacci.
- **How to use:** Use gradient levels as secondary targets (after main RTH targets); if price runs and misses RTH target, look for pullback into gradient level octant.
- **Example in this video:** 7:00–9:00 AM gradient levels were used as targets and support zones throughout the day; price repeatedly touched them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:24](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1404) "7:00 to 9:00 in the morning pre-market session," [0:30:36](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1836) "Sibby. Trade up. Bodies stopping dead in its tracks... goes down into the low of 7:00 to 9:00 range."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Pre-market can vary in strength/volatility; post-holiday pre-market is especially weak.

### Post-Holiday Volume Trading Caution
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The trading day immediately after a holiday (e.g., day after Labor Day) tends to have "funky" or erratic price behavior due to pent-up aggression, fear, and behavioral uncertainty. New traders should reduce contract size, avoid chasing the best exit, and target low-hanging fruit (nearest support/resistance) rather than maximum profit. Don't be greedy; take easy wins and exit early.
- **How to identify:** Identify holidays in the trading calendar (Labor Day, Thanksgiving, etc.); mark the trading day immediately after as "caution day."
- **How to use:** Reduce position size by 50–80% on post-holiday days. Target nearest support/resistance only. Exit early rather than holding for optimal profit. Avoid leverage.
- **Example in this video:** Day after Labor Day Monday (trading resumed Tuesday); ICT traded only 1 contract instead of 6, and exited at the first easy target rather than chasing the bigger move lower that materialized.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:41](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1061) "post holiday... can be a little funky," [0:29:00](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1740) "low-hanging fruit objective."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not a rule against trading; rather, a risk-management rule for elevated uncertainty.

### Macro Time Delivery of Gaps
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When a gap (especially FPFVG) forms at a macro time (1:00 AM, 10:50–11:10 AM, etc.), it gains additional significance because it's delivered during a scheduled algorithmic clustering window. This makes the gap more likely to be referenced intraday.
- **How to identify:** Identify the time a gap forms; check if it aligns with a known macro time window.
- **How to use:** Higher conviction when gap forms at macro time; extend it forward with greater emphasis.
- **Example in this video:** Reflection FVG formed at 1:01 AM (macro window boundary); ICT notes it's "macro delivered" and therefore more significant.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:52](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=292) "it's also part of a macro time," [0:07:18](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=438) "It also makes it stronger when it is a macro delivered inefficiency."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Macro delivery is a filter/confirmation, not a standalone signal.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- FPFVG is the very first gap after 9:30 AM ET.
- FPFVG with displacement is much more significant than plain FPFVG; prioritize it.
- If FPFVG is up-close (buy-side), look for first SIBI (down-close) = Reflection FVG.
- If FPFVG is down-close, there is no significant reflection concept.
- Three PD arrays are taught in context: plain FPFVG, FPFVG-D, Reflection FVG.
- Wick midpoint is a friction level; bodies failing to reach it = weakness.
- Half-wick run is a micro-reversal signal.
- Octant divisions (1/8) of gaps are valid micro-targets.
- 7:00–9:00 AM gradient levels are secondary targets.
- Post-holiday: reduce position size, target low-hanging fruit, avoid leverage.
- Macro time delivery (1:00 AM, macro hours) increases gap significance.
- Bodies in upper half of wick = strength; bodies in lower half = weakness.
- If body closes above midpoint of SIBI, expect upside retest; if below, expect lower move.

## Glossary candidates
- **First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)** — The very first candlestick gap after 9:30 AM ET; primary structural reference for the day.
- **First Presented FVG with Displacement (FPFVG-D)** — FPFVG that is also part of a move breaking the prior swing high/low; receives higher emphasis.
- **First Presented Reflection Fair Value Gap** — Mirror-image gap (SIBI) formed after an up-close FPFVG; opposite characteristic.
- **Displacement** — When a gap is part of a move that also breaks prior swing highs/lows; indicates true breakout/breakdown.
- **SIBI** — Sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency; down-close gap indicating weakness.
- **Reflection** — Mirror-image opposite of prior gap characteristic; second-tier but equally important PD array.
- **Wick Consequent Encroachment** — Midpoint of a wick; critical friction level for bodies.
- **Half-Wick Run** — 50% retracement of a wick; micro-reversal signal.
- **Gradient Levels** — High/low of the 7:00–9:00 AM pre-market session; secondary target zone.
- **Macro Delivered** — Gap formed at a macro time (1:00 AM, macro hour boundaries); higher significance.
- **Low-Hanging Fruit** — Nearest, easiest profit target; protocol to prevent greed and manage post-holiday uncertainty.
