## Summary
ICT reviews a Tuesday ES (June 2022 contract) session. His morning bias called for a run down to 4070. The market never reached it and gave no setup, so he walks through how he dropped that bias and switched to bullish. The signals were: the daily FVG held, a short-term high was taken (MSS), price returned to a 15m FVG, ES showed SMT against NQ, and the low formed at 9:10 ET below the midnight open. He then traded the daily range with a long during the NY lunch hour. He bought sell stops below a short-term low inside a discount FVG and managed it with partials. Along the way he defines "narrative", contrasts his FVG idea with Chris Lori's liquidity voids, and separates the YouTube scalping model from day-trading the daily range. He also gives his rules for consolidation vs retracement into lunch, the TradingView SMT setup, four alternative entry variations, and his contract-rollover rule.

## Concepts

### Narrative / Draw on liquidity  (aliases: where the market is likely to go next)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Narrative is knowing what price should do, why, and what it will run into along the way that proves the assumed narrative is actually underway.
  - He says he is not teaching entry patterns where a precise entry price is what matters most. He is teaching how to work out where the market is likely to go next (the DOL).
  - The DOL is set algorithmically, not by "buying/selling pressure". Read price and the logic behind it instead of basing trades on indicators (overbought/oversold, line crossovers).
  - Once you can read price, entries are "the easiest thing to do".
- **How to identify:** First mark the higher-timeframe objective (here the daily FVG, prior-day high, relative equal highs/lows). Then watch for events at key times that confirm or reject it.
- **How to use:** The narrative alone is not an entry. Even after he believed price would run to the prior-day high, he says buying right away could have stopped him out by being early. He waited for displacement, measured premium/discount, and only bought in a discount.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m. Price traded down into the 15m FVG and rallied from it. That told him the algorithm was "priming itself" for an afternoon run toward the previous day's high (4168.25). At 10:35 ET he tweeted "note previous day's high".

### Abandoning / changing bias  (aliases: changing gears, managing your bias)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The main lesson is how to abandon a bias and "change gears". Sometimes the right move is not to reverse but to step aside and do nothing.
  - These were the signs that the bearish 4070 idea was wrong:
    - multiple lows were taken, but price reversed without offering a high-probability short;
    - the 4070 target never traded, leaving liquidity in place;
    - price stayed inside the daily FVG and did not break below it;
    - a short-term high was taken, giving a bullish market structure shift;
    - price returned to the new FVG and was supported;
    - SMT: ES made a higher low while NQ made a lower low.
  - Once the market showed this, he gave up the morning session. He waited for the algorithm to show that the afternoon session was being priced as bullish, because early in the day price "could still wipe out that low".
- **How to identify:** The objective fails to print. HTF PD array support holds. A short-term high is taken. Price retraces into the new imbalance and is supported. SMT shows accumulation.
- **How to use:** Do not panic or ask others for their opinion. Rebuild the bias from what the market did at key times (9:30 volatility), then wait for a setup that fits the new narrative.
- **Example in this video:** ES daily. Initial DOL was 4070, below relative equal lows. Price made a low of 4076.00 and did not trade below the daily FVG. The low of the day formed at 9:10 ET, with a rally at the 9:30 open.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:22](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=142) — main takeaways: abandoning bias, changing gears, NY lunch; [0:03:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=189) — multiple lows taken with no short opportunity; [0:44:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2661) — gives up the morning session and waits.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Did I lose any money? No." Not trading a wrong idea is not a loss.

### Fair Value Gap vs liquidity void "fill" (Chris Lori comparison)  (aliases: imbalance, fair value)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Chris Lori teaches that the market comes back to fill liquidity voids completely. ICT does not teach that. Full fills can happen, but because he understands the algorithm, it is not likely most of the time.
  - What makes an imbalance a fair value gap is that price comes back down into it (into part of it). It is "a fair value to buy it if you're bullish." He does not insist imbalances completely rebalance.
  - His precision comes from knowing where the market is reaching, then using the imbalances along the way.
- **How to use:** Treat FVGs as places to buy (in a bullish narrative) on a partial return, not as zones that must fully fill.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m. After a short-term MSS higher, price created an FVG and traded back into it. Several imbalances along the leg were each partially revisited.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:43](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=283) — Chris Lori liquidity voids vs ICT; [0:05:31](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=331) — "fair value to buy it if you're bullish".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He calls Chris Lori the only technical analysis educator he recommends, while stressing the "stark contrast" in approach.

### Market Structure Shift + FVG retrace (not support/resistance)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the run took out the short-term high on the daily/15m, he calls it an "absolute market structure shift bullish".
  - Price came back and rebalanced the FVG and dug slightly past the broken high. That is why he is not trading "resistance broken turns support".
  - His logic: price went through a short-term high, created an imbalance, then traded back into the imbalance.
- **How to identify:** A short-term high is taken with displacement and an FVG is left behind. Watch for the retrace into that FVG, not into the broken high.
- **How to use:** The FVG is the area to watch. If price digs into it, repels higher, and takes out the short-term high, that sets the stage for the afternoon move.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m: price ran higher, left an FVG, and traded back into it after the 9:30 open. On 5m it "doesn't look like" an FVG unless you carry the 15m zone down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:43](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=523) — 15m FVG is the area to watch for the afternoon; [0:09:30](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=570) — MSS; digs past the high into the FVG.

### Multi-timeframe workflow: 15m bellwether into 5m
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The 15m chart is the "bellwether chart".
  - Shade the 15m FVG on your chart, then drop to the 5m and watch price trade into the shaded area.
  - If you stay on one timeframe and do not carry HTF analysis down, "you're gonna be trading blind".
  - Same with the daily FVG: shade it, and on the 5m you will see price dip into its lower end and rally.
- **How to use:** Mark the daily PD array, then the 15m FVG, then time entries on the 5m.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:04](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=664) — shade the 15m FVG, drop to 5m; [0:28:22](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1702) — daily FVG shaded, seen from 5m.

### NY lunch hour / PM session: consolidation vs retracement into lunch
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bullish PM rule (from earlier notes): price runs higher in the morning, consolidates into lunch, then after lunch drops to sweep sell stops below a short-term low or below the lunch lows, then rallies.
  - If price is consolidating into lunch: mark the swing lows during the lunch hour, wait for price to sweep them, then rally.
  - If price is retracing (not consolidating) into lunch, that signals "they're going to work through lunch". It is a fast market.
  - Floor-trader origin: in the open-outcry days, floor traders stayed on the floor through lunch in a fast market. The algorithm keeps that same behaviour.
  - The NY lunch hour is noon–1:00 PM NY time.
- **How to identify:** Ask whether price is going sideways (consolidation) or making a directional pullback (retracement) going into lunch.
- **How to use:** In a fast-market retracement, look for a short-term low forming ahead of lunch. Then look for the sweep of that low into a discount FVG during lunch.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m. After failing at 4140, price dropped into an FVG and rallied, leaving smooth relative equal highs. It created a short-term low ahead of noon. During the lunch hour it dropped below that low into the discount FVG, then rallied through 4140 and the old high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:01](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=901) — PM session and lunch introduced; [0:19:44](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1184) — bullish PM liquidity rule; [0:20:30](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1230) — retracement, not consolidation; [0:22:05](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1325) — "work through lunch", floor-trader origin.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This extends the earlier PM-session rules by adding the fast-market/retracement case.

### Dealing range, premium/discount, and choosing the swing to measure
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When trading the daily range, factor in the midnight opening price. Price had already gone above it, and he did not expect it to go back below the earlier low. So measuring from the earlier low is "pointless".
  - He used the market-structure swing low (the one that started the displacement) to the swing high as the dealing range.
  - He wanted a retracement to a discount (below 50%) and the FVG there. "The algorithm reprices to a discount."
  - Checklist: Was there displacement? Was it an energetic run? Measure low to high, wait for a discount, find the FVG below 50%.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m. Fib from the swing low to the swing high: 50% was below, with an FVG under it. Price traded into it, made a short-term low, then dipped again into the FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:16](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1276) — why that swing low (midnight open factor); [0:23:38](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1418) — algorithm reprices to a discount; [0:45:10](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2710) — dealing range low/high, fib, 50%, FVG.

### Buying sell stops below a short-term low (stop run inside the FVG)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Smooth relative equal highs are seen as resistance. Shorts trail their stops just above them, and sell stops sit below the nearby short-term low.
  - Think like smart money: buy sell-side liquidity, with the order resting under the short-term low inside the FVG.
  - He prefers buying the sell stops because "it really fortifies the setup" and gets him in where smart money buys. Had price rallied without the sweep, he would simply have missed the move.
- **How to use:** Place a buy limit just below the short-term low that sits inside the discount FVG. Stop goes below the FVG / lower low.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m, NY lunch hour. Short-term low at 4110.50. His fill was at 4110.25, one tick below it, on the candle whose low was 4108.25. Price rallied, consolidated, pushed through the old high and the 4140 level, and later returned to the FVG once more (an OTE) and rallied again. It still did not reach the prior-day high of 4168.25.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:58](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1798) — buying sell stops, entry candle; [0:36:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2181) — execution details (4110.50 low, 4110.25 fill); [0:46:00](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2760) — "I'm buying that very candle".

### SMT divergence (ES vs NQ) and the TradingView setup  (aliases: smart money tool / technique)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - SMT is "smart money tool or smart money technique" (he never settled on what the T stands for). It is his way of measuring real accumulation and distribution.
  - When bullish, compare lows: for example, ES makes a higher low while NQ makes a lower low. The stronger index is under accumulation, and the weaker index's lower low is "just a stop hunt" below a short-term low.
  - Use it when price retraces into a discount FVG and you expect a move up.
  - TradingView setup: click the "+" compare tab, add NQM2022 (later NQU2022), open its gear settings, and set it to plot the low, because you are comparing lows when bullish.
- **Example in this video:** ES vs NQ. (1) At the morning low near 9:10 ET, ES made a higher low while NQ made a lower low, which set the stage for the run up. (2) At the lunch-hour retracement into the FVG, a further SMT appeared on the low compare.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:48](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=948) — ES higher low vs NQ lower low; [0:39:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2373) — TradingView compare setup; [0:41:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2469) — compare lows inside the FVG after a discount retrace; [0:46:48](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2808) — recap.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says this is how you are supposed to talk to yourself in your journal: "this low versus this low".

### Entry variations on the same bullish setup  (aliases: OTE, turtle soup, FVG entry, buy stop above swing high)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** In answer to "which FVG do I use", he gives several ways to enter the same idea:
  1. **OTE:** fib from the low to the high and buy the 62–79% retracement.
  2. **Turtle soup:** a low, then a lower low violating it, with the OTE on top of that.
  3. **FVG in discount:** low to high, find the FVG below the 50% level, and buy when price trades to the top of it. Stop below the lower low.
  4. **Buy stop above the swing high:** wait for price to trade into the FVG, then put a buy stop above the swing high formed just before price entered the FVG. Stop just below the lowest low after it hits. This is "buying strength". It is not his preferred strategy.
- **How to use:** All of these depend on a bias that has been "corrected and calibrated" by what price did at key times (9:30).
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:46](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2566) — OTE, turtle soup, FVG below 50%; [0:43:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2613) — buy stop above swing high (not preferred).

### Trade management: partials at old imbalances / old buy-side liquidity, stop raising
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- When bullish, buy at or near the midnight opening price. The Power of 3 low of day forms below it. Here it formed at 9:10 ET.
- 9:30 RTH open brings the volatility that he uses to correct and calibrate the bias.
- NY lunch hour is noon–1:00 PM NY time.
- Bullish PM: morning rally, consolidation into lunch, post-lunch sweep of short-term or lunch lows, rally.
- If price is retracing into lunch rather than consolidating, it is a fast market and they "work through lunch". Look for the sweep of the short-term low into a discount FVG during lunch.
- Dealing range = the market-structure swing low to the swing high. Wait for a discount (below 50%) and an FVG there.
- OTE = 62–79% retracement.
- When bullish, compare lows for SMT. Set the TradingView compare series to plot the "low".
- Roll futures contracts when the next contract's open interest exceeds the current one (check barchart.com).
- The 15m chart is the bellwether chart. Shade 15m FVGs and carry them to the 5m.
- The YouTube model is a scalping model. Daily-range trading uses the 15m and 5m.
- Once price has respected an FVG twice, it "should never come back down" into it. If it does, close remaining exposure.
- Levels this day (ES Jun-22): initial DOL 4070; actual low 4076.00; prior-day high 4168.25; intermediate level 4140; entry fill 4110.25 (short-term low 4110.50); partials at 4134.50 and 4141.50.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Not taking a trade is not losing. Not reaching a public call (4070) is fine: "I don't need to be right." ([0:18:56](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1136))
- Trust yourself. Do not reach out on social media for others' opinions when price moves against your idea. ([0:08:43](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=523))
- Do not decide trades with indicators. Read price and its logic, especially with real money. ([0:07:06](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=426))
- Patience in both stages: waiting for the setup, and letting the trade play out once you are in. Sitting at the screen all day does not mean you "deserve" a trade. ([0:44:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2661), [0:45:10](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2710))
- You do not need the highest high or lowest low. The lion's portion of the middle of the move is enough to be profitable. ([0:38:47](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2327))
- He took partials partly to protect against being wrong (a possible failure swing at the old relative equal highs). ([0:37:10](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2230))
- Talk to yourself in your journal in this comparative language ("this low versus this low"). ([0:41:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2469))
- He managed the trade from his cell phone while away from the screens. ([0:25:13](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1513))
- Take your time learning, even a year. "It's going to get real hard." ([0:35:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2133))

## Glossary candidates
`Bellwether chart` — the 15m chart, used as the reference timeframe for intraday FVGs carried down to the 5m.
`Narrative` — what price should do, why, and what it must run into to prove that the expected move is underway.
`Changing gears` — abandoning a failed bias and either reversing it or standing aside.
`Work through lunch` — in a fast market price retraces (rather than consolidates) through the noon–1 PM lunch hour and delivers there, based on floor-trader behaviour.
`Classic buy day` — midnight open, drop to form the low of day, rally, then an OTE / FVG retrace or a short-term low run with SMT.
`Buying sell stops` — resting a buy below a short-term low (inside a discount FVG) to get filled on the stop run, as smart money does.
`SMT (smart money tool/technique)` — comparing correlated indices (ES vs NQ lows when bullish) to spot real accumulation or distribution.
`Stinger` — ICT's short Twitter video clips showing live analysis or executions.
