## Summary
ICT reviews Wednesday March 11, 2026 (CPI day, MNQ) and uses it to release one of the two new PD arrays he promised for 2026. The idea: take the previous day's 2-hour New York lunch window; if a turtle-soup style reversal occurs after liquidity is taken in it, mark the inefficiency (FVG with volume imbalance) that formed right before that liquidity was taken, extend it forward into the next day, and expect price to reprice into it as a high- or low-of-day reversal point. He shows how price traded up into that carried-forward gap after CPI, collapsed, and ran to relative equal lows at 24,892.50, walks through his one-micro short with pyramiding, and introduces "event horizon" (the midpoint between two liquidity pools) as an intermediate target. ## Concepts

### NY Lunch Reversal Inefficiency (new PD array, previous day carried forward)  (aliases: "the new PD array", "yesterday's lunch inefficiency"; ICT gives no formal name in this video)
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry model (PD array)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Look at the *previous day's* New York lunch window, a 2-hour window ending 1:30 PM ET. If there is a reversal after liquidity is taken in that window, find the inefficiency (fair value gap) that formed *immediately before the liquidity was taken*, and carry it forward into the next day.
  - If price trades up into it the next day, it can set the tone for a short (if the lunch event was a buy-side raid); reverse for longs.
  - You pick the gap formed right before the liquidity is taken, not the gap one candle earlier. He says it matters because this gap belongs to a complex reversal at a macro time (lunch), a time when reversals take place.
  - Contrast stated by ICT: this is not classic support/resistance. He does not draw a line on the old high; he marks the inefficiency that sets the reversal. "Everything in here sets the stage for revisiting these specific PD arrays."
  - He says he will release two new PD arrays this year and this is one of them. Nobody taught it; he says it came from his own research.
  - Homework: go back through old price action, find the 2-hour lunch periods with a turtle soup short or long, mark the first FVG right before the liquidity is taken, carry it to the next day, and observe whether the next day's high or low forms there ("many times").
- **How to identify:**
  1. Previous-day NY lunch window (he marks it with vertical lines; lunch ends 1:30 PM ET; 1:30–2:00 is the PM opening range).
  2. Liquidity is taken near the end of lunch (example: relative equal highs into 1:31; buy-side raided just ahead of the 1:30 close, "not random").
  3. The candle sequence just before the raid shows a buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (BISI-type gap with a volume imbalance).
  4. Reversal follows (turtle soup).
- **How to use:**
  - Extend the gap's boundaries to the right into the next session as a rectangle (he drew thin ray lines on his phone and used a rectangle for the recording).
  - Next day, when price trades up into the carried gap (after the 8:30 data, for example) look for shorts; the gap behaves as an inversion FVG (bodies respect it).
  - Other confluences he layered: yesterday's RTH opening range gap (and its CE), first instance of FVG, sell-side liquidity pool at 24,892.50.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ (micro Nasdaq), Tue Mar 10 into Wed Mar 11, 2026.
  - Tue, near the end of lunch: the candle just before the buy-side raid has a close of 25,165.75; the next candle opens 25,166.00 (higher than that close), so a volume imbalance exists and the gap is anchored from 25,165.75 (that candle's close) up to the candle low at 25,177.50 (he says "low here 25,177 and 1/2"). That is the lunch BISI with volume imbalance at its low.
  - Tue PM: after the buy-side raid and 1:30 close of lunch, price retraced lower in the PM session (sell-side liquidity pool + first-instance FVG + the BISI turning into an inversion gap). It dumped through the first-instance FVG, respected CE with bodies, traded down into Tuesday's RTH opening range gap low, reacted, then went up into an inefficiency to close the day.
  - Wed: overnight, then 8:30 CPI ("wonky"); price traded down into old order flow, was back inside Tuesday's RTH opening range gap, then ran up into the carried 25,165.75–25,177.50 level; the bodies stopped at it, price opened just slightly beyond it, then collapsed (he calls it "a complex reversal").
  - Mechanics later in the day: price ran to sell-side at 24,892.50 (two ticks beyond, to 24,892.00), then rallied back in the afternoon; at 1:30 ET it traded into the gap that had become an inversion FVG, then dived to a candle low of 24,873.75 near the event-horizon midpoint (see below).

### Volume imbalance inclusion in a BISI (anchoring rule)  (aliases: buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency, volume imbalance at the low)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** To include a volume imbalance in a bullish gap, the next candle's open must be higher than the previous candle's close. Anchor the gap's bottom to that previous candle's close (not to its body-high or wick). Example: close 25,165.75; next open 25,166.00 or higher qualifies (he states "anything like 25,166 even or higher"). He pulls prices from the candle data window to verify.
- **How to use:** The gap becomes a "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency with a volume imbalance at the low".
- **Example in this video:** the 25,165.75 close / 25,166.00 open / 25,177.50 low example above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:17](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=197) — the open-versus-close test; [0:04:05](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=245) — "volume imbalance at the low"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Consistent with his 2026 teaching to include volume imbalances inside the FVG boundary (see also L26-113).

### Inversion FVG / reclaimed bullish FVG from a BISI  (aliases: iFVG, inversion fair value gap, reclaimed bullish FVG, nested gaps)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - A BISI such as the lunch gap changes character to an inversion FVG once price trades through and fails; bodies then respect it as resistance, so price trading up to it is a reason to anticipate lower prices.
  - Multiple gaps can nest ("this one here also nests together"), producing a very strong inversion area; this is "why you got that big pop" up into it.
  - On Wednesday morning a short-term high formed at 9:42; the next gap above (BISI) was expected to act as inversion as well. First it offered discount sensitivity (price traded down into the gap and rallied), then came back up to the BISI, which became a reclaimed bullish FVG; price ran above the 9:42 high into his level, which is the turtle soup, then sold off.
  - Time-frame note: his inversion FVG on the lower time frame is anchored to a sub-1-minute model; on the 1-minute you can anchor from one candle's high to the other candle's low and be fine.
- **How to use:** Short on the way down inside the gap, on a down-close candle (see Execution).
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:53](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=293) — BISI changes to inversion FVG; [0:12:06](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=726) — nested reclaimed bullish FVG, turtle soup; [0:12:52](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=772) — sub-1-minute anchoring tip

### Event Horizon (new coinage)  (aliases: midpoint between two liquidity pools)
- **Kind:** new coinage / definition
- **What ICT says:** Event horizon is the 50% midpoint between two pools of liquidity. Anchor a fib from one pool to the next and show only the 50% level. He says nobody has this in their books and he learned it from nobody. Used as an intermediate objective on the way to the next pool: he wanted price to reach it before rolling his stop just above a pool of liquidity and then trying for the next pool.
- **How to identify:** Two liquidity pools (e.g., the relative equal lows at 24,892.50 and the next pool below at what he calls "54 quarter", presumably 24,854.25). Fib 24,892.50 to 24,854.25, set to show only 50%.
- **How to use:** Target/partial reference; stop management (roll stop to just above the pool once event horizon is hit); stay near the pool region.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ Wed Mar 11: draw 24,892.50 → 24,854.25 (midpoint is about 24,873.4); later price dived to a candle low of 24,873.75 at the 1:30 PM turn (he notes 24,873.25 on one candle, "slightly higher" than the midpoint, "drawing toward that midpoint").
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:13](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=913) — defines event horizon; [0:16:01](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=961) — how he planned to manage it; [0:17:40](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=1060) — price reaction near midpoint

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- NY lunch = 2-hour window ending 1:30 PM ET; PM-session opening range = 1:30–2:00 PM ET (indices).
- Volume imbalance in a BISI exists when the next open is higher than the prior close (25,165.75 close vs 25,166.00 open here).
- Choose the FVG formed right before the liquidity is taken during lunch, not an earlier one.
- Carry that gap into the next day; reaction on a retest can form the day's high/low.
- Event horizon = 50% between two liquidity pools.
- Pre-announced levels: 24,892.50 relative equal lows (set pre-9:00 ET Tuesday) and Tuesday's RTH ORG.
- Don't predict CPI; let it run, then fade, especially in a range.
- Short-term high formed at 9:42 ET on Wednesday.
- MNQ: $2 per point per micro contract.

`BISI (buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency)` — bullish fair value gap.
`NY lunch reversal inefficiency` — (descriptive, no formal name given) the FVG just before a lunch-window liquidity raid, carried into the next day.
