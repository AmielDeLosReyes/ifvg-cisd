## Summary
A live recording of the NQ short that ICT discusses in the NFP review (video 13), made on NFP Friday of Labor Day weekend. The captions begin about 4 minutes in, so the entry itself is not heard. He is short from the high of the RTH opening range gap (the 9:30 open price), which is also the top of a daily volume imbalance and a daily buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency he has been tracking for about three weeks. The targets are the sell-side below the NFP low (formed inside Thursday's NDOG), Thursday Aug 27's first presented FVG, and the 20%/30% TGIF retracement levels. He ratchets the stop down as price falls, argues from a market-maker perspective why the relative equal highs and the longs' stops would not be left alone, then closes manually before the target, naming the pattern a market maker sell model (second-stage redistribution). He closes with a warning to avoid NFP weeks after 11:00 a.m. Wednesday.

## Concepts
### Short from the RTH opening range gap high (9:30 open) with stacked PD arrays  (aliases: "high of the opening range gap", "daily volume imbalance", "daily bias on the balance sell side inefficiency", "new week opening gap high")
- **Kind:** example only (entry model application)
- **What ICT says:** Price traded up into the high of that day's RTH opening range gap, i.e. the 9:30 opening price. That level coincides with (a) the high of a daily buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (as heard: "daily bias on the balance sell side inefficiency", caption unclear), (b) the upper portion of a daily volume imbalance he shaded gray and has been discussing for about three weeks, and (c) the high of the new week opening gap (purple shaded area). Price "didn't want to take the liquidity out of up here" (the relative equal highs stayed intact), so he expected it to come down for the stops below. He later notes it was also the high of the daily volume imbalance (blue area) and gets "a lot of reaction there".
- **How to identify:** RTH ORG high at the 9:30 open; overlapping higher-timeframe imbalance (daily volume imbalance / daily inefficiency) and NWOG high; relative equal highs left untouched above.
- **How to use:** Short from the premium array, stop above, target the sell side below and the first presented FVG of the previous day. See stop/management below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Aug 28 after 9:30. Price rallies to the 9:30 open (gap high) and stalls; relative equal highs above remain untouched. Price then declines through the NWOG low and the RTH ORG low, into the NFP low and the NDOG.

### Market maker logic: why would they leave the longs' stops?  (aliases: "think like a market maker")
- **Kind:** refinement / reasoning
- **What ICT says:** Ask why, if you were the market maker, you would let longs keep their stop loss right below here after taking price down past the ORG low and to the low of the NWOG. The relative equal highs were left intact above, and longs are trying to hold for the whole day with stops sitting just below. So the expectation is a run down for those stops ("hot knife through butter") rather than a bounce. He adds that there is NFP liquidity resting just below 29,500, and that a "last kiss" retest of the ORG low before going lower is typical.
- **How to identify:** Price has already gone through the gap low and a prior-week gap low, longs' stops sit just below, equal highs are untouched.
- **How to use:** Hold the short for the stop run rather than expect a bounce; keep the stop on the gap side. When price breaks a prior low, lock in profit with a tighter stop, but not immediately, in case of a stop-hunting pop.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: price drops through ORG low, hits NWOG low ("banging the low of the new week opening gap"), clears the NFP low, retests the ORG low once more as the "last kiss before the train leaves", and then keeps dropping.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:57](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=537) — "hot knife through butter", longs holding with stops below, equal highs left intact; [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=774) — "think you're the market maker" argument; [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=822) — repeated; [0:17:57](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1077) — touches the ORG low again, "last kiss".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Presented partly as banter, but it is the logic behind the sell-side-first expectation.

### Market Maker Sell Model — second-stage redistribution  (aliases: "universal model", "smart money reversal", "low-risk sell", "MMSM")
- **Kind:** entry model (naming of the model applied)
- **What ICT says:** What he was trading is a Market Maker Sell Model: original consolidation, then first-stage distribution, second-stage redistribution, and a smart money reversal, with the low-risk sell at the second stage, taking out that low. This is his primary, "universal" model: he likes the second-stage distribution/redistribution of a sell model and the second-stage reaccumulation of a market maker buy model. Taught more fully in his 2016 and 2017 mentorship playlists and another playlist (caption unclear, "charter" as heard). He says it is very close to the most openly he will ever teach the model he trades predominantly.
- **How to identify:** (per the label list) original consolidation, first-stage distribution, second-stage redistribution, smart money reversal; enter at the redistribution stage.
- **How to use:** Wait for the redistribution stage, not the first stage.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:12](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1392) — "can you identify what I was trading?" lists the stages; [0:24:00](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1440) — universal model, mentorship playlists, buy-side equivalent.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Does not draw the model stages on a diagram in the captions; refer to the 2016/2017 mentorship videos for definitions.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Lock in profit in steps but do not move the stop so soon that a stop-hunt pop takes you out ([0:12:54](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=774)).
- Accept risking open profit to get the full move ("you want 10 grand, you got to risk being stopped out") but do not give back more than about 5,000 unrealized ([0:20:09](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1209)).
- Close manually when the idea is no longer wanted even if the target might still hit ([0:22:23](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1343)).
- Think like the market maker: ask why stops would be left alone ([0:12:54](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=774)).
- Live execution rather than market replay is stressed repeatedly ([0:08:09](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=489), [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=822)).
- Banter content (Deadpool technique, "Crayola model", jokes about critics and heat maps) carries no rule; the Deadpool joke is a gag about dragging the live candle.
