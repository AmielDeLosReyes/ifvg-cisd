## Concepts

### Buy-Side Taken / Inefficiency Traded To (aliases: liquidity sweep, buy-side liquidation)
- **Kind:** pattern description
- **What ICT says:** When price breaks above a resistance level and sweeps buy-side liquidity (stops above that resistance), that's "buy-side taken." After the sweep, price often reverses or corrects. This is institutional targeting stop losses. Once taken, use the inefficiency it created as an iFVG target on the retracement.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a spike above a level (buy-side), then a quick pullback or reversal.
- **How to use:** Once buy-side is taken, expect price to return into the inefficiency left behind. Use that as a short or reversal target.
- **Example in this video:** Dollar Index: "buy-side that was taken there. We pulled that back down into this inefficiency and use this as an inversion fair value gap."
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:47](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=47) (buy-side taken, pulled back, used as iFVG).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Buy-side taken is followed by iFVG usage; it's a two-step pattern.

### Premium Array vs. Discount Array (aliases: upper/lower inefficiency zones)
- **Kind:** zone classification
- **What ICT says:** A premium array is the upper portion of a range or inefficiency (where price is expensive). A discount array is the lower portion (where price is cheap). When price breaks below an inefficiency, the inefficiency becomes a premium array on retracements (price unlikely to reach it again, more likely to reverse below). When price breaks above, it becomes a discount array (cheap, likely to be retested).
- **How to identify:** Mark an inefficiency; note which side price breaks below/above; that determines whether it's now premium or discount.
- **How to use:** Premium arrays are resistance on retracements; discount arrays are support on retracements. Use these terms interchangeably with support/resistance, but understand the institutional context.
- **Example in this video:** Pound Dollar: "should we trade lower, they should treat it as a premium array and then attack that minor sell-side."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:57](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=237) (treat as premium array).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Premium/discount are directional terms; they clarify whether an inefficiency is now a resistance or support zone based on which side broke.

### Mohawk Wick (aliases: small wick, acceptable wick)
- **Kind:** iFVG validation detail
- **What ICT says:** When validating an iFVG, small wicks (called "mohawk" wicks for their small, sharp appearance) into the opposite side of the gap are allowed. They don't disqualify the iFVG. It's when BODIES fail to reach consequent encroachment that the iFVG is confirmed. Wicks are noise; bodies are order flow.
- **How to identify:** Look for small, sharp wicks into the gap's upper or lower side while bodies stay on the opposite side.
- **How to use:** Don't invalidate an iFVG because of a small wick. Check where bodies are trading; that's the signal.
- **Example in this video:** Euro Dollar: "It's a tiny tiny little mohawk, I call it. And then we distribute." Wicks into the gap are OK; bodies staying inside = iFVG confirmed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:23](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=143) (tiny little mohawk; body stays inside).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Mohawk is Michael's casual term; the principle is that small wicks don't break iFVG structure.

### Stop Hunt / Stop Loss Sweep (aliases: stop hunt, liquidity grab, spike and reverse)
- **Kind:** institutional pattern
- **What ICT says:** A stop hunt is when institutional traders spike price to sweep stops (take out stop losses) and then reverse. Example: Crude oil spike higher to take out sell-side stops, then reverse down. This is predatory order flow targeting retail stop losses. Recognize it and don't get caught.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a sharp spike or trend reversal that seems unmotivated by news, followed by a quick reversal (often at the market open or during thin liquidity).
- **How to use:** When you spot a stop hunt, don't chase the spike. Wait for the reversal and trade in the opposite direction.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: "to me, I you know, I I I suspect that crude oil's going to go higher. Nothing's really changed over in the Middle East... But this market's going to be affected greatly because of that." Michael suspects crude will be manipulated (stop hunted) and warns to stay away.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:32](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=332) (this was a stop hunt).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Stop hunts are often news-driven (Judas swings); use order flow, not headlines.

### Capitulation High (aliases: exhaustion high, distribution high)
- **Kind:** pattern + reversal signal
- **What ICT says:** A capitulation high is when price makes a series of highs (high, higher high, possibly an even higher high) in a parabolic fashion, exhausting buyers. Once the final higher high is made and price sells off, that's capitulation—institutional sellers finishing distribution and dumping the market to retail. After capitulation, expect significant downside.
- **How to identify:** Watch for three+ progressively higher highs with increasingly sharp reversals; then a hard close below the range. That's capitulation.
- **How to use:** After capitulation highs, expect much lower prices. Use them as shorting opportunities or risk reduction signals if you're long.
- **Example in this video:** Dow: "we have three highs. So we have a high, high, higher high and then giving up the ghost." Michael identifies this as capitulation high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:31](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=571) (three highs: high, high, higher high, giving up the ghost = capitulation).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Capitulation highs are distribution patterns; they're followed by much lower prices.

### Relative Equal Lows (aliases: REL, equal lows, liquidity pool)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Two or more lows at the same price level within a range or across multiple days. These are pools of sell-side liquidity (resting sell orders). When price breaks below RELs and shows commitment (bodies stay down), expect lower prices.
- **How to identify:** Scan the daily chart backward; find two or more local lows within a few ticks of each other.
- **How to use:** RELs are targets and stop-out points. Price often tests and breaks through them cleanly. Use as partial-profit zones for shorts.
- **Example in this video:** Euro Dollar, Pound Dollar, ES, NQ: all had relative equal lows marked as targets for lower prices.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:23](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=143) (relative equal lows where sell-side resides) · [0:12:38](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=758) (relatively equal lows on ES).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** RELs are clusters of institutional sell orders; they're sweeps that accelerate moves down.

### Sell-Side Liquidity Pool and Minor Sell-Side (aliases: SSL, minor SSL, secondary pool)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A sell-side liquidity pool is a zone where sell orders cluster (above/below a level). Minor sell-side is an intermediate pool; major sell-side is the deepest or furthest away. Use minor sell-side for first targets; major for best-case targets.
- **How to identify:** Identify inefficiencies at different price levels; mark each as minor (closer) or major (further away).
- **How to use:** Use minor SSL for partial profit; major SSL for runner targets. If price sweeps minor SSL, expect continuation to major SSL.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple markets had identified minor and major sell-side pools: Euro, Pound, Gold, Silver, etc.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:36](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=96) (relative equal lows where sell-side resides) · [0:03:57](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=237) (minor sell-side and more prominent sell-side liquidity).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Minor/major classifications depend on proximity; closer inefficiency = minor.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) with Consequent Encroachment Validation (aliases: CE close validation)
- **Kind:** rule + validation
- **What ICT says:** An iFVG is confirmed when: (1) price trades through/beyond it, (2) on retrace, bodies fail to reach CE, (3) close is outside the iFVG (below for bearish iFVG). Check the close price against CE: if close < CE, it's a confirmed bearish iFVG. This is the definitive validation.
- **How to identify:** Mark the FVG high and low; identify CE. Watch for a close outside the FVG below (for bearish) or above (for bullish) CE.
- **How to use:** Once iFVG is confirmed by close, expect continuation in that direction.
- **Example in this video:** Crude Oil: "If we were unable to close below the consequent encouragement level. See, look at the close here. Look at that close. 87.76 and that's lower than 88.25, which is consequent encouragement of that wick right there."
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:46](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=286) (close 87.76 < CE 88.25 = confirmed bearish iFVG).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The close is the validation; don't rely on wicks or intraday swings.

### Sell-Side Imbalance / Buy-Side Inefficiency (aliases: bearish inefficiency, weak buyer structure)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When there's sell-side imbalance (sellers willing at lower prices) and buy-side inefficiency (no buyers at those lower prices), it creates a bearish gap/inefficiency. Price is likely to drop into it.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candle that opens lower than the previous close; sellers show willingness at lower; buyers absent.
- **How to use:** Use as a target for shorts or as a bounce zone on retracements.
- **Example in this video:** Pound Dollar: "sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency below the inversion fair value gap."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:10](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=190) (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is the opposite of buy-side imbalance/sell-side efficiency (bullish).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Inversion FVG: bodies fail CE on retracement; close is outside the gap = confirmed.
- Mohawk wicks allowed in iFVGs; bodies are the signal, not wicks.
- Stop hunts: spike with no fundamental reason = likely reversal coming.
- Capitulation high: three+ progressively higher highs = distribution = lower prices follow.
- Buy-side taken = institution sweeping stops = expect retracement into inefficiency.
- Premium array: upper zone of inefficiency; resistance on retracements.
- Discount array: lower zone of inefficiency; support on retracements.
- Minor sell-side: first target; major sell-side: runner target.
- Relative equal lows: liquidity clusters; breaks through them = acceleration down.
- Close validation: confirm iFVG by close price vs. CE, not by wicks.
