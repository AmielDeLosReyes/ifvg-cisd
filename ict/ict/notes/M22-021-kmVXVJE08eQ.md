## Summary
ICT explains risk-on/risk-off: a rising dollar is a flight to quality that pressures foreign currencies and stock indices, and shows that DXY's called low (~99.92) coincided with ES topping inside a daily bearish order block he had pointed to, aided by May's seasonal weakness and daily relative equal lows as the draw. He then clarifies how he uses the New York midnight opening price (London session and daily Power of 3) versus the 8:30 opening price (New York session Power of 3), and what 9:30 means (equity open volatility). The key lesson: on this day (late April 2022, a Tuesday) ES never rallied above either opening price while bearish, which marks an *extremely* heavy market. The normal 8:30 model doesn't form, so you either take small FVG pockets in the direction of the move or stand aside and tape read. He also states a "two FVG" entry/stop rule, his intraday-only holding period (90 min–2 h), and admits he misread the day's expected rally.

## Concepts

### Risk-on / Risk-off and DXY intermarket  (aliases: flight to quality, safe haven, dollar up / foreign currency down)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Dollar up → foreign currencies less likely to have sustained rallies, more likely sustained declines; dollar down → the opposite. A rising dollar is a *risk-off* scenario: a flight to quality/safe haven where money pours into the dollar and out of risk assets (foreign currencies, stock market, index futures). Everything he teaches is universal across asset classes (he personally trades only forex and futures, not crypto).
- **How to use:** Use DXY direction to frame bias for EURUSD and for index futures (inverse relationship).
- **Example in this video:** DXY hourly: the low he called publicly (~99.92, "if I were making the market") formed and DXY rallied through relative equal highs toward his 102/103 objectives; at the same time ES (June 2022 contract) turned lower from its daily bearish order block — inverted relationship. EURUSD made the new daily low he'd been calling.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=100) — dollar up/foreign down, risk on/off; [0:02:28](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=148) — flight to quality, applies to index markets; [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=243) — DXY 99.92 low then REH run; [0:04:51](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=291) — DXY vs ES inverted relationship.

### Draw on liquidity — daily relative equal lows ("heaviness objective")  (aliases: DOL, relative equal lows, sell-side)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** With the daily draw being relative equal lows below, each daily candle should expand lower toward them. Prices keep going lower because they gravitate toward those lows; "the algorithm is marching every minute, every hour, every day to that level." He doesn't know how it will trade once there (violent acceleration vs sharp retracement) and doesn't care yet. While price is above those daily REL, any imbalance is a reason to get short, not long.
- **Example in this video:** ES: rallied slightly above Monday's high, failed, accelerated lower, falling just short of the REL.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:52](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=532) — each daily candle should expand lower; rally above Monday's high then failure; [0:27:59](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1679) — imbalances above daily REL = shorts, algorithm marching to the level.

### New York Midnight Opening Price  (aliases: midnight open, 12:00 a.m. NY candle open)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The NY midnight opening price is used to trade the London session (2:00–5:00 a.m. NY) and to frame the daily range Power of 3. If bearish, the ideal is a rally *above* the midnight opening price (the daily Judas swing) then decline; if bullish, a decline below it. He sleeps through London ~90% of the time; at ~7:00 a.m. he checks: if bearish and price made a Judas rally above midnight open and is now below it, he has an edge on daily bias and expects another short-term rally, ideally above the 8:30 opening price.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m: only two tiny moves above the midnight open — an anemic Judas swing (he wanted ~15–20 handles); a viewer said Power of 3 wasn't in effect, he says it was, just very small. London (3:00 a.m.) broke down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:27](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=627) — midnight open, anemic Judas, expected 15–20 handles; [0:12:50](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=770) — midnight open used for London session and daily Power of 3; [0:15:12](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=912) — bearish want rally above, bullish want decline below; [0:29:34](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1774) — recap + 7 a.m. check routine.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Clarifies Ep. 19's teaching from the bearish side; tiny Power-of-3 moves can still count but may not be useful.

### 8:30 Opening Price = New York session opening price  (aliases: 8:30 open, NY session open, Power of 3 for NY session)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** The 8:30 opening price is the New York *session* opening price (the NY session in his model is 8:30–11:00 a.m. NY local time), not to be confused with the NY midnight open. He uses it for the Power of 3 of the New York session with the same logic as midnight: if bearish, he wants a rally above the 8:30 open — a Judas swing / "false rally" / "suspect rally" — and he fades it (sells into it).
- **How to identify:** Extend the 8:30 candle's opening price forward. (On a 4-minute chart the candle shows as 8:28; don't let that confuse you.)
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:00](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=960) — 8:30 open = NY session Power of 3; Judas swing = false/suspect rally to fade; [0:24:00](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1440) — NY session = 8:30–11:00; [0:25:37](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1537) — 4m chart shows 8:28, open never traded above.

### The 2022 model at 8:30 (as restated)  (aliases: YouTube model, NY session model)
- **Kind:** entry model (restated)
- **What ICT says:** Normally: look to the left, before 8:30, for a short-term high; after 8:30 price rallies above the 8:30 opening price and runs those stops, then breaks down with displacement and an imbalance (FVG); price rallies back into the imbalance and sells off (entry there).

### Extremely heavy (one-sided) market: trading without the Judas rally  (aliases: "too heavy," wilting market, no short-term premium)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** If you're bearish and price fails to rally above *both* the midnight open and the 8:30 open, it's extremely bearish — it can't even reach a short-term premium above the key opening prices at key times. Such a market won't give "stunning little short-term rallies" to fade; either you get in where you can on small pockets of imbalance (FVGs) in the direction of the move, or you miss it. This doesn't mean the algorithm/concepts changed; it happens in futures, commodities (corn, soybeans, gold, oil) and stocks. Leaving out the "rally above the 8:30 open" element takes courage and isn't scalable with the public model; otherwise just tape read.
- **Example in this video:** ES: below midnight open and 8:30 open all day; 15m retrace into a bearish OB + imbalance, rolled over just before 9:30; 3m imbalance and bearish OB re-entries; 2m and 1m FVGs refining entries; a FVG rallies up to a bearish OB and sells off with stop above; price drives into the noon low; lunch retrace; aggressive selling into the PM session/close.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:47](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1007) — no rally above either open while bearish = extremely bearish; [0:17:34](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1054) — small pockets of imbalance or miss it; [0:19:10](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1150) — heavy markets must be traded differently; [0:36:43](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=2203) — 1m/2m imbalances without needing the 8:30 rally.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Modifies the standard model's requirement of a run above the 8:30 open; ICT presents it as coaching, not part of the YouTube model.

### Two-FVG rule: enter at the lower gap, size stop for the upper gap  (aliases: stacked FVGs, signature in price action)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** In the London open setup on ES: displacement lower with a FVG, rally up into a bearish OB and FVG, then a micro structure shift below a short-term low with another imbalance. With two FVGs stacked above price in a short, the upper one *can* be traded to on a spike, but your entry is on the lower FVG and your stop must allow for price trading up into the upper one. Don't assume you'll get the better (upper) entry — here price never went there. He calls this kind of thing a "signature" that repeats algorithmically.
- **How to use:** Short: limit at lower FVG; stop above the upper FVG. If the risk is too large, trade a micro contract or skip.
- **Example in this video:** ES London open (~3:00 a.m. NY) — "the only real sound entry" of the day in his view.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:56](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1196) — London setup, micro structure shift, two FVGs; [0:20:45](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1245) — "this as the entry and this as the risk."

### 9:30 Equity Open  (aliases: 9:30 opening, stock market open, RTH open)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** 9:30 is when stocks begin trading their NY session; it brings initial volatility. It can whipsaw in a small range, or in a large range clear both buy-side and sell-side, and then the real move for the day unfolds; wait for it. On this day 9:30 simply accelerated the move already in motion since 3:00 a.m. London.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:24](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1584) — what to expect at 9:30; [0:27:12](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1632) — today it accelerated the London move.

### Power of 3 (AMD) explained for a bearish day  (aliases: accumulation-manipulation-distribution, fractal Power of 3)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Power of 3 is fractal. Bearish: smart money *accumulates* short positions; then *manipulation* above the opening price, where they add more shorts; then price goes the other way and they *distribute* (cover) at an important low that can be forecast — daily relative equal lows, old daily lows, Fibonacci projections — in agreement with time of day.
- **Example in this video:** ES moved down into the lunch hour beginning at noon, made an important low, retraced during lunch (punched above a high once), broke down, consolidated, then continued into the afternoon.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:48](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1488) — AMD explained, distribution at forecastable low with time of day; [0:25:37](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1537) — lunch retracement then afternoon continuation.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Dollar up = risk off = foreign currencies and stock indices down (inverse).
- Daily OB levels: low, open, mean threshold (50%), high — the high is least likely to be reached.
- May: seasonal decline tendency in index futures.
- London session: 2:00–5:00 a.m. NY; uses the NY midnight opening price.
- NY session: 8:30–11:00 a.m. NY; uses the 8:30 opening price.
- Bearish: want a rally above midnight open (daily Judas) and above 8:30 open (session Judas) to fade; bullish: mirror.
- If bearish and price never rallies above either opening price = extremely bearish; trade small FVG pockets or stand aside.
- Model: short-term high to the left before 8:30 → rally above 8:30 open runs stops → displacement + FVG → retrace into FVG → short.
- Two FVGs: enter at the nearer (lower, for shorts) one; stop must allow price to reach the farther one.
- 9:30: equity open volatility/whipsaw; may clear both sides before the real move.
- Expected Judas size he wanted on ES: ~15–20 handles; expected rally to ~4320 that didn't happen.
- Important low into lunch beginning at noon; lunch retracement; afternoon continuation.
- Holds trades ~90 minutes to 2 hours max; no overnight, no weekend holds; flat before the close.

## Glossary candidates
`Risk off` — dollar strength / flight to quality; risk assets (FX, indices) fall.
`Flight to quality` — capital moving into the dollar as a safe haven.
`Suspect rally / false rally` — ICT's other names for a Judas swing against bias, to be faded.
`NY session opening price` — the 8:30 a.m. NY candle open (session runs 8:30–11:00).
`Heaviness objective` — the liquidity pool (daily REL) that a one-sided heavy market keeps gravitating toward.
`Micro structure shift` — small-scale market structure shift on a low timeframe.
`Signature (in price action)` — a repeating algorithmic pattern, e.g., the stacked-FVG entry.
