## Summary
ICT introduces a day-type classification that sits above draw on liquidity: knowing where price will go is not enough, you must know HOW it will be delivered. "Low resistance liquidity run conditions" are fast, one-way, gap-filled (many FVGs left open) runs to an obvious target. "High resistance" conditions are overlapping, back-and-forth, retracement-heavy deliveries. His calendar-based predictor is: a medium/high-impact news driver (8:30 or inside the first hour after 9:30) favors low resistance. No news, especially on a Monday, favors high resistance. He then walks Monday Oct 28 2024 on NQ (no news, Monday) as a textbook high-resistance day. The bias came from a cluster of two new week opening gaps below price plus a large RTH opening range gap, and the 70% half-gap fill in the first 30 minutes still printed. He shows the execution rules for a beginner ("Caleb" model): take only the first low-hanging-fruit objective, trade one contract, use the maximum stop (candle 1 high + 1 tick), and never trail a stop in high-resistance conditions. The rest is mindset: small fixed daily targets, never narrating "I know it's going to...", being a "sniper" not a crowd, and withdrawing prop-firm payouts instead of letting balances sit.

## Concepts

### High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases ICT uses: high resistance conditions, "harder delivery", fuzzy / scribbling back-and-forth price action)
- **Kind:** definition (new framing; he says no other school teaches it this way)
- **What ICT says:**
  - It is a delivery type, not a direction. You can know the draw (where price will go) and still have a market that will not deliver to it in "a single one-way ticket ride".
  - Price will usually still reach the objective eventually, but through retracements, consolidation, deep pullbacks, and repeated re-tests. Example: the NWOG high target was first touched at 11:43 after hours of sideways price.
  - Inefficiencies (open FVGs) are "far and few between"; the market spends its time overlapping.
  - It is not Seek & Destroy (the aggressive form of this), although he says that day could have become one.
  - It is "not impossible to trade" but much harder for a new trader; he steers beginners (his sons, new students) away from it and tells them to cut leverage and frequency.
- **How to identify:**
  - Pre-session: Monday and no medium/high-impact news at 8:30 or between 9:30 and 10:30 on the economic calendar.
  - In price: each subsequent candle shares/overlaps the previous candle's range by "at least half" or the majority. He calls this the simplest, clearest visual definition.
  - Also: deep retracements, lots of consolidation, reclaimed FVGs/order blocks (see below).
- **How to use:**
  - Do not use maximum leverage; start with one contract (a multi-contract trader still uses one here).
  - Use the maximum stop on an FVG entry (candle 1 high + 1 tick, see stop concept below).
  - Aim only at the first, easiest objective; a draw like the NWOG high does not need to be reached.
  - Do not trail the stop (a "death knell").
  - Expect "heat" (drawdown) after entry and do not bail on it unless the model is invalidated.
  - Best approach: trade the pre-market session instead (see pre-market concept), or accept the day is a no-trade/low-effort day.
  - If the setup does not form in the first 30 minutes, toss the idea.
- **Example in this video:** NQ December contract, 1m (framed on 15s), Mon Oct 28 2024, no news. See "Caleb's FVG entry model" and "Opening Range Gap CE" for the step-by-step; the whole 9:30 to about 11:43 delivery overlaps candle-on-candle and first reaches NWOG high at 11:43.
- **Timestamps:** [1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759) high-resistance defined by overlapping candles · [1:54:28](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6868) "likely to overlap the previous candle's range" · [2:00:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7212) first touch of NWOG high at 11:43 · [2:02:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7356) clearest visual representation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A news driver does NOT guarantee low resistance, and no news does not guarantee high resistance; they are probabilities ("the climate they exist in"). Builds on his previous week's lectures (last week covered Monday/no-news = pre-market trading; first 20 min of this video recaps it).

### Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases: low resistance runs, easy trading, "speed is the mechanism", one-shot run to target)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Very fast runs to obvious targets, with an obvious stop level and an obvious draw. The market behaves "like it's in a hurry".
  - Speed is the mechanism of delivery; price books quickly in one direction. He types "speed and distance / speed and magnitude" as the signature in his recordings.
  - Candles are well separated (little shared range between consecutive candles), big displacement candles, lots of open inefficiencies, no meaningful retracement or consolidation.
  - In these conditions the trade quickly runs in your favor, you can take a partial or cover costs fast, and then manage (trail) the stop. He says he cannot do his "Mr. Wizard" stop management in high resistance to the same degree.
  - Not an every-day condition. That is why you will not trade every day and that is fine.
  - If you are on the right side, the market does not dilly-dally; if you are on the wrong side it is devastating.
- **How to identify (his "recipe", [2:29:00](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8940)):**
  1. A medium- or high-impact news driver at 8:30 or inside the first hour of the 9:30 session.
  2. Not a Monday.
  3. Very clear draws on liquidity (clustered new week and/or new day opening gaps).
  4. An opening range gap of at least 45 handles (NQ), preferably 75 or more.
  - Not guaranteed, but "the probabilities are off the charts" and he is "all over that day like white on rice".
- **How to use:** trade larger/with confidence only here; simple targets hit quickly; take partials or cover costs and then trail the stop. He wants trades that do not sit in spread or initial drawdown.
- **Example in this video:** none shown live; contrast example is the "big, quick delivery and goes lower" versus the Oct 28 overlapping candles ([1:36:46](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5806)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:33](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=273) first definition · [1:36:46](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5806) low vs high contrast · [2:09:49](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7789) why news = low resistance · [2:29:00](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8940) recipe and thresholds

### Economic calendar as resistance predictor / news driver "smoke screen"  (aliases: news driver, medium/high impact news)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Check the calendar on the weekend and plan the week. Look for medium- or high-impact releases at 8:30 or between 9:30 and 10:30 (first hour of the session).
  - News present means the climate for low resistance: the data is a smoke screen, not the cause of the move. Algorithms use the release to run a Judas-style manipulation, put retail on the wrong side in the first 30 minutes, set the session high/low, and then move fast so trapped traders cannot exit.
  - News absent (especially Monday) means higher likelihood of high resistance. The day could consolidate, or become one-sided with a large Judas swing, an "outlier" profile you might miss.
  - Reports never explain the market's direction. If price does the opposite of the headline, the excuse is always "it priced it in", which nobody said beforehand.
- **How to use:** calendar empty and Monday: do the work pre-market (7:00 to 9:30 ET). News present and not Monday: expect the recipe for low resistance and be aggressive there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:48](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=468) absence of news this Monday · [0:17:24](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1044) smoke screen · [2:08:14](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7694) news ≈ low resistance, with the "not guaranteed" caveat · [2:10:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7836) retail trapped, price "falls out of bed"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he explicitly says news is "synonymous" with low resistance climate but "does not mean" you will automatically get it.

### Monday and Non-Farm Payroll week rules  (aliases: Monday rules; NFP week)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He denies the rumor that "ICT never trades Mondays". He has many rules for Mondays; Monday is "amateur hour" (traders who sat on their hands all weekend come out of the gate).
  - NFP week rule: trade Monday of NFP week (cleanest action). Be done trading by Wednesday morning AM session. Rarely trade Thursday or NFP Friday unless something is extremely obvious. Tuesday and Wednesday he does nothing if Monday made money; at worst a London session trade.
  - Non-NFP weeks: first check whether the calendar has a high/medium news driver at 8:30 or after the 9:30 open. If none and it is Monday, trade only the pre-market and avoid the 9:30 open.
- **How to use:** a hard discipline rule: "if these conditions are met, I know I will be punished with harder delivery". It does not mean targets will not trade, only that delivery is high resistance.

### Pre-market session (7:00 to 9:30 ET), AM opening session, lunch reversal  (aliases: premarket, "hit and run")
- **Kind:** rule / time window
- **What ICT says:**
  - Anything before the 9:30 bell is "pre-market" (London and the prior night's Asia included). The specific window he likes is 7:00 to 9:30 ET ("really good operating hours"; professionals kept it to themselves).
  - The "AM opening session" is 9:30 through lunch, defined by the opening bell.
  - Lunch hour is generally a reversal or consolidation of whatever was profitable in the morning (full reversal or large retracement); then from 1:30 PM ET the morning's direction tends to resume.
  - On a no-news Monday the pre-market gives lower risk and is more likely low resistance; the AM opening session is higher resistance.
- **How to use:** pick one window per day, get paid, and leave; he did not care about anything after 9:30 that Monday. Time-based models (e.g. a setup that forms between 10:00 and 11:00 and if it does not form you turn the charts off) create accountability.

### Clustered New Week / New Day Opening Gaps as draw on liquidity  (aliases: NWOG/NDOG clusters, "black hole", "Event Horizon on steroids")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - NWOG = Friday settlement to Sunday 6 PM open. Compare the current NWOG with the last five NWOGs (or last five NDOGs). If any two overlap (even one level of one gap encroaching the range of another is enough), that is a "cluster".
  - Open (unfilled) overlapping gaps act as a magnet: no buying/selling occurred there, "natural value areas". More overlapping gaps mean a higher probability price is repriced there. Whether a gap has been traded through/filled in the past "matters not".
  - The cluster is a draw (right direction), not a requirement to reach it ("just a magnet draw on price"; the stadium-directions analogy: get close to the stadium and then find the signs).
  - A trader shorting from above takes the highest level of the cluster first (NWOG high); for longs, the lowest level.
  - Works as a session bias for any school of thought, and no moving averages, VWAP or HTF top-down needed.
- **How to identify:** current NWOG high / CE / low plus the prior week's NWOG sitting inside the current one (here in its lower half); price is above both.
- **How to use:** use as bias/draw; first (nearest) level is the target for a beginner. Combine with the opening-range-gap fill (below) for a 3/4 or full gap closure or more.
- **Example in this video:** Oct 28 2024 NQ: current NWOG is large (Friday settle to Sunday 6 PM open); last week's NWOG (purple) sits in its lower half. Price hit neither the current NWOG CE nor the low that day; the NWOG high was first touched at 11:43. His X post that morning called the draw down into this area.

### Low-hanging-fruit objective ("Caleb" beginner model)  (aliases: lowest threshold objective, hit-and-run, surgical strikes, enough is enough)
- **Kind:** entry model / management rule (applied to this day)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The teaching model for his son: short bias from a cluster; take the first profitable exit, i.e. the first NWOG level reached plus a spread of one or two ticks, and get out. Do not hold for NWOG CE, NWOG low, or last week's gap CE/high.
  - Fixed daily threshold such as 10, 15 or 20 handles NQ, or 5 to 10 handles ES; "easy" objective in dollars like $100 to $250/day (he says a beginner should not exceed $250; honestly 100 to 200).
  - May need two trades; if trading off a 15-minute framework, one trade. Chart use: 15s, 30s, 45s or 1m entry charts reacting off a higher timeframe array; he says 15 minutes is the "high timeframe for day trading", no need for daily/hourly.
  - 90% of the time his own early exits had hit one or two PD arrays in his favor before the actual target (Terminus) so he made those his objective. The 90% is about trades executed on this basis, not a 90% win rate.
  - Terminus = best-case target; not needed for the model to pay.
  - Aim of the approach: higher strike rate, lower stress, build a consistent track record and rule-following, then graduate to holding for half-gap, NWOG threshold, and so on.
- **How to use:** stand aside on days without low resistance; do not chase momentum; do not use leverage to scale up.
- **Example in this video:** short; NWOG high is the highest cluster level, SIBI/FVG entry, exit below the prior low (see entry model).
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:37](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1477) what Caleb would not hold for · [0:35:03](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=2103) origin: ICT's own exits · [0:59:01](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=3541) 10/15/20 handles NQ, 5/10 ES · [1:07:46](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4066) target vs Terminus · [2:13:52](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8032) $100 to 250/day

### Opening Range Gap consequent encroachment fill (70% in first 30 minutes)  (aliases: RTH opening gap half-gap, half-gap fill)
- **Kind:** rule / statistic claim
- **What ICT says:**
  - Opening range gap = previous day settlement to 9:30 ET first print. Its CE (half) is traded to about 70% of the time in the first 30 minutes ("seven times out of ten").
  - A minute or two before 9:30 you can estimate where the mid-gap will be.
  - If gap-CE fill is also in the direction of a cluster of NWOG/NDOG, expect 3/4 gap, full closure, or more (the NWOG is beyond). You do not just stop at CE: "there is a draw still lower".
  - If it misses in the first 30 minutes but fills later (e.g. 11:00) that is still fine, though less valuable on a high-resistance day.
- **Example in this video:** Oct 28 NQ opened with a big gap up from Friday settlement; price opened inside an inversion FVG, traded down aggressively, and filled the half gap in 19 minutes (within the first 30), then did not reach the NWOG high until 11:43.
- **Timestamps:** [1:14:10](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4450) half-gap + cluster stack · [1:18:26](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4706) how to measure · [1:33:35](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5615) filled in 19 minutes · [1:46:29](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6389) "even in high resistance it trades to mid-gap"

### Caleb's FVG entry model and stop rule (short from a higher-timeframe inefficiency)  (aliases: FVG entry at the low of the gap, Silver-Bullet-like confirmation entry)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - The PD array for this beginner is the fair value gap. Look for price to retrace into a 1-minute inefficiency (the higher-timeframe array here is a 1m SIBI), see displacement away, then use the first clean FVG on the lower timeframe (15s/30s/45s/1m).
  - A first FVG that forms in the 9:30 candle cannot be traded; you cannot enter on the first presented FVG until 9:31 or later. Here the usable FVG formed at about 9:37.
  - Check that candle bodies respect the SIBI low (bodies closing/staying below it), i.e. displacement.
  - Entry is taken as price trades one tick below the FVG's low (not mid-gap, not the high of the gap, no limit orders on wicks above the gap); caption unclear on the word he uses for limit entries on wicks ("Mohawks").
  - Stop: normally for a bearish FVG the low-risk stop is above candle 2 (the middle, gap-creating candle) high + 1 tick. But if price is close to the low of the reference SIBI, or in high-resistance conditions, use the maximum: candle 1's high + 1 tick, so you are inside the pink SIBI box rather than getting stopped on a retest.
  - One contract only on a day like this.
  - Expect heat: price may run in profit, then pull back above entry (but not to the stop), then break lower again.
- **How to identify:** (1) sell-side imbalance/buy-side inefficiency on 1m at the 9:30 open; (2) price rallies into it and overshoots slightly, trading into its upper part; (3) bodies respect/break below its low; (4) displacement leaves a clean FVG at or after 9:31; (5) retrace into FVG.
- **How to use:** short with the NWOG-high cluster as the directional bias; objective is a new low below the entry-leg low (sell-side); take it and leave.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Mon Oct 28 2024, 15s/1m. Pre-market work traded up into old relative equal highs and a 9:00 high, then broke down aggressively at 9:30 on one large black 1m candle (that candle's range is the SIBI; on the 15s chart it takes two candles). Open sat inside an inversion FVG at a premium. Price rallied back up into the SIBI, slightly above it, then broke back below its low; displacement down left the first clean FVG at about 9:37. Price retraced up into that FVG (short entry one tick below FVG low), ran in profit, came back up above entry (stop sat above candle 1 high, inside the pink box, not hit), then broke lower, filled the opening range gap CE (about 9:49, 19 minutes in) and later (11:43) the NWOG high.
- **Timestamps:** [1:17:35](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4655) the SIBI on the 9:30 candle · [1:35:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5712) no first-minute FVG, need displacement · [1:38:20](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5900) bodies respect SIBI low, first clean FVG · [1:47:16](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6436) entry one tick below the gap low
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he says he "covered in mentorship 2024" when to use the middle-candle versus candle-1 stop. The candle-1 stop is the maximum-risk option; the middle-candle stop is the low-risk default.

### Stop-loss placement on an FVG entry (candle 2 vs candle 1)
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A fair value gap is always three candles: candle 1, candle 2 (the actual gap range, shares no space with candles 1 and 3), candle 3.
  - Low-risk stop for a short on a bearish FVG: high of candle 2 + 1 tick.
  - If price is very close to the low of the SIBI/reference range (a retest could reach it) or the day is high resistance, use candle 1's high + 1 tick (the "maximum").
  - Rationale: high-resistance days are likely to retrace into and above the gap, so a tight stop gets run even when the direction is right.
- **Timestamps:** [1:40:02](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6002) choose candle 1 stop due to proximity to SIBI low · [1:40:50](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6050) three-candle definition and candle 2 stop

### Sell-Side Imbalance Buy-Side Inefficiency (SIBI) as the framing array  (aliases: "Cy"/"cellid balance byid efficiency" in captions)
- **Kind:** example only (refinement of use)

### Reclaimed FVGs / reclaimed order blocks (revisits) in high resistance
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** the second touch of a PD array ("any PD array it uses its characteristic twice") is synonymous with high-resistance days. A trailed stop gets hit when price goes back into the FVG (reclaiming it). Reclaimed arrays are unlikely in low-resistance (they are gone in one move: "Elvis has left the building"). Certain PD arrays are more prone to form in one profile than another.

### Trade management: do not trail in high resistance
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** trailing the stop in high-resistance conditions is a "death knell"; the stop gets run by overlapping candles and the target then trades. In his own trades he sometimes sets the stop to cover costs plus about $100 so the trade pays him for his time. Real management and tight trailing are for low-resistance. A recommended approach: if you get stopped out, you re-enter later rather than strangling price with a tight stop.

### Market maker vs dealer; price is "rigged"; the first print
- **Kind:** definition (conceptual)

Retail does not control the Sunday open or the 9:30 first print; both are determined by the prior close in advance. He says it is good that it is rigged: it is where the edge comes from. ### Judas swing / sentiment shift around news
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** around news the engine pushes a Judas swing the wrong way (morning session high can be the high of the day), retail chases or places breakout orders, then price "falls out of bed". The bank/market maker is the counterparty and does not allow slow exits. Showing profit publicly or seeking approval is the "sentiment" tell. In the first 30 minutes the session high/low is set against retail.

### Time-based, rule-based models vs the 12 mentorship models
- **Kind:** definition / opinion
- **What ICT says:** a time-boxed model such as one that must form between 10:00 and 11:00 (Silver Bullet style) forces accountability: if it does not form, you do not trade. He deliberately complicated about 12 models in the paid mentorship (partly because people were leaking/selling it), two were easy bread-and-butter; others are strictly rule-heavy and profitable but demand lots of waiting. Time is "the first aspect to algorithmic price delivery". He is not a "one-trick pony" and tells students to find the model that matches their personality.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:34](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1234) · [1:43:15](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6195) · [1:44:04](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6244)

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Pre-market operating window: 7:00 to 9:30 ET. AM opening session: 9:30 through lunch. Lunch usually reverses/consolidates the morning move; 1:30 PM ET the morning trend resumes.
- Check the calendar for medium/high-impact news at 8:30 or 9:30 to 10:30 ET. None and it's Monday: trade the pre-market only.
- NFP week: trade Monday (and Tuesday); done by Wednesday AM session; rarely trade Thursday/Friday.
- ORG (opening range gap): previous settlement to 9:30 first print; CE filled about 70% of the time within 30 minutes. Low-resistance recipe wants ORG of at least 45 handles, preferring 75.
- Clustering: compare current NWOG with last 5 NWOGs (or last 5 NDOGs); any overlap of two is enough; shorts target the highest cluster level first, longs the lowest.
- No 15-minute opening range; only 30 and 60 minutes.
- FVG entry: no entry on an FVG formed in the 9:30 candle (earliest 9:31); entry one tick below the gap low for shorts (not mid-gap, not high); stop candle 2 high + 1 tick normally, candle 1 high + 1 tick in high resistance or near the SIBI low.
- One contract on high-resistance days. Do not trail stops there.
- Beginner targets: 10/15/20 handles NQ or 5/10 ES; $100 to $250/day max; one trade per day if you can. Exit the first low-hanging objective plus one or two ticks.
- High-resistance signature: each candle overlaps at least half of the previous candle's range.
- Day types: low resistance means news and not Monday and clear clustered draws and big gap; high resistance means no news and Monday.

## Glossary candidates
`HIGH RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN CONDITIONS` — back-and-forth, overlapping-candle delivery with deep retracements and reclaimed arrays; likely on no-news days, especially Monday.
`LOW RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN CONDITIONS` — fast one-way runs with separated candles and many open inefficiencies; favored by medium/high-impact news, not Monday, clear draws, big opening gap.
`CLUSTERED NWOG/NDOG` — two or more new week/day opening gaps whose ranges overlap, acting as a magnet draw.
`LOW HANGING FRUIT OBJECTIVE` — the nearest/easiest profitable PD-array exit, used as the whole target for beginners.
`TERMINUS` — the best-case target of the trade (here the farthest cluster level).
`OPENING RANGE GAP CE` — midpoint between previous settlement and the 9:30 first print; ICT claims about 70% fill in the first 30 minutes.
`SIBI` — sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency (bearish single/clean displacement candle gap), here read as a 1m reference array.
`POINT OF INDICATION` — a "tell"; in trading, saying "I know it will..." as the signal you are off-model.
`SEEK AND DESTROY` — the aggressive form of high-resistance days (mentioned only).
`RECLAIMED FVG / ORDER BLOCK` — a PD array touched a second time, typical of high-resistance days.
