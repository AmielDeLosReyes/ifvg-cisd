## Summary

The lesson is how to hold a trade for a bigger run: buy at or below the 9:30 opening price when the daily chart is bullish (Power of 3 / Judas swing), do not keep tightening the stop, expect a sell-side raid at the 11:30 lunch macro, and hold at least to 3:15 and ideally into the 3:15-3:45 and 3:45-4:00 algorithms. ## Concepts

### Power of 3 / Daily range schematic  (aliases: PO3, AMD (not named in this video), "classic buy day", Judas swing)
- **Kind:** rule / definition (applied to the day)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He sketched one daily range before the live stream: the 9:30 opening price is the reference. Any move down away from the 9:30 open on a bullish day is "the Judas swing" or fake move, meant to get people thinking it will keep dropping.
  - After that comes the "trend following" portion, which is directional and one-sided up. At the close, price comes off the highest high and settles off the high (so the daily candle has open near low, close near high).
  - Larry Williams (1995 video course, "confidential trading course" caption unclear) said it was hard for him to buy below the opening price when bullish, so he bought strength. ICT says this was his opening: Power of 3 is his answer to that problem, with a systematic way to be a buyer at or below the open.
  - Buying at the opening price or lower when bullish gives many more handles/points than buying strength; he says this was solidified last Tuesday (Aug 13).
- **How to identify:** requires a bullish daily/weekly bias first. Then watch for price trading below the 9:30 open (Judas) toward relative equal lows / sell-side liquidity, then an entry drill (see below) at or below the open.
- **How to use:** be a buyer at/below the 9:30 open when expecting higher prices on the daily chart; the low of the day is expected to hold ("low-risk buy"); then hold through time (see hold rules).
- **Example in this video:** ES, Aug 19, 2024, 15m/1m. Price opens at 9:30, drops a little, pops slightly above the open, drops again, then takes out relative equal lows below the open. That is the buy zone. Price then rallies above the new week opening gap and continues up all day to close near the high; daily candle open near low, close near high.

### Institutional Orderflow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: "the drill", "institutional orderflow entry drill", "low-risk buy")
- **Kind:** entry model (applied example only; mechanics not re-taught here)
- **What ICT says:** the entry on the day was the institutional order flow entry drill at or below the 9:30 open. He calls it the low-risk buy: price "should not come back below" that entry and should keep pressing higher. He says you only appreciate where these setups form relative to the 9:30 open after doing the drills for months, not days or weeks.
- **How to identify (as applied):** price sweeps through the new week opening gap, clears inefficiency into an order block (there were two), and then there is a change in the state of delivery. Price comes back to engage the order block, which "upsets" a perfect delivery to the NWOG, and then runs.
- **How to use:** after the entry, do not run the stop up repeatedly (see hold rules). Wicks that poke outside the PD array ("coloring outside the lines") are expected and welcome.
- **Example in this video:** ES 1m, Aug 19. Price drops below the 9:30 open, trades through the new day opening gap ("on the 16th" per captions) and comes back up. ICT says he wanted to see it get above that new day opening gap, then see it trade down again (it does), and that second dip is the entry. A breaker on a 15-second chart was also used (he says he was long ES while a DOM-ladder streamer said it would not make a higher high).

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)  (aliases: new week opening gap, "coloring book")
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** NWOG = Sunday 6 PM open compared with Friday's settlement price. Price was above it all day, and the gap was used as support for the buy. Price is likened to children coloring inside a coloring book: it can color outside the lines and he wants to see that, because it gives the opportunity. "Let the wicks do the damage."
- **How to use:** treat the NWOG as a magnet and support on a bullish day; the retracement into it (and the order block nearby) is the buy zone; the lunch macro sell-off also engaged the NWOG.
- **Example in this video:** ES, week of Aug 19. Price swept through the NWOG, cleared inefficiency into the order block (two stacked), CISD, rally; at 11:30-ish the lunch-macro drop came back to the NWOG and the 11:17 low, then rallied.

### Lunch macro (11:30-1:30) and the "first low after 10:00" rule  (aliases: lunch macro, 11:30 macro)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 11:30 the lunch macro starts and can run to 1:30 (he notes it could also keep going higher with no retracement). It tends to go against the morning's direction to take liquidity from whoever has been profiting.
  - Method: at 11:30, ask who made money from 9:30 to now. If longs (market rallied), the algorithm seeks the stops of traders who trailed their stops under a low. To find that low, go backwards from 11:30 and take the first low you come to, but it must have formed after 10:00 (it factors in any Silver Bullet run and protraction). Lows before 10:00 do not count.
  - After the macro closes at 1:30, there is no more time element for taking those stops, so price is free to continue in the direction of the early session (open near low, close near high).
- **How to identify:** (1) mark 11:30; (2) scan left for the first swing low; (3) confirm it formed after 10:00; (4) expect a raid below it during 11:30-1:30 if the morning move was up.
- **How to use:** expect a pullback/raid and do not be shaken out; he says he told viewers this ahead of time so they would hold. If the sell side has been taken and 1:30 passes, the continuation is free.
- **Example in this video:** ES 1m, Aug 19. The relevant low was at 11:17 (after 10:00). At 11:30 price dropped, took out that low (stops of anyone trailing below) and engaged the NWOG, then consolidated and rallied after 1:30.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:31](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1771) 11:30 starts the lunch macro, who is making money · [0:30:17](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1817) go backwards to first low after 10:00 (11:17 low) · [0:33:34](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2014) why after 10:00 (Silver Bullet run) · [0:34:23](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2063) 1:30 closes the macro, free to continue
- **Nuance / caveats:** the captions have him saying "11:00 for the folks that don't know my macro for lunch" before correcting to 11:30; the macro start is 11:30 per the rest of the passage. He also says the lunch macro explains the sell-side move without any need to look at depth of market.

### Last-hour macros: 3:15-3:45 and 3:45-4:00 (market on close)  (aliases: last hour macro, MOC macro)
- **Kind:** rule / new detail
- **What ICT says:**
  - 3:00 starts the last hour of the day. He told his community "after 3 o'clock, if you have not taken anything, pay the trader" (take something off), and to hold a minimum until 3:15.
  - A macro runs 3:15 to 3:45. Then the 3:45 to 4:00 window is the market on close; he stresses it is 15 minutes (not 10), 3:45 to 4:00 ET.
  - He says there are four macros in the last hour (caption unclear: "four of four macros"); he names the one starting 3:15 and the one starting 3:45.
  - In earnings season, an algorithm fires at 4:01 and produces a run into 4:15; after the regular session closes, electronic trading can go "bonkers."
  - From 3:45 the algorithm refers back to daily and weekly levels, cycling from higher timeframes down to seconds and back up; all inefficiencies and liquidity are incorporated. He calls this "casting your lure" toward the HTF level.
- **How to use:** in a daily trend day, hold into 3:15 at minimum; the 3:15-3:45 retracement can be disregarded as noise; the 3:45 run is aimed at the HTF target.
- **Example in this video:** ES/NQ, Aug 19. Price rallied through the 2:00 hour, had a small retracement into 3:00 (low sitting inside a fair value gap), then from 3:30 a candle spooled up into the blue line (close of the daily SIBI) and traded into the daily volume imbalance and the pink shaded target area, closing near the high.

### Holding trades longer (time-based management)  (aliases: holding for the daily range, "submit to time")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Start from higher-timeframe bias, then enter an intraday setup that is in sync with it. To hold for bigger runs, the first and foremost thing is to submit to the time the daily candle needs to form and close.
  - Having bought at/below the open on a bullish day, "you do not strangle it by running your stop loss up"; do not keep moving it higher and higher.
  - Expect the 11:30 lunch macro raid against the move (so do not trail tight), then hold to at least 3:15, ideally through the last hour.
  - "Just let one contract go": leave one contract as a runner using these rules and you will have bigger runs.
  - These are skills built through months of drills; you cannot force it.
- **How to use:** define the plan by time before entry: low-risk buy at/below the open, expect the lunch raid, then hold to 3:15-4:00.
- **Example in this video:** ES long from the opening drill held through the 11:30 raid to the afternoon run; he says he drew roughly two handles of draw down for a run of over 200 handles (he states "over 200 and some handles").
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:30](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=570) how to hold trades that keep moving without being stopped out · [0:28:43](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1723) do not strangle it by raising the stop · [0:35:10](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2110) let one contract go · [0:38:21](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2301) submit to the time required for the daily candle to close
- **Nuance / caveats:** a mention of "Tanja" (a student) making over 100 handles; his remarks on hold-time apply to bullish-daily context, and shorts against that bias are small size.

### Higher-timeframe narrative and daily-chart targets  (aliases: HTF levels, daily/weekly objectives, "magnets")
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Method he used in the mentorship: give HTF levels where price is likely to reach, then the student only watches lower timeframes for the completion of a run on opposing liquidity and then gets in sync with order flow. He claims high-90% accuracy on this for students.
  - He gave all levels on Tuesday Aug 13; HTF levels act like magnets pulling institutional order flow. Keep them on the chart and on a notepad and check whether price is marching to them.
  - Levels on the daily chart (ES/NQ-scale prices in caption look like NQ, ~19,868): the mean threshold of an up-close candle, a rejection block, buy-side liquidity above, the low of a candle (SIBI: "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency"; that candle's low 19,868.2 vs the day's high 19,871.7, so it was cleared at the close), and a volume imbalance between two candle bodies (pink shaded area).
  - Retracements intraday against that HTF bias are just retracements and do not change the premise; the opposite side's liquidity gets taken first and the market usually goes for the opposite.
- **How to identify:** he lists them in order going up: mean threshold of the up-close candle, rejection block, BSL, the candle low (SIBI), and the volume imbalance between two candle bodies.
- **How to use:** frame buy side and sell side, anticipate the run on opposing liquidity, and enter when it completes. Trade size is the largest in the direction of daily orderflow.
- **Example in this video:** daily chart, Aug 19. Last week he said he was reaching higher (not picking a top); price hit the mean threshold, then the liquidity above, then the SIBI, and traded to its upper level into the pink area; after that, he is content with the weekly objective and unsure whether there is a higher high, range, or drop.

### Volume imbalance  (aliases: volume imbalance on the daily)
- **Kind:** definition (brief)
- **What ICT says:** the space between one candle's close and the next candle's open where there is no body, only a wick, and the bodies of the two candles do not overlap. He calls this a volume imbalance and says he talked about it at the end of the previous Tuesday's stream. It is used as a daily target (pink area).
- **How to identify:** two consecutive candles whose bodies do not overlap, leaving a gap between close and next open that is wick-only on one side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:50](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=230) definition at the daily chart · [0:45:32](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2732) as a target
- **Nuance / caveats:** description is loose in captions; treat as "bodies do not overlap".

### Algorithmic price delivery (vs. depth of market)  (aliases: "the algorithm", price engine, IPDA (not named))
- **Kind:** definition / argument
- **What ICT says:**
  - The algorithm is the price engine: it offers price, and buyers and sellers agree to it. The "commodity" is price action itself. Price is first piped to top-tier institutions and retail gets the scraps.
  - It needs only to know what time and where, not how many contracts are trading. Order books can be spoofed (big banks have been fined), so ladders are bait and do not drive the market.
  - Price cycles through time frames (less than daily down to seconds and back up) so inefficiencies and liquidity get incorporated. Moves are not buying and selling pressure.
  - His example was a streamer calling ICT a scam while saying ES would not make a higher high; ICT went long ES off a breaker on a 15-second chart and it accelerated higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:45](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1245) breaker on 15s chart vs DOM claims · [0:21:33](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1293) algorithm offers price · [0:31:57](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1917) algorithm needs time and where, spoofing

- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The heaviest position size is in the direction of daily bullish order flow ("not overleveraging"); shorts against it are small. For himself he would trade 1, 3 or 4 contracts, not 10, 15 or 20, because most of the expected daily move is already done.

  - He notes no down daily close in over two weeks is unsustainable and speculates that a run into the inefficiency may be where a rug-pull comes if the news breaks.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Daily bullish bias: buy at or below the 9:30 open; the low of the day is the low-risk buy.
- Daily candle expected: open near low, close near high on a "classic buy day".
- Lunch macro: starts 11:30 and can run to 1:30; find the first low going backwards from 11:30 that formed after 10:00 (the 11:17 low here); expect it to be raided if the morning was up.
- After 3:00, start paying the trader; hold a minimum to 3:15 if you want the bigger run.
- Last-hour macros: 3:15-3:45; 3:45-4:00 (15 minutes, market on close); in earnings season an algorithm at 4:01 running into 4:15.
- NWOG: Sunday 6 PM open vs Friday settlement.
- Do not trail the stop up tightly after an entry at the open ("do not strangle it").
- Leave one contract as a runner.
- Size: biggest with daily bias, small when against it; he says he trades 1 to 4 contracts, not 10-20.
- Treat a Jackson Hole-type multi-day event like NFP week: trade the Monday setups and stay patient.
- Daily SIBI example: candle low 19,868.2, day's high 19,871.7 (cleared at the close).
- Live-stream latency options mentioned (ultra low, low, normal); captions cannot be enabled with ultra low latency (housekeeping, not trading).

## Glossary candidates
`NWOG` — new week opening gap: Sunday 6 PM open vs Friday settlement price.
`Institutional orderflow entry drill` — ICT's term for the entry drill he uses at/below the 9:30 open to buy a bullish day; low-risk entry.
`Judas swing` — the fake move away from the 9:30 open (down on a buy day) before the true directional move.
`Trend following portion` — the one-sided directional leg after the Judas swing.
`Lunch macro` — 11:30-1:30 algorithmic window that tends to raid the stops of the morning's profitable side.
`Market on close` — 3:45-4:00 ET window (15 minutes) of the final algorithm.
`Pay the trader` — take partial profit (his community post said to do this after 3 PM if you have not taken any).
`Handles` — points of price (used for ES/NQ moves).
`SIBI` — sell side imbalance, buy side inefficiency (a down-candle FVG type); used here for the daily candle low he said was cleared.
`Volume imbalance` — non-overlapping candle bodies leaving a wick-only gap between close and next open.
`Coloring book` — his analogy: price can color outside the lines of a PD array (wicks), and that is acceptable.
