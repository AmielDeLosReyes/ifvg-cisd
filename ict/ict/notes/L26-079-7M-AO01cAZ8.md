## Concepts

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (fpFVG) Within Context and Narrative (aliases: first FVG with context, operational fpFVG)
- **Kind:** pattern + identification
- **What ICT says:** Multiple FVGs can exist on a chart, but the first presented FVG is the one that matters in "context and narrative." It's the FVG that forms as price is breaking structure (taking liquidity, displacing through swings). Not every gap is the fpFVG; you must consider the price action leading into it. Example: if price breaks through buy-side, then breaks through swing lows, and then an FVG forms, THAT's the fpFVG—it's in the context of the displacement. Other gaps nearby are secondary.
- **How to identify:** Identify where price first displaces (breaks structure with conviction); the FVG that forms in that directional move is the fpFVG. Look for: break of swing, displacement, FVG in that sequence.
- **How to use:** Trade the fpFVG with high conviction; it's the institutional reference. Other FVGs nearby are secondary and may not be tradeable.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: Michael identified two FVGs. One was first formed but NOT used as an iFVG (didn't act inversely). The second was the fpFVG because it formed after a displacement (break of swing lows, take of buy-side). "First presented fair value gap within the context and narrative of what price is likely to do."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:59](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=239) (why is this one the first presented? Because it's in context of the displacement, swing low break, and relative equal highs).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Context matters; you can't identify fpFVG without understanding the displacement/break that preceded it.

### Displacement (aliases: strong move, aggressive break, violent move)
- **Kind:** pattern / directional indicator
- **What ICT says:** A displacement is a strong, committed move through structure (swing lows, support levels) with bodies showing aggression and minimal retracement back into the broken level. Displacements signal institutional participation and continuation. When you see a displacement, the direction it goes is the institutional direction.
- **How to identify:** Look for candles with bodies committed to one side, breaking through a swing low or high, and not retracing back into that level immediately.
- **How to use:** Trade in the direction of the displacement. Expect continuation. Use the displacement as confirmation of your bias.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "This displacement in form of a sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency, this candlestick breaks through this swing low." Displacement down = expect lower prices.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288) (displacement in form of sell-side imbalance, breaks through swing low).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Displacement is conviction; it's different from a bounce or a wick back.

### Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency (aliases: bearish gap, sell-off indicator)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When there's buy-side imbalance (buyers willing at higher prices, creating a gap) paired with sell-side inefficiency (no sellers stepping in; sellers absent), the gap is bullish. Conversely, when sell-side is willing (buyers gap up, then no sellers) and buy-side is inefficient, it's bearish. The structure signals who's in control.
- **How to identify:** Look for a gap up or down; identify which side is willing and which is absent.
- **How to use:** Use the inefficient side as reversal target. If buy-side is imbalanced and sell-side absent, expect price to eventually drop (sell-side inefficiency).
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "There's a buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency prior to this high being taken." This structure eventually led to price dropping into it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:49](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=49) (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The imbalance itself is neutral; the inefficiency (absence of the opposite side) is the signal.

### SMT Divergence / Smart Money Technique Divergence (aliases: intermarket divergence, NQ vs ES divergence)
- **Kind:** macro confluence / warning signal
- **What ICT says:** SMT divergence is when two correlated markets (NQ and ES, for example) move in opposite directions or show unequal strength. Example: NQ makes higher high while ES refuses to break above its high. This divergence signals institutional disagreement; often it precedes a sharp reversal (Judas swing trap). When correlated indices diverge, smart money is likely setting a trap for retail.
- **How to identify:** Compare two correlated markets (NQ vs ES, or ES vs Dow); look for one making higher high/low while the other refuses or lags.
- **How to use:** When you spot SMT divergence, expect a reversal. If NQ goes higher while ES stays weak, shorts into NQ are high-probability; ES is showing weakness.
- **Example in this video:** NQ made higher high; ES made lower high (refused). This divergence preceded a sharp sell-off in both. "We had heavy distribution. S&P at the same time and Q higher. See that? So, the Judas right there and creating the SMT divergence."
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:25](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=145) (Nasdaq made higher high when ES was unwilling) · [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=672) (SMT divergence: NQ higher, ES lower high).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** SMT divergence is a macro-level warning; use it to confirm shorts or to avoid long entries.

### Judas Swing (aliases: trap rally, trap move, institutional trap)
- **Kind:** pattern + psychology
- **What ICT says:** A Judas swing is a rallies or spike designed to trap retail traders into long positions before a sharp reversal. Often accompanied by positive news or SMT divergence (one index higher while another weak). The institutional purpose: get retail long at the top, then dump. Recognize Judas swings and fade (short) them.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a rally that's not supported by all correlated markets (SMT divergence), or a rally on news that doesn't align with underlying order flow.
- **How to use:** Short Judas swings with conviction. Often they're good 2-3 hour scalps into sharp reversals.
- **Example in this video:** NQ rally into new high while ES weak = Judas swing. "The morning session's going to go up into lunch macro time. Goes into first presented fair value gap, consequent encroachment. Trades back above June 1st daily high and taps it one more time on the low right there... starts to work lower."
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:11](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=431) (Judas: morning session goes up into lunch macro; this high is the trap before reversal).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Judas swings are confirmation of reversal bias; they're not entries by themselves.

### Lunch Macro (aliases: lunch period, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM ET, 12:30–1:30 PM preferred hold)
- **Kind:** timing rule
- **What ICT says:** The lunch period is 11:30 AM–1:30 PM ET. Setups usually form in the first hour (11:30 AM–12:30 PM). Delivery to target often happens in the second hour (12:30 PM–1:30 PM). Michael prefers to hold through at least 12:30–1:30 PM to give the algorithm time to execute. If a target isn't hit by early afternoon, it might re-run in the PM session.
- **How to identify:** Mark 11:30 AM and 1:30 PM on your 1m chart; note when setups form and when targets are hit.
- **How to use:** Set entries in the first hour; let targets play out through second hour. Don't exit early expecting a reversal.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: setup formed in morning; Judas swing into lunch; target hit inside the first hour of lunch macro (12:30). "It went right down to it handsomely here... inside the first hour of the lunch macro."

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Lunch macro is reliable; give it time to work before exiting.

### Minor Buy-Side Liquidity (aliases: minor BSL, intermediate buy-side pool)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Within a downtrend or selling move, there are pockets of buy orders (buy-side liquidity) that create small resistance bounces. These minor BSL pools are between the major bounces and are often swept on the way down. Recognize them as pause zones, not reversal zones.
- **How to identify:** On a down move, mark small rallies; these are minor BSL bounces.
- **How to use:** Use minor BSL as a place to add shorts or to avoid getting stopped out. Price is likely to retrace slightly into minor BSL, then continue down.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: after hitting relative equal lows, "minor buy side liquidity pool there. Sweeps it. We break lower."
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:46](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=526) (creating a minor buy-side liquidity pool; sweeps it; break lower).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Minor BSL is swept easily; don't treat it as major resistance.

### Relative Equal Lows and Relative Equal Highs (aliases: REL, REH, equal highs/lows)
- **Kind:** definition + targeting
- **What ICT says:** REL = two+ lows at same price; acts as a sell-side liquidity cluster (support that's also a target for shorts). REH = two+ highs at same price; acts as a buy-side liquidity cluster (resistance). These are institutional reference points.
- **How to identify:** Scan the daily chart; find two or more equal highs or lows within a few ticks.
- **How to use:** Use REL as partial target for shorts; REH as partial target for longs. These are stepping stones, not final targets.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "target here was the relative equal lows." Michael exited into REL.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:25](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=145) (takes out relative equal highs) · [0:05:36](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=336) (target: relative equal lows).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** REL/REH are liquidity clusters; they're profitable exits, not final targets.

### Partial Taking and Trade Management (aliases: profit taking, phased exit)
- **Kind:** risk management
- **What ICT says:** When you have a winning trade, take partial profits at intermediate targets (REL, minor zones) rather than holding the entire position to final target. Example: if you have 10 contracts and REL is 50% down from entry, take 5 contracts off; hold 5 as runners to final target. This locks in profits and reduces emotional burden.
- **How to identify:** Identify REL, minor zones, and final targets before entering; plan where to take partials.
- **How to use:** Set alerts at each partial level. When you hit a partial, take the planned amount off. Let runners ride to next target.
- **Example in this video:** Michael mentions: "I would have the lapse the trade here with a partial. And then when it ran back up here, I would have added the partial here, taken off you see probably would have been half off."
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:23](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=383) (would have taken partial here, added partial there).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Partials reduce stress and lock profits; they're better than running entire position to zero.

### Swing Low and Swing High (aliases: swing point, local high/low)
- **Kind:** reference structure
- **What ICT says:** A swing low is a local low (lower than the candle before and after it). Swing high is a local high. These are institutional reference points. When price breaks through a swing high or low with displacement, it signals institutional participation in that direction.
- **How to identify:** Mark the highest and lowest points in a recent range; identify swings.
- **How to use:** Use swing breaks as confirmation of directional bias. When price breaks through a swing and displaces, expect continuation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: "this candlestick breaks through this swing low. When there's an S&P divergence... the market displaces lower."
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288) (breaks through this swing low).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Swing breaks are high-probability continuations.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- First presented FVG: only the gap in the context of a displacement counts; other gaps are secondary.
- Displacement: strong move through swings with bodies committed = institutional direction.
- SMT divergence: NQ higher, ES lower = setup for reversal (Judas swing).
- Lunch macro: 11:30 AM–1:30 PM; setup in first hour; hold until at least 12:30–1:30 PM.
- Minor buy-side: swept easily on down moves; not major resistance.
- Relative equal lows: partial profit target for shorts.
- Partial taking: 50% off at intermediate targets; 50% as runners to final target.
- Drawdown: tight entry = small drawdown (11 handles in example); good fills validate structure.
- No shortcuts: 5-minute traders trying to learn from videos won't get it; takes time and context.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Experience brings flexibility:** Michael trades both directions (longs and shorts) in bull markets. Experience and order flow reading let you spot shorts even when consensus is "bull market." Don't be locked into one direction.
- **Respect the stop out:** Michael notes he exited at REL, missing a further run. This is OK; taking profits at defined targets is better than holding for max and giving it back.
- **Multi-market monitoring:** Compare NQ, ES, other indices; SMT divergence gives you early warning of reversals.
- **Learner's humility:** "No 5-minute trader mentors that go through my videos... I promise you, if you listen to people like that, you are not going to learn correctly." Fast-learners and shortcut-takers don't truly understand the framework.
- **Partial discipline:** Planning partials before entry removes emotion and locks profits.
