## Summary
ICT shows how the 2022 mentorship model transfers to forex, using EURUSD on a Tuesday with the ISM Services PMI at 10:00 a.m. ET. Daily narrative: buy stops above relative equal highs taken, then sell-side below relative equal lows and the 1.09 big figure become the draw; three down-close days set a bearish bias. He combines the body-anchored Fibonacci swing projection (levels 1.0919 / 1.0900 / 1.0901) with the big figure, then drills down hourly → 15m → 5m → 4m: rally above the midnight opening price (Judas / protraction) into an FVG and bearish order block during the **FX New York open killzone (7:00–10:00 a.m. ET)**, an intermediate-term high protected by an imbalance, a news-driven stop raid, and a final FVG short into ~1.0901. Key FX-specific rules: killzone times differ from indices, midnight vs 8:30 opening-price selection, and "fluff" exits by 3–5 pips (10 for beginners) because of spreads. Example trade risk ≈10 pips for >8:1 reward.

## Concepts

### Daily bias from liquidity (BSL taken → SSL next; "unfinished business")
- **Kind:** rule (applied)
- **What ICT says:** Once price runs above relative equal highs (buy stops taken) and turns lower, the next draw is the sell stops below. Two areas of SSL: relative equal lows ("two little devils") to the left, and a further low. Monday's candle low had not yet taken out the prior low → "unfinished business" below. After three consecutive down-close daily candles, the bias for the next candle is bearish.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD daily: run above relative equal highs, three down-close days, Monday's low not yet below the relative equal lows; Tuesday expected lower.

[0:01:51](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=111) — relative equal lows; unfinished business; bearish bias. ### Institutional price levels / big figure
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Big-figure (00) levels are very influential because a lot of commerce/liquidity transacts around them. Institutional price levels: the 00, 20, 50 and 80 levels.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD 1.0900 big figure as the downside objective, sitting at the daily relative equal lows.

### Fibonacci swing projection on bodies (standard deviation / fulcrum point)
- **Kind:** refinement (targeting) — revisits Ep. 16
- **What ICT says:** Anchor on the highest up-close/open body in the retracement and the lowest open or close of the swing (not wicks). The projected levels are valid **only if the swing low (fulcrum point) is broken**. Choose the target where the projection converges with liquidity/big figure: the lowest projection level that doesn't go through 1.09.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD: projection levels 1.0919, 1.0900 and 1.0901 ("essentially the big figure"); converging with relative equal lows at 1.09 → target 1.0901. The day's low was (he recalls) 1.0901; price reached the Fib standard deviation and the daily relative equal lows (blue line), with candle bodies respecting the level.

### Power of 3 / Judas swing (midnight opening price, protraction)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** At midnight New York the day begins; if bearish, you want a rally first — "market protraction," against the expected intraday direction — the Judas swing / manipulation leg of accumulation-manipulation-distribution. It trades up into a HTF imbalance, which tends to form the daily high; then look for distribution lower the rest of the day. Preferably price trades **above** the midnight opening price — that's where smart money accumulates shorts. The algorithm repeatedly offers price above the opening price; that's "not buying and selling pressure."
- **How to use:** Hunt shorts at or just above the midnight opening price, combined with time of day (killzone) and a PD array.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD 15m: midnight open extended; price went above it several times ("higher once more... and again") before beginning its descent into the daily range low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:29](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=449) — want a rally from midnight = Judas swing. [0:08:18](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=498) — rally into imbalance forms the daily high. [0:09:05](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=545) — trade above the midnight opening price. [0:09:52](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=592) — not buying/selling pressure; add time and price.

### Killzones — FX New York open killzone vs index futures
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - **FX New York open killzone: 7:00–10:00 a.m. ET** — specific to FX pairs; hunt your setup within that block.
  - **Index futures: 8:30–11:00 a.m.** (he says "your local time" meaning NY); he can take index trades up to 10:40–10:45 and still be OK.
  - FX: generally no new trades after 10:00 a.m. unless there's a news driver. A 10:00 a.m. high-impact release extends the NY killzone into the 11:00–11:30 window.
  - Other post-10:00 drivers: e.g. CAD traders must allow for crude oil inventories, usually around 10:30 a.m.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:52](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=592) — 7–10 for FX; 8:30–11 for indices. [0:11:28](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=688) — killzone annotation on 5m. [0:24:54](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1494) — 10:00 news extends killzone to 11–11:30; oil at 10:30.

### Order block (bearish) — defined by FVG + displacement + last up-close candle; use the body
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Price ran up, overshot the imbalance a little, and reached the last up-close candle = bearish order block. How you know it's an order block: the imbalance after a nice move lower (displacement + FVG), and price trading back up to the last up-close candle. He prefers to annotate/expect returns to the **body** of the order block candle, not the long wicks.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD 15m/5m: OB hit early in the NY open killzone around the 7:00 hour; later the 10:00 news spike ran back into the body of the same bearish OB.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:39](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=639) — overshoots imbalance to last up-close candle. [0:15:25](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=925) — return to the OB body, not wicks.

### Opening price selection — midnight vs 8:30 (FX)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** NY-session FX traders use the 8:30 a.m. opening price but also refer to the midnight open ("recalibrate at 8:30"). If bearish and the 8:30 opening price is **lower** than the midnight opening price, use the lower one — it sets the minimum threshold for the Judas swing/protraction up. Reverse for bullish: look for a move below the opening price to go long.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:28](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=688) — recalibrate at 8:30. [0:18:35](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1115) — use the lower of the two when bearish.

### News drivers (ISM Services PMI) as volatility injection / stop raid
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** He doesn't care about the data, expectation, or what the number means — only that the algorithm uses the injection of volatility at or around that time to reprice and facilitate trades ("it's a rigged game" for those not in the know). High-impact news is the "wild card": it's designed to stop out traders trailing stops above recent swing highs, jamming price above the opening price so their buy stops become counterparty to smart-money shorts. You may avoid news if you don't know what you're doing, "but don't be afraid of them."
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD: 10:00 a.m. ET ISM Services PMI; price dropped after the 7:00-hour OB tap, then retraced up into the 10:00 release, back above the midnight open and into the bearish OB body, then fell.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:15](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=735) — ISM Services PMI at 10; volatility only. [0:16:13](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=973) — news as stop-raid wild card. [0:24:54](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1494) — don't be afraid of news.

### Intermediate-term high (protected by an imbalance)
- **Kind:** rule — refers back to Ep. 12
- **What ICT says:** After a swing low took out a prior low, price rallied once more above the midnight open but did **not** take out the earlier high, because there's an imbalance (FVG) below/associated with that high. That makes it an intermediate-term high — if bearish, it shouldn't be taken out. Not merely because there's an imbalance, but because the narrative is lower prices + it went above the midnight open + the imbalance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:50](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=830), [0:14:38](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=878).

### Swing high + displacement + FVG entry (5m/4m)
- **Kind:** entry model (applied)
- **What ICT says:** Return into the OB forms a 3-candle swing high (lower high either side) → look for displacement lower → market structure shift → retrace into FVG → short. On the 4m chart, a clean FVG in the down leg: price runs right up into it, closes it, "then and only then" aggressively runs below the short-term low toward the objective.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD 4m: FVG between one candle's low and another's high; price filled it, displaced lower to ~1.0901 (low of day per his memory).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:25](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=925) — three-candle swing high. [0:17:00](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1020) — find the FVG on the 4m (pause and look). [0:20:11](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1211) — FVG hit, displacement to 1.09.

### Entries, stops & targets in FX (fluffing exits for spread)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Don't aim for the exact level — "fluff up" exits by **3–5 pips**; beginners use **10 pips**. E.g. target 1.0900 → limit to cover shorts at 1.0905.
  - Spreads: every broker can widen the spread when it suits them; overzealous limits have cost him many "amazing exits."
  - Stop above the entry candle's high (or above the swing high if you want more room).
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD hypothetical: entry ~1.096 (as captioned), stop 1.09728 — he'd round the risk to ~10 pips; target just above 1.0900. Risk-to-reward better than 8:1; on a hypothetical $100,000 demo / funded-challenge account this one trade "gives you basically what you're looking for."
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:11](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1211) — fluff exits 3–5 pips. [0:21:00](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1260) — 1.0905 limit for a 1.0900 target. [0:21:46](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1306) — spreads; 10 pips for new traders. [0:22:33](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1353) — entry/stop numbers. ## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- After BSL above relative equal highs is taken, expect SSL next; three down-close days → next day bearish.
- Institutional levels: 00, 20, 50, 80; big figures attract liquidity.
- Swing projection only valid if the fulcrum low breaks; pick the level confluent with liquidity/big figure.
- Bearish day: rally above the midnight NY open first (Judas/protraction); shorts at or just above the opening price.
- FX NY open killzone 7:00–10:00 a.m. ET; indices 8:30–11:00 a.m. (index entries OK until ~10:40–10:45).
- No new FX trades after 10:00 a.m. unless a news driver; a 10:00 high-impact release extends the window to 11:00–11:30.
- CAD: crude oil inventories ~10:30 a.m.
- Use 8:30 open for NY FX trades, refer to midnight; when bearish use the lower of the two.
- Bearish OB = displacement + FVG + last up-close candle; use the body.
- Intermediate-term high with an imbalance should not be taken out in a bearish narrative.
- Exits: fluff by 3–5 pips (10 for beginners); target 1.0900 → cover at 1.0905.
- Example trade: risk ~10 pips, R:R > 8:1.

## Glossary candidates
`Big figure` — the 00 price level (e.g. EURUSD 1.0900).
`Institutional price levels` — 00, 20, 50, 80 levels.
`Market protraction` — the counter-move (Judas swing) away from the day's true direction early in the session.
`New York open killzone (FX)` — 7:00–10:00 a.m. ET.
`Fluff the exit` — set the profit limit a few pips before the exact target to account for spread.
`Unfinished business` — liquidity (old low/high) not yet taken that price is still expected to reach.
`Intermediate-term high` — a swing high supported by an imbalance that should hold given the bearish narrative.
