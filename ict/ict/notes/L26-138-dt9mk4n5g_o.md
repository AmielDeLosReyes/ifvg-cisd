He separates a full SESSION (Asia 7pm–5am ET, London to ~10–11am ET, New York 9:30–4:00) from a KILL ZONE (a narrow sweet-spot window inside a session). He then shows how to scan the whole session range for "smooth" highs/lows (relative equal highs/lows that retail sees as support/resistance) as the draw on liquidity for the 7:00–9:00 am pre-market window. ## Concepts

### Session vs. Kill Zone (Asian / London / NY AM / pre-market)  (aliases ICT uses: "the entirety of the session" vs "ICT kill zone")
- **Kind:** definition / rule (new emphasis; clarifies a common misuse)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A session is the whole stretch of a region's trading; a kill zone is a specific, narrower window inside it. Brokers and others label whole sessions as "ICT kill zones" and that is wrong. "If it was just that easy, I would never have said kill zone."
  - Asian session: starts 7:00 pm ET, closes about 5:00 am ET. Asian KILL ZONE: "7:00 to 9:00" ET, a roughly 2-hour scalping window with usually a first-presented FVG and an old high/low to run for (caption garbled as "7:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m."; read as 7–9 pm (caption unclear)).
  - London KILL ZONE: 2:00 am–5:00 am ET. Sweet spot is 2:00–4:00 am; it extends to 5:00 because of overlap with Asia's close. He says this is a 3-hour window and the same for forex and index futures.
  - London SESSION runs until about 10:00–11:00 am ET (European close). The London extreme is usually set by 10–11 am, so that is a profit-taking time for London/European traders who traded overnight into NY hours; often a tradeable high or low forms then (not guaranteed).
  - New York session reference range: 9:30 open to 4:00 pm close ET.
  - Pre-market session: 7:00–9:00 am ET. Between London KZ end (5:00) and 7:00 am is "dead time": he does nothing there, avoids it ("keeps you out of the molasses"). He admits he occasionally traded it in forex with strong conviction but tells learners to avoid it.
  - After 4:00 am, London-zone moves are only of interest if they make a parabolic run to a higher-timeframe PD array (e.g., a daily suspension block high in the example).
  - Always set platform time to New York local time.
  - You can do every setup between 7:00 and 9:00 am and never engage the 9:30 open/opening range at all; people avoid the open because they do not know what to look for.
- **How to identify:** vertical lines at session start/close for the "session" box (7 pm–5 am for Asia; 2 am–11 am for London) vs. a separate smaller box for the kill zone (2–5 am London).
- **How to use:** the SESSION range is where you search for liquidity (smooth highs/lows, session high/low). The KILL ZONE is where you expect the setup/entry to form. Do not limit the search for equal highs/lows to the kill zone, because price may already have traded beyond it and used the level.
- **Example in this video:** Mon Jan 12, 2026. Asian session box from Sunday 7 pm to 5 am; London kill-zone box 2–5 am (London low formed 3:43 am, "right before 4:00", reacting off a stale daily-chart volume imbalance/suspension block); London session box to 11 am.

### Time-first framing: which look-back range to scan for which trading window
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - "First time, then price." Frame time first, then within that window look for the price feature (smooth high/low).
  - For pre-market (7–9 am) trading, the reference is the overnight Asian session (7 pm–5 am), plus the London range for 2:00–7:00 am; look for smooth highs/lows inside that window.
  - For PM-session trading (afternoon equities), the first frame of reference is the 2:00 am–11:00 am range (close of Asia to London "lunch"). He says use NQ and ES for the PM session; avoid the Dow ("unruly"); a third index in the caption is unclear.
  - For New York session trading, use the 9:30–4:00 range; an Asian or London trader then uses that information overnight.
  - Rolling it forward: each session uses the previous session's range as its reference window.
  - A beginner needs nothing more than: the price action inside the 7–9 am window plus overnight highs/lows and overnight relative equal highs/lows.
  - Look-back for PD arrays: 20, 40, 60 days. Highest-probability, crispest, most responsive PD arrays are formed within the last 20 TRADING days (not calendar days). Within that, the most salient set for the day is last ~5 trading days (a week).
- **How to identify / procedure (as he walks it):** (1) draw vertical lines for the reference window; (2) find the highest high and lowest low in it (time aspect complete); (3) inside that range, scan for smooth highs/lows, compressing/zooming out the chart; (4) decide which are likely to be run in the next window.
- **How to use:** a hypothetical at 1:30 pm: take the 2 am–11 am range, find the high and low; if the high end has been made already (premium market), look for smooth lows (relative equal lows) inside it as the next liquidity run.
- **Example in this video:** the same procedure applied at 8:25 am Mon Jan 12 to the Asian range; Asian highs identified as the draw before 7:00 am.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:54](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2214) · [0:40:04](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2404) — PM session frame 2 am–11 am · [0:41:39](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2499) — time first then price · [1:33:46](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=5626) — NY session 9:30–4:00 as reference for the next session

### Smooth highs / smooth lows = relative equal highs / lows (engineered liquidity, retail S/R trap)  (aliases: "obvious level", "single high / single low" when only one)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He looks for "smooth" highs or lows within a time frame: clean, textbook-looking support/resistance (high, high, high) that retail is taught to trust. Smooth areas tend to be made jagged: price runs to the level, trades through it, and the cleanness is destroyed.
  - He does NOT trade at the level when it forms; he looks back across the session for it, because that is the trap. It is "engineered liquidity": price runs to an obvious level on purpose.
  - Retail logic is "diametrically opposed" to his: if a new trader would see it as support/resistance and he sees it as a likely run for liquidity, he puts it at roughly 90% likelihood of being right, and the last 10% is why you use stops.
  - His definition of the relative-equal-high structure: price runs to a level, a later high falls short of it (closest to current price is LOWER than the anchoring one), then the little "red herring" run. The failed second high is the retail "strong resistance" trap.
  - Why the liquidity is bigger there: new shorts entered at the failed high and existing short stops were moved down to that second high, so the pool just below the first high is larger and a stronger draw. This is how "bigger bubbles" are known without BookMap.
  - If there is no smooth high/low inside the range (e.g., a single swing high followed by continuation lower), then the single high/low (e.g., overnight high) is the target.
  - Odds of a run into the level increase "exponentially" if the smooth level sits at or around a quadrant level of the whole range (e.g., lower half of the 7 pm–5 am range).
  - Zoom out/compress ranges so the smooth edges stand out ("nose to the tree" analogy).
- **How to identify:** eye jumps to the level; flat highs/lows in a session; second one slightly under the first (for highs).
- **How to use:** expect a pre-market (7–9 am) run to it; pair with first presented FVG as the entry catalyst; target is run on the stops above/below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ overnight Sun Jan 11–Mon Jan 12: smooth highs formed Sunday around 10:00 pm ET; price then only fell overnight; a later, lower high failed to reach the first = the trap; at 7:00 am price displaced up and ran to them.

### Relative equal highs/lows — anchor point and wick grading
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Anchor point = the leftmost swing, the one that starts the definition. For relative equal HIGHS the anchor is the higher of the pair; for relative equal LOWS the anchor is the lower of the pair. The one closest to price must be lower (highs) / higher (lows) than the anchor.
  - If the anchor swing has a wick, you must grade the wick (lower quadrant, consequent encroachment, upper quadrant). Price may only trade into the wick (to its upper quadrant, CE, or lower quadrant) and then reject, instead of taking out the extreme.
  - Not every relative equal high/low is equal in quality.
  - Always (he stresses "never ever not do this") take a partial when running to such a level: he likes a partial anywhere between the wick's lower quadrant and its consequent encroachment, i.e., split it in half. You do not need the high taken out.
  - Stopping short of the wick's CE (not even touching it) signals weakness; expect reversal.
- **How to identify:** a candle with a wick forming the anchor; mark quadrants/CE on the wick.
- **How to use:** target the wick (not necessarily the extreme); partial near lower-quadrant-to-CE area of the wick when running up (mirror for lows); read failure to reach CE as a warning.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-minute, after the Asian BSL run (~8:00–9:00 am): two relative equal lows, left one has a wick/shadow; he says if price dives to disrupt it, it might only trade into the wick's lower quadrant or CE and turn.
- **Timestamps:** [1:17:40](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4660) — wick on REL · [1:19:18](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4758) — anchor definition, partial rule · [1:20:05](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4805) — CE as weakness test
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** new refinement; his wording on which quadrant to use for partials on highs is "between the lower quadrant and consequent encroachment" (low side of the wick = the part price meets first when coming from below).

### First presented fair value gap (pre-market 7:00–9:00 and 9:30–10:00) with displacement
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The "first presented FVG" is the first FVG created by genuine displacement in the window (7:00 am start for pre-market; 9:30–10:00 for the open). It must be displacement: a run through a short-term high or low at the specific time; it also shows direction and magnitude ("tipping my hand").
  - Not the tiny gap between two candles that does not displace; not an engulfing candle; the previous candle's body is irrelevant.
  - Rule of thumb: if the gap is under 10 handles in NQ, "80% likely" it is NOT the first presented FVG; wait for another FVG. This explains why his examples sometimes skip an earlier small gap.
  - It is the real "point of control" (his words, versus volume-profile POC).
  - Old first-presented FVGs (Jan 2, Jan 7) are kept on the chart, graded on eighths/quadrants, and treated as PD arrays; his bearish SIBI example: when bullish, don't short a SIBI but watch whether it acts as an inversion FVG, framing high/upper quadrant/midpoint/lower quadrant/low and checking if price respects it (less likely during consolidation).
- **How to identify:** 7:00 am; run through a short-term high; FVG sized more than ~10 NQ handles; within the framework of the Asian BSL.
- **How to use:** enter in the FVG (discount), target the smooth highs; ICT-style partials.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m Jan 12 ~7:00 am (see Asian BSL). Contrast case: if the high had not been run and price instead dropped into the Jan 2 FVG, that drop in the 9:30–10:00 window would be read as a Judas swing and he would look for upside displacement.

### RTH Opening Range Gap (previous settlement → 9:30 open) — procedure and statistics
- **Kind:** entry model / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Distinct from the New Day Opening Gap and New Week Opening Gap. RTH opening range gap = difference between previous RTH close/settlement (4:00 pm ET) and the 9:30 am open. Monday's gap = Friday's RTH settlement to Monday 9:30 open.
  - Procedure each morning: switch the chart to Regular Trading Hours (tab at lower right), read the previous close (OHLC at top left), draw a line at it and label it "RTH opening range gap" (high or low unknown until the open), then at 9:30 draw the open price as the other side. Drop a fib across it and grade it on quadrants/eighths: upper quadrant, consequent encroachment, etc.
  - At the open, the market will likely explore into the gap. Stat stated: about 70% of the time the gap's consequent encroachment (CE) is delivered/tagged by 10:00 am; knowing this is a "huge advantage."
  - The gap needs not be fully closed. Three quarters (upper quadrant) is often enough.
  - Vacuum: RTH had no trading between the settlement and the open, so price rushes back into that unpopulated area even though overnight ETH already traded some of it (soda-cap-release analogy).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Jan 12: Friday RTH settlement 25,933.50; Monday 9:30 open 25,746.25, so a discount gap with the settlement line as the gap HIGH. At 9:07 he already draws the 25,933.50 line (well above current price) and labels it ORG high, then adds the low at the 9:30 open. After the open, CE (about 25,839.9) is tagged within roughly a minute and the upper quadrant (25,887) around 9:39 ET (the 3/4 mark), a run of about 150 handles in total; the last quarter up to full closure at 25,933.50 was not needed. Stacked above the open: Asian session high (the range's highest high, run after the open and read as "cleared"), then this week's NWOG low, with Friday's settlement just below it; daily relative equal highs at 26,133.50 are the higher draw. Exact order of the Asian high vs. upper quadrant is read from his walkthrough (caption unclear).

### Partial profits / trade management on the morning's run
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** after a parabolic quick run ("three quarters of the opening range gap in a few minutes") bank something (partial or full) because a retracement can always be deeper than you can hold, e.g., back into the 25,780s; also take something off when a run on the Asian session high clears overnight highs. If you were long from the 7:00 am first presented FVG and closed at the upper quadrant, you already captured the lion's share of the move and the daily candle. ## Glossary candidates
`Session vs kill zone` — whole regional trading period vs. the specific sweet-spot window within it.
`Asian session buy-side liquidity` — smooth highs inside 7 pm–5 am ET range, the draw for the next-morning run.
`Smooth high / smooth low` — clean textbook S/R-looking extreme; likely to be run and made jagged.
`Engineered liquidity` — a level price is drawn to because retail finds it obvious.
`Anchor point (relative equal highs/lows)` — leftmost swing; the higher of REH, the lower of REL.
`First presented FVG` — first displacement FVG of the 7:00 pre-market or 9:30 opening window; real "control" in his words.
`Common gap` — non-displacement gap of little interest (open-outcry era).
`RTH opening range gap` — previous RTH settlement to the 9:30 open.
`Phantom trend line` — retail's diagonal support/resistance line used as a liquidity pool to attack.
`Dead time` — gap between London KZ close (5:00) and 7:00 am when he avoids trading.
`Naked chart` — workspace with no annotations to avoid pre-committing to levels.
