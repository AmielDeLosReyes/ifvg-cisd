## Summary
ICT goes short live at 10:00 a.m. ET on July 14, 2026, a day with CPI data and the Fed chair speaking at 10:00 (and again the next day). He sells into an inversion FVG that sits over a suspension block, after a run into the CE of the prior day's RTH opening range gap, targeting the liquidity under the 8:30 lows and a "event horizon" midpoint between two old lows. The video is mostly a running commentary on how he sizes, takes partials, removes risk, moves the stop, and refuses to "strangle" the position while the Fed causes wicks. He wanted a single-candle run below the 8:30 low; it only came partly, price reacted off a short-term low, and he was eventually stopped out after banking partials. The point is the process of managing a trade in a Fed-related, hard-to-trade environment.

## Concepts

### Fed-speaker days: wait for 10:00 and expect wicky "Fed-related delivery"  (aliases ICT uses: Fed-related delivery, time distortion, "tomfoolery")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He does not trade until 10:00 because the Fed chair speaks at 10:00 ("give it a little bit of time; work out time distortion"); everything before that is "just to hurt people that think they know what they're doing" (back-and-forth stop runs).
  - After CPI (data that morning/day before) price had gone into the half gap (CE) of the RTH opening range gap as he indicated it would on CPI/PPI.
  - During Fed commentary expect "wild little wick-type moves"; conditions to adhere to depend on the state of the market; today is "Fed-related delivery" and tomorrow too.
  - "Don't strangle your position; give it a chance to cook and let it prove to you one way or the other."
  - Be okay with being stopped out prematurely, or stopped out and then price moves your way; "that is characteristic of the environment."
- **How to use:** for a short at 10:00: stop just above the high, target the liquidity below; give the trade room; don't sit with larger risk than necessary; accept stop-outs.
- **Example in this video:** July 14, 2026, 10:00 ET: short after the Fed chair starts talking; stop just above the high; lots of liquidity below the 8:30 lows.

- **How to use:** short on a run into the CE/quadrant of the previous day's RTH ORG after buy-side is taken; first partial at the next levels below.

### Inversion FVG over a suspension block; adding on taps  (aliases ICT uses: inversion fair value gap, suspension block, "tap it one more time")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** He sells into a fair value gap sitting on a suspension block that "is going to act as an inversion" (bearish iFVG). He wants to add three more contracts inside it ("tap it one more time"). He then adds back inside both the wick and the lower quadrant of the bearish iFVG. After the low is taken he does not want price to return to the iFVG; if it does, the short is in jeopardy. He later notes the gap must not become an inversion against him (a bullish iFVG snapping into his stop).
- **How to use:** short on taps into the iFVG; add inside the iFVG and its lower quadrant; stop above the gap/high; manage as below.
- **Example in this video:** 10:00 ET: price taps the iFVG repeatedly; he adds contracts (total of three then 3 more, he cites "three contracts" repeatedly); price breaks below a short-term low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:37](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=97) — iFVG over suspension block, add three; [0:02:26](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=146) — adds in the wick and lower quadrant
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He misspeaks "pension block"/"inversion pair" (caption) but means suspension block / iFVG.

### Single-candle run below the 8:30 low  (aliases ICT uses: one single delivery, "40 seconds to do this", liquidity below the 8:30 lows)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He wants price to run below the 8:30 low in one fast candle: "just one single delivery ... not multiple candles. Multiple candles doesn't bode well for a run all the way down" to the target; a single candle through the low bodes well.
  - He counts down the candle ("we have 40 seconds ... 25 seconds") on the 1-minute candle; by the time he spoke, the candle touched the low but did not get below it.
  - The low of that candle was 652.5 vs the 8:30 low at 651 even (1.5 handles below; digits partial in captions); his limit order was slightly too soft.
  - If price walks through the low it should accelerate toward the event horizon; he does not want it to go back above the low once taken.
- **How to identify:** 1m candle trading below the 8:30 low with a large body that closes below.
- **How to use:** if the single-candle run does not occur (price stalls below/at the low), reduce risk, take a partial, expect a possible return.
- **Example in this video:** ~10:03: price reaches but does not clear the 8:30 low in the first attempt; later it takes the low then reluctantly stalls ("big beefy candle shoot lower; that's what we're looking for").
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:02](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=242) — single run, not multiple candles; [0:04:49](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=289) — candle low vs 8:30 low, two pools; [0:09:48](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=588) — big candle needed
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A fairly new qualitative "speed of delivery" rule; no hard number beyond the one-candle idea. It did not fully deliver, and he explains why he took partials.

### Event horizon between two old lows  (aliases ICT uses: event horizon, consequent encroachment between an old low and an old low)
- **Kind:** target / reuse of coinage from L26-002
- **What ICT says:** There are two pools of liquidity below: the annotated one and another further down. The midpoint between two old lows is the event horizon (ballparked, "don't be so precise"). He wants a walk through the low and acceleration to the event horizon; if price closes below it, that would indicate a run toward the "545s" (price level, caption partial). First partial: one contract at event horizon; the rest left on the book as limit orders; once at event horizon the stop moves to just below the low of the inversion FVG that sits over the suspension block.
- **How to use:** event horizon = partial target; closing below = run to the next major pool.
- **Example in this video:** targets: 8:30 lows then event horizon, then ~545 area.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:39](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=339) — event horizon, ballpark; [0:16:16](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=976) — at event horizon between an old low and an old low
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In L26-002 the event horizon is the 50% of an unrealized dealing range for fading a rally; here it is the 50% between two lows for continuation to the downside.

### Stop management in a news/Fed environment: risk-free first, stop above swing high, never "strangle"  (aliases ICT uses: take the risk out, free look, lower the stop, stop just above the high)
- **Kind:** rule (trade management)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Initial stop just above the high of the entry area.
  - After price breaks the low, he lowers the stop to "take the risk out of the trade," but not too low.
  - He did not want to bring the stop to just below the low of the inversion FVG until price got below the low with a body, because the Fed could create a quick jump and wick; a stop at that level "becomes a mechanism for me to worry. I don't want to worry."
  - There was a nice swing high just above where shorts have stops; they could wick it once more while bodies stay below the CE of the wick and still deliver lower prices.
  - "If I'm stopped out, I'm okay. I had a free look." Maximum risk after reducing: a small locked-in amount (he cites dollar figures, caption-garbled).
  - With only one contract left he moves the stop at selected levels; wants a close below the (wick) gap before dropping the stop to just above it; "give it room" so as not to be stopped prematurely.
  - After the partial, stops moved down to a candle level to avoid retracement.
- **How to use:** (1) enter with stop above high; (2) after a partial and after the low is taken, reduce risk; (3) only lower the stop below the iFVG low after a body closes below the low; (4) give room in Fed windows.
- **Example in this video:** short; first partial below the old low; second at event horizon region; stop moved; final stop-out at a small profit/flat.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:26](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=146) — takes the risk out; [0:10:37](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=637) — doesn't lower the stop below the iFVG yet; [0:11:25](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=685) — swing high, free look; [0:17:05](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1025) — close below the gap first, give it room
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He emphasizes not putting the stop where it causes worry; a mental-comfort rule as much as a technical one.

### Partials: take one off, then three contracts, then keep one; bank what was made  (aliases ICT uses: partial, locking in, "a bonus")
- **Kind:** rule / management
- **What ICT says:**
  - Position size stays what it is ("I don't need a sell limit order now"); he takes one contract off below the 8:30 low; later takes three contracts off ("I like the idea of this being three contracts"); then re-buys one to cover (small scalp); finally only one contract left.
  - After a partial: "it's not high probability that we're going to take these lows out; it's a best-case scenario for me"; it took out the low and then refused to go lower, which is why he took a partial.
  - "Making what was made here is sufficient enough. This down here is just a bonus."
  - Lesson on being nimble: an aggressive trader who saw the early wicks (a warning sign of adverse delivery) can close the position early rather than wait for the stop; it depends on experience.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:14](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=194) — one contract off below 8:30 low; [0:14:38](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=878) — three contracts off; [0:18:41](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1121) — what was made is sufficient; [0:21:06](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1266) — wicks = early morning warning sign

### Premium sensitivity of an old 8:30 low  (aliases ICT uses: premium sensitivity, treat the wick as a gap, CE of wick)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price worked back up into the old low from 8:30, it showed "a little bit of premium sensitivity" (wilting at that level); he wants it to wilt on that candle or the next or his stop is in jeopardy. The gap/wick is treated as a gap: bodies must stay below the CE of the wick (as long as they do, the move remains heavy). If the gap flips to an inversion FVG against him, price will snap to his stop.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:34](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=394) — bodies must stay below the CE of the wick; [0:18:41](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1121) — premium sensitivity at the old low; [0:19:28](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1168) — don't want an iFVG against me
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Uses the same bodies-below-CE logic seen in L26-049.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Do not take the trade before the 10:00 Fed-chair speech; go short at 10:00 after the first reaction (with a stop above the high).
- Stop just above the high; reduce risk after the low is taken; do not drop the stop to the iFVG low until a body closes below the low.
- Single-candle run through the 8:30 low is the ideal; multi-candle grinding is a negative.
- First partial: below the 8:30 low; next: event horizon (midpoint between two old lows); closing below the event horizon points to the ~545 area.
- Keep bodies below the CE of the wick; no bodies back above the old low once taken.
- Never use market replay for demonstration; show live executions.
- Numbers: candle low 652.5 vs 8:30 low 651.0 (digits partial); dollar figures quoted are caption-garbled (risk-free about $837 free look; locked about $3,587; one contract remaining stop/target about $1,692.5 / $4,192, caption unclear).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Don't strangle the position; give it room; be okay with getting stopped out and then seeing price move your way ([0:12:12](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=732), [0:13:00](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=780)).
- Don't put the stop where it becomes a source of worry ([0:13:47](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=827)).
- A stop-out after banking partials is not a loss; "you had a wonderful trade. You just got kicked out"; analogy of a cruise missing a port ([0:20:17](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1217)).
- Failure on one trade does not disrupt the efficacy of your model; confidence comes from repetition and desensitization ([0:17:52](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1072), [0:21:54](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1314)).
- If you never give a trade enough room you will never get the bigger wins ([0:18:41](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1121)).
- The difficulty you feel is normal when you are against something holding price back ([0:13:00](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=780)).
- Balance aggression to your experience: nimble traders can exit on early warning wicks ([0:21:06](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1266)).
- He mocks "fake trend line support mythology" and Elliott wave as irrelevant to the delivery ([0:00:00](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=0), [0:06:34](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=394)).

## Glossary candidates
`FED-RELATED DELIVERY` — choppy, wicky price behaviour around a Fed chair speech; treat with wider room.
`EVENT HORIZON` — the 50% level between two old lows (or an unrealized range) used as partial target.
`FREE LOOK` — a trade whose risk has been reduced so any stop-out costs nothing/little.
`STRANGLE THE POSITION` — managing the stop/size so tightly the trade can't work.
`SINGLE DELIVERY` — one-candle run through a level, required for a clean continuation.
