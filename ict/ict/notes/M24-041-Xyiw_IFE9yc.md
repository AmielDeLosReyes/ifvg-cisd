### RTH opening range gap and its midpoint  (aliases: "opening range gap", "mid gap", "settlement")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a chart set to RTH, price delivery is 9:30 ET until 4:14 PM ET. The previous day's RTH settlement (at 4:14 ET, as he states it) to the 9:30 open is the opening range gap. The rectangle drawn between those two prices, together with the time, is the opening range gap and the opening range.
  - The midpoint of the gap is the key level. 70% of the time the gap gets filled to halfway. He pushes back on a Twitter study claiming less, saying he has data back about 40 years and has lived it.
  - Practical use: an opening gap down means the first-30-minutes bias is bullish (up to the midpoint). He calls this a nice scalping opportunity.
  - Next, price often reaches to the previous day's settlement as well.
- **How to identify:** Mark the previous RTH settlement price and the 9:30 open. Measure the 50% (CE).
- **How to use:** Gap down at the open means expect the first-30-minute run up to the gap midpoint. After that, look for a reversal if the higher-timeframe bias is bearish.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Thu 10-03, gap-down open. Price traded right back up to the mid-gap, pulled into a 1m FVG, found support at the mid-gap, rallied to previous settlement, formed another FVG, then cleared the buy-side and spent time there (he reads that as buy-stop accumulation by smart money so they can sell short). The market then broke lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:23](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=263) RTH lens and gap definition · [0:05:11](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=311) 70% claim and defense · [0:06:00](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=360) buy-stop accumulation then short

### Respect of the lower half of a bearish FVG (SIBI) as a continuation tell  (aliases: "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency")
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 1m (ETH chart) price traded up to buy-side, broke lower, and came right back into a sell-side imbalance gap. Later, after much chopping, price entered only the lower half of that gap and never the upper half. That tells you price is more likely to break lower.
  - Wicks in the consolidation reached up to their own 50% (CE) levels, which supports the read.
  - After displacement lower there was a gap he first expected to be revisited, then corrected himself: it was a breakaway gap.
  - Price then attacked the sell-side below, then the weekly BISI CE, made the low of the day, and consolidated into the afternoon.
- **How to identify:** For a bearish FVG, note whether the highest price into the gap since formation stays below the midpoint (CE). If so, the gap is respected as resistance.
- **How to use:** Lower-half-only respect = lean continuation lower. Target the pool of sell-side liquidity below and the weekly CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:00](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=360) 1m RTH view · [0:06:49](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=409) lower half only, breakaway gap · [0:07:37](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=457) delivery to weekly CE
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is the same logic as M24-040's "stay in the lower half" and "breakaway gap" rule, applied to a bearish gap on the opposite side.

### iFVG short with scaling out (live trade disclosure)  (aliases: "inversion fair value gap", "institutional order flow entry drill")
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 15s chart there was a BISI (bullish FVG). Once price traded outside (below) it, price came back up into it. That is the inversion FVG, the entry (short).
  - Entry 1: short 6 contracts at about 20,010.75.
  - Price traded lower, spiked up once more, then took out a candle's low. He added 4 more contracts there (10 total).
  - Partials: at 19964 (caption garbled around "half"; the price then traded into an institutional order flow entry drill level), another partial at 19925, a pullback into an FVG, then 1 contract off at about 19882.25, roughly 3 handles from the low of the day and below the weekly BISI CE.
  - Two contracts were left as a runner in case it extended. They were stopped at about 19941.5 (as stated).
- **How to identify:** An FVG that price has traded through and then re-enters from the other side acts as the entry zone. He says you cannot see it on the 1m, only the 15s.
- **How to use:** Enter at the iFVG, add on a break of a candle low, take partials into level targets (including an institutional order flow entry drill level and sell-side pools), run the remainder to the weekly CE.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s Thu 10-03, after the 9:30 open consolidation. All prices as stated by ICT. Number of contracts at the first two partials not stated.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- New student rule: trade Monday and Tuesday of NFP week, skip Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.
- RTH = 9:30 ET to 4:14 PM ET (his figure for settlement).
- RTH opening gap midpoint is reached about 70% of the time (he claims 40 years of data).
- Gap down at the open means bullish bias for the first 30 minutes (to the midpoint).
- Bearish if price stays at or below a given higher-timeframe candle's open (caption unclear which).

## Process, risk & psychology
- As a mentor he steers new students away from news-week trading because experienced eyes can find setups but inexperienced ones lose their bearings ([0:02:47](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=167), [0:10:02](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=602)).

## Glossary candidates
`RTH opening range gap` — the gap between the prior RTH settlement and the 9:30 ET open. The midpoint is the first-30-minute draw.
`Breakaway gap` — an FVG left behind after displacement that price does not need to revisit.
`Institutional order flow entry drill` — an ICT entry-level concept (mentioned only in passing here, caption unclear).
`Buy-stop accumulation` — smart money absorbing buy stops above price in order to sell short.
