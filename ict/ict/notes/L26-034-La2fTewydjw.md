## Concepts

### Weekly range "fulfilled" and leaving yourself free time
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** after publishing a draw on liquidity (Monday London session highs) and the opposite side (the relative equal lows below), once the market has taken both, "the weekly range is fulfilled" for him. He does not care what PPI or Friday's consumer sentiment does. He prefers to define where the high and low of the week can complete, then use the freed time for other things instead of worrying about extra moves.
- **How to use:** define the high and the low of the week you are interested in early in the week; once both are taken, stop forcing trades.

### Know the draw first (first thing ICT teaches)
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:** the very first thing he teaches is to know where the market is likely to go (the draw on liquidity). If you do not understand that, "everything you do afterwards is going to be incorrect". The dealing range and its octants/quadrants then tell you where PD arrays should form and, if they form at graded levels, they are perceived as higher probability or "validated". That does not remove the need to know the draw.
- **How to use:** pick the prominent price leg (high to low or low to high) as the dealing range, apply octant and quadrant levels, and see whether the PD array sits on a graded level.
- **Example in this video:** the swing from the high down to the low that stands out as a prominent leg, graded with octants and quadrants; he projects those levels through price on the 1-minute chart.

### Trading a high-impact 8:30 release: do not trade the first candles  (aliases: CPI/PPI 8:30 candle, "two-stage delivery")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - You cannot participate in the 8:30 candle on CPI/PPI. Anyone showing a fill on that candle was on a demo account; limit orders are not respected and slippage is severe ("literally impossible" to get a good fill).
  - Wait one or two candles (8:30, 8:31) and start looking around 8:32–8:33 for what the news volatility left behind.
  - CPI can deliver in two stages (like FOMC rate announcements): either a one-sided run, or one swing which then reverses and returns to the initial higher low. People who chase the first dump place protective buy stops just above, and that is where price goes.
  - Since a Monday London session relative equal high pool sat only a little above, a run above the initial 8:30 high was likely; it makes little sense to take out only the nearby high on a data release when a bigger pool is close.
- **How to identify:** news candle first, then raid of the sell-side (or buy-side) opposite the run, then a gap or imbalance formed in the aftermath.
- **How to use:** wait 2–3 minutes, then enter on the imbalance left behind (see next concept).
- **Example in this video:** on CPI morning price rallied on the 8:30 candle up toward a level, came back down through the New Week Opening Gap, raided sell-side resting below, then printed a BISI on the 15-second chart that he bought. Later the rally took out 29,984 with ease.

### Bearish breaker: two consecutive down-close candles as an order block, and mean threshold  (aliases: "breaker", "mean threshold (MT)", "VIP seating")
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the head fake above 29,984, price dropped, failed to touch the NWOG, filled an inefficiency and formed a bearish breaker. The structure was high, low, high: the lowest part between two highs, with the second being the higher high. Two down-close candles in a row are taken together as the order block ("you have to take both of those together").
  - It is an inversion because the block was first used as a bullish order block to run the liquidity above (29,984 was the target; he says nothing above that was ever targeted this week).
  - Breakers are basically a run on liquidity. It is validated when price then trades below the block's low (displacement through it; the SIBI created is the big sell-side imbalance).
  - The best seat ("VIP seating") is at the midpoint: for an order block / breaker it is the mean threshold; for gaps and wicks it is the consequent encroachment. Entry is in the lower half for a bearish breaker.
  - Stop: the high of the candle that created the SIBI through the low (the candle that validates the breaker). If that stop is too big, "you might have to trade with lower leverage".
  - Retail's "break and retest" short at the low of the candle gives you unnecessary heat; he prefers letting price dig into the range. Support/resistance retests can work if you understand liquidity first.
- **How to identify:** price runs buy-side liquidity (29,984), reverses, and trades below the lows of the last up-move's two consecutive down-close candles.
- **How to use:** short at the mean threshold or deeper (graded levels below), stop at the high of the displacement candle, target the new week opening gap low / relative equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-second after CPI: two consecutive down-close candles inside the run up to 29,984, then price broke below; he shorted into the breaker's lower half; price dove to the relative equal lows and back into the NWOG. A bearish-breaker rejection was also pyramided (see next).

### Grading levels inside a PD array: five specific limit-order levels  (aliases: octants and quadrants, percentile high/low)
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:** inside a breaker (from the mean threshold to the low) there are exactly five price levels for fills: (1) mean threshold (midpoint), (2) the lower octant (between the midpoint and the lowest quadrant, i.e. 0.375 of the range), (3) the lowest quadrant (0.25), (4) the lower octant but not the low (0.125), (5) the low of the bearish breaker. The numeric values are my reading of "quadrants and octants" on a 0–1 scale; he did not recite them in this video. "Any one of these levels you could use as a limit order." The midpoint is the best seating; lower levels are for scaling. Anything above the midpoint is a bonus. He rejects "zones" and the shotgun approach.
- **How to use:** place limits at these levels (or get close), stop at the displacement candle high, treat deeper levels as the better entry and use lower leverage if the stop is wide.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:03](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=903), [0:15:51](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=951), [0:16:38](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=998)

### Pyramiding with a core position and small adds  (aliases: "singles", "core positioning in my sweet spot")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** on a SIBI with a volume imbalance (include it), the range from the volume imbalance high down to the candle's high is graded the same way; the best seats are low in the range, the rest is bonus. He usually already has core positioning at his sweet spot, then adds small portions at the bottom end of the zone, and picks off market-order singles at the graded levels. As price attacks the short-term high, that is where he may add more.
- **Example in this video:** an add around a volume imbalance high down to the SIBI candle high, with bodies respecting the low of the range.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:35](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1235), [0:21:23](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1283), [0:22:09](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1329)

### Grading a wick: upper half and the .625 octant
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:** he grades wicks with a rectangle and a midpoint. The upper half of a wick is where the most sensitivity ("discount sensitivity") is, not at an assumed support level below. In the final example, bodies built inside the upper half of the wick, right on the .625 octant, then price traded down to the low of the NWOG and popped back through. He calls this a bread-and-butter 15-second scalp that occurs 50–60 times a day.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-second late in the session; a wick graded, a body at .625, then price runs to the NWOG low and pops back up.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Do not trade the 8:30 candle of CPI/PPI; wait 1–2 candles and look around 8:32–8:33.
- CPI can be two-stage; chasing the first dump puts stops where price will go.
- Level 29,984 = Monday's London session relative equal highs (target all week; "nothing above that").
- Breaker levels: midpoint (mean threshold) + lower octant + lowest quadrant + next octant + low = 5 levels.
- Stop for a breaker short: the high of the candle that created the SIBI through the low.
- Midpoint: mean threshold for order blocks/breakers; consequent encroachment for gaps and wicks.
- Draw first, then dealing range with octants/quadrants.
- Extend the NWOG through the entire week.
- A wick's upper half is the sensitive area; body at the .625 octant in the example.
- He reports about 9 handles of heat on his long after the CPI release.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Use less leverage if the technical stop is large (0:18:13).
- Strike rate: a 30–40% strike rate can make money with sound money management, no over-leverage and no over-trading, but it wears on developing traders. Some of his students run about 50–55% and made millions of real dollars (0:22:09–0:23:44).
- What matters is your own income from your executions, not other people's opinions. Don't trade to show you are smart or to spar with trolls; the point is money: "pad yourself out, bless your family, build a financial legacy" (0:23:44–0:24:31).
- The first thing is to know the draw; tools like moving averages are not what makes a concept smart. It is precision and repeated rules (0:07:11).
- People will say "complicated" or "not real"; he says that only means they are not equipped to use it (0:19:49).
- Do the math: he claims the week came to six figures (0:26:58); it's a claim about his result, not a statement to be generalized.

## Glossary candidates
`Mean threshold (MT)` — midpoint of an order block / breaker, equivalent of CE for a gap or wick.
`VIP seating` — his name for the best entry location (midpoint) within a PD array.
`Bearish breaker` — a failed bullish order block (two consecutive down-close candles) that was used to run liquidity, then broken through; used as a premium array to short.
`Two-stage delivery` — a news release that delivers one swing, reverses, then returns toward the starting level.
`Weekly range fulfilled` — both the high and low he named for the week have been taken.
`Institutional order flow entry drill` — (caption unclear) a retracement entry into a freshly formed array.
