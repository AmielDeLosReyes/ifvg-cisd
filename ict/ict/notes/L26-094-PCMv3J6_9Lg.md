## Concepts

### Principle of First Utilization (PFU)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** Every PD array (fair value gap, imbalance/inefficiency, order block, etc.) has an original utilization—the direction it forms and the price movement it facilitates. Once that PD array is traded **through** (i.e., price goes past it in the opposite direction), its characteristic reverses and it becomes an inversion. Once it inverts, it can be used again (in the opposite sense) and often becomes a much more formidable level than it was originally.
- **How to identify:** Step 1: mark the original FVG (e.g., bearish FVG in a down move; this is the original utilization zone). Step 2: if price rallies back up and trades **above** that FVG, it has been "traded through"; now it is inverted. Step 3: when price falls back into the inverted FVG, expect it to repel price (act as resistance), not support.
- **How to use:** Don't blindly trade every FVG. Ask: has this FVG been traded through yet? If yes, it's inverted and acts in the opposite direction. If no, it's still in original utilization. Bodies staying out of the upper half of an inverted FVG (below midpoint) = strong bearish rejection; bodies entering the upper half = loss of rejection / potential for the FVG to be fully filled (bullish).
- **Example in this video:** ES, April 21, 2026. A bearish FVG forms in the down move (original utilization = bearish, acts as supply/resistance from below). Price rallies back and trades above it (through), so it inverts. Now, when price retraces back into it on the way down later, it acts as resistance and repels price away (inversion utilization = bullish delivery into resistance, then reversal). Shown multiple times: the 26,695 level forms a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency that first acts as resistance (original utilization), then price trades below it, so when price rises back up into it, it acts as inversion (resistance/repulsion).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The FVG doesn't "fail" when traded through; it **changes characteristic**. A failed setup (bodies entering the upper half of an inverted FVG) is not the same as trading through the FVG in the original direction. The transition from original to inverted is instantaneous and deliberate by smart money; recognize it quickly.

### RTH Opening Range Gap (Regular Trading Hours)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + filters
- **What ICT says:** The gap between the previous day's close (or end of RTH) and the next day's 9:30 ET open. These gaps are pools of liquidity. You only need the **three most recent RTH gaps** to do intraday trading without bias or higher-timeframe bias. Use them hierarchically: today's gap, yesterday's gap, Friday's gap. Mark both the high and low of each gap as price targets.
- **How to identify:** Chart tool: mark the zone between yesterday's close (4:00 PM ET) and today's 9:30 AM ET open. The high of the gap is the "RTH opening range gap high"; the low is the "RTH opening range gap low". Also mark the "consequent encroachment" level—the midpoint or lowest wick touching the gap.
- **How to use:** When targeting a move, use the RTH gaps as sequential targets: first target = today's RTH opening range gap (high or low, depending on direction); if taken, next target = yesterday's RTH opening range gap; if taken, next target = Friday's RTH opening range gap. When price reaches a gap, expect it to stop, pause, or reverse at that level. Mark the gap on a notepad to keep the chart clean. Use the octant or quadrant (dividing the gap into 8 or 4 zones) for finer entry points.
- **Example in this video:** ES, April 21, 2026. Three gaps: (1) Tuesday RTH (9:30 ET open to Friday close), high ~26,800+; (2) Monday RTH, high ~26,800+; (3) Friday RTH, high 26,695 (the target ICT tweeted in the morning). Price rallies, falls short of ATH, then sells off. First stop: Monday/Tuesday RTH gap high (~26,800). Then sold off through both those gaps. Second stop/target: Friday RTH gap low (~26,695). Price reached it, reversed (the octant level stopped it perfectly). Also dug into Friday's gap with a specific octant level (the 1/8 mark).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Opening range gaps from older days (more than 3 days back) are old data and less reliable. The three-gap rule works because it captures the current market structure; older gaps are stale. Use a notepad, not chart annotations, to track the levels—it keeps you focused on price action, not visual clutter.

### Sell-side vs. Buy-side Imbalance / Inefficiency
- **Kind:** definition + pair of terms
- **What ICT says:** When price makes a down move fast and aggressively, it leaves behind imbalances: (1) **sell-side imbalance** = unmet sell orders above (when price ran down, sellers didn't have a chance to exit); (2) **buy-side inefficiency** = untested buy orders below (buyers weren't active on the way down, their bids were skipped). Together: "sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency" (SIBI). This is a barrier. When price retraces back up, it must respect that barrier or re-engage in the down move.
- **How to identify:** After a down move, mark the zone from the top of the down run back to the bottom. That zone is the SIBI. It's a visual imbalance (wide candles, aggressive drops, no bodies in certain areas).
- **How to use:** SIBI acts as resistance on retrace. Expect price to struggle to break above it on the way back up. If price does break above it with bodies in the upper half, the down move is losing conviction and you should reduce or exit your short. Use the SIBI as a reason for price to reverse lower again (entry for shorts, or hold shorts).
- **Example in this video:** After the rally from 26,695, a SIBI forms. When price retraces back up into it from the lower low, bodies must stay in the lower half of the SIBI to show continued selling pressure. When price breaks above it (bodies in upper half), the setup is weakening. Also, at 26,830, a SIBI / buy-side inefficiency acts as a target on the way down, then when price rallies back into it, it becomes an inversion resistance.

### Engineering Liquidity
- **Kind:** concept + advanced technique
- **What ICT says:** Smart money deliberately creates patterns that appear to be targeting buy-side or sell-side liquidity, luring retail traders into false breakouts and reversals. "Engineering buy-side liquidity" means leaving relative equal highs (false highs that look like resistance) so that when price rises again, retail traders place buy stops just above them. Smart money then runs price up, takes those stops, and reverses. This is not random; it's deliberate market structure manipulation.
- **How to identify:** Watch for patterns: price makes a high, retreats, makes another high at roughly the same level (relative equal high). This looks like failed breakout resistance. Next, watch if price rallies back up and above those equal highs with bodies in the upper portions of FVGs (usually a bullish sign), but then reverses sharply downward. That's engineered buy-side liquidity (the equal highs were the bait).
- **How to use:** When you see equal highs being formed, don't assume they're traditional resistance. Ask: am I being set up to buy here and get stopped out? Watch the bodies—if they're in the upper half of inversion FVGs on the way up, it's a trap. Respect it and don't go long. Expect a reversal once the equal highs are cleared. Use this as a sign to place short entries instead.
- **Example in this video:** ES, April 21, 2026. Equal highs are engineered at 26,800+, then price rallies back up, takes those highs, but bodies print in the upper half of the inversion FVG with no continuation. This signals engineered buy-side liquidity—the rally is a trap. Price then reverses and sells off aggressively. This is exactly what ICT predicted on the livestream.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:19](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1759), [0:30:08](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1808), [0:31:00](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1860)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Engineering liquidity is not the same as simple support/resistance. It's active market making, not passive level holding. Recognize it by the deliberate pattern and the body placement in FVGs—not just the wick behavior. Bodies in upper halves of inversion FVGs + equal highs = trap in progress.

### Balanced Price Range (BPR)
- **Kind:** definition + defensive structure
- **What ICT says:** A price range that bounces between two levels (high and low) over a period of time. Unlike entry models (which get you into trades), a BPR serves a **defensive** function—it's a barrier that keeps price outside of it, protecting your stop loss (like a "bolo" or fortress wall). If price breaks through the BPR, it signals a significant market structure shift and suggests strong momentum in the opposite direction.
- **How to identify:** Chart tool: mark a high and a low that have been tested multiple times over several candles or hours. Price respects both levels, bouncing between them.
- **How to use:** Use BPR as a mental or charted reference for where your stops should sit (just outside the BPR). If the BPR fails (price breaks through it), don't despair—it's giving you information that smart money has shifted direction. Take the new PD array that's forming in that opposite direction and catch the bigger move ("catch a tiger by the tail").
- **Example in this video:** Not directly shown as a BPR in this video, but ICT references the concept when discussing how a level can act as defense. The RTH gaps themselves (when they hold multiple times) form BPR-like structures.

### Charting Strategy: Notepad vs. Annotations
- **Kind:** process / discipline
- **What ICT says:** Don't clutter your chart with every annotation. Instead, write down your key levels in a notepad: today's RTH gap high/low, yesterday's RTH gap high/low, Friday's RTH gap high/low, key daily wicks, etc. When you're looking at price, reference the notepad numbers, not the visual boxes on the chart. This builds tape-reading intuition and prevents the chart from forcing certain information into your attention at the wrong times. Eventually, you want to see price action naked (no annotations) and intuit where support/resistance is.
- **How to identify:** Set up: (1) open TradingView with a clean 1m chart (or 5m), (2) open a notepad, (3) write down your key levels based on the day's gaps and daily highs/lows.
- **How to use:** Glance at your notepad, find the current price on the chart, and ask: "Where are we relative to my key levels?" Don't look at the chart first; look at the number. As you improve, reduce the annotations on the chart. For new traders, annotations are fine to learn, but move away from them.
- **Example in this video:** ICT shows how looking at a clean chart (no annotations) builds intuition. He demonstrates this by removing all the boxes and asking the audience to read price action naked. Then he re-adds the annotations to show what was actually happening. The message: learn with annotations, but train your eyes to eventually not need them.

### Tape Reading & Naked Chart Analysis
- **Kind:** skill + mindset
- **What ICT says:** The ability to look at price action without annotations and intuit support/resistance comes from studying annotated charts first, but eventually building a mental library of how price behaves. Tape reading is asking: "Why did price turn here? What caused this hiccup? Why are there relative equal highs here? What's next?" Begin with annotations to answer these questions visually, then gradually move toward naked charts to develop the skill.
- **How to identify:** Study historical price action on annotated charts (see where FVGs, imbalances, gaps form and get used). Observe patterns: price runs, pauses at certain zones, reverses. Write observations in a journal.
- **How to use:** Use annotated charts when learning. Move to a notepad + clean chart as you improve. Eventually, glance at a naked chart and know where support/resistance should be. This skill is developed through consistent observation and practice, not by studying theory alone.
- **Example in this video:** ICT shows a naked ES 1m chart and asks the audience to identify key turning points. Then he adds annotations and explains what he was looking at (the relative equal highs, the liquidity pools, the RTH gaps). The contrast builds the mental model.

### Daily Wick as Bias / Volatility Reference
- **Kind:** filter / daily context
- **What ICT says:** On the daily chart, look at recent wicks. The midpoint of a key wick (e.g., a large wick on October 30, 2025 in this example) can be a price level that smart money uses as a "reach target" for intraday runs. If the daily chart shows a wick at a certain level, it tells you where smart money might be targeting price intraday.

- **How to use:** Use the wick midpoint as a long-term reach target. If you're planning intraday shorts, you might expect the up run to reach that wick level before reversing. This gives you a "proof of concept" that smart money is targeting a known level.

### Bodies vs. Wicks (Price Action Filter)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When evaluating whether a level is being respected or broken, focus on the **candle bodies**, not the wicks. A wick can touch resistance and retreat; that's normal. But if the **body** closes above or below a level, it shows intent. In inversion FVGs, bodies must stay below the midpoint to show continued selling pressure; if bodies enter the upper half, the down move is losing conviction. Similarly, "no candlestick bodies in the upper half" of an FVG is a bearish structure.
- **How to identify:** Draw a level (e.g., inversion FVG top). Watch the next few candles. Check: do the bodies stay below the midpoint, or do they cross above? Wicks can go above; bodies must stay below for the setup to remain valid.
- **How to use:** When managing a short position, watch the bodies. If bodies start closing in the upper half of your target zone, reduce conviction. If bodies stay in the lower half, hold and expect acceleration lower. Use this as a filter for partial exits and stop placement.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple instances: "no candlestick bodies in the upper half" of the SIBI / inversion FVG. Also, "bodies being just below the low of today's regular trading hours opening range gap low" indicates that smart money wants to dig lower (bodies respecting a threshold below a zone = bearish intent).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Three RTH gaps rule:** Only use three most recent RTH opening range gaps (today, yesterday, Friday) for intraday trading without bias.
- **Directional bias:** If daily chart shows 10+ consecutive up closes, it's reasonable to anticipate a pullback and go short. Don't "pick the top," but be ready to short below the clear structure failure (failed ATH, break below a key wick midpoint).
- **Entry confirmation:** When targeting a down move, wait for price to close a candle body at or below a key level (e.g., consequent encroachment of a wick). Wicks alone don't confirm; bodies do.
- **Partial strategy:** (1) First target = today's RTH gap high; (2) second target = yesterday's RTH gap high; (3) third target = Friday's RTH gap low. Use octant levels (divide the gap range into 8) for finer entries/exits.
- **Inversion FVG bodies rule:** When price retraces into an inversion FVG, bodies must stay at or below the midpoint to show respect. Bodies above midpoint = loss of rejection, expect continuation higher (setup weakening).
- **Engineering liquidity trigger:** When equal highs are formed and bodies are in upper halves of inversion FVGs on the way up, expect sharp reversal. Don't buy the break; prepare to short.
- **Bodies vs. wicks:** Always evaluate candle body placement, not wick extremes. A wick touching resistance is normal; bodies crossing it signals intent.
- **Twitter/notepad levels:** Post targets publicly to document real-time analysis (e.g., "26,695 minimum"). This creates accountability and builds credibility when the levels are hit.
- **Time context:** Fed speakers, news (Iran/Israel, Trump tweets), CPI/NFP data can cause "Phil" (erratic, large-candle wicks) volatility. Expect 50/50 conditions during these windows (9:45–10:15 ET during Fed commentary).
- **Equity at risk:** Demonstrated on ES with no stop losses (but acknowledges this is advanced; retail traders should use stops). Micro contracts used for lower notional risk.

## Process, risk & psychology

**Pre-market & bias setup:**
- Review daily chart for extended up closes (overbought) or recent wicks (reach targets).
- Set up notepad with three RTH gaps and one key daily wick level.
- Tweet your directional thesis and key target to document it and build accountability.
- Accept that the market can go either direction (50/50 during news); don't force it.

**During the day:**
- Watch how price interacts with your key levels (RTH gaps).
- When it reaches a level, pause and observe: does it respect it or break through?
- If it breaks through, update your next target and stay in the trade.
- Watch bodies, not wicks. Bodies in the "wrong half" of a zone = loss of conviction.

**Managing confusion & clutter:**
- Don't clutter your chart. Use a notepad to stay mentally sharp.
- Don't look at the chart and ask, "What should I do?" Look at your levels and ask, "Where are we?"
- Build your intuition by studying annotated charts, then moving to naked charts.

**Handling adversity & maintaining edge:**
- If your initial thesis fails (e.g., price breaks above your reach target and doesn't reverse), it's OK. You weren't forced to trade. ICT's rule: "If I were wrong, I wouldn't participate. It wouldn't hurt me."
- When you're right and levels are hit, you've **proven** your edge. Take it as a win and document it (tweet, video, notes).
- Dismiss critics who say "you added annotations after the fact" or "it's random luck." You've earned the right to call levels before they're hit (documented in livestreams and tweets). Stick to university-level price action and stop engaging with doubters.

**Tape reading skill development:**
- Begin with annotated charts. Study why levels matter and where smart money shows intent.
- Graduate to a notepad + naked chart combination.
- Eventually, you'll intuit support/resistance without writing anything down.
- Don't skip steps; the visual learning (annotations) is essential to building the intuition.

**Psychology & discipline:**
- Admit when you're wrong early. Don't wait for a bigger loss.
- Be thankful when your analysis pans out; it confirms your model and builds confidence.
- Never trade without a stop loss (unless you're advanced and managing risk through position sizing and mental discipline).
- Log everything: entries, targets, exits, observations. Build a journal.

## Glossary candidates

`RTH opening range gap` — The gap between the previous day's close and today's 9:30 AM ET open (for US index futures). High and low of this gap are key liquidity pools intraday.

`Consequent encroachment` — The midpoint (or lowest wick) of a gap, representing a threshold above which price losing respect signals loss of bearish intent (or bullish intent, depending on direction).

`SIBI` — Sell-side imbalance / Buy-side inefficiency; a PD array formed by an aggressive directional move that leaves unmet orders in the opposite direction.

`First utilization` — The original direction a PD array formed in. Once price trades **through** the PD array in the opposite direction, it inverts and the utilization reverses.

`Inversion` — When a PD array trades through and reverses its original utilization. An inversion FVG that was originally bearish (price ran down, leaving it) becomes bullish (acts as resistance when price retraces into it).

`Engineering liquidity` — Deliberate market maker practice of creating the appearance of false support/resistance (equal highs, for example) to trap retail traders, then reversing sharply to take their stops.

`Equal highs / Equal lows` — Two or more price levels at approximately the same price over time. Often engineered as false breakout points.

`Relative equal highs` — Two highs that are very close to each other, forming a resistance-like zone that often breaks in a trap pattern.

`PD array (Price Delivery array)` — Any market structure element that delivers price: FVGs, imbalances, inefficiencies, order blocks, balanced price ranges, etc.

`Body vs. Wick` — The candle body is the open-to-close range; the wick is the high or low that extended beyond the body. Bodies indicate intent; wicks indicate attempts (often rejected).

`Octant / Quadrant` — Dividing a gap range into 8 (octant) or 4 (quadrant) equal parts. The octant levels are price magnets where price often stops or reverses.

`Phil` — Nickname for erratic market-maker volatility and manual manipulation (spoofing wicks, large candles, news-driven moves). "Phil is online" = expect unusual candle behavior.

`Notepad trading` — Keeping your chart naked (no annotations) and writing down key levels in a notepad or notebook. This builds tape-reading intuition and prevents chart clutter from dominating your attention.
