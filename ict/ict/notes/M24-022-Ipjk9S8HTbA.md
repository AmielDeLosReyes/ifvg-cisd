## Summary
ICT live-streams NQ on Wednesday 9/4/2024 (the day after a very large down-range day that followed the Labor Day holiday) while "teaching" his son Caleb, so he explains everything slowly. The core lesson is the RTH Opening Range Gap (ORG): when NQ opens below the prior settlement (a discount ORG), ICT says ~70% of the time price trades back up to the gap's midpoint (its consequent encroachment) between 9:30 and 10:00 ET, and a New Day Opening Gap sitting in the upper half of the ORG is a natural extension target. He pairs this with the 30-minute opening range (9:30–10:00) and the "first presentation" 1-min FVG, and warns that after a large-range day (or holiday) the AM session is distorted ("fickle"), so students should sit on their hands in the morning and trade the PM session starting 1:30 ET. The second half is mindset: low expectations for 6–12 months, journal feelings not ego, never judge progress by funded-account P&L, don't feel pressure to perform on a live stream, and take one clean piece of a move rather than the whole thing.

## Concepts

### RTH Opening Range Gap (ORG) and its 70% midpoint rule  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap, "regular trading hours gap", "midgap", "half the gap", "the gap" between settlement and open)
- **Kind:** rule (statistical tendency) / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Toggle TradingView to regular trading hours (RTH) to see it: the gap runs from the previous day's closing/settlement price to the first traded price at the 9:30 open. He toggles the chart between RTH and ETH (electronic trading hours) to show it.
  - If price opens below the prior settlement it is a **discount ORG**; if it opens above it is a **premium ORG** (he only walks through the discount case in detail, so the premium side is the implied mirror).
  - The midpoint of the gap is the key level: "70% of the time" price will trade to that midpoint ("half the gap") **between 9:30 and 10:00 ET**. He says he derived the figure going back to 1987 using pre-electronic/Globex numbers, and tells students to journal it daily to verify it themselves.
  - Because the open was lower than settlement, price is more likely to trade **up** first, seeking buy-side liquidity. As a student you are not trying to call full gap closure, just the move into the midpoint.
  - 70% is not 100%: the previous day (Tue 9/3) opened lower, only a tiny wick got into the gap, never reached even a quarter of it, and the market "fell out of bed" — he uses this as the proof it is a probability, not a guarantee.
- **How to identify:** Switch to RTH; draw the box from prior RTH close to 9:30 open; mark its midpoint (50%). Note the size — on 9/4 NQ was "over 100 handles" below prior settlement, which he calls a very large discount gap. Log in your journal whether the midpoint traded between 9:30 and 10:00.
- **How to use:** Treat the midpoint as a first target (partial) for longs taken after a discount open, or as an area where a trade is "done" for the learner — "you don't care what it does after that." Combine with the first-presentation FVG as an entry and a New Day/Week Opening Gap above the midpoint as the next objective (see below).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Wed 9/4/2024. Large discount ORG (>100 handles below Tue's settlement). At the 9:30 open price makes a small drop, then rallies, creating the first 1-min FVG (shaded white). Price then trades up to the ORG midpoint inside 9:30–10:00 — ICT: "there's the 70% strike rate." The prior day (Tue 9/3) is the counterexample (gap opened lower, midpoint never tagged).
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:48](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=888) — "70% of the time between 9:30 and 10:00" the midgap is traded to · [0:31:55](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1915) — premium vs discount ORG and the midpoint · [0:32:47](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1967) — Tuesday's failure case · [0:33:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2015) — origin of the 70% figure (back to 1987)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as a "low-hanging fruit" model, not the best-of-the-best setup. Wicks may exceed levels; the probability is for trading *to* the midpoint, not holding it.

### Opening Range (9:30–10:00 ET) and "first presentation" FVG  (aliases ICT uses: first Fair Value Gap, first presentation of the fair value gap, "the real opening range")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening range is **9:30 to 10:00 ET, the first 30 minutes**. He says any other definition of opening range is "a gimmick"; this is what the algorithm references.
  - The first FVG that forms in that window is the "first presentation" FVG. Sometimes the trade sets up inside the window, sometimes after it, but you always get to study/seek that first FVG.
  - On **trending days** you only get one chance at it before price keeps going; on **non-trending days** price returns to it several times, offering a trade or a target.
  - On normal days candle **bodies stay inside** the first FVG's range (wicks may poke out). After a large-range day the bodies spill outside the FVG ("colors outside the lines") and the setup looks muddy.
- **How to identify:** From the 9:30 open, find the first 3-candle FVG (he uses the 1-min chart live); shade it; note its high, low and midpoint (CE). Track whether candle bodies respect it.
- **How to use:** Enter on the retrace into the first FVG in the direction of the gap-implied draw; partial at the ORG midpoint; extend to the New Day Opening Gap (see model below). A contrarian trader can instead fade it once it has been violated (see iFVG concept).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 9/4/2024: small drop after 9:30, rally creating the white-shaded first FVG at ~9:31; price trades through it, bodies close outside it (the distortion he highlights at 0:13:06), later comes back, hits it, then rallies again to the relative equal highs and lays a candle body over the NDOG (he teases Twitter critics who said the FVG "failed" — he expected it to be reclaimed).
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:31](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=691) — opening range defined 9:30–10:00 · [0:12:17](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=737) — trending vs non-trending days and the first FVG · [0:13:06](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=786) — body outside the FVG = post-large-range distortion · [1:01:28](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3688) — price returns to the "reclaimed" first FVG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says the FVG is "not the best PD array I have" — he leans on it because it is obvious to new students ("deer in headlights") — and that "the best ones you'll never learn" ([0:18:15](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1095)). Use of the 9:30–10:00 window as the single definition of opening range is stated as a firm rule here.

### NDOG / NWOG inside the ORG as the extension target  (aliases ICT uses: new day opening gap, new week opening gap, "Terminus" for the final target)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - If a New Day Opening Gap (or New Week Opening Gap) sits **inside the ORG and above its midpoint** on a discount-gap day, the odds improve that price keeps going from the midpoint up to that gap — "a very strong 70%."
  - It does not need to be both an NDOG and an NWOG, and you do not need the whole gap: "it could be just the low" of the gap — the first level reached is the target ("low-hanging fruit"). Do not hunt "the best of the best."
  - The model as he states it at 0:59:51: **buy the first fair value gap → partial at the midgap → Terminus at the NDOG low.**
  - After that, expect price to possibly sweep around both sides of the NDOG and reach for a full gap closure (the ORG high) — treated as a bonus, not the plan.
- **How to identify:** On RTH, draw the ORG; mark its midpoint; plot the NDOG and check whether it lies in the upper half of the ORG. NDOG on 9/4 was around 18993.75 and 18999.75 (caption unclear: "18993 and three quarters, 18,999 and three quarters").
- **How to use:** Long entry in the first FVG, take a partial at ORG midpoint, run the remainder to the NDOG low. Longs that did not take profit at the NDOG got run over fast — he stresses taking something off there.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 9/4/2024: after the rally to the ORG midpoint, price continues to the NDOG (upper quadrant of the ORG), then sells off sharply around the 10:00 ET news print (see liquidity concept) toward Tuesday's daily low. Later in the session price pushes slightly above the relative equal highs and the NDOG top — "went just a little above it" — and he leaves open a further attempt to the full gap closure.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2015) — NDOG/NWOG inside the ORG above midpoint · [0:36:47](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2207) — the whole sequence recapped as a model that "will hit, will stick" · [0:37:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2255) — only one level needed, low-hanging fruit · [0:59:51](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3591) — FVG → partial midgap → Terminus NDOG low
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Terminus" is used loosely here as the final target of the trade (he also says "wait for my partials and wait for my Terminus to be hit," [0:56:43](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3403)); he does not define it as a named model in this video.

### Post-large-range-day / post-holiday morning rule  (aliases ICT uses: "sit on your hands," "sit still," fickle morning session)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a very large range day (up or down), or the day after a holiday, **do not trade the AM session while you are learning** — price tends to be "fickle," chopping back and forth with unclear, muddy setups.
  - The higher-probability setups come in the **afternoon (PM) session, which begins at 1:30 PM ET**, and he also points to the **2:00 macro, 1:50–2:10 ET**.
  - Experienced traders can trade the morning, but only if they know what is most likely to happen; beginners who force trades "go into tilt," buying and selling back and forth.
  - He says this advice appears in the 2016/2017 mentorship content, so it is not form-fitting after the fact.
  - Context for 9/4: Tue 9/3 was a very large-range down day (post-Labor Day), closing on the low; the daily chart shows a big down-close candle. That is what made Wed AM "wonky."
  - Why: everyone saw yesterday's down day, so the late-to-market crowd wants to short at the open; that tends to engineer buy-side liquidity to be run (he adds "not always but generally").
- **How to identify:** Daily chart shows an unusually large-range candle the day before (or the day follows a market holiday). Check that price opened with a large gap.
- **How to use:** Skip or heavily limit AM trades; mark your levels; journal what happens; focus on PM 1:30 onward and the 2:00 macro.
- **Example in this video:** On Tue 9/3 he says he waited and traded the PM session (he refers to yesterday's PM trade, shown on Caleb's channel). He says that, in hindsight, a short AM entry would have been at the CE of the daily imbalance and he would have held 2–3 contracts into 1:30.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:20](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=440) — sit on your hands after large-range days · [0:13:54](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=834) — why retail shorts the open · [0:19:02](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1142) — "it's in the 2016/2017 content" · [0:27:02](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1622) — PM begins 1:30 ET, 2:00 macro 1:50–2:10 · [0:27:51](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1671) — where the AM short entry would have been
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** It is a protocol to protect learners, not a claim that no AM trade exists. The "2:00 macro 1:50–2:10" here is stated as a time window to learn the PM, not as a standalone entry model.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) of the Aug 13, 2024 daily FVG  (aliases ICT uses: midpoint, "mid gap," BISI = buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - An FVG is bounded by the low of one candle and the high of another (the gap between candles 1 and 3); its midpoint is the consequent encroachment. He describes the Aug 13 daily FVG as a BISI.
  - He draws the CE as "the middle of the range defined by this candle's low and that candle's high" and uses the same midpoint idea on the daily FVG, the ORG and the 1-min first FVG.
  - If price intends to rise from a lower level, it is better if it does **not** come back down to touch a midpoint below (which gap's midpoint is not clear from the captions).
  - He notes a small volume imbalance in the Aug 13 FVG area that "gets worked out" naturally by the candle's high.
- **How to identify:** Mark the gap between the two outer candles' wicks; add a line at 50%.
- **How to use:** Use the Aug 13 daily FVG high / CE / low as the daily-timeframe draw for the pre-open plan: price ranging between the FVG's high and CE before the open; if price rolls over and takes the opening-range low, the next draw is into that daily FVG below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-min pre-open: price ranges between the Aug 13 daily FVG high and its CE; ICT's stated plan ([0:09:45](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=585)) is to see price trade to the ORG midgap, possibly reach the upper part of the ORG, then roll over and, if it takes the low formed in the new opening range, drop into the Aug 13 FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:33](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=393) — Aug 13 daily FVG definition and midpoint · [0:10:34](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=634) — plan: midgap then maybe the Aug 13 FVG below · [0:22:08](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1328) — "here is consequent encroachment"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He explicitly says he is "not trying to push a narrative" pre-open — exploring both sides.

### Buy-side / sell-side liquidity, relative equal highs and the "news smokescreen"  (aliases ICT uses: opposing liquidity, SSL below Tuesday's low, REH)
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Tuesday (9/3) daily low and the lows formed in the new opening range are the sell-side liquidity below; relative equal highs plus the top of the NDOG are the buy-side liquidity above ("that's the liquidity" — take a screenshot).
  - Stops for traders who shorted the open sit above; the market runs them first, then forms the first FVG.
  - He says that **high or medium-impact news** on the economic calendar is used as a "smoke screen" so a drop looks like selling pressure. The 10:00 reaction after NDOG was an example: longs who stayed in past the NDOG were hit quickly and price went down to Tuesday's daily low, where sell-side liquidity rested.
  - After a large down day, "street money" (retail) wants to repeat yesterday's profitable direction (short). Smart money buys into the lows and rallies to the NDOG in the upper quadrant of the ORG. (His narration of who is being drawn in is loose; paraphrase with care.)
- **How to identify:** Mark prior day low/high, REH/REL, and the NDOG edges; note scheduled news times.
- **How to use:** Expect price to run the nearest obvious liquidity first and reverse into the PD array; avoid holding longs through a stop-run at the target level without a partial.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 9/4/2024: price drops/runs lows first, forms first FVG, rallies to ORG midpoint and NDOG, reverses sharply around 10:00 ET into the Tuesday daily low, later returns and pokes through the REH/NDOG top.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:12](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2112) — "invisible barrier" at the NDOG · [0:36:00](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2160) — news as smokescreen · [0:43:58](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2638) — REH + top of NDOG as the liquidity · [0:47:07](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2827) — street money vs smart money positioning
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The event is not named (he does not say which 10:00 release); do not assume one.

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) contrarian short  (aliases ICT uses: inversion fair value gap, reclaimed fair value gap)
- **Kind:** entry model (contrarian alternative) / example
- **What ICT says:** A trader who is bearish can fade the move by shorting an **inversion FVG** — "extend it over here, you can see the body respecting it" — and targeting the sell-side liquidity he had outlined. He says several of his students took exactly this trade, as well as the long from the first FVG to the 70% midpoint.
- **How to identify:** An FVG that price has closed through, which then acts as resistance on retest (bodies respect it from the other side).
- **How to use:** Short on the retest of the inverted gap; target the sell-side below (the Tuesday low / opening-range low).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-min, after the first FVG was violated, the gap flips to resistance; price retests it and falls toward the sell-side he outlined. Later he calls the original first FVG a "reclaimed" FVG as price comes back, hits it, and rallies again to the REH.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:39](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3639) — short at an iFVG, target sell side · [1:01:28](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3688) — "reclaimed fair value gap" (caption unclear on exact term)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He offers this only as a contrarian option while his preferred read was the long to the midgap/NDOG. No stop or size is given.

### Entries, partials and "one setup is enough"  (aliases ICT uses: low-hanging fruit, early-onset maturity)
- **Kind:** rule (execution mindset)
- **What ICT says:** All you need is one repeatable piece — e.g. buy the FVG, get to the halfway point of the gap — "and you don't care what it does after that." Beginners want the full swing and expect to capture the whole move; that is unrealistic. If you can only frame a trade one way and you are in it, you have no anxiety, wait for partials/Terminus, and accept being stopped out in profit on a trail.
- **How to use:** As he frames it: pick one clear objective (midpoint, NDOG low), take partials there, trail any remainder with a stop, and do not second-guess a trade framed in one way.
- **Example in this video:** The long from the first FVG to the ORG midpoint was his "model"; the NDOG low is the extension.
- **Timestamps:** [0:49:30](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2970) — one setup to the midpoint · [0:56:43](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3403) — partials, Terminus, trailing stop
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** No stop-loss rule, size or R multiple is stated in this video.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET (first 30 min). Other definitions are "gimmicks."
- RTH ORG = prior settlement to 9:30 open; midpoint traded to ~70% of the time between 9:30 and 10:00 ET (stat said to go back to 1987 on Globex data); discount gap implies initial move up.
- On 9/4 the NQ gap was over 100 handles below prior settlement; 70% ≠ 100% (Tue 9/3 failed to reach even 25% of the gap).
- NDOG/NWOG above the ORG midpoint strengthens the case for a run to it; only the near edge (the low) is needed.
- Trending days: one shot at the first FVG; non-trending days: multiple returns to it.
- Normal days: bodies respect the first FVG; after a large-range day bodies spill out and the AM is distorted.
- After a large-range or holiday day, do not trade the AM while learning; PM session begins 1:30 PM ET; 2:00 macro window 1:50–2:10 ET.
- 9/4 reference levels: NDOG about 18993.75–18999.75 (caption unclear); "19,100.5" mentioned at 1:13:28 as a draw level from the previous day's markup (caption unclear).
- Use RTH view in TradingView to get the opening range gap; keep screenshots/labels in a journal.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Low expectations for the first 6–12 months ([0:20:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1235)): celebrate each small milestone in your journal, not on social media; share with a fellow student, not a spouse/partner (money strains relationships); no competition with other students.
- Journal and screenshot recurring events (e.g., whether the midgap traded 9:30–10:00) so you can verify the 70% yourself ([0:15:49](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=949), [0:24:37](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1477)). Do not try to learn by pressing the button or demo-trading for results.
- Do not measure progress by funded-account or real-money results; that weights outcome too heavily and delays real learning ([0:51:56](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3116)). Track understanding instead.
- Journal your emotions (euphoria) but not ego statements ("I'm the goat") — ego blinds you to mistakes ([0:53:32](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3212), [0:54:20](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3260)).
- Over-leverage and over-trading are what hurt premature students ([0:19:49](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1189)).
- Live streaming creates pressure to perform; he does not take a trade unless it fits high-probability criteria and will not apologise to an audience for having no setup ([0:55:55](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3355)).
- Trading the PM after a missed AM is normal; the setups return daily ([0:57:31](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3451)).
- Final exercise: strip the chart to price only, screenshot it, and study it ([1:12:39](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=4359)).
- Much of the remaining runtime (about 0:43–0:47, 1:02–1:12) is defence of the method against critics, Goldman-style "you can't time the market," and personal history; no additional trading rules there.

## Glossary candidates
`ORG` — RTH opening range gap: prior settlement to 9:30 open; premium if above, discount if below.
`Midgap` — the 50% / CE of the ORG; ~70% of the time traded to between 9:30 and 10:00.
`Opening range` — 9:30–10:00 ET only, per ICT.
`First presentation FVG` — first FVG formed in the opening range.
`Terminus` — final target of the trade (loose use here; NDOG low).
`Street money / dumb money` — retail participants chasing yesterday's direction.
`Handles` — NQ points (e.g., "100 handles").
`Large range day` — day with an unusually big range; the next AM is treated as unreliable.
`Full gap closure` — price reaching the far edge of the ORG (settlement).
`Reclaimed FVG` — a violated FVG that price returns to and uses again (caption unclear).
`Low-hanging fruit` — take the nearest, easiest level rather than "the best of the best."
