## Concepts

### News drivers: trade the price reaction, never the data  (aliases: "news release tape reading", "smoke screen")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The numbers in high/medium impact reports are "already baked in"; trying to infer intraday volatility from what the data "fundamentally means" is useless. Markets often do the opposite of what data suggests; he learned this in the 1990s.
  - "Price is going to tell me everything I need to know, and if I'm wrong, price will tell me." Do not put your opinion on what the data suggests.
  - News events at 8:30 are a "smoke screen": sometimes they help run price into a layer of liquidity he has already outlined; other times they drop price to make everyone think sentiment is bearish and then at the 9:30 open they make the run up. Either way, watch what price does at 8:30 and then judge the open.
  - With a single report at 8:30, the first run is usually the Judas swing. With MULTIPLE reports at 8:30 (as today), the initial rally or decline is staggered ("feels like it's done, it's not"); give it a couple of seconds, there may be another impulse, and give it a chance to take both sides of the marketplace.
  - Normally he likes to hold a position before the 9:30 open on a high/medium impact news day, but today there was nothing he would have entered on, so he sat still.
- **How to use:** On a news day, outline liquidity pools beforehand, observe the 8:30 delivery, mark the range/volume imbalances created, and decide on the plan for 9:30 from that.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, 17 Oct 2024. The 8:30 reports "didn't do very much"; price stayed in a range created at 8:30 (this low not taken, this high not broken) with wicks and volume imbalances; "nothing in here that I would want to be buying."

### Election-year / presidential-year bullish tendency (seasonal context)
- **Kind:** rule (bias filter)
- **What ICT says:** In an election (presidential) year he says it is very difficult to short the market and hold; "they keep sending it higher and it's all artificial ... fluffing up." He says he has repeated this since the start of the 2024 mentorship and that it is a recurring presidential-year phenomenon, not unique to 2024. Shorts can be taken intraday but it is hard to hold them. Therefore "focusing on the buy side is always going to be my first go-to." He also says it is easy to pick bottoms in bull markets but very hard to pick tops, and his goal is to avoid the biggest mistakes, which are trying to pick tops and bottoms.
- **How to use:** Default to buy-side draws on liquidity; do not try to pick the top; if price is going higher "it can run without me."

### Draw on liquidity: nested relative equal highs and prior-day high as targets
- **Kind:** example | rule
- **What ICT says:** On the daily chart there is sell-side liquidity "still building underneath" and buy side above, including Tuesday 15 Oct's daily high. On the 60-minute chart the area is "too clean" (begging to be run). He anticipates a run up to the "640s at the minimum," then Tuesday's high (caption: 20,659), then a higher high (caption: ~680) — digits are caption-derived. On the 15-minute chart he points to "two nested relative equal highs" (a high, then a higher-but-nearly-equal high, then a high above both): when the targets are that obvious, "it's easy to just side with that as the initial draw until price proves it's not interested," which he reads at 8:30.
- **How to use:** Treat obvious relative equal highs as the initial draw. Ignore nearer minor buy-side (the "first pass up here I would completely ignore that") and focus on the two relative equal highs. If price runs through them it can visit the next high; alternatively it can only reach the consequent encroachment of the range between the relative equal highs and the high above (about halfway) and then roll over to take relative equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15m, 17 Oct: price consolidates after 8:30; ICT wants an up move to the CE of the relative-equal-highs-to-next-high range; wicks do reach that CE but candle bodies stop below it ("bodies respecting the level while the wicks are tossed above it"), and then price snaps down. He wanted it to come back down, disrupt the lows, and work down into a volume imbalance, and said he would NOT be interested in being long yet.

### Volume Imbalance — how to draw it when there are indecisive candles (new refinement)
- **Kind:** refinement (drawing rule)
- **What ICT says:** When a fair value gap is accompanied by two candles with small bodies/indecisive (open ≈ close) next to it, the naive way to frame the inefficiency is not accurate enough. The correct framing: use the LOWEST open/close (the lowest body price) among those candles — here the same price as the low of the volume imbalance — and extend the zone from there across the whole group so it covers the gap and the volume imbalances entirely. "It's not technically wrong but it's not as accurate as it needs to be." Then you can use the consequent encroachment of that zone. Reason: with indecisive candles there are many reasons for price to drop into the volume imbalances, and it would otherwise overshoot the standard FVG you assumed would be used.
- **How to identify:** An FVG sitting next to doji-like/indecisive candles; take the extreme body price (lowest open/close) as the boundary.
- **How to use:** Draw from the lowest body price, then use CE of the whole inefficiency as the reference.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m, ~8:30 range: below the lows there is an FVG plus two small-body candles; he draws the zone from the lowest open/close (same as the low of the volume imbalance) up over the cluster.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:26](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1226) — "something you've never learned about"; [0:21:13](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1273) — use lowest body, then CE.
- **Nuance:** Presented as new material ("something you've never learned about").

### Volume Imbalance — behavior, the "paint roller" analogy, and when it becomes a Balanced Price Range  (aliases: "most flexible PD array")
- **Kind:** definition | rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The volume imbalance is "the most flexible PD array" — it is normal for price to go through it back and forth. Just because it is there does not mean it stops price to the tick. You cannot demand the precision you expect from a bullish FVG's CE or high.
  - First expectation: price trades THROUGH it. A body gets laid over it.
  - How it gains meaning: the imbalance is created between two candle bodies (an up candle's close and the next candle's open). If an up-closed candle goes through that range (buy-side delivery) and later a down-closed candle lays a body over the same range (sell-side delivery), then both sides have delivered and it is "efficiently delivered." He then treats it as a (small) Balanced Price Range, and the imbalance becomes more meaningful. He says he does not hang much on tiny, insignificant ones.
  - Paint roller analogy: roll paint onto a wall; the first foot delivers ample paint, then pockets appear as paint runs out; you roll back over to fill the pockets. Price does the same inside a volume imbalance — delivers one direction first, then returns to deliver the other side.
  - After both sides have delivered and price leaves it, the balanced price range acts as a DISCOUNT array (support) if price returns to it from above; as a premium array if the reverse. If long, you can roll your stop loss to just below that volume imbalance, because "it's done its work in framing out a balanced price range."
  - Contrast: a volume imbalance with NO body ever laid over it — only wicks — behaves like a breakaway gap: price traverses it with a wick and rips away in the same candle; this is not the same as being delivered on both sides. "Two different dynamics."
  - It is not "liquidity" in itself; the buy-side/sell-side delivery means the market has been offered on the up and the down, not that stops are resting there.
  - Practical: annotate the volume imbalances inside the 8:30 delivery range, extend them to the right, and keep referring to them while price is inside that range. One example is not enough; collect many instances in your charts.
  - Management: he often uses volume imbalances to start positioning (as in London); then when time (a macro) kicks in and price displaces and "spools" toward the target, he can use the volume imbalance again as a new entry or as a way to manage the stop. It needs management ("a little bit of management is required").
  - He criticizes people who teach volume imbalances improperly or monetize his material without fully understanding it.
- **How to identify:** (1) Find the gap between two candles' bodies with overlapping wicks; (2) check whether later candles have laid bodies over it from above (sell-side) and below/through it (buy-side); (3) both delivered then left behind = BPR; wicks only = breakaway-gap-like.
- **How to use:** Don't demand a tick-precise reaction; let it trade through then come back; use a delivered one as support/discount and trail your stop beneath it; use as an entry when the macro time kicks in.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/5m, 8:30–9:00 ET range 17 Oct 2024: a bullish body was buried in one volume imbalance (buy-side delivery), later a down-close candle laid over it (sell-side delivery) — he calls it efficiently delivered/BPR (small); price then went back above it, and he wanted price not to dip back below. A second volume imbalance had only wicks (a single candle traversed it down and back up) — treated like a breakaway gap, not delivered.
- **Timestamps:** [0:34:26](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2066) — extend 8:30 volume imbalances; "most flexible PD array"; [0:35:15](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2115) — bodies laid over it; [0:37:34](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2254) — paint roller analogy; [0:40:49](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2449) — BPR on discount basis, roll stop below; [0:41:36](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2496) — wick-only imbalance = breakaway gap.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Refines the earlier statement (repeated in the Oct 7 video) that volume imbalances are the least rigid; adds a criterion for when they gain weight (both-side body delivery) and a stop-management use.

### Staying still when the algorithm is "held in a range" / manual intervention
- **Kind:** rule (execution/psychology)
- **What ICT says:** When price holds a range (low not taken, high not broken, many wicks, volume imbalances, no cleanness) it is a holding pattern: the "algorithm" is not doing anything; "the hands in there" are stirring the pot. Sit still. You are competing against an entity that can take both sides of the market and still go nowhere, and by entering you relinquish your "power of stillness" and are at the mercy of the outcome. This is manual intervention (repricing) by the market maker rather than the algorithm running on script. Observation/tape reading is what you do before you even use a demo/paper account: study what you feel and how you perceive price, and look for signatures that the algorithm is on script vs. interfered with.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:31](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1771) — holding pattern; [0:30:21](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1821) — power of stillness; [0:31:11](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1871) — tape reading precedes demo trading.
- **Nuance:** If price takes off without you, "it left without me; it's fine ... I'll catch the next bus stop."

## Glossary candidates
`Smoke screen` — news release used as cover to run or reverse price before the real move.
`Nested relative equal highs` — two near-equal highs where a third higher high sits above; an obvious buy-side draw.
`Volume imbalance (VI)` — gap between candle bodies where wicks overlap; the "most flexible" PD array.
`Efficiently delivered` — a volume imbalance that has had bodies laid over it from both buy and sell side, becoming a (small) BPR.
`Paint roller analogy` — price delivers one side first, then returns to fill the "pockets" on the other side.
`Breakaway gap (wick-only VI)` — volume imbalance traversed by wicks only, no body laid over it.
`Horseshoes analogy` — orders tossed near a level (wicks) while bodies show real acceptance.
`Propulsion block` — an order block that price trades back into repeatedly during a run (taught in 2016 mentorship Month 4).
`Held in a range / manual intervention` — market maker repricing instead of algorithm on script; stand aside.
`Opening range gap 50%` — mid gap between RTH settlement and 9:30 open; quoted as ~70% likely to trade in first 30 minutes.
