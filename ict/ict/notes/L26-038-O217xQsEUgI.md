## Summary
ICT goes long NQ live on a paper account on a messy Monday ahead of CPI (Wednesday) and PPI (Thursday). The idea: a bullish inversion FVG, qualified twice, should send price back up to the New York midnight opening price (29,878.25) and, in the best case, to Monday London's relative equal highs. He narrates every order: the initial 4 contracts and stop at a candle low, adds on the high of the inversion FVG, stop moves in steps that cut risk and then lock in cost, a 5-contract partial just below the midnight open, and a final protective stop on 2 contracts that is deliberately used as a "partial on a retracement". Along the way he states precise grading rules for wicks, bullish FVGs that should be left partly open, and what price must do next. The recurring theme is that you predict and manage, rather than react or demand that a trade reach the target.

## Concepts

### Inversion FVG qualification and long entries on its high  (aliases: "kiss" the high, "qualified two times")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - The setup begins with a bearish FVG that price closes above, which turns it into an inversion FVG when the market is bullish. He wants it "qualified" before using it for entries. Qualification here was twice: a close above it, then price trades back down through it, and a candle that opens, trades down, comes back up and closes around the high of the gap. That is a second qualification and confirmation. Only after those does he anticipate using it for entries.
  - Entries are made inside the gap, not just at the close: the first buy is at the low end of the iFVG, and he adds each time price comes back and "kisses" the high of the inversion FVG. He adds on the upper quadrant and at the deep end of the gap.
  - Price did not even reach the CE of the inversion FVG (the caption puts the CE at 29,796.25; unclear whether this is the CE or the low of the pullback), which he treats as strength.
  - His average across the adds ended up essentially at the high of the inversion FVG.
- **How to identify:** bearish FVG closed above (iFVG), then a second test that holds on a closing basis; the 9:30 open was in close proximity to its high.
- **How to use:** buy at the iFVG low end, add at its high and on retests; stop under its low.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday morning pre-market through 9:30 ET: price opened close to the high of the iFVG, dipped lower and trapped breakout shorts at the low (smart money buys those sell stops), then rallied back above it. The first long was 4 contracts, stop at the candle low (plus or minus a tick or two), planning to add 2 more when price came back to the high of the gap. Later total sizing is stated as 12 contracts (see below).

### Stop management in steps (cut risk, cover costs)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Initial stop: the candle's low minus a tick or two.
  - After adding, the stop moves to just below the midpoint of the iFVG.
  - Once the average entry sits at the iFVG high, raise the stop to the low of the iFVG minus a tick, "reduced some of the risk".
  - When a candle closes above a key level (a wick's high, the iFVG), roll the stop to cover costs (breakeven). He does this again after a close above the earlier area.
  - After a partial, increase "paycheck" by lifting the stop to just below a wick high.
  - Final structure: the stop on the remaining position sits just below a wick, since a close below it leaves the swing low as the last defense.
- **How to use:** each step is tied to a price structure (candle low, iFVG midpoint, iFVG low, wick low), not to a fixed number of ticks.

### Grading a wick: no bodies buried below its CE  (aliases: "wick consequent encroachment", "PD array number two")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When long, grade a wick below price by its CE. He does not want bodies buried south of the CE. A body can dip, but then the next candle must show immediate willingness to go higher, preferably a close above.
  - Entry orders can sit at the wick's CE; a lower wick may be "flirted with", but if you are on side price should not take out that low.
  - A body closing above the CE of a wick is bullish. A close above the open of the candle that made the wick is "extremely positive" (here about 29,873.75, which he rounds to 874).
  - He calls this wick "PD array number two". If price closes below it, the last defense is the swing low, which is why the stop sits just under the wick.
  - A close below a small bullish FVG but above in the upper half of the wick is a mixed signal; "we are balancing several things".
- **How to use:** use the wick CE as the line in the sand for stop and for add decisions; watch the next one or two candles.

### Bullish FVG should be left partly open (breakaway / measuring gap)
- **Kind:** refinement (restates earlier teaching)
- **What ICT says:** in a bullish run, a BISI (and a volume imbalance inside it) that price does not fully fill is "one of the strongest signatures" that price still wants to go higher. The ideal is that price leaves a portion open or does not trade into it at all; then it acts like a breakaway or measuring gap anchored to the start of the run. If price fills it completely it is not necessarily bad when still low in the run, but it makes it harder for price to prove it wants to keep going. Tether yourself to the idea that order flow is bullish and look for high-probability entries.
- **Example in this video:** a small BISI left a portion open on the run to the midnight open; later a BISI with a volume imbalance at its high was being tested, and he wanted a close above half of its wick (the CE).
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:43](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=583), [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=822), [0:14:28](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=868), [0:17:44](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=1064)

### Midnight opening price as a magnet and session bias
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** the New York midnight opening price (here 29,878.25, the open of the midnight candle on Monday) is "like a magnet" and "a very strong draw on liquidity". It need not be the daily bias; it can be a session bias. The target for the long was to get to and through it; the best case was Monday's London session relative equal highs (buy side). He took 5 contracts off just below it. The midnight open then offered "a whole lot of initial resistance": price spent time above it, dropped, and was not able to hold above the opening price of the wick candle.
- **How to use:** use it as the first take-profit area for the partial; expect resistance; don't rely on it as an extension.

### Partial profits: 5 of 12, then a protective stop on 2 of 7 as a "partial on a retracement"
- **Kind:** rule / trade management
- **What ICT says:**
  - He took 5 contracts off just below the midnight open. He says this is a bit less than half the position (position of 12 contracts; the numbers in the captions vary, with "seven" meaning the 7 contracts still held). The profit banked from that is described as a hypothetical $4,000 plus, so "even if it all comes back and stops me out, it's a win. I had a free look."
  - After that he had 7 net long. He placed a stop-loss for 2 of the 7 below a low, so that if price drops below, it sells 2 as a partial. He says that is not ideal (the ideal partial is a limit above market while bullish) but it is better than a full stop-out on all contracts, and it lessens the psychological load of needing to be right.
  - He also set a plan to take 2 off just above a minor buy-side liquidity pool at about 29,920 (caption "29,009 ... call it 920").
  - After the 2-contract stop filled, the stop on the other 5 was the next line. If a close below the gap follows ("it also gives me more intel"), the rest could go all the way down; he lets it play out to show it.
- **How to use:** once the first partial is banked, use a stop on a portion at the first structural failure so you are paid on both outcomes; keep the final stop under the key wick/swing low.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Midnight opening price on the day: 29,878.25 (the open of the midnight candle).
- First entry: 4 contracts, stop at the candle low plus or minus 1–2 ticks, plan to add 2.
- Stop steps: candle low → below midpoint of iFVG → iFVG low minus a tick → roll to cost after a close above → below the wick for the remainder.
- Position: 12 contracts at most (as stated), 5 off just below the midnight open, 7 held, 2 on a protective stop, 5 left.
- Minor buy side at about 29,920 (caption unclear); iFVG formed at 7:29 a.m.
- 9:30 open near the iFVG high; breakout shorts were trapped.
- iFVG is qualified twice before it is used.
- Wick CE: bodies must not be buried below it; a close above the candle open (about 29,873.75) is very positive.
- A bullish gap left partly open is a strong bullish signature.
- No market replay: live feed, hard right edge, with the countdown to candle close visible; no replay controls.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Predict, do not just react: "do not listen to people that say don't try to predict, learn to react" (retail is trained to react); he teaches anticipation (0:18:32).
- Leave empty space on the right side of the chart so you can form an idea of what price could do and still be fine with it going higher or lower than expected (0:04:05).
- Learn why a setup should work and when it will be problematic; he says viewers who only want quick answers resist longer videos (0:18:32).
- Using a stop on part of the position as a partial reduces the need to be right, and "subdues the aching of please go to my target or please don't stop me out" (0:23:15–0:24:03).
- You will not get the best of the best; build rules, protocols and procedures for decisions based on predicting what is likely, so you do not panic (0:24:03–0:24:50).
- If you do not let the trades pay you by any means possible, "you're not trading, you're gambling": that insists your will be done in the market (0:25:37–0:26:24).
- A "free look" after the first partial: once the first partial is banked, it does not matter whether the last portion is stopped out (0:10:31).
- He trades on a paper account for teaching; caption says he is "licensed to give trade advice" but avoids litigation by teaching price action (caption unclear whether it is "not licensed") (0:05:42).
- He criticizes teaching through market replay: if you understand what you teach you can do it on a live feed (0:08:55–0:09:43).

## Glossary candidates
`Kiss` — price returns to touch the high of an inversion FVG (or similar boundary) so he can add.
`Qualified (iFVG)` — an inversion FVG that has been closed above and then retested and held on a closing basis, twice, before being used for entries.
`PD array number two` — the next defensive PD array (here a wick) after the first array fails.
`Hard right edge` — trading with no chart history beyond the live candle, no replay.
`Free look` — banked partial profit that makes the remainder a no-loss trade.
`Breakaway / measuring gap` — a bullish gap left open that anchors the run's start and signals continuation.
`Overnight swing highs` — relative equal highs from the London session used as the buy-side target.
