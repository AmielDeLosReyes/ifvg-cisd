## Summary
ICT reviews a live NQ (Sept contract, 1-minute) long he actually took on Jackson Hole day one, explaining the daily-chart logic that made him look for longs (a daily BISI graded in octants, a suspension-block SIBI above, a failure of price to reach a lower objective), why the day was a likely seek-and-destroy, exactly which wick midpoint, iFVG and equilibrium rules he was watching, and how he managed and pyramided the position. The most transferable pieces are: (1) the pyramiding rule (add longs only at or below equilibrium of the range from the low to the intended target, shorts at or above), (2) wick-midpoint behavior as the confirmation of whether an array is "on side", (3) how a daily-chart inversion FVG/suspension block produces a clean short-term-bullish/long-term-bearish rejection, and (4) a hindsight stop-placement lesson (stop belongs at the open of the order-block candle, not below the change in state of delivery). He also explains why he will not trade live "so you can copy", and why futures are better for tight stops than forex.

## Concepts

### Daily BISI graded in octants (upper octant 29,451) and "following the leftmost anchor down"  (aliases: buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency, gray box, octant, quadrant)
- **Kind:** refinement (re-application)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The gray box on his intraday charts is the daily BISI, graded from one candle's close up to the low of the candle that created it, including the volume imbalance at the low. "Entirety of the range" = the gray box.
  - Levels inside a graded array, from the top: the high (where the gray turns white), upper octant, upper quadrant, then the first octant above consequent encroachment, CE/midpoint (blue), then octant, lower quadrant, octant, and the lowest percentile at the bottom. The uppermost octant is 0.125 = 29,451 here.
  - Price dropped into the gray area at the high and stopped at the upper octant 29,451 at the first drop of the day: "It's not random. It's bullish and it's going to use that level." The bodies did not close at or below it, which marks it as a key level.
  - Tracking tip: to read any anchored fib, find the leftmost anchor point and look straight down; all the levels line up that way.
  - In the afternoon, price traded back into the gray area near its high with a lot of energy and reacted again ("daily PDA, it will have a reaction, it's tradable").
- **How to use:** upper octant is the first line you come to; if price drops into the array but bodies do not close beyond that line, the level is respected and favors longs from it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Thursday Aug 27, 9:30 open: price opened, traded down to take sell-side, rallied to take buy-side (leaving a slightly lower buy-side high), then dropped into the gray box and stopped at 29,451 (upper octant). From there it rallied, took the shorts' stops and the highs, and ran into the inefficiency above.

### Suspension block (SIBI with volume imbalance at both ends); BISI vs suspension block  (aliases: "cibby" = SIBI in captions)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - A suspension block is a SIBI that has a volume imbalance at its high AND a volume imbalance at its low. The SIBI above price here was one.
  - The daily BISI he uses (gray box) has a volume imbalance only at the low, so it is not a suspension block: "just simple buy side of balance sell side efficiency".
  - Price trading up into the volume imbalance at the SIBI's high, then into the CE of a wick, then through to the gap above, was the sequence of "orders of business" he laid out for longs.
- **How to use:** the sequence of levels (volume imbalance, wick CE, gap, old high) is the ordered list of draws on liquidity above.

### Buying below a low (sell-side raid) with minimum criteria and wick-midpoint confirmation  (aliases: false break below a low, buying sell stops, "turtle soup" logic)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Minimum criterion for buying sell-side liquidity: price must trade below the low. "Anything at that price or lower you're buying where smart money buys", buying the breakout sellers' stops ("I'm standing in front of them saying you're wrong").
  - His planned path: from the low (relative equal lows) down toward last Friday Aug 21's first presented FVG (the first FVG after 9:30 ET). He watched the candle going down and wanted a close below the midpoint of the range between the low and the high of that first presented FVG. If it closed below that midpoint he would wait for price to touch the high of that gap and buy there. It could not close below the midpoint, and neither could the next candle, so he bought earlier: "I can't hold any longer, I've got to get in now because once it starts going above here, it's going to take off".
  - He verbalizes his concern in real time. If a low is pierced while he is talking about going long, he is treating it as a false break; and "the middle of that wick" is where he wants price to get above.
  - Once price rips back above the low quickly, he needs to see a close above the CE of the wick, then a close above an inverted gap (see below).
- **How to identify:** a swing/relative equal low being traded through; a candle that cannot close below the 50% of the range to the discount array below; fast reclaim of the low.
- **How to use:** buy below the low; first target a nearby buy-side high (limit order one tick under the high to be sure of a fill); extend the terminus to the old high to the left if the run is strong.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m Thu Aug 27 (approximately the 9:50–10:07 ET window; the transcript does not state the fill time): long below the relative equal lows ahead of Aug 21's FPFVG; first target the nearby high (4 contracts off just above it); terminus the old high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:36](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=696) plan: low then Friday's first presented FVG · [0:14:50](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=890) why he did not wait · [0:15:38](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=938) fill below the low · [0:16:26](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=986) first target
- **Nuance / caveats:** He did not draw the midpoint line live ("my experience says I can eyeball it").

### Wick consequent encroachment and "upper half of the wick supports price"  (aliases: wick CE, mile marker, bodies stay in the upper half)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price is above a wick and the market is bullish, the upper half of the wick should support price: the bodies should stay in that upper half. He highlights that upper half in blue.
  - Bullish: bodies in the upper half and not touching CE, even if wicks touch or come within one tick. Bearish mirror: in a bearish market the lower half of a key wick or array should cap price; bodies cannot get to or above CE.
  - On a candle that opened right below the middle of the upper half he was nervous, but trusted that a probe lower would be a wick and "not likely to touch the inversion fair value".
  - A candle that opened and wicked into the upper half and then responded immediately to the upside, coming within one tick of his stop, meant "we're done", the wick now acts as a barrier.
  - He copies the same measurement down onto another candle's open (anchoring the half-wick measure at that candle's open down to 523.75 captioned) to show the logic repeats at each scale; price respected the upper half of each ("same thing here"). He then expects a big up-close candle, "a strong large green close".
  - Minor buy-side relative equal highs plus bodies in the upper half and no touch of CE = bullish momentum signal.
  - "Event horizon" (as he used it here): the midpoint between the high of the iFVG and the middle of the wick; no bodies should be put down there if longs are on side.
- **How to use:** continually measure wicks; use their CE as a checkpoint rather than a hard stop; if the CE is not violated by a body the long is still "on side".
- **Example in this video:** the 10:07 ET candle opened, wicked down to NQ 29,502.00 (his stop was at 29,501.75, one tick away), then rallied.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:50](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1610) upper half supports price · [0:27:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1660) the close below the middle made him nervous · [0:28:30](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1710) event horizon midpoint · [0:34:05](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2045) body stayed inside, "good, that's what I want to see" · [0:34:53](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2093) wick becomes a barrier
- **Nuance / caveats:** His use of "event horizon" here (midpoint between an iFVG high and a wick CE, a no-body zone) differs from [[L26-019]] (halfway to the target, a partial level). It also says this wick lesson is "just an intro to one full chapter".

### Inversion FVG as confirmation after a close; entry re-tests  (aliases: iFVG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**

  - Afternoon: an overtaken SIBI plus a market structure shift above gives "a strong probability" of becoming an iFVG when price drops back in; he extends it right and the buy there rips to the CE of the green gap.

### Pyramiding rule: add only at or below equilibrium (longs), at or above (shorts)  (aliases: scaling in, equilibrium rule)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - "I teach my pyramiding needs to be if I'm long it needs to be at consequent encroachment or equilibrium of the range that you're trading in or lower. If I'm shorting I need to be shorting at equilibrium or higher."
  - After price breaches equilibrium, the halfway point of whatever run you expect to reach the target, you are "not permitted" to add or pyramid: "just a rule of thumb and it serves me well".
  - The range widens every time price takes stops: after the stops are taken, the range becomes the new low to the new high/target. Equilibrium is measured from the entry area's low to where you think the move completes (terminus), not just to the existing high.
  - Example: his second entry (four contracts) was above equilibrium of the range between the original low and old high (blue line 29,514.50), so by a strict reading it did not fit. But he had already planned a target above that high, and re-measured the range from the low to his actual intended target (eyeballed higher); the blue line then moved higher and the entry fit. He answers the "you're form fitting" accusation by saying he has taught this behind a paywall and the live commentary shows he thought it on the fly.
- **How to use:** before adding, state the range (low to terminus), mark 50%, and ask whether the add is on the cheap side of it.
- **Example in this video:** 6 contracts long first, then +4 pyramided in discount (total 10); 4 taken off just above the first high; the remaining 6 meant for terminus.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:01](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1321) rule stated · [0:22:48](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1368) range widens after stops · [0:23:39](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1419) equilibrium 29,514.50 · [0:30:58](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1858) re-measuring to the target · [0:41:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2500) contract counts

### Trade management on the example: partials, terminus and the hindsight stop lesson  (aliases: terminus, runner, order block stop)
- **Kind:** management / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Plan: 6 contracts long, add 4 at discount (10 total); 4 off at the first high (limit one tick below the high); of the 6 left, take 5 off at the next high (the high of the green imbalance) and leave one as a runner for the old high. The original terminus was the first high; it was moved higher as price showed strength.
  - Price reached very near the target, then came back and stopped him out.
  - Hindsight: the stop should have been at the open of the candle (the last two up-close candles act as an order block) rather than below it. "I don't want to be holding a stop below change in state of delivery." Price using the green inefficiency as a draw should not overcome that candle's open; the two candles' range becomes an order block and the CE of the green inefficiency protects price from going higher.
  - Price then returned to the same gap: "how's this support and resistance?", unfinished business above.
  - "Cork" observation: a flat block of price slightly sloping up (opposite of a bull flag, which slopes down) is like a champagne cork; pressure builds, then it pops higher ("a very slow bleed before it got to it").
  - Leave the gap partly unfilled: he wants the upper half of the inefficiency to stay open on the way to target. When price is near equilibrium or the high end of the run and close to the target, gaps rarely fill before it reaches the target because it is "in a hurry to get to the liquidity".
  - If stopped out, he says he would have re-entered immediately with the full position and a stop below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:26](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=986) limit one tick below the high · [0:41:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2500) 6+4 contracts, 4 off, 5 of 6 planned · [0:43:14](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2594) stop should have been at the candle's open · [0:44:52](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2692) cork · [0:45:42](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2742) gaps rarely fill before target

### Futures vs forex for tight stops
- **Kind:** rule / execution
- **What ICT says:** In futures every participant sees the same low and high (e.g., 29,502.00 on the Sept NQ at 10:07 ET), the broker is only a facilitator and cannot widen the spread; so tight stops are viable in futures. In forex each broker has its own highs/lows and its own pool of liquidity and can widen the spread to take your stop. "A gentleman's market is the futures market. You're a cowboy, you trade forex." Manipulation exists in futures too.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:39](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1179) · [0:20:26](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1226) · [0:29:18](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1758)

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Pyramid longs at or below equilibrium of the range (low to terminus); shorts at or above; after price passes equilibrium no more adds.
- Minimum criterion for buying a sell-side raid: price trades below the low.
- Wick rule: bullish = bodies stay in the upper half of the wick; do not let a body close below the wick's midpoint.
- iFVG confirmed when a candle closes above the gap; a retest of the gap should hold.
- Place the stop at the open of the order-block candle, not below the change in state of delivery (hindsight lesson).
- Entry and exit orders: limit one tick below the target high to be filled.
- Numbers: 29,451 (0.125 / upper octant of the daily BISI); 29,514.50 equilibrium used for the pyramid check; 29,501.75 stop vs 29,502.00 low; 29,662 CE of a daily iFVG wick; position 6 + 4 = 10 contracts, 4 off, 5 of 6 planned off at the second high.
- Time windows: first presented FVG = first FVG after 9:30 ET; final-hour macro 2:50–3:10 ET; MOC final 10 minutes (3:50–4:00 ET).
- Jackson Hole day one = highest-likelihood seek and destroy day; avoid it as a student.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Prediction not reaction: "We're not reacting. We're predicting. Every trader is predicting whether they want to admit it" ([0:26:05](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1565)); anticipating is how you avoid emotional reactions to unrealized profit or fear.
- Over-leverage, overtrading and entering before you should wreck even a good model: "Even if they have a winning system, you'll lose because you're the person that's going to wreck it" ([0:25:16](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1516)).
- He says his stop management lets him sit within one tick of the stop because he knows the structure; stops placed where price "never goes".
- He will never trade live so others can copy: if everyone wants the same fill nobody gets it; "I'm not going to dilute my efficiency" ([0:32:31](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1951)–[0:34:05](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2045)).
- Learn his language so the cursor is not necessary; his speech is more important than the cursor in fast markets ([0:13:14](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=794)).
- Slow down; go back through your own charts, annotate and log for backtesting; next week he will take his son Kaden through Monday-Friday daily on what to look for, log and annotate ([0:40:02](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2402)).
- A live trade has real risk; hypothetical position boxes are not a trade; market replay cannot reproduce live fills ([0:35:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2140), [0:41:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2500)).
- Scalping the sell-off is fine, but it is a different style from day-trading with position in mind ([0:09:58](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=598)).

## Glossary candidates
`Seek and destroy` — a day where price sweeps stops on both sides in an expanding sequence before the real move.
`Suspension block` — a SIBI with a volume imbalance at its high and at its low.
`Upper octant` — the first 12.5% line from the top of a graded array (0.125).
`Terminus` — his chosen final target of a trade.
`Cork` — a flat, slightly upward-sloping consolidation that precedes a pop higher.
`Event horizon` — (as used here) midpoint zone between an iFVG high and a wick CE where no bodies should print.
`MOC` — market-on-close window, last 10 minutes of the RTH session.
`Gentleman's market` — futures, where all participants see the same prices.
