## Summary
With a bullish higher-time-frame draw (relative equal highs at about 20,214.75 and the August 22, 2024 old daily high above), ICT reads a small (<40 handle) lower gap opening and shows how he uses the first presented FVG (which must not be the one on the 9:30 candle) as a bullish inversion-FVG setup: price should trade down into it, get above it, then come back into it without using the lower portion. He clarifies the hierarchy of PD-array "rigidity" (volume imbalances are the most flexible, wicks give a "foothold" at their midpoint, order blocks are more exact), how to use wick midpoints for partials and targets, and why exits should be placed ahead of a premium level and before the final "terminus". He defines a Seek & Destroy day (every high and every low gets taken) and links it to Monday's session in which price camped inside every inefficiency. He ends with advice to mid-range traders: you do not need the exact high or low, and quality of study beats quantity.

### First-FVG bullish inversion-FVG setup and its signature  (aliases ICT uses: treat it as inversion, "leaves the lower half untouched", upper quadrant)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bullish plan: let price trade down into the first presented FVG, then rally ABOVE it, then come back down into it; at that point treat the gap as an inversion FVG (support). If price instead breaks below it and fails to hold, the same gap becomes a short-side iFVG for trading lower (he stays with the higher-time-frame long bias).
  - The ideal signature: on the first dive into it as an inversion, price trades down to the lower quadrant but leaves the lowest portion (lower half / lowest part) untouched, and bodies stay in the upper half (from the CE to the top of the gap). He calls this "the signature that the algorithm is absolutely going to send it higher".
  - Do not want price camping inside the inefficiency making many candles. If the market is delivered into an inefficiency, it is only there for a moment (smart money expects the drop to onboard new longs).
  - After going above the first FVG, he wants an energetic move through the midpoint (CE) of the candle's wick and a drive straight toward the target level.
  - He enters by looking for a small 1-minute inefficiency (volume imbalance) near yesterday's first presented FVG when price trades down into it — retail sees such areas as support.
- **How to identify:** first FVG marked; price leaves it, returns, shows lower portion untouched with bodies in upper half.
- **How to use:** long entry from the retest; target relative equal highs / old daily high; scale out early (see terminus).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Sept 25: price traded down into the 9:30 area FVG, worked back up toward the top of the first presented FVG (9:33 candle), rallied above it, then on the retest dove into the lower quadrant, left the lowest portion open and sent price higher; the narrative target was the equal highs (about 20,214.75, caption-derived).

### Wick midpoint (CE) at old highs — intel, partials and targets  (aliases ICT uses: mid-wick, premium CE of the wick, scale out early)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - At old highs and turning points, look inside the wicks and go to the midpoint of the wick; you often get intelligence you would not otherwise notice. Price traded right to the CE of a wick at the old highs.
  - For targets: if a buy-side run targets a level (e.g. about 20,214.75) and there is a wick just below it, the wick midpoint is a partial for him — he starts scaling out as price enters the mid-wick, then waits for the rest.
  - When reaching for a premium level above, always exit ahead of it and scale something out early; price can fall short by a little (it did here).
  - Every turning point/consolidation: look for inefficiencies (an FVG or a wick); a long wick gives price a "foothold/handhold" at its midpoint; they are not the strongest.
- **How to identify:** long wick above an old high or below a low; midpoint of the wick.
- **How to use:** partials at wick CE; do not rely on exact fill of target.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s/1m: price took the wick CE at the highs and was still short of the 20,214.75 level.

### Hierarchy of PD-array rigidity: volume imbalance < wick < order block  (aliases ICT uses: volume imbalance (weakest), foothold, mean threshold)
- **Kind:** refinement / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance (separation between two candle bodies) is the weakest/most flexible PD array — "weakest" in that price moves back and forth through it easily; it can be an entry model but you must be flexible about it. Students often expect them to be rigid and are disappointed.
  - Wick midpoints are footholds (not the strongest).
  - A down-closed candle is a bullish order block; it is best seen with price touching the opening price down to the mean threshold (the middle of the candle), with no body of a later candle trading lower than the mean threshold; ideally price just touches the opening price and then goes higher — "a perfect delivery of a bullish order block".
  - He uses volume imbalances while already in a working trade (confidence to hold, especially a large position), not as a rigid reason to enter.
  - "The wicks do the damage; bodies supporting the volume imbalance is fine."
  - Flexibility is key; characteristics vary by PD array.
- **How to identify:** body-to-body gap = volume imbalance; wick = candle shadow; order block = last down-closed candle before up move.
- **How to use:** adjust stop/expectation by array: tight for OB, flexible for VI.
- **Example in this video:** price traded down into the first FVG's CE, back up, then back down into a volume imbalance with bodies supporting; rallied to the relative equal highs — "that could be your setup".
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:52](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1312) volume imbalance is the weakest / most flexible · [0:23:26](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1406) bullish OB: open to mean threshold · [0:24:13](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1453) don't expect rigidity · [0:24:59](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1499) bodies support the VI, wicks do the damage

### Terminus and scaling out  (aliases ICT uses: Terminus, final target, partials, exit strategy)
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:** "Terminus" is where you think price ultimately goes — your final target, where nothing is left on the position, even if partials were taken along the way. Have an exit strategy before the terminus; without one emotions take over and a trade that would have been profitable gets lost. Taking partials as you approach the target is "always sound logic". Any remaining portion should have a stop below the structure (where price shouldn't go unless the idea is wrong).
- **How to use:** set partial exits before reaching the terminus; fully close at the terminus.
- **Example in this video:** terminus for the trade was the relative equal highs at about 20,214.75 (Tuesday noon-hour relative equal highs), not the August 22 high, because he had an appointment and could not guarantee the market would travel that far; if price reaches the Aug 22 high or higher it is a "moral victory" not something to hold for.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:50](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1790) long: start distributing as price engages the Aug 22 high · [0:33:49](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2029) exit strategy before the terminus · [0:34:36](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2076) terminus for this trade
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** the term appears in the canonical list of ICT's model names; here it is the final-target concept.

### Seek and Destroy day  (aliases ICT uses: Seek & Destroy)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** a Seek and Destroy day is one where every high gets taken out and every low gets taken out. The tell is that price spends time inside every inefficiency that forms (consolidating inside gaps instead of leaving them). ### Mid-range / momentum entries are fine — don't need high or low  (aliases ICT uses: setups between the high and the low, Silver Bullet-type setups, momentum trades)
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:** he does not need the highest high or lowest low; setups between the extremes are generally Silver Bullet-type setups. He gave Caleb (called "Cameron" once in captions) a framework to get into something already in motion, as long as the bias (draw on liquidity) is right, the move to the next liquidity is likely. Many profitable traders trade only the middle of ranges and momentum; that is the best way to start because it removes the need to be right. You can be wrong and still be profitable. Look for your own segment of price action you trust; forcing a specific pattern makes it hard.
- **How to use:** pick the first FVG / volume imbalance in the middle of the move, aim for the next draw.
