## Concepts

### RTH Opening Range Gap (Monday's / Tuesday's) as a draw on liquidity  (aliases ICT uses: regular trading hours opening range gap, "first minute fair value gap"-style gap between prior RTH close and 9:30 open)
- **Kind:** example only / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Earlier in the week (Monday/Tuesday) he stated price would likely rise at least into the CE of Monday's RTH opening range gap; today (Wednesday) it did that and went on to close the whole gap.
  - Tuesday also has its own RTH opening range gap, shown on the 1-minute chart as a gray shaded zone from the gap to the opening price; price was "extended beyond" it.
  - Each session is read in relation to the previous session; ICT says this is what he has been suggesting "in terms of each session and how it relates to the previous session."
  - The chart is switched to regular trading hours to see these gaps clearly.
- **How to identify:** Set chart to RTH; gap between the prior day's RTH close and the 9:30 open; use its CE (50%) as the first target and the far edge as the full-fill target.
- **How to use:** As the target/draw for a long: aim for the CE, expect large-range candles once price clears the lower portion of the gap's remaining unfilled half.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily: small consolidation between lows and a high. 1m: Monday's RTH ORG is the large orange-shaded zone above price. At the 9:30 open price took out a low, rallied, hit Monday's gap CE, pulled back into a "weird consolidation," then rallied to close the entire gap.

### Inversion FVG entry (close-proximity market/buy-stop entry)  (aliases ICT uses: iFVG, inversion fair value gap, reclaimed bearish FVG)
- **Kind:** entry model / example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the 9:30 open ran up into Monday's gap CE and made a small consolidation, he treated a small gap there as an inversion FVG, even though it was not annotated in his execution recording.
  - Two things were "blended": the top of the gap and the upper half of the wick. He did not get in on the initial drop or at the tap of the iFVG; he took a close-proximity entry instead: a market buy "just above that wick high." He says it would have been equivalent to a buy stop at that candle's high.
  - Stop was placed on the other side of the setup (exact level not clear in caption).
  - He expected large up-close candles because (a) the upper half of the gap he wanted to close had not yet traded, and (b) small ranges inside the consolidation, then "once we get above here, large ranges would incur" - a range-expansion expectation.
  - Management: partials taken on the way up, final order placed "on the out last octant" (caption unclear; likely a pre-set final target), exited on the highest candle. He wanted to add during the run but "didn't feel inspired."
- **How to identify:** a gap that gets traded through and reclaimed as the opposing type; small-range candles forming below the high; unfilled upper half of a higher-timeframe gap above as the draw.
- **How to use:** Enter at market or via a buy stop at the high of the candle sitting just above the wick, with a stop under the setup; target the unfilled gap above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, 9:30-9:40ish (times not stated): the open takes the low, rallies up, hits Monday's gap CE, drops into a small consolidation. He buys just above a wick high, stops below, and price closes the entirety of Monday's RTH ORG. He scales out, last order at the final target, exits on the highest candle.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:07:29](https://youtu.be/kRShZKZlvJ4?t=449) — fill just above the wick high; same as a buy stop at that candle's high

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Mentions he could not get in during the drop or at the inversion FVG tag, i.e. a missed entry replaced by a worse-priced continuation entry.

- **Kind:** refinement / example only

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Read each session relative to the previous one; use prior days' RTH opening range gap CE as the first draw.
- Pre-market focus window: 7:00-9:00; news at 8:30.
- Expect large-range candles after small-range consolidation once price clears the unfilled portion of a gap.
- Wicks beyond an important high with bodies staying below = bearish order flow.
- On NFP week, inexperienced/impulsive traders should lock out from Wednesday morning (this video is Wednesday 09/30/2026) through the rest of the week.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Use prop-firm "lockout" features: lock yourself out of trading the rest of NFP week (Wed-Fri) if you are new, inexperienced, impulsive or prone to tilt; the Thursday/Friday price action is detrimental to people with low self-control. Experienced non-impulsive traders can ignore this ([0:11:28](https://youtu.be/kRShZKZlvJ4?t=688), [0:12:15](https://youtu.be/kRShZKZlvJ4?t=735)).

- Posting predictions publicly ahead of time (the Twitter quiz) as a drill.

## Glossary candidates
`RTH OPENING RANGE GAP` — gap between prior RTH close and the 9:30 open used as a draw/array; Monday's was the target here.
`FAILURE SWING` — reversal in the pre-market that flips a trend, used with a news driver.
`PREMIUM WICK` — a wick into premium/above a key high whose bodies stay below; bearish order flow.
`CLOSE-PROXIMITY ENTRY` — entering just beyond the array after missing the ideal tap (e.g., buy stop at a candle high).
`LOCK YOURSELF OUT` — using a prop-firm lockout feature to prevent trading on dangerous weeks.
