## Concepts

### Inside Days (Back-to-Back Range Containment)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When two consecutive days' trading ranges do not extend beyond each other's highs and lows, they form "inside days." Example: Monday high, Monday low, Tuesday high, Tuesday low all remain within a contained band. This pattern signals consolidation before a breakout (usually 3 or more days before expansion).
- **How to identify:** Mark Monday's high and low; mark Tuesday's high and low; if all four points remain clustered (not extending beyond each other), it's inside days.
- **How to use:** Expect range expansion after inside days (usually within 1–3 days). Use the inside day highs/lows as breakout boundaries.
- **Example in this video:** Monday and Tuesday formed inside days; price was contained until Wednesday FOMC, when it broke downward through the Monday low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:48](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=168) "inside days back-to-back."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not a guarantee of expansion; FOMC or other catalysts trigger the breakout.

### Relative Equal Highs / Lows (REH/REL)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Swing highs or lows that are at approximately equal prices across multiple sessions. When price reaches REH or REL, it often acts as liquidity resistance/support. Breaking above REH or below REL confirms directional bias.
- **How to identify:** Mark all swing highs across multiple sessions; identify those at similar price levels = REH. Same for lows = REL.
- **How to use:** Use as breakout trigger; wait for close above REH or below REL to confirm direction; buy-side liquidity rests at REH, sell-side at REL.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's high and Monday's high are at similar levels (REH); price pierced this twice on Wednesday pre-FOMC, then broke through on the second attempt post-FOMC.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:24](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=264) "relative equal highs," [0:05:12](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=312) "Tuesday's daily high and the relative equal highs... we pierced that already at 2:00."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Visual smoothness of REH/REL (when charted) indicates strength of the liquidity level.

### Daily Suspension Block
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A price zone between consecutive daily lows (e.g., between Monday low and Tuesday low) that acts as a consolidation area. During FOMC or other big moves, price often targets this zone as a mid-range support before continuing lower (or higher, depending on direction).
- **How to identify:** Identify the low of day N and day N+1; the void between them is the suspension block.
- **How to use:** Expect price to trade into this block during large directional moves. It acts as a magnet or rest area before continuation.
- **Example in this video:** Daily suspension block shaded between Monday and Tuesday lows (around 18650–18600 area); post-FOMC, price targeted this block and then broke below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:20](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=500) "daily suspension block that's shaded in blue," [0:10:47](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=647) "sets the stage and tone for the market wanting to reach down into that daily suspension block."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** First FOMC meeting may see wider suspension block testing due to uncertainty; ICT took partials rather than running full to Monday low.

### FOMC Two-Stage Delivery
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** FOMC meetings typically deliver price moves in two stages: (1) pre-decision expectation move (before announcement), (2) post-decision response move (after announcement). Price may test resistance twice before breaking, using the second break to enter the main directional move.
- **How to identify:** Track price action in hours before FOMC announcement (usually 2 PM ET); observe the pre-move rally/sell-off; then watch the post-announcement move.
- **How to use:** Anticipate two attempts at resistance (REH/REL); the second break often confirms the direction. Entry on the second break is lower-risk.
- **Example in this video:** Pre-FOMC, price pieced Tuesday's high at 2:00 PM, came down, then at 2:40 PM (post-announcement ~2:15 PM) broke through again and continued downward.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) Post-Breakout Entry
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** After price breaks above a resistance level (REH/REL), look back for any inefficiency (gap or imbalance) formed just before the breakout. That inefficiency becomes an iFVG entry zone; price often retraces into it, allowing a pullback entry into the confirmed direction.
- **How to identify:** Identify the breakout point (close above REH); look back 1–3 candles for a gap or volume imbalance (candlestick body doesn't cover entire range).
- **How to use:** Enter into the iFVG when price pulls back; stop below the wick low; target the next liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** After breaking REH, price created an iFVG at 29,467–29,483 range; this became the retracement entry zone.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:47](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=407) "inversion fair value gap," [0:08:20](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=500) "absolute characteristics of an inversion fair value gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Must identify within the context of a confirmed breakout (two-stage FOMC pattern, in this case).

### Swing Projections (Fibonacci Expansion)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** After identifying a swing low and high, use Fibonacci expansion (100%, 150%, 200% of the swing range) to project downside targets. Example: measure wick from high to low; add equal distance below the low (100%) or 1.5x the distance (150%) to find support zones.
- **How to identify:** Mark a swing high and low; measure the distance; project downward from the low using multiples of that distance.
- **How to use:** Use these projections alongside other support levels (daily lows, suspension blocks) to confirm targets.
- **Example in this video:** Wick from high to low measured; 100% projection taken below low confirms alignment with daily suspension block and Monday low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:59](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=599) "swing projections," [0:09:59](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=599) "100 percentiles to the range."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not magic; just mathematical extension of price structure.

### Volume Imbalance in Candlesticks
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When a candlestick's body doesn't fill the gap between the prior candle's close and current candle's open, a volume imbalance exists. If the body doesn't touch the wick extremes, the imbalance is even more notable. This signifies institutional step-and-repeat movement.
- **How to identify:** Look for gaps between candle bodies (not filled by wicks); note bodies that stay within upper or lower half of the wick.
- **How to use:** Use as entries into the imbalance zone; expect price to respect the imbalance.
- **Example in this video:** Body at open 29,467; prior close 29,467 even (no volume imbalance at open); but wick highs created imbalance above and below the body.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:47](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=407) "volume imbalance there, you can see between the difference between the bodies."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Must also check if candlestick wick high has a gap (if body's gap is too small).

## Glossary candidates
- **Inside Days** — Consecutive days with ranges contained within each other (no new highs or lows beyond prior day).
- **Relative Equal Highs (REH)** — Swing highs at approximately equal price levels across multiple sessions; liquidity resistance.
- **Relative Equal Lows (REL)** — Swing lows at approximately equal price levels; liquidity support.
- **Daily Suspension Block** — Price zone between consecutive daily lows (e.g., Mon low to Tues low); magnet zone during directional moves.
- **Two-Stage FOMC Delivery** — Pre-announcement retest of resistance, followed by post-announcement breakout and continuation.
- **FOMC Sweet Spot** — 2:40–2:50 PM ET window, typically marking the main directional move post-announcement.
- **Swing Projections** — Fibonacci expansions (100%, 150%, 200%) of prior swing range, used to project support/resistance levels.
- **Volume Imbalance** — Candlestick body that doesn't fill the gap between prior close and current open.
