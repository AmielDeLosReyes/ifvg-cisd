## Concepts

### Macro Time Windows (9:50–10:10 ET)
- **Kind:** time filter / algorithmic trigger
- **What ICT says:** The period from 9:50 AM to 10:10 AM ET (approximately 20 minutes into the RTH open) is a high-probability algorithmic time window (a "macro"). During this window, smart money is actively executing orders. Setups identified before or during this macro are more likely to trigger. Price action during this window is often more decisive than earlier (9:30–9:50).
- **How to identify:** Mark the time on your chart: 9:50–10:10 ET. Watch price action during this window. If a pattern forms (wick touch, FVG formation, liquidity pool test), it's a macro setup.
- **How to use:** Don't trade before 9:50. Wait for this window. If your setup (turtle soup, FVG entry, etc.) aligns with macro time, conviction is higher. If price doesn't move decisively by 10:10, the setup may have failed.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Macros vary by market and day. The 9:50–10:10 ET macro is typical for US index futures. Other macros occur at noon, 2:00 PM, etc. Learn to identify them through observation.

### Turtle Soup Entry Model
- **Kind:** entry setup + liquidity trap
- **What ICT says:** A turtle soup is when price runs above a **two-stage buy-side liquidity pool** (buyers holding stops above two different levels), then reverses sharply into the zone between the two pools, where sell-side liquidity rests. The "soup" is the trap: retail buyers place buy stops above the first pool, smart money runs price up and through those stops, then reverses and trades through the second pool, creating panic selling. Entry is in the zone between the two pools (or just above the upper pool, expecting reversal). Target is the sell-side liquidity below the lower pool.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Identify two buy-side liquidity pools (two previous highs or resistance levels).
  2. Price runs above the first, then above the second (taking both buy-side pools).
  3. Expect sharp reversal and downside run.
  4. Entry: above the second pool (where reversals form) or in the "soup" zone (between the two pools).
- **How to use:** Turtle soup setups in macro time windows have the highest probability. Short aggressively once both pools are taken and bodies show reversal (up close candles shift to down close). Target the sell-side liquidity below.
- **Example in this video:** Two buy-side pools were identified on the Asian session chart. Price ran above both during the macro window. ICT then entered short, targeting the sell-side liquidity below. The trade worked as expected.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:26](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=206)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Turtle soup is one of the most reliable setups when macro time aligns. However, in very strong bull markets, it may fail—price may run through and keep going. Identify bull market bias first before using turtle soup.

### Wick as Gap / Fibonacci on Wicks
- **Kind:** analysis tool + reference
- **What ICT says:** A wick that extends beyond a previous level can be treated as a gap for Fibonacci purposes. You can draw a Fibonacci retracement from the wick low to the wick high (or from one major wick to another). Use consequent encroachment (the midpoint of the wick) and the low as your key Fibonacci levels. This creates micro-level targets within the wick zone.
- **How to identify:** Find a large wick that extends beyond a key level (e.g., a wick that drops below an FVG or support). Mark the high and low of the wick. Draw Fib from low to high.
- **How to use:** When price is trading below the wick, place limit orders at the Fib levels (especially 0.5, or midpoint/consequent encroachment, and the low). These act as micro-targets during retracements or consolidations. When price pulls back up and touches the wick, expect resistance at consequent encroachment.
- **Example in this video:** A wick on the daily chart was treated as a gap. ICT drew a Fib on it to find precise entry and exit points during the intraday trade.

### Bearish Fair Value Gap (Entry Model)
- **Kind:** entry signal + reversal filter
- **What ICT says:** When price forms an up close candle, it leaves a small gap above it (the space between the close and the next candle's open/high). This is a potential inversion FVG. When price later comes back up and trades into this gap, it's a bearish setup. Entry is when price closes above the gap (confirming the inversion), and target is the sell-side liquidity below.
- **How to identify:** After an up close candle in a down move, mark the gap left above the close. This is the inversion FVG. When price retraces back up and trades into this gap, watch for bodies staying in the lower half. If bodies break above the midpoint, the setup is weak. If bodies stay below, it's strong.
- **How to use:** Short when price trades into the inversion FVG and bodies stay in the lower half. Target the next sell-side liquidity pool below. Use consequent encroachment (midpoint of the gap) as a mental stop—if price breaks above it, exit.
- **Example in this video:** An up close candle left an inversion FVG. Price later came back up into it, and ICT shorted, targeting the sell-side liquidity. The trade worked.

### Breakaway Gap
- **Kind:** structure + continuation signal
- **What ICT says:** When price trades through a fair value gap, exits the gap completely, and leaves the upper or lower portion of the gap open (unfilled), it's a breakaway gap. This signals that the move is likely to continue in that direction without immediately retracing back into the gap. Breakaway gaps are bullish or bearish continuation signals depending on direction.
- **How to identify:** Price trades through a gap, leaves a portion unfilled (visually, you see gap space above the candles in a down move, or below in an up move). This unfilled portion is the breakaway gap.
- **How to use:** When a breakaway gap forms, the move is likely to continue. Don't expect price to immediately retrace and fill it. Instead, use the gap as a reference for stops or partial targets. If price does come back and fills the breakaway gap, it's a reversal signal.
- **Example in this video:** After the short entry, price traded down through an FVG and left a portion unfilled (the upper portion). This was a breakaway gap, indicating the down move would continue.

### Premium Array (Wick-based Resistance)
- **Kind:** filter / reversal zone identification
- **What ICT says:** A wick that extends into a zone above price, combined with an FVG or gap, creates a "premium array"—a resistance zone where price is unlikely to close above. Even in strong bull markets, premium arrays act as speed bumps. Don't ignore them; instead, use them as shorting opportunities (turtle soup, etc.).
- **How to identify:** Find a wick that extends significantly into a zone above recent price action. This wick + the zone = premium array. Mark it.
- **How to use:** When price approaches a premium array, expect resistance. If it's combined with a turtle soup setup, short it. If price closes above it, the bullish bias is confirmed and the premium array is neutralized.
- **Example in this video:** A premium array was identified on the daily chart wick. This gave ICT confidence that the turtle soup short setup would work, even though the market was bullish overall.

### Two-Stage Liquidity Extraction
- **Kind:** market structure + execution model
- **What ICT says:** Smart money often extracts liquidity in two stages: first, they take out the obvious buy-side pool (the first high that everyone sees), then they take out the second, less-obvious buy-side pool. Only then do they run price lower to the sell-side liquidity. This two-stage extraction is a signature pattern. When you see both stages hit, conviction is very high that the move will continue in the opposite direction.
- **How to identify:** Watch price run higher. First high = first buy-side pool. Price pulls back slightly, then runs higher again. Second high = second buy-side pool. Both are hit. Now expect reversal lower.
- **How to use:** Don't short after the first pool is taken; wait for the second. Once both pools are taken, short aggressively. This reduces false signals and aligns your trade with smart money's order flow.
- **Example in this video:** Two buy-side pools were taken during the macro window. ICT waited for both, then entered short with conviction.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Macro time window:** 9:50–10:10 ET. Setups during this window have higher probability.
- **Two-stage liquidity rule:** Take both buy-side pools before entering the short. Don't short after the first pool.
- **Turtle soup entry condition:** Both pools taken, bodies starting to show reversal (down close after up closes).
- **Entry level:** Above the second buy-side pool or in the "soup" zone between pools, expecting reversal.
- **Target levels:** Sell-side liquidity pools below (marked before entering).
- **Partial strategy:** First partial at the first sell-side pool below, second partial at the second sell-side pool below.
- **Consequent encroachment (wick):** Midpoint of a wick treated as Fibonacci 0.5 level. Use as micro-target or mental stop.
- **Breakaway gap:** Upper/lower portion left unfilled when price trades through a gap. Indicates continuation without immediate retrace.
- **Premium array:** Wick extending into upper zone + gap = resistance area. Expect shorting opportunity or completion of reversal.

## Process, risk & psychology

**Pre-setup planning:**
- Identify buy-side pools (previous highs, resistance levels).
- Identify sell-side pools (below; previous lows, FVG zones, support levels).
- Mark premium arrays and key wicks.
- Wait for macro time window.

**During macro window:**
- Watch price approach and take the first buy-side pool.
- Don't enter yet; wait for the second pool to be taken.
- Once both pools are taken, watch for reversal signal (down close candles).
- Enter short aggressively, either above the second pool or in the soup zone.

**Trade management:**
- First target = first sell-side pool. Scale out 1/3 to 1/2 position.
- Second target = second sell-side pool. Scale out another 1/3.
- Final target = deeper sell-side pool or daily bias target.
- Move stop to breakeven after first partial taken.

**Documenting trades (for credibility):**
- Record the entire trade from entry to exit (keep video/screenshot).
- Toggle between market replay and live mode to prove real execution (green dots persist).
- Post on social media with timestamp for public accountability.
- This prevents critics from claiming "hindsight" or "cherry-picking."

## Glossary candidates

`Macro (time window)` — Algorithmic time window (e.g., 9:50–10:10 ET) where smart money executes orders decisively. Setups during macros have higher probability.

`Turtle soup` — Entry setup where two buy-side liquidity pools are taken (stop-running), then reversed sharply to trap buyers and sell into sell-side liquidity below.

`Buy-side liquidity pool` — Cluster of buy stops resting above a resistance level. When taken (run by price), it creates panic and reversal.

`Sell-side liquidity pool` — Cluster of sell stops/orders resting below a support level. The target of a bearish move after buy-side pools are taken.

`Two-stage extraction` — Smart money taking out two sequential buy-side pools before reversing. Shows strong intent and high probability of continuation in opposite direction.

`Breakaway gap` — Upper/lower portion of a FVG left unfilled when price trades through and continues in that direction. Indicates no immediate retrace.

`Premium array` — Wick-based resistance zone (wick extending above, combined with FVG or gap). Acts as speed bump or shorting trigger point.

`Market replay` — TradingView mode simulating past price action. Useful for backtesting, but trades executed only in replay mode don't persist in live mode (green dots disappear).

`Real execution` — A trade that was placed and filled during live trading (not simulated or hypothetical). Proven by persistence of execution markers when toggling between market replay and live.

`Wick as gap` — Treating a wick that extends significantly as a gap, for the purpose of Fibonacci calculations and micro-level targeting.

`Consequent encroachment (wick Fib)` — The 0.5 Fibonacci level (midpoint) of a wick. Used as a micro-target or mental stop within wick-based gaps.
