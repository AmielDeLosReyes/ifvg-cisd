## Concepts

### Event horizon (50% of a range between two lows / old high and target)  (aliases ICT uses: "event horizon", "consequent encroachment of the range", "halfway point")
- **Kind:** definition / rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Anchor a fib on a low and drag to the next low (or from an old high to the target), delete all levels in the settings, and keep only the 0.5 level. That halfway point is the event horizon.
  - It is a "very strong candidate" for price to reach; price may simply touch it and turn around. He uses it to build structure for predetermined partials and to weigh the magnitude and velocity of a price run.
  - A close beyond it (below, in a short) increases confidence that the low/target behind it will be taken out. If it fails to close beyond, treat it as a possible bounce or reversal.
  - Event horizons can be nested: draw one from the first low and another from the second; treat the zone between the two levels as an inefficiency/gap ("good enough for government work"). A close below both raises the confidence that both lows will be taken out.
  - At the event horizon, "it's an event on the horizon you must make a decision about": where to move the stop, and whether to take a partial. He says you should take partials ("pay the trader"); don't demand target-or-nothing, because nothing is easy to hit.
  - Halfway is "always a really good rule of thumb."
- **How to identify:** two reference points (lows/highs/old high and target); midpoint between them.
- **How to use:** use as first target and partial level; adjust stop when reached; read closes beyond it as confirmation; grade the range with octants/quadrants if desired (below).
- **Example in this video:**
  - GBPUSD (short): objective reached; lowest of two relative equal lows is the next level; he says not to demand a target below it and to take something off at that low or at the CE of its wick.
  - NQ long (Monday): old short-term high anchored at roughly 30,869.75 to the target (minor buy side/ATH area) at 30,975.50; midpoint is the event horizon at about 30,922.75 (he had it on 30,923 by eye; "the actual tick is 922.75"). Price reached it, and then could not trade to the 30,975.50 minor buy side before breaking lower.

### Sell-stop entry when the anticipated draw fails to trade  (aliases: "sell stop as an entry mechanism")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you anticipate price going to a level (all-time high, or a weekly high) and it fails to get there, a sell stop works as an entry: if already long, trail the stop to just below the low to lock profit; if flat, place a sell stop just below the low, so when it breaks the low you are not stopped out of a long but are put net short.
  - Measure from the high (lower than the event horizon), use the event horizon level, and after the break you want to see price become "very, very aggressive".
  - Anywhere in the range where price breaks below that low is a valid short on the sell stop.
  - It doesn't have to be the all-time high; weekly highs work the same way.
- **How to identify:** a stated target level (here 30,975.50 minor buy side / ATH) not traded to; a small low beneath the event horizon.
- **How to use:** sell stop just under that low; stop above the high; first target the lower liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: price jumped up to the event horizon (~30,922.75) but failed to reach 30,975.50; the little low beneath was broken; price ran lower aggressively.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:58](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2458); [0:41:45](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2505); [0:42:34](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2554).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** stated as a general technique; no fixed risk or size given.

### Previous day's range retracement rule  (aliases: "how much of the previous day's range", "lower half of the previous day's range")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Look at previous day's high/low/range and grade the sensitivity around it: how much of the previous day's range is price retracing? When the market is bearish, it should retrace only into the lower half of the previous day's range. If it does retrace up to the half, expect an afternoon or late-session continuation or the next day; you may have to cut a losing day-trade and look for the downside to resume.
  - Example (EURUSD): price retraced into the lower half and sold off; on another day it did not even reach the half, which tells him "algorithmically" the market is bearish; on a day that opened where it closed he used the wick instead.
  - He taught this in his paid mentorships (2016–2021) and says it underlies many of his executions.
- **How to identify:** plot a fib on the previous day's high to low; mark 50%.
- **How to use:** retrace into the lower half = bearish continuation expected; a trade into the half = caution/resumption later.
- **Timestamps:** [1:05:27](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=3927); [1:06:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=3976); [1:07:50](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4070).

### Draw on liquidity as expectation, not a requirement  (aliases: "drawing liquidity", "reasonable expectation")
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** Draw on liquidity is the premise that builds the idea before the trade; price does not have to reach it. "If you're going to be long, you have to know where it's going to reach to." Without that you are trading blind, which is gambling. If you are new and lack conviction, taking a profit before the target is fine; if wrong, a hypothetical trailing stop below the low gets hit and you "accept it, number one" because it is one transaction. He compares these small, partial wins to earning belts in martial arts: they keep a beginner motivated and teachable.
- **Example in this video:** DXY run into 101.977 target; price near the target by this date.

## Glossary candidates
`EVENT HORIZON` — the 0.5 (CE) level of a range between two lows/highs or an old high and target; a built-in partial/stop checkpoint and confidence test.
`NESTED EVENT HORIZONS` — event horizons from two successive lows; the zone between them acts as a gap.
`PAY THE TRADER` — take a partial at a logical level instead of demanding the full target.
`LAY OF THE LAND` — his chart of key levels (e.g. ATH, minor buy side) posted without a buy or sell call.
`BISI / SIBI` — buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (bullish FVG) / sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (bearish FVG).
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — candle suspended between two volume imbalances (daily BISI example).
`PROTRACTED TARGETS` — longer-range targets located using volume imbalances (toggle on).
`HERDING` — followers clustering stops at the same level, creating liquidity.
`SCHEDULED DELIVERY` — his term for time-based, non-random price delivery.
