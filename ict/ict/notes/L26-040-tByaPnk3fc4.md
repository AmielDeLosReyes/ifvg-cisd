## Summary
ICT walks through one live, one-minute-chart long taken in the 7:00–9:00 ET window that was anticipated from the daily chart: after a four-day rally and one soft day, price dropped sharply into a daily inversion FVG, a volume imbalance and a key daily wick. The lesson is "anticipation, not reaction": know in advance which level should produce a tradable run, why it should, how that run should begin, and which "footholds" (levels price must keep respecting) prove the run is sustainable. Along the way he defines a "suspension block", says the upper half of a BISI is the strongest place for price to hold, and gives an if/then plan for what price must do if it dips back. The second half is trade management and psychology: roll the stop into profit, never move it back, take partials ("give yourself the cookie"), and never let the need to be right drive you. A long aside explains why he does not trust NFP/fundamental data for index futures.

## Concepts

### Anticipation framework: daily PD array into a 7:00–9:00 ET one-minute entry  (aliases: "science of anticipation", "anticipate, not react", "hunting")
- **Kind:** entry model / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Profit needs movement; it is "literally impossible" to profit without motion in price. So spend time finding places where price is likely, on a consistent basis, to provide a tradable run, then take measured risk there.
  - A key level on the daily chart gives "a large degree of probability" that price will produce a tradable run. That is odds in your favour, not a guarantee of profit.
  - Anticipate, then verify with footholds: (1) know where the run should start (inception), (2) know what level each stage should respect, (3) watch for continued feedback that the run is sustainable. You are "hunting", not waiting for the deer to start running before you chase it.
  - The one-minute chart gives no room to "fluff" it: you must know exactly what price should do, should not do, and what is permissible. This gets easier as you learn to recognise repeatable volatility and price runs.
  - Time focus he reiterates: 7:00 to 9:00 ET, applying the daily PD-array logic. Here, price traded down into daily discount arrays at 7:00, did so aggressively, rallied, consolidated, folded, then held the upper half of the imbalance.
  - He plans a separate (pre-recorded, YouTube-hosted) talk on "generic price structure that yields continuous opportunities": his claim is that his full teaching compendium already contains enough framework to find a setup every day.
- **How to identify:** daily chart shows a sharp one-way run into a daily PD array (here an inversion FVG) that is also an old high/wick area; that arrival happens during the 7:00–9:00 ET window; price on the 1m chart then shows a reactive gap/imbalance to enter at.
- **How to use:** mark the daily levels first, delineate the 7:00 to 9:00 window, drop to 1m, enter at a refined level inside the daily array (see next concepts), manage with the partial/stop-roll rules below.
- **Example in this video:** daily: four straight up days off FOMC, one pullback day, today opens softer. ICT expects price not to settle on a down close and to try higher (a rally this hard normally takes a break). Price drops sharply into the daily inversion FVG (which also contains an old high and a daily volume imbalance). On the 1m chart in the 7:00–9:00 window he goes long at the consequent encroachment of a gap (see below), targets the relative equal highs above, scales out, trails the stop and finally closes the session note with price holding the upper half of the imbalance below a failed push.

### Inversion FVG on the daily chart as a support level  (aliases: daily iFVG, "reversion fair value gap" in captions)
- **Kind:** example only (applies known concept)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price dropped fast into a daily inversion fair value gap and was supported. The speed of the drop ("in a hurry to get here") is part of why a pop was reasonable to anticipate.
  - The 50% (consequent encroachment) of a daily wick he had tweeted about equals the high of that inversion FVG, so two reference points coincide.
  - He tweets a level only when he also sees something technically align with it.
  - The daily wick is a level he wants price to overcome (get above and hold above) "until we get through" NFP week.
- **How to use:** the iFVG is the daily-timeframe reason; the 1m refinement (CE of a gap, suspension block) is the execution.
- **Example in this video:** price at/near an old daily high and wick; relative equal highs sit just above; after the sharp one-candle decline price comes back and runs over the whole decline.

### Volume imbalance and the "daily inefficiency range"  (aliases: VI)
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the daily chart there is a volume imbalance up to yesterday's low, which he calls the range for daily inefficiency; another area shows no volume imbalance.
  - On the 1m chart price "traded down into" a volume imbalance and reacted from it with extreme precision: the open of one candle was 29,390.75 and the low of the other candle was 90.75, meaning price touched the exact edge, and he calls this "perfect".
  - A volume imbalance can sit outside an inversion FVG and that is permissible.
  - On a drop, "walking back to the left" from the lowest candle, the last inefficiency of the run (here a volume imbalance that had already been used, no true gap) is the area where he would have entered and pyramided.
- **How to identify:** two consecutive candles whose bodies do not overlap or connect, even though wicks do (from his wording about "bodies don't connect or overlap").
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:36](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=96) daily volume imbalance · [0:28:51](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1731) exact 29,390.75 reaction · [0:24:48](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1488) last inefficiency of the run

### Suspension block  (aliases: none stated)
- **Kind:** new coinage / definition
- **What ICT says:** when there is a volume imbalance at the high of an inefficiency and another at the low of that same inefficiency (bodies on both ends do not connect or overlap), that structure is a suspension block. You carry that information forward as a level.
- **How to identify:** inefficiency (gap) with a volume imbalance at its top edge and one at its bottom edge.
- **How to use:** he likes it as a place to go long; he entered at the midpoint of the gap inside the structure.
- **Example in this video:** 1m chart near the daily iFVG: a small volume imbalance where bodies do not connect, then a section where two candles' bodies do not connect or overlap, bracketing the inefficiency; he buys at its CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:13](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=253) "A suspension block. I like that." · [0:26:26](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1586) definition
- **Nuance / caveats:** first time seen in these notes as a named structure; the caption wording is somewhat loose, so treat the exact definition as "VI at the high and low of an inefficiency".

### Entry at the consequent encroachment of a gap, with stop under the key level
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He enters "exactly at the consequent encroachment" (50%) of the 1m gap right after the daily inversion FVG tag.
  - The stop loss for a trade like this has to start "right at that level and under": at or just below the level (the 50% of the wick/top of the iFVG) that must hold for the idea to be valid.
  - Later stop logic: stop sits below the midpoint of the wick; if price is bullish it need not trade below that area; worst case it wicks to it.
  - He placed a limit order to sell one contract just above entry as a first partial.
- **How to use:** entry at CE, stop below the key level, first partial quickly, then trail (see trade management).

### Midpoint "threshold" test: bodies above 50% of the decline
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - There was a big vertical decline in one candle. Take the high of that inefficiency to the volume-imbalance low; the midpoint of that range is a threshold ("think of it like a ladder", not depth of market).
  - Price must show willingness to get above the midpoint and start laying bodies above it. Only after a candle closes above halfway does he roll the stop up to just below the lows of the decline.
  - Ideal sequence: overlap all of the decline, rip above, stay above, and then the next order of business is the order flow around the relative equal highs.
  - You can add all the grading levels (fib/his usual levels) on top if you want to be a "purist"; he shows his fib settings on request. The caption does not say which exact levels.
- **How to identify:** closes (bodies) above the 50% of the one-candle decline; no bodies below it later.
- **How to use:** a close above 50% = permission to trail the stop; bodies below the wick's CE later is why price got "heavy and weighty".
- **Example in this video:** after entry price pops, closes above the line, he rolls the stop up under the decline's lows, then rolls it again to cover commissions.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:34](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=454) the ladder/threshold idea · [0:09:14](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=554) close above halfway then roll stop · [0:21:36](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1296) none of the bodies close below the half
- **Nuance / caveats:** none stated beyond "willingness" language (price must keep showing it).

### Upper half of a BISI as the strongest support; small portion left open  (aliases: "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency", upper portion of the gap)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - If price trades back into a BISI that is acting as support, the upper half is where the strongest buying for an entry comes from.
  - You want a small portion of the gap to remain open: a small portion of a gap staying open means the market is unwilling to go lower; if it is unwilling to go lower while you expect higher prices, that is by definition strong.
  - He says nobody else taught it this way and that this is his own idea, not supply/demand boxes.
  - If-then clause he gave live: ideally the wick supports price. If price goes below it, it should stay in the upper half of the gap. If price shows discount sensitivity (below) there and does not want to go lower, the idea stays valid; if it closes below the low (on a closing basis), this is the last line of defence for bullishness.
- **How to use:** stack the three PD arrays he lists as the "support zone" for a long: (1) the old high, (2) the upper portion of the BISI, (3) the wick/its CE. Third and lowest of the three is the upper portion of the BISI (as captioned).
- **Example in this video:** after the first push, price came back toward the area, closed outside the gap, then held the upper half; later he notes "now we're supporting the upper half" as a defensive view of market structure.
- **Timestamps:** [0:34:31](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2071) three PD arrays, upper portion of the BISI · [0:35:17](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2117) small portion open = unwilling to go lower · [0:32:55](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1975) the if/then clause
- **Nuance / caveats:** he attributes the claim to himself; whether others teach it is irrelevant to the lesson.

### Discount sensitivity
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement
- **What ICT says:** he draws a box from the midpoint of an inefficiency up to its high and calls the behaviour inside it "discount sensitivity". If price comes down into that zone it must react to the upside and not be inclined to keep going all the way down. "Worst case" it wicks to the stop-area but he prefers it does not.
- **How to use:** a way to judge strength on a pullback: reactive bounce from the upper half of the box means the long is still intact; if it fails to react, the idea weakens.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:43](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2023) · [0:40:08](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2408) repeated for the next inefficiency
- **Nuance / caveats:** term appears here as a descriptive coinage; no numeric criteria.

### Trade management: partials, stop-rolling and never loosening a stop  (aliases: "give yourself the cookie")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Roll the stop as price proves itself (after the close above halfway; later "just to cover commissions"; later to lock in profit). Once you move a stop into profit, commit to it: never move it back down (long) or back up (short).
  - He takes partial profits at obvious liquidity (one contract at the first limit, "tuck two off of three" at the high where price had stretched like "taffy": it can give up at any time).
  - Reason to take a partial when anxious or inexperienced: it rewards you for what you have done and removes the need to be right. "Give yourself the cookie."
  - Stopped out after moving into profit = you get paid; it is not being victimised.
  - He says market "victim" thinking comes from moving the stop back and failing to take profit at a higher price.
- **How to use:** (1) partial at the first liquidity objective; (2) stop to cost/commission after price proves itself; (3) leave a small runner; (4) do not widen once raised.
- **Example in this video:** entry at CE; limit sell of 1 contract just above; stop rolled twice; two of three contracts off at the high; leftover with a stop under the midpoint of the wick.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:02](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=602) rolling the stop up · [0:36:56](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2216) "you don't need to be right" · [0:39:21](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2361) two off of three · [0:42:31](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2551) do not move the stop back

### NFP week rules and fundamentals view
- **Kind:** rule / opinion
- **What ICT says:**
  - For brand-new traders in NFP week, the cleanest price action is Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday up to about 11:00 ET. Admitting you are new is maturity, not weakness. Don't try to take every possible move.
  - NFP (first Friday of the month, 8:30 ET) is mostly an "invitation to a day of anticipation" with large timed volatility; he believes the data is untrustworthy and it is "one of the largest reports misinterpreted by traders". He never cared about the figures.
  - He sees real fundamental supply/demand in agricultural commodities and livestock (corn, grains, soybeans, wheat, oats, meats), not in bonds, stocks or index futures, where price is driven by "perceived" supply and demand. He calls fundamental analysis of indexes pseudoscience.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Beginners in NFP week: trade Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday up to ~11:00 ET only.
- Focus window for this idea: 7:00–9:00 ET (New York local time), 1-minute chart, after reading the daily PD arrays.
- Enter at the consequent encroachment (50%) of the 1m gap; stop at/under the key level (50% of the wick / top of the iFVG).
- Price must close bodies above the 50% of the decline before the stop is rolled up.
- Rejection block = highest up-close price in a swing high.
- Suspension block = volume imbalance at the high and low of an inefficiency.
- Upper half of a BISI = strongest support; a small piece of the gap staying open signals unwillingness to go lower.
- Volume imbalance exact test: candle open 29,390.75 matched by the other candle's low 90.75 (this instrument/date not stated).
- Never move a stop back once it has been raised; scale 1 then 2 of 3 contracts at liquidity.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Anticipate (what, why, from where, what proves it) rather than react to price [0:27:15].
- Reward yourself with partials when the setup delivers: "give yourself the cookie"; trading is the one thing where consuming more makes the account bigger [0:36:56–0:39:21].
- The urge to "be right" is managed with a partial and by committing to the raised stop [0:36:05, 0:42:31].
- Stop = a hired employee that pays you when it trails in profit; moving it back "gives back" the unrealized gain [0:43:17, 0:44:55].
- Look for markets that were suppressed or blew off, then hit a key daily level; those who sold the low are the last bears, rewarded briefly (they should have taken profits) [0:10:51–0:11:40].
- New traders: be honest about experience, don't try to catch everything [0:03:19].
- Personal asides (stress eating in early drawdown, comparisons to poker) are the psychology illustrations, not rules.

## Glossary candidates
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — an inefficiency with a volume imbalance at both its high and its low.
`DISCOUNT SENSITIVITY` — price reacting upward from the lower/mid part of a box instead of continuing down.
`REJECTION BLOCK` — highest up-close price within a swing high (his wording here).
`VOLUME IMBALANCE (VI)` — two candles whose bodies do not overlap, wicks do.
`INVERSION FVG (iFVG)` — an FVG that price has traded through and now acts as support/resistance on the other side.
`CONSEQUENT ENCROACHMENT (CE)` — the 50% level of a gap or wick.
`GIVE YOURSELF THE COOKIE` — take a partial profit when the setup delivers.
`TAFFY` — his image of a stretched run that thins out and can give up.
