## Concepts

### RTH Opening Range Gap and half-gap (CE) delivery  (aliases: half gap, gap and go, previous RTH settlement to opening)
- **Kind:** rule / entry framework
- **What ICT says:** At 9:30 ET the regular-trading-hours gap is the difference between the previous RTH settlement and the 9:30 open. Pull the fib from the previous RTH settlement to the opening price; the half-gap (CE of the gap) has about a 70 percent chance of being traded back to within the first 30 minutes (he also says "by 10:00 in the morning"). A large gap is better: 100 handles or more is ideal because that offers about 50 handles to the half-gap. After it reaches the half, the next question is whether it continues to the 3/4 gap and the full close, or rejects the half-gap and runs in the direction of the gap ("gap and go").
- **How to identify:** Premium gap (open above prior RTH close) -> expect a trade back down to the half; discount gap -> back up. Mark the half gap, plus any pool of liquidity sitting there (here a sell-side pool just under last Wednesday's RTH ORG low).
- **How to use:** In the beginning, coach yourself to submit to the idea that price goes to the usual draw; combined with a short first-presented FVG at 9:31 onward, the half gap is the target. He frames this as "one of the things I had for my son" (look for that move when there is a sizeable gap). Don't take his 70 percent on faith: test it on your own data every day there is a gap, for as long as you can, and keep recording going forward (he cites 34 years of use and data from 50 years before him).
- **Example in this video:** Premium gap on Feb 11: 9:30 open at ...326.75 (last digits). Price ran the pre-session high, took the bearish-breaker area, sold off, and reached the half-gap about 2 minutes before 10:00 ("there's the delivery"). He also writes the ORG levels manually using the last three numbers of price.

### News-day rule: NFP on an unusual day; wait for 9:30
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Employment data came out on a Wednesday, which is atypical, so he treats it as "exploration mode" and does not rush to trade. He thinks no one should trade ahead of 8:30 data, normally Friday or today; wait until the opening bell (9:30) to mitigate unrealistic risk. Reasons: limit orders around NFP usually don't fill, market orders give heavy slippage; much of the NFP activity is lottery trading. After the open, watch whether the market gives reasons to participate; if not, "take a step back" and wait for the afternoon session, or do nothing ("always an option"). He used the session to see whether a 10:00 silver bullet, lunch hour trade or PM session was warranted.

### Parent and subordinate time frames  (aliases: 1m parent, 15s/5s children)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The 1-minute chart is the parent; the 15-second and 5-second charts are subordinate and hyperactive. While price is consolidating inside the prior candle's range on the 1m, he ignores the lower time frames. When the 1m starts to move and displace, the lower time frames become useful; he also checks the small charts for unruly gaps/inefficiencies left open ("I have to babysit them"). With smaller time frames you get precision and support for an idea.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:24](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1344) — ignore lower TFs while consolidating; [0:23:10](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1390) — parent/child analogy

### High resistance on the 1m: "bar coding"  (aliases: picket fence, UPC)
- **Kind:** definition / application
- **What ICT says:** Test: is the candle you are in sharing the same space as the previous candle(s)? If yes, high resistance liquidity run conditions (inside bars "bar coding" like a UPC barcode or picket fence). He can trade targets in this condition but dislikes it; it is hard on a new trader's patience; you can't move to break-even quickly without being stopped out; he'd rather share when the market is ready to rip to the target. In "clown world" (corruption/volatility) conditions, the risk is astronomical so be even more careful.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:46](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2026) — candle inside prior candle = high resistance; [0:34:34](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2074) — can trade but prefers not; [0:35:22](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2122) — skittish moves, can't rush BE

### Suspension Block (daily) and its gradient levels
- **Kind:** definition (applied), possibly new coinage
- **What ICT says:** On the daily chart, a three-candle structure: candle 1 Feb 3, candle 2 Feb 4, candle 3 Feb 5 (last Thursday); there is a volume imbalance at the top and another at the low, so the middle candle is suspended between them. Toggle the chart's volume-imbalance setting (the Settings tab) to see both imbalances; if your toggle differs you won't see the lower volume imbalance and his commentary will confuse you. When a toggle gives two on the same imbalance he favours that one. He then lays the fib from the highest high to the low of the suspension block to draw gradient levels (the horizontal lines) which he extends to the right.
- **How to use:** Gradient levels of the daily suspension block act as targets and as places where he expects FVGs to form; price traded just above the block's top at the open and then moved down through the gradient levels.
- **Example in this video:** Orange shaded area on the 1m chart = last Wednesday's suspension block (as he speaks, the daily block is Feb 3-5); price used the suspension block high, then explored lower and traded down to more gradient levels late in the video.

### Valid FVGs touch a gradient level inside a defined range
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He anticipates inefficiencies forming in close proximity to key gradient levels; "high probability" FVGs have some form of the gap touching a gradient level, and he trades them as targets, stop reference, partials or entries. "Not all fair value gaps are created equal": a gap by label alone (three candles with a gap) is not enough; the valid one lays over and touches a gradient level inside a defined range. ### Pre-session buy-side, 9:30 opening price and first presented FVG
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Pre-session = anything before 9:30. At 9:30 price runs pre-session high, and the 9:30 opening price is a very sensitive level (annotate it). From 9:31 onward, anticipate any FVG that displaces in the direction you expect price to gravitate. The "first presentation" of an FVG after the open is key; it has to displace toward the target. Price took out a low and closed below it, left the buy-side pool that had absorbed buy stops: "smart money is short here". Entry: the bearish breaker (down-close candle between the high, the low and the higher high) and the opening price; the first presented FVG above; overshoot is allowed ("color outside the lines"). After the sell-off, wait through high resistance conditions; once price moves lower, drop to the lower time frames for inefficiencies, e.g. trade to one, sell off, use the daily institutional block's gradient levels.
- **How to use:** Short into the first presented FVG/breaker with the half-gap as the target. A turtle soup at the high is the context. Use gradient levels as stops/partials.

### Smart-money entry, two-stage offset distribution, and the implied dealing range
- **Kind:** definition / model of order flow
- **What ICT says:** Buy stops above the high are buyers entering on a breakout or protecting shorts; smart money sells into them (counterparty) and "will give the liquidity to the pending buy orders". Retail trades patterns (flags, pennants, head and shoulders, trend lines); smart money sees the run as an entry then targets retail longs: those longs protect with sell stops, and when price trades there the stop becomes a market sell, creating liquidity for smart money to buy back cheaply. Smart money buys where others sell at a discount (retail sells at a discount to professionals "all day long"). Offset distribution happens in two places: stage 1 at discount inefficiencies (an FVG below) and the half gap/sell-side pool (partials, banking profit); stage 2 further down at the next sell-side pool (here below the employment-data low and other old lows), where they buy back what remains. The range from the high (entry) to the final low is the implied dealing range.
- **How to identify:** Mark the entry high, the first sell-side liquidity (half gap and SSL pool), and the second pool (old low / data low).
- **How to use:** Frame trades as selling above the high and targeting the first pool for partials, then the second; "you don't need depth of market, book maps or liquidity maps": know the time of day and the old highs/lows.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:51](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2511) — smart money collected buy stops; [0:42:40](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2560) — handshake with breakout buyers; [0:43:26](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2606) — two places to offset: discount inefficiencies; [0:46:43](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2803) — sell stops become market orders; [0:47:31](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2851) — implied dealing range and partials; [0:49:13](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2953) — two stages of offset distribution

### Wick midpoint (half gap) as an immediate rebalance
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Half-gap theory also works on a wick, a half range, or a half order block: it helps define bullish/bearish during a run. If price can't leave a body above the midpoint of a wick when trading up into it, the market is bearish ("extremely heavy"). Immediate response is powerful: the next candle after the hit should open and become aggressively weaker.
- **Example in this video:** Late in the session: price traded up into a wick; the wick's CE was 342.50 and the high was 342.75 (one tick off); it then made a small gap and broke down lower to more gradient levels of the suspension block.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Don't trade ahead of 8:30 NFP; consider participation only after 9:30 (or the afternoon).
- Fib from previous RTH settlement to the 9:30 open; half gap ~70 percent within the first 30 minutes (his figure, verify yourself).
- Ideal opening gap: 100+ handles (about 50 handles to the half).
- 15-20 handle runs are acceptable targets for beginners ("does it afford you 20 handles? 15?").
- Opening range = 9:30-10:00 ET.
- Parent chart 1m; lower time frames only when the 1m displaces.
- Valid FVG must touch a gradient level within a defined range.
- Write opening range gap levels manually using the last three digits.
- Keep the chart clean: anchor to the candle open and remove excess gradient lines.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Observation is the learning drill: when you see price deliver, you should feel "that's neat", not "I wish I had shorted"; don't tie observation to speculation or profit-seeking, eval-passing or payouts (0:36:11-0:37:51).
- Don't copy his live commentary into your account (you learn codependence and attach stimuli/outcome to the first showing); do your own annotations, don't just use screenshots.
- Keep size small; he uses micros for commentary, mini volatility is too high.
- The goal is to keep money first; it's more prudent to sit still and get more information than to chase every data day.
- Test claims on your own data (e.g. 70 percent).
- He uses the lunch hour/10:00 silver bullet/afternoon only if the market proves its behavior.

## Glossary candidates
`HALF GAP` — 50 percent (CE) of the RTH opening range gap; the usual draw.
`PRE-SESSION` — everything before the 9:30 open.
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — daily three-candle structure with volume imbalances above and below; fib across it gives gradient levels (details caption unclear).
`GRADIENT LEVELS` — fib levels drawn across a PD array; FVGs form on them.
`OFFSET DISTRIBUTION (stage 1 / stage 2)` — where smart money takes profit/buys back (discount FVG, then next sell-side pool).
`IMPLIED DEALING RANGE` — range from smart-money entry high to the final liquidity pool.
`BAR CODING` — string of inside candles; high resistance.
