## Summary
ICT reviews Monday on NQ against a daily BISI (grayed zone) that he had told followers price would be drawn down into. He grades the array with fib levels (octants/quadrants, CE as midpoint) and shows how the day's price action keeps keying off those lines, including the new week opening gap. He then revisits his "7:00–9:00 am" pre-market rule: whether that two-hour window is consolidating (and merely running liquidity at its edges) or trending predicts a big one-directional move at 9:30. He admits he missed the short because the market fell "like an elevator" at the open, but uses the rest of the day to show how a 10:00 Silver Bullet first presented FVG, a bullish iFVG, and order blocks are validated by being "tethered" to graded octants of the daily array. Ends with a NDOG mark at the 6 pm open, a seasonal-tendency note, and a Jackson Hole caution.

### Daily BISI graded with fib levels; wick CE close as the confirmation to go down  (aliases: buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency, consequent encroachment, volume imbalance between bodies)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - He had told followers price was likely to draw down into this daily BISI (grayed in the background; compare the white of the price axis side).
  - First thing to check on the way in: did price close below the midpoint (CE) of the big wick above? It did ("we got it right there"), so "we knew last Thursday that likely it was going to go down" (Thursday Aug 20).
  - Next order of business: the BISI and the volume imbalance between two bodies that are separated, "even though there's wicks connecting the two candlesticks. The bodies are telling us useful information."
  - The market had cleared the highs, then the SIBI and the CE of the wick above, then failed to show willingness to go higher and displaced sharply lower; "nothing up here is smooth"; there are several pockets of inefficiency (two volume imbalances and the remaining part of the BISI).
  - He draws the fib from the high of the array down to the low of the volume imbalance (that candle's close) and lets the horizontal lines act as magnets: "Watch how price gravitates to them."
- **How to identify:** daily BISI = gap-type array; grade from the high down to the low of the volume imbalance; levels: CE (midpoint), octant just above/below CE, quadrant lines (0.75, 0.25), upper/lower octants.
- **How to use:** a daily close below a wick's midpoint is the go-down trigger; once inside the array, price steps between its graded lines.
- **Example in this video:** Monday session on NQ: price opened Sunday 6 pm, dropped, rallied up into a quadrant level of the BISI, used the upper half of CE, rallied to an octant; later (London) dropped aggressively to CE with a volume imbalance, then an octant below CE, and right back up through CE.

### The 7:00–9:00 am ET consolidation rule (pre-market)  (aliases: "seven to nine", pre-market range, 7-9 rule)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Mark 7:00 am ET and examine 7:00–9:00: whether price consolidates or trends over that window "tells you how the morning session's going to move". He says nobody else teaches this and it is "the rules".
  - If the window consolidates, expect big one-directional movement at 9:30 ET; the direction comes from the higher-timeframe narrative (here the daily BISI and the seasonal tendency toward lower prices, which he had told followers on Saturday's space).
  - Distinguish a pop from a trend: in the example price ran up for about 40 minutes at the start of the window only to take the buy side out ("just running liquidity, that's not a trend"), then dropped back inside the low-high range and gyrated around its middle: "not being allowed to protract. So, it's consolidating."
  - Read the bodies, not the wicks: smooth = where the bodies are; jagged = where the wicks are; "the wick is where the damage is allowed to be done". The range's liquidity is at the extreme of the bodies. Expect price to approach 9:30, clear the bodies on the high side (buy-side liquidity of the opening minutes) and "flutter and run above" in the first minutes of the opening range.
  - Once price is above the CE of a prior wick, that wick is no longer a factor; the normal bearish rule (stay in the lower half of the wick) does not apply when you expect the bodies' buy side to be cleared first.
- **How to identify:** (1) mark 7:00; (2) grade the 7:00–9:00 range; (3) is it consolidating around its middle (no protraction) or a trend; (4) locate the extreme of the bodies as the liquidity.
- **How to use:** consolidation in the 7–9 range + a daily bias → plan for a one-directional move at 9:30 and look to enter on the setup that follows (here a short after clearing the buy side).
- **Example in this video:** Monday Aug 24, 7:00–9:00 NQ: a ~40 minute run up took the buy side, then a drop back inside and a rotation around the midpoint; 9:30 then produced a very fast fall out of the open (he missed it).

### FVG validity filter: candle three tethered to an octant  (aliases: useful FVG)
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage
- **What ICT says:** A fair value gap is "valid" and "useful" when its third candle (candle three; candles one-two-three) is tethered to an octant line of the daily array: "Candlestick three is tethered to that octant. That validates this as a fair value gap." He used it after price bounced into the bullish FVG and looked for the draw up to the high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:49](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=829)
- **Nuance / caveats:** Not in his classical three-candle definition; treat as a filter for HTF-aligned gaps. He does not say whether a gap not tethered is invalid or merely lower probability.

### Silver Bullet at 10:00: first presented FVG (SIBI), then bullish iFVG  (aliases: Silver Bullet, first presented FVG)
- **Kind:** entry model (example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He sets a rectangle for 10:00 to 10:30 ("for the sake of marking out enough time") and hunts the 10:00 Silver Bullet. At 10:00 the first presented FVG is a SIBI (bearish).
  - "While the market is not allowed to go lower": price tried to sell off using the SIBI as a bearish FVG, took out the swing low and bodies, then came back above it. Because price closed back above the bearish gap it became a bullish inversion FVG.
  - He notes the run lower counts as a run below even though the wick was not taken out: "the wick is allowed to do the damage"; the volume you need is "all in the candlestick".
  - After the iFVG, price rallied toward the high of the SIBI/its low draw ("look for it to trade up into this high because we have the low of this SIBI right here").
  - Subsequent path: consolidates, trades back down to quadrant 0.75, accumulates, rallies to the next octant, consolidates, trades into the order block, rallies to the low of the SIBI, then into a small gap; later hits the upper quadrant of the daily BISI and sells off sharply.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 7:00–9:00 am ET: trending vs consolidating decides how 9:30 behaves; a pop that merely takes buy side is not a trend.
- Bodies define smoothness and the liquidity extreme; wicks are where "damage is allowed".
- Daily close below the midpoint of a wick = the confirmation to expect lower prices.
- FVG valid when candle three is tethered to an octant; order block valid when anchored to an octant.
- Silver Bullet hunted at 10:00 (marked 10:00–10:30 on the chart).
- Grade the daily BISI from the high to the low of the volume imbalance: CE, octants, quadrants (0.75 level mentioned).
- Jackson Hole week: do not over-leverage.
- NWOG/NDOG marked each time: NDOG at the 6:00 pm open, NWOG from the Sunday 6:00 pm open.

## Glossary candidates
`Tethered (to an octant)` — a candle/array anchored on a graded octant line of a higher-timeframe array, which validates it.
`7–9 rule` — the 7:00–9:00 am ET trending/consolidation read that previews 9:30 behavior.
`Volume imbalance (between bodies)` — separated bodies, ignoring connecting wicks.
`Elevator` — his term for a fast, unbroken fall that gives no entry.
