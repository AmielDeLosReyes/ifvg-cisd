## Concepts

### 7:00–9:00 am ET rule: trending 7–9 means a consolidating AM session  (aliases: 7–9 rule, pre-market session rule)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Make sure charts are on New York (Eastern) time. Mark 7:00 am and 9:00 am with vertical lines; take the highest high and lowest low between them.
  - "The algorithm will absolutely go into a consolidation during the AM session if 7 to 9 is trending." The AM session here is 9:30–11:30; it will likely go sideways, frustrate and chop traders; inexperienced traders complain.
  - "If you're going to be trading morning session, you remove 75% of the adversity if you understand this simple principle."
  - The probability of a hard market (consolidation) increases further if price trades to a key level during 7–9. Here the daily key level was the August 6 low (29,241.25); 7–9 hit 29,240.00 ("modestly below it, but it still got there"), met a daily objective while trending, and no later candle in the window went lower ("it stopped right there on the lowest candle").
  - These lessons are found in the "long dry portions" of his lectures: take notes of rules, caveats and "quirky little things" that tell you when to dismiss something.
- **How to identify:** 7–9 high and low; is price progressing in one direction (trend) or rotating around the middle (consolidation); did it tag a key HTF level.
- **How to use:** trending 7–9 + key-level tag → plan for a sideways AM; trade smaller or wait for traps; do not demand a trend.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Sept, 7–9 low 29,240.00 at the Aug 6 daily low.

### 7–9 range equilibrium and octant sensitivity (bodies cannot touch the 0.625 octant in premium)  (aliases: premium sensitivity, discount sensitivity)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the 7–9 window closes, the equilibrium of the 7–9 range (the 50% line) tells you where premium is: price above it is "absolutely in a premium".
  - Price rallied to the 0.625 octant (caption "625 octant") of the 7–9 range, but the bodies could not touch or lay down on it while above in premium: "the algorithm can't hide it; it's simply going to be visible in the price delivery." Market then "breaks down aggressively".
  - Premium sensitivity = the lower half of a PD array when bearish (price should be capped/supported below it); when bullish, the upper half is the discount sensitivity. Wicks are allowed to do the damage; the bodies say "absolutely not, can't go up here".
  - Later a SIBI on the intraday chart: price rallied into the gap, threw a wick up, and left bodies in the lower half: "exactly what you want to see with real order flow, algorithmic order flow."

### Waiting for traps on a consolidation day; unfinished business at a shallow low  (aliases: soft bias, traps)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**

  - What a trap looks like here: an inefficiency above (a SIBI), the market rallies into premium to take highs (relative equal highs) and run buy stops/shorts' stops, drawing breakout buyers in (their stops sit right below the low), then breaks lower.
  - He holds a "soft bias" for lower prices because of the unfinished business below, while expecting consolidation; he would prefer the Aug 6 low be taken "more convincingly" but says "today's just not a day that you want to be pushing it".
  - Mechanics of the day: after the 9:30 open, price rallied to premium, gave indication that order flow was not continuing higher, broke lower, and consolidated around the 9:30 opening price.

- Tagging a key daily level during 7–9 raises the odds of a hard consolidation day.

- Premium = above the 7–9 range's equilibrium; bodies cannot overcome the 0.625 octant in premium.

- Event horizon = midpoint between two lows (target).
- Keep the 9:30 opening price visible.

## Glossary candidates
`7–9 rule` — 7:00–9:00 am ET trend/consolidation read predicting the AM session.
`Premium sensitivity` — lower half of a bearish PD array; bodies should not trade above its sensitive level.
`Discount sensitivity` — upper half of a bullish PD array.
`Event horizon` — midpoint between two lows (target) in this usage.
`Traps` — setups where price lures breakout traders before reversing, awaited on consolidation days.
