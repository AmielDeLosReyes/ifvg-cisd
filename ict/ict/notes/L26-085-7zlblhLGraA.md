## Concepts

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) as Entry Foothold
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** From prior structure (earlier in the day or prior day), a bullish FVG was formed. Now approaching from below, that same gap acts as a foothold to climb higher. Price will use the low / consequent encroachment (the "in" point of the gap) as a launch point to spring up and treat the gap as resistance on rallies.
- **How to identify:** Mark the prior bullish FVG; as price approaches from below, watch for a candlestick body that touches the gap; that touch is your confirmation that the iFVG is acting as a foothold.
- **How to use:** Buy side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency → if price comes down and touches the low of that gap, expect it to spring higher. Entry is at that touch point or just inside the gap.
- **Example in this video:** ES final hour. Price came down into a buy-side imbalance (from earlier structure), touched it, then sprang up. Treated the gap low as a foothold and climbed higher. Entry at that foothold; target was relative equal highs above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:50](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=110) — buy-side imbalance identified · [0:03:11](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=191) — iFVG explained as foothold · [0:04:05](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=245) — treating gap as foothold on climb
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The iFVG must be approached from the correct direction (below) to act as a foothold; arrival from above would be different.

### Relative Equal Highs as Targets
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** Throughout the day, price creates multiple highs at similar levels (or with small wicks near the same level). These become targets on up moves. Use relative equal highs as accumulation and scaling points.
- **How to identify:** Mark intraday swing highs; if multiple candles top out near the same price, that level is a relative equal high.
- **How to use:** On up moves, target relative equal highs. Scale out at each equal high. Use them as reference for where price "wants" to reach.
- **Example in this video:** ES final hour. Multiple prior highs near 3400, 3450, 3475, 3500 area. Used each as a scale-out point. Price attacked these "clean highs" repeatedly.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=53) — relative equal highs as targets · [0:05:12](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=312) — accumulating toward highs · [0:13:16](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=796) — clean highs to target
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Clean highs" do not need to be exact twins; even relative equal highs within a few ticks are valid targets.

### Macro Windows (3:15–3:45 PM ET)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The 3:15–3:45 PM ET window is a "macro" (algorithmic trading window) where the market often produces clean, tradeable price action. This is one of the known killzones / algo windows. During this window, expect strong directional moves with less noise, spoof, or whipsaw.
- **How to identify:** Clock time: 3:15 PM to 3:45 PM ET on a 1m or 5m chart.
- **How to use:** During the macro window, focus on accumulating in foothold areas and targeting the higher / lower levels with conviction. The action is usually clean during this window.
- **Example in this video:** ES final hour, 3:15–3:45 PM. ICT notes that price "came alive" at 3:30 and the move was "brilliantly implemented." The clean action matched expectations for the macro window. Price did not go lower after a certain point (respecting support structure) and ripped higher within the window.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:03](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=423) — macro terminology · [0:14:18](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=858) — 3:15–3:45 macro confirmation · [0:18:09](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=1089) — macro window performance
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT notes that people misusing the term "macro" are trading at wrong times (e.g., 10:40 is not a macro); also cautions that the 3:15–3:45 window is specifically for the final hour of RTH and is algo-driven.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) as a Stop Point
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** In order blocks and FVGs, CE is the boundary. On upside accumulation, if you are going to take risk via a stop loss, place it just below the consequent encroachment of a prior PD array or order block. If CE is taken out, the structure is broken and your assumption is wrong.
- **How to identify:** Identify the PD array (FVG, order block); find the body that enters it; the extreme of that body is the CE.
- **How to use:** Place stop loss just below CE. As price moves up, raise the stop to just below the next PD array's CE. Trail stops up the chain of PD arrays.
- **Example in this video:** ES. Entry at buy-side imbalance; stop placed below the consequent encroachment of that order block. As price climbed and formed new PD arrays, stops were raised to just below the CE of each new level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=53) — stop placement below low (CE) · [0:04:05](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=245) — raise stop up to reduce risk · [0:15:06](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=906) — CE as foothold
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### Order Block & PD Array Accumulation
- **Kind:** rule + execution
- **What ICT says:** After identifying a PD array (order block, FVG), price often returns to it and bounces / accumulates inside it. Use the order block as a "footing" from which to scale positions. Add to longs at each subsequent order block; trail stops tighter.
- **How to identify:** Mark order blocks (reversal candles, supply/demand zones); watch for price to return to them.
- **How to use:** On up moves, add (scale up) at each order block; on down moves, add shorts at each order block. Scale out at targets (relative equal highs/lows).
- **Example in this video:** ES. Price came down into order block / FVG, accumulated there (body stayed inside, small candles), then sprung up. As it climbed, it touched other order blocks and accumulation points, each used as a scale-up point.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:05](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=305) — added inside gap and order block · [0:08:48](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=528) — accumulating, then snap up
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Order blocks may have small gaps (volume imbalances) inside them; these are valid accumulation zones.

### Spoof Identification & Market Conditions
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Spoof candles are artificial price moves (likely market maker manipulation) that do not follow natural order flow. They appear as sudden wicks or bodies that reverse sharply without reason. On certain days ("lazy afternoons"), spoofs are prevalent. Recognize them and trade around them.
- **How to identify:** Look for candles with exaggerated wicks that reverse immediately or bodies that gap and reverse without trading through order blocks naturally.
- **How to use:** When you see a spoof, do not react; let the market settle. Use the spoof to your advantage: if price spoof-wicked into a stop, it will likely bounce; if you're short and see a spoof wick up, expect it to reverse down.
- **Example in this video:** ES final hour. ICT notes "lazy afternoon," sees several spoofs / artificial highs, and comments "anytime spoofs" (meaning the spoofing was obvious). He hopes the summer does not continue this pattern.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:57](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=657) — lethargic, slow price action · [0:11:57](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=717) — spoof recognition "too clean" · [0:18:09](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=1089) — "spooze" and macro window interaction
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Too clean" price action can indicate spoofing or algorithmic manipulation; natural order flow has more friction.

### Twin Candle Highs / Lows as Liquidity Targets
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When two candlesticks in a row have the same high (or within 1–2 ticks), that is a "twin candle high" and represents a very small, specific liquidity level. These are targets for market makers to run stops and sweep liquidity. Do not mistake these for larger relative equal highs; they are micro-level targets.
- **How to identify:** Look at consecutive candles; if two in a row share the same high/low, mark it as a twin high/low.
- **How to use:** Twin highs are often hit briefly then reversed; use them as quick scale points. On short-term moves (final hour), these are valid targets.
- **Example in this video:** ES. Multiple twin candle highs were hit during the final hour; each was a small liquidity target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:55](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=1135) — twin candle highs on 15s chart · [0:19:46](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=1186) — not requiring exact match; relative equal highs suffice
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Do not "demand" exact matching; even relative equal highs within small range are valid.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 3:15–3:45 PM ET is a macro window (algorithmic); expect cleaner action.
- Stop loss: place just below consequent encroachment of order block / PD array.
- Raise stops as price climbs; trail them up the chain of order blocks.
- Entry: at buy-side imbalance / order block foothold.
- Targets: relative equal highs (or highs from prior session/day).
- Scale out at each target; do not hold all the way.
- Market replay data cannot be used for tape reading; must be live data.
- Spoof candles: recognize and trade around them; do not panic.
- Twin candle highs/lows: micro liquidity targets; valid on 1m and 5m.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Positioning:** Start with a small entry at the foothold; add (scale up) on subsequent bounces / order blocks; scale out at targets.
- **Time management:** Final hour is often cleaner; focus energy there.
- **Adaptation:** Adapt to market conditions; if day is "lazy" and spoofed, trade smaller and use micro targets.
- **Experience:** ICT notes "a little rust" from time away, misspoke on one order block analysis, then corrected himself mid-stream. Recovery and self-awareness are key.
- **Market quality:** "Lazy afternoon" = lower-quality trading day; "certainly tradeable, but there are certainly better days to be had." Know when to reduce size.
- **Macro trading:** ES used to be ICT's market when younger; later found Nasdaq "more loose" (exaggerated moves). Different markets suit different traders.

## Glossary candidates
`Macro window` — an algorithmic trading window (e.g., 3:15–3:45 PM ET) where clean, predictable price action often occurs; also called a "killzone."
`Foothold` — a price level (order block, FVG low, etc.) where price accumulates and launches higher; used as an entry or scale point.
`Twin candle high/low` — two consecutive candles with the same or nearly identical high/low; a micro-level liquidity target.
`Spoof` — an artificial candlestick or wick created by market maker manipulation; appears exaggerated or illogical given order flow.
`Accumulation` — when price moves slowly / sideways inside an order block or PD array, gathering liquidity before the next move; a signal to add positions.
