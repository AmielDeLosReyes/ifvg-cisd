## Concepts

### Suspension Block (vs Regular Order Block)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A suspension block is an order block with a volume imbalance at its END (bottom for down-close, top for up-close). The distinguishing feature: bodies don't touch across the gap (separation exists between candlesticks). This creates a "suspension"—the inefficiency is unmet. When price tests this level later, it's a strong reference because the gap was NEVER filled before.
- **How to identify:** Down-close order block. After it forms, look at the gap between its low and the next candle's high. If there's separation (bodies don't touch), it's a suspension block. Mark the gap.
- **How to use:** Suspension blocks act similarly to regular order blocks BUT have higher probability because they show unmet inefficiency over a longer period. Use as strong support (for longs) or resistance (for shorts). Stop loss should respect this level carefully.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq daily: Suspension block identified from prior candlesticks. Price later returned to test it, confirming its relevance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:08](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=488) — explaining suspension block structure; [0:09:43](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=583) — showing suspension block on chart
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Suspension blocks are a refinement of order block theory. Not all order blocks are suspension blocks; only those with volume imbalance gaps at their ends.

### Relative Equal Highs/Lows & Liquidity Reversal
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** When price makes a high at approximately the same level as a prior high (within a few ticks), it's a "relative equal high." These are magnets for order flow because they're psychological levels. Price often fails at relative equal highs on retest (lower high = weakening), or uses them as springboards.
- **How to identify:** Mark on chart significant prior highs/lows. When price approaches the same level, note it as relative equal.
- **How to use:** At relative equal highs, expect either: (1) failure and reversal lower, or (2) breakout above. Don't sit at the level; use it to anticipate direction. If bearish bias, shorting at relative equal high is high-probability (shorts cover above, pushing it down).
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq overnight session 7:00 a.m. ET: relative equal highs from prior trading. Price traded to them, failed, reversed lower. This is the draw used for the day's trading plan.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:07](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=727) — "relative equal highs... why is this important?"; [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=822) — showing how market used this level
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Relative equal highs are psychological. They're not perfectly precise levels; use them with inefficiencies (FVGs, order blocks) nearby for confirmation.

### Gradient Levels (Fibonacci/Octant Subdivisions on Wicks)
- **Kind:** definition / rule / how to use
- **What ICT says:** Gradient levels = Fibonacci or eighth (octant) subdivisions of a wick or range. When a wick extends above/below a candle's body, the gradient levels show algorithmic precision. Price often respects these sub-divisions to the tick.
- **How to identify:** Identify a wick (highest/lowest point of a candle that the body didn't touch). Apply Fibonacci or eighths from the body's extreme to the wick's extreme. Mark these gradient levels.
- **How to use:** Entry or target placement using gradient levels shows precision and reduces slippage. Targets: consequent encroachment (CE/50%) or octant levels (12.5%, 25%, 37.5%, etc.). This ties to his teaching on "octant levels from RTH ORG" = same concept applied to intraday wicks.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq 1-minute: wick from low to high has gradient levels. Price later returns and uses these levels; bodies stop at 25% or 50% of the wick, showing algorithmic behavior.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=822) — "Measure that. Consequent encroachment, it hits that"; [0:16:05](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=965) — demonstrating gradient level precision
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Gradient levels are tools for CONFIRMATION, not primary signals. Combine with directional bias (SMT, macro, geopolitical) for better odds.

### Model 2022 & Market Structure Shift

Involves a shift from one order flow direction to another, marked by specific candlestick patterns (e.g., down-close reversal candle, then up-close impulse). - **How to identify:** Candlestick pattern: first a reversal candle (down-close in uptrend), then confirmation candle (up-close). The model 2022 represents the TURN from sellers to buyers.
- **How to use:** Entry: once the model is confirmed, trade in the new direction. Stop loss: below the reversal candle. Target: trace to next liquidity above.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) on Intraday Charts
- **Kind:** entry model / example only
- **What ICT says:** An iFVG appears when a candlestick doesn't trade into the opposite direction. Example: a down-close candle leaves a gap below, but price doesn't come back to fill it immediately. Later, price approaches the iFVG from above and uses it as support (iFVG acting like a buy signal).
- **How to identify:** Candlestick with upper gap unfilled (bullish). Note the iFVG level.
- **How to use:** Entry: when price tests the iFVG from above and bounces, go long. Stop: below the iFVG. Target: prior high or next inefficiency.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute Nasdaq: iFVG identified from earlier candlestick. Price came down, touched the iFVG, bounced = long opportunity.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:05](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=965) — showing iFVG as potential entry; [0:16:53](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=1013) — price using iFVG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** iFVG on intraday timeframes is similar to daily iFVG but with tighter targets and faster exits. Ideal for scalping one micro contract.

### Range-Bound Market Strategy
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** In range-bound conditions (consolidation), price will use inefficiencies and stay within the range rather than trend. Do NOT expect breakout continuation. Use inefficiencies as reversal points. If price breaks range, market is shifting to trending mode (high resistance → low resistance or vice versa).
- **How to identify:** Price oscillating between two levels for multiple days/candles without trending directionally. Inefficiencies within the range are being used repeatedly.
- **How to use:** Scalp inefficiencies inside the range. Short highs, buy lows. Don't hold across range breaks. If range breaks on a close, shift to trend-following bias.
- **Example in this video:** S&P 500: currently in heavy consolidation. He would NOT trade ES (too messy), but would trade Nasdaq (slightly better structure).
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:29](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=1109) — "ES is literally a mess. Like, there's nothing in here. I would not touch this."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Range-bound trading is LOW probability for macro directional trades but HIGH probability for scalp entries. Use tight stops and small position sizes.

### "Unicorn Price Run" & Smart Money Reversal
- **Kind:** definition / example only
- **What ICT says:** A "unicorn price run" is his term for a specific, near-perfect impulsive move where price doesn't touch any inefficiencies or levels until it reaches the target. It's a clean, uninterrupted move—rare and beautiful. Smart money reversal = the moment distribution reverses to accumulation (or vice versa), marked by a sharp candlestick reversal.
- **How to identify:** After smart money reversal candle, price starts running without stopping.
- **How to use:** Once smart money reversal is identified (candlestick closing opposite prior trend), anticipate unicorn run. Don't try to catch every piece; just ride the move to next major level.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq 1-minute: after reversal, price had a "unicorn run" up to the target (buy-side inefficiency). The move was clean and didn't pause.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:53](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=1013) — "There's your unicorn price run"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Unicorn runs are rare and only possible when smart money is REALLY pushing. In choppy markets, they don't happen. This is aspirational trading—if you see conditions, take it; if not, don't force it.
