## Summary
Introductory video to the free 2022 YouTube mentorship. ICT explains that he will teach a deliberately "stripped down" single trading model (designed so his daughter could follow it) to avoid the analysis paralysis his full content causes, delivered mainly through index futures (ES/NQ) on TradingView, with a live TD Ameritrade account replacing his "demo baller" demo-account teaching. He frames the course as three stages (yearner, learner, earner), warns that he will point out where price is likely to draw but that these are study prompts, not trade signals, and sets the realistic goal of one good setup a week / about 25 handles a week in ES. The only chart content is a 2-minute ES March 2022 contract showing shorts and a long using optimal trade entry targeting buy stops above a high. Its value is in setting the mindset, the "precise on bias, forgiving on entry" principle, and the futures contract sizing (minis vs micros vs CFDs).

## Concepts
### 2022 Mentorship model (framing)  (aliases ICT uses: "stripped down version", "stripping it down to its chrome", "a model my daughter could look at")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The 2022 YouTube mentorship will teach one simple model with few moving parts, no "bells and whistles" or advanced theories, keeping everything germane to that model. His advanced private-mentorship concepts create exceptional traders but most students get bogged down and suffer analysis paralysis ("kid in the candy store"). The model is meant to show both what to do and when NOT to do something.
- **How to identify:** n/a (course framing).
- **How to use:** Learn one model and its setups; setups repeat with common characteristics but are never carbon copies.
- **Example in this video:** None beyond the ES live-account trades below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:38](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=158) — built for his daughter, minimal moving parts; [0:03:26](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=206) — advanced content causes analysis paralysis; [0:04:12](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=252) — stripped-down version, mentorship style, traded with index futures.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Explicitly narrower than his private mentorship content; he says he will also show how to scale the concepts to any timeframe and that forex traders can still use them.

### Draw on liquidity — study prompts, not trade setups  (aliases ICT uses: "where price will likely draw to next")
- **Kind:** rule

Students must not grow dependent on him calling moves; independent decision-making is the goal so results (good or bad) are owned by the student.

### Buy-side / sell-side liquidity (liquidity matrix)  (aliases ICT uses: "buy stops", "liquidity matrix")
- **Kind:** definition (brief)
- **What ICT says:** Markets work within a liquidity matrix: buy side and sell side. His charts stay simple: a line for a level he wants to see breached, and a line for a level he wants price to reach up into. Buy stops sit above a high.
- **How to identify:** Highs (buy stops above) and lows (sell stops below) marked with simple lines; avoid "chart graffiti".
- **How to use:** Target the resting liquidity (e.g. buy stops above a high for a long).
- **Example in this video:** ES 2m long targeting buy stops above a prior high (see OTE concept).
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:11](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1451) — above this high is buy stops, targeting into that; [0:26:33](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1593) — simple lines; liquidity matrix of buy side and sell side.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** To be taught in detail later in the series.

### Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) — "forgiving entries"
- **Kind:** example only / rule
- **What ICT says:** He calls the long shown a "very very simple strategy, optimal trade entry". You do not need to be highly precise about entries; you need to be precise about directional bias and where price is reaching for. He will teach "forgiving entries".
- **How to identify:** Not defined in this video.
- **How to use:** Prioritize bias and draw on liquidity over pinpoint entry price.
- **Example in this video:** ES (E-mini S&P 500, March 2022 contract), 2-minute chart, live TD Ameritrade account: two shorts the previous session (entry and cover shown, plus a small scalp), no further trades after; then a long entry later, exit into buy stops above a high. Only a few handles captured. Account shown at about $23,694.11.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:33](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1353) — ES March 2022, 2m chart, short entries/covers; [0:23:22](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1402) — long setup "one of the setups you're going to learn"; [0:24:11](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1451) — OTE, forgiving entries, precise bias.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Entry/exit prices are not stated in the transcript (only pointed at on chart).

### Micro vs mini / futures vs CFDs (contract specs)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** ES handle = 4 ticks (e.g. 4567 to 4568); $50 per handle, $12.50 per tick on the E-mini S&P. Margin for one ES contract roughly $12,000–13,000 (his estimate). Micros (MES, also micro Nasdaq) pay $5 per handle with much lower margin, so you can graduate up over time. Outside the US you could trade a CFD (e.g. US500 via MT4), but for US traders this is illegal because it does not go through a regulated exchange; US traders must use futures.
- **How to identify:** n/a.
- **How to use:** If margin prevents trading ES, trade micros; scale up later.
- **Example in this video:** ES tick/handle math.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:11](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1451) — handle = 4 ticks, $50/handle, $12.50/tick; [0:24:58](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1498) — 25 handles; micros $5/handle; [0:25:46](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1546) — CFDs, illegal for US traders.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Margin figure is approximate ("12 13,000 or whatever it is").

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Teaching instrument: index futures (ES, NQ); forex still applicable.
- ES: 1 handle = 4 ticks = $50; 1 tick = $12.50. Micros: $5/handle.
- Target framing: ~25 handles per week in ES; at least one good setup per week.
- Be precise about directional bias and draw; entries can be forgiving.
- Levels he marks in advance are study material, not trade signals.
- US traders: futures only (CFDs not legal via unregulated venues).
- Platform: TradingView for teaching; live results via TD Ameritrade (regulated by the CFTC); he avoids MT4 for showing results because it can be faked/edited.
- Bar replay shows only completed candles, not the live open-to-close formation; he records entries live, not on replay.
- Schedule: first episode Friday, then Tuesday/Thursday uploads; no content in March 2022; series about 3–4 months; PDFs/assignments via community tab.
- He uses limit and market orders to enter and closes ("collapses") the trade at a target of his choice.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Personal responsibility is paramount: if you succeed you earned it; if you fail you own it ([0:17:47](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1067)).
- Don't use "live ammunition" before you are properly trained; the tools have sharp edges ([0:04:12](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=252)).
- Do the drills and homework; some students fail because they shortcut or don't follow instructions; if you can't watch live, review afterward ([0:10:31](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=631), [0:11:19](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=679)).
- Live vs demo: in a live account every tick matters; making money can be worse than losing because it creates the urge for "another taste", leading to over-trading and over-leveraging ([0:17:00](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1020)).
- Showing very large results early can discourage beginners; he shows modest live results deliberately ([0:15:22](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=922)).
- Not aiming for perfection; setups repeat with similar but not identical characteristics ([0:05:45](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=345)).
- Prefer a broker that offers many asset classes, since some asset classes underperform at certain times of year ([0:19:23](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1163)).
