## Summary
ICT applies the week's new day opening gap (NDOG) theory to the 6 PM ET restart and the Asian session (7–9 PM ET), giving a step-by-step "initial buy-side / initial sell-side liquidity" protocol: mark the 6 PM NDOG, wait for price to leave it, raid a short-term high and a short-term low on either side of it, then trade the 7 PM retrace into the NDOG toward the initial liquidity pool. He breaks down his own Thursday-evening NQ long (entry below the body of a down-close order block, stop below NDOG CE, break-even after the short-term high is taken, exit between the -1 and -1.5 standard-deviation projections of the prior leg). He then shows how those same initial reference levels acted as support/resistance overnight and into the NY open, previews the London opening range (12:00–12:30 AM ET), explains RTH vs ETH charts, the 30-minute 9:30–10:00 opening range and the ~70% "midgap" tendency after a large opening gap, the Friday "TGIF" 20–30% weekly-range retracement, and how he weighs SMT between NQ, ES and YM. The promised turtle soup lecture only got its introduction (history/name) before the stream ended.

## Concepts

### Turtle Soup (name origin & premise)  (aliases: turtle soup, fake out, fade)
- **Kind:** definition (introduction only)
- **What ICT says:** The name comes from Linda Raschke & Larry Connors' book *Street Smarts* (he paid $175 for it in the mid-90s; the book most influenced his understanding of stop hunts). It snubs Richard Dennis's "Turtles" trend-following system, which bought breakouts of the 20-day high (sold below 20-day low) and had a very low strike rate but caught big trends. Turtle soup does the opposite: when price breaks above a 20-day high and fails back into the range, trade the reversal. ICT says he does not just use a 20-day high/low — "there's other things" — and promised to show those characteristics at the end, but the stream ended before he got to it. He calls turtle soup "my version of a reversal pattern": retail resistance gets pegged, then reverses toward the opposite side (discount, and vice versa).
- **How to identify (as applied in this video):** Price trades above a high and immediately goes right back in — "that's your turtle soup, it's a fake out" — as opposed to a strong "nitrous oxide" push through the level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:20](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=320) — announces turtle soup lecture; [0:06:09](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=369) — Richard Dennis, 20-day breakout; [0:07:49](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=469) — Raschke's turtle soup; [0:51:33](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3093) — failure back inside = turtle soup.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Full turtle soup characteristics not delivered in this lecture (stream cut short). He also teased talking about "wicks" — also not delivered.

### NDOG at the 6 PM restart (Asia application)  (aliases: new day opening gap, NDOG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** At 5 PM ET indices take a one-hour break and reopen at 6 PM. NDOG = 5 PM close vs 6 PM open; 6 PM open above the close → it's the NDOG high; below → NDOG low. CE = midpoint. If the gap is over 20 handles, he always has the CE annotated; under 20, he eyeballs the CE and the quadrants.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday Aug 8 evening (for Friday's trading), NQ: the 4:59 PM candle closed at 18,558; 6 PM opened at ~18,550.25 (caption "18550 point2") — a gap lower of less than 10 handles, so just eyeball the middle.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:52](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1192) — 6 PM derivation; [0:20:40](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1240) — 20-handle rule; [0:31:22](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1882) — 18,558 close / 18,550 open; [0:32:12](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1932) — less than 10 handles, eyeball.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Do not trade as soon as the gap is noted — time must be the trigger first.

### Initial buy-side / initial sell-side liquidity (the 6 PM / NDOG protocol)  (aliases: initial BSL, initial SSL, minor buy-side/sell-side liquidity pool, "initial range for building and engineering liquidity")
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry model framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the 6 PM open, you want to see price do two things: (1) move away from the NDOG and take a short-term high (a minor buy-side liquidity pool), then (2) cross back over the NDOG and take a short-term low (a minor sell-side liquidity pool) — or the reverse order. Simply trading below the NDOG isn't enough; a short-term low must be taken.
  - Once both happen, that new high and new low after 6 PM are the **initial buy-side liquidity** and **initial sell-side liquidity** for the day — "the initial high and low that every trader that trades at that moment is going to refer to for where their stop loss is going to be." This visually represents how the algorithm "calibrates" and engineers liquidity.
  - Price "coloring outside the lines" of the NDOG (crossing through it) is expected — it is not a failure of the level.
  - If after 6 PM price "dilly-dallies" and doesn't bump above or below into 7 PM, Asia will likely be dull — don't trade; go to bed.
  - These initial levels later act as the real support/resistance the algorithm refers back to (overnight, London, and even the NY open).
- **How to identify:** Mark NDOG → raid of short-term high → cross back through NDOG → raid of short-term low (or mirror). Label initial BSL small at top-left of the line, initial SSL bottom-left; keep them muted. Then look left for larger pools of liquidity (e.g. relative equal highs).
- **How to use:** At the key time (7 PM for Asia), look for price to retrace into the NDOG while not going back to the opposite initial liquidity; then trade toward the initial liquidity on the other side (and the larger pools beyond it).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Thursday Aug 8 evening: after the 6 PM open price ran above a short-term high (initial BSL), dropped back below the NDOG to take a short-term low (initial SSL), crossed back above the NDOG with displacement before 7 PM. On the 7:00 PM candle it dipped back and touched the NDOG high — did not go near the initial SSL. Relative equal highs and the initial BSL sat above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:16](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2176) — the two things to see; [0:41:06](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2466) — why they're labeled "initial"; [0:41:56](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2516) — algorithm calibrating; stops reference; [0:43:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2612) — dull Asia if no bump either side.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says the same "initial BSL/SSL" logic applies to the 9:30–10:00 opening range and (with the 12:00–12:30 range) to London.

### Asian session window & the 7 PM trigger  (aliases: Asia killzone, Asian session)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Asia key times are 7:00 PM to 9:00 PM New York time — a small window. The algorithm "comes online" at 7 PM; buy/sell programs begin at or just after 7. Markets don't move until it's time — time first, price second. Asia is the least enticing session for him personally, but valid for people who work 9–5 in the US. Same concepts apply to forex. He does not trade crypto.
- **How to use:** Do the 6 PM protocol, then trade the 7 PM reaction; if it isn't there, don't push a button but still study it.

### Asia long — order block entry, stop, management (his live trade)  (aliases: bullish order block, breakaway gap, BISI)
- **Kind:** entry model (worked example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the displacement above the NDOG leaves a BISI, he does not want price to return into it — that makes it a **breakaway gap**. Then he wants momentum/speed through the relative equal highs and initial BSL ("nitrous oxide"), not a push above and right back in.
  - Order block entry: buy in the discount of the down-close candle — **below its body** (here its close), anywhere in the wick. People who just "buy down-close candles" only heard his first introduction to order blocks.
  - Stop: below the consequent encroachment of the NDOG.
  - Once price takes out the short-term high, move the stop to break-even, or at minimum to the top of the NDOG. Don't rush the stop.
  - Additional entries: a gap-up retraced on the next candle, and a classic volume-imbalance retrace.
  - "Every displacement does not have to see it trade back down into" — it's better when the BISI is never revisited; a return into it means the move is deferred, with uncomfortable waiting, drawdown or chop.
  - Two qualifiers that build confidence: (1) displacement ahead of 7 PM after working above and below the NDOG, keeping the initial SSL intact; (2) taking out the short-term high.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 8 ~7 PM ET: down-close candle closed at 18,560; his entry 18,559.75; price rallied through relative equal highs, consolidated around the level, then ran into the initial BSL and beyond. Exit 18,607.5 (limit order).
- **Timestamps:** [0:50:46](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3046) — breakaway gap, relative equal high run; [0:53:07](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3187) — buy below the order block body; [0:53:57](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3237) — entry 59.75, stop below NDOG CE; [0:55:33](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3333) — break-even / top of NDOG; displacement shouldn't be revisited.

### Measured-move targeting with standard deviations (-1 / -1.5)  (aliases: range finding, measured move, standard deviation projection)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take the price leg prior to the run (the "slingshot/catapult" wind-back) — here a high down to a low — and project it with a Fib.
  - -1 = a perfect measured move (the leg added to the high): the "low-hanging fruit", no-brainer exit, especially if it reaches into an old high / relative equal highs where real stops rest. Partial there with a limit order if you have more than one contract.
  - -1.5 = where price can extrapolate and "get ahead of itself".
  - He aims for the "gray area" between -1 and -1.5 (roughly the midpoint; caption says "like 1.05 something" — unclear, likely ~1.25), and exits at or above the midpoint because trying to be too precise often means the limit isn't filled.
- **How to use:** Simplest rule for beginners: for a long, measure the price leg before your run and project -1; if it reaches an old high or relative equal highs, that's the draw.
- **Example in this video:** Asia long: measured from the high to the low of the leg before the 7 PM run; exit 18,607.5, "just above halfway" between -1 and -1.5. Price later traded up to the projection.
- **Timestamps:** [0:57:57](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3477) — prior leg as the range to measure; [0:59:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3572) — -1 and -1.5; [1:01:09](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3669) — exit 607.5; [1:03:34](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3814) — gray area vs low-hanging fruit.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He admits he wants to nail highs but accepts that perfect exits aren't a "steady diet".

### Initial liquidity levels as real support/resistance overnight; previous day high/low
- **Kind:** example / rule
- **What ICT says:** The initial BSL/SSL levels are what the algorithm keeps referring back to — this is the "real" support/resistance, not lines drawn at random swing points. ### RTH opening range gap & the 9:30–10:00 opening range  (aliases: regular trading hours gap, opening range, midgap)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Switch the chart from electronic trading hours (ETH) to regular trading hours (RTH) to see the RTH gap: the difference between the previous day's 4:15 PM ET settlement close and the 9:30 AM open. (4:00 bell, 15 minutes settling, 4:15 day session done; electronic trading continues to 5 PM, restarts at 6 PM.)
  - The opening range is algorithmically 30 minutes, 9:30–10:00 — "there is no 15-minute opening range." Within it the market establishes initial buy-side and sell-side, like the 6 PM NDOG protocol. Sometimes there's no opening-range impact and price just runs.
  - Highlight the whole gap range and watch. With a large gap, price will normally trade a bit against the open, then work back, often taking/approaching the low formed.
  - After a big gap lower, there is about a **70% chance** price returns to **midgap** (the gap's midpoint) — even if bearish overall; "not 100%".
  - Whether the gap closes fully is judged using the initial BSL/SSL levels and other factors.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 9: gapped down on RTH; first candle green (open = low); price ran up because it had already taken a low, reached midgap, bumped the low again, then ran up to the initial SSL reference level from the 6 PM protocol — not a full gap closure (it could have reached the 4:15 close or half the wick).
- **Timestamps:** [1:21:06](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4866) — ETH vs RTH; [1:22:41](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4961) — 30-minute opening range; [1:24:19](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5059) — highlight the gap; [1:25:09](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5109) — 70% midgap.

### TGIF (Friday weekly-range retracement)  (aliases: TGIF setup, Thank God It's Friday)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When the week has been strongly one-sided, Friday is inclined to retrace back into the week's range (Sunday open through the week) by 20–30% of the weekly high-to-low range; in some cases up to 40% (he has rules for that he won't teach). Shorts on Friday after a bullish week can target ~30% down from the weekly high. Sometimes it's a straight shot then dies sideways; sometimes it meanders and breaks down in steps. Not a prediction of Friday's close. Driven by nobody wanting to hold risk into the weekend; a 30% pullback doesn't break a long-term bullish picture.
- **Example in this video:** Week of Aug 5–9, 2024 NQ moved strongly up; no news Friday → he expected possible meandering lower.
- **Timestamps:** [1:36:24](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5784); [1:37:15](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5835); [1:38:03](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5883).

### SMT divergence between NQ, ES, YM (how he weighs it)  (aliases: SMT, smart money technique)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He doesn't trade the Dow (YM, "dirty 30") — only uses it to confirm market breadth and continuity. A divergence involving the Dow is taken "with a grain of salt": he'll be cautious and may consider a partial, but won't close the whole trade because of it. A divergence between NQ and ES (e.g. long NQ, NQ makes a higher high and ES doesn't) is more valid — he'll likely take a partial because it signals weakening momentum.
- **Example in this video:** Aug 9: ES "looks even worse than NASDAQ"; both ES and YM have relative equal highs that are "too clean" — he'd expect them to be disrupted (smoothness taken out) before any move lower; he wouldn't trade ES there.
- **Timestamps:** [1:40:24](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=6024) — ES/YM too clean; [1:41:14](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=6074) — Dow divergence grain of salt; [1:42:01](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=6121) — NQ vs ES divergence → partial.

### Clean charts / levels on a notepad ("trading naked")
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:** He keeps key levels written on a notepad/tablet next to him, not on the chart; annotations on his charts are only for teaching. Lines clutter the chart, draw the eye, and in his own development made him hold losing ideas stubbornly ("it's going to happen because I have these lines"). Removing annotations made him flexible to see when price was in trouble. Beginners should keep annotations at first and not rush to go naked — it takes weeks/months of experience.
- **Example in this video:** NQ at 18,482.5 with his notes saying NWOG high 18,513.25 — in close proximity, so price could gyrate back up there; relative equal highs above still "too clean".
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:12](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=852); [0:15:50](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=950); [1:27:34](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5254).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 5 PM ET close / 6 PM ET open = NDOG; gap > 20 handles → annotate CE; < 20 → eyeball CE and quadrants.
- Don't trade the NDOG as soon as it forms; wait for time.
- 6 PM protocol: leave NDOG → take a short-term high → cross back through NDOG → take a short-term low (or mirror) → initial BSL/SSL set.
- No bump above/below after 6 PM into 7 PM → Asia likely dull; skip.
- Asia window: 7:00–9:00 PM NY time; algorithm online at 7 PM.
- Long entry: below the body of the down-close order block; stop below NDOG CE; move stop to break-even or top of NDOG once the short-term high is taken.
- Best displacement: BISI never revisited (breakaway gap); revisit = deferred move / chop.
- Targets: prior leg projected -1 (measured move, low-hanging fruit) and -1.5 (extension); exit in the gray area between, at/above the midpoint.
- Bearish: FVG retrace that fails to reach CE = weakness → next OB/FVG entry likely wins.
- London opening range: 12:00–12:30 AM ET; London delineation ~2:00 AM.
- RTH gap = previous 4:15 PM close to 9:30 open; opening range = 9:30–10:00 (no 15-minute opening range).
- After a big opening gap lower, ~70% chance of reaching midgap.
- TGIF: after a one-sided week, Friday can retrace 20–30% (sometimes 40%) of the weekly range.
- SMT: NQ vs ES divergence → take a partial; Dow divergence → caution only.
- Take one good setup, then stop.
- Majority of big setups form around previous day high/low.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Beginners should annotate initial liquidity levels and take screenshots after the fact — study, don't predict; then walk forward without pushing buttons ([0:35:28](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2128)).
- Choose the session that fits your life (London, NY AM, NY PM, Asia); Asia is fine for 9–5 workers ([0:11:02](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=662)).
- Goal of the mentorship is independence — never needing to watch another ICT video; "TikTok mentality" summaries strip out the counseling you need to get through hardship ([0:27:05](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1625)).
- Keep pursuing perfection in execution but accept you won't be precise every time; once you think you've figured it all out you get lazy and break rules ([1:05:56](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3956)).
- He'll be more impressed by your ability not to over-trade than by many trades; take your one good setup and stop ([1:20:18](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4818)).
- He has Caleb focus on trading FVGs because they're visually obvious; observers who switch to other PD arrays or larger stops after he's in profit will feel more anxiety. If you didn't do it when/how you were supposed to, do nothing and observe — don't break rules ([1:32:26](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5546), [1:34:01](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5641)).

## Glossary candidates
`Initial buy-side liquidity` — the first short-term high raided after the 6 PM NDOG forms (and price leaves the gap); a reference level the algorithm returns to.
`Initial sell-side liquidity` — the first short-term low raided after price crosses back through the 6 PM NDOG.
`Coloring outside the lines` — price trading through a PD array/NDOG as a normal part of engineering liquidity, not a failure.
`Breakaway gap` — a displacement FVG that is never revisited; the best sign the move is ready.
`Nitrous oxide` — fast, energetic displacement through a level (vs. a turtle-soup fake back inside).
`Gray area` — target zone between the -1 (measured move) and -1.5 standard-deviation projections.
