# L26-053 — Trading In High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions

## Summary

ICT trades NQ on July 21 during high resistance liquidity run conditions—a market state where price whipsaws dramatically within tight ranges, stopping out traders repeatedly despite ultimately moving toward the theoretical target. This video teaches how to identify these conditions, why they're dangerous for inexperienced traders, how to manage positions through them, and when to sit aside instead. The core lesson: during high resistance runs, use multiple timeframe structure and obvious liquidity targets (gap midpoint, daily bias) to bias direction, but keep size small and accept that you may be stopped out before the move executes.

## Concepts

### High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions
- **Kind:** definition · rule
- **What ICT says:** A market state where price shares a large amount of price action in a relatively few number of candles—compressed, violent back-and-forth movement where bodies keep returning to the same range, rejecting breaks lower (or higher), then suddenly accelerating once it escapes. Opposed to low resistance runs where price stretches out taffy-like with one or two candles covering large ranges. These conditions are intentionally engineered; they punish traders already in positions and wipe out those trying to enter. "They're coming for everybody. They'll move the price lower because everybody knows the gap is likely to get filled. Everyone that's trying to go short, they're ripping back against them." Occurs when markets want to stop out multiple traders before delivering to an obvious level.
- **How to identify:** (1) Visual: many candlesticks sharing the same price range; bodies repeatedly rejecting at the same level; wicks extending beyond bodies but bodies staying in a narrow band. (2) Behavioral: price goes down, comes right back up, goes down again, repeats. (3) Context: opening range gap present; obvious daily/4H/1H draw on liquidity below; economic calendar or session-specific timing.
- **How to use:** Do NOT participate with live funds. Instead: tape read it, paper trade it, or demo trade it. When not in these conditions, price is obvious and fast—that's when to engage. Sit aside when you identify them. If already in a trade, keep size very small (ICT reduced from standard 6-4-10 contracts to 3 contracts), use tight stops, be prepared to exit quickly, and manage psychology impeccably. Multiple exits in tiers; take partial profits at obvious levels; don't try to capture the full move.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-minute, July 21, 9:30–11:00 ET RTH. Price opens with a huge premium gap (up), goes down to take out relative equal lows from 0:02:02 area. Then: 9:30 area, price runs up to 60-minute buy-side balance sell sign efficiency around 0:02:55, ICT shorts 1 contract. Gets stopped out around 0:08:45, immediately re-enters. Gets stopped out again around 0:23:40. Enters again, takes partials at intermediate levels (relative equal lows) around 0:33:18 and 0:49:02. The entire 0:22:06–0:51:25 span is high resistance: bodies keep rejecting below the relative equal lows, wicks extend lower but bodies stay upper-half of ranges. Only at 0:48:14 onwards does price finally break below and accelerate down.

The danger: inexperienced traders either close too early (missing the move) or average down (increasing losses). The protection: obvious daily/4H liquidity targets (gap midpoint, relative equal lows) + only trade when those are present + always use stops.

### Opening Range Gap & Gap Midpoint (Half-Gap)
- **Kind:** rule · entry model
- **What ICT says:** At market open (9:30 ET for NQ), price often gaps up or down. This gap is the area between the previous close and the new opening candle's low (if gapped up) or high (if gapped down). The midpoint of this gap is a powerful magnet; price often retraces into it as a secondary objective after the first target (relative equal lows or structural lows). "Find the mid-gap. There's your target. Aim for that. You might miss it, but it gives you a simple framework to start with."
- **How to identify:** On the 1-minute open candle (9:30), mark the high (if gap up) or low (if gap down). Draw a horizontal line at the midpoint. In this video, ICT marks the half-gap multiple times around 0:13:23–0:21:18 and later around 0:49:02.
- **How to use:** Use as a secondary target after taking partials at the first objective (relative equal lows or sell-side liquidity). Helps with position management: take one partial here, hold for further range. Provides psychological anchor—a hard level to aim for rather than "as low as possible."
- **Example in this video:** Opening gap: 9:30 open at ~18480 (approx), previous settlement lower; huge premium gap marked around 0:02:02. Half-gap calculated and marked as a secondary objective around 0:13:23. Reached and confirmed around 0:49:02 and 0:51:25.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:13:23](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=803) — Marking half-gap: "I'm going to remove all risk here... take one off underneath those relative equal lows... if it takes that out and fills me... then I'm going to bring the stop down."

### Relative Equal Highs & Lows (Old Highs/Lows)
- **Kind:** rule · liquidity target
- **What ICT says:** A previous swing high or low from an earlier session that is retested at roughly the same price level. When price approaches these levels in a new session, they act as draw-on liquidity points (magnets) because market makers know traders want to break/clear those levels. Price often reverses near them after a retest, or uses them as "trap" zones where stops are run.
- **How to identify:** Chart previous 1-3 sessions' highs and lows; when price returns to those zones, note them as zones of interest.
- **How to use:** Use as bias confirmers: if price reaches and respects (reverses off) relative equal low, it's bearish; if price reaches and reverses off relative equal high, it's bullish. Use as stop-placement guides (put stops slightly above relative equal highs if shorting). Use as entry or exit targets.
- **Example in this video:** Relative equal lows marked around 0:02:02 and repeatedly referenced. Price reaches and respects them around 0:22:06–0:33:18, then revisits them around 0:49:02. ICT places orders to exit at these levels.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:02:02](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=122) — Marking the target lows.
  - [0:15:03](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=903) — "I'll take one off underneath those relative equal lows we were aiming at earlier."

### Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
- **Kind:** definition · liquidity concept
- **What ICT says:** Resting sell orders (limit orders waiting to be filled at higher prices) left behind when price falls sharply. Traders who longed near lows and want to exit at breakeven or profit place sell orders; when price returns up into that zone, those orders get hit. For a short trader, this is a drag on momentum upward—the zone resists upward movement.
- **How to identify:** Look at the 4H or 1H chart: find a recent sharp fall. The zone where the rise stopped (relative low before that fall) often contains SSL. Visually: bodies above a level but no bodies below (suggesting sellers waiting above, not below).
- **How to use:** As a short trader, use SSL zones as profit-taking zones or as "drag" zones where upward momentum stalls. Use as a pullback magnet if price is falling through SSL—price will pull back into it before resuming down.
- **Example in this video:** Around 0:04:40, ICT notes "sell side there" and uses it as a target for the short.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:04:40](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=280) — "Next point of interest is this sell side here."

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) / Inversion FVG / SIBI
- **Kind:** definition · PD array · price delivery method
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG is an inefficiency (void) left on the chart when price gaps up on a single candle, leaving a gap in price (no candle body in that range). Later, price retraces into that void to "fill" it. In this video, ICT references these as "inversion fair value" and uses them as retracement magnets. BISI (buy-side inefficiency) and SIBI (sell-side inefficiency) are the terms for the upper and lower sides of these gaps.
- **How to identify:** Look for a single candle that opens above the prior close with space between (a gap). Highlight that gap zone. Price will eventually retrace into it.
- **How to use:** Use as a magnet for retracements. If shorting, expect price to bounce into an inversion FVG before resuming down. Use as a "re-entry zone" if stopped out.
- **Example in this video:** Marked around 0:07:09, 0:15:49, 0:32:25. ICT draws them with dashed lines and annotates them as defensive levels or retracement zones.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:07:09](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=429) — "This is an inversion fair value gap."
  - [0:15:49](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=949) — Managing retracements: "Keep bodies out of the upper half. Touching that is okay. I don't want to see it leave a body up there."

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) / Midwick Level
- **Kind:** definition · price level · entry/stop guide
- **What ICT says:** When a candle closes with a long wick (upper or lower), the midpoint of that wick is a "consequent encroachment" level. It represents the point where the body rejected that extreme move. Price often retraces back to that midwick level after moving beyond it. Can be used as a stop-placement guide or as a retracement magnet.
- **How to identify:** Identify a candle with a long wick. Calculate the midpoint between the body close and the wick tip. Mark that level.
- **How to use:** Place stops slightly beyond this level (to allow for one more spike but to exit before a full reversal). Use as a "last line of defense" for a trade.
- **Example in this video:** References around 0:07:09, 0:15:49, 0:62 (end of transcript).
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:07:09](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=429) — "This longer wick, I'm watching that, it's consequent encroachment or midwick level right there."

### Body Placement as Market Bias Indicator
- **Kind:** rule · market structure
- **What ICT says:** Where the candle body (open to close) closes relative to a price level tells you the intent. If bodies keep closing above a level, it's bullish; if bodies keep closing below, it's bearish. Wicks that extend below a level but bodies that stay above = likely reversal / retracement coming. Bodies buried below a level = confirmation to break lower.
- **How to identify:** Look at the last 3–5 candles at a key level (relative equal low, FVG, etc.). Are bodies above or below? Are wicks alone touching the level?
- **How to use:** Use as a refusal/confirmation filter: refusal to stay below (bodies above despite wicks below) = likely to retrace or reverse. Confirmation to continue (bodies below) = likely to break further.
- **Example in this video:** Central theme from 0:22:06 onwards. ICT repeatedly emphasizes: "Keep bodies out of the upper half," "Whenever you're watching a liquidity pool right below an old low and you want to keep going lower, you don't want to see this type of event [bodies above]. You want to see them laying bodies underneath."
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:22:53](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1373) — "Whenever you're watching a liquidity pool right below an old low and you want to keep going lower, you don't really want to see this type of event. You want to see them laying bodies underneath."
  - [0:28:30](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1710) — "Laying bodies underneath a level = confirmation to break. Bodies above = likely reversal."

### PHIL (Market Maker / "Person Handling Institutional Liquidity")
- **Kind:** new coinage · narrative / teaching device
- **What ICT says:** Personification of the market maker—the entity controlling price and running stops. "PHIL" is shorthand for the person/algorithm that sets prices, runs liquidity, and manages the order flow. Used pedagogically: "If I were PHIL, I would..." ICT uses this to predict likely price actions because PHIL's job is to run stops and extract liquidity, not to move price smoothly.
- **How to use:** When analyzing a trade, ask "What would PHIL do here?" to predict stops that will be run or liquidity that will be drawn before the main move.
- **Example in this video:** Around 0:29:17, 0:34:54, 0:35:45, 0:37:45, 0:41:18—ICT repeatedly predicts what "PHIL" will do next, then watches it happen.

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- In high resistance liquidity run conditions: keep size low; use 1 contract instead of 6-4-10.
- When approaching a stop loss, do NOT wait for it to be hit; proactively take off a partial to "remove risk" and protect psychology.
- Never share your stop loss publicly on social media or live stream—"Phil" will run it.
- Use the morning session bias (9:30–10:30/11:00) to establish a primary target (relative equal lows, structural lows from previous sessions).
- Use the gap midpoint (half-gap) as a secondary objective.
- Bodies tell the narrative; wicks are noise. Watch for bodies closing in the upper or lower half of key ranges.
- During high resistance days: do not expect immediate feedback; accept multiple stop-outs; do not increase size to "make back" losses.
- Keep stop loss below bodies, not wicks. Place it slightly beyond the consequent encroachment level (midwick) to allow for final spikes but to exit before reversal.
- Low resistance runs (easy days): price stretches like taffy—use normal position sizing, engage fully.
- High resistance runs (hard days): keep small size, tighten stops, manage psychology, accept limited profits.
- Economic calendar context, day of week, time of day all matter—use these as bias filters before taking trades.
- Use multiple timeframes (daily, 4H, 1H, 5m, 1m) for bias and structure confirmation; only trade when all align.

## Process, Risk & Psychology

**Position Management:**
- Once a trade is entered and showing profit, if retracement happens and your stop is at risk, proactively take a partial off (even a small one) to "remove risk" and ease psychology. This is not a loss; it's risk management. (0:23:40)
- Once you take a partial, the remaining contracts become "easier" to hold through retracements because the psychological weight is lighter.
- If a stop-out happens, do not immediately re-enter unless the structure still supports it. ICT re-enters in this video but only after confirming the original thesis still applies. (0:23:40, 0:33:18)

**Psychology & Conviction:**
- High resistance days are testing your conviction. "It needs to show really strong just dump out of there." You'll feel tempted to exit early if it's taking longer than expected. Resist that temptation only if the daily/structural target is still valid.
- Fatigue in these conditions is real; ICT acknowledges this: "It's so fatiguing. I'm fatigued by this." But conviction in the original thesis and proper size/risk management allow you to continue.
- Do not copy live trades if you don't understand the structure—ICT's students copying trades in this market would "wear them out." (0:49:49)
- Avoid being guilt-tripped into live trading by followers who demand it. Execute your own plan.

**Journaling & Backtesting (stated implicitly):**
- Mark key levels (relative equal highs/lows, gaps, FVGs) before the move happens; this proves you're not hindsight-biasing.
- Log your stop-outs and re-entries; high resistance conditions will produce multiple stop-outs on the way to the final target.
- Study days when you were wrong (body placement showing reversal) vs. days when you were right (bodies below confirming breakout). This refines your read.

**Risk Management:**
- Never risk more than you're willing to lose on a single trade, but in high resistance conditions, your realized loss may be larger because of multiple stop-outs. Expect this.
- Use tight stops (consequent encroachment levels) to limit loss per stop-out.
- Only engage when you have obvious daily/4H liquidity targets—don't trade blind.

**When to Sit Out:**
- If you identify high resistance conditions and you're inexperienced, do NOT trade live; tape read, paper trade, or demo trade instead.
- If you're in a trade and the daily/4H structure no longer supports the original thesis, close the trade. Don't hold hoping for a miracle.

## Glossary Candidates

`High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions` — A market state characterized by compressed, back-and-forth price action within a tight range; candles share a lot of price movement in a small number of candlesticks. Marked by repeated rejection of key levels, stop-running, and multiple retracements before finally delivering to the target. The opposite is low resistance, where price stretches out in few candles covering large ranges.

`PHIL` — Shorthand for the market maker or institutional liquidity provider; personification used in ICT teaching to predict stop-runs and liquidity draws. "If I were PHIL, I'd run this stop now."

`Half-Gap` — The midpoint of an opening range gap; a secondary target for retracement after the primary target (relative equal lows) is reached.

`Consequent Encroachment / Midwick Level` — The midpoint of a candle's wick (distance between body and wick tip); used as a stop-placement guide or retracement magnet.

`Body Placement` — The location of the candlestick's body (open–close range) relative to a key price level; indicates market bias (bullish if bodies above, bearish if bodies below) and can signal reversal if bodies refuse to close below a level despite wicks reaching lower.
