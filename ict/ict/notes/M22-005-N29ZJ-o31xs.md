## Summary
Lecture on "intraday order flow and understanding the daily range" for index futures. ICT covers futures contract mechanics (month codes, third-Friday expiration, rolling by open interest), then lays out the NY-time intraday template: mark the most obvious 15m swing high/low before 8:30, trade the morning 8:30–11:00 (to noon), no trading at the noon–1:00 lunch hour, and an afternoon session that starts at 1:30 (an "algorithm macro") with its own swing high/low stop hunt, accelerating into the close via market-on-close flows (~3:40, 3:50, 4:00). He defines displacement (the elephant-in-a-kiddie-pool analogy) as the required energetic move before trusting an FVG, introduces the three drives pattern into an old high, and states the "only two patterns you need": a stop hunt of a swing point followed by reversal, or (if no swing is raided) a sudden displacement and FVG retrace. Daily profiles (morning move vs afternoon: reversal, continuation/measured move, consolidation-then-trend) are assigned as homework.

## Concepts
### Index futures contract months, expiration and rollover  (aliases ICT uses: "front month", "nearby contract", "next month out")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Index futures (ES, NQ, YM) trade only four delivery months: H = March, M = June, U = September, Z = December. TradingView symbol example ESH2022 (TD Ameritrade uses "22"). Expiration is the third Friday of the delivery month; do not trade after expiration. Around the first or second day of the week before expiration, he monitors open interest (e.g., on barchart.com) and trades whichever contract month has the larger open interest (the larger liquidity pool).
- **How to identify:** Compare open interest of front month vs next month out.
- **How to use:** Roll to the next month once its open interest exceeds the front month's.
- **Example in this video:** At recording, March ES was the front month; June open interest only ~57,124 (caption says "million"; caption unclear) so March remains the contract to trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:49](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=109) — month codes; [0:02:38](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=158) — third Friday expiration; [0:04:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=255) — roll by open interest.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says Asian-range ideas (from his forex teaching) are not applicable to these index markets.

### Intraday daily-range template (NY time)  (aliases ICT uses: "frame your day", "daily range on an intraday basis", "boundaries")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Set the chart to New York local time (otherwise calibrations break). Vertical lines at 8:30 AM, noon and 1:00 PM: a morning session, a one-hour New York lunch, and an equal amount of time after lunch. Morning trade: 8:30 (news releases come out at 8:30) to noon, preferably done around 11:00 — he generally avoids new trades after 11:00, wants to be positioned before 11:00 and ride into lunch, then square or take some off if holding through lunch toward the close.
- **How to identify:** Vertical lines 8:30 / 12:00 / 13:00.
- **How to use:** Only look for setups in the morning and afternoon windows.
- **Example in this video:** ES March 2022, day of recording (date not stated).
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:36](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=456) — 8:30 vertical line; [0:08:23](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=503) — morning, lunch hour, afternoon; NY time; [0:10:50](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=650) — morning trade 8:30–noon, preferably before 11.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He had said in Ep. 3 the afternoon was out of scope; here he decides to teach it because of "nonsense on YouTube".

### New York lunch: no-trade hour  (aliases ICT uses: "lunch hour", "New York lunch")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Noon to 1:00 PM NY is a no-trade period — not even in demo. It can do weird things or simply go sideways; it is usually not clean price action. Swing lows/highs formed during lunch should be ignored for the afternoon structure. "The algorithm reserved the price run until later in the day."
- **How to identify:** 12:00–13:00 ET.
- **How to use:** Stand aside; re-engage after 1:00 (preferably 1:30).
- **Example in this video:** ES: right before lunch price went slightly above highs, traded down, then lunch.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:11](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=551) — noon–1:00 no trade; [0:22:03](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1323) — don't trade it; [0:25:21](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1521) — ignore lunch swing lows.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Pre-8:30 swing high/low as the stop-hunt reference  (aliases ICT uses: "most significant or obvious swing high and swing low prior to 8:30")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** On the 15m chart, find the most significant/obvious swing high and swing low before 8:30. Swing high = three-candle pattern with a lower high on each side; swing low = higher low on each side; candle close direction doesn't matter. Above the swing high = buy-side liquidity; below the swing low = sell-side liquidity. You only need the first one; no need to go back days. Then drop to the 5m chart (first lower timeframe for entry).
- **How to identify:** As above.
- **How to use:** These are the levels a stop run is framed on, the catalyst for the morning setup; the opposite one is the target.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m: pre-8:30 high and low marked with horizontal rays; transposed to 5m.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:16](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=796) — what highs/lows a stop run is framed on; [0:14:05](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=845) — swing high/low three-candle definition; [0:14:53](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=893) — drop to 5m.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** 15m called the "bellwether" timeframe for key highs/lows and FVGs.

### Three drives pattern into an old high  (aliases ICT uses: "three drives", "three little Indians" from Raschke & Connors' Street Smarts — caption reads "three little endings")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A swing high, then a higher swing high, then a higher swing high — price pressing up three times — into an old high, on any timeframe. You don't need the third high to take out the old high: each swing high that fails gets sold by bears who put buy stops above the previous high, which keep getting taken, so liquidity is already being run and smart money is establishing shorts. He credits Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' book (Street Smarts) for helping him understand stop hunts.
- **How to identify:** Three ascending swing highs approaching or around an old high/relative equal highs.
- **How to use:** Treat as liquidity being run; then wait for displacement lower and an FVG to sell.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m: old high at about 4514.50 (caption unclear: "45145 / 4514 A5") matched by a later candle's high exactly (relative equal highs) with multiple successively higher short-term highs = three drives.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=942) — relative equal highs and three drives; [0:16:31](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=991) — three little Indians pattern; [0:17:19](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1039) — third high need not take the old high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** If there are no stair-stepping higher highs and just one steady run up, anticipate the old high to be taken out (doesn't need to be by much), then look for the energetic move lower.

### Displacement  (aliases ICT uses: "elephant in a children's swimming pool", "energetic/animated move", "obvious displacement")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** After price goes above an old high and trades back down, you want an obvious displacement — like an elephant falling into a kiddie pool — not a lethargic or anemic move. Only when displacement occurs do you look for the FVG (the low and high) and sell the retrace into it. Used to answer viewer criticism of a prior example where a swing low "looked violated" but he didn't use it: the break lacked displacement.
- **How to identify:** A pronounced, energetic candle (or candles) breaking the swing low, leaving an FVG; weak drifting breaks don't count.
- **How to use:** Displacement + FVG = trade; anemic break without FVG = wait.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m morning: first poke above the swing high with a weak anemic move lower (no); a wick that snapped back without an FVG (no); a matching high, a slight anemic dip below (no); then it trades up and "smashes down", next candle closes leaving an FVG = short; target the sell-side liquidity below the pre-8:30 swing low; price hits it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:06](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1086) — displacement analogy; [0:18:52](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1132) — then FVG and short; [0:20:25](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1225) — anemic vs energetic; [0:21:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1275) — smash down, FVG, target SSL.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Patterns sometimes fail (news events, market rolls over); hence the stop loss and money management.

### Afternoon session: 1:30 PM macro, swing high/low stop hunt  (aliases ICT uses: "afternoon trend", "algorithm macro at 1:30")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** 1:30 PM is the earliest he wants to trade the afternoon, because "an algorithm macro" starts running at 1:30 in equities (details beyond this mentorship's scope). After 1:30, look for the first swing high and swing low and apply the same stop-hunt logic as the morning. If your daily bias says the daily candle expands higher, look for an afternoon trade in that direction using the morning logic. Levels that are "too clean" (straight-line edges) tend to be disrupted.
- **How to identify:** First swing high/low forming at/after 1:30 ET.
- **How to use:** Bullish afternoon: wait for a swing low to be violated (a small stop hunt is all that's needed), buy the sell stops, target buy stops above (e.g., the day's high/relative equal highs), and hold for the close.
- **Example in this video:** ES afternoon: the high of day with buy stops above it intact, daily context bullish (an important low formed last week, so the daily is retracing up and shorts are getting squeezed). After 1:30 a swing low is violated (small stop hunt), starting a buy program; buy-side liquidity runs aggressively and price trends into the close with only small retracements. NQ: at 1:30 a swing low almost equal to an earlier low (relative equal lows = retail support) is traded below, with an FVG to the left and relative equal highs (BSL) above; on the 1m the sudden drop below the equal lows ("floor dropped out") is where you buy. His demo entry was near the lowest candle and it rallied.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:47](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1907) — 1:30 swing highs/lows; [0:32:35](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1955) — algorithm macro at 1:30; too-clean levels; [0:33:22](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2002) — swing low violated starts a buy program; [0:37:25](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2245) — NQ at 1:30, relative equal lows; [0:39:01](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2341) — buy the sudden drop.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** First mention of "macro" in this series.

### Buy program / spooling  (aliases ICT uses: "buy program", "sell program", "spooling")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A buy program is when the algorithms continuously offer higher prices (spooling), regardless of volume; sometimes volume is good, sometimes not — that inconsistency is the signature of manipulation.
- **How to identify:** After a stop hunt, persistent repricing in one direction with small retracements.
- **How to use:** Get long after the stop hunt and hold rather than scalping small moves.
- **Example in this video:** ES afternoon rally after the 1:30-area swing-low raid.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:22](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2002); [0:34:10](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2050).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Market-on-close acceleration (3:40 / 3:50 / 4:00)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The afternoon has built-in mechanisms that make the market accelerate into the close. A repeating phenomenon typically around 20 minutes to 4 (3:40), 10 minutes to 4 (3:50) and 4:00 PM, based on market-on-close orders; algorithms produce very aggressive pricing, forcing traders to cover/exit; if going up it ramps and accelerates.
- **How to identify:** Lower-timeframe price action in the last 20 minutes.
- **How to use:** Hold afternoon positions in the daily-bias direction into the close.
- **Example in this video:** ES ran aggressively into the close.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1422) — runs into the close; [0:26:09](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1569) — 3:40, 3:50, 4:00 MOC.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Daily bias / daily range expansion; daily profiles  (aliases ICT uses: "market profiles", "daily profiles", "morning trend vs afternoon")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Like the weekly candle, ask whether the daily candle (today's or tomorrow's) is more likely to expand higher or lower — not predicting the close. The day is designed with a morning move, the lunch hour, and an afternoon move. Analogy to forex: the morning's low of the day acts like the "London low" in a buy day, then consolidation, then a New York continuation in the same direction — but here all in NY session time. Afternoon possibilities: reversal of the morning; continuation higher; a measured move (afternoon duplicates the morning move, e.g., 200 points morning + 200 afternoon = 400-point range); or morning consolidation then afternoon trend. Consecutive higher swing lows ("every one higher") show accumulation/underpinning.
- **How to identify:** Classify each day's morning (bullish/bearish/consolidation), what preceded it on prior days, and what the afternoon did.
- **How to use:** If expecting expansion higher, take the afternoon setup in that direction; relative equal highs forming in a bullish context = retail resistance building buy-side liquidity to be run.
- **Example in this video:** ES: bullish context (important low last week on the daily, daily retracing higher; crowd expects a crash and keeps shorting); afternoon ran buy stops into the close.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1422) — morning move, lunch, afternoon design; [0:27:46](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1666) — daily candle expansion; London-low analogy; [0:29:20](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1760) — afternoon profiles incl. measured move.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Bias and entry techniques to be taught "next week"; not volume profile.

### The only two patterns you need
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** (1) A swing low (high) is violated in a stop hunt, then price rallies (drops): buy the sell stops. (2) If no swing low is raided, look for a sudden displacement higher, then an FVG; buy when price trades back down into the FVG. No breakers, no order blocks, no "15 gimmicky names" needed; the logic has to be there for either.
- **How to identify:** As described.
- **How to use:** Applies in both morning and afternoon sessions.
- **Example in this video:** ES/NQ afternoon (pattern 1); ES morning short (displacement + FVG).
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:47](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2147) — buy sell stops; [0:36:34](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2194) — two patterns.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Always use New York local time on charts.
- Contract months H/M/U/Z; expiration third Friday; roll when next month's open interest is larger (check from the 1st–2nd day of the week before expiration).
- Morning: 8:30 AM to noon, prefer entries before 11:00.
- No trading noon–1:00 PM (not even demo).
- Afternoon: earliest 1:30 PM (algorithm macro); find the first swing high/low after 1:30.
- Pre-8:30: the first/most obvious 15m swing high and low = morning liquidity references; then 5m for entry; 1m for refinement.
- Swing point = 3-candle pattern; candle color irrelevant.
- Displacement must be energetic; anemic breaks don't qualify; FVG required.
- Three drives into an old high: third drive need not exceed the old high.
- MOC acceleration around 3:40, 3:50, 4:00 PM.
- Measured move: afternoon may duplicate the morning's range.
- Index futures less reliable in July and August ("hit or miss").
- After unexpected news/volatility, stand aside; may take a day or a week for markets to resync.
- If you get a morning trade, don't trade the afternoon (especially while new); go practice in demo.

## Glossary candidates
`Front month / next month out` — the nearest-expiring futures contract / the one after it.
`Bellwether timeframe` — the 15m chart, used for key highs/lows and FVGs.
`Displacement` — an energetic, obvious move (elephant in a kiddie pool) that confirms intent and leaves an FVG.
`Three drives` — three successively higher swing highs (or lower lows) running liquidity into an old high/low.
`Buy program / spooling` — the algorithm continuously offering higher prices regardless of volume.
`Algorithm macro (1:30)` — a time-based algorithmic event at 1:30 PM ET that creates movement in the afternoon.
`Measured move (session)` — afternoon range that duplicates the morning range.
`MOC` — market-on-close orders driving 3:40/3:50/4:00 acceleration.
