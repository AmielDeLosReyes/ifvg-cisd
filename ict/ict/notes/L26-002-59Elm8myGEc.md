# L26-002 — NonFarm Payroll October 2026 Lecture
ID: 59Elm8myGEc · URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59Elm8myGEc · Duration: 41:20
Content type: mixed (post-trade review of an NFP fade + mindset talk)
Teaching density: high (one new/rare framework taught in detail, plus a long philosophy segment on simplicity, NFP/CPI/PPI/FOMC behaviour and why not to trade ahead of news)

## Summary
ICT reviews his Friday NFP morning on NQ (December contract, 1-minute, electronic trading hours chart). He does not trade the 8:30 release itself. He waits 15-20 minutes for the news "wake" to leave a short-term high, then builds an unrealized dealing range from that high up to an old daily high (June 22, 2026, 31,285). The 50% of that range ("event horizon") is combined with a negative standard-deviation projection of the 7:00-8:30 range to find a convergence level where he fades the move with a short. The entry is confirmed by an inversion FVG / SIBI whose consequent encroachment price fails to reach. The rest of the lecture is about keeping the toolbox small, not trading ahead of NFP/CPI/PPI, and using extreme final targets with partials on news days. It matters because it shows a predictive (not reactive) way to fade news-driven runs, and it explicitly strips away indicators, volume profile, SMT and COT.

## Concepts

### Unrealized dealing range / "Event horizon" (50% of a projected range)  (aliases ICT uses: event horizon, mid-range reading, finite specific arrays, "phantom zone")
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry model component
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Event horizon" is his name for the midpoint (50%) of a dealing range that has not been fully realized yet: one end is a real, already-formed high (here the short-term high made after the NFP release), the other end is an old daily high that price has not reached (the June 22, 2026 daily high of the December NQ contract, 31,285).
  - It does not matter whether price ever gets to the old high. It is a forecast: "we're predicting, not reacting to price."
  - Both ends are "finite, specific arrays" that cannot be hidden; the middle of the range is the level to hunt for shorts because a price run into premium of that range is expected to be sold.
  - On its own, the event-horizon level means nothing. "Isolated levels are useless"; it needs a convergence of several smart-money concepts to be viable.
  - The market-efficiency logic: the new leg higher will leave buyers' sell-stops (sell-side liquidity) resting below the post-news low. Shorting from the event horizon gives willing sellers a lower target to pay out into.
- **How to identify:**
  1. Mark the 8:30 news release. Wait roughly 15-20 minutes into the session (do not guess ahead of it).
  2. Wait for a short-term high to form after the first rip and a retracement.
  3. Wait for price to take that short-term high out (a shift in market structure on the lower timeframe); only then can the unrealized range from that new high to the old daily high be measured. (He anticipates it before price gets there.)
  4. Draw a fib with the 50% (equilibrium) of that range; the 0.5 is the event horizon.
  5. Make sure the fib sits "on air" (anchored exactly on highs/lows, not on candle bodies).
- **How to use:**
  - Look for shorts at/near the event horizon when the daily draw is higher (he was bullish for the day but was fading a news-driven intraday run). He is "contrarian" intraday: he scalps against the grain of the daily range.
  - Stop loss goes above the entry array (above the gap's upper boundary / CE zone), not far above the level.
  - Set an extreme final take-profit (see Targets below) and bank partials along the way.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Dec contract, 1m, Friday of NFP week (October 2026 NFP; exact date not stated). At 8:30 NFP prints and price rips higher (price had been drawing up toward the old daily high; 9:30 opening came later). A short-term high forms at 8:38 (about 31,146.75, caption-garbled "838" and "31146.75 ... the high of that candlestick"). Price retraces, then rallies again through that high. Projecting the 8:38 high to the old daily high 31,285 gives a 50% level (his "event horizon") of about 31,216 (arithmetic from his numbers; he does not say the figure). Price trades up to the event horizon, where he sells.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:10:42](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=642) — "if I know this level and this level, I want the middle of that range: event horizon"
  - [0:36:23](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2183) — recap: two ways to use the high; mid-range reading is the event horizon
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He stresses this is "a volatility-based, news-driven, once-in-a-while scenario", not an every-day Silver Bullet-style setup, and "it's not going to appear every single time." Intended for advanced students who already have models; he says newer students should not try to copy it. Note: it relies on a prior directional draw (higher NQ prices).

### Pre-8:30 range framing + negative standard-deviation projections (-1, -1.5, -2, -2.5)  (aliases ICT uses: standard deviations, calibration, "pre-market session hours between 7:00 and 9")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When a high-impact driver prints at 8:30, frame the range from 7:00 to 8:30 (not just a 9:00 reference): take the highest high and the lowest low between 7:00 and 8:30.
  - Anchor the fib on the low and draw it up to the high ("drawing it from low to high"), with negative levels so projections extend above the range.
  - He steps through the levels: -1 (a "big purple line"; one standard deviation delivered "beautifully" but he's "not interested at that level because it's isolated"), then adds 0.5 steps (-1.5, -2) and finally -2.5. The -2.5 level lands right on the event horizon midpoint, creating the convergence.
  - He claims this specific combination is not found in "retail books."
- **How to identify:** Draw 7:00-8:30 high/low on the 1-minute chart; extend negative levels on the fib's level list; note where one of them coincides with the event-horizon 50% (here -2.5).
- **How to use:** The convergence of the projection level and the event horizon is the zone to look for a short entry; then confirm with candle behaviour (below). Isolated single levels are not traded.
- **Example in this video:** 7:00 candle's high is the top of the pre-news range; lowest low also in 7:00-8:30 range. Fib low-to-high; -1 reached earlier (not used); -2.5 coincides with the event horizon zone. (Exact -2.5 price not stated.)
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:11:30](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=690) — "isolated levels are useless", walks through -1
  - [0:13:54](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=834) — -2.5 hits the event horizon: "I'm going to save you a lot of time. It's not [in the books]."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Caption wording of the level list is partly garbled ("add a 0.5 ... it's going to be two, -2 ... 2.5"); my reading is -1, -1.5, -2, -2.5. Mark as (caption unclear).

### Inversion FVG / SIBI that fails to reach its consequent encroachment (entry confirmation)  (aliases ICT uses: iFVG, sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency / "sibby", bearish fair value gap, consequent encroachment)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After price runs into the event horizon, he sells while a bold bullish candle prints (an aggressive early entry of 3 contracts), then adds 3 more at the event-horizon line (2 fills), then one more as price starts to pass down through again.
  - Confirmation seen on candles: a bullish candle into the area "fails to touch the consequent encroachment" of the inverted gap; that failure shows weakness, "a weak member of the herd."
  - An inverted FVG must behave as a bearish FVG: price may poke up into the gap, even go into the upper quadrant (above the CE) briefly, but must leave quickly and the bodies must keep closing back down.
  - Stop loss sits just above the gap (he says "Stop loss above here is fine").
  - If price goes into the premium half of the gap, it "needs to stay there briefly and then leave" (like a wick that tags near the CE and immediately retreats).
  - Every upward poke into the gap is smart money selling into public buyers wanting all-time highs: "accumulation of shorts."
  - He refined his gap box during the trade (he cancelled one gap order, re-drew on the next break lower) and notes this is on the recording.
- **How to identify:** (1) a prior bullish FVG/BISI gets traded through to the downside = inversion; (2) the next candle(s) rally into it but do not reach its 50% (CE) or reach it only with a wick and close back below; (3) the following candle trades lower.
- **How to use:**
  - Enter short on those failures at/near the event horizon; stop above the array; use a trailing stop on what is left.
  - Take partials into the next imbalance and into rejection blocks (see below).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, after 9:00 (execution time not stated; before the 9:30 opening). Candle 1 prints bold bullish (he says "203 right about there", likely ~31,203, caption unclear); ICT sells 3 at that candle, 3 more at the red event-horizon line (2 filled), adds 1 more. Next candle tries up and fails at the CE; next candle lower. He sells again at the second SIBI, which tags CE then drops to the low of the previous candle's close/open (the entire SIBI). Partials at ~31,115 and ~31,140; "singles off" as it rebounds; final two contracts stopped at the trailing stop.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:19:27](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1167) — adds executions to the chart
  - [0:21:52](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1312) — candle fails to touch CE: why he sells there
  - [0:30:42](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1842) — second SIBI, stop above, reverts down
  - [0:31:31](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1891) — SIBI behaving as a bearish FVG; shorts accumulate in it
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He insists the whole read comes from each candle's open/high/low/close, "time-based", visible to all, not from volume or order counts. The caption renders "SIBI" as "sibby/cibby" and "buy side balance sell side efficiency"; the standard terms are SIBI = sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency and BISI the opposite.

### Waiting 15-20 minutes after a news release; fade the reaction rather than front-run it  (aliases ICT uses: two-stage delivery, "the wake", fading NFP)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Never position ahead of the 8:30 NFP number; he calls that guessing/gambling and says he lost heavily doing it in his 20s (indicators, signal services). Being right on direction does not save you.
  - Wait about 15-20 minutes into the session; see "what liquidity ... will be left in its wake."
  - After the first burst, look for a meaningful retracement and then measure from the resulting high; "whatever they do first, I will trade back into the range that was delivered because of the news."
  - Same for CPI and PPI ("I'm generally wrong" ahead of them; he calls NFP/CPI/PPI "the three-letter trades" to avoid). You do not have time to read the data, decide and execute, and limit orders will not work.
  - FOMC is "many times treated the same way": a move at 2:00, then at 2:30 usually a retracement the other way. Not always: sometimes the 2:00 move runs straight to the close (trend days "if you didn't get in, your chances are you're probably not getting in anyway").
  - He anticipates a deeper retracement going into the 9:30 opening after an 8:30 straight-up move.
- **How to identify:** a one-directional rip from 8:30 with no retracement; then the first pullback leaves a short-term high/low.
- **How to use:** trade the retracement back into the news range (shorts from premium after a rally, per this example); he wagers only part of his position for deeper levels.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:04:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254) — do not position ahead of NFP; wait 15-20 min
  - [0:34:46](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2086) — NFP/CPI/PPI "will hurt you"; no time for limit orders
  - [0:37:57](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2277) — two-stage delivery; FOMC 2:00 then 2:30 reversal tendency
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this for advanced, contrarian students only; do not encourage newer students to engage with NFP at all.

### Extreme final target and partials on news days  (aliases ICT uses: best case scenario, bank it, limit order at the extreme)
- **Kind:** rule (trade management)
- **What ICT says:**
  - On news-driven trades he always places an extreme final take-profit way beyond what he expects, as a resting limit order, in case the move runs ("then you can sail off into the weekend").
  - But NFP can take unrealized profit away quickly, so partials must be banked along the way: "Just because it trades to your level and you haven't taken anything off, don't fall in love with it."
  - Here the best-case target was the low of the 8:30 candle (price came "real close" but did not deliver while he was still in).
  - He wagers only some of the position for the deeper levels; the rest is taken at nearby imbalances/rejection blocks.
- **How to use:** (1) enter on the failure/CE rejection; (2) take partials at fair value gaps/rejection blocks on the way; (3) trail the remainder; (4) leave a limit order at the extreme.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:17:52](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1072) — extreme target limit order, partials
  - [0:33:07](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1987) — singles off, trail stop takes the last two
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He reports a P&L "just less than $25,000" in under half an hour (futures, several contracts).

### Rejection block (body-based) and wick/body relationships as a PDA  (aliases ICT uses: rejection block, "another PDA")
- **Kind:** example only / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - At the low of the drive down he looks "not simply at the low" but at the bodies: the area where the bodies stop is a rejection block; if trading there he wants some position off before it turns on him.
  - At the end of the session he shows two candles "sharing the space" with wick low and wick high; "see where the bodies are stopping? That's not random."
  - If he had been still in, he would have taken one off at the gap down there, just below the high of that candle.
- **How to identify:** a drop that leaves wicks with bodies clustering at a boundary; draw the wick extremes and mark where bodies stop.
- **How to use:** target/partial zone and a reaction point; no entry rules given.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:40:21](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2421) — wick low/high and bodies stopping

### Keep the toolbox small: draw on liquidity first, a few PD arrays  (aliases ICT uses: note the draw on liquidity, "you only need a couple things")
- **Kind:** rule (process)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first lecture topic is knowing the draw on liquidity (where price is going and why). With that, you only need a couple of PD arrays: inversion FVG, bullish FVG, bearish FVG, a breaker, and perhaps an immediate rebalance (it signals speed afterward).
  - "You got 80% of this game licked" by spending time on why price should go to a specific level.
  - You do not need SMT, Commitment of Traders, volume profile or indicators on every trade. He did not use SMT today or yesterday, nor COT.
  - Choose the one PD array that clicks for you first; stay with what you identify easily in hindsight; do not chase new tools.
  - Futures markets share one high, low, open and close for everyone, so the candlestick's OHLC is the only data needed. He argues volume-profile traders already single out time but over a broad range of what has already happened; he predicts what is not there yet.
  - Do not look at the crowd: "I don't want to be where the majority is"; smart money hunts singly or in small packs and will be in the herd without doing what the herd does.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Do not position ahead of the 8:30 NFP number; wait 15-20 minutes into the session before engaging.
- Frame the 7:00-8:30 range (highest high / lowest low) before a news driver at 8:30; "back it back to 8:30."
- Fib anchored low-to-high with negative levels (-1 ... -2.5); fib must sit "on air" exactly on the extreme.
- Event horizon = 50% of [post-news short-term high to old daily high (June 22, 2026 Dec NQ high 31,285)]; the post-news short-term high (8:38) was about 31,146.75.
- Isolated levels are useless; require convergence of several concepts.
- Entry staging: 3 contracts early, 3 more at the level (2 filled), 1 more later. Take partials at ~31,115 and ~31,140; trail the rest.
- Always put an extreme final TP limit order on news trades, and always bank partials.
- Consequent encroachment of an inverted gap must not be reached (or only briefly poked) for the short to stay valid; stop above the gap.
- FOMC: 2:00 move, then 2:30 retracement is a common (not guaranteed) pattern.
- Stay away from NFP, CPI, PPI ahead of the release.
- Backtest and journal daily; annotate the empty spaces on your chart; "talk about it in hindsight like you saw it beforehand" to train the subconscious.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Guessing the reaction to NFP is gambling; being right on direction still gets you hurt by volatility and slippage ([0:04:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254), [0:35:34](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2134)).
- Impatient new traders chase quick runs; if they win one they re-enter the same day and give it back ([0:09:54](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=594)).
- Don't fall in love with unrealized profit; bank partials ([0:18:41](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1121)).
- Study price action, not signal services or indicators; he lost money to those as a young trader ([0:09:05](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=545)).
- Backtest and journal daily for weeks; annotate the chart ([0:29:06](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1746)).
- Stand aside if you cannot see what is likely to occur ([0:39:32](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2372)).
- Several off-topic digressions (criticism from detractors, crude nightclub analogy) carry only the "be where the crowd isn't" message.

## Glossary candidates
`EVENT HORIZON` — ICT's term for the 50% level of an unrealized dealing range (post-news high to an old daily high); used as a convergence level for fading.
`UNREALIZED DEALING RANGE` — a range where one end is a formed high/low and the other is an old high/low price hasn't reached; used to predict a midpoint.
`FINITE SPECIFIC ARRAYS` — real, exact price points (old highs, short-term highs) that define a measurable range.
`ISOLATED LEVEL` — a level with no convergence of other concepts; "useless" on its own.
`TWO-STAGE DELIVERY` — news-driven first move, then a retracement back into the range the news created.
`THE WAKE` — the liquidity and highs/lows left behind by the first burst after news.
`SIBBY / CIBBY` — caption rendering of SIBI (sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency).
`PHANTOM ZONE` — a price level where nobody else has the nerve to sell/fade (his phrase for the event horizon).
`THREE-LETTER TRADES` — NFP, CPI, PPI, the news events to avoid positioning ahead of.
