## Concepts

### Daily bias from body-based Inside Day  (aliases: "inside day despite the wick")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** He judges the daily candle by its body relative to the previous day's body. A candle whose body could not go below the prior day's body and could not go above it is an inside day, even if the wick pokes above the prior high. He had warned the prior day that price might bump up and challenge his bearish stance; it hit the high and "gave up the ghost", so he kept the bearish read.
- **How to identify:** Compare today's candle body to yesterday's body, not the wicks.
- **How to use:** Used to decide whether a push up into a high is a real shift or a liquidity grab; here it supported staying bearish.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ daily: price rallied, took out a prior high, wicked above, then came back; he says it "revisited that volume imbalance and took the liquidity" and then lost the level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:21](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=441) — body-vs-previous-body inside-day logic
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as a concern he flagged earlier ("go back and listen to the presentation"); the specific lecture is not named here.

### News-day rule: PPI / CPI / 8:30 releases  (aliases: economic calendar, red-folder event, sit on your hands)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** He is "usually wrong" on PPI and CPI, has a very low strike rate on them, and does not position ahead of them. Rule: sit on your hands and wait for the carnage; then whatever liquidity is left in the wake of the run is what you focus on. These releases act "a little like FOMC": a big run, possibly a trap that "rips people's faces off", and then price goes to where it really wanted to go.
- **How to identify:** The high or low that formed before the 8:30 move is the liquidity to target afterward, even if it is several hundred handles away; price "eventually finds its way back there" (may not happen the same day, e.g. on a Friday).
- **How to use:** Do not be in the market in the seconds before/around the release; watch only. After the spike, plan for the pre-release extreme as the target. He ignores the data itself ("could care less about the data", does not trust it) and follows only price action/liquidity logic.
- **Example in this video:** 8:30 PPI; price swept yesterday's low right at the release then drifted lower; he stays flat and only comments. He notes a close below a wick/low after the release usually "warrants a lower low".
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:19](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=679) — rule of sitting on hands; [0:13:41](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=821) — target the liquidity from before the 8:30 move; [0:40:27](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2427) — economic calendar as the main driver of distraction/traps

### Event Horizon  (aliases: midpoint between two static price points, halfway-point draw)
- **Kind:** new coinage / re-teach
- **What ICT says:** When two obvious static price points (old daily lows, old highs, or a NWOG/NDOG above and another below the current price) bracket the market, measure the range between them and mark the halfway point. Price will likely gravitate to that halfway point, and once it breaks beyond it "generally" a sustained price run follows (retracements possible). He says this is the midpoint (a consequent-encroachment-style level) between two key PD arrays. He originally taught it with one new week opening gap above and one below price.
- **How to identify:** Lay a fib across the span between the two points (e.g. yesterday's low to the next old-low pool on the daily). He watches how much "energy" price spends reaching the upper quadrant and then the 50% level.
- **How to use:** Use the 50% as the draw; a break below it (in a bearish case) signals likely sustained delivery toward the far pool. He eyeballs the fib and says it can be "off a couple points".
- **Example in this video:** MNQ daily old lows; yesterday's low (taken at 8:30) to the next outlined pool of sell-side liquidity below; price traded down into the upper quadrant of that range, then made three lower lows on 1m, aiming to gravitate to the halfway point.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:10](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1090) — static old lows as hard objectives, fib laid over; [0:19:51](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1191) — gravitate to the halfway point; "that's event horizon"; [0:21:26](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1286) — NWOG-to-NWOG version
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He stresses that old highs/lows are precise, finite locations ("Wyckoff doesn't talk about that stuff") and that being old PD arrays they invite price to gravitate to the midpoint.

### Daily Volume Imbalance as a higher-timeframe premium PD array
- **Kind:** example only / application
- **What ICT says:** A volume imbalance on the daily chart (red box) is a higher-timeframe PD array. Being bearish, he treats the top of it as the place to sell (premium), its consequent encroachment (CE) as the first draw, and its low as the next objective. He says CE "is always a draw. Always a draw."
- **How to identify:** Daily chart gap between candles (volume imbalance); mark top, CE (dotted midpoint) and low.
- **How to use:** Enter shorts when price trades to the top (even a tick beyond it) and rejects; target the SSL below, then CE, then the low of the VI; expect a possible retracement back up to CE after the low is reached, but if bearish you want it not to do so.
- **Example in this video:** Overnight/London: price runs above, trades into the top of the daily VI (about one tick above it, "colouring outside the lines"), then reverses lower toward the CE and the old-low pool below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:43:46](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2626) — the pink/red daily VI box in London killzone; [0:47:42](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2862) — top of VI used; [0:56:22](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3382) — CE is always a draw
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He uses it because price rejected the recent highs on the daily after Nvidia earnings; he calls "Nvidia propping the market up" a retail myth.

### FVG next to a wick: use the wick, not the gap  (aliases: "new extra charge")
- **Kind:** refinement (new)
- **What ICT says:** If the SIBI sits directly against a wick (the wick candle immediately adjacent to the gap, with no candle between), the fair value gap is not to be used; instead measure the wick with its gradient levels. In the example there is one candlestick between the wick and the SIBI, so the SIBI is valid and counts as a premium PD array. He says go back to old price data to see the benefit of this distinction.
- **How to identify:** Check whether the wick candle is immediately adjacent to the SIBI's candle structure. If there is a one-candle barrier between them, the FVG is valid.
- **How to use:** Valid SIBI: use as the premium PD array for a short; any short entry inside it; stop above the high of the candle that forms it (setup-timeframe high).
- **Example in this video:** 1m chart in London session after the run to the daily VI top; SIBI with one candle separating it from the wick, so the gap is valid.
- **Timestamps:** [0:46:07](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2767) — wick vs SIBI; [0:46:55](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2815) — if wick were immediately left of the SIBI use only wick gradient levels
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Captions are confusing about left/right of the candles; the exact geometry is (caption unclear). Treat the rule as "adjacent wick -> use the wick levels, else the FVG".

### High-frequency (sub-1-minute) entry: lower half of the small FVG after a close below CE
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Inside a higher-timeframe PD array, drop to 30s/15s/5s charts and enter in the lower half of the small FVG (for shorts). He wants the 1m bodies to have stayed in the lower half of the gap, then you wait for how the market trades into the upper half. This is the "high frequency trading window of entry". After a candle closes below the FVG's CE, the next candle anywhere below that midpoint but above the low of the gap is an entry area. You may miss the trade if price shoots up and runs; that is fine because many such setups form on small timeframes. Use the smallest leverage, one micro. Do not expect to learn everything from short-form teaching; it is a bullet-point view.
- **How to identify:** 1m FVG (SIBI) formed at the top of the daily VI; a big green candle runs up, then a candle closes below the CE of the 1m gap; entry zone is below CE and above the gap low.
- **How to use:** Entry in the lower half; stop per volatility rule below; target a first scaling point at a nearby SSL pool; use CE of the daily VI as the draw.
- **Example in this video:** London session (2:00-5:00 ET London killzone): price runs, drops into a 1m inefficiency which could be used as a 2022 model entry, stops out; another run higher; then enter in the lower half of a darker-shaded 1m FVG at the daily VI top, stop above the 1m candle high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:09](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2529) — sub-1-minute topic starts; [0:50:02](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3002) — lower half = entry window; [0:51:39](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3099) — close below CE, next candle entry zone
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He admits entering in the upper half works for him sometimes but advises beginners to use the lower half so they are in the move without fear of missing it.

### Stop placement under high volatility: frame the stop on the setup timeframe
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** In normal volatility you could place the stop one tick above the high of the sub-1-minute gap/swing. But when volatility is extreme (e.g. around news), ranges overlap the prior candle's range, so go back to the 1-minute setup timeframe and use its high for the stop. Tight stops on tiny timeframes get stopped repeatedly, and commissions and "paper cuts" compound the pain.
- **How to identify:** Elevated volatility = wide candles, wicks overlapping.
- **How to use:** Stop one tick above the 1m high that formed the setup (or just above the candle high); accept fewer contracts. Entry in the lower half, stop from the higher timeframe where the setup formed. "You're probably going to trade with less contracts, and that's a good thing."
- **Example in this video:** The stop sits above the 1m candle high which formed the SIBI at the daily VI top.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:29](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2909) — stop on the 1m high due to volatility; [0:52:29](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3149) — go outside the small-timeframe range; [0:53:16](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3196) — fewer contracts

### Trailing a one-contract position with displacement  (aliases: stop loss management, strangling the position)
- **Kind:** rule / trade management
- **What ICT says:** With one contract you cannot take a partial (it would close the only position). Instead, wait for displacement below a key low (a short-term low that would be a partial point) and move the stop to just above the high of the displacement candle. Displacement means price must go below a key low or above a key high; it creates a short-term low, then dives lower, closes, and the next candle keeps running. Repeat at each key low.
- **How to identify:** Price pierces the key low to the downside and leaves a SIBI; that confirms displacement. A candle that merely creates a new low without piercing the prior low is not displacement.
- **How to use:** Stop moves: initial stop above the 1m high -> above the first displacement candle's high -> above the high of the leg that starts the next run lower, etc. When it reaches the SSL/low of the VI, close (or let the stop take you out), then anticipate a retracement to the VI CE.
- **Example in this video:** Short from the top of the daily VI: price takes out the first key low (displacement, SIBI) -> stop moves to that candle's high; price gravitates to the daily VI CE; below it more SSL; each time price takes a low and leaves a SIBI, the stop is moved to the candle starting the run; final target low of the VI.
- **Timestamps:** [0:57:11](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3431) — move stop under displacement with one contract; [0:58:44](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3524) — pierce low and leave SIBI; [1:00:21](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3621) — displace below the low, then move the stop to that candle's high
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Stop being greedy": he would not have caught the full drop; closing near the VI low is acceptable.

### Sell-side delivery vs sell-side liquidity
- **Kind:** definition / clarification
- **What ICT says:** The run lower of price action is sell-side delivery, not sell-side liquidity. Sell-side liquidity is the resting stops below a low (the target); delivery is the leg of price moving down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:58:44](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3524) — "This run is sell-side delivery, not sell-side liquidity"

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Always keep the chart on New York time. London open killzone: 2:00-5:00 ET (0:42:58).
- No positions ahead of PPI/CPI; watch only at the release; then target the pre-release liquidity.
- Event horizon: midpoint between two static points (old lows/highs or NWOG/NDOG) is the draw; break beyond it = sustained run likely.
- Daily VI: top = premium entry, CE = always a draw, low = further objective.
- FVG immediately against a wick: use the wick gradient, not the FVG.
- High-frequency entry: lower half of the 1m/30s FVG; one micro; stop on the 1m setup high if volatility elevated.
- Minimum target 15 handles (10 handles is what some take).
- One contract: trail the stop above the displacement candle high each time a key low is taken.
- He closes the stream at 9:10-9:15 ET; PPI at 8:30 ET.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Drawdown mitigation (0:23:46-0:37:15): practise on demo deliberately going into drawdown and building it back so a negative balance stops frightening you; nobody avoids drawdown (intra-day, intra-week, multi-week). Not for live accounts.
- Teaching exercise with his kids: they call buy/sell, he clicks with a large real balance; they feel the weight because it is "dad's money". Point: people treat their own money casually in winners/losers (patience in losers, none in winners).
- Curiosity entries (0:30:09-0:31:43): entering with no model because you are bored, planning to reverse if wrong, is curiosity, not a model, and curiosity blows accounts.
- Money in the account is like breath: it goes in and out; do not hold losers until margin call; use a stop. Back-test so you know session duration vs daily range.
- Do not use super-tight stops with many contracts; use small size and a stop that matches the setup timeframe.
- Public statements: he does not trade ahead of PPI/CPI; fundamentals/data are untrustworthy, rely on price action.
- Short-form teaching is bullet-point only; long-form is where nuance lives.

## Glossary candidates
`EVENT HORIZON` — midpoint/CE between two key static PD arrays (old lows, opposite NWOGs); price gravitates to it and a break beyond signals sustained delivery.
`HIGH FREQUENCY ENTRY` — sub-1-minute entry in the lower half of a small FVG inside a higher-timeframe PD array.
`SELL-SIDE DELIVERY` — the run lower in price, distinct from sell-side liquidity (the resting stops).
