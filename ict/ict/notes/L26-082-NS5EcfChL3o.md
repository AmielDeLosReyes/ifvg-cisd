## Concepts

### Two Pools of Liquidity and Range Grading (aliases: dual-level liquidity, high and low pools)
- **Kind:** framework / measurement
- **What ICT says:** To define a trade zone, identify two pools of liquidity: a higher pool (upper level) and a lower pool (lower level). Grade these two levels; the range between them is your operational zone. Use this range to identify equilibrium (midpoint), premium side (above midpoint), and discount side (below midpoint). Shorts are taken on the premium side; longs on discount. This framework predicts where price will consolidate and where it will break.
- **How to identify:** Scan the daily/15m chart; find two significant support/resistance zones (old lows, inversion FVGs, swing points); mark them as high and low pools.
- **How to use:** Use the range to frame your trading zone. Target breaks below the lower pool or above the higher pool for true directional moves.
- **Example in this video:** Michael identified two pools: 30,055.75 (high) and 29,886 (low). The range between them (169.75 points) was the zone. Equilibrium = 30,055.75 - 29,886 = ~29,970. Above equilibrium = premium (short zone); below = discount (long zone).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This framework is institutional; price oscillates between pools, and breaks predict the next move.

### Equilibrium and Premium/Discount Sides (aliases: midpoint, range midpoint)
- **Kind:** measurement / zone classification
- **What ICT says:** Equilibrium is the midpoint between the two pools of liquidity. Above equilibrium is the premium side (where shorts are taken); below is the discount side (where longs are taken). When price is in premium and showing weakness, expect a drop to equilibrium and then to the lower pool. Conversely, when in discount and strong, expect a run to equilibrium and then to upper pool.
- **How to identify:** Calculate: (High Pool + Low Pool) / 2 = Equilibrium.
- **How to use:** Use equilibrium as a first-take profit zone for shorts (from premium). Use as a reversal point for longs (from discount).
- **Example in this video:** Equilibrium = roughly 29,970 (between 30,055.75 and 29,886). Michael planned to "sell short in the premium side of that range... divided by this little red dashed line here. That's equilibrium."
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:10](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=430) (equilibrium divides range; short premium side) · [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=822) (equilibrium at 30,055.75 and 29,886; signal when market gets through halfway point).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Equilibrium is precise; it's where smart money often adjusts positions.

### Deferred Delivery (aliases: delayed move, two-stage vs. one-stage drop)
- **Kind:** pattern refinement
- **What ICT says:** Sometimes price moves in the expected direction but not in the expected structure. Michael expected a two-stage drop (down-consolidate-down), but price "ripped the entire daily range down all in one fell swoop." This is still a valid move—it's just a deferred delivery (delayed structure). The market still hit the targets; it just took a different path. Don't disqualify a move because it doesn't match your time/structure expectation.
- **How to identify:** Compare expected structure vs. actual price action; if direction is right but structure differs, it's deferred delivery.
- **How to use:** Trust the direction and targets even if the structure isn't textbook. Deferred delivery still delivers.
- **Example in this video:** Expected two-stage drop over multiple candles; instead, price dropped in one candle move. Still delivered to the correct level.

### Stop Hunt (aliases: stop loss sweep, false reversal)
- **Kind:** institutional pattern
- **What ICT says:** A stop hunt is when price spikes in one direction to sweep stop losses (rout out traders), then reverses. Example: price runs up, triggers sell-side stops, then drops. Michael notes: "It was a stop hunt. It runs low." Recognize these and don't get shaken out; they're opportunities to add or re-enter.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a spike that wicks above/below a level, then immediately reverses without closing in that direction.
- **How to use:** When you spot a stop hunt, prepare to trade in the direction AFTER the hunt (when price resumes). Don't panic-exit; the hunt is often followed by the true directional move.
- **Example in this video:** Price ran up, triggered stops above a high, then dropped sharply. The drop was the true move.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:56](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=536) (stop hunt: runs low, took out short-term high, it was a stop hunt).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Stop hunts are predatory institutional tactics; recognize them and turn them to your advantage.

### Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency with Gradient Level Anchoring (aliases: anchored iFVG, grid-aligned inefficiency)
- **Kind:** validation method + pattern
- **What ICT says:** An iFVG is more reliable if its candlestick "lays on top of" a gradient level (a graded support/resistance from the two pools of liquidity). This anchoring validates that the inefficiency is on the institutional radar. Michael emphasizes: "The candlestick has to be laying on a gradient level. That's what validates your smart money concept."
- **How to identify:** First, grade your two pools of liquidity and create gradient levels between them. Then, identify candlesticks that sit directly on these grades. If a candlestick is on a grade and contains a buy-side imbalance, it's a high-probability iFVG.
- **How to use:** Prioritize iFVGs that align with gradient levels. These are institutional reference points; trade them with higher conviction.
- **Example in this video:** A buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency candlestick was sitting directly on a gradient level from the two pools. Michael noted: "What makes this one so good is because it's anchored to a gradient level." This validated it as a tradeable iFVG.

### Speed, Distance, Velocity Confirmation (aliases: momentum confirmation, acceleration signal)
- **Kind:** confirmation signal
- **What ICT says:** Once price breaks through a key level (like equilibrium or consequent encroachment), look for speed, distance, and velocity—i.e., accelerating movement without hesitation. This is confirmation the institutional move is real. If price breaks slowly or with retracements, conviction is questioned.
- **How to identify:** After price breaks a key level, check: does it move fast and far without reversing back? If yes = strong; if no = weak.
- **How to use:** Use speed/distance/velocity as a filter. If your break doesn't show these characteristics, the move may fail. If it does, hold and let it run.
- **Example in this video:** "I wanted to see speed and distance and velocity. Once we get through the halfway point or consequent encouragement between these two pools of liquidity..." Michael was waiting for acceleration after equilibrium break.

### Low-Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions (aliases: clean price action, efficient movement)
- **Kind:** environment recognition
- **What ICT says:** When price shows "willingness to escape and really run and leave little inefficiencies," that's low-resistance liquidity run conditions. Contrast this with choppy price action (many candles sitting inside one inefficiency, lots of retracements). Low-resistance conditions = tight stops, big runners. Choppy conditions = skip or dial back.
- **How to identify:** Watch for inefficiencies followed by clean, quick retracements (1–2 candles), then a run. Repeat this pattern = low-resistance.
- **How to use:** In low-resistance conditions, dial down risk per trade; let winners run. In choppy conditions, take profits earlier; don't add; maybe skip altogether.
- **Example in this video:** ES showed clean inefficiencies with small retracements = low-resistance. NQ had consolidations with many candles = choppy. Michael preferred ES structure.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:32](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=1112) (low-resistance liquidity run conditions: inefficiencies with small retracements) · [0:19:19](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=1159) (high-resistance choppy conditions: many candles in one inefficiency).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Environment recognition is key to position sizing; don't over-size in choppy markets.

### Price and Time Alignment (aliases: convergence, setup maturity)
- **Kind:** principle
- **What ICT says:** "If those things occur, chances are you probably got the tiger by the tail." When price reaches its target AND time aligns (macro, fibonacci time, swing high/low timing), the move is validated. Focusing solely on price without time is incomplete.
- **How to identify:** Combine price targets (FVGs, liquidity) with time confluences (macros, swing counts, Fibonacci time).
- **How to use:** Wait for BOTH price and time to align before entering with size. Setups that align both are highest probability.
- **Example in this video:** Michael identified price targets (two pools, equilibrium, lower pool); also noted macros and timing. When both aligned, conviction was highest.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:19](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=1159) (price and time lineup; if those occur, you've got the tiger by the tail).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Price without time = lucky; time without price = guessing; both = institutional setup.

### Gradient Levels (aliases: graded levels, range subdivisions)
- **Kind:** measurement tool
- **What ICT says:** Gradient levels are intermediate levels graded between the two main pools of liquidity. They're shown as light lines between the high and low pool. These serve as micro-targets and confirmation points. When a candlestick sits on a gradient level, it's on institutional radar.
- **How to identify:** Between your two pools, divide the range into multiple sub-levels (e.g., every 25–50 points).
- **How to use:** Use gradients as interim targets and as validation anchors for inefficiencies.
- **Example in this video:** Michael showed gradient levels between 30,055.75 and 29,886; candlesticks sitting on these lines validated their institutional importance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:21](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=381) (gradient levels shown between high and low pools).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Gradients are finer detail; they're where true ICT traders see institutional reference points.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Two pools of liquidity: define high and low reference points.
- Equilibrium: midpoint; premium above, discount below.
- Shorts in premium side when structure is bearish; longs in discount when bullish.
- iFVG validation: candlestick must lay on gradient level.
- Algorithmic macro 9:50–10:10 ET: high-probability execution window.
- Speed, distance, velocity after key breaks = confirmation of real move.
- Low-resistance conditions: inefficiencies with 1–2-candle retracements = great for runners.
- Choppy conditions: many candles in one inefficiency = reduce position size.
- Deferred delivery: direction right but structure different = still valid.
- Price and time alignment: both must match for highest conviction.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Precision over speed:** Michael spends time grading levels and aligning candlesticks to gradient levels; this precision predicts moves. Most retail traders skip this.
- **Flexibility on structure:** Deferred delivery is OK; focus on targets, not perfect textbook execution.
- **Premise-driven trading:** "You have to have a premise. You have to know where the market's going to go." Not reactive; intentional.
- **Selective iFVG choice:** Don't trade every gap; only trade those anchored to gradient levels.
- **Environment recognition:** Know the difference between low-resistance and choppy conditions; size accordingly.
- **Stop hunts aren't losses:** Recognize them for what they are (institutional tactics); they often precede the true move.
- **Transparency:** Michael shows his executions (not just past wins) to prove the framework works consistently.
