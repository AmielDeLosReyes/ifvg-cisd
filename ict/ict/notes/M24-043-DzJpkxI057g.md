## Summary
ICT tape-reads the NQ open on Monday 7 Oct 2024 while deliberately talking very little (he says he is losing his voice). He frames the day around the opening range gap, using its midpoint (consequent encroachment) as the initial draw, and sets a strict entry condition on the one-minute chart: trade above the first presented FVG, then use it as an inversion and displace higher into a new FVG. That setup never forms, because the market is in HIGH resistance liquidity run conditions (price spends a lot of time inside inefficiencies, deep retracements, no precision). The core lesson is that you must classify delivery as high vs. low resistance, and that in high resistance a "perfect" entry is unlikely to be offered, so staying out is a valid, no-loss outcome. He also shows how a volume imbalance worked as the reference that attracted price up toward the mid-gap target.

## Concepts

### High / Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases: "high resistance liquidity run", "low resistance liquidity run")
- **Kind:** definition | rule (market-condition filter)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ask yourself early in the session which condition delivery is in. He asks the viewers explicitly "are we in low resistance liquidity run conditions or high resistance," and answers: high resistance, "that's why it's [so] deliberate" (slow/choppy).
  - Diagnostic he gives: "are you seeing fair value gaps spending a lot of time inside them?" If price keeps spending time inside FVGs / inefficiencies, you are in high resistance.
  - High resistance means: lack of precision; moves are deferred; retracements are deeper; price runs pull back "real deep each time"; targets (PD arrays) are not delivered to as precisely. It is described as uncomfortable, especially when building larger positions.
  - Low resistance means: easy to time, easy to frame low risk, easy to find low-hanging objectives for partials and ultimate targets; price "goes where you want it to go"; smooth run to the target, then you go to the sidelines and wait for the next setup. It rewards patience and goes to targets "almost near effortlessly."
  - You can trade high resistance conditions, but expect many more stop-outs, frustration and anxiety in and during the trade.
  - Just because you see a setup does not make it high probability; in high resistance "nothing in here is high probability." The danger is looking back afterwards and thinking "I wish I'd done this."
  - He refers to this as a concept "I shared with you last week" (prior video in the series), and notes that knowing it lets you sit on your hands.
  - Observation that carries forward: if the morning session is high resistance and unclear, the lunch macro and afternoon session should be cleaner (setups easier to identify).
- **How to identify:** (1) FVGs/inefficiencies are being traded inside for long stretches rather than displaced away from; (2) price is range-bound and "disorganized" after the open; (3) retracements are deep and not precise; (4) no clean displacement leaving fresh FVGs.
- **How to use:** If high resistance: be slow to act, do not force the A+ model, expect the textbook return-to-PD-array entry NOT to come, and consider framing a different reference (here, an inefficiency that price was likely to be drawn to). If low resistance: take the clean model (see below) and target quickly.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday 7 Oct 2024, 9:30–10:30 ET. Price chops around the opening range gap area and the first presented FVG without displacing; the mid-gap is not reached within the first 30 minutes. ICT labels it high resistance at [0:51:04] and says that is why nothing "perfect" developed.

### Opening Range Gap (RTH opening gap) — high, midpoint (CE), quadrants, as initial draw  (aliases: "opening range gap", "mid gap", "new week opening gap" mentioned separately)
- **Kind:** rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Always starting the opening range with that initial bias": the first 30 minutes of the session he frames price toward the opening range gap midpoint, which he calls the "mid gap" (the consequent encroachment of the gap). It is "always the initial draw for the first 30 minutes."
  - The gap here was a discount-side gap: price had been trading below Friday's settlement ("we kept lower than Friday's settlement price"), so the mid gap (CE of the discount opening gap) is the upward objective. Full gap closure is above the mid gap; he also mentions upper and lower quadrants of the gap and the previous Friday settlement as further levels.
  - Captions give the opening range gap high at "2222.5", the mid gap near "2,179.5"/"21795" (garbled; consistent magnitude would be NQ ~20,xxx but the caption digits are unreliable — caption unclear). Do not use these numbers literally.
  - He keeps his bias to one side of the market: because he does not want to see it as a shorting opportunity, "if it goes lower it's going to go without me."
  - He notes price "traded back to the lower quadrant of the opening range gap" and later says any movement above the mid gap takes price to the new week opening gap low and its CE.
- **How to identify:** Mark the gap between the previous RTH close (settlement) and the 9:30 open; split into quadrants with the midpoint (CE) as the central target; note which side of the gap price is on relative to Friday's settlement.
- **How to use:** Use the mid gap as the initial draw on liquidity for the first 30 minutes; only take longs (in this bias) after a specific model sequence (see next concept); do not sell just because price drops below the gap or trades lower, unless other information provides a bearish read.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 7 Oct 2024. Bias: reach for the mid gap of the opening range gap. Price instead trades below, tags the minor sell side, bounces, spends the first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00) not reaching it, then later in the session rallies to the mid gap after tagging a volume imbalance low.

### One-minute First Presented FVG + Inversion + Subsequent FVG entry model (opening range, tape-reading version)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Entry conditions he sets for himself (and for "Caleb") on the 1-minute and 15-second charts:
  1. Identify the FIRST PRESENTED fair value gap on the one-minute chart formed in the opening range (the first FVG after 9:30; he defines the specific one by a candle's high and another candle's low).
  2. Wait for price to trade above that FVG, come back down, react off it (treat it as an inversion/support), and "displace higher."
  3. That displacement must leave a subsequent fair value gap (he looks for it on the 15-second chart).
  4. THAT subsequent FVG is the entry, "not just trading back into" the first one; trading into the first FVG and sitting in it is "not the entry."
  - Price "just can't be sitting in it and trade away from it." It has to displace away.
  - The entry is a run to the mid gap (opening range gap CE).
  - Optionally: after trading below it, price coming back up to treat it as inversion; displacing lower and creating an FVG on the 15-sec chart would be how he'd trade the afternoon short side if bearish, but he stresses he is not bearish now.
- **How to identify:** (a) first presented FVG on 1m marked; (b) price trades above, returns, reacts; (c) displacement higher; (d) fresh FVG on 15-sec; (e) all of this inside his one-sided bias.
- **How to use:** Enter at/in the new 15-sec FVG after the displacement, target the mid gap. No FVG = no trade. He says explicitly "no FVG formed, no trade, if/then or else."
- **Example in this video:** NQ 7 Oct 2024 — price gives "a little bit of a run" off the first presented FVG but no FVG forms on the 15-sec chart, so he does not trade; later he highlights that a 1m FVG formed around 9:31–10:00 (shaded orange) and that price traded above it without the required inversion + FVG sequence.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:18:25](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1105) — first presented FVG on 1m, treat as inversion, subsequent FVG after that = entry.
  - [0:24:29](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1469) — "that's the trade ... not the trade" (distinguishes entry from merely trading into the gap).
  - [0:32:45](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1965) — wants an attempt to get back into the first presented FVG; if no run above, no inversion and no FVG = not touching it.
  - [0:36:57](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2217) — "no fair value gap formed, no trade."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In low resistance conditions he says this would deliver precisely: price trades above the gap, comes back down touching it (maybe to the mid gap), displaces higher and creates an FVG on the 15-sec chart (that would be Caleb's setup). In high resistance that sequence is unlikely, so you do not force it.

### Patience / Standing aside in the first 30 minutes ("slow to act")
- **Kind:** rule (execution/psychology)
- **What ICT says:** After a weekend everybody is "champing at the bit" to get into a trade — "this is when you want to be slow slow to act." Price within the first 30 minutes is "so far a little disorganized"; the fact it came down and hit a level "puts you on the sidelines till 10." He gives it until about 10:15 for something to materialize; otherwise "cut bait for today" and treat it as a patience exercise.
- **How to use:** If your predefined conditions do not materialize, there is "no trade, no losing trade, no missed opportunity." Knowing "why you're still" (why you are standing aside) is more important than where the next entry is.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:45](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1185) — slow to act after weekend; [0:39:22](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2362) — wait until 10:15 then cut bait.

### Volume Imbalance as a magnet / "least rigid" PD array
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Volume imbalances "are the least in terms of rigidness" — price is expected to trade through them and then return above/below, not necessarily respect them hard. "I mentioned this to you many times that means you've got to let it trade through it, then come back above it."
  - In the example, he flagged a volume imbalance inside the move (5-minute volume imbalance) and said price could "spike down into that"; price did spike into it, making the very low of the volume imbalance — the candle's low hit 2163.25 (caption), the imbalance's boundary, i.e. a one-tick variance — then rallied to the mid gap.
  - He used the volume imbalance because, in high resistance conditions, price was not likely to come back down to the first presented FVG ("already proven ... the absence of precision"), so he redirected attention to a nearer inefficiency (the shaded volume imbalance) where bodies stopped and a small wick below it was "the damage," followed by a rally into the CE of the opening gap.
  - Relative equal highs were noted just above the imbalance as the next liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Open space between candle bodies where wicks overlap (volume imbalance); watch how closes (bodies) behave relative to it; look at the close/low of the candle that tags it.
- **How to use:** Use it as a nearby reference in high resistance when the "perfect" FVG is too far away; expect it to be traded through with a wick before the move.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/5m, 7 Oct 2024, late in the morning: price rallies, makes relative equal highs, pulls back into a 5-minute volume imbalance, wicks to its low within one tick (around "2163.25" caption value), then rallies to the mid gap (CE).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Start every session's opening range with an initial bias; for the first 30 minutes the opening range gap mid gap (CE) is the draw.
- Entry sequence (long bias): first presented 1m FVG → trade above it → return and react (inversion) → displace higher → new FVG on 15-sec = entry; target mid gap. No FVG = no trade.
- Do not enter just because price returns into an FVG; it has to displace away from it.
- Classify the day as high or low resistance liquidity run by looking at how long price lingers inside FVGs; in high resistance expect no precision and deeper retracement; do not expect textbook entries.
- Wait until at least ~10:15 ET for conditions to materialize; otherwise stand aside.
- First 30 minutes = 9:30–10:00 ET. The first presented FVG on 1m is drawn between 9:31 and 10:00.
- Volume imbalance: allow it to trade through, then see price come back above/below it.
- Calendar context: no news Monday/Tuesday; CPI and PPI later that week, so expect volatility at the end of the week and slow but clean price early week.
- Prices quoted in captions (2222.5, 2179.5, 2163.25) are garbled; do not rely on them.

## Glossary candidates
`High resistance liquidity run` — market delivery with lots of time spent inside FVGs, deep pullbacks and imprecise delivery to PD arrays.
`Low resistance liquidity run` — smooth, precise delivery that goes quickly to targets.
`Mid gap` — the consequent encroachment (50%) of the opening range gap, used as the initial draw.
`First presented FVG` — the first fair value gap that forms after the session open (1m chart), used as a reference.
`Volume imbalance` — gap between candle bodies with overlapping wicks; "least rigid" PD array.
`Cut bait` — stop waiting for a setup and stand aside for the session.
