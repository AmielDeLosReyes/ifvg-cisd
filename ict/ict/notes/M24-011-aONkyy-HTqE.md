## Summary
ICT builds a stripped-down, learn-it-in-order framework for his son Caleb ("Caleb's model") and demonstrates it live on NQ, Monday Aug 19, 2024 (Jackson Hole week). The core loop is: mark the liquidity above and below (relative equal highs/lows in the last two hours), mark the gap references (RTH opening range gap, new week opening gap, old new day opening gap), sit out the first 30 minutes (9:30-10:00 opening range), wait for ONE side to be taken first (the Judas side), then enter on a fair value gap with the "Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill" (IOFED). That means a limit order at the gap's high edge plus one tick, with the gap expected to stay partly open, aiming at an easy, low-threshold target such as the lower quadrant of the new week opening gap. He calls the IOFED fair value gap live before the candle closes and it plays out. He then re-examines the same move on a 15-second chart to teach the "price delivery continuum" (sub-minute charts are anchored to the 1-minute, 5-minute and 15-minute logic), balanced price ranges, wicks as gaps, immediate rebalance, and why a level that "should" be touched but isn't touched is a strength signal. Smaller teachings: Jackson Hole week characteristics (overlapping, back-and-forth delivery, so take early partials), the two-stage FOMC reaction, the 3:15 last-hour macro, the open-to-low "sweet spot" for buying a Power-of-3 up day, and a new coinage, the **Reaper fair value gap** (a month-anchored FVG).

## Concepts

### Caleb's model (the beginner framework / learning ladder)  (aliases: "Caleb's entry mechanism", "Caleb's model", "session draw on liquidity first")
- **Kind:** entry model + curriculum (new coinage)
- **What ICT says:** He is deliberately forcing his son (a beginner who cannot yet determine the draw on liquidity) to use ONE entry mechanism, the fair value gap, inside order-flow context (price, not level 2 / DOM / volume profile). The aim is a baseline understanding of price action. The FVG may later lead him to see order blocks, breakers and inversion FVGs. It "should not necessarily be yours", and ICT says it does not matter which PD array you start with (order block, OTE, etc.) as long as you start and watch price daily.
- **How to identify / the steps as taught:**
  1. Outline liquidity above and below: relative equal highs and lows. Caleb only looks to the right of 7:00 ET, i.e. the last two hours before 9:00-9:30. Advanced students may also use the London range and Power-of-3 context from earlier teaching.
  2. Mark gap references: RTH opening range gap, new week opening gap, the nearest new day opening gap(s).
  3. Do not trade the first 30 minutes (opening range, 9:30-10:00). Watch which side of the liquidity is taken first. ("Water disturbed, jagged" = the taken side; smooth = the untouched side.)
  4. The side taken FIRST is the trap (Judas swing). The second side is the draw. ICT frames it with the Bible line "the first shall be last".
  5. After the first side is taken, wait for price to reclaim a reference (e.g. back above an old NDOG), then enter on a fair value gap using IOFED (see below).
  6. Take partials at an easy, low-threshold target (e.g. lower quadrant of the NWOG).
  7. Graduated later steps: hold through lunch and close at about 1:30 if in profit; eventually frame the full day (open-to-low zone) and then daily bias from weekly/monthly. He says you cannot derive daily bias until you can read the session draw.
- **How to use:** Session draws on liquidity are "dynamic" (exist above and below). Learn session bias first, then daily bias.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 19: relative equal highs sit above, relative equal lows below. Price takes the sell side first after 9:30, sweeps below old lows and an old NDOG, then reclaims and runs to the buy side and NWOG.
- **Timestamps:** [1:32:04](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=5524) session draws are dynamic, learn this before daily bias · [1:46:27](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=6387) only look back two hours from 9:00 to 7:00 · [2:08:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7693) take first side, second comes later · [2:29:47](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=8987) graduated steps: lunch hold, close at 1:30

### RTH Opening Range Gap (and RTH vs ETH awareness)  (aliases: opening range gap, ORG)
- **Kind:** definition + refinement
- **What ICT says:** The opening range gap is the difference between the regular-session settlement (4:15 pm ET on the prior day; the last tick is the "close") and the 9:30 ET regular-trading-hours opening price. He explains the session mechanics: electronic trading continues until 5:00 pm ET, stops, and resumes at 6:00 pm ET on a normal day. On Friday (or a holiday) it does not resume until Sunday 6:00 pm ET. On an RTH chart there is no candle at 4:15, so the next candle is Monday 9:30.
- **How to identify:** Anchor one edge at the prior-day 4:15 pm settlement (Friday Aug 16 here) and the other at the 9:30 open. Draw a fib on the low and high to get quadrants (lower quadrant, consequent encroachment, upper quadrant). Shade it.
- **How to use:** Price uses the gap's quadrants as reaction levels: "found footing" off the lower quadrant, support at the midpoint, "bodies staying inside" while it grinds. Spending too long inside the gap means they are "not in a hurry" to pierce the high. What he wants to see for upside: a close above the gap's CE, then a next candle that comes down and fails to touch the CE, then a spring toward the upper quadrant (0:25:58).
- **Example in this video:** Price came back up about three-quarters of the gap early (just short of the upper quadrant), later respected the CE and the high repeatedly. Bodies stayed inside, wicks overshot. Candle pokes above the high did not close above it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1033) what makes it the opening range gap · [0:18:02](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1082) 4:15 pm settlement, 5:00/6:00 restart rules · [0:55:11](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3311) RTH vs ETH toggle warning
- **Nuance / caveats:** (My inference from his remarks) The ORG's high edge is Friday's 4:15 pm settlement (19,604), which is also the NWOG's low edge, so the pink ORG sat just below the NWOG; price had to climb through it to reach the NWOG low/lower quadrant. ICT warns he has mis-weighted a level before because ETH vs RTH was toggled on the chart. His habit is to say aloud "I'm back in electronic trading hours" and then confirm the chart label reads ETH.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)  (aliases: new week opening gap, NWOG high/low/CE)
- **Kind:** definition + refinement
- **What ICT says:** NWOG = Friday's settlement price (the last tick/close) vs. Sunday's 6:00 pm ET opening tick. Whichever is higher is the NWOG high, whichever is lower is the NWOG low. Annotate it as "New Week Opening Gap August 18 2024". A viewer asked why the gap isn't measured lower; he answers that the last tick is the close and that is what you anchor to. In this video Friday's settlement is 19,604 (NQ) and the gap is just under 20 handles (exact edges garbled, caption unclear).
- **How to identify:** Use the 1-minute chart to calibrate the exact settlement and open prints. Drop a fib on the gap low and high for CE, lower quadrant (25%) and upper quadrant (75%). Extend it forward.
- **How to use:** The NWOG is "highly influential ... like a black hole", and price gravitates to it. "If you don't extend the new week opening gaps on your chart and keep them on there for a few weeks you're trading blind." It is a PD array that can serve as a draw once you have identified the nearest liquidity. See *Target selection* below.
- **Example in this video:** Early: price comes back up to the NWOG low and reacts off the lower quadrant of the opening range gap (0:11:28). Later: price runs from the IOFED entry to the NWOG low, then CE and the NWOG high, with bodies respecting each level. The pre-7:00 exit target was the NWOG's lower quadrant (see Target selection).

### Quadrants, consequent encroachment and wicks treated as gaps  (aliases: CE, upper/lower quadrant, "wicks are gaps")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Any range or gap (NWOG, opening range gap, a candle wick, a fair value gap) is measured in quadrants: low, lower quadrant (25%), CE (50%), upper quadrant (75%), high. A wick can be "treated like a gap": half of the wick is its consequent encroachment. Price often stops at the quadrant rather than the CE. Example: the sweep low stopped exactly (to the tick) at the upper quadrant of the first wick and never touched the midpoint of that wick or of the lesser wick.
- **How to use:**
  - Not touching CE is a strength tell: if you are long and price cannot even dip into a level that "could" support it, it is immediate feedback that price wants to go higher. ICT calls this a paradigm shift. Support does not "push" price; the failure to reach it is the information.
  - Bodies versus wicks: bodies closing at a level matter; wicks are "allowed to do the damage".
  - Conditions he states: "no close below the midpoint of the wick", "I'd like to see this stay open", "OK for the candle to touch while open as long as it doesn't close below".
- **Example in this video:** Wick CE of a 1-minute candle watched from the 15-second chart; price came down toward it and did not touch it, then rallied (3:15:10). His recorded pre-7:00 market-order entry went in as price dropped into the CE of a 1-minute wick treated as a gap (2:55:12), coinciding with the 15-second gap plus order block (3:14:22).
- **Timestamps:** [0:52:03](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3123) sweep low stops at the upper quadrant of the wick, not its CE · [2:55:12](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10512) wicks are gaps, CE of the wick · [3:02:30](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10950) why not touching a support level is the signal
- **Nuance / caveats:** He contrasts this with support/resistance thinking ("support ain't pushing anything").

### Candles are still forming: let wicks happen  (aliases: wicks outside the lines)
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** A wick can only form because a candle is first a full-bodied candle at the extreme of the wick. While a candle is open and forming, price can trade outside a gap's boundary or level. That is normal. Do not demand price stay inside the lines. When ICT first showed Caleb an FVG, Caleb assumed price would fill exactly to the middle and never go outside, and was alarmed by a candle coloring outside the gap's low. ICT compares it to a child coloring outside the lines: not a failure.
- **How to use:** Judge the level on the close, not on intra-candle excursions. Give the candle room to form its wick or you will be "humbled fast" and lose trust in reading price.
- **Timestamps:** [0:47:14](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2834) Caleb's fair value gap story · [0:48:48](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2928) "no close below it, can touch it" · [1:43:14](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=6194) coloring outside the lines analogy (approx.)

### Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: "institutional order flow entry drill", "entry drill", Caleb's entry mechanism)
- **Kind:** entry model (this lecture's core)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A fair value gap is formed during a displacement move. While the displacement candle is still forming you anticipate the FVG. Once the next candle opens, you drop a buy limit at the gap's high edge (the top of a bullish FVG, i.e. the low of the third candle) plus ONE tick. That assures a fill. You do not try to buy the low end of the gap or its midpoint.
  - The expectation: price retraces into the gap and goes a tick or so below the reference candle's low, "leaving a portion of the gap open" (does not close the whole FVG), then displaces higher. Each FVG used "as a propellant" should stay at least partly open; the failure to fill completely is the signature of strength ("cracks in order flow").
  - The gap can fill completely and still be a valid trade; you just take more drawdown. From the candle high ~19,576.75 to low ~19,580.50 he quotes "five handles or so" (his figure; captions unclear) as the extra risk, plus commissions.
  - Sizing the fill logic: he has had limit orders not filled even when price traded to the level, with real brokers too, so always include the +1 tick.
- **How to identify:** 1) Displacement after the first side of liquidity is taken. 2) A fresh FVG forming on the candle that is still open. 3) Price first reclaimed a reference (NDOG, a prior low/wick) so you are on the right side. 4) You want to see it NOT close the gap and NOT close below the CE of the relevant wick.
- **How to use:** Entry: limit at gap high + 1 tick. Stop: below the swing/wick lows where price has been "traveling back and forth" (he does not give a fixed number of ticks; e.g. below the wick-low area and the lower low). Targets: first threshold = NWOG low (he calls it the first threshold), then CE, then upper quadrant, and he expects a pierce through the NWOG high because there are stops resting at the smooth highs. Take partials at the low-threshold target. Preplacing limit orders "100-some handles away" and dragging them in when you expect a Silver Bullet-type FVG is a practical tip (1:19:19).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Mon Aug 19, about 10:20-10:25 ET (derived from the video clock; he calls it a 10:00-window/Silver Bullet-type FVG): price sweeps equal lows, runs into the Aug 16 NDOG low area, reclaims and closes above, drops to the NDOG high, then a displacement candle forms. While it is forming ICT says the FVG will form and that price will dip about one tick below the candle low. Result: dip of two ticks to about 19,580.00, then a second dip to about 19,578.50. Net drawdown versus the 19,580.50 reference = 2 handles = 8 ticks = $40 per NQ contract. The gap's lower portion stayed open (low did not reach the candle high 19,576.75). Price then rallies to the NWOG low, CE and upper area and pushes through the NWOG high with energy. Later he wicks through the buy-side highs but admits surprise that they were not cleanly taken (he had thought the first punch was the real run). He admits he was off on the dip ("expected one tick, got two" and, by the $40 figure, two handles).
- **Timestamps:** [1:04:46](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3886) FVG below the wick, leave a portion open · [1:09:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4178) limit price and drawdown math (8 ticks, $40) · [1:22:31](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4951) buy the high of the gap plus one tick · [1:32:52](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=5572) what if it closes the gap · [2:00:54](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7254) called before it formed, not closed, "off to the races"
- **Gaps as a strength gauge (1:28:07):** whenever a PD array such as a gap is not traded to, or is not completely overlapped, that tells you about the strength of the underlying move.
- **Nuance / caveats:** ICT notes he is not forcing every student to use it. He says people "prematurely teaching" his concepts say to buy the low end of the gap for a tiny stop. He says that precision is not needed; where the market will draw matters more. He admits he cannot always get fills at the exact tick and treats the tick buffer as essential. ### Order block, rejection block, breaker and "actual gap" as used here
- **Kind:** example only / light refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Order block: the down-closed candle ("means absolutely nothing" to others) before the up-move; its high (~19,591.25) is traded above by the next candle. He notes that a pyramid add at the low of the candle above it would have missed its fill by one tick.
  - Rejection block: "the lowest down-close candle in the swing"; the sweep cleared it and stopped.
  - Bullish breaker: the last up-close candle in a range after a retracement breaks to the upside. Price trades down into it and then shifts structure.
  - "Actual gap" with an order block: an actual open/close gap (no overlap) following a down-close candle while you are bullish is a strong PD array: as soon as price trades into it, it is a buy. Even without the order block it is still an FVG. Gaps are also classed as breakaway vs common (common gaps can fill in; he labels a green level a breakaway because price never retraced into it).
- **Example in this video:** The pre-7:00 trade: a gap with an order block after a down-close candle; he bought at market as price slammed into it (3:14:22-3:15:10). The 15-second sequence showed MSS, breaker, FVG and CE together (3:25:43-3:26:31).

### Immediate rebalance
- **Kind:** definition + rule (on-side indicator)
- **What ICT says:** A big down-close candle followed immediately by a candle that opens and goes right back up to the previous candle (before the down close, or the larger-range candle). He calls it one of the best on-side indicators you could get, provided liquidity has not already been taken. He adds that if he were short he would pyramid on a retest of that candle's low and look for speed through it, then treat that as the first partial (captions ambiguous about direction).
- **Example in this video:** 15-second chart, second drop inside the opening range: institutional order flow entry drill, then an almost immediate rebalance up to the pre-drop candle.

### Balanced Price Range on the 15-second chart ("brick wall")  (aliases: BPR)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:** Where one candle's range and an adjacent candle's range are passed through in both directions (down once, back up once), that overlap box is a balanced price range. Price is expected to respect its far edge like a brick wall. In the example a single 15-second candle passed down through the previous candle's range and a single candle passed back up, so the region was offered in both directions. A trading-range analogy: price that has traversed a defined range in both directions needs something significant to go beyond it.
- **How to use:** Do not expect price to trade below the BPR low when you are long. A BPR that gives way = "manual intervention" ("the hand in the mix is the market maker"). The Matrix "black cat deja vu" analogy: change in the pattern means something changed behind the scenes, so step aside.
- **Example in this video:** 15-sec chart: price drops from the 1-minute inefficiency into the BPR, does not take out the BPR low, then rallies; later retracements into the BPR leave it intact. (His geometric explanation is hard to follow in the captions.)
- **Timestamps:** [3:29:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12578) BPR defined on the 15-second chart · [3:34:02](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12842) failing BPR means intervention · [3:36:25](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12985) tracking: price goes into it but not past it

### Price delivery continuum (time-frame hierarchy, sub-minute charts)  (aliases: "price delivery Continuum theory", matrix of time frames)
- **Kind:** rule / concept
- **What ICT says:** Identify the PD array on a higher time frame, then use the lower time frame to execute. A 1-minute chart is the "higher time frame" for 5s/15s/30s/45s charts, but only a mild one; it is itself subordinate to the 5-minute and 15-minute. Do your initial study on 15m, 5m and 1m for a solid base. He watches a continuum from weekly, daily, 4H, 3H, 2H, 1H, 30m, 15m, down to 5/4/3/2/1 minute and sub-minute, because the algorithm "cycles down and then right back up through the time frames".
- **How to use:**
  - Measure each PD array on the time frame it exists on (a 15-second imbalance may not exist on the 1-minute).
  - The higher time frame PD arrays supporting the trade keep confidence up; as price moves away from a 15m/5m PD array, your focus drops to lower frames; the 1-minute PD arrays stay "hawkish" in focus while you work on sub-minute.
  - Speed is a perception: a 15-second chart moves no faster than an hourly chart. Magnitude of a move is set by the time of day it begins, not by the chart time frame. 15-second charts only give more opportunities to engage.
  - Do not always expect the same gaps on a 15-second chart as on the 1-minute.
- **Example in this video:** The 1-minute wick CE + displacement shown on the 15-second chart: the 15-second FVG (a different shade) sits inside the 1-minute gap and must stay open; the 15-second breaker, MSS and BPR line up with the 1-minute logic.

### Relative equal highs/lows: validity rule and the 7:00-9:00 look-back  (aliases: relative equal highs, relative equal lows, REH/REL)
- **Kind:** rule + refinement ("from this 2024 mentorship")
- **What ICT says:**
  - A valid relative equal high is a high that has a slightly LOWER high to the right of it. That is where liquidity is most likely tapped. A high slightly higher to the right can still be traded through; if it only wicks above and the bodies stay below, the earlier high is still a valid relative equal high and price will go into the wick. You then need to see price trade above and close with speed and magnitude.
  - Look-back window for Caleb: only from 7:00 ET onward. If you start watching at 9:00, look back just the last two hours (7:00-9:00).
  - Buy stops rest above those highs (shorts protect with buy stops); sell stops below lows. You don't need a book map/DOM to know that.
- **How to use:** Mark the equal highs and lows, wait for one side to be taken; note which was taken first. In this video the early run took the sell side first; the buy side was left intact and was the draw. Equal-lows sweep went to the left-most/lowest of the cluster (use the lowest swing low as the reference).
- **Example in this video:** Equal highs sit above (the 9:00 high is "lower than that one" so it qualifies). After 9:30 price takes the sell side first.

### First side taken = the Judas swing; engineering liquidity
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The side offered first (the "wrong" side, the "primrose path") is the trap. Retail sells the drop; their stops sit above; then price runs the stops. Who made money so far (shorts) is who gets squeezed. Longs from a low are "unseated" by a sweep of the low. After the sell-side is taken, the move up is predominantly the draw. Smooth means resting liquidity; jagged is where it has been run.
- **How to use:** Do not trade the first side. After sell-side is taken, look for a reclaim of a reference (e.g. old NDOG) and a pullback that holds above a wick CE; enter via IOFED.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 19: shorts profit early, price drops to take lows (down to ~19,544.5, caption unclear), reclaims the Aug 16 NDOG, runs to take buy stops over the equal highs and into the NWOG.

### Target selection: draw, then the easiest low-threshold exit  (aliases: low-hanging objective, lowest quadrant exit)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Steps for "where is price drawing to": (1) find the relative equal highs/lows; (2) ask how far beyond them price can reach; (3) find a NDOG/NWOG beyond them, because that is where it gravitates; (4) if it is in close proximity use it and, if the range permits, break the gap into quadrants; (5) the lowest quadrant (lower quadrant of the gap you reach into) is the easiest threshold to exit profitably. He wants a barrier that is the lowest possible to profit from, not a "lofty" target.
  - You cannot use the NWOG low as the target when the buy stops (equal highs) sit above it. Price must go through the equal highs, so the exit is just above the highs, i.e. the NWOG's lower quadrant.
  - Fills: a limit exit at a level needs price to trade one tick through, and sometimes does not fill even when price touches. He sets the exit one tick beyond where he wants price to reach and takes "a steady diet" of easy targets.
- **Example in this video:** Pre-7:00 NQ trade: entry at market into the gap/order block, rally to the lower quadrant of the NWOG (~19,610.25, caption unclear; another quoted level "19,618" is garbled). Stated ordering from low: NWOG low (19,604), the buy-side/equal-high "blue line" slightly above it, then the lower quadrant, then CE. He exits one tick through the quadrant and says he raised the target to it as price approached.
- **Timestamps:** [3:06:28](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11188) NWOG low is not usable because the buy stops sit above · [3:08:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11330) how far beyond equal highs? look for NWOG/NDOG · [3:12:01](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11521) why he picks the easiest barrier · [3:19:12](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11952) logic restated with the exit price

### Power of 3 open-to-low zone for a bullish day  (aliases: Judas swing range, open to low range, classic buy day)
- **Kind:** refinement / teaching diagram
- **What ICT says:** He sketches a daily open-high-low-close bar (open at 9:30, close at about 4:15 pm). On a bullish day the initial drop from the opening price to the day's low is the fake move/Judas swing where smart money buys. Caleb is not expected to buy the exact low; he hunts for a long anywhere inside the range between the open and the low (e.g. IOFED inside that zone), then holds toward lunch. Measure where a lunch-macro retracement could go and do not keep the stop within reach of it. Eventually, if you are bullish from monthly/weekly, you buy at the open or below it.
  - Days that close near the high: the last hour makes the extreme and comes off a little before the close. The classic buy day just closes up near high, not necessarily one way.
  - He built the lesson in response to paid students who said they can't participate in a classic buy day if the low forms before 9:30 (an 8:30 entry he cannot teach from a 9:30 stream).
- **Story:** Larry Williams said he could not find how to buy below the opening price, so he bought strength above the open; ICT's Power-of-3 work is the answer to that. (Used as motivation, not a rule.)
- **Example in this video:** The 1-minute IOFED gap sits inside the open-to-low zone (box shaded blue on the chart at 3:25:43).

### Old highs and old lows as PD arrays (after they are broken)
- **Kind:** definition

Every time price breaches a PD array, look back at the range just traveled, map every PD array in it, and check whether any were left untouched ("did you miss a PD array not traded to? did it disregard it? That's a good thing, it means you're on side").

### Partials, stops and holding trades  (aliases: partial profits, trade management)

### Jackson Hole week characteristics  (aliases: Jackson Hole Symposium behavior)
- **Kind:** rule / day-of-week-style seasonal tendency
- **What ICT says:** During Jackson Hole week (Aug 19-23 here; Powell expected to speak several times, Wed-Fri volatile) price delivers with a lot of overlapping back-and-forth: it re-delivers over ranges it normally would not retrace so often. This is a "seek and destroy" climate; if you roll your stop to cover costs or breakeven it generally comes back and hits it. ICT says he has noticed this only for this event, no other economic event.
- **How to use:**
  - Decide where your first line of partials is before entering and keep it easy/low-threshold; do not try to outsmart the market with a greedy hold.
  - If long and price trades into a premium inefficiency (sell-side imbalance, buy-side efficiency) or clears a short-term high, take something off.
  - Do not over-trade. Expect one-directional runs to frustrate.
  - Check it yourself: on the weekend, review the 1-minute charts for the overlapping delivery.
  - ICT says moves attributed to Powell's words are really intervention; he prefers to be out of the way before the speech.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:45](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1845) overlapping delivery in Jackson Hole week · [0:34:43](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2083) where to place first partials · [0:39:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2390) rolling the stop gets hit · [0:41:26](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2486) don't over-trade

### FOMC rate decision: two-stage delivery and the 3:15 last-hour macro

### Lunch-hour retracement behavior
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** In lunch, price retraces against whatever has been profitable: it seeks sell stops after an up move and buy stops after a down move. Therefore measure the possible lunch retrace and keep your stop beyond it.
- **Example in this video:** After the run to the NWOG, the lunch-time 15-second drop sweeps relative equal lows and does not touch the NWOG high again.

### Reaper Fair Value Gap  (aliases: "Reaper", month-anchored FVG)
- **Kind:** new coinage (introduced in an earlier lecture; clarified here)
- **What ICT says:** A fair value gap anchored to a meaningful day: the first fair value gap that forms after 9:30 on a Monday (first day of the week) or on the first trading day of the month. Draw it out through the whole week/month. The algorithm goes back to specific dates as frames of reference (he lists: first trading day of the month, first day of the week, Wednesday, previous Friday; "not all of them"). "Reaper" is a scythe that cuts through the whole month and makes it tradable. Use it when it also matches the time of day the market should deliver.
- **How to use:** In a new month (August here) go back to the first day of the month, take the first pertinent PD array, and carry it through the chart. Treat each FVG as unique: if it shares space with another PD array it changes its character, so do not rename or re-teach it.
- **Example in this video:** The Reaper FVG he showed the previous week from August 1 was referenced repeatedly through the month.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30-10:00 ET (30 minutes), never 15. Beginners: no entries in it.
- Liquidity look-back for Caleb: from 7:00 ET; if you start at 9:00, look back two hours.
- Regular-session settlement 4:15 pm ET; electronic trading until 5:00 pm ET; restart 6:00 pm ET (Friday/holiday: restart Sunday 6:00 pm ET). RTH opening range gap = 4:15 pm settlement vs 9:30 open.
- NWOG = Friday settlement (last tick) vs Sunday 6:00 pm ET open; annotate it and its CE/quadrants.
- Valid relative equal high = a high with a slightly lower high to its right; a wick above with bodies below still counts.
- IOFED: buy limit at gap high + 1 tick; expect a dip ~1 tick through the reference low; gap should stay partly open; do not demand the low end of the gap.
- NQ numbers: 2 handles = 8 ticks = $40 per NQ contract (E-mini); example heat on the entry.
- First threshold target ladder: NWOG low, then CE, then upper quadrant; the easiest exit is the lowest quadrant just beyond the buy side.
- Don't want to see a close below the CE of the key wick; "no close below the midpoint of the wick".
- FOMC: roughly 70% chance the 2:30 move reverses the 2:00 direction; hold until ~3:15; 3:00-4:00 has multiple macros, no 20-40 or 30-40 min macros.
- Jackson Hole week: take early partials, do not roll stops to breakeven, avoid over-trading.
- Caleb's risk rules (stated): trade one contract; adding a contract needs 1.5x the margin per contract so overleveraging is impossible; stop for the day after a loss; after a winning day (trade closed, partials taken) close the charts until the next day.
- Study with 15m/5m/1m first; use 1m as the guide for 5s/15s/30s/45s charts.
- First fair value gap after 9:30 on a Monday / first day of month = Reaper candidate; extend across the week/month.
- Lunch: retrace against the profitable move; in profit, close around 1:30 (for Caleb's graduated plan).

## Glossary candidates
`IOFED` / Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill — enter on an FVG with a limit at the gap high + 1 tick, expecting it to stay partly open.
`Opening range (30 min)` — 9:30-10:00 ET; high/low become liquidity.
`RTH opening range gap` — prior-day 4:15 pm settlement vs 9:30 open.
`NWOG` — New Week Opening Gap: Friday settlement vs Sunday 6 pm open.
`NDOG` — New Day Opening Gap: prior-day close vs next-day open (here the Aug 16 gap).
`Reaper fair value gap` — first FVG of a meaningful anchor day (Monday/first trading day of month) drawn through the week/month.
`Price delivery continuum` — the time-frame hierarchy from weekly down to sub-minute; the algorithm cycles down then up.
`Immediate rebalance` — down-close candle followed by a candle that returns directly to the previous candle.
`Balanced price range (brick wall)` — an overlap region traversed in both directions that price will not break.
`Judas swing` — the first-side move that traps retail before the real move.
`Jackson Hole week` — overlapping, back-and-forth delivery; partial early.
`Breakaway gap / common gap` — gaps that don't fill vs. gaps that can fill.
`Low-threshold exit` — the easiest target to reach; the lowest quadrant just beyond buy-side liquidity.
`Smooth vs jagged` — resting liquidity vs. liquidity already traded through.
