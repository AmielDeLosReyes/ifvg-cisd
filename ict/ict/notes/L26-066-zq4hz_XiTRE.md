## Summary
Michael reviews two days of Nasdaq trading (Monday/Tuesday, early July 2026) to demonstrate one-shot-one-kill setups: high-conviction, single-entry trades with defined, achievable targets that are executed early in the week and then avoided for risk management. He uses daily discount wicks as bias anchors, the 15-minute chart as a bellwether timeframe, and blends macro timing with order flow logic (buy-side/sell-side liquidity, inversion fair value gaps, and relative equal levels) to identify where smart money will draw price. Emphasis: understand the daily chart first; intraday setups are confirmations of the daily thesis, not standalone trades.

## Concepts

### Discount Daily Wick (aliases: daily wick, discount wick)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** A wick on the daily chart extending below the body, indicating price exploration to a lower level. This wick's low and its consequent encroachment (the deep point) form the "discount" region. When lower timeframes pull back into this discount wick area, smart money likely to accumulate short positions there or ride the momentum into it.
- **How to identify:** Look at the daily chart; find a candle with a significant wick below the body (usually a reversal or consolidation day).
- **How to use:** Use the discount wick low and its consequent encroachment as target zones for lower-timeframe shorts. If price on a 15m or 1m chart gravitates into the discount wick CE, it signals institutional participation. This is also known as a "draw to liquidity."
- **Example in this video:** Daily Nasdaq discount wick low ~29,021 area, CE at 29,221.75. Price rallied on 1m/15m, then pulled down into this discount wick, targeting it as the delivery zone and delivering exactly to that level before sharply reversing higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=53) (discount wick intro) · [0:02:28](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=148) (discount wick boundary: 29,021, CE 29,221.75) · [0:08:05](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=485) (discount wick acts as low hanging fruit objective) · [0:20:02](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=1202) (price delivered down to discount wick).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The discount wick acts as a strong institutional target; once swept, expect mean reversion and sharp reversal higher.

### 15-Minute Timeframe as Bellwether Chart (aliases: 15m, bellwether)
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** The 15-minute timeframe is a "really strong go-to" for intraday and short-term trading. It is a bellwether chart—strong, reliable enough to frame all day trading and scalping. It's versatile: you can use it standalone for day-trade setups, OR you can use it to confirm daily bias. It bridges the gap between daily context and 1-minute scalps. Unlike the 1-minute (which is noisy), the 15-minute shows clean, actionable price structures.
- **How to identify:** Pull up the 15m chart alongside your daily; note how 15m moves are cleaner and easier to define structures on.
- **How to use:** If you can't structure a daily thesis, don't trade intraday—your 15m scalps will be guesses. Build your daily case first, then use 15m to refine entry timing and stop placement. The 15m can also be a standalone timeframe if your daily is unclear.
- **Example in this video:** The entire review is grounded in daily discount wick theory; the 15m chart showed clean relative equal highs/lows, SIBI levels, and gaps that confirmed the daily thesis. Price action was much crisper on 15m than 1m.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:53](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=53) (15m as bellwether) · [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=243) (15m as frame for all day trading and scalping).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Using 15m doesn't mean ignoring daily; it means daily is foundation, 15m is execution timeframe.

### Buy-Side Liquidity and Sell-Side Liquidity (aliases: BSL, SSL, old high, old low, relative equal liquidity pools)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** Buy-side liquidity is resting above a recent high (where buy orders sit unfilled during rallies; smart money looks to take them out with a spike, then reverse lower). Sell-side liquidity is below a recent low (where sell orders sit; smart money spikes lower to take them, then reverses higher). These are the pools of resting orders that institutional order flow "takes out" or sweeps. When you see a spike to a level and then a reversal, that level was likely liquidity being targeted.
- **How to identify:** Mark recent swing highs and lows on your 15m or daily; label them as BSL and SSL respectively.
- **How to use:** Use them as targets. When you're short, your target is often an SSL (take out the sell-side, then reverse). When you're long, target BSL. Smart money adds to positions when these are being swept. When all BSL is taken and price reverses down, expect a continuation of the downtrend.
- **Example in this video:** Monday's relative equal highs contained buy-side liquidity; price spiked to take it, and Michael announced beforehand he expected this. After BSL was taken, price rolled over and delivered down into discount wick.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=243) (BSL resting above old high) · [0:05:40](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=340) (BSL taken, then expected to work toward SSL) · [0:10:26](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=626) (smart money accumulates shorts when BSL taken, SSL defined).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Once BSL/SSL is taken, the direction flips; don't expect another reversal back to it in that move.

### Relative Equal Highs / Relative Equal Lows (aliases: REH, REL, equal levels, relative equal liquidity)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** Relative equal highs are two or more swing highs at approximately the same price level. Same for lows. These represent a "pool of liquidity"—likely where many stop losses or limit orders cluster. When price approaches a REH/REL, it often pauses, reverses, or sweeps through. These are NOT support/resistance; they're institutional reference points.
- **How to identify:** Scan backward for the last 5–20 candles on your 15m or 1m; find two or more local highs or lows within 5–10 ticks of each other.
- **How to use:** Use as targets on reversals. If you're short and price is near a REL, that's a likely target (where the sweep finishes before a reversal up). If long, REH is your target.
- **Example in this video:** Monday lunch macro setup formed relative equal highs; smart money hammered them (took BSL), then reversed down. Later, new RELs formed below the old swing low, and price targeted those as well.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:15](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=435) (REL as target: relative equal lows) · [0:12:01](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=721) (REH during lunch macro) · [0:13:37](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=817) (REL based on old pool of liquidity, algorithm likely to draw down into it).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** RELs can be formed on new data (e.g., a current low at same level as an older low), creating a fresh institutional reference point.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (aliases: iFVG, inversion inefficiency, bullish/bearish inversion)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG is a gap where price enters but does not commit (i.e., the bodies stay on one side and wicks fail to reach the other side). Example: price gaps up, but all bodies trade in the lower half of the gap, showing no real upside commitment—bearish inversion. It's expected to reverse and use the gap as a target on the reversal. Inversion FVGs act as "first presented" gaps on subsequent moves.
- **How to identify:** Look for a gap (open to close imbalance); then watch if price's bodies respect one side. If bodies stay in lower half and wicks don't reach upper half, it's a bearish inversion FVG.
- **How to use:** Inversion FVGs are reversal filters. If you're short and price approaches a bearish inversion iFVG, it's likely to reverse back down (use it as a target below). On the reversal lower, the same iFVG may act as first presented FVG and offer targets on the new direction.
- **Example in this video:** An inversion FVG formed on Monday opening range; bodies stayed in lower octants, wicks couldn't touch upper half—bearish sign. On subsequent moves down, this same gap was used as a bounce/target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:05](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=485) (inversion FVG acts as target) · [0:14:26](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=866) (first presented FVG is an inversion, treated as such on next move) · [0:18:24](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=1104) (correct perspective on iFVG: all volume in lower half, no real upside reach).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Inversion FVGs change their role; if initially bearish (bodies in lower half), on the next move they often become bullish (liquidity target on the bounce).

### Model 2022 / ICT Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) (aliases: OTE, swing low model, oldest model)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** A simple structure: find a swing low, watch for price to trade through it (break), then immediately come back up testing that same level as support. This "test back" is the OTE—the optimal trade entry. Enter longs at or just above the swing low on this retest. Stop below the swing low. Targets are usually defined by higher-level inefficiencies or old highs.
- **How to identify:** Identify a swing low on your timeframe; wait for price to break below it; then wait for price to rally back and test it.
- **How to use:** Enter long on the retest (or on the candle break back above). Risk is below the swing low. This is ICT's oldest, simplest model—rock-solid and repeatable on any timeframe.
- **Example in this video:** A swing low was broken on Monday; price came back to test it. This was identified as the OTE setup, and Michael noted low-hanging fruit targets at the first gap level above and then higher inefficiencies.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:26](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=626) (Model 2022 / OTE structure) · [0:11:13](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=673) (OTE low-hanging fruit objectives defined).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This model is timeless; it works because institutional traders use swing lows as reference points and test them for liquidity.

### Lunch Macro (aliases: lunch period, 11:30 ET – 1:30 PM ET, 11:50 to 12:10 macro)
- **Kind:** timing rule
- **What ICT says:** The lunch period is 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM ET. Within this, a setup usually forms between 11:30–12:30 (first hour). Then, a macro algorithmic window at 11:50–12:10 AM is when the algorithm "spools" and begins moving the market. The 10:00 low (from a prior swing) is a key liquidity reference during this macro.
- **How to identify:** Mark 11:30–1:30 PM on your chart; look for structures forming in the first hour and confirming between 11:50–12:10.
- **How to use:** If a setup is forming during lunch, wait for the 11:50–12:10 algorithm window to trigger. If it doesn't deliver during the second hour (12:10–1:30), hold for entry into the PM session.
- **Example in this video:** Monday's lunch setup formed relative equal highs; at 11:50–12:10, the algorithm took buy-side liquidity and then reversed. The 10:00 low from earlier was used as a target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:01](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=721) (lunch macro 11:30 to 1:30 PM, setup in first hour) · [0:12:01](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=721) (11:50 to 12:10 macro when algorithm spools).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** If the algorithm doesn't confirm during the second hour, it may re-run in the PM session; don't force it in the first hour.

### SIBI (aliases: single imbalance, SIBI price level)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** SIBI refers to a single imbalance level on a lower timeframe (often 15m or 1m). It's a candle with a small gap between open and close (or a wick), creating a micro-inefficiency. Mentioned in context: price "utilized" the SIBI, then used it again, indicating it's a liquidity reference point for smart money to target.
- **How to identify:** Look for 15m candles with very small bodies or wicks; these are SIBIs.
- **How to use:** SIBIs can act as entry points for smart money; if a SIBI is broken, expect a retest or a continuation through it. They're also stop-loss reference points.
- **Example in this video:** Michael noted SIBIs on the 15m chart (visible on his slides but not detailed in transcript); price used them as entry/exit points.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:40](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=340) (SIBI price utilized) · [0:13:37](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=817) (SIBI on 15m timeframe).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** SIBI is a lower-timeframe detail; don't use it in isolation; always confirm with daily bias.

### Suspension Block (aliases: suspension, suspension candle)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** A suspension block is a candle with volume imbalance at both high and low—neither side commits. It signals neither bulls nor bears are in control; the next candle often delivers direction decisively. Michael notes it as "bearish" in context when it appears near resistance and precedes a breakdown.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candle with a small body and wicks at both ends (near equal high and low wicks).
- **How to use:** Suspension blocks are pause signals; they precede directional delivery. If you're near a level and see a suspension, prepare for a breakout (direction TBD until confirmed).
- **Example in this video:** A suspension block formed on Tuesday morning near the overnight high; this was bearish because it was at resistance, and the market subsequently broke lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:16](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=916) (suspension block, volume imbalance at high and low, bearish context).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Suspension blocks are neutral by themselves; context (location on chart, higher-timeframe bias) determines bullish or bearish significance.

### One Shot One Kill Model (aliases: OSOK, short-term trading swing, 2–5 day trade)
- **Kind:** trading model + psychology
- **What ICT says:** A one-shot-one-kill trade is a single, high-conviction entry with a defined, achievable target, executed with the expectation of being closed by the end of a few days (2–5 days max). Once the target is hit, you're done trading for that swing. Don't hold over weekends due to gap risk. Don't look for the next trade immediately; be content with the win. This is a discipline/psychology lesson as much as a technical one.
- **How to identify:** You execute one trade per multi-day swing, with clear entry, stop, and target. That's it.
- **How to use:** Use daily bias (order blocks, discount wicks, macros) to identify the ONE setup to trade. Enter, manage stops, hit target, exit. Don't override discipline by chasing the market for more; that's when traders get wrecked (especially with high volatility).
- **Example in this video:** Monday–Tuesday setup delivered "several hundred handles" in a clean move from setup to target. Michael took it and was done; he's not looking for more trading this week.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:28](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=388) (OSOK explained: given all analysis upfront, now execute one trade) · [0:24:46](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=1486) (OSOK model: two to five day trade, out by end of week).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** OSOK is not greedy trading; it's high-quality, highly-confluenced trades with defined exits. One big move beats chasing ten small scalps.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 15-minute timeframe: use as bellwether for day-trading structure; if you can't frame daily thesis, don't scalp intraday.
- Lunch macro: setup forms 11:30 AM–12:30 PM; algorithm window 11:50 AM–12:10 PM.
- Lunch macro 10:00 low: key reference point for targeting during lunch period.
- Inversion FVG: bodies stay on one side, wicks fail to reach the other side = rejection signal.
- Discount daily wick: once swept, expect sharp reversal and use as low-hanging fruit objective.
- Relative equal highs/lows: pool of institutional liquidity; smart money targets these for stop-loss sweeps.
- One Shot One Kill: 2–5 day swings; execute one trade, close at target, don't over-trade. No weekend holds.
- Volume imbalances (SIBIs): micro-inefficiencies on lower timeframes; smart money uses them as entry/exit points.
- Suspension blocks: dual volume imbalance (high and low); precedes directional delivery.
- Open-high-low-close candlesticks: use only OHLC; don't use Renko, Heiken Ashi, or other manipulated candle types—it's gambling.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Daily foundation first:** Never start with intraday; build your daily thesis (discount wicks, order blocks, macros, daily bias). Then use 15m to refine timing.
- **One-shot discipline:** When you nail a high-conviction setup and it delivers, don't push for more. Take the win and step back. Many traders get wrecked when they try to "make up" for a slow week or overtrade after a big win.
- **Gap risk:** Don't hold positions over weekends; gap risk is "absolutely ridiculous." If you enter Monday and exit Wednesday, that's "short-term trading" to Michael (vs. day trading, which closes before 5 PM ET settlement).
- **Position sizing in volatility:** The market had "over 100 handles" move in 45 seconds on the open. Large position sizing without stops is catastrophic. Size down in high-volatility environments.
- **Avoid ego:** If you're watching a high-quality setup deliver (like this week did) and you're not in the trade, don't blame the market. Study it, learn it, demo-trade it, observe. But don't go live on a whim out of FOMO.
- **Content with the process:** When analysis delivers "to the tick," it's a sign the framework is sound. Push for consistency of process, not consistency of daily P&L. Some weeks are big, some are small.
- **Chart management:** Use OHLC only; volume profiles, footprint heatmaps, and manipulated candles are "gimmicks" that mislead retail traders. They do not add edge.

## Glossary candidates
`Bellwether` — a reliable, responsive timeframe (like 15m) that can frame an entire day of trading; bridges daily context and intraday scalps.
`Buy-side liquidity (BSL)` — resting buy orders above recent swing highs; taken out (swept) by institutional traders before downtrend resumes.
`Sell-side liquidity (SSL)` — resting sell orders below recent swing lows; taken out (swept) by institutional traders before uptrend resumes.
`Relative equal highs (REH)` — two or more swing highs at the same price level; a pool of institutional reference liquidity.
`Relative equal lows (REL)` — two or more swing lows at the same price level; a pool of institutional reference liquidity.
`Inversion FVG (iFVG)` — a fair value gap where price enters but bodies commit to one side; wicks fail to reach the opposite side, signaling rejection.
`Consequent encroachment (CE)` — the deep point of a wick; graded for commitment signals (if price closes beyond CE, continuation; if not, reversal).
`Model 2022 / OTE` — swing low break and retest structure; retest is the optimal trade entry.
`Lunch macro` — algorithmic timing window 11:50 AM–12:10 PM ET during the lunch period (11:30 AM–1:30 PM).
`SIBI` — single imbalance; a lower-timeframe candle with micro-inefficiency used as institutional reference point.
`Suspension block` — a candle with volume imbalance at both high and low; precedes directional breakout.
`One Shot One Kill (OSOK)` — a 2–5 day trade based on daily bias, executed once per swing, closed at target; no over-trading.
