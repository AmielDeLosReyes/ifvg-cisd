## Summary
This is the "start here" lecture for the opening range gap (ORG) and the first FVG after 9:30. Live on NQ, ICT plots the previous settlement (4:14 PM ET) against the 9:30 open. He says the gap is more than 20-30 handles roughly 70% of the time (his figure) trades back to at least its consequent encroachment inside the 9:30-10:00 window. He then shows the first FVG of the day (formed 9:31-10:00) being used and inverted, and then "reclaimed" and respected to the tick. He explains why smart money accumulates below relative equal lows while an unfilled gap sits above price, and why price speeds up as it nears a draw on liquidity. New or sharpened PD array rules appear along the way. These include the upper-half / lower-half rule (a bearish array should never trade into its upper half), body-versus-wick tolerance ("mohawk"), the propulsion block and rejection block as applied to a short, and the 20/40/60 *trading*-day look-back. The second half is a long speech on journaling as positive self-talk and back-testing. The last hour reviews a demo-account NQ short (order block / propulsion block / inversion FVG / rejection block stop, target the Aug 16 NDOG) and an ES short into Monday's low (previous-day-low raid).

## Concepts

### Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG) — previous settlement to 9:30 open  (aliases: opening range gap, "settlement gap", discount opening range gap)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - The ORG runs from the previous day's settlement, the last print at 4:14 PM ET, to the 9:30 AM ET opening price. The session ends at 4:15, so there is no 4:15 print. Electronic trading continues until 5:00 PM, pauses for an hour, and resumes at 6:00 PM ET.
  - The "opening range" is 9:30-10:00, a 30-minute interval, not 15 or 5. He calls the other timeframes people teach wrong.
  - First order of business each day is to determine whether there is a gap. A "significant" gap is more than about 20-30 handles on NQ.
  - The midpoint (consequent encroachment, CE) is "our first threshold". Roughly 70% of the time, price trades at least to the CE in the first 30 minutes. He restates this as "you got 70% chance" that at least half the gap is filled. He calls this algorithmic, and the captions once render it as "7%" in error.
  - No bias is needed to study this: no daily bias and no session bias. It is just a way to watch how price is delivered.
  - Plot quadrant levels (25/50/75%) on the gap. Set an alert on the CE so a one-tick cross rings.
- **How to identify:** High = previous settlement (4:14 PM print). Low = 9:30 opening tick (when the open is below settlement, a "discount" ORG). Reverse if the open is above settlement. Extend the lines across the day.
- **How to use:**
  - Pre-plot the settlement line before the open (he did this at ~9:25). Park a second line away from price during electronic hours, then drag it to the 9:30 print when it appears.
  - Watch for the first reach to the lower quadrant, then the CE, then the full closure. The partial-fill sequence is the study.
  - If no FVG has formed yet on the 1m and there is minor sell-side liquidity just under price, he flags a profile to study: drop, clear the sell side, then return up and hit the mid-gap.
  - "Don't use it to pick every entry". Use it to learn what constitutes a valid run from a level.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 1m, Tue 8/27/24. Settlement is quoted as "568 and 3/4" (likely 19568.75, caption unclear). The open was well below it, so the gap is a "very deep discounted" ORG, and the settlement price sat inside the Aug 16 NDOG.
  - Price first dipped to the lower quadrant and could not reach the CE (swept minor sell-side liquidity first).
  - It later ran back up and closed about 3/4 of the gap, "fell short" of full closure. ICT notes this meant the 70% half-fill happened before the 10:00 mark.
  - Afterwards price consolidated near the gap high (the settlement price) and then ran on into the NDOG/NWOG cluster.

### First Fair Value Gap after 9:30 (inside 9:30–10:00)  (aliases: "first presentation", "initial FVG", "the first FVG after 9:30")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first FVG that forms between 9:31 and 10:00 is the one to extend across the entire day. He says he taught this in Twitter Spaces in 2023.
  - "Your FVG can't form on the 9:30 candle," so the window starts with the candle after the open (exact three-candle mechanics are not spelled out; caption unclear).
  - On the 1m chart, study what the market does to this one gap while the ORG is still unfilled. He calls the first interaction the "first presentation".
  - Earlier in the session, a pre-9:30 FVG (outside the opening range) was the only gap in use. He wants to see it traded to once more before the real first-FVG appears.
- **How to identify:** Take the first 1m FVG that completes after the 9:30 candle and before 10:00. Annotate it in grey and extend it forward "in perpetuity throughout the day."
- **How to use:**
  - Price tends to go above it, close above, then return to it on a later candle and reverse "to the tick".
  - Once price has swept sell-side liquidity below (disturbing the water), the next draw is the smooth, unfilled side (short-term buy side, ORG remainder).
  - ICT repeats there should be "no more sellers below that candle's low" only because the code says the level holds. His point is that nothing in buy/sell pressure explains stopping on the tick.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 1m. Price opens, drops below relative equal lows (accumulation of sell stops), then pushes back above the initial FVG and closes above it.
  - A later candle opens above, trades down into the gap and stops exactly at its top. ICT gives the candle low as 19555.25 (caption reads "1955.25"; read as 19555.25).
  - Price then runs to the short-term buy side and on to the ORG gap high.

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) and "Reclaimed" FVG — role reversal  (aliases: inversion fair value gap, reclaimed bullish FVG, PD array that changes its state)
- **Kind:** refinement / new coinage ("reclaimed")
- **What ICT says:**
  - A PD array is not static. A bullish FVG used as support (price rallies off it from discount) can be traded below, so it becomes an inversion and sends price lower. If price later gets back above it, it reverts: a "reclaimed bullish FVG" that should again act as real support.
  - He says he was not wrong in his earlier lecture; the lesson is how the array is used and whether its mode of delivery changes. "Stop saying all fair value gaps are the same."
  - He colors an inverted gap orange in his charts ("if it's ever a hue, it's orange: its role has reversed").
  - Intraday order: price must first get above the gap's high ("reclaimed"). Typically that is accompanied by a sudden, abrupt market structure shift (he wants them "sudden and abrupt").
  - Climbing metaphor: a PD array above price is first a handhold; after price gets above it, it becomes a foothold.
- **How to identify:** A gap that held price, then price traded/closed through it, and later closed back through it again. Orange = inversion; reclaimed = back to the original role.
- **How to use:**
  - Frame a paper/tape-reading short at the inverted gap's CE-to-low (the candle's high). Partial one tick below the low it makes, then extend.
  - As an inversion short, "the easiest objective" is the next low-hanging low or relative equal lows. Quick method: quadrant the range you are working in and project.
  - When reclaimed: if price opens above and trades back down into it, that is where support should show.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 1m: the first gap (outside 9:30-10:00) acts as discount support, price takes relative equal highs above, then trades below it (inversion).
  - He frames a hypothetical short at 19493 (the high of the candle that forms the gap's low). Price runs the low to 19476.
  - His arithmetic for the target: "one quadrant extension of the range" gives 19460.25, about 30+ handles. How the range is measured is not clear in the captions; it is roughly the 19493–19476 range projected below the low.
  - Later price closes back above the gap: now a reclaimed bullish FVG, and the sell-side raid at the low becomes the springboard.

### Liquidity accumulation: why smart money drops price below relative equal lows first  (aliases: accumulation, sell-stop accumulation, "time-based delivery")
- **Kind:** definition / explanation
- **What ICT says:**
  - When a large unfilled range (here the ORG) sits above, price often first drops below relative equal lows. Smart money needs a counterparty. It "accumulates" the sell stops/sell orders resting there, which become their buy orders in size.
  - It does not matter whether those sell orders are protective stops on longs or breakout entries. Relative equal lows are the visual shorthand he uses so he does not have to reveal how he distinguishes them.
  - Accumulation means price spending time at a pre-determined liquidity level. Inefficiencies (FVGs) are different: you do not want price meandering inside them (no "bull flags"). Price should trade to them, through them, and use them as a "springboard / rocket fuel".
  - Smart money never buys on an uptick or breakout. It buys on sell-side (momentum-down) delivery at discounted prices. The first down-close candle after a run of up-closes is the first "discounted" delivery. They add as price trades up, but the volume comes from the loading below.
  - The longer price spends below a pool, the larger and more protracted the later run ("it's spending time now because it's going to be a longer protracted run").
  - Delivery speed is in proportion to how close the inefficiency or liquidity is. Once the buying is done, candles expand and the run accelerates toward the full closure.
  - He contrasts this with a prior-day observation where price stabbed down inside a wick's CE and immediately came out: that is not accumulation.
- **How to use:**
  - If price keeps ignoring a PD array in the narrative direction and spends time at a liquidity pool, expect the repricing leg to be fast.
  - If price goes "off script" after a run to a level, do not over-leverage or force a trade; sit still.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: price opens, runs a little above, then drops below relative equal lows (inside the lower ORG half) and sits there, forming the pre-move FVG and ORG remainder above. After the sweep it reprices up to the first FVG and on to the ORG gap high.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:46:43](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2803) — what benefit for smart money to engage sell stops.
  - [0:57:17](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3437) — unfinished business above, accumulation below.
  - [0:58:50](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3530) — liquidity vs inefficiency: spend time below liquidity, never inside FVGs.
  - [1:02:50](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3770) — smart money never buys breakouts; off-script = sit still.
- **Nuance / caveats:** He says the "delivery to terminus" has already started (caption unclear on exact meaning; "terminus" appears here as the destination of the delivery, likely not the named model).

### Smart-money counterparty / why "buying and selling pressure" is rejected  (aliases: algorithm, Composite Man, market maker, draw on liquidity)
- **Kind:** rule / philosophy
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price is delivered by an algorithm. Stops "to the tick" at pre-announced levels cannot be explained by random buying and selling pressure. Depth of market is a facade; orders can be pulled or spoofed, so a big book at a price is not a magnet.
  - PD arrays are "arrays" in the programming sense: reference data points the algorithm refers back to, like ingredients in a recipe. The outcome is sometimes over- or under-cooked but still profitable for the market maker.
  - Draw on liquidity analogy: you go to the shoe store because you want shoes; the store is the draw.
  - Smart money does not need much volume at the end of the move because it loaded earlier. Retail can push orders into a single candle that smart money cannot.
  - Market structure is a controlled environment that can be "manipulated and disturbed".
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:33:48](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2028) — start of the "no algorithm" rebuttal.
  - [0:46:43](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2803) — draw on liquidity / counterparty.

  - [1:14:10](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4450) — what an "array" is.

### Bullish Breaker (as used here) and PD array body-versus-wick rules  (aliases: breaker, "coloring outside the lines")
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - After sell-side was engaged and price ran above the short-term high (a market structure shift), an up-close candle with a long discount tail is called the bullish breaker. It "broke the market structure and took anyone that's long out," leaving buy side "neutral" with no held longs above.
  - His breaker candle: it opened, wicked down into the first FVG's top (low 19555.25) and closed up. The wick did the damage; the body is what you reference.
  - Use the upper half of the body for a bullish breaker: between the body's midpoint (the CE) and its high. Do not worry about the wick.
  - Price held in the upper quadrant of the body without ever touching the halfway point, consolidating (accumulating) before expanding.
  - Tolerance: a body should not exceed a PD array's boundary, and if it does, one tick, "no more than two". A wick can go a few ticks outside. On NQ he allows about two ticks; on ES it is tighter, generally one tick.
- **How to use:** Treat the breaker as a brick wall for rejecting lower prices. If bullish, price should not trade into its lower half. Accumulation inside the upper quadrant is the signature.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m after the first FVG tap at 19555.25: breaker candle closes up; price ranges inside its upper half, then rallies to the ORG gap high (settlement), sits sideways, then expands into the NDOG/NWOG cluster.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [1:19:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4744) — "this up close candle here is your bullish breaker".
  - [1:21:28](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4888) — wick versus body, tick tolerance (NQ two ticks, ES one).
  - [1:23:52](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5032) — use the body's upper half.
  - [1:24:41](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5081) — accumulation in the upper quadrant.
- **Nuance / caveats:** This is not the classic definition of a breaker (failed order block that flips). ICT here ties the label to the candle at which structure broke and longs were cleared. The captions are noisy on candle-by-candle identification.

### Upper-half / lower-half rule for PD arrays  (aliases: "rules of engagement with my PD arrays", highest form of order flow around them, premium/discount inside an array)
- **Kind:** rule (he says he was not going to teach it to paid students)
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you are bullish, you do not want the lower half of a PD array to be traded to. If you are bearish, you do not want the upper half to be traded to. The array should show an inability to deliver price in that half.
  - The lower half can be traded into, but then price must respond immediately off the deepest part. If it does not, "chances are you have a dud": cancel the trade, take a scratch or lose only commission.
  - The same applies to inversion FVGs (a bearish inverted gap should not be revisited in its upper half) and to order blocks.
  - His mentorship-era shortcut was just the low of the up-close candle or the opening price; this half-rule is the more refined version.
- **How to use:**
  - Enter in the allowed half (for a short, the lower part of an order block or candle). Place the stop where the invalid half and the supporting reference would both have to fail (see rejection block).
  - Having the other half untouched also lets you pyramid with more confidence.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ short: price kept the upper half of the volume imbalance/inversion FVG untraded while wicking into the lower half and CE ("mohawk" above the halfway), which gave him confidence to add.
  - ES short: "I trust we're not going in the upper half of this range".
- **Timestamps:**
  - [1:23:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4984) — if bullish, lower half should fail.
  - [1:35:08](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5708) — respect upper half when bullish; respond at the deepest part or cancel.
  - [2:54:01](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10441) — bearish: upper half never redelivered.
  - [3:18:01](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11881) — "the highest order of order flow around them".
- **Nuance / caveats:** Refines the simple "enter at the low of the up-close candle" version of an order block entry. He insists this is not supply and demand.

### Mohawk (tolerance beyond a PD array's boundary)  (aliases: coloring outside the lines)
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A "mohawk" is a small wick or a body slightly outside a PD array's exact boundary, behavior you as the analyst allow. Annotate it as a mohawk when journaling.
  - A close a tick or two beyond the edge (example: a close just above a volume imbalance high at 19598.25, caption approximate) is permissible and does not change what you expect.
  - When an FVG overlaps a volume imbalance, use the volume imbalance range as the true inefficiency, not just the wick, because two PD arrays agree on the same context.
- **How to use:** Do not abandon a thesis for a one/two-tick mohawk. In journaling, label it so a bold candle poking through does not scare you in real time.
- **Example in this video:** NQ short: a wick poked above the volume imbalance high and the CE of the inefficiency, closes stayed outside the gap, price then dropped.

### Propulsion Block  (aliases: part of the order block family; taught in 2016 core content)
- **Kind:** definition applied / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - A propulsion block is a candle that trades back into a prior order block (an inefficiency-reaching OB) and whose body does not breach, close above or cross the order block's mid-point (mean threshold). He treats that as a tipping of the hand that price should repel away.
  - The propulsion block's entire range is referenced, and he uses its opening price as an entry reference. A later candle that digs into that previous order block adds conviction.
  - He points to the PD array lectures in the 2016 mentorship (his "month four" content) and says the full rules will be in the book trilogy.
- **How to identify:** A candle moving back into a prior OB, with its body holding below (for shorts) the OB's mean threshold, and an immediate move away.
- **How to use:**
  - Short entry near the propulsion block's opening price, then add on the CE of the wick/inefficiency.
  - Required supporting context: the order block must not stand alone (see Order-block caveats).
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ demo short: order block reaches the high end of the inefficiency; a following candle pushes into it but its body does not cross the OB's half; ICT shorts the propulsion block's open, then adds at the CE of the next wick.
  - ES short: later order block with an indecisive candle that creates a volume imbalance. Every candle that comes back up into the OB fails to reach half.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [2:36:26](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9386) — "this candle's body cannot breach half of the order block".
  - [2:39:37](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9577) — why it is a propulsion block.
  - [2:54:49](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10489) — entries on the propulsion block's open and wick CE.

### Order block caveats and the reclaimed order block
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage ("reclaimed bearish order block")
- **What ICT says:**
  - An order block "is not just an up-closed candle and certainly not just the last up-closed candle before the down move"; it must have other supportive PD arrays around it.
  - An inversion FVG alone is not a trade either unless supported by an OB etc.
  - A "reclaimed bearish order block": price moved away from the OB, then comes back up into it. It needs an immediate response lower. When it did, he took it as a sign of heaviness.
- **Example in this video:** ES 1m: after the propulsion block/volume imbalance, price came back up and dug into the original order block; it dropped again immediately, then retraced into an FVG, displaced, took out the low, matched the high of the inefficiency, and showed no willingness to enter its upper portion.
- **Timestamps:**

### Rejection Block (as a stop-loss reference)
- **Kind:** application / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - His stop on the NQ short sat above the rejection block: the highest up-close in the swing (high, higher high, lower high) — the closing price of that up-close candle.
  - A rejection block should be traded to one time; if it is good, price does not go back above it. The later run reached exactly 19592.75, the same as the close of the up-close candle, then reversed.
  - Because everything else lined up (propulsion block, inversion, upper half untouched), he was not afraid the stop would be hit: "the CE won't go back to the rejection block".
  - Taught in the 2016 mentorship month-four content.
- **How to use:** Put the stop above the rejection block's level and only after the supporting conditions are present. The stop is logic-based and tight.
- **Example in this video:** NQ short demo: wick up to the CE of the inefficiency; price reverses; rejection block price 19592.75 never traded above. (The transcript also reads "19552.75" once, which is a caption error.)
- **Timestamps:**

### Previous Day High/Low and the 3-day high/low reference list  (aliases: PDH/PDL, "honey pot", old highs/lows, buy-side/sell-side)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Keep a written list of the highest high and lowest low of the last three trading days, plus each of those days' own high and low (e.g., on Tuesday: Monday, Friday, Thursday). Sundays do not count; the only thing taken from Sunday is the 6:00 PM ET opening, for the NWOG.
  - Markets will refer back to these. Liquidity sits above old highs (buy stops) and below old lows (sell stops). They act as draws on liquidity "just like a new week opening gap or a new day opening gap".
  - Mark them by hand with a horizontal ray rather than relying on an indicator ("high touch over high tech"). He criticizes dependence on indicators such as LuxAlgo and says some of their smart money plotting is not what he would use.
  - Simple rule: when you sit down to trade and the market is in the lower half of the previous day's range, always look for a return to the previous day's low. The PDH/PDL is "like a honey pot", available every single day.
  - Tendency while approaching the target: speed and distance should increase as price nears the previous day's low. Anyone with a protective stop there can quickly pull it, so price runs to the pool fast, jumping and skipping (visible on 1-second or 5-second charts) before they can. One contract is enough to book the print.
- **How to identify / use:**
  - Opening in the lower half of the prior day's range = bias toward a sweep of PDL (and vice versa for the upper half).
  - After the sweep, look for the reaction: price rallies, takes relative equal highs.
- **Example in this video:**
  - ES 1m, Aug 27: price drops toward Monday Aug 26's low. Short entries at the order block / propulsion block, then at the volume imbalance as price opened and tagged it. The exit filled one tick from the candle low just above Monday's low area, then price spiked below it.
  - After taking Monday's low, price rallied easily above the relative equal highs, which shows the run on liquidity done.
  - He calls these two or three a week "easy ones", fractal to smaller ranges.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [3:10:03](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11403) — Sunday: only the 6 PM opening matters.

### ES short review: sequence of order flow before the previous-day-low run
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says (step by step):**
  1. Price is dropping toward Monday's (8/26) low. Every candle returning to the order block fails to take half, so it is a propulsion block.
  2. An indecisive candle (open equals close) builds a volume imbalance; the next candle comes up and fills the void with no bodies ("patchwork", his term for the algorithm filling inefficiencies).
  3. Price displaces lower. It comes back and digs into the original OB (reclaimed bearish OB), responds immediately, retraces into an FVG, hits it, displaces lower, takes out the low, retraces to match the high of the inefficiency with no willingness to enter, and then breaks lower.
  4. He sells in the lower part of the large down-close candle as the next candle opens and digs into it, then adds in the volume imbalance, expecting the upper half never to trade.
  5. The market "rolls over energetically" and runs just below Monday's low; his fill was one tick from the candle's low.
- **How to use:** The "heaviness" signature is repeated refusal to trade the upper half of arrays on the way down. He said the market was "indicating in every shape, facet or form that it's heavy".

### New Day / New Week Opening Gap clusters ("black hole") and shelf life  (aliases: NDOG, NWOG, nested opening gaps)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement

  - Do not treat them as stale after one visit. "Keep them" and extend them. Do not do the "old pivots (R1, R2) trace it and throw it away" behavior.

  - Backtest note: log how many candles it took to reach the CE of the NWOG and how much drawdown in handles it had before.
- **How to use:** Draw on liquidity, targets, and confluence for reversals ("the algorithm is going to refer to them").

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- ORG = previous settlement (4:14 PM ET last print) to 9:30 AM ET open. Opening range is 9:30-10:00 ET (30 minutes, not 15). Gap > ~20-30 handles is significant.
- ~70% of the time (his figure) price trades to at least the ORG's CE inside the first 30 minutes; first threshold is the midpoint; quadrant levels also useful.
- Settlement/session mechanics: futures session ends 4:15 PM ET (last print 4:14), electronic trading until 5:00 PM, 1-hour pause, resumes 6:00 PM ET (also Sunday 6:00 PM open for NWOG).
- First FVG after 9:30 forms between 9:31 and 10:00; the 9:30 candle itself cannot form it. Extend across the day.
- Pre-session / operating hours: set up from 7:00 ET; "don't worry about anything before 7 o'clock". His own pre-7 trades are for study.
- PD array body tolerance: one tick, no more than two ticks for the body outside the array; wicks a few ticks. NQ ~2 ticks; ES ~1 tick.
- Bullish: do not let the lower half of a PD array trade. Bearish: do not let the upper half trade. If an array's deep half is traded to and does not respond immediately, cancel the trade.
- Breaker/body: use the body, not the wick; bullish breaker upper half (between CE and high).
- Smart money does not buy breakouts/upticks; it buys discount on sell-side delivery.
- Volume imbalance overlapped with an FVG: use the volume imbalance range as the true inefficiency.
- Order block needs supporting PD arrays; not just the last up-close before the move.
- IPDA look-back: 20 / 40 / 60 trading days (not calendar). 20 most sensitive; older than 60 are ignored.
- Beginner scope: NDOGs within 5 days and NWOGs within 5 weeks, for a month or two.
- Keep a list of the last 3 trading days' highest high and lowest low and each day's own high/low (not Sunday).
- If the open is in the lower half of the previous day's range, look for a return to the previous day's low.
- Lunch macro: 11:30-1:30 ET.
- Stop reference example: above the rejection block (19592.75 in this trade). Partial at 50% to target (19574 here: 3 of 12 contracts).
- Day-of-week note: stream schedule (Wed afternoon session 1:30-4:00 PM ET; Thursday early end 10:30); not trading content.

## Glossary candidates
`ORG` (Opening Range Gap) — gap between the previous day's 4:14 PM ET settlement print and the 9:30 AM ET open; the 9:30-10:00 window is "the opening range".
`Settlement (4:14 PM)` — last futures print before the 4:15 PM close; used as the ORG high/low edge.
`Handles` — NQ/ES points, i.e. whole price points of range.
`First presentation` — the first interaction of price with the first FVG formed after 9:30.
`Reclaimed (FVG / order block)` — a PD array that price traded beyond, then returned to or above, so it reverts to its original role.
`Inversion (orange)` — an FVG whose role reversed after price traded through it; he colors it orange.
`Mohawk` — a tick-or-two wick/body outside a PD array's boundary that is allowed without changing the thesis.
`Coloring outside the lines` — same idea; bodies stay within one-two ticks, wicks may go further.
`Propulsion block` — candle trading back into an OB without its body crossing the OB's mean threshold, signaling departure.
`Rejection block` — highest up-close in a swing (for a short) used as a stop reference; price should touch it once and not return.
`Patchwork` — his term for the algorithm filling a volume-imbalance void.
`Honey pot` — previous day's high/low as a standing magnet.
`Foothold / handhold` — a PD array above price is a handhold first, then a foothold once price gets above it.
`Black hole` — nested NDOG/NWOG cluster that keeps drawing price back.
`Low-hanging fruit` — the nearest, easiest objective for a target (e.g. the nearest NDOG or relative equal lows).
`Heaviness` — price repeatedly refusing the upper half of arrays (bearish) or lower half (bullish); sign of continued delivery.
`Spooling / jumping and skipping` — rapid, gappy candle delivery as price approaches a liquidity pool.
`Mean threshold` — the 50% (CE) point of a candle or order block.
`Composite man` — ICT's name for the market maker/algorithm personified (term used in passing).
