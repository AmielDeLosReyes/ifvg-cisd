## Concepts

### Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL) Above Structural Highs
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** "I'm wanting the buy side is resting above the pre-market session highs. And then this high up here, which is overnight relative equal highs." The market is biased to run higher and collect stops/liquidity resting above these structural resistance points.
- **How to identify:** Identify overnight session highs (electronic trading, pre-market). Mark where relative equal highs formed. These become BSL pools above the current price.
- **How to use:** Use these points as targets for long entries. Enter on wicks or pullbacks into demand (discount areas), with the expectation that price will run up to collect these higher BSL pools.
- **Example in this video:** NQ on Jan 6: overnight relative equal highs were marked; ICT wanted to see price rally up to challenge and take out these levels. Entry was taken on a wick down into demand, with BSL above as the target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:03](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=183) (identifying BSL above pre-market highs and overnight equal highs)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** BSL is not the same as a simple "resistance"—it's specifically liquidity pools where stop-losses and limit orders rest, waiting to be hit.

### First Presented for Everybody Gap (FPEG)
- **Kind:** definition · reference level
- **What ICT says:** The gap formed from the previous Friday's close into Monday's open. "This orange box up here is first presented for everybody gap from Friday." It's a reference point for assessing whether price is trading in premium or discount to last week.
- **How to identify:** Identify Friday's close and Monday's open; the gap between them is the FPEG.
- **How to use:** Use FPEG as a support/resistance zone and as context for bias. A gap-up entry (trading above FPEG) opens with premium bias. A gap-down open would be discount. Price will often test these extremes before extending.
- **Example in this video:** NQ gapped up from Friday's close on Monday, Jan 6 (first full week of 2026). The FPEG was a reference to understand whether price action was extended or retraceable.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:03](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=183) (FPEG identified as orange box) · [0:03:50](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=230) (FPEG context in evaluating morning session)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** FPEG is a structural reference, not a standalone trade signal. Context (bias, volatility, NFP week, etc.) determines how to use it.

### Electronic Trading Hours vs. Regular Trading Hours (RTH) Settlement
- **Kind:** definition · reference level
- **What ICT says:** Electronic trading hours (Sunday open through Friday close, continuous) have a settlement price; RTH settlement (9:30 to 4:00 ET) is different. "Regular trading hours settlement is down here... we're showing electronic trading hours." The reference changes depending on which "official" settlement you're watching.
- **How to identify:** Note the settlement price for electronic trading (usually from Friday night's settlement) and RTH settlement (from previous day's close at 4:00 ET). These are marked as distinct levels on the chart.
- **How to use:** Use RTH settlement as a reference for the "official" previous day's close in terms of technical analysis. Electronic settlement is where overnight futures trading cleared. Both matter: RTH for daily technicals, electronic for context on overnight positioning.
- **Example in this video:** NQ's RTH settlement was marked below the current price; ICT noted that if price were to trade down to that level, it would represent a full gap closure and bearish rejection of the initial gap-up.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:50](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=230) (both settlements marked and explained)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Many traders miss the distinction; always note which settlement you're referencing for daily bias confirmation.

### Premium Gap-Up Entry & The "Non-Participation" Test
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** When a market gaps up (premium opening), ICT tests whether the gap will fill (bearish) or be rejected (bullish). "So, what I'm looking for is it to kind of like shun that, not want to do it at all. And then if it does negate that and not go back down into this orange shaded area, then this was a draw." If price refuses to fill the gap down to the previous RTH settlement, it proves the gap is a legitimate bullish move.
- **How to identify:** Gap up opens. Draw a line at the previous RTH settlement (the fill point). Watch if price wants to test it or rejects it.
- **How to use:** If price shuns the gap-fill and rallies instead, the gap is real; stay long. If price fills the gap and closes below it, the gap was a trap (what's sometimes called "trap and run" or rejection).
- **Example in this video:** NQ gapped up on Jan 6; ICT was watching to see if it would pull down to RTH settlement (blue line) or immediately reject that level and rally. His longs were predicated on price NOT wanting to fill the gap.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:38](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=278) (setting up the test: "if it's going to go back down here...I think it's going to be bearish")
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a live trading decision point. Many trades fail because price DOES fill the gap; only reject participation when price truly shuns the fill level.

### Minor Buy-Side Liquidity Pool (Short-Term High)
- **Kind:** reference level · entry model
- **What ICT says:** A recent short-term high (often just 1-2 bars back) where buy orders may be resting, expecting price to go higher. "There's like a minor short-term high. So, that would be a minor buy side liquidity pool."
- **How to identify:** Mark the previous 2-5 bars' highs; the most recent significant high (relative to the current bar) is a minor BSL.
- **How to use:** Use minor BSL as an initial target for long entries. Once reached, consider taking partial profits or rolling stops above it to let the trade run further. Multiple minor BSL pools at different levels create a ladder of targets.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-minute chart: there was a recent high marked as "minor buy side"; ICT wanted to see price tag it on the opening rally, take partial profits there, and then assess whether to roll and run the remaining position higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:02](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=422) (identification of minor BSL) · [0:14:11](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=851) (price tags minor BSL, ICT takes half the position)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Minor BSL is distinct from major BSL (overnight equal highs, pre-market highs). Use both in a hierarchy: enter at minor BSL, target major BSL above.

### Suspension Block (Buy-Side Imbalance, Sell-Side Inefficiency)
- **Kind:** definition · reference level
- **What ICT says:** "This green shaded area here, that is the daily chart's suspension block, which is a buy side imbalance, sell side inefficiency within volume imbalance at the top and the bottom of it." On a bullish day, the suspension block from the daily chart becomes a target the intraday move should take out without resistance. "We want to see it cut right through that like a hot knife through butter."
- **How to identify:** On the daily chart, identify the suspension block (volume at extremes, balanced range). Then drop to intraday (1-min) and see where that daily range sits relative to the intraday price action.
- **How to use:** On long entries, the daily suspension block high is a major target. Price should run through it aggressively if the move is legitimate. Hesitation or wicks into the block signal weakness.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily suspension block (green shaded area) was above the intraday price action. ICT wanted to see the 1-min rally aggressively break through this green zone without hesitation, proving strong bullish momentum.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:01](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=601) (identifying the daily suspension block as BSL imbalance / SSL inefficiency) · [0:12:13](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=733) (price moving through the suspension block aggressively)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On intraday charts, the daily-level suspension block is a macro target. Failure to break through it aggressively suggests the move is not as strong as it appears and could be a trap.

### Bull Flag & Follow-Through Confirmation
- **Kind:** pattern recognition · refinement
- **What ICT says:** "This is one of the times where I'd like to see a potential bull flag be valid and go higher. Not just simply because I have a position on, but I want to see it kind of like shake off any bearishness." A bull flag (brief pullback within a larger uptrend) is valid only if it breaks out above the flag's highs with conviction and follow-through.
- **How to identify:** After an initial rally, a minor pullback within the rally forms a "flag" pattern. For a bull flag to be valid, the next rally must break above the previous highs before the pullback.
- **How to use:** Don't trade the flag itself. Wait for the breakout above the flag and then take action. Assess follow-through: does price continue higher aggressively, or does it stall (indication the flag failed)?
- **Example in this video:** NQ formed a minor pullback/flag structure during the opening rally on Jan 6. ICT was waiting to see if the next rally would break above the prior highs with conviction, proving the bull flag was valid.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:02](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=422) (bull flag setup and the desire to see follow-through breaking above the high)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A bull flag that fails to break above prior highs is a weak setup; walking away is often the correct trade decision. Not all patterns deliver; the smart trader knows when to abandon a setup.

### Position Management: Rolling Stops & Taking Partials
- **Kind:** execution · risk management
- **What ICT says:** Entry at a wick or pullback into demand. At the first target (minor BSL), take 50% profits. Roll the stop up to just below the half-way point or the entry-point level. Allow the remaining 50% to run to the next target (major BSL or daily suspension block high).
- **How to identify:** Mark your entry, first target, and secondary target before entering. Plan your partial-profit level (50% at first target is common).
- **How to use:** Mechanical execution: enter signal triggers → place stop at market risk level → if first target hit, immediately close 50%, roll stop up → let remaining 50% run. This locks in profit, removes the risk of giving back gains, and keeps you in the game for the bigger move.
- **Example in this video:** ICT entered long on a wick, set initial stop (to be adjusted). Minor BSL (previous high) was the first target; at that level, he placed a limit order to sell 50%. If filled, he would roll the stop to just below the minor BSL low to protect the remaining half.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:01](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=781) (stop management one minute before opening bell: "I don't want to roll the stop...I want to see it just rip aggressively higher") · [0:14:11](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=851) (executing the partial profit at minor BSL and rolling the stop) · [0:14:58](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=898) (technical difficulty with TradingView's paper trading fill execution)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Take partial profits to lock in gains and reduce risk; it's not "leaving money on the table"—it's professional trade management. Many trades fail; securing 50% ensures you're not underwater if price reverses.

### Market Timing: Avoiding NFP Week & Geopolitical News

- **How to identify:** Note the economic calendar (NFP, CPI, FOMC dates). Note the date of significant geopolitical events. These create uncertainty and wider ranges.
- **How to use:** Plan to reduce or pause trading the first 2-3 weeks of January and during major news weeks. If you do trade, reduce size and only take high-confidence setups.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Gap-fill test:** In a gap-up open, watch if price rejects the previous RTH settlement (the gap-fill point). If it shuns the fill, the gap is valid; if it fills, re-evaluate bullish bias.
- **Buy-side liquidity hierarchy:** Minor BSL (recent short-term highs) are first targets. Major BSL (overnight equal highs, pre-market highs) are secondary targets.
- **Partial profit rule:** Take 50% at the first target (minor BSL). Roll the stop on the remaining 50% to protect profit and allow the trade to run.
- **Daily suspension block must be broken aggressively:** If the daily macro target (suspension block high) is approached on intraday, price should cut through it "like a hot knife through butter." Hesitation or wicks = weakness.
- **Minor position sizes during uncertain periods:** NFP weeks, first weeks of January, post-election periods: use 1-3 micros, not 10+ minis.
- **Wait until Feb for fresh positions:** ICT generally avoids putting new positions on until the second week of February, after the initial-year chaos settles.
- **Bull flag confirmation:** Bull flags are valid only on follow-through above prior highs *after* the flag forms. Without follow-through, the pattern failed.
- **Time-of-day: 1 minute before opening bell** → stop placement decisions; right at open → entry execution; 10:00 hour → re-evaluate if stopped out.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Hands-on trading discipline:** ICT manages his own positions in real-time, taking partial profits and rolling stops mechanically. This removes emotion and keeps the focus on pre-planned levels.
- **Accepting partial fills and technical failures:** Even on a demo account, execution is imperfect. On live accounts, always verify fills immediately. If a platform fails, contact the broker immediately.
- **Being patient with early-week volatility:** Jan 6 is a complex setup; instead of being frustrated by choppiness, ICT uses the opportunity to demonstrate how to trade it with reduced risk.
- **Transparency about trade results:** He shares both the live execution and the platform's failure, demonstrating that even experienced traders deal with technical issues. No shame in it; just adapt.
- **Knowing when to sit out:** If the environment is too uncertain (NFP, geopolitical news), the professional choice is to reduce size, wait, or walk away entirely.

## Glossary candidates

`Buy-side liquidity (BSL)` — Resting buy orders, typically above structural resistance points (overnight equal highs, pre-market highs). Price runs up to hit these orders, then often reverses.

`First presented for everybody gap (FPEG)` — The gap formed between the previous Friday's close and the Monday open, serving as a reference point for premium/discount assessment.

`Minor buy-side liquidity pool` — A recent short-term high (previous 2-5 bars) where buyers may be waiting. Common first target for long entries.

`Suspension block` — A consolidation range with volume at both extremes; on a bullish intraday move, the daily suspension block high becomes a major macro target.

`Bull flag` — A brief pullback within a larger uptrend, forming a small range. Valid only on aggressive follow-through above the prior high.

`Partial profit (50% rule)` — On reaching the first target, take 50% profits to lock in gains; roll the stop on the remaining 50% to let it run to a higher target.

`Rolling the stop` — After taking a partial profit, move the stop-loss order higher (closer to current price or to a breakeven level) to protect remaining capital while allowing the trade to extend.

`Gap-fill test` — Watching whether a gap-up open will retrace to the previous close (gap fill) or reject that level (gap hold). Determines if gap is valid or a trap.

`Electronic trading hours vs. RTH settlement` — Electronic trading (Sunday–Friday, continuous futures) has a different settlement price than regular trading hours (9:30–4:00 ET). Both are reference points.

`NFP week` — Non-farm payroll week; a period of elevated volatility and uncertainty. Professionals typically reduce position size or sit out entirely.
