## Summary
ICT answers viewers who said he "couldn't have known" the 7:00–9:00 ET low and high. His answer: the 7:00–9:00 ET pre-session is a finite, time-based range that closes at 9:00, so every high and low inside it is known at 9:00. He draws a fib low-to-high on that range, uses the -0.5 extension to project the day's high (28,723, hit within 2.75 handles) and grades the range in halves, quadrants and octants. After 9:00, every new PD array must be tethered to one of those levels. He then shows the trade, a short at the projected high with partials, a target of 28,400 (tweeted in advance) reached about 1 hour 45 minutes later. In the second half he uses a daily chart and a 3-day lookback to project where the daily low should form: clusters built from the previous day's range projection and a daily wick treated as a gap. The theme is that precise levels come from time-defined ranges, and you should test his claims yourself.

## Concepts

### Time-based range: the 7:00–9:00 ET pre-session as the "data window" for the day  (aliases: pre-session, "the 2-hour window", "the multiplication table")
- **Kind:** rule / definition (core method)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A time-based range has a specific start and end (like a session or RTH), so it is finite and specific, unlike an arbitrary range. An old high or low that is not inside such a range is not important (that is why he ignored a higher high on this chart).
  - If an algorithm exists it must collect data from a defined range with a beginning and an end. The 9:30 ET RTH open begins a new session, so pay attention to the 2 hours before it: 7:00 to 9:00 ET. He has taught this for decades.
  - You could build a whole career on that 2-hour window and nothing else (no 9:30 open, no PM, no London or Asia); some days the window is advantageous to participate in, other days it tells you the morning will be eventful.
  - The highest high and lowest low in 7:00–9:00 are final once 9:00 passes (he shows a red line at 9:00 to rebut the comments that he "didn't know the low was there yet").
  - Also usable as "key information that warrants more attention to specific price levels when time agrees with it".
  - If you dislike the algorithm idea, treat the range as "where the buying and selling pressure ends and starts". He says "buying and selling pressure is a myth" but it is not necessary to agree.
  - A mid-day window such as 12:45–1:15 pm is poor input: aside from a macro period there is nothing salient about it. Quality of the information source matters more than quantity: two hours of data is enough.
- **How to identify:** shade 7:00 to 9:00 ET; mark its highest high and lowest low.
- **How to use:** anchor a fib to the low and drag to the high (or reverse), then use the levels (below) after 9:00 for projections and PD-array tethering.
- **Example in this video:** Friday July 31, 2026: price drifted lower through the 7–9 session; its high is slightly lower than a prior high, creating relative equal highs; expectation was a run up to buy side and a minor sell-side sweep before the range closes.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:23](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=143) time-based ranges explained · [0:03:10](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=190) algorithm collects data from a defined range, 7:00–9:00 · [0:10:18](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=618) "you didn't know that low" rebuttal · [0:52:38](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=3158) quality of the data window
- **Nuance / caveats:** consistent with earlier "7:00–9:00" focus teaching (also in L26-040); here it is formalised as the source range for fib projections.

### Tethering rule: every PD array after 9:00 must touch one of the range's levels
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After 9:00, any PD array that forms must be anchored ("tethered") to one of the 7–9 range levels: the candle has to touch it or lay across it, i.e. have a reference point to one of the specific levels. This makes price delivery look like "Swiss timepiece precision".
  - Levels are specific price points, "not zones", and levels are carried into the future "like an algorithm" that pulls its data from the predetermined range.
  - Everything after 9:00 gets referred back to the quadrants, octants, and high and low of 7–9.
- **How to identify:** a gap or candle that overlaps one of the fib levels; if not, it is "out there in the middle of nowhere".
- **Example in this video:** a BISI at 9:01 sits at the lower quadrant of the range (the array price is 28,400.75).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:56](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=956) tether rule · [0:19:09](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1149) anchored to lower quadrant

### Suspension block and breakaway gap at the 9:01 start of the run  (aliases: BISI, "time-based range" run)
- **Kind:** example only (with rules)
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 9:01 (one minute after the range ends) a candle forms a BISI inside the lower quadrant. There is a volume imbalance on it, which makes it a suspension block (see L26-040 for the definition): expect price to rally. He says it is no coincidence that the run starts one minute after the 9:00 close of the time-based range.
  - The rally makes another BISI that is also a suspension block; he wants to see it act as a breakaway gap: rip higher and take out the short-term high, which it does.
  - Volume-imbalance inclusion rule: whenever a gap has a volume imbalance, include the VI candle; the gap is tethered to it. Example: a candle's close at 28,508.50 and the next open at 28,509.00 (two ticks higher) = VI, so include that candle in the gap. "Very simple rule."
  - Price then trades down into that gap, hits it and rallies; he calls this "anticipating, not reacting".
- **How to identify:** BISI after the 9:00 range close; VI between bodies; gap overlaps a range level.
- **How to use:** longs off BISI at the lower quadrant after 9:00, targeting short-term highs and ultimately the projection.
- **Example in this video:** 9:01 candle in BISI (anchor 28,400.75), run higher, second BISI = suspension block, takes out the short-term high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:59](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1199) 9:01 BISI, suspension block · [0:20:45](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1245) breakaway gap and VI inclusion rule
- **Nuance / caveats:** in this video "suspension block" is applied to a BISI with a VI; in L26-040 it is defined as VI at both high and low of an inefficiency.

### Inversion FVG behaviour at the 7–9 range midpoint and quadrants  (aliases: iFVG, first utilization)
- **Kind:** example only (with rules)
- **What ICT says:**
  - An inefficiency formed by a down-close candle at the midpoint of the 7–9 range was originally sell-side delivery; if price goes above it and closes north of its high, it becomes an inversion FVG and supports price.
  - Example numbers: the open of one candle was 28,530.00 and the low of the other was 28,529.00, so price tapped into the iFVG by one point on a wick, then rallied.
  - Bullish order-flow conditions when price is above a SIBI/iFVG: bodies should stay out of the lower half; price only trades to the upper quadrant of the gap, cannot even touch the CE, and leaves the body outside; another return falls just short of the CE and rallies.
  - When a gap is first utilised as sell-side delivery, a bearish market would use it to go down; here price falls short at the CE and the next candle opens back down into the lower area, showing the failure of the sell-side use and bullish intent.
- **How to use:** hold longs while bodies avoid the lower half of the iFVG; the more shallow the retrace (upper quadrant, short of the CE), the stronger.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:32](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1292) the halfway-point inefficiency · [0:22:18](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1338) 28,530.00 vs 28,529.00 · [0:23:54](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1434) upper quadrant, bodies avoid the lower half · [0:24:43](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1483) just shy of CE and rallies

### Daily low projection: three-day lookback, previous-day range projection and wick-as-gap  (aliases: "algorithmic approach to the daily low")
- **Kind:** rule / entry model (target-finding)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After price trades through the called level (28,400) intraday, project a reasonable daily low from the daily chart. First reference: yesterday's range: fib from its low up to its high (he is "above" the area and projecting down). That gives a reasonable objective of 28,111.50.
  - Second reference: a wick from three days back, treated as a gap/range (measured from the wick's low up to its high) because price is above it and trading down into it; use the gradient (octants/quadrants/CE) of that wick. A level from it plots at 28,097.25.
  - Verify the numbers agree by anchoring a fib at each level and reading the values back (28,111.50 and 28,097.25 each match). The midpoint between those two projections is a CE level of two projections: "a high probability draw"; bodies or consolidation will probably respect it, or price may reach the very extreme.
  - More levels: the low of the three-days-ago wick up to the high gives 28,070.25 (the cluster extreme) and a high-side level at 28,124.25. He draws the cluster as a box and then plays the one-minute chart: price trades down into those levels, wicks through, rallies, makes smooth highs and returns to the same level.
  - Lookback: your universe of liquidity and inefficiencies can be summed up in the last 3 days (day one, day two, day three). Once price breaches the last 3 days' range, look further back. Intraday scalpers only need 3 days of data.
  - Daily highs and lows form inside a cluster of PD arrays, not a single one: blend them.
- **How to identify:** yesterday's range; a daily wick within three days that price is trading down into; price at/through a pre-called sell-side.
- **How to use:** draw both, mark CE of the two projections, expect consolidation/bodies near it, extremes at the cluster edge; use for target, partials and the day's low.
- **Example in this video:** Friday afternoon after the 28,400 level was cleared: price went to the cluster 28,070–28,124, with the day's low in it.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Time-based range: 7:00–9:00 ET pre-session; finite; known fully at 9:00.
- Fib anchor low to high of 7–9; -0.5 extension = projected daily high (28,723.00 here; actual high within 2.75 handles).
- Grade the range: high, upper octants, upper quadrant, half/CE, lower quadrant, octants, low.
- After 9:00 any PD array must touch or lie across one of those levels (tethered); levels not zones.
- VI rule: include the volume-imbalance candle when drawing the gap.
- Bullish iFVG: close above its high, bodies stay out of lower half, no CE touch.
- Rank of references: 3-day lookback for daily high/low clusters (28,111.50, 28,097.25, 28,070.25, 28,124.25 in this example).
- Pre-9:30 drift lower: minimum expectation is a run to the relative equal highs first.

## Glossary candidates
`TIME-BASED RANGE` — a range with fixed start and end, e.g. the 7:00–9:00 ET pre-session.
`TETHERED` — a PD array that touches or lies across one of the range's graded levels.
`PROJECTED DAILY HIGH` — the -0.5 extension of the 7–9 range.
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — see L26-040; here applied to a BISI carrying a VI.
`BREAKAWAY GAP` — gap after consolidation that leads to a run through the short-term high without being revisited.
`FIRST UTILIZATION` — the delivery direction (buy-side or sell-side) the gap had when it was printed.
`CONSEQUENT ENCROACHMENT OF TWO PROJECTIONS` — the midpoint between two projected price levels used as a high-probability zone.
`3-DAY LOOKBACK` — only the last three days' range and inefficiencies matter until breached.
