## Concepts

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) in Entry Strategy
- **Kind:** entry model / example only
- **What ICT says:** An iFVG is a candlestick (or small range) that hasn't traded into the opposite direction, leaving an inefficiency. Using it as an entry: wait for price to trade INTO the iFVG, then break back OUT, confirming order flow. Entry on the break above/below the iFVG with body confirmation (not just a wick).
- **How to identify:** Candlestick with upper gap unfilled (bullish context). Body stays in upper half. No close below midpoint = iFVG candidate.
- **How to use:** Entry: Buy above the iFVG high once bodies confirm bullish order flow. Stop loss: below the iFVG low (or below the wick, slightly). Trail stop as price runs.
- **Example in this video:** Early morning, a candlestick left an iFVG. Instruction: watch for bodies trading into it; if they do, check if the body closes below the midpoint (invalidates iFVG). In this case, bodies came into the iFVG but stayed in upper half = bullish. Entry signal confirmed. Trade executed at the break above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:02](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=2) — "using this portion as iFVG"; [0:00:51](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=51) — showing wick structure and what bodies should NOT do (not close below iFVG midpoint); [0:02:34](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=154) — confirming the level still valid
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** iFVG is NOT automatically a buy signal. It's a FRAMEWORK. Confirmation = bodies respecting the boundary, not closing below midpoint on attempt(s).

### Stop Loss Placement & Trailing Strategy
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** Place stop loss (SL) just below the wick of the iFVG. Once price takes out a higher high (confirms bullish), roll the SL up to just below that new high. This "minimizes open risk." He uses the term "rolling the stop" or "trail the stop." Goal: turn a losing trade into a breakeven or small profit within seconds of confirmation.
- **How to identify:** Mark the wick/low of key candlesticks. This is your SL anchor point.
- **How to use:** 1. Entry with SL at iFVG low. 2. Price breaks higher = first target hit, roll SL to breakeven or just below new high. 3. Price breaks another high = roll SL again, now locked in $50 profit (example). 4. Keep rolling until target or stop is hit.
- **Example in this video:** Initial SL just below iFVG. Price breaks high of previous candlestick = roll SL to just below that high, now "risking less than it would cost for pizza delivery." Price continues, roll SL again to now lock in $50. Final exit at intraday high target = $100 (before commissions).
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:34](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=154) — rolling SL up; [0:05:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=306) — mentioning the micro assignment ($50 daily goal); [0:09:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=546) — rolling SL as price breaks highs
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rolling SL is NOT the same as moving stop loss DOWN (which would be bad risk management). Always roll UP (in favor of longs) or DOWN (in favor of shorts). Never against your position.

### Targets Using RTH Opening Range Gap Levels
- **Kind:** rule / example only
- **What ICT says:** Targets = known liquidity levels. Examples: RTH ORG high (if opening is below it), consequent encroachment (CE) of RTH ORG, intraday high, previous highs. He states: "I wouldn't need it to trade the consequent encouragement [he means consequent encroachment, or CE] of the RTH opening range gap; this is easy, low-hanging fruit."
- **How to identify:** Mark on your chart: previous day's settlement (4:14 p.m. ET close), today's 9:30 a.m. open = RTH ORG. Divide by Fibonacci (eighths) for gradient levels. Intraday highs and lows = liquidity.
- **How to use:** Plan targets BEFORE entry. Partial profits at easier targets (first high, CE of ORG), hold remainder to second or third target (buy-side liquidity pools). Example: $50 target = cover commissions and profit. $100 = bank a second win. Trailing to higher targets = let winners run.
- **Example in this video:** Target 1 = previous high (covered commissions at ~$50). Target 2 = RTH ORG high. Trade delivered to both on the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:23](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=203) — mentioning consequent encroachment as first target; [0:09:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=546) — scaling out as targets are hit
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't over-complicate target selection. Use obvious levels (previous highs/lows, RTH ORG, order block resistance). If price gets there and stalls, take the profit; don't be greedy.

### Order Block Entry (Bullish Delivery Confirmation)
- **Kind:** entry model / example only
- **What ICT says:** A down-close candlestick (order block) that, when price trades ABOVE its opening, signals bullish extrapolation. The open is the trigger; when breached, expect acceleration (propulsion).
- **How to identify:** Down-close candlestick (already formed). Price later trades above its open.
- **How to use:** Entry: on the open breach. Stop: just below the order block low or mean threshold (50% line). Target: trace to next unmet buy-side liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** Not explicitly called out as "order block," but the structure used (down-close candles with later breakouts above opens) is order block theory applied.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:30](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=990) — "This down close candle, it's bullish order block. That's what's being utilized there."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Order block is a STRUCTURE, not a standalone signal. Needs context (trend, SMT, RTH ORG levels, etc.).

## Glossary candidates

`Micro Contract` — One-hundredth of a standard futures contract; ES micro = $5 per handle vs $50 for mini; allows smaller position sizes for skill-building and risk management.  
`iFVG Entry` — Setup where price trades INTO an inversion FVG (testing it), bodies stay above midpoint (bullish), confirming bullish order flow, then BREAK above the iFVG high = buy trigger.  
`Trail Stop` — Moving a stop loss in FAVOR of your position as price moves favorably; locks in profits incrementally and removes risk.  
`RTH ORG` — Regular Trading Hours Opening Range Gap (previous day's 4:14 p.m. settlement to today's 9:30 a.m. open); serves as intraday level and target reference.  
`Order Block` — A candlestick(s) representing institutional activity; down-close order block becomes buy signal when price breaks above its open.
