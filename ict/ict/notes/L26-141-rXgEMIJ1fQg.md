## Summary
ICT's closing lecture of the series is about simplifying: pick one market, use "one or two, maybe three" moving parts, and always begin by "casting a vision", meaning knowing where price is likely to draw (a liquidity pool or an inefficiency) right now, and doing nothing if that is unclear. He demonstrates this on a bearish NQ day. Price rallies in pre-market into an old Dec 23 first-presented FVG (formed after a turtle soup), rejects, forms a first-presented FVG after 9:30, and then grinds down to the overnight (midnight to 7:00 ET) relative equal lows, with each macro window and each small FVG playing a "leading" or "supporting" role in that storyline. He introduces a new rule of thumb (keep the first FVG that forms after a turtle soup raid on your chart for 20 days) and a new daily-chart PD array, the suspension block. He argues that narrative beats market structure, that time distortion explains choppy structure, that the daily chart must always be consulted, and that back-testing candle by candle is the only way to learn this. It matters because it shows how the "first presentation" idea, overnight liquidity, macros and the daily PD array fit into one top-down story, and it states his intent that the algorithm and the teaching are not changing.

## Concepts

### Casting a vision / Draw on liquidity first  (aliases: "cast forth a vision", narrative, "the plot", "where is price drawing to")
- **Kind:** rule (core method)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Every trader is projecting the future of price; if you are not, you are gambling. The "don't predict" line is dismissed as nonsense, since the vision is exactly what you are doing.
  - The first primary skill is knowing where price is likely to go next (the draw on liquidity). Without knowing what price is reaching for, you cannot get in rhythm with it.
  - Veteran students do not try to work out the next two or three runs. They look at price at this moment and ask what it is likely to do now. If nothing is clear, they do nothing, which removes worry and fear of missing out.
  - Once the vision is set (e.g. "price is reaching for overnight lows") you keep it on the chart and "do not abandon the idea" until price proves you wrong and it is obvious.
  - Price can only be doing one of four things: drawing to sell-side liquidity, drawing to buy-side liquidity, running for a premium inefficiency above, or running for an inefficiency below. Only a geopolitical event breaks that.
- **How to identify:** ask "from the market price right now, where is it drawing?" Name the pool (here: relative equal lows from the overnight session) and then judge each candle by whether it serves that plot.
- **How to use:** the vision sets which candles matter and what is "permissible" for price. Ask what limits you would place on price if you controlled it. If you cannot build a narrative for why price should do what you want, you are forcing a setup.
- **Example in this video:** the Dec 2025 NQ day below. Vision: price rallied in pre-market to an old FVG, rejected, so it should go back down and take the overnight lows. The vision held from before 9:30 until the lows were taken in the late-morning macro.

### First presented FVG after 9:30  (aliases: first FVG after the open, opening range FVG, "first presentation")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (the video's most repeated idea)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Watch the opening range, 9:30 to 10:00 ET, for the first fair value gap that forms after 9:30, specifically in the direction of the plot (here, sell side is the draw, so a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency, SIBI).
  - That first FVG is "the PDA" (premium array in a bearish plot) that price reacts from. It is not support and resistance; it is the turning point because it is the first presented imbalance.
  - Before 9:30 he is already "waiting for a price run that creates a long protracted down-close candle", i.e. the displacement that will leave that FVG.
  - It acts as a premium array: price should be repelled from it and not trade into its upper half. If price cannot even reach its consequent encroachment, that tells you it is "exceedingly weak" and the plot (sell off) holds.
  - Over December he watched this "hammer opportunities after opportunities" repeatedly. He states he has no intention of changing the algorithm or the teaching, and "the concept of first presentation is very important".
- **How to identify:** after 9:30, the first clear displacement leg that leaves an imbalance (three-candle gap). It lines up with the direction of the draw (overnight lows below = first SIBI).
- **How to use:** expect price to use it more than once ("it's using it here and here"), retrace into it only to its lower portion (bearish case), and reject. It also defines the top of the dealing range (see Range/equilibrium below).
- **Example in this video:** the reviewed NQ day, 1-minute. Pre-market rallies into the old Dec 23 FVG, starts to sell off. Just after 9:30 a long down-close displacement candle leaves the first SIBI. Price retraces to the low of that gap (not into the upper half) and rolls over. About 50% of the whole run down happened fast, the other half was slow give-and-take reaching toward the overnight lows. During the 9:50–10:10 macro price again returns to the low of this first FVG, "breezes" to its low, and is rejected. In the PM session price again approaches this first presented FVG before the afternoon drop.

### First presented FVG after a turtle soup  (aliases: first FVG after a liquidity raid; keep it for 20 days)
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a turtle soup (price runs a swing high, fails, and snaps back down through the prior run), the first FVG that forms during the displacement after that is a level you keep on your chart. Bearish version here: buy-side taken, turtle soup, sell off, first SIBI. He says it can be done on the buy side too (mirror image); for the bullish mirror he describes it as the first FVG after a sell-side raid that then runs higher.
  - The market does not need to actually reverse or even take the opposing swing low. Price just has to show displacement through a short-term swing and leave a gap that "stands out". If it jumps off the chart when you zoom out, it counts.
  - Zoom out (not in) so your eye jumps to the standout swing highs and lows where the manipulation occurred.
  - Keep that FVG on the chart and project it forward. In line with the updated ranges (the shortest lookback being 20 days) he keeps it on the chart for 20 days (caption unclear on the exact phrasing). He says that is why his Dec 23 level was still useful.
  - Doing so puts you "in the future": you know the level will matter long before volume profile or Elliott wave stuff shows it, and you then wait for time to agree with the price.
- **How to identify:** (1) a recognizable raid of a short-term high/low (turtle soup); (2) displacement through the swing the other way; (3) the first FVG left by that move.
- **How to use:** log it (TradingView workspace, or paper notepad "old school" as he does), carry it 20 days, and watch for price to return to it (as a premium array if it is a sell-side imbalance, or discount for the mirror).
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday Dec 23, 2025, NQ 1-minute, around 9:54 ET. Price ran straight up from the 9:30 open, made a short-term high, rallied above it (false break), turtle-souped back down into the initial run, then displaced below the swing low. The first FVG after that turtle soup is the standout. A few trading days later, pre-market price runs straight up to this FVG's high and rejects, which is the setup for the reviewed day.

### Overnight range lows as sell-side liquidity draw  (aliases: overnight lows, midnight-to-7:00 range, Asian/London lows)
- **Kind:** rule (routine)
- **What ICT says:** the easiest routine: mark off midnight ET to 7:00 AM ET and take the lowest low (and the highest high) of that window. If those lows are relative equal lows, "that's a loaded deal", it is where sell-side liquidity rests and price will want to seek it if the day is bearish. It does not always happen that day, "but that's how I classify it".
- **How to use:** it is the draw you cast the vision toward. Hold the vision (see Casting a vision) while the opening-range FVG, macros and small FVGs play out beneath it.
- **Example in this video:** overnight low was a relative equal low and a clear standout. Price took it later in the session after the 10:50–11:10 macro displacement, where the first run lower goes through the nearby sell side and then the overnight sell side.

### Relative equal lows (and highs): the "slightly higher low to the right" rule  (aliases: equal lows, RELs)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** two lows are relative equal when the one on the right is slightly higher than the one on the left. That is the pattern that makes a strong candidate for an algorithmic sell-side raid. If the right-hand low is instead lower than the left (not relative equal), the raid is less likely to be immediate; price can eventually trade through, but first retraces for an inefficiency above or a short-term high (an "efficiency premium" or buy-side pool) to the left.
- **How to identify:** compare successive swing lows; right low marginally higher than the left = relative equal. He says "that's how your eye is supposed to identify it".
- **How to use:** treat as a high-probability target when the plot is bearish. If you see the non-equal version, expect a deeper retracement before the run.
- **Example in this video:** (1) zoomed in during the opening-range consolidation: two lows, the right one slightly higher, flagged as a strong candidate for a sell-side raid; price later takes them. (2) PM session: minor sell side with relative equal lows (lows to the right slightly higher) just above an old AM-session RTH low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:59](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1439) what makes them relative equal · [0:24:46](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1486) the non-equal version needs a deeper retracement · [0:40:48](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2448) PM relative equal lows

### FVG as premium/discount array: behavior inside the gap  (aliases: consequent encroachment (CE), lower quadrant, upper half)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - In a bearish plot a SIBI is a premium array: it should repel price or stop it from entering the upper half. A move that only "breezes" to the low of the gap and says "no thanks" confirms the plot.
  - A later FVG formed in that rally (a leading candle) is then tested by a supporting candle that reaches its CE (50%) and no further; then price dives.
  - Body versus wick signature: when wicks poke almost to the top of the FVG but the candle bodies stay in the lower half (lower quadrant), "that's the signature that you know it's getting ready to start falling".
  - When bodies cannot reach the high of the gap, only the wicks respect it, then price drops from the CE on that wick.
  - Using the same logic on a higher timeframe: how far into an inefficiency above price a bearish market trades tells you the strength. Only reaching the lower half signals weakness (bearish bias intact); slicing through the upper quadrant has different implications (see Suspension block). (caption unclear in this passage)
- **How to use:** watch where bodies close relative to the gap's midpoint and top, not just wicks. Cut through candles (use mid-ranges) rather than painting whole supply/demand zones: "I'm cutting through candles." He insists this is not supply and demand renamed.
- **Example in this video:** mid-morning 1-minute: price comes up into an old SIBI, wicks nearly to the top, bodies stay in the lower quadrant, then it sells off to a new minor sell side. Again later, a small gap left after that low: price comes up, bodies do not reach its high, wicks respect it, CE on the wick, then price moves lower (sideways first until the 10:50 macro).
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:11](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1391) SIBI leading candle, CE test by supporting candle · [0:30:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1824) "cutting through candles", not supply/demand · [0:31:59](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1919) wicks to the top but bodies in the lower half = signature of falling · [0:33:37](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2017) bodies don't reach the high, wick respects, CE
- **Nuance / caveats:** the "bodies in the lower quadrant" signature is stated for a bearish case; the bullish mirror is implied but not shown.

### Leading vs supporting candles ("leading man / extras")  (aliases: candle as actor, narrative candles)
- **Kind:** new coinage (analogy for the narrative method)
- **What ICT says:** every candle has a role in the plot, but not every candle is a lead. Leading candles are the ones that carry the story at the right time (e.g. the SIBI-forming candle in the 9:50–10:10 macro). Supporting actors are the later candles that "use" the leading candle (reach its CE, fail at its high). "Extras" are candles and gaps that do not matter to the plot. During a consolidation, hunt for the candles that deliver the performance at the right time, not the noise.
- **How to identify / use:** ask whether a given gap or candle is on the path to the draw. Example of an extra: a tiny FVG that forms before the swing low has even been broken ("a common gap") is unimportant. It would become salient only if price had broken below that low.
- **Example in this video:** leading candle = the macro-time candle that creates a SIBI after price returns to the first FVG; supporting = the next candle reaching the CE of that SIBI; extras = the small FVG inside the consolidation before the low is taken.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:25](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1345) leading man/woman candlestick · [0:26:20](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1580) why the tiny FVG is an extra · [0:34:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2064) supporting actors that use earlier leads
- **Nuance / caveats:** an analogy, not a formal concept, but it answers "why did he pick that FVG?" (because it was a leading candle in the plot, tied to the draw and to time).

### Dealing range defined by PD array and liquidity, with equilibrium inefficiency  (aliases: the range, equilibrium inside the range, "plot twist")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After price sells from the first presented FVG into a new (minor) sell-side low, you define the range from that PD array (the high) to the new low that was just taken. "Defined simply by time and my PD arrays": no volume profile, point of control or value area involved.
  - The retracement after the minor sell-side raid has an upper limit: the equilibrium (midpoint) of that range, and there should be an inefficiency (a SIBI) sitting around that midpoint. Extend that gap through, and price comes back to it, then "the story is still the same". Retracements people read as a short-term low and reversal are only plot twists that reach to the equilibrium.
  - Later, that same equilibrium SIBI is revisited at a time period and used as resistance (price comes up to it, then sells off to new minor sell side).
- **How to use:** after a shallow run on minor sell side, project how high the retracement can go by the midpoint of the range and the FVG there; use this to avoid being tricked into a long.
- **Example in this video:** range from the high of the first presented FVG (the premium array it sold off) down to the minor sell-side low; mid-range holds a SIBI; price retraces to it then sells to a new minor low.

### Inversion FVG as a small-timeframe Model 2022 entry  (aliases: iFVG, "model 22")
- **Kind:** entry model (example only; restates old teaching)
- **What ICT says:** in the PM drop, an inversion FVG is the last major upside displacement (an FVG) before price hits a premium array and folds over. That sequence, he says, is his Model 2022 "on a very small time frame", and it is the first FVG after the rotation over. Afterward, price uses the iFVG's CE, sells off, uses another FVG, sells off again, consolidates (a bearish pennant), breaks lower and fizzles into the low into the close.

- **Example in this video:** PM session, 1-minute: price rallies into the first presented FVG, a small iFVG forms as price folds over, price retraces to its CE then drops, takes the low, clears the RTH AM-session low, dives, consolidates in a bearish pennant, breaks, and fizzles into the close.

### Suspension block  (aliases: ICT suspension block; daily-chart PD array released in 2025)
- **Kind:** new coinage (new PD array)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A PD array he released in 2025, an inefficiency that sits between candles. The usual view is to take one candle's low to another candle's high as the inefficiency. That is an inefficiency, but he says the real inefficiency is framed by the volume imbalance on each side (volume imbalance and volume imbalance), i.e. from one candle's open to another candle's close. The candle in between is what is highlighted as the suspension block. (caption is unclear on exactly which candles' open and close; this is the best reading.)
  - It is "another form of buy-side / sell-side inefficiency" (BISI/SIBI).
  - It is explicitly not a liquidity void. A liquidity void is where there was no trading at all, an actual gap with no buying or selling. That is the only void that exists in price.
  - A bearish daily suspension block's high acts as a premium array on the daily chart: price rallies into it and is sent lower.
- **How to identify:** on the daily chart, find a bearish-delivery inefficiency in which the body-to-body imbalances (volume imbalances) frame a candle. The block's high is that candle's open (as stated when he points at the top of the block on the day).
- **How to use:** put the daily suspension block on the chart as a premium array. Watch how deeply an intraday rally into it gets: if price reaches only the lower portion, the market is weak and it will go lower; he notes that on this day price ran into the upper quadrant, then sliced down, and the implication is a reach for the half of the daily range below (see Daily context).
- **Example in this video:** the reviewed day: price (after dropping) retraces up against the high of the daily suspension block and the first presented FVG, then sells off. At around 1:30 PM ET price makes a small run up to the top of the daily suspension block (that candle's open), "going up to go down", then falls, which he says many people were caught off guard by because they only watch 1-second to 15-minute charts.

- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** a liquidity void is only a real gap where there is no trading at all, "no buying and selling in there". A suspension block, FVG or volume imbalance is not a void; each is an inefficiency with trading on both sides.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- One market; one to three moving parts at most.
- Cast a vision of where price is likely to go next (draw on liquidity). If unclear, do nothing.
- Four possible draws: buy-side liquidity, sell-side liquidity, an inefficiency above (premium), an inefficiency below. Only a geopolitical event breaks this.
- Overnight range: midnight to 7:00 ET; take the lowest low (and highest high). Relative equal lows there = loaded; not guaranteed that very day.
- Pre-market review window: 7:00 to 9:30 ET. If not calling every candle, wait until ~10:00 and let the first 30 minutes trade.
- Opening range window: 9:30 to 10:00 ET; look for the first presented FVG after 9:30 in the direction of the draw.
- Macros named: 9:50–10:10 ET, 10:50–11:10 ET, and lunch macro (11:30 to 1:30 ET).
- Beginners: do not trade 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM ET; come back at 1:30 PM.
- Relative equal lows: the right-hand low must be slightly higher than the left.
- Keep the first FVG after a turtle soup raid on the chart and project it forward 20 days.
- Range = high of the premium array price reacted from, to the new minor sell-side low taken; equilibrium of that range should hold an inefficiency (SIBI), and the retracement stops there.
- A small FVG that forms before the swing low has been broken is not important.
- Bearish plot: SIBI is a premium array and should not be traded into in its upper half. Bodies staying in the lower half while wicks poke the top is a sign of impending decline.
- Always check the daily chart. Never trade without it.
- Suspension block (2025): daily-chart inefficiency framed by volume imbalances (open to close of the bounding candles); not a liquidity void.
- Liquidity void = actual no-trading gap only.
- He takes partials, and states relative equal lows are perfect partial candidates.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Doing nothing is liberating: accept that there will be times you do not know where price is going. Disengage, no FOMO ([0:05:37](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=337), [0:08:49](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=529)).
- Pick your shots; losing money is never fun. As a young trader he tried to add to every position and then milk it as one "mega trade"; later, with larger risk, he preferred picking shots. A winning-but-frequently-losing profile feels counterintuitive and makes new traders think they are lucky and will blow up; he blew accounts at 20 by not following a plan ([0:08:00](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=480), [0:51:18](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=3078)).
- He does not trade around the holidays: thin, unruly markets that run farther than expected and feel like guesswork. He demands "Swiss precision" delivery or he does not engage ([0:09:37](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=577)).
- Learning path for new students: if the lecture feels disjointed, turn it off, note it in your journal, return after three months of experience and again at six; if you do everything he has taught, it will make sense within six months ([0:06:25](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=385), [0:07:12](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=432)).
- Back-testing is mandatory: go through price candle by candle and write the storyline of why it did this and did not do that. Recurring phenomena (running for inefficiency, running for stops, waiting during time distortion) repeat; collect them from old data, even from moves you could not have profited from. "There's no shortcut." If you do not want to back-test, follow a signal-giver instead ([0:30:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1824), [0:31:11](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1871), [0:32:48](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1968)).
- Short clips, PDFs of notes or "five-minute mentors" are not a substitute: a fragment without the supporting logic is like a forced card in a card trick, you do not know where it fits (card-force analogy). Slow, careful study saves time ([0:36:46](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2206), [0:37:34](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2254), [0:47:17](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2837)).
- Watch what price did yesterday and last week and break it down on your own charts ([0:48:05](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2885)).
- If you cannot build a narrative for why price should do what you want, you are forcing a setup, gambling, and heading for tilt (boredom or impatience, not following a model) ([0:50:30](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=3030)).
- Trade with a model and a plug-and-play recognition: "I've been here before"; if he cannot read every candle he steps back ([0:51:18](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=3078)).
- Low-tech logging is fine: he writes down his first-FVG levels by hand; a TradingView workspace works too ([0:15:14](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=914)).
- Illustration of a small holiday trade: a tiny move made a ~$2,100 paper gain while coaching family members that week; the point is that any narrative-based read that makes sense can be exploited, even in holiday volume ([0:49:42](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2982)).

## Glossary candidates
`CAST FORTH A VISION` — projecting where price is most likely to draw to right now (the draw on liquidity), then trading the path to it.
`FIRST PRESENTED FVG` — the first fair value gap formed after a defined event (9:30 open, or a turtle soup), in the direction of the plot; treated as the key PD array.
`LEADING / SUPPORTING CANDLE` — his actor analogy: leading candles carry the narrative at the right time; supporting candles reference them; "extras" do not matter.
`PLOT / PLOT TWIST` — the intended path to the draw; a plot twist is a retracement that looks like a reversal but only reaches equilibrium.
`TIME DISTORTION` — algorithm-driven consolidation or delay that disrupts current market structure before delivery.
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — his 2025 daily-chart PD array: an inefficiency framed by volume imbalances (open/close of bounding candles), not a liquidity void.
`LIQUIDITY VOID` — strictly, a price area with no trading at all; the only true void.
`RECLAIMED FVG` — an old FVG revisited and used again as a reaction level.
`LOADED (equal lows)` — relative equal lows in the overnight range, where liquidity is concentrated.
`CUTTING THROUGH CANDLES` — using mid-ranges/CEs inside candles and gaps instead of drawing full supply/demand zones.
