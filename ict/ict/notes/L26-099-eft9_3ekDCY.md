## Summary
ICT continues the "pantomime" from the previous day: he plays a student one to three months in who has dropped the idea of calling every move and is only practising one simple repeatable thing, the regular trading hours (RTH) opening range gap at 9:30. The drill is: stage the gap before the open, anchor the consequent encroachment (CE) when the 9:30 price prints, take a trade in the direction that fills the gap, and aim for the half gap (about 70% of the time) with a stretch to full closure. He then adds a newer refinement: when price has traded through the whole gap, measure the gap with a Fibonacci tool and project the negative 0.5 level for the next algorithmic run. The second half is a long talk about how to train: many executions in the first four weeks, then study winners versus losers, and no shortcuts. TradingView lag wrecks his demo fill, which he uses as a lesson in how things go wrong.

## Concepts
### RTH Opening Range Gap (ORG) and the 70% CE tendency  (aliases: regular trading hours opening range gap, opening gap, "the 9:30 gap", half gap, CE of the gap)
- **Kind:** rule (reinforcement of a known concept, with new framing for beginners)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The gap is framed only on regular trading hours: the previous day's RTH settlement price (he says "4:14 p.m." ET, the last RTH print/candle) to the 9:30 ET opening price. He insists it is always framed on RTH, not on the 24-hour session.
  - If a gap forms at the 9:30 open, there is about a 70% probability that price makes an attempt to trade back to half the gap (the CE of the gap). It does not have to reach it, and it does not have to reach full closure, though it can.
  - Best-case targets for the drill: the half gap first, then 3/4 gap closure, then full gap closure (the RTH settlement price = "opening range gap low" when the gap is a premium gap).
  - Even after price trades through the gap, "always allow for return back to consequent encroachment of the opening range gap".
  - The 9:30 opening price itself is a level that can be re-traded and revisited at any time ("retested, rebooked, repriced").
- **How to identify:** compare the 9:30 opening price with the previous RTH settlement. Open above settlement = premium gap (bearish bias for the drill). The CE is the midpoint between the two prices.
- **How to use:**
  - Pre-stage the chart before the open: no indicators, just a line pre-drawn at the previous settlement and a second line/tool parked near price. When the 9:30 candle opens, drag the second anchor to the candle's open price and read off the CE so you can state the level immediately (his example CE read as "370 and 3/4").
  - Premium gap = look only for shorts, only for things that will participate in going lower. Use the gap's lower pools of sell-side liquidity as the draw.
  - Before the open, do not treat a pending price as the open price. Wait for the opening bell.
- **Example in this video:** 16 Apr 2026 (Thursday). Roughly ten minutes before the open price is inside a small sloppy range, above the prior RTH settlement, so a premium opening gap is expected. The CE is staged ahead of the bell. At 9:30 the gap opens higher ("premium gap opening's up here"); he wants price to return to the gap, with two sell-side pools below as the draw, the nearer one higher probability. He shorts (demo on TradingView), price trades down to the sell side, then later runs through the gap low area and on to the negative 0.5 projection, before coming back up to the CE of the gap.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:29](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=329) 70% probability of trading to CE of the gap, not necessarily full closure · [0:07:23](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=443) anchoring CE to the 9:30 candle open and tweeting it · [0:13:01](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=781) "half of the gap ... 70% of the time it makes an attempt to trade to half gap" · [0:55:19](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3319) student plan: shorts only in premium gaps in first 30 minutes, aim for half gap, best case 3/4 or full
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** frames the 70% as a statistical tendency to be confirmed by the student over about 20 days of observation, not a guarantee. He says repeatedly it need not close fully. The "4:14 p.m." settlement reference is stated by him; treat the exact timestamp as his convention for the last RTH print.

### Opening Gap Bias Rule for the Drill  (aliases: "we only want to look for shorts")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** if the 9:30 open is higher than the previous day's RTH settlement, that sets the drill bias bearish; only look for shorts. The mirror (open lower than settlement) is implied but not spelled out in this video.
- **How to identify:** 9:30 open versus 4:14 p.m. ET prior-day settlement.
- **How to use:** anchor the short on an old FVG from before the session or one that forms during the opening range, and aim for the gap's CE and sell-side liquidity. He prefers a pre-session FVG over one formed inside the opening range, because the latter is sometimes favorable and sometimes not (he says he has tried to teach people not to lean on those so much).
- **Example in this video:** the FVG he used was a pre-session one formed in the 7:00 to 9:30 window; he notes bodies were being shared (tight, overlapping) inside it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1362) open above settlement sets bias bearish, only look for shorts · [0:23:28](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1408) pre-session FVG (7:00 to 9:30) as anchor
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** daily context added to the bias: after about nine consecutive up-close days with ES and NQ at all-time highs, a retracement was "healthy"; a possible short-term or intermediate-term high, so both the gap and the daily context pointed lower ([0:26:20](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1580)).

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) as the entry trigger in the drill  (aliases: inversion fair value gap, "act as inversion", reclaimed bullish FVG)
- **Kind:** entry model (applied example) with refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A gap formed by an up move can later act as an inversion gap: after price trades up through a bearish FVG it can be used as a bullish FVG; and when price comes back up into a bullish gap and fails, that gap flips to resistance. The wording "this gap here could return back to its original characteristic of bearish fair value gap" shows he sees a gap as able to flip back and forth.
  - Price comes back up to half of the buy-side imbalance (a gap that is also a sell-side inefficiency) and should act as an inversion, then sell off hard below the prior low into the sell side.
  - Wants heavy, aggressive selling below the low, not a "toe beneath the water" touch of the sell-side liquidity.
  - Bear-flag analogy: he would like the pullback into the gap to behave like a bear flag, because there is unfinished business below (sell-side liquidity and the remainder of the ORG).
  - For a Tuesday-evening Asian session short he had liked an iFVG that "respected the lower half" (price should not trade into the upper half of the gap), after multiple attempts higher.
- **How to identify:** a gap price has already traded through; price returns to it and is rejected; the candle bodies close back on the other side. (Details of the exact closing rule are not restated here.)
- **How to use:** short the rejection of the iFVG, stop above the gap/recent high, target the sell-side liquidity, then the lower part of the ORG. He puts the position on first and then shows stop and target, and dislikes drawing a hypothetical risk/reward box instead.
- **Example in this video:** 16 Apr 2026 after the open. Price comes back up into the gap ("we come out, hit that, so far that's positive"), then drops to the sell side but only barely wicks it at first. A possible alternative: price swipes the stop above and still runs lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:47](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=827) "if it can come back up and touch it, it could act as inversion fair value gap and send us into sell side" · [0:17:00](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1020) wants it to act as inversion and smash through the low · [0:17:49](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1069) bear flag analogy
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** on the MES chart he flags another possible iFVG ([0:28:55](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1735)) as something to be mindful of.

### Opening Range = first 30 minutes (9:30 to 10:00)  (aliases: opening range, "the first 30 minutes of trading")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** the opening range is the first 30 minutes of RTH, 9:30 to 10:00 ET. It is not the 15-minute opening range and not the popular 5-minute ORB. That first 30 minutes gives the rhyme and reason for the whole morning session and, if you know the daily range calculations, the full daily range (he promises to teach this before the 2026 lectures end).
- **How to use:** wait for the bell, then for the first presented FVG. If price keeps sharing ranges (overlapping bodies) and gives no gap to key off, the scenario is simply to wait until 10:00. Focus on the reaction in price at time and the delivery after.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:38](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=638) wait for first presented FVG after the bell, otherwise wait until 10:00 · [0:57:39](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3459) not 15-minute, not 5-minute ORB, it is 30 minutes
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he says "not the orb everybody's talking about". He also calls the 7:00 to 9:30 span the pre-market/pre-session window used for anchor FVGs.

### Opening Range Gap Negative 0.5 Projection  (aliases: negative 0.5 standard deviation of the ORG, algorithmic run, "measuring the gap")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price fills the gap, reaches the gap low and goes through it, the question is where to aim next. Measure the whole opening range gap (9:30 opening price to the RTH gap low) with the Fibonacci tool and toggle the negative 0.5 level. That level projects outside the gap and is a standard-deviation style measurement for the next run.
  - He calls that run algorithmic, based on the 9:30 opening price and the RTH ORG low.
  - In practice the body often finishes right on or just above the level and the wick goes a tad below it, and then price reacts.
  - It is not found in Wyckoff or older technical analysis, in his view.
- **How to identify:** fib drawn across the whole gap, with -0.5 beyond the far end (below the gap low for a premium gap).
- **How to use:** target for a short that has traded through the gap low; after reaching it, expect a return to the CE of the ORG (his stated outcome in this video).
- **Example in this video:** gap high = 9:30 open, gap low = RTH settlement. Price trades down through the gap low, reaches the -0.5 projection (wicks a little lower), then retraces to the CE of the gap. He says it "wasn't perfect" but was worthwhile, and it found "no lower" before returning to the CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:32](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2192) fib drawn on the gap, -0.5 projection · [0:37:21](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2241) "where do you aim for" after going through the low · [0:55:19](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3319) body on the level, wick slightly below
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** presented as the "next lesson" after the basic half-gap drill (two to four weeks into practising it).

### Opening Range Gap Octants and Quadrants  (aliases: lower octant, full gap closure level)
- **Kind:** refinement (chart tool)
- **What ICT says:** he overlays octant (eighths) and quadrant levels on the ORG, then removes them to reduce clutter. A "lower octant" of the gap is a level he wants price to reach after the sell side is taken ("if we can touch that same low and carry us into the lower octant level, that'll be sufficient"). At one point bodies did not touch the lower octant and only the wick went through it.
- **How to use:** progression of targets inside the gap: sell-side liquidity first, then the lower octant, then the full gap closure (RTH settlement).

### Line in the Sand and Closing-Basis Confirmation  (aliases: "line in the sand for the bears", minor BSL, CE of a wick)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price is hemming and hawing, a recent high becomes the line in the sand for the bears: if price comes back above it the short idea is in doubt.
  - Bears need price to go to the gap low and through it now; if it takes the low out on a closing basis (not just a wick), the wick below becomes a point of interest and its CE becomes the new line in the sand: bodies must then stay below that midpoint.
  - He wants bodies below the CE of that wick, and a return back to it later is allowed.
  - Stop placement logic: with the stop above the high, "if I were making the market, I'd run that high before I took it lower", so he does not take a short with no clean framework.
- **How to use:** minor buy-side liquidity (the small high) can be taken first; he would rather see price run that high (he calls the spike a "mohawk") and then drop, and short after that.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:08](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1928) no entry framework, stop would sit where the market maker would run · [0:33:58](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2038) closing basis, CE of the wick becomes line in the sand · [0:40:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2442) would have preferred a run of minor buy side then a short
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "mohawk" is the caption's word; meaning inferred from context (a spike through a high).

### No-Trade Conditions: Wait When Bodies Are Shared  (aliases: sharing the bodies, disorganization, "sit still")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** when candle bodies overlap and price stays in a tight range, there is nothing to frame an entry on: sit still and wait. While price consolidates under or around a premium gap with unfinished business below, he will not enter just because it is likely to fill (the stop would be too far and exposed).
- **How to use:** wait for the opening bell and the first FVG; if there is none, wait until 10:00; do not force.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:50](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=590) staying in the range is the signal to wait · [0:32:58](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1978) "nothing in here that I can frame an execution or enter on ... you sit still"

### Contrarian Return to CE and the Terminus  (aliases: terminus, "done for the session")
- **Kind:** example only / rule
- **What ICT says:** once price has hit the projection and bounced, there is a 50/50. A contrarian (long) idea is for price to pull up into the CE of the gap; the first target is that area, and the terminus (end of the move) is just below the CE. Once that is done you are done for the session, not for the day: use the lunch macro and PM session to observe and study.
- **Timestamps:** [0:56:53](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3413) contrarian pull up to just below CE, terminus
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he earlier also mentions that the run to sell side back up into a gap can become a "reclaimed bullish fair value gap" with a buy-side target ([0:21:06](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1266)).

### Drill: One-for-One Baseline  (aliases: pantomime of a new student, "1 to 1 model", first four weeks)
- **Kind:** rule / training drill
- **What ICT says:**
  - The drill: a fair value gap entry, then a draw on liquidity, then whatever you risk, put the same measure as target (1:1 reward to risk). You are not trying to be directionally correct or know the draw every time; you are looking for simple small runs to practise on and get a baseline.
  - One-to-one makes money only once you are educated in price action; for an uneducated trader it fails, which is why "risk to reward" talk is a misnomer.
  - First four weeks: take many executions and do not fear overtrading. This desensitizes you to each outcome.
  - After that, study winners against losers and look for features that repeat in the winners. These become your own setup checklist.
  - Then spend two to four weeks only taking shorts in premium opening gaps in the first 30 minutes (and presumably the mirror); then learn the projection measurement.
  - He says you should do price-action study for at least 6 to 8 weeks before even demo trading with the expectation of following a model.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:13](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=733) 6 to 8 weeks study before demo trading · [0:43:05](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2585) one-to-one works only with education · [0:43:54](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2634) lots of executions in the first month · [0:49:00](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2940) filter winners from losers

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- RTH opening range gap: previous day's RTH settlement ("4:14 p.m." ET) to the 9:30 ET opening price. Always framed on RTH.
- 70% probability that a 9:30 gap trades back to its half (CE); targets half, 3/4, full closure.
- Opening range is 9:30 to 10:00 ET (30 minutes), not 5 or 15 minutes.
- Pre-session (pre-market) window used for anchor FVGs: 7:00 to 9:30 ET.
- Negative 0.5 fib projection of the whole ORG is the next target after the gap low is traded through.
- Open above the previous settlement means look only for shorts (drill bias).
- A close below the gap low (not just a wick) makes the wick's CE the new line in the sand; bodies need to stay below.
- No frame to enter on means do not enter; wait for the first FVG after the bell or until 10:00.
- Do not trade one-to-one until trained; train first 4 weeks with many executions; 6 to 8 weeks of study before demo-trading a model.
- Add back pyramided entries if the first entry works (he mentions wanting a lower, pyramided second entry).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Beginning stage: take many trades to find your triggers, impulses, how you derail yourself (fear, greed, anxiety, uncertainty) ([0:03:51](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=231)).
- Narrate the internal dialogue out loud; he describes the thinking he does not allow himself silently ([0:40:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2442)).
- If-then logic is normal thinking: "if it does this, I like it more; if it does that, I lose interest"; he compares it with everyday decisions ([0:51:20](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3080)).
- Make it enjoyable, a pastime like word searches; if it is a chore, the money will not keep you ([0:52:08](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3128), [0:52:55](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3175)). Word searches train observation.
- No shortcuts; failed students look for shortcuts ([0:46:33](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2793)).
- Platform problems: TradingView lag and slippage prevented a clean fill and stop update; he says a real broker would not slip like that. He mentions AMP fills are fast. Lesson: things will go wrong and distract you; the concepts repeat and will work again ([0:39:00](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2340)). He prefers to enter live rather than hypothetically.
- Study with context and boundaries, not by staring aimlessly; use the lunch and PM sessions to observe ([0:56:53](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3413)).
- Love of reading price action is required; "reticular activating system" analogy ([1:00:00](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3600)).
