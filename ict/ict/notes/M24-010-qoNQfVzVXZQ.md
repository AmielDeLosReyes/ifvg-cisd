## Concepts

### Opening Range (9:30–10:00 ET) and its quadrants  (aliases ICT uses: "OR", "first 30 minutes", "opening range CE")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The opening range is the first 30 minutes of trading, 9:30 am to 10:00 am New York time. There is **no 15-minute opening range** (he explicitly rejects that idea as spread by "another YouTuber"). You mark the highest high and lowest low formed 9:30–10:00 and divide the range into quadrants: high, upper quadrant (75%), midpoint = consequent encroachment (CE, 50%), lower quadrant (25%), low; the 9:30 opening price is also a reference level. If price opens and trades up, the high formed by 10:00 relative to the open defines the range; if it trades down, the low formed by 10:00 does. He stresses this is not Quarters Theory, pivots, Wolfe waves, or Elliott wave.
- **How to identify:** Measure on a 1m chart after 10:00 (he draws it as lines, not rectangles, on live streams, because the rectangles "fight" him). He abbreviates in notes, e.g. "8/15 OR CE".
- **How to use:** Yesterday's (and the previous two days') opening range levels are reference points for today's reactions: price returned to Thursday's opening range high/upper quadrant and bounced; the CE and lower quadrant of Thursday's range were not yet "booked" (explored) on Friday, so the CE was the minimum he wanted to see traded to. A level that is confluent with another (e.g., 20% weekly retracement, an opening range gap level two days back) and with a PD array in a macro window is expected to give a strong reaction.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Fri 8/16/2024. Early in the session (about 9:30–9:35 ET by stream clock), price dropped to a level that was both the 75% (upper quadrant) of Thursday 8/15's 9:30–10:00 range and 20% of the week's range, and rallied from it ("kinging off of it" more than once).

### Opening Range Gap (ORG) vs. New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)  (aliases: "opening range gap high/low", "RTH gap", "new day opening gap")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The **opening range gap** is the difference between the previous day's regular-session (RTH) settlement price and the first print at 9:30 (first tick). On a gap-down open (his example) the settlement price is the "opening range gap high" and the 9:30 open is the "opening range gap low"; for a gap-up the settlement would be the low edge and the open the high edge (my inference, consistent with lecture 9, where the 9:30 open was the gap high). It is an inefficiency (a gap), whereas the opening range is a travelled distance; "one's an inefficiency as a gap, one's an actual travelling from an opening price at 9:30 to the extreme". The **NDOG** is the separate gap between the 5:00 pm settlement and the 6:00 pm reopening (globex), from yesterday's formation. Quadrants can be run on the ORG too, but only when price is near it.
- **How to identify:** Toggle the platform from electronic trading hours (ETH) to regular trading hours (RTH): in RTH view the gap between the previous RTH settlement (he mentions a 4:15 settlement time, caption slightly unclear) and the 9:30 open is visible; in ETH view it gets filled and disappears, so you must draw the settlement and open levels manually (and the same applies on platforms lacking an RTH toggle).
- **How to use:** Treat gap highs/lows, CE, and the 9:30 open as targets and reaction levels; keep them on a notepad rather than the chart.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday: settlement of Wednesday's session vs. Thursday's 9:30 open. On Friday price traded up into the Thursday ORG area and the NDOG (a wick traveled through the previous settlement; relative equal highs at the open swept to roughly the ORG high). Levels shown on a 1m chart by dropping the RTH view, then back to ETH to show the gap vanishes.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:24](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1404) settlement vs yesterday's opening price = ORG · [0:25:38](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1538) settlement and first print define the gap; chart view change shows it · [0:26:28](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1588) naming gap high vs gap low · [0:41:19](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2479) ETH vs RTH, draw levels if unavailable
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Opening range gap" in this video is strictly the RTH close-to-9:30-open difference; the earlier uses of "new day opening gap" (6 pm) remain separate. The exact wording of which end is "high" depends on gap direction (he gives the gap-down case).

### Carry-forward of OR / ORG levels for three days  (aliases: "key levels list", "naked chart with a notepad")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Every opening range and opening range gap, if the levels have not been completely used (explored, tested, traded to, repriced), is carried forward as a look-back reference for **three days**. "Very influential in terms of price delivery"; wonderful setups form around them. He keeps the levels on a notepad (or a screenshot of it on his phone) and trades off a **naked chart**, because lines on the chart pull his eye and bias him; he is deliberately quiet about a few purely personal notes that "aren't make-or-break".
- **How to identify:** Yesterday's and the previous day's: OR high, CE, low, lower/upper quadrant, opening price, ORG/NDOG levels, plus weekly 20%/30% levels.
- **How to use:** Ask which of these levels have been "booked" already; use the unvisited ones as draws. If price rips through all intraday levels, go to the 15m/60m/4H/daily to see what it is reaching for (e.g., an old daily high discussed Tuesday).
- **Example in this video:** Friday: today's price had touched the Thursday OR high and upper quadrant but not the CE or lower quadrant, so he watched for an attempt at the CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:04](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=484) carry forward three days · [0:12:56](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=776) notepad abbreviations · [0:14:33](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=873) why a naked chart · [0:43:41](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2621) if it rips through all of them, go to a higher timeframe

### TGIF — Thursday-1 pm to Friday retracement of the weekly range  (aliases: "20–30% of the weekly range", "TGIF 20%", "weekly Power of 3 done")
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage refinement
- **What ICT says:** On an up week, after the weekly high forms, expect a retracement of **20% to 30% of the total weekly range**. TGIF can start forming at **1:00 pm ET Thursday** (give noon–1 pm the chance to do its thing first) and runs through Friday's close; he expects this particularly when the weekly range is elongated (larger than the last few weeks). Measured from the highest high formed by Thursday 1 pm going into Friday. If price has made the 20% retracement, and he has no trade on, he is done for the week (weekly Power of 3 has "done its work"). If a 20% retrace happens on Thursday before the close, he doesn't trade Friday at all. If TGIF doesn't form on Friday, carry it to Monday: look for the 20–30% on Monday's trading (it did that this week).
- **How to identify:** Weekly chart (September NQ contract): draw the fib from the low up to the high of the week (an up week; for down weeks reverse), toggle the 0.5 level off so only the 0.2 and 0.3 show. Quadrants are 0.25/0.75.
- **How to use:** After the 20% retracement the rest of the week/Friday becomes lackluster/"fickle": price overlaps back and forth with no sustained runs. A higher high after 20–30% retracement requires "manual intervention" (FOMC or something un-scheduled), which tends to appear at low volume. Take most of an upside trade (75–80%) off at upper levels, because you can't know when the Thursday-1 pm retracement will arrive. A 30% retracement would not change the weekly bullish bias; he says it'd be healthy.
- **Example in this video:** NQ week of Aug 12–16, 2024: two big relief rallies (Middle East de-escalation) after a two-week up move; Friday morning price retraced to the weekly 20% level (also overlapping the Thursday OR upper quadrant), then chopped back and forth for the rest of the morning; he would not engage the afternoon.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:43](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=223) 20% of weekly range plus OR upper quadrant · [0:04:31](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=271) how to draw the fib · [0:05:20](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=320) carry to Monday if not formed Friday · [1:58:09](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7089) 20% on Thursday means avoid Friday · [2:00:39](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7239) TGIF can form 1 pm Thursday; 20–30% from Thursday-1 pm high · [2:09:40](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7780) take 75–80% off
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Related day-of-week notes: he doesn't trade Mondays (demands the market show him information for Tuesday's directional bias/draws), and often avoids Fridays after a big run-up; if no morning money was made on a Friday he does not go in during the afternoon.

### Power of 3 applied to daily and weekly candles (retracement of 20–30%, close in upper third)  (aliases: "daily Power of Three", "weekly candlestick has done its job")
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule (he flags it as "new information")
- **What ICT says:** On a bullish daily candle, after the objective is met, price typically retraces 20–30% of that day's range and the close tends to be in the **upper third** of the candle (not on the high). If a day does close on its high, expect a retracement or short opportunity the next day. Once the 20–30% retracement has happened, it is less likely to revisit the high; price tends to settle within the upper third. Apply the same logic to the weekly candle (this is the TGIF idea): once the retracement has occurred, the weekly candle has completed its role.
- **How to identify:** Draw a hypothetical candle: low, open, high, and close in the upper third; measure the high-to-low range for 20–30%.
- **How to use:** Don't try to extract a new high after the retracement; late-day action is small-range "not trying to make new ground".
- **Example in this video:** A crude candle diagram he drew by hand (not a real chart), mapping daily to weekly.
- **Timestamps:** [2:04:50](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7490) hypothetical daily range · [2:05:41](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7541) close in upper third; 20–30% retrace · [2:07:16](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=7636) same logic for weekly candle
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Not always" is his own caveat.

### Lunch macro — 11:30 am to 1:30 pm ET retracement rule  (aliases: "lunch hour", "midday retrace/reversal")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The lunch hour is a two-hour window, **11:30 am–1:30 pm ET**. Whenever price has run in one direction into 11:30 (one big move or staggered), anticipate a retracement. Find the easiest relative equal low or single low in the run up **after 10:00 am** (it must be after 10:00; he is "not always looking for something before 10"); the lunch macro will retrace to that low, even if price later goes higher. Reverse logic for a down run: from 11:30 hunt setups up to the short-term high or relative equal highs formed after 10:00. Retracement is the minimum; other subtleties/macros capturing larger reversals or pyramids will be in his book. Rationale: whatever has been in motion since the start of the day (he frames the day from 7:00 am ET to 11:30) is the longest-held winning ticket, so the lunch hour runs against it; sometimes the midday retracement becomes a whole-day or weekly reversal.
- **How to identify:** Mark the relative equal lows (or the singular low) that formed between 10:00 and 11:30.
- **How to use:** Fade the morning trend toward that low/high; his Thursday short (Turtle Soup, below) was taken in this window.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday 8/15: one-sided up run; he took a short about 1.5 handles from the high in the lunch window. Friday: choppy; at 11:15 he said the morning session was about to end and the lunch macro to begin; he identified the day's low area (a "high-resistance" zone) and the longs being the longest winners as the target group for the lunch run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:39](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1839) lunch hour is 11:30–1:30, not 60 minutes · [0:31:27](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1887) retrace to the easiest REL/single low after 10:00 · [0:32:17](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1937) reverse for down runs · [1:50:48](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6648) first part of day vs lunch hour; longs hold the longest winner
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In lecture 9 he used the lunch macro as an 11:00–11:30 fade; here the window is formally 11:30–1:30 ET. "Don't take my word for it; go back through your charts, it's there every day."

### Observations / price-delivery measurements (tape reading journal)  (aliases: "baseline", "tally", "heat on the move", "study not trade")
- **Kind:** process / refinement
- **What ICT says:** While watching price live, state what you expect it to do, then write down: the time you observed it, the time it took to reach the level or to close over (count 1m candles), how much heat (adverse movement) there was, and whether it ever came back to take the low/high before delivering. Do it after the fact on recordings too if you can't watch live. Keep a daily tally of how many opportunities you saw in real time with no help; early on it will be near zero, weeks later 3–4 a session, by year-end many; the ratio of correct to incorrect will drift to about half and half, and after that the failures diminish. Record failures honestly and study them; do not "pretend it never happened" because no button was pushed. Do not attempt this on live money.
- **How to identify:** An observation = an FVG/PD array that, if it holds, suggests a draw to a specific level (e.g., a new day opening gap), plus an explicit target.
- **How to use:** Minimum thresholds: **ES 5 handles**; **NQ at least 10 handles, he prefers 15**; and in framing a trade he wants a **range of at least 20 handles** from where it could begin to where it could draw (a "minimum range for targeting purposes"); if 20 handles are available, 10–15 is reasonable to capture. Don't demand a trade pan out to the full target.
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: an FVG formed 10:22, price moved in favor for 9 minutes (until 10:31), could not get above the high, and a stop would have been hit at 10:37; he logs this as a failed first data point. Second example: a gap that became an inversion FVG and rallied toward the NDOG; count the candles on the 1m chart and note whether heat occurred, and whether price returned to take the low before the run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:57:01](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3421) record the time observed and time to deliver · [0:58:37](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3517) daily tally and baseline · [1:09:04](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4144) worked example: 10:22 FVG, 9 minutes, stopped 10:37 · [1:14:36](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4476) ES 5 handles, NQ 10 to 15 · [2:26:41](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8801) 20-handle minimum NQ range
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as what a student should do for weeks before pressing a demo button; the numbers are thresholds for study, not signals.

### Fair Value Gap entry framework for beginners  (aliases: "Caleb's model", "BISI/SIBI", "volume imbalance", "institutional order flow entry drill")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Caleb's entry model is a fair value gap used **only when there is an obvious draw on liquidity**: buy **one tick below the high of a discount (bullish) FVG**; sell short **one tick above the low of a bearish FVG**. He chose the FVG because it's the easiest to see, not because it is the best. He calls a candle that trades into a gap without closing it an "institutional order flow entry drill" (the marker to start). In another example he used the **high of the gap plus one tick** as the limit because it is the easiest to get filled (using the CE or low of the gap would never have filled), with the stop below the candle's open/low of the volume imbalance. He prefers the gap to stay partly open.
- **How to identify:** Clear displacement leaving a single FVG/volume imbalance, with a candle that wicks or trades into it without closing it.
- **How to use:** Entry at the gap's near edge ±1 tick; stop just beyond the gap-forming candle; first partial at the first threshold (10 handles in his example); define the Terminus (see below).
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: "that candle would be institutional order flow entry drill because it goes below these candles but doesn't close that gap in"; a 10-handle run to a prior high would be taken; the larger target (relative equal highs) is optional.
- **Timestamps:** [0:54:36](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3276) institutional order flow entry drill, wick over NDOG · [1:16:12](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4572) marker to start; 10 handles then out · [2:28:21](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8901) fill at gap high plus one tick; stop below the candle · [3:17:18](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=11838) the stated Caleb entry rule

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) mechanics  (aliases: "reclaim", "treat it as an inversion gap")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A bearish FVG becomes an inversion FVG when a candle **closes above** it; then a **new candle** must come back down and touch it (the high, CE, or low of the gap); a single candle cannot do both. A wick spiking above does not count as the close. Once touched, price can use it as support and rally ("treat it as an inversion FVG as it did here, then sent it higher"). Reverse for bearish. He wants it to "stay open", i.e., not be completely closed.
- **How to identify:** Close beyond the gap, then a retest by a following candle; the touch can be as deep as the low of the gap.
- **How to use:** In a bearish context he lists a very nice confluence: iFVG + a bearish FVG + bearish order block, where the order block is the candle with the highest up-close inside the FVG and the iFVG (not the more obvious-looking one). Test the wick: if price trades up but does not touch the midpoint of the gap/wick, that is weak (bearish); if it trades up and prints a volume imbalance, that could be an entry.
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: an orange FVG: "see it go above it, close above it, open a new candle" then touch it; a second gap with a body inside and a tail down was acceptable; a wick-up into a gap that fails to reach the midpoint was called weak.
- **Timestamps:** [1:17:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4622) iFVG: gap taken, comes back, touches · [1:20:29](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4829) bearish order block inside both gaps; wick must not touch the midpoint · [2:24:44](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8684) must close a candle beyond, start a new candle, then touch · [2:38:00](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9480) close above, new candle, then touch

### Terminus, partials and the 50% rule  (aliases: "running down equity" (caption unclear), "first threshold")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** **Terminus** is where you think the trade will draw to; frame the start and the Terminus before taking anything. Inside a range, look for short-term lows/highs (single or relative equal) and any inefficiency (as in market maker buy/sell models); choose the easiest to get to (top, CE, or low of a gap) and don't demand the halfway point or the low. For partials: in a long, if there is a short-term high **at least 50% of the way** to your target and price takes it out even once, take a partial there (he presents this as a rule his private students know); the stop on the balance stays beyond. Don't take a partial just because you are up $500 and afraid (emotional); define the first threshold that must be breached first. His own scaling: 15 contracts, peel 5 at the first target and trail the stop; get 10 to 15 handles as the typical second partial; move the stop to cover costs/commissions if the full 20 doesn't come.
- **How to identify:** First short-term high (or low) located beyond halfway to the target.
- **How to use:** Log the time at entry (candle 1), partial (e.g., minute 5), stopped on the balance (minute 9), and the distance (about 10 handles including spread in and out).
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m, a hypothetical (not a live trade) logging exercise on the 10:22 FVG: the earlier high sat beyond the halfway point; price passed it once, so the partial is taken there; the balance is stopped about 9 minutes into the setup.

### High-resistance liquidity run and the "maximum carnage" low-of-day sweep
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** An area that price has traded in many times (multiple legs straight down, then reversal) is hard to trade through; a sell-off through it is a **high-resistance liquidity run**. But if price takes the day's low aggressively with speed, taking out everybody, that is "maximum carnage" and not a high-resistance run, especially on a Friday after a huge up week where even a 30% weekly retracement would not alter the bullish backdrop.
- **How to use:** Identify the lows where the day's longs (the longest winners) hold stops; if the lunch macro takes the low with speed, expect continuation lower into the close.
- **Example in this video:** Friday NQ 1m: price was drifting around a prior low area in the late morning (about 11:15–11:20 ET by his clock comments); he wanted any move below it to be quick, not drifting; the relative equal highs above were the alternative.

### Draw on liquidity: which relative equal high is valid, and how he'd engineer the sweep
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He walks through which highs count as valid relative equal highs (each high he points at is higher than the one to its right; audio is hard to follow, caption unclear) and compares the nearby relative equal lows (one is lower than its neighbour, so less clean); he concludes the buy-side is the more probable draw. ### Clean vs. unorganized ("sloppy", "fickle/thickle", "picket fence") price action  (aliases: "choppy day", "one-sided train day")
- **Kind:** rule / filter
- **What ICT says:** A day or session is *clean* if you see impulsive legs leaving a singular FVG, a singular short-term low, a run to an obvious level, a retracement with a single FVG, and a quick move to another obvious level. If not, don't push hard and demand more conviction. *Unorganized/sloppy*: overlapping candles ("picket fence"), balanced price range with no speed, many little gaps and highs/lows but no obvious side; it's usually the day when people "get chopped up". Wait for displacement; once an inefficiency exists, watch how price reacts when it returns into it. A **one-sided "train"** day (like Thursday and Tuesday) is the other bad environment: don't chase; get in before it starts or stay out; hold and be out of the way. Explanatory apple analogy: with one green apple among reds your eye finds it; with many reds you get distracted — clean = only one obvious draw.
- **How to use:** Screenshot examples of both and label them; avoid 8:30 news for beginners (he told Caleb to wait until after). On a choppy Friday: do your one setup and be done.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:51](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1791) one-sided train day (Thursday) · [0:46:11](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2771) apples analogy · [1:38:31](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5911) criteria for a clean day · [1:54:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6842) picket fence · [1:44:12](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6252) balanced price range, no speed

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET; no 15-minute opening range. OR levels: high, 75%, CE, 25%, low, plus the open.
- ORG = RTH settlement (4:15 for futures) vs 9:30 first print; NDOG = 5 pm settle vs 6 pm open.
- Carry unused OR and ORG levels forward three days.
- Weekly TGIF: 20–30% retracement of the weekly range; may form from Thursday 1:00 pm ET; carry to Monday if not formed; after a Thursday 20% retrace skip Friday; after a Friday-morning 20% retrace, expect fuzzy action and don't trade the afternoon.
- Weekly fib: low-to-high on an up week, toggle off 0.5 so 0.2/0.3 show.
- Daily bullish candle: retrace 20–30% of range, close in upper third (not always).
- Lunch macro: 11:30–1:30 ET; retrace toward the easiest relative equal low/single low (or high) formed after 10:00.
- Macro windows: 10 minutes before to 10 minutes after the top of the hour (e.g., 9:50–10:10).
- Thresholds: ES 5 handles; NQ 10 minimum, 15 preferred; 20-handle minimum range for NQ framing; stop to cover costs once partials taken; typical TP2 about 15.
- Partial rule: take a partial when price takes a short-term high that is at least 50% of the way to the target.
- Entry (Caleb): buy 1 tick below the high of a discount FVG; short 1 tick above the low of a bearish FVG; only with an obvious draw on liquidity.
- iFVG: need a candle close beyond the gap, then a new candle touching it (high/CE/low).
- Don't trade Mondays; avoid Fridays after a big run; one-sided days: don't chase.
- Beginners: avoid the 8:30 reports.
- Expect no real profitability before about six months.

## Glossary candidates
`TGIF` — the Thursday-1 pm-to-Friday 20–30% retracement of the weekly range.
`Opening range (OR)` — the 9:30–10:00 ET high-to-low range, with quadrants.
`Opening range gap (ORG)` — prior RTH settlement to 9:30 first print; its high/low are targets.
`Quadrants` — 25/50/75% divisions of a range or gap; the 50% is CE.
`Booked` — a level that has been traded to/explored.
`Terminus` — where you expect the trade to end; the framed target.
`Handles` — NQ/ES points, used as thresholds.
`Institutional order flow entry drill` — a candle trading into a gap without closing it; the marker to start.
`Fickle / thickle day` — overlapping, indecisive price action ("picket fence").
`Maximum carnage` — a fast low-of-day sweep taking out all of the day's longs.
`Enigma` — a model he mentions he will teach later (understanding it requires these baselines).
`Manual intervention` — his term for a rally to a new weekly high after the 20–30% retracement without a scheduled news event (low volume, "rigged" by market makers).
