## Summary
This is a live Monday-morning session (Mon Aug 12, 2024) the day before PPI (Tue) and CPI (Wed), taught as a continuation of Lecture #5 for his son Caleb, who is learning to trade the morning session. ICT re-applies last week's stripped-down framework: start the clock at 7:00 ET, wait for relative equal highs (or lows) to form after the start time, check where price sits relative to the new week opening gap (NWOG) and the cluster of new day opening gaps (NDOGs) to get a session bias, wait for the opposite-side liquidity to be raided, then enter on a PD array (here a bearish order block and a fair value gap / inversion FVG). Price then delivers to the layered sell-side liquidity, the NWOG, and the Friday NDOG. The second half is a long statement of process and psychology: partials, one trade a day, 1% max risk, stop after a loss, walk-forward tape-reading before demo, demo for at least 6 months, journaling, and using other streamers' emotional chat as a sentiment gauge.

## Concepts

### Session start-time delineation (7:00 ET) and "wait for the first sweep before annotating"  (aliases: "7 o'clock rule", the initial run, last week's rules)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (re-statement of Lecture #5 framework, applied live)
- **What ICT says:**
  - If your trading day starts at 7:00 a.m. ET (New York local time), you start watching price at 7:00 and mark the first short-term high/low formed by the 7:00 candle area. The chart clock must be ICT time (New York local). "7 o'clock isn't changing"; the rules are never optimized or "morphed".
  - He does NOT annotate the 7:00-candle high as the initial buy-side liquidity just because it is a high. He waits for the initial run to take the high out (gives it "some allowance"), then waits for a short-term high to form and price to trade lower and take out the low. Only then does he highlight both the high and the low.
  - The first 30 minutes after 7:00 (7:00–7:30) is used to form the initial high and low he delineates.
  - Start time variants: if you start at 8:00, use any relative equal highs/lows formed after 7:00 that have not been pierced; if they were already taken, sit and wait for your own relative equal highs/lows to form after 8:00. If you use the 9:00 hour you have additional factors, e.g. the regular-trading-hours opening range (he had shown Caleb that on Friday).
  - He frames the whole approach as: where is it going, why should it go there, how do I know when it will start, what is the beginning of that move.
- **How to identify:** a defined start time; a marked high/low pair after that time; the first run takes one side; then the other side is raided (see Relative equal highs/lows below).
- **How to use:** it defines which liquidity is "initial" for your session and tells you where to be patient. "Waiting" is the entry condition.
- **Example in this video:** Mon Aug 12, 2024. A high exists to the left of 7:00 and the 7:00 candle also makes a high. Price ran above those (relative equal highs, plus the London-session high), sold off, then broke lower leaving a fair value gap and displacement; the lows below (5:00 a.m. low, 7:00 range low) became the targets.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=595) — "I'm not trying to annotate that high" until the run happens and the low is taken; [0:35:27](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2127) — recap of why the 7:00 candle high is not labeled initial BSL; [0:36:17](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2177) — what to do if you start at 8:00; [0:48:22](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2902) — first 30 minutes post-7:00.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Teaching is explicitly a recap of "last week" (Lecture #5). He repeats that the rules do not change and no settings are tuned.

### Relative equal highs / lows as the first liquidity after the start time  (aliases: REH/REL, "layered sell-side pool", initial buy side)
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 7:00 you wait for relative equal highs (or lows) to form after the time marker, then wait for price to pierce them. The pierce is the raid; the reaction after it is what you trade. "They cannot hide 7 o'clock."
  - A "literal" relative equal high is needed: a lower high next to a high to the left counts (he checks: is this high higher or lower than the one to its left; lower, so it meets the criteria).
  - The run only has to trade above the relative equal highs that formed after 7:00; it does not need to reach the London high or any other level.
  - Highs formed earlier (London) are lower priority ("the level of least importance to me"); the ones that form after the start time are the interest.
  - Lows: he sees a layered sell-side pool: relative equal lows, a slightly higher low, and the 5:00 a.m. low (end of the London session) that price "stopped short of". The small low inside the range from 7:00 (and inside the London-run range) is the minor sell-side liquidity.
  - At 9:00 the same logic applies: relative equal highs at 9:00 are "your 9:00 setup"; and at the 9:30 open there are fresh relative equal highs plus a gap just under them.
- **How to identify:** two or more highs (or lows) within a small tolerance, formed after the start time and not yet breached; for sell-side, the low inside the session range is "minor" sell-side liquidity.
- **How to use:** when bias is down (see NWOG/NDOG bias), wait for relative equal highs to be pierced, then look for a bearish PD array to short. Mirror for longs implied but not walked through in this video.
- **Example in this video:** Highs at the 6:xx/7:00 candles and London highs are run; price then trades below the 7:00 range; the relative equal highs after 7:00 are what the bearish order block sits on.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:46](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=646) — relative equal highs taken, leaves minor sell-side low inside the 7:00 and London range; [0:11:39](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=699) — layered sell-side pool; [1:45:34](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6334) — 9:00 relative equal highs "your 9:00 setup"; [2:24:22](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=8662) — at 7:00 with NWOG/NDOG below price you know you are waiting for a relative equal high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Minor sell-side" means a low inside the range between the 7:00 start and the high, not an external swing.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) and New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) as the draw and as bias inputs  (aliases: "old inefficiencies", "clustering of new day opening gaps", "black hole")
- **Kind:** definition + rule (re-stated from Lecture #5)
- **What ICT says:**
  - NWOG for this week: Friday's 5:00 p.m. closing/settlement price to Sunday's 6:00 p.m. opening price (Sunday Aug 11 here). NDOG: the prior day's 5:00 p.m. close to the 6:00 p.m. open one hour later. Example: the Friday Aug 9 NDOG was formed from Thursday evening's 5:00 p.m. close and the 6:00 p.m. open. A new NDOG forms each evening.
  - NDOGs have a shelf life / life cycle; he suggests working with them as if about five days, "not that it's limited to five days". The market gravitates back and forth to these inefficiencies, like a magnet; they are the "first central tenets" of bias.
  - A gap under 20 handles is not subdivided into quadrants (he does not annotate quadrants or the midpoint on it). He eyeballs it (roughly upper/lower half). Implication: quadrants matter for larger gaps.
  - The algorithm "has to refer back" to old inefficiencies at the opening bell, so you gravitate toward them rather than watching order-book heat maps.
  - Price on Monday morning was above the NWOG and above the Friday NDOG and the cluster of earlier NDOGs and last week's NWOG; therefore the weight of the market is down, to discount arrays below.
- **How to identify:** compare price at your start time to the NWOG and NDOG cluster. Above them = expect sell-side delivery toward them.
- **How to use:** primary draw on liquidity for the session; targets are the NWOG (bodies stopped at it), then the next NDOG (Fri Aug 9), and the midpoint (CE) of the NDOG as the end of the morning's interest.
- **Example in this video:** Price traded lower from the bearish order block, through layered sell-side and the upper quadrant region, and the candle bodies stopped right at the NWOG; it then continued to last Friday's NDOG and traded to the NDOG's midpoint (consequent encroachment), where he said "that ends my interest in the morning session". Price then showed willingness to go higher and traded back over the area.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:14](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1634) — NWOG as the draw; [0:28:19](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1699) — NDOG life cycle and the <20 handles quadrant rule; [0:29:16](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1756) — how the Friday NDOG is calibrated; [0:32:49](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1969) — trading to the NDOG midpoint ends the morning's interest.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says he introduced NWOG/NDOG publicly in spring/summer 2023 on Twitter Spaces and deliberately let public interest fade because it is "too good" to be in everyone's hands.

### Session-bias checklist at 7:00 (price vs NWOG/NDOG cluster)  (aliases: "first elements of bias", session bias, "weight of the market")
- **Kind:** rule / entry framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - If at 7:00 price is above the new week opening gap and the NDOGs are clustered below price (all of last week's NDOGs, Friday's NDOG, last week's NWOG), the market is likely to enter a sell program: sell-side delivery, a sustained run lower. He then does not look to go long even if price springs higher; he expects that rally to fizzle above old highs formed after 7:00.
  - Bias here is a session bias; it need not be the daily bias (predicting close above/below the open). Focus on the morning session: a run that is measurable, one-sided, that you can participate in.
  - When the weight is down, the market "discovers / probes into discount arrays" below: inefficiencies, deep retracements into the previous week's range, or short-term lows that can be identified on the 15-minute chart.
  - The run has to be "definitely a protraction", one-directional, going to a level that can be predetermined, "not random".
  - He is not trying to predict the day's close, only one session run.
- **How to identify:** (1) price vs NWOG; (2) where the NDOG cluster sits; (3) TGIF/weekly-range retracement status (below); (4) time of day (7:00+).
- **How to use:** with bias down: wait for relative equal highs after 7:00, wait for the pierce, then enter a bearish PD array (list below) and aim for sell-side liquidity, NWOG, and NDOG.
- **Example in this video:** exactly the Aug 12 morning described above.
- **Timestamps:** [1:09:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4195) — session bias vs daily bias, probing discount arrays; [1:35:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5709) — "if we're above NWOG and the clustering of NDOGs"; [1:38:13](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5893) — above NWOG is a checkbox for sell-side delivery; [2:16:16](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=8176) — strip it down to the chrome.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He calls this "bland, not sexy, rule-based". He says the mirror for longs (price below NWOG with NDOGs above) follows the same logic but does not walk through it here (inference, not stated).

### TGIF: Friday retracement of 20–30% of the weekly range, deferred to Monday  (aliases: "Thank God It's Friday", weekly-range retracement)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** In an up-close week he looks for a retracement of about 20–30% of the weekly range into Friday ("TGIF"). If it does not happen on Friday, it is deferred; the likelihood of that 20–30% retracement of the previous week's range shifts to the next week's Monday. It is not required to be profitable; it is "one more little box to check" that puts probability on shorts over longs. He had told Caleb on Friday the retracement could happen; instead price pressed into Friday's close.
- **How to identify:** prior week closed up; Friday did not retrace 20–30% of the weekly range.
- **How to use:** use as a supporting factor for short bias on Monday (with NWOG/NDOG below price). It applies even with CPI/PPI later in the week.
- **Example in this video:** Last week rallied; no TGIF on Fri Aug 9; Monday Aug 12 morning sold off, consistent with the deferred retracement.
- **Timestamps:** [1:07:05](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4025) — TGIF 20–30% not formed Friday; [1:07:57](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4077) — deferred to Monday; [1:32:29](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5549) — restated.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Treated as a probability tilt, not a trigger.

### Bearish Order Block: opening price as the change in state of delivery  (aliases: "my order block", up-close candle order block, CISD)
- **Kind:** definition + entry model + refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bearish OB = an up-closed candle that is followed by price trading below (a close below) that candle's opening price on the time frame you are using. The opening price on an order block is the "change in state of delivery". Validation: a later candle closes below the OB candle's opening price.
  - It does NOT require trading below the OB candle's wick/low. "My order block does not require the trade below the wick" when the narrative is understood.
  - It is only meaningful if narrative and time agree. He mocks the old "down-close candle gets touched, market goes up" approach (baby pips), and says there is no such thing as an "institutional candle". Candles alone fail without narrative.
  - It is a premium array in a sell program: up-close candles are used to stave off advances higher, but not every up-close candle works; price may go back up into inefficiencies and touch them again because time is afforded for it.
  - Each candle's opening price: he explicitly labels the change-in-state-of-delivery level on every order block.
- **How to identify (as stated):** (1) a run above relative equal highs or buy-side liquidity (the sweep), (2) an up-closed candle at the top, (3) a subsequent candle opens below the OB's opening price and (4) the OB candle's body is displaced away from, leaving an inefficiency (FVG) behind.
- **How to use:**
  - Entry: sell as price trades back up to the OB's opening price, on an uptick, after the next candle has opened below that open. The sweet spot is the opening price, or between the opening price and the candle's low. "Hammer that as a short"; trade it the first time it returns. He does not worry about slippage near the low of the gap.
  - Stop: he says "rejection block, which is the close on the order block" (stop just beyond the close of the OB candle; caption slightly unclear). Risk can be defined as far as the midpoint (mean threshold) of the OB; "it doesn't need to be up here" at the high.
  - Target: the layered sell-side below, then NWOG, then NDOG.
  - Heat: price traded only about one tick above the entry area; accept some heat since the algorithm returns to the area first.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart, morning of Aug 12. An up-closed 1m candle went above the relative equal highs (formed after 7:00 / London). The next candle opened below its opening price. Price traded up, filling the short at the OB opening price on the uptick. A second 1m green candle (also validated by closing below its open) is another bearish order block. Price then dropped through the inefficiency to sell-side liquidity.
- **Timestamps:** [1:07:05](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4025) — OB needs no trade below the wick; [1:10:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4253) — validation mechanics on the 1m chart; [1:11:48](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4308) — entry on the opening price, stop at the close/rejection block; [1:19:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4793) — second bearish OB labeled the same way; [1:39:07](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5947) — validation not because it traded above London's high but because it ran stops and then went below the open.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a more specific statement than earlier "OB = last up/down candle" teaching: validated by close below the open, no wick/low trade required, entry at the open price, stop at the close of the OB body. ICT acknowledges the opening-price entry may not always fill.

### Mean threshold vs. Consequent Encroachment (CE)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Consequent encroachment is the midpoint of any gap/inefficiency (FVG, volume imbalance). Mean threshold is the midpoint of a specific range formed by a single candle, such as an order block candle. Some students call both "consequent encroachment" and he corrects that.
- **How to identify:** gap or inefficiency → CE; single-candle range (the OB) → mean threshold.
- **How to use:** CE and mean threshold are where the bodies are held; entries/stops are placed against them. Stop can be as far as the OB's mean threshold. He wants candle bodies to respect the upper half of the gap.
- **Example in this video:** The bodies on the retrace "stay at the high of that fair value gap and essentially the midpoint of this order block, which is mean threshold".
- **Timestamps:** [1:21:45](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4905) — the definitional correction; [1:42:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6173) — bodies held at the gap high / OB mean threshold.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The midpoint of a gap being "CE" (not "mean threshold") is the key naming rule here.

### FVG measurement with volume imbalance, and "upper half stays open" as the algorithmic signature  (aliases: respecting half of a gap, SIBI/BISI, "bid/sell efficiency")
- **Kind:** refinement + rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When measuring the gap, do not use only the wick of the candle: the body close vs. the next candle's open leaves a separation (a volume imbalance), and you need to combine that with the gap measurement to get the true consequent encroachment level.
  - In a sell program, for a bearish gap/inversion gap/reclaimed FVG (a premium array), you want the upper half of the inefficiency left open: price should respect only the lower half. That is "how you know the algorithm is respecting the PD array" and a measurable displacement in your favor should follow. Wicks poked above the gap by only one tick; bodies respected the lower half.
  - If price trades up to the top of the gap, he needs an immediate rejection there and then a displacement. If it does not give him that, he will not touch it again until a new FVG forms on a lower time frame.
  - He admits there are times where the inefficiency closes entirely and price still goes lower, and he does not like it in a trade.
  - Signatures are "things not printing in the candlesticks".
- **How to identify:** gap forms during displacement, price retraces, bodies stall at the CE/upper half while wicks poke slightly past it.
- **How to use:** short only on the retrace into the lower part of the gap (see lowest-threshold entry below); invalidation: bodies trade through the upper half.
- **Example in this video:** After the bearish OB, the 1m FVG formed; price returned and only the lower half was respected; wicks went above by one tick, bodies stayed under.
- **Timestamps:** [1:12:42](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4362) — measuring with the body close and the volume imbalance; [1:14:27](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4467) — only the lower half respected; [1:17:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4629) — upper half should act as resistance / stay open; [1:18:58](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4738) — need immediate rejection and displacement at the top.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The captions garble "SIBI/BISI" as "cellid balance bid efficiency" (caption unclear). The "half the gap respected" idea was previously taught elsewhere as CE; here he uses the upper half as the algorithm's tell.

### Inversion FVG and Reclaimed FVG as premium arrays  (aliases: iFVG, reclaimed fair value gap, orange-shaded box)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - An orange-shaded area on his chart means: he wants to see price go below it, spend time below, then come back up and treat it as an inversion FVG, a barrier price does not want to get back above. If candles promote that idea and then displacement appears, he can short using a FVG, an institutional order flow entry drill (IOFED), or a bearish order block.
  - A "reclaimed FVG" is when the narrative and time are not in agreement with the simple read of the gap: even though price trades back up into the inefficiency, it is not a buy; it is a premium array. Beginners think "gap = it will sell off when touched" or "gap = it will go up".
  - He lists the bearish arrays to use once price rejects the pierce: FVG IOFED, bearish breaker, bearish order block, bearish mitigation block, inversion FVG ("used to be viewed as bullish but once it trades below it and returns, it becomes usable").
- **How to identify:** a (formerly bullish) gap that price has broken below and then returns to from beneath.
- **How to use:** short on the return into it with narrative aligned (above NWOG/NDOG, sweep of REH complete); the lowest part of the gap is the lowest-threshold entry.
- **Example in this video:** Early in the stream: the live trade at 0:30 "touches the bottom of the inversion fair value gap — that's a trade" before the market rips lower through layered liquidity. Later he describes price trading up into the gap and the bodies respecting the lower half.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:47](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1307) — orange-shading convention for an inversion FVG; [0:53:45](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3225) — reclaimed FVG explanation; [0:30:07](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1807) — touch of bottom of iFVG as the trade; [2:17:10](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=8230) — list of bearish arrays.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Reclaimed fair value gap" is used here as a premium array descriptor (caption partly unclear).

### Entering at the lowest threshold of a gap; limit orders that do not fill; Price Delivery Continuum (drop to a lower time frame)  (aliases: lowest probable resistance entry, IOFED-style entry)
- **Kind:** entry model / execution rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - For a short, he uses the "lowest probable resistance": the lowest level of a FVG that he thinks will hold price down (the low of the gap). It is the "easy" entry, the one he knows he will get, so he does not have to fear a limit not filling. Reference: the high of the up-close candle defining the BISI (gap).
  - Even with precision he has seen limit orders where price prints the level and still does not fill. The fix: if price trades to the top of the gap and rejects and he misses it, he waits for the displacement and a new fair value gap on a lower time frame (on a 1m chart that means 15-second/30-second) and enters there. "That's the price delivery continuum theory" from last week. He does not need to enter on the time frame he is studying.
  - After a stop-out or missed fill the entry is forgiving when you know what you are looking for.
  - Entry zone for the OB: between its opening price and its low; take it the first time price returns, and do not worry about heat.
  - Scaling in: when a new FVG forms on the way back and he adds, he does NOT roll the stop aggressively just because he has another entry. He waits for the market to roll out of the area after proving it (bodies kept at the gap high / OB mean threshold) before tightening.
- **How to identify / use:** as above.
- **Example in this video:** Adds into a second gap on the retrace; stop kept; trade stopped out with a gain later (trailed).
- **Timestamps:** [0:51:56](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3116) — lowest probable resistance/lowest threshold entry; [1:18:58](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4738) — lower-time-frame fallback, price delivery continuum; [1:41:57](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6117) — use the lowest-threshold entry; [1:42:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6173) — don't roll stop just because you added.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Contrasts with the common instinct to chase the best price (top of gap).

### Seek and Destroy (Monday/pre-news chop)  (aliases: S&D, jagged, "made jagged")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Seek and Destroy is where the market goes just above a short-term high, then goes back lower to take an old short-term low, then back up to take the short-term high just formed, and so on. It runs you over even when you are right on direction. It is typical on a Monday morning ahead of big news days (here PPI/CPI), because large pools are not positioned and everyone is "chomping at the bit". Price action is "skittish" and "sloppy". He talks about waiting for the market to be "made jagged" (liquidity engineered) versus smooth areas.
- **How to identify:** repeated rotation above a fresh short-term high and below a fresh short-term low.
- **How to use:** be conservative: take partials, protect quickly, do not hunt home runs, and give yourself one shot.
- **Example in this video:** The opening stretch of the stream: his limit short filled, stop was brought down to protect, price rotated back and stopped him out in profit.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:21](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1581) — definition; [0:19:56](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1196) — limit order filled, stop brought down, price came back into the entry area; [0:44:33](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2673) — back-and-forth before PPI/CPI days.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Describes day-type, not a model.

### Trading around high-impact news; Mondays; early-session timing  (aliases: PPI/CPI, NFP Monday, Silver Bullet, morning session)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He does not care what CPI/PPI/NFP numbers say; he looks at the time when price is likely to run. These reports (8:30 a.m. ET) tend to give protracted one-directional moves (sometimes an aggressive reversal) and are "face rippers"; he sits out and waits for information after the manipulation.
  - Mondays: generally not interested, because he wants other traders to put their neck out and see consensus first. Exception: every Monday of a week where non-farm payroll falls on Friday, he trades.
  - Do not trade Monday morning ahead of PPI/CPI as a rule for his son; if you do, give yourself one chance early.
  - Why trade before 9:30 or before the 10:00 Silver Bullet window: when a big event is on the following day, take trades early, because by the open everyone is trying to do something and it gets volatile; he wants to be in "when the market's cleanest" (the 7:00–8:00 / first 30 minutes after 7:00 framework).
  - Without news data in the morning, sustained runs are less likely: know where you take partials, move quickly to protect, do not look for home runs. Having an economic calendar event behind the technical idea is better.
  - After the report, he would wait for information (he also remarks it is probably better to trade the afternoon on a no-data morning; wording ambiguous).

### Partials, stop trailing and "pay yourself"  (aliases: Terminus, trade management)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take a partial before the main objective ("Terminus"); once the first partial is taken and the stop is moved to cover costs "you cannot lose", barring a gap, and the need to be right disappears. "Partials always pay you."
  - Do not punish yourself if it does not run. In volatile markets price can return many times to the entry area; if you lack a plan, take the win.
  - He shows a limit order, expand it beyond the initial level when price really moves, and take single partials at levels he likes.
  - Once the stop has been reduced (moved to a gain), do not widen it back. "If I moved it too prematurely I'm going to eat it."
  - If a trade can be closed prematurely by a failing narrative, move the stop to an advantageous location so a reversal still leaves you with something. He asks you to know why the trade is still viable, why hold, and what lets you re-enter after a stop.
  - If you realize you made a mistake entry (accidental fill), close the trade and see how you would manage it; this removes the "it has to be right" weight. Do not partially pyramid into a mistake.

### Walk-forward (tape-reading) learning path, demo and journaling
- **Kind:** process rule
- **What ICT says:** First goal: learn where price is going and why (the draw), "your first mutant power". ## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Chart clock = New York local time (ICT time). Start watching at 7:00 a.m. ET; first 30 minutes after 7:00 (7:00–7:30) forms the initial high/low; 8:00 start uses unpierced relative equal highs/lows formed after 7:00; 9:00/9:30 have their own relative equal highs; London killzone low around 5:00 a.m. ET is a liquidity reference.
- NWOG = Friday 5:00 p.m. close to Sunday 6:00 p.m. open. NDOG = 5:00 p.m. close to 6:00 p.m. open. NDOG life cycle roughly five days (not a hard limit). Gaps under 20 handles: no quadrants/midpoint annotation.
- Short setup: price above NWOG and NDOG cluster at start → wait for relative equal highs formed after 7:00 → wait for a pierce → wait for rejection/displacement → enter bearish PD array (FVG IOFED, bearish breaker, bearish OB, bearish mitigation block, inversion FVG).
- Order block: up-close candle; validated by a later close below its opening price; no trade below the wick required; entry on uptick at opening price (or between open and low); stop around the close (rejection block) / up to mean threshold.
- Gap midpoint = consequent encroachment; single-candle midpoint = mean threshold.
- Want the upper half of a bearish gap left open; need immediate rejection at the top and then displacement; otherwise wait for a new lower-time-frame FVG.
- TGIF: 20–30% of the previous week's range retracement; if it doesn't occur Friday, expect it Monday.
- Monday: trade only if NFP Friday that week (exception); on Monday ahead of PPI/CPI: one early chance, then stop. PPI Tue Aug 13 and CPI Wed Aug 14 at 8:30 a.m. ET.
- Risk: never more than 1% risk per trade; no more than one trade per day; do not trade every session; a loss ends the day.
- Demo at least 6 months before any real account; only start demo after weeks/months of boring walk-forward tape-reading.
- Take partials before the main target; move stop to cover costs; once reduced, do not widen.
- No indicators on the chart; no setting changes/optimizing; the rules are the same every day.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Partials and stop to breakeven remove the "need to be right" ([0:45:30](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2730)).
- Stopped out in profit after reaching only part of the move is not a loss; do not chase back in or "save face" ([1:31:36](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5496)). Reframe a loss as "what did I do wrong in my hands" rather than "the stuff does not work".
- If mental capital is spent worrying about a trade, close it and walk away; he wore himself down doing this as a young man ([0:42:44](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2564)).
- One trade a day: a positive result is good; a negative result is an opportunity to study. Overtrading and wanting more after a good trade are what make profitable people unhappy ([0:43:41](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2621)).
- Casino shoe analogy: when a dealer changes the shoe because a card counter is winning, advantage has changed; likewise when price does something that upsets what is normal, treat it as a warning, a "Matrix deja vu", not something to overcome ([0:59:01](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3541)).
- Journal honestly but not toxically; keep a list of where accounts were blown or tilt began and create protocols; he used his own good trades as encouragement ([1:00:49](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3649), [1:01:41](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3701)).
- Do not share stop-loss and entry levels publicly: it creates liquidity ("take out the king and the pawns fall") ([0:52:51](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3171)).
- Do not go home trying to fix a loss; "take it home, sleep on it"; tilt and revenge trades blow funded accounts and combines ([1:26:15](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5175)).
- Notice your own feelings: if you chase the adrenaline of being right before the trade has reached Terminus, that is a problem ([1:50:43](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6643)).
- You cannot beat the market; you can only get in sync with it. Pick the pieces of the week you can use, follow rules, be content, turn charts off ([2:31:22](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9082), [2:32:20](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9140)).
- Max 1% risk, one trade a day, stop after a loss; let money management compound the account ([2:34:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9249), [2:35:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9355)).

## Glossary candidates
`CISD / change in state of delivery` — here, the opening price of an order block candle; price closing beyond it validates the OB.
`Mean threshold` — midpoint of a single candle's range (e.g. an order block); versus consequent encroachment = midpoint of a gap.
`Consequent encroachment (CE)` — midpoint of any gap/inefficiency.
`Rejection block` — in this video, the close of the order block candle, used as the stop reference (caption slightly unclear).
`NWOG` — new week opening gap: Friday 5:00 p.m. close to Sunday 6:00 p.m. open; primary weekly draw.
`NDOG` — new day opening gap: 5:00 p.m. close to 6:00 p.m. open; has a ~5-day life cycle.
`TGIF` — Friday retracement of 20–30% of the weekly range in an up-close week; deferred to Monday if it fails to deliver.
`Seek and Destroy` — rotating above a fresh short-term high then below a fresh short-term low and back, running both sides.
`Made jagged` — liquidity engineered on one side (relative equal highs/lows) vs. "smooth" areas.
`Minor sell-side liquidity` — a low inside the session range (e.g. 7:00 to the high, within the London run range).
`Layered sell-side pool` — several nearby lows (relative equal plus a slightly higher low plus the 5:00 a.m. low) treated as one target.
`Lowest probable resistance / lowest threshold entry` — entering a short at the low of a gap to maximize fill probability.
`Price delivery continuum` — if you miss the entry on your study time frame, enter on a lower time frame once displacement and a new FVG appear.
`Reclaimed FVG` — a gap price has broken through and returned to, acting as a premium array in a sell program (caption partly unclear).
`Terminus` — the main target/end of the trade.
`Forward walk testing` — watching price form in real time without clicking buttons, anticipating the FVG/targets.
`Sentiment indicator (chat)` — using emotional live-stream chat as a contrarian read when technical criteria align.
`IOFED` — institutional order flow entry drill (named in the premium-array list).
