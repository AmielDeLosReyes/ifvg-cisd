## Concepts

### Swing Trader's Model and Fulcrum Point
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** A fulcrum is a swing high (short-term high) where if you measure the distance from the low before it up to the high, that same distance (measured down from the high) often projects where price will swing to on the downside. This is based on swing trading principles and represents a natural balance/measurement in market structure. Use it to estimate targets before entering a short.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify a swing low, (2) measure the vertical distance to the swing high (fulcrum), (3) from the fulcrum, measure the same distance downward. That projection is the expected low.
- **How to use:** Target planning. Don't use it as the *only* target, but combine it with relative equal lows, liquidity zones, and order blocks for confirmation. Entry can come from a retracement into the high after it forms.
- **Example in this video:** ES has a swing low, a swing high (fulcrum), measured distance = X. Projected downside = same distance X below the high. The actual relative equal lows happen to be near that projection. ICT uses this to justify the trade and set a reasonable target.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Fulcrum is not a hard rule; it's a *reasonable* estimate. Always combine with other levels (FVGs, relative equal lows, liquidity).

### Relative Equal Lows and Entry Patience
- **Kind:** definition + rule + example
- **What ICT says:** When a market makes two or more lows at the same (or very close) price level, that's a relative equal low. It represents sell-side liquidity below the market, which will be targeted on any decline. More importantly, the sell-side liquidity pool *resting just below the RELs* is the true target; you don't need to hit the exact low—just get close enough to run the liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Look at prior swings (same-day or previous days); mark any two lows at approximately same level.
- **How to use:** Short target. Place limit orders just above the REL (to avoid hitting the exact low and getting stopped out). If you hit the REL midpoint, that's success.
- **Example in this video:** ES shows "perfect" relative equal lows from Friday's London session. ICT places limit orders just *above* that level to capture the sell-side liquidity just below it, exiting before the market reaches the absolute low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:47](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=47) — identifying RELs; [0:01:34](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=94) — entry/exit planning; [0:03:57](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=237) — actual fill at planned level
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't be greedy and wait for the exact low; capture the liquidity pool just above it.

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** definition + observation
- **What ICT says:** Below a relative equal low, there's often a "suspension block"—a zone where selling pressure eases temporarily, creating a small rebound before potentially continuing lower. ICT identifies this but intentionally avoids trading into it, preferring to exit earlier at the liquidity above it.
- **How to identify:** Below a REL, look for a small consolidation or bounce zone before the next potential drop.
- **How to use:** Stop-loss or no-trade zone. If shorting, don't expect to go much beyond the REL into the suspension block; that's a danger zone where you might get run and stopped out.
- **Example in this video:** ES REL has a suspension block just below. ICT avoids planning any trade target there; exits above REL to stay clear of it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:47](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=47) — identifying suspension block; [0:01:34](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=94) — avoiding it
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a subtle level; not all markets show it clearly.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) on Lower Timeframes
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** (Refresher) A gap that was originally bearish (sell-side driven) becomes an inversion when price trades above it. On a short, as price rallies into a bearish gap, that gap becomes a resistance zone (iFVG) where bodies will fail to close above the gap's midpoint or fail to touch it at all. Watch for bodies respecting the gap midpoint to the tick.
- **How to identify:** Track where bodies align relative to the gap's midpoint (using Fib 0.5).
- **How to use:** Stop-loss or partial-taking level. If bodies stop at the iFVG midpoint, that's a partial-taking zone. If price closes above the iFVG, the gap has lost its character.
- **Example in this video:** ES trades down through a bearish FVG, then rallies back up. That FVG becomes an iFVG (inversion). Bodies rally into it but can't close above the midpoint—exact precision at 7,754.50 multiple times. This signals the short is still valid; exit partial here, continue short lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:03](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=483) — identifying iFVG; [0:08:50](https://youtu.ye/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=530) — bodies respecting to the tick
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The precision shown in ES (exact 7,754.50 multiple times) is rare but possible on cleaner markets.

### Macro Time Windows
- **Kind:** observation + rule
- **What ICT says:** (Brief mention) At certain times during the day, the market exhibits predictable directional moves. A "macro" is a time window (e.g., 1:50–2:10 PM ET) where the algorithm (market makers, institutions) tends to execute runs or reversals. You don't need to know *why* it happens; just note that it does and use it as confirmation for your setup.
- **How to identify:** Track historical times when the biggest moves occur; mark them on your chart as reference windows.
- **How to use:** Confirmation bias. If a setup aligns with a macro time window, it's more likely to work. If price breaks a key level just as a macro starts, expect a strong run.
- **Example in this video:** ES 1:50–2:10 PM ET macro is marked. Price action during that window is more decisive; bodies are clean, reversals are sharp. This is when ICT looks for his biggest moves.

### Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency on Intraday Pullbacks
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** (Refresher) When price trades down through a bearish gap and later rallies back up, that gap becomes a premium (iFVG). When price is rallied up inside this premium gap, it's inside a buy-side imbalance (from the earlier downside move). Eventually, price will resume lower to work its way below the sell-side liquidity pool. The gap serves as a reference for multiple round-trips.
- **How to identify:** Track the gap structure (upper half = buy-side imbalance zone during rallies; lower half = sell-side inefficiency zone when trending down).
- **How to use:** Entry zones for re-shorts, partial targets, and confidence levels.
- **Example in this video:** ES gap with buy-side imbalance zone. Price rallies into this zone multiple times; each time, ICT sees it as an opportunity to take a partial or add to short.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### 15-Second vs. 1-Minute Timeframe Coordination
- **Kind:** observation
- **What ICT says:** A 15-second chart shows the same price movement as a 1-minute chart; it's just more granular. The levels that work on 1-minute also work on 15-second, but 15-second gives more candles and more entry opportunities within each 1-minute candle. You can use 15-second to fine-tune entries/exits, but the overall structure (FVGs, RELs, support/resistance) is the same.
- **How to identify:** Compare the two charts; note that key levels (midpoints, highs/lows) align on both.
- **How to use:** Entry refinement. Use 1-minute for overall direction and targets; use 15-second to optimize entry/exit timing within that 1-minute move.
- **Example in this video:** Shows both 1-minute and 15-second side by side; demonstrates that the FVGs, RELs, and order flow patterns are identical on both, just represented by more/fewer candles.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:05](https://youtu.ye/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=965) — explaining 15-second not faster, just smaller interval
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** 15-second looks fast, but it's not moving faster; it's the same price action compressed into smaller time intervals.

## Glossary candidates
`Fulcrum point` — a swing high where a measured distance (from a prior low) projects the same distance downward, estimating the natural swing target on a selloff. Based on swing trading principles.

`Relative equal low` — two or more lows at approximately the same price level; represents a sell-side liquidity zone. Traders should target the liquidity pool *just above* the exact low, not the low itself.

`Suspension block` — a zone just below a relative equal low where selling pressure eases temporarily, creating a bounce risk. Avoid trading targets into this zone; exit before it.

`Macro time` — a time window during the trading day when algorithmic/institutional runs tend to occur with predictable direction. Example: 1:50–2:10 PM ET for ES.

`Inversion fair value gap (iFVG)` — a bearish gap (sell-side driven) that becomes a resistance zone (premium) when price rallies back up into it. Bodies respecting the iFVG midpoint (to the tick) indicate the short is still valid.

`Buy-side imbalance zone` — the upper portion of a bearish gap; when price rallies into this zone, it's trading in an area where buy-side liquidity rests, creating pullback opportunities.

`Tick accuracy` — when a candle's body close/open aligns exactly (to the tick) with a prior high/low or Fibonacci level. Demonstrates order flow precision and algorithmic adherence to price levels.
