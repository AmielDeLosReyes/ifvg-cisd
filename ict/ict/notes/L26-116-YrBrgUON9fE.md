## Concepts

### Time Distortion
- **Kind:** definition / context filter / core concept
- **What ICT says:** Time distortion occurs when price spends an extended period in a *range* (high to low) without clear directional intent. On a 1-minute chart, this looks chaotic and confusing ("feels like a root canal"); on a 15-minute or higher timeframe, it becomes clear that price is simply working toward a specific inefficiency or target. Time distortion is not price being "hard to trade"; it is *you being on the wrong timeframe*. When you see time distortion on your current timeframe, your protocol is: go UP to a higher timeframe, find what inefficiency or level price is working toward, then come back down to your trading timeframe with clarity.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. You are on a 1-minute chart (or 5-minute, etc.).
  2. Price is moving up and down inside a defined range (high to low), repeatedly testing both.
  3. You attempt 3+ trades and get stopped out or chopped around.
  4. No clear breakout; just oscillation.
  5. This is time distortion on that timeframe.
- **How to use:** 
  1. *Do not try to trade a distorted timeframe.* You will get stopped out repeatedly.
  2. Go up to the next higher timeframe (1m → 5m → 15m → 1h, etc.).
  3. Ask: "What inefficiency or level is price trying to reach on this higher timeframe?"
  4. Once you identify it (e.g., a 15-minute gap), come back to your 1-minute chart.
  5. Now trade *toward* that target, not *within* the distorted range.
  6. Example: price is choppy on 1m, but on 15m, it's clearly working toward a gap at a specific price. Trade 1m entries that advance toward that 15m target.
- **Example in this video:** 
  - Nasdaq 1-minute chart (0:52:40–0:53:27): price is oscillating in a tight range, "feels like a root canal," no clear direction. ICT marks it as "time distortion."
  - Then goes to 5-minute (0:54:13–0:55:00): still distorted, but less so.
  - Then goes to 15-minute (0:55:00–0:55:51): *suddenly clear*—price is working toward a specific inefficiency (a gap). The 1-minute distortion makes sense now.
  - The protocol: start at 1m, see distortion, jump to 5m (still distorted), jump to 15m (clear), then come back to 1m trades with a target.
  - ICT also notes: "Ranges that spend a lot of high resistance on the higher time frame, the way you can combat that is drop down to lower time frames." (The inverse: if 15m is distorted, go to 1h; if 1h shows a range, drop to 15m or lower to find trades within it.)

### Continuous Contract vs. Delivery Month Contract (Index Futures)
- **Kind:** technical / tool / critical distinction
- **What ICT says:** When analyzing index futures (ES, NQ, YM), use the *continuous contract*, not the delivery month contract (March, June, September, December). The continuous contract automatically rolls from one front month to the next, ensuring smooth price action and no artificial discontinuities from contract rollovers. Delivery month contracts show "spotty" price action at rollovers and miss information. ICT's targets, levels, and analysis are *always* based on the continuous contract; if you use a delivery month contract, you will not see the same levels and will miss opportunities.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Look at the chart title (upper left corner) or symbol in your charting software.
  2. Continuous contract: symbol like "NQ" with *no month code* (no "June," "March," etc., in the title).
  3. Delivery month: symbol like "NQM26" (M = June 2026), "NQZ25" (Z = December 2025), etc. The letter indicates delivery month.
  4. Continuous: smooth price action, no gaps at rollovers. Delivery month: "spotty" price action, visible gaps when rolling from one month to the next.
- **How to use:** 
  1. Always switch to continuous contract for top-down analysis, higher-timeframe targets, and daily/weekly structure.
  2. Trades are *executed* on the delivery month (the front month actively trading), but your *levels* and *targets* come from continuous.
  3. Example: you see a target of 22,779.75 on the continuous NQ chart (summer target). That level is valid because continuous always factors in the front month. If you were only looking at the June contract, you might see the same level but not understand where it came from or why it's significant.
  4. When checking intraday trades, verify your target on continuous, then execute on front month.

### Order Flow and the "Do Not Trespass" Rule (Body Placement)
- **Kind:** core concept / execution rule
- **What ICT says:** When looking at an inefficiency (gap/SIBI/BISI) in the direction of your bias, price can *wick* into the opposite half (e.g., wick above midpoint while bearish), but it *cannot lay down a body* in that opposite half if the inefficiency is to remain valid. The body placement is the "do not trespass" rule: if you're bearish and price lays a body in the upper half of a sell-side imbalance, that's a violation. When bodies stay in the "correct half," that is real institutional order flow. Wicks can go anywhere; bodies tell the truth.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Spot an inefficiency (SIBI or BISI).
  2. Determine your bias (bearish = expect bodies in lower half; bullish = expect bodies in upper half).
  3. Watch each candle: is the body in the correct half, or has it crossed into the "do not trespass" zone?
  4. If the body never lays in the wrong half, the inefficiency is valid.
  5. If a body lays in the wrong half, that's a violation of the inefficiency and suggests a change in direction.
- **How to use:** 
  - Bearish: "I want to see price stay in the lower half of this gap. Wicks above = okay. Bodies above = violation."
  - Bullish: "I want to see price stay in the upper half of this gap. Wicks below = okay. Bodies below = violation."
  - If violated, consider exiting the trade or reversing the bias.
  - This is a *visual* rule requiring no complex calculation or footprint data; just look at where the close is.
- **Example in this video:** 
  - SIBI (sell-side imbalance, bearish bias): 0:57:25–0:58:16: "You want to see price stay in the lower half of this gap… if it does, as soon as it touches it, you are watching to see what the body does. Does the body close below it? If you do see that, that's real institutional order flow."
  - 0:59:53–1:01:29: Extended teaching on "do not trespass": "Anticipated trading to midpoint. It can wick above midpoint, but it cannot lay down a body in the upper half if you're looking for it to go lower… Do not trespass. You better get the hell off here before I shoot your ass. Well, what does price do? I'm out of here… Bodies in the lower half. Exactly what real institutional order flow looks like visually."
  - 1:01:29–1:02:15: Bullish inverse: "We want to see it trade down into an inefficiency, and then it can wick in the lower half, but it can't leave bodies down there… don't trespass in the lower half."

### SMT Divergence (Market Breadth / Distribution)
- **Kind:** definition / confirmation signal
- **What ICT says:** S&P (500-stock index) Divergence with Nasdaq (or other mega-cap-heavy indices) indicates market breadth weakness. When Nasdaq makes higher highs but S&P makes lower highs (or fails to make new highs), it shows that the overall market is not broadly supporting the move; only a few stocks (mega-cap tech) are driving it. This is heavy distribution: accumulation of shorts, distribution of longs. When market breadth fails like this, the move higher is not sustainable; lower prices are likely.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Plot Nasdaq (NQ) and S&P (ES) on the same timeframe (daily or weekly).
  2. Compare the highs: do they move together or diverge?
  3. If NQ makes a higher high but ES makes a lower high (or fails to reach the same relative level), that's divergence.
  4. This is bearish and indicates heavy distribution.
- **How to use:** 
  - Use as a *confirmation* of bearish bias on the indices.
  - If you have a bearish technical setup (e.g., price below iFVG) AND you see SMT divergence, the bias is confirmed: lower prices likely.
  - If SMT divergence appears but technicals are bullish, be cautious; the breadth weakness may presage a reversal.

### Market Structure Shift / Change in State of Delivery (in the Context of Consolidation)
- **Kind:** definition / context
- **What ICT says:** When price is consolidating (range, time distortion), look at the *higher timeframe* (weekly, monthly) to see if there's been a shift in market structure. ICT refers to "lower low, a lower low, a lower low, and then we have a shift in market structure" on the DXY weekly chart (0:04:51). This indicates that the consolidation on the daily is *respecting* a higher-timeframe shift; the daily lethargic action is price waiting for the fulfillment of a higher-timeframe event or target. Once that higher-timeframe target is hit, the consolidation will break.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Daily chart shows consolidation (time distortion).
  2. Go to weekly chart.
  3. Look for a break in the pattern of lower lows, higher highs, etc.
  4. A "shift in market structure" = a break from the recent pattern (e.g., after 3 lower lows, a higher low).
  5. This indicates the consolidation is likely temporary; price will resolve once the weekly pattern is clear.
- **How to use:** 
  - When you see daily consolidation + time distortion, check the weekly for structure shift.
  - If there's no structure shift on weekly, consolidation will likely continue.
  - If there is a shift on weekly, anticipate the daily to resolve in the direction of that shift soon.

### Reclaimed Bullish / Bearish Fair Value Gap
- **Kind:** definition / structure state
- **What ICT says:** A FVG or iFVG is "reclaimed" when price trades above it (bullish reclaim), or below it (bearish reclaim), and then pulls back into it but *does not close bodies back in the gap's original inefficient half*. For example, a sell-side imbalance (bearish iFVG) becomes a "reclaimed bearish FVG" if price re-enters from below and respects it as support (bodies not in the upper half). This converts it from an inversion to a standard FVG acting as support/resistance.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Start with an iFVG or inversion scenario.
  2. Price trades in the opposite direction and re-enters the gap.
  3. Check: do bodies stay in the "new correct half"?
  4. If yes, it's reclaimed and now acts as support (bullish) or resistance (bearish).
- **How to use:** Once reclaimed, the gap is no longer a reversal tool; it's a continuation support/resistance. Trade *with* the reclaim bias, not against it.
- **Example in this video:** EURUSD (0:11:12–0:11:59): "if it's going to trade up there and touch that again, to me, this might trade above it, then use this as footing to go higher. And then the seasonal tendency for euro dollar… might materialize." If EURUSD trades above the iFVG and reclaims it as bullish, higher prices follow.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:05:39](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=339) — DXY monthly: "would need to get above and enter into to area here and then leave this alone to be a reclaimed bullish fair value gap"

### Suspension Block (Structural Support/Resistance)
- **Kind:** definition / PD array
- **What ICT says:** A suspension block is a consolidation range (high/low) that price tests and then breaks out from. If the block is bullish (price breaks above), it acts as support on pullback; price should respect the midpoint of the block and not re-enter below it. If bearish (price breaks below), the block acts as resistance above; price should not re-enter above it. (Also called "breaker block" in some contexts.)
- **How to use:** Use as a structural level on daily/weekly. If price breaks below a bullish suspension block on the daily, that's weakness; if it breaks above a bearish block, that's strength.
- **Example in this video:** DXY daily (0:03:15–0:07:13): "This is a suspension block. And the reason why we're having all of this difficulty here after trading to the objective… if we lose this, then I'm I'm not so optimistic for dollar."
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=195) — suspension block ID'd
  - [0:07:13](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=433) — "line in the sand" if lost
- **Nuance:** Suspension block is a structural level; treat as a critical support/resistance, not a zone.

This is why the indices are so lethargic: not a directional breakdown, but a flight to safety / reduced risk-on appetite.

## Glossary candidates

`Time Distortion` — Price spending an extended period in a range (consolidation) on a specific timeframe, appearing chaotic or choppy. Fixed by jumping to a higher timeframe to see the true target, then coming back down with clarity.

`Continuous Contract` — An index futures contract symbol with *no month code* (e.g., "NQ," "ES"), which automatically rolls to the next front month. Always smooth price action; used for all technical analysis and targeting. Compare to delivery month contract, which shows "spotty" action.

`Delivery Month Contract` — An index futures contract with a specific month code (e.g., "NQM26" = June 2026 Nasdaq). Used for actual trading/execution. Shows artificial gaps and discontinuities when rolling; less reliable for technical analysis.

`SMT Divergence` — S&P (500-stock) Divergence with Nasdaq (or other indices). When the S&P makes lower highs while Nasdaq makes higher highs, indicating weak market breadth, heavy distribution, and likely lower prices coming.

`Suspension Block` — A consolidation range (high/low) that price breaks out from. Acts as a structural support (if bullish) or resistance (if bearish). Critical level; if broken, bias is negated.

`Do Not Trespass` — The rule that candle bodies must not lay in the "wrong half" of an inefficiency if the inefficiency is to remain valid. Wicks can cross the midpoint; bodies cannot (unless violation is intended/expected).

`Reclaimed Bullish/Bearish FVG` — A FVG that has been traded away from and then re-entered, with bodies respecting the correct half. Once reclaimed, the FVG acts as support (bullish) or resistance (bearish), not as a reversal.

`SIBI` — Sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency. An inefficiency below a swing high where the sell-side liquidity has been taken. Bearish bias: bodies should stay in lower half.

`BISI` — Buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency. An inefficiency above a swing low where the buy-side liquidity has been taken. Bullish bias: bodies should stay in upper half.

`Flight to Safety / Flight to Quality` — Market behavior during geopolitical crisis or uncertainty: big money moves away from risk assets (stocks) into safe havens (DXY, bonds, precious metals). Causes consolidation in equities and indices.

`Event-Driven Markets` — Commodities like gold, silver, crude oil whose prices are heavily influenced by geopolitical or supply-shock events (war, OPEC decisions, sanctions, etc.). Tend to see larger moves during uncertainty.

`Market Structure Shift` — A break in the recent pattern of lower lows, higher highs, etc., indicating a potential change in trend direction. Observed on higher timeframes (weekly, monthly) to explain daily consolidation.

`Consequent Encroachment` — The midpoint of a gap or wick; critical level for determining strength/weakness (failure to touch = weakness in that direction).

`Relative Equal Low (REL)` — A prior swing low that price is likely to revisit or break through on corrective lower move.

`Relative Equal High (REH)` — A prior swing high that price is likely to revisit or break through on corrective higher move.

`Real Institutional Order Flow` — Price action that shows clear intent: bodies in the correct half, quick moves through targets, no time distortion. Opposite of retail-driven choppy action.

`Seasonal Tendency` — A recurring directional bias for a currency pair or commodity at a certain time of year (e.g., EURUSD, GBPUSD stronger in spring). Used as a filter, not primary signal.
