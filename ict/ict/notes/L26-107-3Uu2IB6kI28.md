## Concepts

### Bodies Cannot Touch (Key Levels) — Algorithmic Signature of Bearishness
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When price is in a bearish phase (downtrend, breaking lows), candlestick bodies will not close above certain key levels, even if wicks reach those levels. This is an algorithmic signature that smart money is preventing price from holding above that level. Once you spot this signature (bodies unable to touch a level multiple times), you know the market is bearish and price will go lower. This rule applies universally: lower quadrants of daily suspension blocks, event horizon levels, old resistance turned support, etc.
- **How to identify:** Watch a key level (e.g., lower quadrant of daily suspension block). If price retraces back up to it during a downtrend, do the bodies close above that level or stay below it? If bodies consistently stay below, it's bearish.
- **How to use:** This is your confirmation signal. Do not switch your bias until bodies finally breach and close above the level. The longer bodies refuse to touch it, the more bearish the signal.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ daily chart: lower quadrant of daily suspension block at ~25,000. During the downtrend, price retraced up multiple times; bodies never closed above this level, confirming bearishness each time. When price finally ran lower, it proved the signature was correct.

### Lower Quadrant (aliases: 25% level, lower quarter of suspension block, Q1)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A suspension block (large volume imbalance) can be divided into four equal parts (quadrants). The lower quadrant is the bottom 25% of that range. This level acts as support during downtrends and resistance during uptrends. Bodies that fail to close above the lower quadrant signal continued bearishness.
- **How to identify:** Suspension block low + [(high - low) × 0.25] = Lower Quadrant level.
- **How to use:** During downtrends, use as a retracement target and reversal zone. If price retraces into the lower quadrant and bodies cannot hold above it, the downtrend resumes. If price is below the lower quadrant, look for it to drop lower (to lower octant or event horizon).
- **Example in this video:** MNQ daily: lower quadrant at ~25,000; bodies repeatedly failed to hold above this level during the downtrend, confirming each attempt to find support was rejected.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:12](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=372) marks upper quadrant and lower quadrant visually; [0:13:26](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=806) applies lower quadrant rule to bearishness.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Lower quadrant is not a definitive support; it's a filtered key level where bodies matter more than wicks.

### Daily Suspension Block (aliases: daily FVG, daily range inefficiency, gray box)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The daily suspension block is the largest, clearest volume imbalance on the daily chart. It brackets a range with a high and low, creating a gray-shaded box on the chart. Intraday price will gravitate toward and eventually fill this range. The low of the daily suspension block is often the deepest intraday target.
- **How to identify:** On the daily chart, find the clearest volume imbalance (where bodies don't connect between two days). Shade it. This is your daily suspension block.
- **How to use:** Use as an intraday target for shorts (if price is above it and expected to drop) or for longs (if price is below it and expected to bounce). Partial profits at the edges (quadrants, octants, midpoint) of the daily suspension block.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: daily suspension block spanned a huge range (gray box); ICT used it to grade all intraday price action. The low of this box was the intraday target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:57](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=177) identifies daily suspension block; [0:06:12](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=372) divides it into quadrants and octants; [0:08:39](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=519) shows price using it as support/resistance.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not every daily chart has a clean suspension block; wait for one that's obvious before using it.

### Event Horizon (aliases: midpoint of daily suspension block, center void, balance point)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The exact midpoint of the daily suspension block appears empty (a void, the ether), but price gravitates to it with algorithmic precision. This "event horizon" is a balance point where the market pauses or reverses temporarily. It's not marked on most charts, but it exists as pure geometry.
- **How to identify:** (Daily suspension block high + low) ÷ 2 = Event Horizon.
- **How to use:** Use as a mid-point reversal zone, pullback target, or continuation staging point. If price overshoots event horizon, expect it to come back.

### Discount Sensitivity (aliases: bullish body placement, upper half usage)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When a PD array is at discount (premium level, resistance), and price pulls back, if bodies enter and close in the upper half of that array (above the 50% line) without entering the lower half, price is showing "discount sensitivity" or bullish bias. The bodies are saying, "We respect this level as discount, but we're still bullish enough to hold above mid-range."
- **How to identify:** Mark a PD array's 50% line. On pullback, watch where bodies close. If in upper half, it's discount sensitivity. If in lower half, it's weakness.
- **How to use:** Discount sensitivity = continuation signal. When price shows it, expect another run higher after the pullback. If bodies breach into lower half, bullishness is broken.
- **Example in this video:** Not directly shown, but the inverse is shown: bodies failing to reach levels = lack of discount sensitivity = bearishness.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:00](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=660) references discount sensitivity concept (bodies in upper half = bullish).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This concept is best understood through its inverse (bodies failing to show discount sensitivity = bearishness).

### Premium Sensitivity (aliases: bearish body placement, lower half usage)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When a PD array is at premium (discount level, support), and price bounces, if bodies enter and close in the lower half of that array (below the 50% line) without entering the upper half, price is showing "premium sensitivity" or bearish bias. The bodies are saying, "We found support, but we're not really bullish; we're still fighting to go lower."
- **How to identify:** Mark a PD array's 50% line. On bounce, watch where bodies close. If in lower half, it's premium sensitivity (bearish). If in upper half, it's strength.
- **How to use:** Premium sensitivity = continuation signal downward. When price shows it, expect another leg lower after the bounce fails. If bodies breach into upper half, bearishness is broken.

### Relative Equal Highs / Lows (aliases: equality of swings, matched highs/lows, second and first swings)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When two swing highs are at roughly the same price level, or two swing lows, the market is creating "relative equal highs" or "relative equal lows." These are key reference points for breakouts and breakdowns. If price breaks below a relative equal low, it signals weakness; above a relative equal high, strength.
- **How to identify:** Plot swings on a daily/weekly chart. If two highs (or lows) are within 50–100 points of each other, mark them as relative equal.
- **How to use:** Relative equal lows act as support; relative equal highs act as resistance. Breaks of these levels confirm directional intent.
- **Example in this video:** Implied in the review (price uses old lows as reference).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Bodies must not touch key levels when bearish.** Wicks are allowed; bodies are not. Repeat rejection = strong bearish signal.
- **Quadrants divide suspension blocks into quarters.** Lower quadrant (0–25%), upper quadrant (75–100%), midpoint (50%).
- **Octants divide suspension blocks into eighths.** Use for finer-grained targets.
- **Event horizon (50% midpoint) is a balance point.** Price gravitates to it; expect pause or reversal.
- **Relative equal highs/lows are key support/resistance.** Breaks confirm directional intent.
- **Retracement rules:** Once you leave the lower portion of a suspension block on downside, any retracement back up must stay below the lower quadrant (or at max, touch but not close above).
- **Daily suspension block spans entire intraday range.** Use low as deepest target, midpoint as balance, high as upper boundary.
- **70% rule still applies to opening range gaps.** Consequent encroachment is filled in first hour 70% of time.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Setup:** Identify daily suspension block, quadrants, octants, and event horizon at the open.
- **Tape reading:** For each candlestick that retraces back up, ask: "Do the bodies touch the lower quadrant level?" If no → bearish → expect lower leg. If yes → weakness → prepare for reversal up.
- **Confirmation layers:** Stack bodies-don't-touch observation with other signals (wicks, CPI lows, relative equal lows) to build conviction.
- **Scaling out:** If shorting and price hits lower quadrant without body touching it, take 1/3 profit. If hits event horizon, take another 1/3. Hold final 1/3 to the low of the daily suspension block.
- **Risk management:** Use closes above lower quadrant as stop-loss signal. If bodies finally breach it, exit short.
- **Patience:** This methodology rewards waiting for the signature (bodies refusing to touch). Do not chase if the signature isn't there.

## Glossary candidates

`DAILY SUSPENSION BLOCK` — The largest volume imbalance on the daily chart; bracketed by high and low; guides all intraday price action.

`LOWER QUADRANT` — The bottom 25% (0–25%) of a suspension block range; acts as support in downtrends, resistance in uptrends.

`UPPER QUADRANT` — The top 25% (75–100%) of a suspension block range; acts as resistance in downtrends, support in uptrends.

`EVENT HORIZON` — The exact 50% midpoint of a range or suspension block; a balance point where price often pauses or reverses.

`BODIES CANNOT TOUCH` — Rule stating that during downtrends, bodies will not close above key levels; wicks may tag them, but bodies refuse. This is an algorithmic signature.

`DISCOUNT SENSITIVITY` — Bodies in upper half of a PD array, showing bullish bias and willingness to hold above mid-range.

`PREMIUM SENSITIVITY` — Bodies in lower half of a PD array during a bounce, showing lack of bullishness and likelihood of continued downside.

`RELATIVE EQUAL HIGHS/LOWS` — Two swing highs or lows at similar price levels; act as resistance/support and breakout points.

`ALGORITHMIC PRECISION` — The market's pre-planned, rule-based behavior; bodies don't touch levels by coincidence but by algorithmic design.

`OCTANT` — One-eighth division of a range; used for fine-tuning target and support/resistance levels within a suspension block.
