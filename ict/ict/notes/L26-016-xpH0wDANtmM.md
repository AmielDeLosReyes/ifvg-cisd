## Concepts

### New Week Opening Gap (High Resistance Liquidity Run)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The gap between Friday's RTH close and Monday's RTH open (similar to daily RTH gap, but spans the weekend). This gap is a significant liquidity magnet. On NFP week Thursdays (high volatility days), price often runs from intraday highs down to this new week gap low, targeting stop-losses above the gap high. This is called a "high resistance liquidity run" because price must overcome significant resistance (prior highs, relative equal highs) to reach the target.
- **How to identify:** Mark Friday's RTH close; mark Monday's RTH open; the void between is the new week opening gap.
- **How to use:** Use the low of this gap as a short target; use the high of this gap as stop-loss placement (just above it) for shorts.
- **Example in this video:** Monday's gap was targeted for a short entry; price ran from intraday highs down to this gap low throughout the day.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** NFP week Thursdays see aggressive liquidity runs; use caution (position sizing, early profit-taking).

### Quadrant Level (1/4 Gap Division)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Divide any gap (new week, daily RTH, etc.) into 4 equal parts using Fibonacci. The quadrant level (1/4 or 3/4 point) acts as short-term friction. For shorts below a gap, the upper quadrant is a retracement resistance; price often wicks to this level and reverses back down. This is a "kiss level" — price touches and respects it.
- **How to identify:** Identify gap high and low; measure distance; divide by 4; mark the 1/4 and 3/4 points.
- **How to use:** Shorts: expect retracement into upper quadrant on wicks; don't panic when price spikes there. Longs: use upper quadrant as breakout resistance confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** Quadrant level of the new week gap was identified; price spiked to this level, wicked, and reversed lower (confirmed weakness).
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:29](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=509) "quadrant level," [0:09:17](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=557) "Spiked up to that quadrant level. And look at the reaction."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Quadrant is a friction level, not a reversal guarantee; context (bodies, other support) must confirm.

### Octant Level (1/8 Gap Division)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Divide any gap into 8 equal parts using Fibonacci. Octant levels (1/8, 2/8, 3/8, etc.) are micro support/resistance points. An octant level is more precise than a quadrant for identifying weakness (bodies failing to close below an octant = weakness; bodies below = strength). Often used as "key gradient level" (especially 2/8 octant of major gaps).
- **How to identify:** Gap high and low; divide by 8; mark all 8 points.
- **How to use:** Shorts: bodies must close below target octant to confirm continuation. Failure = reversal. Longs: bodies closing above octant = strength.
- **Example in this video:** Octant level at 29,291.25; bodies closing below this level confirmed the short thesis; failure to reach this level would have signaled weakness.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:06](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=606) "octant level," [0:22:41](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1361) "I'm watching every little detail."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** More granular than quadrant; requires precise charting.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (Short Entry Mechanics)
- **Kind:** entry model (for shorts)
- **What ICT says:** When shorting from a high, identify an inefficiency (gap or volume imbalance) just below the entry point. This inefficiency becomes the "inversion FVG" for shorts — the equivalent of a long entry into an iFVG. Shorts should see the inversion FVG close below its low (bodies not floating in upper half) to confirm the move down.
- **How to identify:** Identify high; scan downward for the first gap or imbalance (low close, high open on next candle).
- **How to use:** Short entry should be at or above the inversion FVG; stop loss above it; target below; confirm by watching bodies stay in lower half of the gap.
- **Example in this video:** Inversion FVG identified at ~29,292–29,250 range; bodies needed to close below this range to confirm the short; when they did, continuation was confirmed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:48](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=108) "I want to see this little gap in here behave like an inversion fair value gap," [0:02:51](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=171) "weakness."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Opposite of long iFVG entry; bodies must stay below the gap (not above) for confirmation.

### Market Maker Sell Model (Three Stages)
- **Kind:** definition (advanced)
- **What ICT says:** A market maker sell event typically unfolds in three distinct stages:
  1. **First Stage Distribution** — Institutional selling begins; price moves down steadily.
  2. **Second Stage Redistribution** — Main sell-off; most animated, fastest price movement; longest duration.
  3. **Third Stage Accumulation** (implied) — Sellers have left; price consolidates near lows before resuming or reversing.
  The second stage is the most violent; stop-losses are typically placed at the boundary between stage 1 and stage 2 to preserve capital if the model fails.
- **How to identify:** Track candlestick patterns and volume/bodies; early stage = steady decline + retracements touching prior highs; second stage = aggressive decline with minimal retracement; final stage = consolidation near lows.
- **How to use:** Identify which stage you're in; in stage 2, expect minimal pullback — hold position. In stage 1, be ready to add on weakness. Exit at the end of stage 2 or early stage 3.
- **Example in this video:** ICT identified transitioning from first stage distribution (early sell-off) to second stage redistribution (main acceleration); he did NOT lower stops during second stage to let price breathe and find larger move.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:57](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1017) "first stage distribution," [0:16:57](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1017) "second stage redistribution," [0:20:07](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1207) "second stage redistribution, which means it's going to be the biggest, the most animated portion."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not to be confused with Wyckoff distribution; ICT's model is his own specific categorization.

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG) as Daily Reference
- **Kind:** definition (context-specific)
- **What ICT says:** Each day, the FPFVG (first gap after 9:30 AM) serves as a daily reference level. If that gap forms on a 1-minute scale, the midpoint and boundaries of that gap (especially its consequent encroachment) act as support/resistance throughout the day. On multi-day reviews, prior days' FPFVGs are extended and monitored.
- **How to identify:** Mark the first gap that forms after 9:30 AM on the 1-minute chart for each day; extend those levels forward to future days.
- **How to use:** Use FPFVG boundaries to confirm or negate short/long thesis. Bodies respecting these levels = confirmation; bodies breaking = shift in bias.
- **Example in this video:** Wednesday's FPFVG, Tuesday's FPFVG, and Monday's FPFVG were all extended and monitored; price interactions with their boundaries confirmed the short bias throughout the multi-day sequence.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:29](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=509) "Tuesday of August 25th first present fair gap," [0:10:56](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=656) "Wednesday of last week, August 26th. First presented fair value gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Multiple days of FPFVGs create a framework; each one is a potential inflection point.

### Daily Volume Imbalance (as Support/Resistance)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** On the daily chart, gaps (high to low over daily candle) represent imbalances. These imbalances attract intraday price; they act as magnets. An imbalance low acts as support; an imbalance high acts as resistance. When price reaches a daily chart imbalance zone intraday, expect a reaction or consolidation.
- **How to identify:** Look at daily chart; identify gaps (wicks not filling the full range between open and close on consecutive days).
- **How to use:** Intraday, when price approaches a daily imbalance zone, anticipate friction or a bounce (even if overall trend is down, expect intraday hiccup).
- **Example in this video:** A daily chart volume imbalance (shaded blue area) was identified from Wednesday; when intraday price approached this zone, it reacted (bounced) before continuing lower.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Daily imbalances override intraday moves in terms of friction; they slow down intraday price.

### Body Placement as Order Flow Confirmation
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** For shorts, bodies MUST close below target levels (FPFVG low, octant level, etc.). If bodies close above or in the upper half of these levels despite price wicking below, it signals weakness in the downside move (indecision, potential reversal). Bodies in lower half = strength (institutional conviction). No bodies = best case (pure continuation).
- **How to identify:** After each candlestick close, note where the body sits relative to key level. Upper half = weakness; lower half = strength.
- **How to use:** Shorts: bodies in lower half = continue holding; bodies in upper half = start thinking of exits. Longs: opposite.
- **Example in this video:** Bodies consistently closed in lower half of FPFVG ranges, confirming the short thesis throughout the move. One retracement where bodies floated in upper half = warning signal, but ICT understood it was a stage-1 event, not a reversal.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:22](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=802) "we don't want to see any bodies in the upper half," [0:18:31](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1111) "That's bearish."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Bodies are the most precise order flow indicator; use them above everything else.

### NFP Week Trading Caution (High Manipulation)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Non-farm payroll (NFP) weeks — especially Thursday and Friday post-release, and Wednesday post-11 AM ET — see high market manipulation from dealers and real market makers. Manual intervention is heavy. As an educator, ICT recommends new traders avoid these days entirely. Experienced traders can trade NFP days, but should expect adversity (whipsaws, stop-runs). Position sizing should be reduced 50–80%; take early partials rather than holding for full moves.
- **How to identify:** Check the economic calendar for NFP release dates (typically first Friday of each month); treat that week (Wed–Fri) as high-risk.
- **How to use:** New traders: don't trade. Experienced: reduce size, take partials early, expect adversity, don't be shocked by stopped-outs.

- **How to use:** Use narrative as contrarian filter; if retail is bullish but chart is set up for shorts, shorts are likely the smart move.
