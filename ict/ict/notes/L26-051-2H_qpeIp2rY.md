## Concepts

### 1-Hour / Daily Buy-Side Imbalance Sell-Side Inefficiency (BISI) from Prior Sessions
- **Kind:** observation + rule
- **What ICT says:** A gap from one session (e.g., Wednesday noon, 1-hour timeframe) that shows buy-side imbalance (buyers moved price higher, leaving an unfilled gap below) persists across multiple sessions. When price returns to test that gap overnight or the next day, it's a key reference level. If price breaks below it (fails to hold above), it's bearish confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** Wednesday's 1-hour high (noon ET) to the next candlestick shows a BISI. This gap is tested overnight and early the next day. Because price fails to hold above it and breaks lower, the BISI becomes a resistance zone, signaling bearish bias.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** BISI from longer timeframes (1-hour, daily) carry more weight than intraday gaps.

### Premium Array Failure as Bearish Signal
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** (Critical insight) If a bullish PD array (an up-close gap, a BISI that should support price if bullish) fails to be traded to—meaning price never even touches its midpoint (CE) and certainly never closes in its upper half—that's extremely bearish. It shows the market has no interest in supporting the bullish bias. A premium array that can't even be traded to is a red flag to flip to short.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify a bullish gap or up-close PDA, (2) Watch if price tests it, (3) If price breaks away before touching the midpoint, that's failure, (4) If it never even comes back to touch it again, that's extreme failure.
- **How to use:** Reversal entry. When a premium array fails this way, it's a high-probability short setup.
- **Example in this video:** A bullish fair value gap is visible on the chart. ICT expects it to be traded to on any bounce (bullish expectation). Price barely probes the top of the gap but never trades into the midpoint. Instead, it rolls over and breaks below. This failure signals that the bullish scenario is dead; flip to short.

### Breaker Block (Specific Candlestick Pattern)
- **Kind:** definition + refinement
- **What ICT says:** A breaker block is formed by three consecutive candlesticks: (1) a high, (2) a lower low, (3) a higher high than (1), but (3) closes as a down-close with the lowest close of the three. The breaker block is formed by this bottom candlestick. When used correctly (anchored to the lowest close), it represents rejection of the bullish phase. It can be misidentified if you use two consecutive candles instead of all three + the correct anchor.
- **How to identify:** (1) Find a swing high, (2) followed by a lower low, (3) followed by a recovery high, (4) note the candle with the "lowest close" among the pattern, (5) use that candle's low as the breaker anchor.
- **How to use:** Stop-loss placement or resistance. If shorting from above a breaker block, the block's high is initial resistance; if price closes below the block's true low (the lowest-close candle's low), that's strong bearish confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** Pattern: high (candlestick 1) → lower low (candlestick 2) → higher high (candlestick 3, but closes down). The breaker is identified by the lowest close. If you use the wrong candles, you'll misidentify the breaker level, which is why ICT emphasizes the correct selection.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:18](https://youtu.ye/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=678) — identifying the breaker block pattern; [0:12:06](https://youtu.ye/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=726) — explaining correct vs. incorrect interpretation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Breaker blocks require precise candlestick selection; getting it wrong ruins the level.

### First Utilization vs. First Presentation
- **Kind:** definition refinement
- **What ICT says:** "First presentation" = the time a gap forms (e.g., 9:31 ET). "First utilization" = how the gap functions based on the candlestick that created it (e.g., up-close = bullish FVG, down-close = bearish FVG). A gap's first utilization tells you its original intended function. If the market is bearish, you apply the inversion aspect, not the first utilization.
- **Example in this video:** A candlestick creates a BISI at 9:31 (first presentation). Its first utilization is bullish (up-close). But if the market is bearish, ICT treats it as a potential inversion from the start, not waiting for price to break below and then flip it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:54](https://youtu.ye/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=534) — distinguishing first presentation and first utilization
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This distinction is semantic but important for understanding candle analysis.

## Glossary candidates
`Premium array failure` — a bullish PD array (BISI, up-close gap) that cannot be traded to (price never reaches its midpoint) and certainly not bodies in upper half. Indicates bearish rejection and is a strong short signal.

`Breaker block` — three consecutive candlesticks: high → lower low → higher high with down-close. The candle with the "lowest close" of the three is the breaker anchor. Indicates rejection of bullish phase.

`First utilization vs. first presentation` — first presentation is the time a gap forms (e.g., 9:31 ET); first utilization is how the gap functions based on the candle that created it (up-close = bullish, down-close = bearish).

`High resistance liquidity run condition` — when price is near multiple buy-side liquidity pools (REH zones, prior highs, etc.), upside is harder to achieve; shorts are favored.

`Event horizon` — the 0.5 Fibonacci midpoint between an entry and a primary target, used as first partial-taking level in a multi-partial exit plan.

`1-hour BISI from prior session` — a buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency from a 1-hour or longer timeframe that persists across sessions, acting as resistance. Failure to hold it is bearish signal.
