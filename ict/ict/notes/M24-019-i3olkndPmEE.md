## Concepts

### Previous days' highs and lows as liquidity / algorithmic reference points  (aliases: old highs/lows, last three days' highs and lows, PD array, draw on liquidity)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** Note the high and low of each of the last three days on your chart. Even if a later day has already traded through the stops above/below one, the algorithm still refers back to that same price. The algorithm does not know how many orders are resting there and does not need to; the reference point is defined by time (the daily interval's highest high / lowest low). Large, deep-pocket institutional participants keep buying/selling interest around those levels, and coded logic assumes resting stops (trailing stops of trend followers sit just under higher lows / over lower highs). So price keeps being drawn back to old levels and market participants can trade the fluctuation right after the level is touched. "One and done" is retail logic.
- **How to identify:** Mark the daily high/low of each of the last ~3 sessions as horizontal lines (he marks them on both charts so the same price shows on the 1m and 15s).
- **How to use:** Use them as draws on liquidity (targets) and as catalysts for turtle soup: e.g. a break above a previous high while bearish is a place to look to short, even on a daily chart for a position trader. Same logic works on daily/weekly/monthly charts; you just wait much longer for it to play out. "If you note the previous days highs and lows you're never going to run out of setups."
- **Example in this video:** NQ on Wed Aug 28: the area around Tuesday's (Aug 27) daily low is the focus. Morning: price sells down into and below Tuesday's low, makes an intraday short-term low, retraces, then dives lower again. Afternoon: after the sell-off to a lower low of the day, price is drawn back up toward Tuesday's low again (the "fishing" target at the end).
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:21](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=501) — why old highs/lows still matter; [0:12:19](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=739) — algorithm only needs the time-based reference; [0:13:56](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=836) — use as DOL, turtle soup catalyst, higher-timeframe use.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The area just above and just below a previous day low is "rich in order flow" and can be "mined" repeatedly, but only if you know what you are looking for ([0:39:55](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2395)).

### Taking partials when targeting a previous day's low  (aliases: partial profits, graduated approach to holding runners)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** When you are inside the range and reaching down for an old daily low (previous day's low), do not take your partial the first time price pierces it. Let it make a short-term low below the old low, retrace, and dive again; take the partial beneath that low. If you take profit just under the old low you may sell yourself short of a large run, because you cannot know how far the first drop goes. It teaches patience and holding.
- **How to identify:** Distinguish the type of low you are holding for: (a) a previous day's low, vs (b) an intraday low (a low formed inside the range between the day's highest high and lowest low). He says he does not like applying the wait-for-the-second-leg method to intraday lows (caption unclear on exact wording).
- **How to use:** A graduated plan for those who cannot yet hold runners: Stage 1 — if price breaks the same-day low, measure the day's high down to that low and project one quarter (one quadrant) extension below the low; getting out around there is a reasonable first partial ([0:26:09](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1569), [0:31:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1863)). Stage 2 — wait for price to make the short-term low below the target, then use the dynamic trailed partial (next concept). He says it is fine to simply exit below the low while you are learning, then watch whether the run materialises and grow into the trail.
- **Example in this video:** Price runs down through Tuesday's low, pops back above it with a wick, then dives again; the second leg is where the partial belongs. He also notes the "low of the day" keeps being reset: first low, then a lower one, then the current low of day, and attention shifts to each new one.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:59](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1079) — don't partial on first pierce; [0:26:09](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1569) — range + 1 quadrant extension for same-day low; [0:31:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1863) — stage 1 then stage 2.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as "a graduate perspective" so students do not force themselves into holding like he does and feel "like a deer in headlights". The one-quadrant extension references earlier teaching (range from high to low, quadrant projections).

### Dynamic trailed partial (trail stop on part of the position)  (aliases: dynamic trailing stop, trail buy stop on one contract, runner management)
- **Kind:** entry/exit model (trade management) — new emphasis
- **What ICT says:** Instead of a limit exit at a guessed level, let the market decide your partial. Example: short 2 contracts. After price breaks below the target low and builds a short-term low, every down-closed candle that creates an imbalance gets a buy stop for ONE contract placed just above that candle's high; as lower candles print, lower the stop to the previous candle's high. When it triggers you take that partial and keep the other contract (core position) running. It adds no risk, no extra leverage, and no new position; you are only managing a portion of the size.
- **How to identify / conditions:** Use it only on the 1-minute chart — "never never" on sub-one-minute charts, which are spiky/fuzzy with overlapping candles. Start trailing only after price has broken below the target low and is "spooling" (running). Stop goes above the previous 1m candle's high, ultra tight.
- **How to use:** Works for a partial or for the entire position (e.g. two contracts, trail all of it). Alternative of trailing from larger/structural candle highs gives back too much range intraday; he considers it better for swing trades held over a week (reading slightly uncertain from captions). Accept that you will be stopped out very close to the low; do not berate yourself for exits that are "not good enough". Keep trailing the remainder lower after each new lower low; do not be involved in retracements back into the old low. Averaging matters if you pyramided: your average entry moves as you add, and adding too much early can put you below the waterline (unrealised red) for a while.
- **Example in this video:** On the 1m NQ chart, price breaks Tuesday's low, makes a short-term low, retraces, then drops again; trailing the one-contract buy stop above each successive 1m down-candle high gets stopped out only a candle or two off the swing low. He cites a move quoted as "278 and a half" to "302 and 3/4" (digits of the full NQ price are unclear in the caption), implying a large capture compared with exiting beneath the old low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:22](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1222) — mechanism for 1 of 2 contracts; [0:52:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3123) — 1-minute only, never sub-minute; [0:53:39](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3219) — ultra tight trail, accept being knocked out near the low; [0:56:50](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3410) — objective, mechanical, "all you're doing is dropping your stop to the previous candle's high".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Answers repeated viewer questions about how to take partials and hold runs. He also says the same dynamic trail can be used on the whole trade. His recorded trade videos show stops far from entry because he is "not rushing to strangle the position."

### Intraday highs/lows tracking and post-session review  (aliases: low/high of day, same-day low, daily range expansion)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Keep track of the highest high and lowest low since the 9:30 open. If bearish, note each successive lower high / lower low as it forms; the current low of day is what you watch (older lows stop being the low of the day). It is a dynamic study of whether the daily range keeps expanding or caps. Study your charts after the close (5:00 PM or later) and list everything that was in play and the catalysts.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:25](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2005) — intraday highs/lows since 9:30; [0:34:15](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2055) — low of day shifts; study after 5 PM.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He ties this to how price "dives" through each successive low once structure has shifted.

### Fake bull/bear flag at relative equal highs  (aliases: stop hunt then consolidation; fake bull flag)
- **Kind:** definition / identification
- **What ICT says:** Relative equal highs followed by a retracement/consolidation that holds above them looks like a bull flag/breakout to the unlearned (trend lines, waiting for a breakout "that never comes"). When the market is predisposed lower, that is a fake bull flag: the stop hunt above the highs followed by time spent there, then displacement down. Taught in the 2016/2017 mentorship (fake bull flags and bear flags).
- **How to identify:** A stop run above equal highs, then price lingers (spends time) inside inefficiencies or above the highs instead of leaving fast. Companion rule: you do not want price to stay in an inefficiency; you want it to spend little time and leave; for a bearish premium array you want the upper half not filled, and ideally not even traded to.
- **Example in this video:** NQ morning: highs relatively equal, price bumps them and spends time, then displaces down, entering several models at once.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:02](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2102) — fake bull flag logic; [0:36:40](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2200) — what the unlearned see.

### Stacking models and pyramiding  (aliases: OTE, 2022 Mentorship model, Silver Bullet, institutional order entry drill)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** In the same morning drop he points out several valid entries: optimal trade entry on the high-to-low swing (his "flagship pattern" for about 10 years), the 2022 model, a Silver Bullet, and an institutional order entry drill ("lowest hanging fruit"). All are profitable and can be used together: enter on a higher one, and if a lower setup appears you can pyramid and build the position bigger. ### Heaviness, speed and distance (grading the price swing)  (aliases: heavy vs light price, acceleration into liquidity, midpoint rule)
- **Kind:** rule / confirmation
- **What ICT says:** Grade how price trades once it breaks a low. If it is lethargic, it may reverse; if it becomes quick and heavy, jump to the next pool of liquidity — a lower low should be "not even a speedbump". Heavy (bearish) = a series of candles making lower highs, struggling to make higher candle highs; light price climbs easily toward premium. Before the target low, expect acceleration: the magnitude of the move should increase as price approaches the old low, to "get to it and through it" and drive past it, not stall and consolidate. He annotates "speed and distance" / large down-closed candles in his executions.
- **How to identify / rule of thumb:** Take the range from the high to the low you are targeting; once price trades below the 50% point of that range it should move fast, each candle lower than the previous. He admits there is no fixed number of points (an example was about 25 handles on NQ) — it is relative to the range and a "knack" thing.
- **How to use:** Be patient and hold; long stops sit under the lows and the stubborn ones will be wiped when price accelerates through. Retail often thinks the first drop is the low and will do harmonic/ratio trades there and exit badly.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:44](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2444) — grading speed and acceleration into lows; [0:46:23](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2783) — halfway-point rule; [0:48:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2883) — heavy vs light.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "We're not trading support and resistance — we're attacking people that use support and resistance for their liquidity." Narrative blends time of day, day of week, week, and monthly chart ([0:49:40](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2980)).

### Breakaway gap  (aliases: breakaway FVG)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A breakaway gap forms from a consolidation, at the start of the first or second stage of distribution or reaccumulation (market maker sell model: smart money reversal, low-risk sell, area of redistribution). A gap left behind by the run through the first low after a shift in structure that stays open signals that price will go much lower and fast, so "don't be in a rush to get out". It can be closed and price can still drop, but unfilled is better — "a cherry on top", a hallmark, not a requirement.
- **Example in this video:** (1) After the shift in structure, price breaks the post-MSS low and leaves a small gap that remains open; the drop continues lower highs ("heaviness"). (2) The 9:31-10:00 first FVG left open (partly) as the sell-off proceeds. (3) At the 2:50 macro on the 15s chart, an inefficiency formed by a candle's high and a later low acts as a breakaway gap for the rally.
- **Timestamps:** [0:43:58](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2638) — definition and market maker sell model; [1:15:23](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4523) — breakaway gap after first-presentation FVG; [1:22:50](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4970) — 15s version at 2:50.

### Opening range gap (RTH opening gap, ORG)  (aliases: opening range gap, RTH gap, premium/discount gap)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** In regular trading hours view, the ORG is the space between the previous day's RTH settlement (the 4:14/4:15 PM ET close shown on the chart) and the 9:30 AM opening price. If the open is lower than the settle it is a discount gap (he colours it blue; premium gap would be the opposite). He expects price to retrace at least half of the gap within the first 30 minutes of trading, with a roughly 70% chance. Annotate it by drawing a rectangle from the opening price to the settlement price. For a discount gap, the expectation is buy-side delivery (rally) back toward the settle price; minimum 50% in 30 minutes.
- **How to identify:** Toggle RTH on; the 9:30 candle gaps from the prior 4:14 candle. In electronic (24-hour) trading that 9:30 candle has no gap, so the gap does not exist there.
- **How to use:** Treat the ORG's low (the 9:30 opening price) as a magnet/reference. In this video the ORG low coincided with NDOG levels and acted as the target for a sell-off after price ran up to the first FVG.
- **Example in this video:** Wed Aug 28: opens below the 4:14 PM Tuesday settlement (discount gap). Price rallies to take half the gap and the full gap (also overlapping the 6 PM NDOG's two-tick gap), sweeps buy-side, then reverses.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:07](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3607) — ORG is not an FVG; [1:06:30](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3990) — RTH view, 4:14 settle vs 9:30 open; [1:07:21](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4041) — 70% chance half in 30 minutes.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He is re-teaching because students rushed. The new day opening gap (6:00 PM ET open vs 5:00 PM close) here was only about two ticks, overlapping half of the ORG.

### First presentation of a fair value gap between 9:30 and 10:00  (aliases: first FVG, first presented FVG, 9:31-10:00 FVG)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:** The 9:30 candle cannot be counted as part of an FVG because it is the opening range gap candle. The earliest a legitimate FVG can form is with the 9:31 candle as the imbalance (middle) candle, confirmed after 9:32. Look for the first FVG formed after 9:30; the 9:30-based gap is not it. Once found, price returning into it and rejecting it (not closing above the upper half of a bearish FVG) is a high-quality entry; place the stop one tick above the high of the wicking candle (high plus one tick). He says if a trade is missed you can short a return into the inversion FVG using the same stop idea.
- **How to identify:** Step through 1m candles from 9:31: a candle that overlaps the previous candle's low (for a bearish FVG) is not a gap; the first true three-candle gap defines it. Count candles carefully (caption unclear on exactly which candle he calls the "third").
- **How to use:** Narrative: liquidity taken (Judas swing), a shift in structure, then a return up into the first FVG; short from there targeting lower levels. Should not close the upper half. Better if the gap left behind by the displacement stays open (breakaway gap).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m Aug 28: no FVG until 9:35. At about 9:35 price runs the minor BSL (relative equal highs) in a Judas swing and the first FVG forms on the 9:35 candle. Price breaks down under the 8:15 inversion FVG, then wicks back up into the first-presentation FVG and the bottom of the ORG, closes without taking the upper half, then sells hard to Tuesday's low and below the Aug 16 and Aug 27 NDOGs.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:54](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3654) — earliest possible FVG is 9:31; [1:03:20](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3800) — the day's first FVG forms at 9:35; [1:12:07](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4327) — check it does not close the upper half; [1:14:34](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4474) — keep screenshots; first FVG used and hammered.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He stresses this was in response to many comments and that it is not new. Journaling many examples is the route to seeing it live.

### Inversion fair value gap (iFVG) with minor buy-side liquidity  (aliases: IFVG, inversion FVG, reclaimed FVG (caption unclear))
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Before the open (shared in Tanya Trades' live chat with time stamps), he flagged the 8:15 candle as an inversion FVG and a minor buy-side liquidity pool at the relative equal highs. Price rallied from the iFVG, hit the BSL ("if you had something there and partialed off you have a free look"). Later, when price broke lower beneath the 8:15 iFVG, that gap flips and should be used as resistance; the original bullish FVG reading is "not the right time to take that trade". Not buying it just because it looks like a bullish gap: wait for it to trade below and see whether the first FVG shuts off higher prices.
- **How to use:** Once price trades down through the gap, selling a return into its midpoint/inside it works; stop above its high (plus a tick). He wants it to trade into the iFVG and fail.
- **Timestamps:** [0:58:28](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3508) — 8:15 iFVG and minor BSL; [1:04:08](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3848) — not trying to buy it, waiting; [1:09:43](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4183) — original bullish vs inverted role.

### 2:50 PM macro and 15-second chart delivery  (aliases: 2:50 algorithm, macro, volume imbalance, CISD, order block mean threshold, institutional order entry drill)
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The 2:50 macro runs until 3:10 PM. At its start price needs to be delivering. On the 15s chart he reads: a down-closed candle's order block (mean threshold is the most sensitive point; price should not trade far below it); a close above the open of a down-closed candle = change in state of delivery (CISD); a volume imbalance on the 15s is all the algorithm needs to begin a rally; delivery cycles through lower timeframes seeking any FVG or volume imbalance. With no FVG and no swing low left (the sell-side stops already taken), price rallies into the nearest inefficiency. Institutional order entry drill: price trades into a gap formed by a candle's high, leaving the maximum range open, then runs — that signals a breakaway gap. At 2:50 on the dot price rallies into the low of an inefficiency, stalls and consolidates (accumulation), rallies to its consequent encroachment, stops just short, retests the volume imbalance, then trades through the high end. A bullish order block: only the wick should enter the upper half; bodies hold. Then price should pierce the volume imbalance on the 1m chart quickly.
- **How to use:** Manage the dealing range properly (the day's high to low); do not just say "it goes to Tuesday's low". Target (Terminus) for the move was Tuesday's low, with an intermediate target as a partial.
- **Example in this video:** NQ afternoon: 15s and 1m show price rallying from the 2:50 candle through successive inefficiencies, respecting the 50% of wicks, order block upper half, to just short of Tuesday's low, which it then tags.
- **Timestamps:** [1:18:47](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4727) — inside the 2:50 algorithm; [1:21:13](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4873) — mean threshold / volume imbalance; [1:23:40](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=5020) — institutional order entry drill and ride; [1:26:07](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=5167) — order block upper half and delivery.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Terminus" = the target/end of the run (as he uses it). Fishing analogy: casting at the 2:50 candle with the previous day low as the target, line spooling = the run ([1:27:46](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=5266)).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Mark the last three days' high and low; they stay valid even after being traded through.
- Do not partial on the first break of an old daily low; partial beneath the lower low made after the retracement.
- Same-day low broken: day's high-to-low range plus one quarter (quadrant) extension is a reasonable first partial.
- Dynamic trail: 1-minute chart only (never sub-1m); buy stop above the high of each down-closed imbalance candle (for a short), for just the partial size, tightened to the previous candle's high each time.
- Heavy price = consecutive lower highs/lower candle highs; once price is below 50% of the high-to-low range toward the target low, expect acceleration into the low.
- ORG: discount/premium; at least 50% of the gap retraced in the first 30 minutes with roughly 70% probability; RTH view needed to see it.
- First FVG after the open can form no earlier than 9:31 (9:30 candle excluded); watch 9:30-10:00 for first presentation; it should not close the upper half of a bearish one. Stop: one tick above the high.
- 2:50 PM macro runs to 3:10 PM (about 10 minutes after 3:00).
- NQ delivery months: March, June, September, December; expiry third Friday; roll after expiry.
- Review charts after the close (5:00 PM or later).

## Glossary candidates
`ORG (opening range gap)` — RTH gap between prior day 4:14/4:15 PM settlement and the 9:30 AM open.
`First presentation FVG` — the first valid FVG after 9:30 (earliest 9:31 middle candle) used for the session's narrative.
`Dynamic trailed partial` — a partial exit by trailing a buy/sell stop above/below each 1-minute candle on part of the position.
`Heaviness` — price making consecutive lower highs, struggling upward; bearish signature.
`Breakaway gap` — gap left behind a displacement from a consolidation that stays open, signalling continuation.
`Institutional order entry drill` — price trades just into a gap formed by a candle's high, leaving the maximum of the range unfilled, then runs.
`Terminus` — the intended target/end point of a run.
`Spooling` — price starting to run steadily, like line off a reel.
`Mean threshold` — the 50% (most sensitive) point of a candle/order block.
