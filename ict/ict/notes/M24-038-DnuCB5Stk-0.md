## Summary
ICT introduces "volatility pinball", a demo-account drill for learning to enter with limit orders and desensitising yourself to pulling the trigger. You take one-contract trades on a 15-second or 1-minute chart, alternate long and short, enter on every FVG that forms (or whichever PD array you are practising), target the nearest old high/low or inefficiency, and always use a fixed stop and a fixed target. Before the drills he reviews his Asian-session NQ long from the night before, which gives the richest concept content of the video. That review covers a stop run through relative equal lows into a real gap, a daily-chart "dual volume imbalance" graded with quadrants, an order block with a volume imbalance on top (which he calls the "Indigo entry model"), a body-based redefinition of volume imbalance, and breakaway versus measuring gaps. He stresses repeatedly that the drills are not a trading model, not Enigma, and not signals. The drill portion also contains useful side lessons on partials, consequent encroachment of wicks, and inversion FVG stop placement with stacked gaps.

## Concepts

### Volatility Pinball drill  (aliases ICT uses: "price action drill", "limit order drill", "pinball")
- **Kind:** new coinage (practice method, not an entry model)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A demo-account exercise to learn limit-order entries and to desensitise yourself to entering (and to selling short). He says he built it as a game in the 1990s, writing down hypothetical entry, stop and target on paper, using the candle's open as the hypothetical fill (e.g. for a bearish order block).
  - Chart: 15-second chart (more opportunity), 1-minute as the highest timeframe. Occasionally a 5-minute chart if it works there. No higher-timeframe bias or draw-on-liquidity is framed for the drill.
  - Size: exactly ONE contract. No two contracts, no pyramiding (he will teach pyramiding later for his son).
  - Orders: all entries are limit orders; stop and take-profit are placed at entry. You may not move or cancel the stop. The trade ends either at the stop or at the limit target, whichever trips first, and you wait before the next one. Market orders are not allowed in the drill. No partials in the platform, though you annotate hypothetical partials (see below).
  - Reason to enter: each time a fair value gap forms (for his son's practice) you engage it, aiming at the nearest pool of liquidity (old high/low, relative equal highs/lows, short-term highs/lows) or inefficiency (an FVG, the NWOG/NDOG). He says you can swap in order blocks or other PD arrays. Do not demand that it be "the right one".
  - Alternate buying and selling (he considered flipping a coin) and deliberately include counter-trend drills so you feel what that is like. Range-bound days are better for drilling than strongly directional days.
  - Volume: minimum 10, ideally 15–20 per day, which he says takes about an hour to an hour and a half. Do it in London, NY, the lunch hour and PM if you can. Do it for weeks, up to a few months; progress is visible in a couple of weeks and notable by 2–3 months.
  - Journal every drill (see Process). Over time your eye begins to jump to setups you have seen repeat in the drills, and you start to see setups you never noticed.
  - It is explicitly not Enigma, not a model, not a signal service, and he says you will lose if you copy it with a real or funded account.
- **How to identify:** n/a (practice method). A drill entry = a PD array that has just formed + a stop framed at the level that would prove you wrong + a target at the nearest liquidity or inefficiency.
- **How to use:** Frame stop and target first (entry is secondary; see Limit order mechanics). Log entry time, time to stop or target, how much unrealized profit it gave, and your emotional state. Review at the end of each weekend and note which drills lined up best with what delivered.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-second chart, 10 drills after the 9:30 ET open, net about +$3,575 with 6 of the 10 losing (ICT's own count). Walk-through of the first few:

  - Later: the first FVG of the day at 9:31 on the 15s chart delivered quickly down to the 15-minute relative equal lows, which sat on September 23rd's weekly low. A sell at the inversion FVG (see below) and a final buy on a FVG aimed at relative equal highs closed the set at 10 drills.
  - The target-based exits (take profit) were all limit orders. The earlier market-order buttons were used only to show it was not Market Replay.

### Limit order mechanics (buy limit / sell limit, stop, target)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Selling short: place a SELL limit ABOVE market price; the market has to rise to your price. Buying: place a BUY limit BELOW market price; the market has to trade down to it.
  - Fill: the market must trade at least ONE TICK through your limit price to be sure of a fill. Just touching it may not fill you, and larger size may need more.
  - Frame the trade in this order: (1) the draw (where you think price goes), (2) the stop (where you are wrong), (3) the entry is the "secondary concern". For a bearish idea the entry is some premium array above market.
  - Example placement: for a short at a small gap, sell limit one contract just below the candle's high, stop dragged to the high, target one tick above the draw level.
  - A stop is mandatory every time ("your insurance policy"). It is the market telling you your idea was wrong and a "good time to be wrong"; do not treat it as an enemy.
  - The only permitted market order is to exit a trade you know you are offside on or that is about to turn on you.
  - Entering with a market order signals rushing, impulsiveness, emotion.
- **How to identify:** n/a
- **How to use:** Decide target (old high/low, inefficiency such as the NDOG) and stop first, then sit a limit at the PD array. Log how long a trade lasts, average profit, average risk; calculate these yourself manually rather than only trusting a platform.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s, near the open: market price around 20,241.5 (he says "241 a half"); he explains selling at a gap above and targeting the NDOG/inefficiency below.

### Partials in drills (annotated, hypothetical)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Because the drill uses one contract, you ANNOTATE where a partial would have been taken and screenshot it. This trains you to hunt for places to "fund the trade", remove risk and reward yourself.
  - Candidate partial levels he names: below each prior low as price makes new lows, each time price makes a low and retraces and then goes below it again (even by one tick); the consequent encroachment (50%) of a large wick as price trades down into it; the midpoint of gaps price trades through; below relative equal lows; after a partial you can roll the stop to cover costs.

### Volume imbalance — body-based definition and the "real" liquidity void
- **Kind:** refinement (he says he is correcting an insufficient description from the previous day's stream)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the separation between the BODIES (open/close) of consecutive candles, not the wicks. Wicks can still "do the damage" and trade through the area.
  - If a candle has no body (open = close, a doji-type), the algorithm ignores that candle entirely. The reference is the separation between the bodies of the candles on either side of it. Drawn by the wrong rule (using the open/close of the doji) the imbalance would look different.
  - A "real common gap" (his phrase; i.e. a true liquidity void) is where no trading took place between one candle's high and another candle's low. Only this is a liquidity void by definition; a body separation where wicks overlap is not.
  - Paint roller analogy: price delivering down and back up over an area offers both sell-side and buy-side delivery, so that area becomes "balanced". Wicks can still print outside the box.
  - A candle that closes with a volume imbalance above it (down-close candle, then price up) — when the next candle trades up, expect price to return into that imbalance as a discount array. He wants the imbalance to be left intact as price rallies.
- **How to identify:** Draw the rectangle between the body of candle A and the body of candle C, skipping any bodiless candle between. The imbalance is "still in its true form" even when wicks have crossed it.
- **How to use:** Mark the box from the bodies, expect price to return into it after it is created, and read wick pokes outside it as stop-running rather than a failure as long as the bodies keep resting on the box (see Indigo).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Asian session, about 8:10 PM ET. The body of a candle stops at 20,229.5, the very next candle's low is 20,229.5 "to the tick"; later a candle's low and the low of another are both 20,231.50. He uses these to show the algorithm references candle bodies, and that the wick did the damage while bodies rested on top of the box.
- **Timestamps:**

### Dual volume imbalance on the daily chart (unnamed PD array) and grading with quadrants
- **Kind:** definition / new coinage (he withholds the name)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Specific PD array on the daily chart (NQ December 2024): two volume imbalances stacked, one on each side of a middle candle. The middle candle's body sits inside the span between the two outer bodies. He says it must be a candle "surrounded by a volume imbalance on both sides".
  - Encompass the first imbalance to the second and draw the box out in time (the blue shaded rectangle).
  - He says he will not give the array's name because people will rush out books; the name will come once his books are in print.
  - "Grading": put a Fibonacci on the low-to-high of the box and add the quadrants: low, lower quadrant (25%), midpoint/equilibrium (50%), upper quadrant (75%), high. Then you anticipate PD arrays forming around the quadrant levels rather than reacting.
  - A price run into the upper quadrant of this box is the "qualifier" for entry: price should reach it, not necessarily the whole box.
- **How to identify:** Find a candle whose body is contained between two consecutive volume imbalances (one before, one after). Box from the low of the first imbalance to the high of the second; fib with quadrants. (Exact construction in captions is partly unclear.)
- **How to use:** Look for liquidity runs (stops below relative equal lows) that carry price into the upper quadrant, then take the order block there (see Indigo). As targets, the top of the box was reached on the rally.

### Indigo entry model  (also: "ICT's Indigo model entry")
- **Kind:** new coinage (entry pattern)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A purge of liquidity followed by an order block that trades into that liquidity, with a volume imbalance on top of it (for a bullish setup) or below it (bearish). Simply: "order block inside of liquidity with a volume imbalance on top".
  - There are two possible reasons for a long entry: price goes to liquidity (sell stops below relative equal lows), or it goes to a discount inefficiency such as a real gap. The main one is liquidity.
  - Filters: the sweep must go beyond the lows into a higher-timeframe reference (here the upper quadrant of the daily dual imbalance); the smooth lows / the NDOG low alone are too insignificant to buy at; do not buy early into "too smooth" levels.
  - He says it is "a combination of several factors" and more will come later; it is not fully taught.
- **How to identify:** (1) Run on stops (sell side for longs); (2) reaches a higher-timeframe level (quadrant); (3) a down-close candle forms (order block) with a volume imbalance above it; (4) price crosses that candle's open (validation).
- **How to use:** Enter anywhere in the order block (open to high or wick, "the watering hole"); add as price touches the top of the volume imbalance; stop below the sweep; target the minor buy-side pools then the buy-side level (20,287) and the top of the daily box.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Asian session, Monday 9/30 evening into 10/1 (around 8:00–8:10 PM ET). Price made relative equal highs and then dropped aggressively. Breakout buyers had bought above and had stops below the equal lows and a wick; below sat a real gap and the NDOG. Price drove below the equal lows into the real gap and into the upper quadrant of the daily dual imbalance. A down-close candle formed with a volume imbalance on top; ICT went long inside the candle and added at the top of the imbalance. He did not buy early. Price rallied through the minor buy-side pools to the 20,287 buy-side level.

### Order block validation and anticipation (refinement)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - An order block is not merely a down-close candle, and does not need to be engulfed. It is validated by other factors around the idea that the market should turn.
  - Here: as soon as price crossed the candle's open to the upside (it need not close above) it validated as a bullish order block.
  - He anticipates and predicts WHERE the order block, FVG or imbalance will form, based on the liquidity and the higher-timeframe level; he is not reacting to it after it forms.
  - PD arrays are not designed to stop price in its tracks; they give intelligence on what to look for. Here price was expected to trade to and through the real gap "like a hot knife through butter".
- **How to identify:** Down-close candle (bullish OB) at a liquidity purge and a reference level, with a volume imbalance above it.
- **How to use:** Entry anywhere between the open and high (or wick) of that candle. Anything lower in the area is extra entry.
- **Example in this video:** Same Asian-session trade as above.

### Retail breakout logic and why stops are the draw  (Buy-side/Sell-side liquidity, relative equal lows)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Retail buys the breakout above a short-term high (buy stop) and places stops below "support" (relative equal lows, a smooth gap, a wick low) and trails stops under wicks.
  - When price creates relative equal highs and then drives down hard, it is going after those sell-side stops. Read price by looking at where retail already got long and where their stops are.
  - The macro window starts the move; the target is the inefficiency or liquidity pool above or below.
  - He wants to be "diametrically opposed" to John Murphy's technical analysis book, which he recommends only to learn what the retail mindset sees; he has "zero affinity" for its content.
- **How to identify:** Relative equal highs followed by a quick drop; breakout-buy high; smooth swing lows and a clean gap below as "support".
- **How to use:** Wait for the stop run, then use PD arrays at the HTF level.

### Breakaway gap vs Measuring gap
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price leaves a higher-timeframe PD array (the daily box), he wants to see a buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency created on the way out (bullish) or the reverse (bearish), and to see it STAY OPEN (unfilled).
  - A gap at the beginning of a move that does not get filled is a breakaway gap: a lot more move is coming.
  - A gap in the MIDDLE of the move is a measuring gap: the second leg should equal the first leg. Fib method: draw from the start of the move; the gap's 50% area marks the halfway point so the second leg mirrors the first.
  - Do not want to see price merely touch the top of the HTF box and fall back.
- **How to identify:** Imbalance left as price exits an HTF array; where it sits relative to the whole move decides which gap it is.
- **How to use:** Measuring gap gives a projected target; a breakaway gap means expect more.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m rally out of the top of the daily box left a volume imbalance; he says in this instance it is a breakaway gap, not a measuring gap, so more move is coming (and shows what a measuring projection would look like).
- **Timestamps:** [1:21:45](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4905), [1:23:21](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5001)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** His fib instruction in the captions is hard to follow (caption unclear).

### Inversion FVG — stop above the gap's midpoint, body in the lower half
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A short entered on an inversion FVG should NOT see price reach the halfway point of that gap. For a beginner (his son) he suggests a stop at the high of the gap, but adds you have to allow for delivery up into a higher FVG when the leg that displaced lower contains two FVGs (as in the 2022 model).
  - So the stop goes above the midpoint (consequent encroachment) of the higher gap, not necessarily the extreme.
  - A bearish iFVG has to trade away first and then come back up. The body should stay in the LOWER half; the wick can poke into the upper half. In the example the wick stabbed outside the orange box, which he calls normal here because two FVGs are stacked on the distribution side.
- **How to identify:** Former bullish FVG broken by displacement lower; price returns; two stacked FVGs inside the displacing leg.
- **How to use:** Stop above the CE of the higher gap; partials at consequent encroachment of wicks and gaps and at each new low.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s drill, short at the iFVG after a displacement lower; price Wick'd into the upper gap but stayed below its midpoint; partials noted at the CE of a wick, a low, a gap's CE, and a new low.

## Glossary candidates
`VOLATILITY PINBALL` — his name for the limit-order desensitisation drill of taking one-contract trades off every FVG on a 15s/1m chart.
`INDIGO ENTRY MODEL` — order block inside a liquidity purge with a volume imbalance above (bullish) or below (bearish) the block.
`DUAL VOLUME IMBALANCE` — (descriptive label here) a candle body sandwiched between two volume imbalances, graded with quadrants.
`GRADING` — applying equilibrium and quadrants (25/50/75) to an imbalance or range to anticipate PD arrays.
`BREAKAWAY GAP` — imbalance left at the start of a move that stays open, signalling more move to come.
`MEASURING GAP` — an imbalance mid-move that marks half the move, so the second leg equals the first.
`REAL COMMON GAP` — a true void with no trading between one candle's high and another's low (his usage).
`SPOOLING` — the start of an algorithmic run at a macro window; not the same as reaching the target.
`TERMINUS` — final target of a trade, after partials remove the need to be right.
