## Concepts

### FVG on the left side (buy side of the curve) of a swing high — acts as an inversion FVG / premium array  (aliases ICT uses: "inefficiency on the buy side of the curve", "left side of the swing high", "premium fair value gap")
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement (new PD-array twist; he calls it "two more PD arrays")
- **What ICT says:**
  - When a swing high forms and there is an inefficiency (volume imbalance / FVG) on the LEFT side of it, i.e. on the rising "buy side of the curve" before price rolls over, use that one.
  - Boundaries as he describes them: take the swing-high candle's wick high and the volume imbalance high and call that a fair value gap; the volume imbalance itself is the separation between one candle's body close and the next candle's body open (between the bodies). Exact box edges are not fully clear in the captions.
  - It will act as an inversion fair value gap because it is part of the buy side of the curve before the market rotates lower. It is a premium array.
  - Compare with the usual FVG on the RIGHT side (sell side of the curve) of the swing high, which is what Model 2022 or the turtle-soup false breakout uses. He prefers the left-side one: it is "hyper-sensitive", more precise, because price can wick through the right-side gap.
  - The downside: you may not get filled. A smaller FVG lower down (right side) can be used by price and the market never reaches the left-side one, so your limit/market order never fills. Otherwise being wrong is just being wrong.
  - Not wrong to use the right-side gap (it still worked that day and he says that is fine), but to be a purist in his concepts you must not disregard the volume imbalance.
- **How to identify:** a swing high; look to its left for a gap/volume imbalance on the up-move; mark it from the volume imbalance to the wick high.
- **How to use:** with a bearish daily bias, wait for price to trade up into the left-side FVG (premium); short from it, preferring entries above its consequent encroachment (midpoint).
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily swing high with a left-side FVG; on Fri Jan 2 price opened, rallied hard up into it (premium), failed to quite reach its consequent encroachment, rotated down. On the 1-minute he marked a red box: volume imbalance with candle close at "52.25" and next open at "52.00" (hundreds digit not stated; caption read) and secured entries above its midpoint/CE.

### Turtle soup false breakout + first presented FVG carried forward (Model 2022 in fractal form)  (aliases: Model 2022, "high, higher high, failure")
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sequence: a short-term high, then another short-term high above it (breakout), which rejects and pulls back into the range. This is the turtle-soup false breakout. It need not be a complete reversal; it only needs that characteristic.
  - The FIRST presented fair value gap after that failure is the one to carry forward. This is a fractal of Model 2022: the same pattern amplified to higher timeframes or reduced to lower ones.
  - New this time: in Model 2022 as originally taught you did not keep that FVG; the revelation of the previous night is to keep it and extend it into the future.
  - Short-term high definition applied: a candle high with a lower high on each side (he says people argue it's not a swing high; "they didn't short the high. I did").
  - In this day: the short-term high was pierced and price went into the premium array he was waiting for; then the swing low gets taken out, which sets the stage for Model 2022. He says he uses it every day, multiple times on lower timeframes, and rejects claims the algorithm changed.
  - He criticizes people who want to find it going forward without backtesting.
- **How to identify:** high, higher high, rejection, break of the swing low (MSS), first FVG left behind.
- **How to use:** short from the first presented FVG, when its context is bearish (premium array, daily bias down). He wants to see the FVG offer no resistance (see next entry for this day's version).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Fri Jan 2: after the 9:30 open price rallied, blew through an old buy-side pool (relative equal highs), made a short-term high at the 9:48 candle (captioned "9:48 p.m."; presumably a.m.), whose neighboring highs were both lower; price tagged the left-side FVG premium array, then broke the swing low, left the first presented FVG, and sold off.

### Daily suspension block (volume imbalance at both ends of a BISI/SIBI range)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:** a daily buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (BISI) range containing two volume imbalances, one at its high end and one at its low end, is a suspension block. ### Trade management: partial near target, roll the stop, never let it go stale
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When you are close to target (10–15 handles) after a straight run and feel the urge to get out ("please just get to my target"), take a partial immediately; it relieves the pressure. A partial is also planned before the target is reached.
  - After the partial move the stop; roll it so the remaining position cannot give back everything.
  - Trail: each time price makes a new swing low (or an elongated candle breaks below), move the stop down, but do not strangle it, give about three handles of room, closer to price.
  - Never let the stop go stale: each new equity high (unrealized), find a way to move the stop closer to the exit. This is mindfulness about money, not paranoia.
  - If you take partials you can be somewhat more liberal about leaving the stop where first adjusted, but if you see overlapping PD arrays, a fast move can erase unrealized profit (stop left at break-even or a little plus).
  - In a "normalized" lower-volatility market you don't need to be this sensitive; in the recent break-neck volatility, do it. For new traders, getting stopped out of winners and making slightly less is a good thing to grow into.
  - Add to a short on a retest of the lower quadrant of the first presented FVG (he would have).

## Glossary candidates
`Left-side FVG / buy side of the curve` — inefficiency on the rising side of a swing high, preferred over the right-side gap.
`Inversion FVG (premium array)` — here, a left-side gap that acts as resistance as price rallies into it before rotating down.
`Turtle soup (high, higher high, failure)` — false breakout above a short-term high that rejects back into range.
`First presented FVG (carry forward)` — first FVG after the turtle-soup failure; kept and extended.
`Suspension block` — range with a volume imbalance at both ends inside a BISI/SIBI.
`Quadrants on a wick` — upper quadrant / CE / lower quadrant grading applied to a candle wick.
`Mohawk` — wicks beyond a gap with bodies staying inside it (name used in the captions).
`Heaviness` — price cannot retrace to retest a level after breaking it; very bearish.
`Stale stop` — a stop not moved up/down as unrealized profit grows.
