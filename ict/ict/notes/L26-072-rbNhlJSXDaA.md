## Summary
ICT teaches what he calls his "Enigma" lens: price delivery is algorithmic and can be measured, and fair value gaps (together with volume imbalances) can be projected into an "unrealized dealing range" to give two targets: a low-hanging-fruit objective and an extreme. He demonstrates it on the DXY daily chart: the displacement candle's open, the preceding candle's close (the volume imbalance), and the later candle's low are fib-anchors, a 1.25 projection lands at 101.795 (low-hanging) and 101.805 (extreme), and the midpoint of the two, 101.80, is exactly the Wednesday high before the retracement. The rest of the video reviews EURUSD, GBPUSD, gold, silver, crude, Bitcoin, YM, ES and NQ against the targets he gave in earlier recordings (analysis video of June 7, 2026), re-teaches how a volume imbalance converts into an FVG/BPR/inversion FVG, wick-CE grading, order block/CISD on the largest down-close candle, and the "suspension block" (a single candle suspended between two volume imbalances). A strong risk theme: knowing when not to trade, and controlling losses rather than winning big.

### Enigma FVG projection: low-hanging-fruit objective and extreme (1.25 projection)  (aliases: "projections and protractions", "unrealized dealing range projection")
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry-target model (target measurement)
- **What ICT says:**
  - There are two distinct ways he teaches targets: the low-hanging-fruit objective and the extreme. The "story is clearest" in between the two, which is why the consequent encroachment / mean threshold of that band matters.
  - Evolution of FVG teaching: first wicks only (classic FVG, candle 1 high to candle 3 low), then the volume imbalance was added (use the candle's close rather than the wick high), giving two measurements of one inefficiency. The two measurements are then projected into the future to find very specific price points in an unrealized dealing range.
  - Steps on the DXY daily (as best reconstructed from the captions and on-screen pointing, anchors partly unclear):
    1. Find the displacement: candle 1 closes at about 100.997; candle 2 opens higher (over 101.005) leaving a volume imbalance (gap between the two bodies) and an opening range gap; candle 2 trades down slightly (small manipulation wick), then rallies. This is "part of the power of three" for that day, carried into the next day.
    2. Low-hanging fruit: draw a fib from the open of candle 2 (the low of its body; the point where the displacement begins) up to the low of the later candle that "showed its willingness to go higher" (discount sensitivity on that low). The 50% of that range is just the CE and "is not the focus". Add the 1.25 level (125%) and project it forward. That gave 101.795.
    3. Extreme: because the volume imbalance sits lower, re-anchor the same fib to candle 1's close ("the body tells the story, the wicks do the damage") instead of candle 2's open. The 1.25 level then gave 101.805.
    4. Fib the band between the two projected levels (101.795 down to 101.805, drawn as a blue line). Its CE is 101.80.
  - Result: the Wednesday high printed at 101.80, equal to the CE of the two projected levels, and price then retraced into the close of that day. He claims this was called live, not replayed or "form fitted", on the recording before it happened.
- **How to identify:** a displacement candle whose open is above (or below) the prior close (volume imbalance + opening range gap) and a following candle whose low (for bullish) marks the first test; price still has an untouched dealing range above.
- **How to use:** targets = low-hanging fruit (shallow anchor) and extreme (deep anchor); expect a reversal/retracement in the band around their midpoint; take profits there (he told viewers to close shorts in EURUSD and GBPUSD while DXY was at this band, and to close the bullish DXY idea).
- **Example in this video:** DXY daily, June 7 analysis said price would use a bullish inversion FVG, take the minor buy side, close above the CE of the range between two liquidity pools, and then draw to an old high. The final terminus he posted was 101.977; the Enigma projection said take profits at 101.795–101.805 on Wednesday. DXY topped at 101.80, then retraced.

### Unrealized (implied) dealing range  (aliases: "implied dealing range", "unrealized dealing range")
- **Kind:** definition / new coinage
- **What ICT says:** A dealing range whose far end has not yet traded. Example: start where the displacement candle opened for the day and go up to the high that price has formed so far; because price has taken out the previous day's high, an extension to the next draw is an "all but guarantee". It is "unrealized" because price has not traded there yet, so you have an opinion that price will go there but must submit to time.
- **How to identify:** a candle opens above the prior close, trades down, then rallies; the area above the day's current high is the unrealized portion; the range from the candle's open to that high is the dealing range for projection.
- **How to use:** feeds the Enigma FVG projection (above).
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:21](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=981) — definition; [0:18:02](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1082) — start where the candle opened to the high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he first says "unrealized dealing range", then "implied dealing range" — same thing.

### Volume imbalance and the two FVG measurements  (aliases: "volume imbalance", "classic FVG", "FVG including volume imbalance")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Volume imbalance = the gap between consecutive candle bodies (e.g. candle 1 close 100.997 and candle 2 open above 101.005 = volume imbalance 100.997 to ~101.005) even though wicks overlap.
  - When he first taught FVGs (the "classic" version), he used wicks only; the volume imbalance was added later as a "graduated revelation" so as not to overload students. Now the FVG can include the volume imbalance (disregard the short-term wick high and go down to the close of candle 1).
  - Toggling "adjust for contract rollover" (the tab at the bottom of the chart) shows where the volume imbalances are; later he adds that it also reveals SMT divergence (see below).
- **How to identify:** body-to-body gap between consecutive candles.
- **How to use:** two measurements = shallow (open of candle 2) and deep (close of candle 1) for projections.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:37](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=637) — graduated teaching; [0:11:25](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=685) — definition with numbers; [0:48:45](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2925) — "this little tab holds a lot of secrets".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** consistent with video 71 ("include the volume imbalances" when measuring FVG CE).

### Volume imbalance converting into FVG, BPR and inversion FVG  (aliases: "changes its characteristic", "inversion fair value gap", "balanced price range")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance created by down delivery (sell-side delivery, not liquidity) is first measured as a volume imbalance. When price later trades up through it, then back down and leaves the range, it becomes a balanced price range, and is used through the lens of a bearish FVG. If price then trades above it, it is used as an inversion FVG (support). He had labelled this on the June 7 DXY chart.
  - Example on DXY after June 7: a beautiful run off the iFVG; buy side delivered; the old high behind did not even need to be traded.
  - ES example (opposite direction): a volume imbalance began as a bullish PDA (bodies of the two candles do not touch), delivered by buy-side delivery; price traded down through it, another higher/lower round, and left it again, so it becomes a BPR; but with the inversion character it is now bearish and is expected to act as resistance. Warning in advance: this is more complicated and more likely to fail.
- **How to use:** treat inversion as resistance/support after body-close violation; wait for the retrace.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:58](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1858) — volume imbalance changes characteristic; [0:32:35](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1955) — iFVG labelling; [0:42:16](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2536) — ES; [0:43:04](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2584) — caveat "more likely to fail".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he tells you in advance when a setup is lower probability, to show why it might not deliver.

### Wick CE grading: discount wick vs premium wick; wicks take precedence  (aliases: "wicks take precedence", "premium wick", "discount wick")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Whether a wick is a "discount wick" or "premium wick" depends where market price is in relation to it: the same wick is discount when price is above it and premium when price is below it. "You have to know where at market price you are in relationship to the PD array or the wick."
  - A wick takes precedence over the body-based order block "just like a gap", because a wick is a balanced price range (price went from the candle's close to the low and all the way back to the same price: traded both directions).
  - ES example: an enormous wick beside the largest down-close candle; it needs a close below the CE of that wick before a run lower becomes high probability; until then it isn't. NQ: price opened, traded up, could not reach CE, dumped lower and cleared lows into the upper quadrant of a wick; he wants a close below that level.
  - EURUSD example: price traded up and just fell short of the wick's CE, hit the "375" octant level, repelled, and closed below the low he had given on June 7.
- **How to use:** a close beyond the CE of the wick is the trigger for continuation; otherwise treat the move as not yet validated.
- **Timestamps:** [0:34:09](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2049) — discount vs premium wick; [0:45:28](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2728) — wicks take precedence (BPR); [0:46:18](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2778) — needs a close below the CE.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** cross-refs video 71's wick-CE grading rule (bodies may not cross the CE).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Two target tiers: low-hanging fruit and extreme; CE (mean threshold) of the band between them is where reaction is most likely.
- Fib projection level used: 1.25 (125%). DXY low-hanging fruit 101.795; extreme 101.805; CE 101.80 = Wednesday high.
- DXY terminus from June 7: 101.977.
- Volume imbalance example: candle 1 close 100.997, candle 2 open over 101.005.
- Close above CE between two pools of liquidity raises confidence of a draw to the far pool.
- Wick CE: close beyond it needed for continuation; discount/premium depends on where price sits relative to the wick; wicks take precedence over bodies as BPRs.
- Largest down-close candle in a series = CISD order block.
- Suspension block = candle suspended between two volume imbalances; treat as iFVG; NQ level 28,517 (maybe 513).
- Take profits when the target band is reached and a full-body candle sits near the high; Wednesday = midweek reversal risk.
- Use "adjust for contract rollover" to see volume imbalances and SMT.
- Do not trade lower-probability / "more likely to fail" setups; he flags them in advance.

## Glossary candidates
`ENIGMA` — ICT's name for his measured, algorithmic way of reading and projecting price delivery.
`LOW-HANGING FRUIT OBJECTIVE` — the first, shallower projected target of a measured range.
`EXTREME (PROJECTION)` — the deeper projected target using the volume-imbalance anchor.
`UNREALIZED / IMPLIED DEALING RANGE` — a dealing range whose far end has not yet traded.
`PROTRACTION` — the extended run away from the displacement/FVG (caption-level meaning).
`EVENT HORIZON` — the CE midpoint between two liquidity pools used to judge the draw.
`VOLUME IMBALANCE` — gap between consecutive bodies where wicks overlap.
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — one candle sandwiched between two volume imbalances.
`TERMINUS` — the final target level he states for an analysis (here 101.977 on DXY).
`DISCOUNT WICK / PREMIUM WICK` — same wick, named by whether price is above or below it.
