## Summary
On the first trading day after a holiday (the Labor Day weekend: he refers to "Labor Day" and to Friday August 28th's first presented FVG, so this is the Tuesday after Labor Day 2026; the date itself is not stated), ICT trades NQ in paper mode with a single contract "just to get in alignment with price." ## Concepts

### Post-holiday engagement rule  (aliases: "how to start back after a holiday")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a holiday, go in light — "like one contract" — even if you are trading paper, to test price. It helps you get into alignment: either you're offside or onside with your initial expectation.
  - Post-holiday volume is thin and the market can be "a little wonky" with a slow start while participants return; "people tend to not want to move the market too much after a holiday." So "why throw good money after a gamble? Let it work out the kinks."
  - He lets the morning session go and wants a "pulse" on what the market does. It's "not about the money; it's about getting in sync with price action and not trying to force a will." There are plenty of days in the rest of the week to capture money.
  - Because of the holiday, the new week opening gap high was not his default objective as it normally would be (he mentions he would otherwise aim for it), but it ended up as a reference.
  - Paper trading disclosure: he says he's in paper trading this morning so viewers can stop worrying about "counting pennies."
- **How to use:** First session after a holiday: reduced size (1 contract/micro or paper), look for reasons the market agrees with your expectations, scale up only after price confirms.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:31](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=211) — one contract, paper trade, aligning with price; [0:06:58](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=418) — why paper trade after a holiday; [0:15:30](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=930) — rule of thumb: after holidays go in light.
- **Nuance / caveats:** A sizing/process rule, not a PD-array rule; he explicitly says the information (gap/FVG logic) stays the same.

### Premium Opening Range Gap + Friday's First Presented FVG as framing for a short
- **Kind:** entry model (applied)
- **What ICT says:**
  - At the 9:30 open there was a premium opening range gap of roughly 100 handles (open above the prior RTH settlement). He frames it from the RTH settlement to the 9:30 open, mid gap marked, with half-gap and full gap closure levels.
  - He also uses last Friday's (Aug 28) first presented FVG as a key reference ("we're using Friday's first presented fair value gap").
  - Plan: look for a reason for price to want to get down to the mid gap; place the short; stop above the second wick high; aim for the sell-side liquidity beneath.
  - After price traded "premium gap opening ... pull down half the gap" and then "just past half the gap" into a sell-side liquidity pool and the suspension block (below), full gap closure and the new week opening gap lie further down. He states he is not demanding the full closure.
- **How to use:** Short a premium gap on a reason (premium PD array), target the half gap first, then liquidity under it; keep a stop just above the high that invalidates the premium array (here the second wick high).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, first morning after Labor Day: open ~100 handles above settlement; price first runs buy side, then pulls down to half the gap; a short entered into the upper part of the SIBI wick; stop just above the second wick high; half gap hit; risk removed; down into the suspension block and then below its low (sell side).
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:00](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=0) — premium gap ~100 handles, Friday's first presented FVG, mid gap; [0:01:55](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=115) — stop above second wick high, aim for sell side; [0:06:10](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=370) — buy side first, then half the gap, sell-side pool, full gap closure, NWOG below.

### Suspension block  (new coinage / label, caption partly unclear)
- **Kind:** new coinage
- **What ICT says:** A wick range with a volume imbalance at both ends: "inside that wick, and volume imbalance at both ends, so that's a suspension block." He treats it as a discount array when price reaches the lower part/low of it and says liquidity rests just below its low. He also divides the SIBI "with the volume imbalance low" for a consequent encroachment and remarks "there's liquidity resting right below that low."
- **How to identify:** Candle wick range bounded by volume imbalances at both its top and bottom (per his words; no further definition given in this clip).
- **How to use:** Short from the upper half / sell the premium side and expect the lower half (discount) to be tested and a sell-side pool just under it to be the target; remove risk once the half gap/block high is traded.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: down into the suspension block high after the half gap fill; below its low there is a sell-side pool that is cleared.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:42](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=162), [0:05:20](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=320).
- **Nuance / caveats:** Definition is thin and the caption is noisy; later he mentions a "semi mouse hole" (caption unclear — possibly the same structure). Needs confirmation from a dedicated lecture.

### Premium sensitivity of a bearish FVG (SIBI): bodies in the lower half, upper half untested
- **Kind:** rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:** For a SIBI (bearish FVG), the upper half is premium; if price trades into it but the BODIES do not close above the midpoint (CE) and the upper half is not tested at all, that is a sign of bearish order flow ("it's allowing for the market to want to pull up into this deeper" only if a close above CE happens). A close above the consequent encroachment means price is likely to trade back up toward 9:30's opening price again (reasonable, not unusual). He wants "some of that stay open ... remain completely untested at all" — the portion he wants untested is the upper part of the SIBI. When price goes into the lower half and looks "heavy," expect sells in the next few candles toward the new week opening gap high; if it goes into the upper half, treat it with caution. At the end he re-states: "premium sensitivity: not seeing the upper half of that SIBI traded to or even tested ... and we sold off."
- **How to identify:** Draw the SIBI, find its CE; track closes of bodies relative to CE; note whether the upper half was touched.
- **How to use:** Hold or add to shorts while bodies stay below CE; stop just above the high of the premium array; a body close above CE changes the expectation (back to 9:30 open). If price rallies only into the lower half of the gap and gets heavy, add to the sell.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, post-Labor Day: price rallies into the lower half of the SIBI/suspension block, bodies never close at or above its midpoint, "got heavy," and drops to new lows into the new week opening gap high (caption "NWOG high for this week").
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:53](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=533) — bodies didn't close above/below the midpoint; [0:09:40](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=580) — lower half, "heavy"; [0:15:30](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=930) — premium sensitivity restated; [0:16:17](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=977) — final rule review.
- **Nuance / caveats:** The wording about "upper/lower half" conflates the SIBI with the suspension block in the captions; the rule (bodies below CE, upper half untested = continuation lower) is clear.

### Scaling out: "running down equity" / partial at every new low
- **Kind:** rule (trade management)
- **What ICT says:** Remove risk when the half gap is filled. Any position still open: stop just above the high. Anytime price creates a new low and you haven't taken a partial yet, it's a good idea to "pay yourself" — especially if sell-side liquidity rests below the low and has just been cleared. He calls this "running down equity": pursue new increases in your account balance rather than demanding that your entire "terminus" (final target) be the only way you get paid. He says this matters while you're developing and working out your model.
- **How to use:** Partial at the low where sell-side was just cleared; move stop above the high; leave the remainder for the final objective.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:42](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=162) — remove the risk at half gap; [0:10:29](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=629) — partial at new low; "running down equity."
- **Nuance / caveats:** "Terminus" is the term he uses for the ultimate target (also the name of one of his models; here used generically).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- After a holiday: trade one contract (or paper), test price, don't force; plenty of other days that week.
- Premium gap short: stop above the second wick high; target half gap first, then sell-side below.
- Bodies below the midpoint (CE) of the SIBI and the upper half untested = bearish continuation; body close above CE = likely return to the 9:30 open.
- Remove risk at half gap filled; partial at each new low if none taken yet ("running down equity").
- First presented FVG of the prior Friday (Aug 28) and the new week opening gap are active references.
- Premium gap size that morning: about 100 handles; mid gap reached quickly.
- Overnight trade used three contracts with roughly 10–20 handles of drawdown (caption unclear).

## Glossary candidates
`Suspension block` — wick range with volume imbalances at both ends, treated as a discount array (definition thin).
`Premium sensitivity` — the upper half of a bearish FVG is where price should not be able to close/test if order flow is bearish.
`Discount sensitivity` — price using a discount array (e.g., NWOG) with bodies holding above it.
`Running down equity` — taking partials at new extremes to keep adding to your account balance instead of waiting for the final target.
`Terminus` — the final/ultimate target of a trade (as used here; also a named model).
`First presented FVG (prior day)` — first FVG of the previous day/Friday, kept as a reference.
