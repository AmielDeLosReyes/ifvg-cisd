### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG), bullish — used as discount support  (aliases: "inversion fair value gap", "reclaim")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A bearish FVG that price trades above (and "reclaims as bullish") becomes a bullish inversion FVG. "If it can trade above it, we should see that as an inversion of fair value gap, a bullish fair value gap."
  - Once inverted it should be used as a discount-sensitive area: price trades into it, runs up, clears the high. He will enter long inside that gap on the retest.
  - Colour convention (his own chart habit): the gap is shown green once it has turned bullish, so he can see "what it was originally and then when it changes its characteristic". He admits this is a mental aid and he "might be incorrect in this assumption".
  - Invalidation: the stop goes just under the inversion FVG after the entry works ("raise the stop up just underneath this inversion fair value gap now, 'cause if it goes there, I don't want to be a part of this anymore").
  - He says he will "see it reclaim as bullish" — the reclaim is the thing he waits for before treating it as bullish.
- **How to identify:** bearish FVG (SIBI) on the chart; price closes/trades back above it; flip it to a bullish iFVG (BISI-like behaviour) and treat as support.
- **How to use:** buy a retest into the inverted gap; initial stop below the low of the area with "a little bit of a cushion"; once price moves up and clears the high, trail the stop to just below the iFVG; targets are event horizon, then the 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** the bearish FVG that he first shorted into gets reclaimed; he buys inside the newly bullish (green) gap, stop under the low with cushion; after the first partial the stop moves to just under the inversion gap.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:32](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=212) — bearish gap → bullish iFVG explanation and colour scheme; [0:05:11](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=311) — stop raised under the iFVG.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** consistent with the standard iFVG teaching; the novel bit is pairing it with "event horizon" as the first target and the opening-range context.

### Event Horizon (halfway point between a 1-hour minor BSL pool and an old high)  (aliases ICT uses: "event horizon", spoken in captions also as "either/any horizon")
- **Kind:** new coinage (as used here; the term may have been defined in an earlier video)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Event horizon is "the halfway point between the 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity pool and then the old high" — i.e. the midpoint (50%) of that span.
  - It is the first objective after entry: "rip right on into event horizon, and then accelerate to minor buy side, which is up here from the hourly chart" (the 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity he had tweeted about that morning).
  - He plans to take one contract off at/just below event horizon and then move the stop up.
  - Later he notes bodies are not "laying on" the halfway point yet: price has wicked just above it a couple of times but not closed above it.
- **How to identify:** take the 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity pool and the old high above/near it; compute the midpoint.
- **How to use:** first partial / scale-out level, with the stop trailed after it; full target is the 1-hour minor BSL (and he explicitly is "not going to aim for the full new all-time high", though "it could do it").
- **Example in this video:** long taken inside the bullish FVG; plan: partial at event horizon, stop to under the iFVG, remaining contracts to the 1-hour minor BSL. Price reached event horizon (wicks above, bodies not yet on it) but the orders ended up closed out through his own TP-drag error rather than the planned partial structure.

### Consequent Encroachment of an old 1-hour imbalance: wick yes, body no  (aliases ICT uses: "old 1-hour buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency", "halfway point", "consequent encroachment")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He identifies an "old 1-hour buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency" (a 1-hour BISI) that price has already accumulated inside and ramped away from.
  - If the market is to stay bullish it should stay above the halfway point (consequent encroachment) of that gap. "It can wick down below it. We don't want to see it leave a body down there."
  - A body below the CE "makes it more difficult for the market to continue", at least for the remainder of the opening range.
  - He says there is "no real reason" for price to come back below the inversion FVG or below that area, given the sequence so far.
  - Price then wicks down fully through the area and recovers ("full closure on the wick") and is back in the gap: wick tap OK.
- **How to identify:** locate the halfway point of the 1-hour gap; watch whether candle bodies close below it versus only wicks.
- **How to use:** as a hold/fail test for the long: wicks through CE tolerated; bodies below CE = lose conviction. Also small 1-minute gaps stacked above ("a small little gap here, then this one here") can be used as stepping stones.
- **Example in this video:** after price pushes to event horizon, it drops back; the wick goes all the way down and fills the area, but no body closes below the CE, so the structure still holds; he is neutral at the moment because price is mid-area.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:19](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=499) — no reason to return below the inversion FVG; old 1-hour BISI; [0:09:07](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=547) — CE: wick allowed, body not.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** same "bodies vs wicks at CE" logic as in the Reaper FVG video (L26-077): the CE is a boundary respected by candle bodies, not necessarily wicks.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range remaining: "16 minutes, 18 minutes" left at the time of the final commentary (caption phrase; exact opening-range window not given).
- Event horizon = midpoint between the 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity pool and the old high.
- A bearish FVG that price trades above becomes a bullish iFVG; treat it as discount support.
- Stop: below the low with a cushion; once working, just below the iFVG.
- Take one contract off at/just below event horizon, then trail the stop.
- Targets: event horizon first, then 1-hour minor buy-side liquidity; he is not aiming for a new all-time high.
- Consequent encroachment of the old 1-hour imbalance: wick below is acceptable, body below is not.
- Neutral zone: when price sits in the middle of an area with no edge, he has no opinion and stays out.

`OCTANT` — eighth-division of a range; he references a ".37" (0.375?) octant level (caption unclear).
