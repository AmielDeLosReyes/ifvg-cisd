## Concepts

### Fair Value Gap (FVG / volume imbalance)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + entry model
- **What ICT says:** Volume imbalance—an inefficiency where price traded—forms when candles do not overlap; can act as support (bullish FVG), resistance (bearish FVG), or become an inversion FVG if price approaches from the opposite side. After a big blow-off run, these gaps want to be filled for price to feel "balanced" and "fair."
- **How to identify:** Look for candlestick bodies that do not touch a gap left above/below them. High of one candle < low of next candle = bullish FVG. Low of one candle > high of next candle = bearish FVG.
- **How to use:** Target low of FVG (bullish) or high (bearish) as a magnet on price. If FVG is utilized first as bullish (filled as support), and price later approaches from below, it becomes an inversion FVG and will likely offer resistance on the way down. Enter at best price within the gap. Exit or take profit when price leaves the gap cleanly.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily, May 27, 2026. High-of-day run left a volume imbalance at ~676.25. Price later fell and used that gap as inversion FVG, respecting its high and consequent encroachment on the way down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:02](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=302) — importance of volume imbalance post-runup · [0:18:23](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1103) — perfect precision of FVG bodies · [0:33:20](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2000) — how first utilization predicts inversion FVG behavior
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Must identify whether gap was used as bullish or bearish first; only then can you predict its next role. Do not "cherry-pick" gaps; use strict rules about which ones to trade.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE)
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:** The high or low of the candlestick that completes a fair value gap (the body reaching into it). Must be respected as a boundary; if price breaks below CE on a bearish move, it signals weakness; if price cannot return to CE on a retracement, it shows the lower area is strong. CE is literally part of the PD array; you must not skip it in your analysis.
- **How to identify:** Draw the FVG high or low, then identify the first candlestick body that entered the gap; the extreme of that body is your CE level.
- **How to use:** Use CE as a tighter stop loss than the full gap. Enter short only if you trust price won't return to CE; if it does, exit or reduce. Use CE as a clue: if price barely penetrates CE and cannot return, weakness is confirmed.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, May 27, 2026 PM session. Bullish FVG in pre-market had a CE line; on the bear move, price sliced right through it, and bodies could not even return to touch CE on retracements—textbook weakness signal.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:54](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1434) — CE must be broken cleanly · [0:37:20](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2240) — CE performance tells story of strength/weakness · [0:42:03](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2523) — CE as tight entry buffer
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The bodies respecting CE is the key (not wicks); if a second retracement low is higher than the first, it's "hawkish" and you should demand it.

### Standard Deviations for Price Targeting
- **Kind:** new coinage + refinement
- **What ICT says:** Use standard deviations (same tool used for Asian range, Central Bank Dealers Range, opening range gaps) to project a price move. Measure a key price range (high to low, or body to body), then move it down (or up) by one, two, or more deviations. Example: if a range is 100 points, one deviation down gives a target. Apply negative-two deviation to the day's range to find support zones that are statistically likely to be tested.
- **How to identify:** In TradingView, set up standard deviations on the daily or intraday chart. Read the negative-two line; watch price gravitate toward it.
- **How to use:** Negative-two std dev line acts as a magnet and key support/resistance. If price trades below a key high and low, project deviations downward; use that level as a target on short setups. Combine with FVGs for confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily, May 27, 2026. Negative-two std dev = 29,878.75. Price was aimed at that level and hit it. In the 1m intraday chart, measured the blow-off range, duplicated it downward using Fibonacci, then applied standard deviations to find the exact low area (~877.5 actual vs 878.75 projected—very tight).

### Climax Reversal Patterns
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The "flavor of 2026" teaching. A climax is a parabolic, unsustainable blow-off run at the end of a directional move (daily high achieved on pre-market spike, then continued intraday spike), followed by fast reversal. Use it as a signal that the move has exhausted itself and shorts become high-probability in that direction.
- **How to identify:** Look for sustained up-move, then a parabolic "grand finale" spike (micro spike, volume spike, wicks extended). Price often leaves a gap or volume imbalance at the top.
- **How to use:** After a climax spike, expect a fast reversal into the gap left behind. Short entries become valid. Use FVG and CE rules to fine-tune entry and exit.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily and 1m, May 27, 2026. 7 weeks of consecutive up closes + all-time high reached + pre-market parabolic spike = climax. Immediately reversed hard into volume imbalance. Reward-to-risk was excellent.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** definition + refinement
- **What ICT says:** When a bullish FVG (gap created on an up move) is later breached from above, it becomes an inversion FVG and acts as resistance on the way down. Price will respects its high and CE the same way it respected the low on the way up. The first utilization dictates the second role.
- **How to identify:** Identify the original bullish FVG (or any FVG), watch whether price uses it, then observe when price approaches from opposite direction. That opposite-direction approach uses the gap as iFVG.
- **How to use:** Treat iFVG high / CE as resistance on retracements during downside moves. Do not expect price to exceed iFVG high on rallies; if it does, the iFVG is "overruled" and you must re-evaluate bias.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, pre-market bullish FVG at ~9:26–9:30. On the PM session bear move, that gap became iFVG; price's body could not even touch its high on the way down, showing conviction in the downside.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:37](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1057) — inversion FVG described · [0:42:03](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2523) — testing the iFVG high and failing
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** An iFVG can be overruled; if price enters and closes above it solidly, assume the direction has shifted.

### Relative Equal Lows
- **Kind:** definition + use
- **What ICT says:** When the market creates a low, then retraces, and later creates another low near the same price, that level becomes a magnet on price. Market makers target these for liquidity runs and stop sweeps. On daily charts, relative equal lows are key resistance to the downside in bear moves.
- **How to identify:** Look at prior swing lows; if a new low is within ~5–10 points (on NQ), it qualifies as a relative equal low.
- **How to use:** In bull markets, target relative equal highs (upside). In bear markets, target relative equal lows (downside). These are "universal" targets; accumulate shorts when aimed at them; use them as targets on short entries.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily, May 27, 2026. "Relatively equal lows" from prior month to current level were discussed as magnet on price for potential downside draw.

### Shorting in Primary Bull Markets
- **Kind:** refinement + psychology / mindset
- **What ICT says:** Can be done profitably at intraday levels when you identify climax reversals or FVG setups, BUT requires 1–2 years minimum experience. Beginners should stick to longs; shorting too early causes fear-based exits and losses. When shorting, conviction and stop-loss discipline are essential. Start small; do not over-leverage.
- **How to identify:** High-probability short setups in bull markets: (a) climax spike creating gap, (b) inversion FVG that has been utilized as bullish, (c) failed retracement back to a prior level (weakness signal), (d) bodies not reaching CE on bounce attempts.
- **How to use:** Enter in upper half of iFVG (buffer position), aim for FVG low / prior level. On first entry, keep size small. If conviction confirmed by price action (e.g., bodies can't return to CE), scale into larger shorts on next pullback.

### Trade Management via Time & Consequent Encroachment
- **Kind:** rule + execution
- **What ICT says:** After identifying a down target (e.g., FVG low), watch how fast price gets there. If it's slow/lethargic, you may be wrong; if it's fast and direct, conviction in the downside is strong. Also track whether price can/cannot return to CE on retracements—repeated failures to return = strong signal to stay short and add size.
- **How to identify:** Track the clock; observe the pace of down moves vs horizontal grinding. Look at candlestick bodies: can they touch CE? If not, that's weakness confirmation.
- **How to use:** If move is slow, reduce or exit short; assume you're wrong. If move is fast and CE is broken hard, scale in larger on the next pullback. Use CE failures as your "permission" to pyramid shorts.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, PM session. Initial drop from ~9:26 to ~9:37 was very fast (one directional), hitting the initial target at 877.5 in that range. Then bodies couldn't return to CE—ICT wanted to take more shorts but market had already done the move, so he just took what was there and exited.

### Position Sizing & Buffer Entries
- **Kind:** rule / execution
- **What ICT says:** On entries in high-volatility markets (first 10 min post-open, e.g., 9:30–9:40 ET), build in a buffer. Take a small long entry in the pre-market, then use the profit as a cushion to allow an aggressive short entry at open with some adverse price action tolerance. Do not risk more than you can afford to lose; use micro contracts if your account is small.
- **How to identify:** Identify your conviction level; size accordingly.
- **How to use:** Recommended approach: micro contracts for small accounts or prop-firm attempts (alleviates margin/rules issues). For entries near open, take a small long buffer trade 5–10 min before, then use its profit to cushion a short entry.
- **Example in this video:** NQ. Entry long in pre-market / early AM, gains ~50–100 points. Then entry short at open within iFVG; initial risk is offset by prior long gain. Reduces forced stop-out risk.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Shorting in primary bull markets requires 1–2 years experience minimum; beginners should focus on longs.
- Relative equal highs and lows are universal targets across all markets.
- Fair value gaps always want to be filled (for price to feel balanced); identify which direction they will fill.
- First utilization of a gap determines its next use (if bullish FVG filled as support, it becomes iFVG on next approach from above).
- Consequent encroachment must be respected; bodies refusing to return to CE = weakness signal.
- Open, high, low, close (OHLC) contain all the information needed; no external indicators required.
- Pre-open volatility (7:00–9:00 ET) sets the tone; watch for parabolic blow-offs or gaps.
- Negative-two standard deviation = key magnet level on daily charts.
- If a down move is slow/lethargic to target, re-evaluate bias; fast/direct moves = strong conviction.
- Use micro contracts on small accounts or prop-firm accounts; position size = risk tolerance.
- Never over-leverage; position sizing = core risk management.
- Relative equal lows/highs: target them, do not buy/sell into them (if shorting, target the low; if longing, target the high).

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Conviction vs. greed:** Only take trades that align with your conviction and experience level. Do not force trades; wait for clean setups. If you're not sure, do not enter.
- **Missed entries:** If you miss an entry, do not chase. Wait for a retracement into a fair value gap or use a different timeframe. "Cast again" and let the water settle (analogy given at 0:43:38).
- **Drawdown tolerance:** Use buffer entries and smaller size to afford adverse price action without panic exit. ICT took a small heat on one short entry but had no fear because prior long entry cushioned it.

## Glossary candidates
`Climax reversal` — a parabolic spike finishing a directional move, followed by fast reversal; indicates exhaustion and high-probability reversal entry.
`Consequent encroachment (CE)` — the high or low of the first candle body to enter a fair value gap; a key boundary that must be respected as support/resistance.
`Inversion fair value gap (iFVG)` — a fair value gap that was previously utilized in one direction, now acting as resistance/support in the opposite direction.
`Volume imbalance` — an inefficiency where no price traded between two candlesticks; the gap left behind wants to be filled for price to feel balanced.
`Relative equal highs/lows` — prior swing highs/lows that the market re-visits; these are liquidity targets and magnets on price.
`Buffer entry` — a small initial position taken before a higher-conviction entry; used to offset adverse price action and reduce forced stop-out risk.
`Fulcrum point` — a price level that, when broken, projects a measured move downward (like a door hinge pivoting open).
