## Summary
ICT teaches his Obsidian model: two opposing wicks, graded at their consequent encroachment, whose second wick must be higher than the first when you are bearish; the zone between the two CEs is where he anchors and builds a short while price keeps tapping inside it ("a false consolidation that bull-flag traders will see"). He insists it only works inside a narrative taken one PD array at a time from the daily chart: last week's premium arrays (relative equal highs, a wick, a SIBI), an early warning when Friday closed below the SIBI's low, a Monday that opened below the wick/gap CE and could not follow through after poking above the SIBI high, plus the 7:00–9:00 am rule (trending 7–9 means a choppy morning), a Judas-style protraction after 9:30, and a negative-extension projection off the 7–9 low as the first target (30,137.50). He walks through the entries, the iFVG validation (a close one tick beyond the gap), partials at the lows, relative equal lows and the NWOG upper quadrant, and finishes with the principle that you must work from the daily chart down, never from a one-minute chart alone.

## Concepts

### Obsidian (two opposing wicks, second wick higher for shorts)  (aliases: Obsidian model/PD array)
- **Kind:** entry model / new coinage (first teaching; he promises more details later)
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Obsidian is two opposing wicks within a narrative", and the second wick has to be higher than the first one when you are bearish. You split both wicks in half (CE of each); price action will "do these types of things" between the two CE levels.
  - You look for such wicks when you expect price to eventually turn lower: a candle with a wick above it and a candle with a wick below it, grading both and extending the midpoints to the right. The opposing wick that forms later must be above the first.
  - Sequence in the example: price trades lower and closes below the structure; on the first run back up into the zone price is not willing to put a body in the upper half of it; the focus zone is between this candle's CE and the other candle's CE.
  - He has already shorted higher but wants to build the short every time price enters the zone, targeting low-hanging fruit.
  - A SIBI/gap that sits between the wick low and wick high can be used as the entry reference ("I see a gap, so I'm putting something in there because I'm pretty confident that we're not going up here"). When the second wick forms higher, "that confirms to me that now I have obsidian".
  - Anytime price keeps tapping inside the zone it is "building a false consolidation" that bull-flag traders will buy, and it will deny them. Bodies later respect the wick's CE.
  - Requirement: you have to be right on the direction/narrative, on side; "otherwise this isn't going to work in your hands".
- **How to identify:** (1) bearish narrative from the daily chart; (2) two opposing wicks (an upper wick and a lower wick) with the later one higher; (3) mark CE of each and extend right; (4) price closes below, then retraces into the zone without bodies above its upper CE.
- **How to use:** short and add inside the zone; first target the low-hanging fruit projection (30,137.50 in the example); add more if price reaches the upper quadrant of the 7–9 dealing range; partial at lows and the first relative equal lows; lower stop as price drops. Entries were by market orders.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, a Monday: after a Judas-style run up that swept the Friday morning buy-side pool, two opposing wicks formed (wick high above, wick low below, the second higher), a FVG sat between them anchored to the 7–9 range CE; he shorted inside, price dropped below a BISI whose close (253.75) validated an iFVG one tick under the gap high (254.00; leading digits not clear in the captions), bodies could not touch it on retests, then partials at the lows, relative equal lows and the NWOG upper quadrant; the final order rested just above 30,137.50.

### Take one PD array at a time ("marrying the vein"); daily premium PD arrays that built the bearish narrative  (aliases: narrative, daily PD arrays)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Last week he gave two specific daily premium PD arrays: relative equal highs (which price cleared) and a wick (premium array). Take one array at a time: it's easy to say "we're going to all-time highs"; but price has to "prove itself" at the next array first. "Marrying the vein" (gold-miner expression): don't assume the mother lode because you found a few flakes.
  - Wick rule for the daily wick: if bullish (heading for the high, relative equal highs, maybe all-time highs) price must be able to post bodies above its midpoint; if it cannot, "that's a problem". Bearish: bodies stay in the lower half. For PDAs below market: the upper half should show discount sensitivity, with no willingness to put bodies in the lower half. "Nobody else has that."
  - A SIBI he drew is not a supply/demand zone: those become stale and want fresh zones; he deals with no expiration dates ("only the futures contract expires").
  - The relation of the three nested arrays (wick high lower than the SIBI candle's low; gap high above the wick but below the candle's high) is "what I talked about last week".
  - Warning to beginners: don't build the idea from will, desire or the thrill/anxiety of demo, funded challenges or live money; "the experience you're going by" is emotion.

### 7:00–9:00 am rule, graded and projected forward; Judas swing after 9:30  (aliases: 7–9 rule, Judas swing, protraction)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Carry the weekend trader round-up's logic into Monday: if 7:00–9:00 is trending, expect initial consolidation/rangebound action in the morning ("Did we not get that? Sure it did."). Grade the 7–9 range high-to-low with quadrants and octants; the 50% is equilibrium; carry the levels forward.
  - Forget the daily wicks; the real story is between the 7:00 and 9:00 bookends.
  - After 9:30 the ideal is a Judas swing, "some measure of protraction higher" where smart money draws the crowd to chase longs: price goes above half of the range (premium) and sweeps a buy-side pool, in this case Friday's morning session high. If a leg out of 7–9 reaches the upper quadrant of the dealing range he would have added more; it did not.
  - He explains why he even cares about old highs: electronic trading hours (overnight Globex/London/Asia) trading needs to be booked as real delivery in RTH, so levels seen overnight may need redelivery in RTH; "we're going to need to see this level be traded to if we're opening up down here at 9:30."
  - Early in the morning price hit the NWOG several times (bearish, a weak showing) and rallied to clear the buy side.
- **Example in this video:** Monday: 7:00 high and 9:00 low graded; price traded down to the NWOG, started protraction higher after 9:00, high at 9:32 (not special, just the high after 9:00), swept Friday's morning high and the SIBI high on the daily, then collapsed.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Take one PD array at a time; don't assume the final target (e.g., all-time highs).
- Wick rule: bearish → bodies in lower half; bullish → bodies must post above the midpoint (else problem); below-market PDA → upper half shows discount sensitivity.
- Obsidian: two opposing wicks, second wick higher (bearish), graded at CE, extended right; build short in the zone between the CEs; needs the right narrative.
- Daily early warning: close below the SIBI low; Monday open without gap and without CE; a few handles above the SIBI high on a daily is permissible.
- 7:00–9:00 trending → consolidation/range initially; grade the 7–9 range; add at the upper quadrant if reached.
- iFVG validated by a close one tick beyond the gap; bodies shouldn't touch it on retests.
- Numbers (NQ): SIBI low 30,157.75; Friday close 30,154.00; SIBI high 30,337.50; Monday high 30,343.00; target 30,137.50 (projection 30,137.00); standard-deviation projection 30,090.00; iFVG close 253.75 vs 254.00.

## Glossary candidates
`Obsidian` — two opposing wicks within a narrative, second wick higher for shorts, graded at CE; the zone between the CEs is where shorts are built.
`Marrying the vein` — his gold-miner phrase for working one PD array at a time.
`Judas swing` — protraction higher after 9:30 that lures longs before a drop.
`Salient` — anchored to a level of the 7–9 dealing range (or other key level).
`PDA 2` — second FVG used in the same sequence, anchored to the 7–9 CE.
`Evolving projection` — fib projection re-anchored as higher highs print.
