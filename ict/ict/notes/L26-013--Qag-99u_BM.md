## Summary
A Saturday review of Friday Aug 28 (NFP, Labor Day long weekend) on NQ. ICT shows how pre-published levels explained the day. The weekly range had TGIF 20% and 30% retracement levels marked in advance. The RTH opening range gap (Thursday 4:14 p.m. settle to Friday 9:30 open) gave a premium array to sell from. Price ran short of the old high without a body close above it, then dropped through the NDOG low to the 20% level, and later to Thursday's first presented FVG and the 30% level. The late-morning pullback was explained by the lunch macro, with 11:30 as the latest time it should start. He explains why he abstained from or managed risk tightly on NFP Friday, and that on a normal day he would have shorted the iFVG and pyramided. The second half is a long encouragement and mindset talk to new students: do not follow him, do not rush, predict rather than react, and track what he teaches against what the market does. It is mostly non-technical.

## Concepts
### TGIF (Thank God It's Friday) — weekly range retracement  (aliases ICT uses: "TGIF", "20 to 30% of the weekly range")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When a week has a lot of range, or moves in one direction, price tends to retrace 20–30% of that weekly range late in the week. Draw a fib from the weekly low to the weekly high and mark the 20% and 30% levels. The retracement can begin as early as 1:00 p.m. ET Thursday or in the later stages of Friday trading. There is no single specific time, so you must know what direction the market should be going to agree with that retracement.
- **How to identify:** One-directional or large-range week. Fib from the week's low to its high (for a bearish retracement; the reverse for an up retracement) with 20% and 30% levels annotated. He also keeps a weekly candle on the right-hand side of the chart to see the week's high, low, open and close.
- **How to use:** Use 20% and 30% as retracement targets or reaction zones on Thursday afternoon and Friday. He publicly tweeted "TGIF" early Friday before NFP and 9:30. He says he has a separate short video on TGIF on his channel.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, week ending Fri Aug 28. 20% level = 29,549.00 and 30% level = 29,471.25. After NFP, price dropped through the low and hit the 20% level "handsomely" on the number. Later in the day it fell to Thursday's first presented FVG low and the 30% level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:00](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=0) — TGIF set-up with the weekly candle; [0:00:49](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=49) — the 20–30% retracement rule and its timing window; [0:01:37](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=97) — the two price levels; [0:18:32](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=1112) — 30% hit, tweeted in advance.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Given as a tendency, not a trigger. Direction still has to come from your read of what the market should do.

### Regular trading hours (RTH) opening range gap  (aliases: "opening range gap", "RTH ORG")
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** The gap between the RTH settlement (4:14 p.m. ET the previous day) and the 9:30 a.m. ET open the next day. This is not the electronic overnight data, which he distinguishes from regular trading hours. The 9:30 open price is the high of the gap (here a gap-down), the prior close is the other edge, and the midpoint is the consequent encroachment (CE).
- **How to identify:** Mark Thursday's 4:14 p.m. ET RTH settlement, mark Friday's 9:30 opening price, and shade between them (he used bright yellow).
- **How to use:** If price trades back inside the gap, use it as a premium array on the way up, expecting premium-sensitive selling. The expected path is a run up into the 9:30 opening price (the high of the gap), then a move lower that takes out the CE. The gap's high edge acts as resistance.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Aug 28. Thursday RTH settlement = 29,521.25 (4:14 p.m.). Friday 9:30 open sits above it. After price traded back below the low formed in the NDOG, he looked for a run up into the 9:30 open and then a move lower through the CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:17](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=437) — Thursday 4:14 p.m. ET settle and 9:30 Friday open explained; [0:08:05](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=485) — shades the gap and defines it; [0:08:53](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=533) — premium-array expectation, CE, targets.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says it is "inside the gap", so it uses that area as a premium array. Tied to his conviction after the low was taken out. The 4:14 settlement time is as heard in the captions (caption slightly unclear).

### NDOG used as a liquidity reference (new day opening gap)  (aliases: "orange box")
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** The low that price ran through on NFP "formed randomly" inside Thursday's new day opening gap (the orange box on his chart). Price took that low out and then continued lower.
- **How to use:** Marked in advance, the NDOG low was a visible liquidity point.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Thursday's NDOG low taken on NFP after the 8:30 move, then price kept falling toward the 20% retracement level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:19](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=919) — low formed in Thursday's NDOG, and price "delivered to the tick".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Mentioned as proof that the pre-marked reference levels explain price; no new rule.

### First Presented FVG (FPFVG) of Mondays / Fridays / days  (aliases: "first presented fair value gap")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The first FVG formed after 9:30 ET is the "first presented" FVG of the day. He tracks Monday's and Friday's first presented FVGs for the last two weeks. The market "many times" makes significant intraday or daily highs at them, and sometimes a weekly or monthly high. He asks students not to take his word for it but to keep records.
- **How to use:** Keep the prior two weeks of Monday and Friday FPFVGs on the chart as reference levels for reversals and targets. You can also keep several separate layouts: one for FPFVGs only, one for NWOG only, one for NDOG only, one for RTH opening range gaps. How you split them is up to you.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Friday Aug 28: the first FVG after 9:30 formed on the rally up. Price failed to take the highs of the wicks. Thursday's (Aug 27) FPFVG low was later a target on the way down. During the lunch-macro pullback, price rallied into a prior Friday's FPFVG ("last Friday's", caption unclear which Friday) and then dropped.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:16](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=196) — Friday's first presented FVG formed; [0:13:41](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=821) — Monday/Friday FPFVGs of the last two weeks marking highs; [0:14:28](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=868) — layout suggestions.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The claim "many times" is a tendency.

### Run on liquidity does not need to take the wicks  (aliases: "run on old high / old higher low")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** At 8:30 the wicks rallied above the short-term high without a candle body closing above it. After 9:30, price rallied again and failed to take the highs of the wicks. A run on liquidity or an old high does not necessarily need to take out the wicks. If the bodies of the recent candles are significantly higher than the close of the earlier candle, that can be enough (caption partly unclear).
- **How to identify:** Compare bodies, not just wicks, against the old high.
- **How to use:** Do not insist on a wick-through of every old high before treating the liquidity as taken. But on NFP Friday he still did not trust a short here, for the event-risk reason below.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 8:30 NFP: wicks poked above the short-term high without a body above it; the post-9:30 rally again fell short of those wick highs.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:29](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=149) — 8:30 wicks above, no body above; [0:03:16](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=196) — rally fails to take the wick highs and the "run does not need the wicks" idea.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as something "taught in the past".

### NFP / event-day risk: abstain or reduce  (aliases: "don't trade Mondays and Fridays" joke)
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** He normally abstains from trading NFP Thursday and Friday. On this NFP Friday before a long weekend he did not trust shorting the inversion FVG or the immediate rebalance, as he did not want to be caught in a quick run-up that took out the highs. If it had not been NFP day, he says he would have shorted the inversion FVG, the immediate rebalance, and pyramided into more entries on the SIBI-type bounce that later became an inversion FVG. The "don't trade Mondays and Fridays" line is a running joke of naysayers. He used his model and a few factors rather than blanket rules.
- **How to identify:** NFP day, long weekend, price near a short-term high with unresolved buy-side above.
- **How to use:** Respect event risk; either stand aside or wait for a clean confirmation (here, the low being taken out) before engaging.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Aug 28. Price rallied into a short-term high after 9:30, creating an inversion FVG and immediate rebalance he skipped. Only after the low was taken out did he treat it as a market maker sell model that would not run up to take out the buy side at 29,704.50 (his words: "that's not weakness. That's knowing what I want to see").
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:05](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=245) — why he did not trust shorting on NFP / long weekend, and what he would have done on a normal day; [0:05:42](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=342) — MMSM conviction once the low is taken, buy side 29,704.50.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Says he is teaching students to respect risk on NFP Friday, so he should behave the same way himself.

### Lunch macro (11:30 latest start) and the 10:00-hour first high  (aliases: "lunch macro")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** 11:30 a.m. ET is the latest time at which you should anticipate a delayed lunch macro starting. At that time price tends to pull back into the range built earlier. To find the target: take the 10:00 hour (10:00–11:00), and from the 11:00 line scan left to the first high you hit inside the 10:00 hour. That high is what the lunch macro retracement reaches. Order flow: if bullish, price stays in the upper half of the PD array / inefficiency and the bodies stay in the upper half. In this example, price did not reach the full high and respected the midpoint (CE) of the area before dropping.
- **How to identify:** (1) Draw a box across the 10:00 hour; (2) from 11:00 go left to the very first high in that hour; (3) check time is about 11:30; (4) check bodies are respecting the upper half.
- **How to use:** Used to time a short and to place a stop just above the first 10:00-hour high; or to expect a retracement, then continuation to lower targets (here 30% of the weekly range).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 28: price traded lower into 11:30 without retracing; at 11:30 it pulled up into the first high of the 10:00 hour (where his short was taken and covered) and into a prior Friday's FPFVG, then dropped to the 30% level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:18](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=678) — 11:30 lunch macro latest start, go back in the 10:00 hour; [0:12:06](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=726) — blue box 10:00–11:00 and "first high to the left" method; [0:16:09](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=969) — upper half order flow and the 11:30 repeat; [0:20:07](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=1207) — "at 11:30 it's got to start following its script".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is the clearest statement of the "first high of the 10:00 hour" lunch-macro target in this batch; ICT specifies 11:30 as the last line, not the standard start.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- TGIF retracement = 20–30% of the weekly range; can begin 1:00 p.m. ET Thursday through late Friday. Levels in this video: 29,549.00 (20%) and 29,471.25 (30%).
- RTH settlement = 4:14 p.m. ET (as stated); RTH open = 9:30 a.m. ET; the gap between them is the RTH opening range gap. Thursday settle 29,521.25.
- Lunch macro: latest expected start 11:30 a.m. ET; target = first high found scanning left from 11:00 inside the 10:00 hour.
- On NFP Thursday/Friday (and long weekends) he normally stands aside; if trading, he waits for the low to be taken before trusting the sell model.
- Buy side to respect for the bearish model that day: 29,704.50.
- Keep Monday and Friday FPFVGs of the last two weeks, NWOG, NDOG, and RTH ORG on the chart; use separate layouts if it gets busy.
- Do not take his word for it: record what he teaches and compare with the chart.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Predict, do not react ([0:24:10](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=1450)): reacting means you do not know when something will happen, which is fear of missing the setup. He says he has no fear of missing a trade because price is scripted.
- He will not put students into live trades to copy ([0:24:57](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=1497)): he wants independent traders, not followers, and tells students to learn what they need and move on.
- Study, do not rush ([0:58:08](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=3488)): you cannot speed up the process; demanding success by a deadline only lengthens it. Let experience come organically.
- Keep a record of what he teaches versus how the market delivers ([0:57:19](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=3439)); ask the right questions, such as why critics never address his calls made before the fact.
- Trading real money is never advised by him; he stresses that his content is free and not trade signals ([0:29:44](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=1784)).
- Long non-technical stretch from about 0:26 to 0:57: reputation, critics, faith and life advice, motivation beyond money (not trading content). The only trading-relevant theme is his closing view that how you behave and manage money matters as much as winning, because losing a large account is hard to recover from ([0:49:13](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=2953)).

## Glossary candidates
`TGIF` — retracement of 20–30% of the week's range late Thursday/Friday after a big or one-directional week.
`RTH opening range gap` — gap between prior day's RTH close (4:14 p.m. ET settle) and 9:30 a.m. open, with its CE as pivot.
`First presented FVG` — the first FVG formed after 9:30 ET; Monday's and Friday's of the last two weeks used as reference levels.
`Lunch macro` — late-morning retracement window; latest start 11:30 ET; target first high in the 10:00 hour.
`Pyramiding` — adding further entries at later PD arrays (iFVG, immediate rebalance, bounce) in the same direction.
`Immediate rebalance` — price quickly trading back into a freshly formed gap/inversion to fill it before continuing (as used here, caption slightly unclear).
