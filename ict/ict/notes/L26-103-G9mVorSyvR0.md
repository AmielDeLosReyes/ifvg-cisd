## Concepts

### Suspension Block  (aliases: breaker)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** An anchor point for identifying the highest probability liquidity pool when price is moving toward premium. The block itself (formed by high, low, higher-high, low-below-first-low) validates as bearish structurally but serves as reference frame for future DOL targets. Comparing suspension block relationships between continuous contract vs delivery month contracts reveals different degree targets.
- **How to identify:** High, low, higher high (breach check), low breaks below first low = bearish breaker validated.
- **How to use:** When bullish and moving toward premium, use it as anchor to project higher targets. Don't obsess over one target; grade them higher and higher as market gives feedback that move is bullish.
- **Example in this video:** NQ June contract suspension block with high at 25,715 (delivery month) vs 25,495 (continuous). PPI news on April 14 at 8:30 ET triggered drop that took support, then ripped to 25,715 target. Reached by ~10:10 ET.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:29](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=89) suspension block intro, [0:04:42](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=282) PPI low marked, high then targeted, [0:10:23](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=623) breaker relationship shown.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Don't rely on one target—read market feedback and escalate targets as move confirms bullishness; continuous vs delivery month differences are degrees of liquidity, not conflicting signals.

### TGIF (Thank God It's Friday)  (aliases: weekly retracement, Thursday pull-back)
- **Kind:** rule + entry model
- **What ICT says:** Algorithmically, on Thursday around 1 p.m. ET, price tends to retrace 20–30% of the weekly range (sometimes to 40% as extreme outlier). It's tied to the highest value-high reached by 1 p.m. Thursday. If 20–30% retracement doesn't occur by Friday close, watch for it to occur on the following Monday. The retracement is real and measurable via Fibonacci settings (50 level, change to 30 for 30%, or 20 for 20%).
- **How to identify:** Weekly candlestick stretched one direction. Mark the high by Thursday 1 p.m. ET. Measure range. At 20–30% level, look for retracement candle that opens inside the range, trades down (if bullish week), then closes above mid-range but below the high.
- **How to use:** On Thursday approaching 1 p.m. ET, be ready for retracement into that level; this is a normal, repeating signature, not trade entry per se, but a place to understand where price will pull back before continuing higher Friday.
- **Example in this video:** Previous week's range measured; this week (starting Sunday evening opening) had already retracted into the prior week's 30% level on Monday, then never returned—showing TGIF delivered (deferred to Monday instead of Thursday) and then rocketed higher. Continued higher all week toward May Fed target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:04](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=964) TGIF intro on weekly, [0:17:41](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1061) 20–30% retracement rule, [0:23:21](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1401) TGIF deferred to Monday shown live.
- **Nuance / caveats:** This is algorithmic market structure, not buying/selling pressure; the signature repeats regardless of trader emotion or news; if you don't see it in a week's close, watch the following Monday open.

### Judas Swing  (aliases: opening drop, opening drawdown)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** On a bullish range (any timeframe), measure the distance from open to the low of that day. Add that same distance to the opening price. That sum is the "smart money accumulation zone" where you can still be a buyer beyond that low without chasing; below that level is chasing. It's the same measurement used in a Judas swing—drop from open, then add that drop to open to get buy-zone.
- **How to identify:** Bullish day candle: opens, immediately trades down, then rallies. Measure open to low. Add that distance to open.
- **How to use:** Entry zone if day is bullish-biased; anything below that zone is overextended/chasing.
- **Example in this video:** Referenced generally for understanding bullish structure; not detailed live example.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:20](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1160) Judas swing rule stated.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Applies to any timeframe if you're reading it with the correct bias (bullish).

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) / Inversion FVG  (aliases: imbalance, inversion gap, discount/premium gap)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** FVG is an imbalance (price gap unmet by bodies). On micro/30s/15s charts, wicks define discount (sell-side) imbalances; bodies in upper half = bullish. Inversion FVG is when price trades above a gap, leaving the gap below as potential support. When bodies stay out of lower half of a FVG, market remains bullish. Don't need to touch midpoint; if body closes in upper half, that's strength.
- **How to identify:** Candlestick gap (wick-defined). Bodies above = bullish inversion. Wicks that don't touch midpoint = strength.
- **How to use:** On retracement, if price approaches FVG but bodies stay above midpoint, that's continuation signal. If body closes above high of FVG, that's continued bullish. Screenshot the range on 1m, then zoom to 30s or 15s to confirm bodies staying in upper half.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: After drop into discount gap (wick-defined), price rallies, bodies stay above midpoint of gap. Lower-timeframe (30s) retest shows bodies not touching midpoint—very strong, continued up. Equilibrium measured via Fib, price touches it, bounces away = bullish. 15s chart shows same signature repeating.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:19](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2419) inversion and gap anatomy, [0:45:25](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2725) bodies closing above midpoint = bullish agreement, [0:46:14](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2774) retracing inside FVG shows strength.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Don't confuse wicks (accept them as discount taken) with bodies (which show real institutional flow); "real point of control" is FVG + wicks, not market profile value area.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE)  (aliases: CE midpoint, wick midpoint)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint of a wick that has been "encroached upon" (price has traded through the gap it represents). Used as a reference level; price behavior around CE midpoint indicates whether buyers or sellers are in control. If price overshoots a wick without touching CE midpoint, that's very strong (no congestion).
- **How to identify:** Wick on chart. Find midpoint visually or via measurement. Note whether price touches it or overshoots it.
- **How to use:** If price drops into a CE zone and bounces away without touching the midpoint, that's institutional rejection (strength). If it trades through midpoint and bodies don't touch, still bullish.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m drop: Wick measured for CE midpoint; price drops but doesn't touch CE midpoint of the wick below the recent low, then bounces. That lack of touch = strength. Repeated on 30s chart.
- **Timestamps:** [0:39:27](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2367) CE midpoint explanation.
- **Nuance / caveats:** It's a reference, not a hard stop; the instruction is "if it overshoots, still watch for inversion."

### Order Flow Reading (Real Institutional Order Flow)  (aliases: candlestick read, body/wick structure, real OOF)
- **Kind:** rule + method
- **What ICT says:** Read price action by candlestick structure alone (open, high, low, close). No indicators, no paid services, no gimmicks needed. Body position (upper half vs lower half of a range) + wick position (overshoot vs touch) tells you where institutions are; bodies closing in upper half of a range = agreement above that level; bodies staying out of lower half of a gap = rejecting that level. This is real order flow, not random buying/selling, and it repeats statistically predictable.
- **How to identify:** Candlestick. Is body in upper or lower half? Does wick touch the gap midpoint? Overshoots?
- **How to use:** Score the market: upper-half body closes = buyers won that price; staying out of lower half = sellers rejected lower level. String these together across 1m, 30s, 15s to confirm bullish or bearish bias.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m → 30s → 15s: Each shows bodies closing above halfway of prior gap, wicks not touching previous support midpoints, bodies rallying up and making higher highs with minimal retracement below those bodies. This is institutional bullish order flow confirmed at every timeframe scale.
- **Timestamps:** [0:44:38](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2678) bodies need to stay out of lower half rule, [0:45:25](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2725) body closes at midpoint = agreement, [0:47:02](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2822) wick rejection at gap midpoint = strength.
- **Nuance / caveats:** This is "visually represented" institutional flow, not volume-weighted; don't confuse it with market profile (which groups price by time at level, irrelevant to directional intent).

### Equilibrium Price  (aliases: midpoint, Fibonacci equilibrium)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Measure a range (low to high). Use Fibonacci (50 level = 50% = midpoint = equilibrium). Price trading above equilibrium = potential inversion (premium setup); trades back to equilibrium = no directional advantage (break-even area). No cost, no indicator needed.
- **How to identify:** Range marked. Fib applied. 50 level is equilibrium.
- **How to use:** If price is above equilibrium and retraces to it, that's a neutral zone; if it bounces away, it's still bullish. If it closes below equilibrium after being above, that's potential shift.
- **Example in this video:** 1m chart: Range from low to high marked, Fib applied, price trades above, retraces to 50 level (equilibrium), bounces. Repeated on 30s; no advantage at equilibrium, but once above it, strength shows in bodies staying above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:38](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2918) Fib 50 level as free equilibrium measure.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Market profile value area high/low is not equivalent; equilibrium is geometric midpoint, not time-weighted price consensus.

### SMT Divergence / Six Sisters  (aliases: cross-market divergence, weak market, strong market)
- **Kind:** rule + context
- **What ICT says:** When NQ, ES, and YM (the three major indices) are not moving in lockstep, you have divergence. The weakest index (making lower lows, lower highs) will have the least retracement and the smoothest move lower (fewest consolidations). The strongest index (making higher highs, higher lows) will have deeper retracements and more time distortion. Use the weak market as short target and the strong market to wait for retracement before shorting (more noise). This is "six sisters"—the behavior of the three averages around each other.
- **How to identify:** Compare the three indices on the same timeframe. Which is making higher highs (strong)? Which is making lower lows (weak)? Which is in between?
- **How to use:** If shorting, short the weak one; take long retracements into shorts on the strong one. If long in a weakening market (ES weak, NQ strong), the weak one (ES) can drag on NQ—use ES weakness as a timing tool to get NQ long when ES bottoms first.
- **Example in this video:** Monday/Tuesday: YM took out low (weaker); NQ made lower low; ES did not take out that low (strongest). This SMT divergence suggests ES can be slow, so use ES levels as delayed-timing confirmation for NQ entries.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:33](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1833) Dow (YM) taking lower low, NQ lower low, ES did not, [0:32:58](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1978) six sisters concept explained, [0:34:39](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2079) weak market smooth moves, strong market more retracements.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Dow can act independently due to different weighting (30 vs 100 vs 500 stocks); use ES/NQ divergence more than YM/NQ.

### Opening Range Gap (NWOG / NDOG)  (aliases: RTH opening range gap, weekly opening gap)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Gaps formed at session opens (electronic open at 6 p.m. ET or 9:30 RTH). If a gap is not closed by near-open action, it becomes a reference level. Regular trading hours (RTH) opening range gaps are marked and tracked for potential fill or reversal.
- **How to identify:** Mark the gap at open. Track whether bodies close the gap or bodies stay above/below.
- **How to use:** If bodies stay above a gap, that's bullish (don't go back for fill quickly); if bodies move into it, that's retracement (potentially weakness).
- **Example in this video:** Sunday evening NQ opening: large premium gap opening (way below previous close). This became NWOG to reference. Monday low retracted into prior week's 30% (TGIF), then never returned to the gap—bodies stayed above, showing no fill intended near-term.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:42](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=282) regular trading hours opening range gap annotated.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Gaps are reference points, not automatic fill targets.

### News-Driven Price Magnets (Red Folder Events)  (aliases: high-impact news, PPI/CPI/NFP)
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** Red folder (high impact) or orange folder (medium impact) news events on economic calendar create obvious price magnets. They polarize the market's high and low formed around the event time. PPI, CPI, non-farm payroll, FOMC are examples. Price is drawn to them like magnets; they mark starting points or pivots.
- **How to identify:** Check economic calendar. Red or orange folder = impactful. Watch the time it's released and the price bars around it.
- **How to use:** If you know a red-folder event is coming, that's an anchor point for subsequent liquidity targets (price will run to a target post-event).
- **Example in this video:** PPI at 8:30 ET on Monday morning created the low (magnet). Price then ripped to the predicted 25,715 high post-PPI.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:32](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=332) red folder news and polarization mentioned.
- **Nuance / caveats:** News is a magnet for swing highs/lows; the liquidity target (25,715) is a separate concept anchored to the suspension block, not the news itself.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Weekly range retracement:** If the week doesn't show 20–30% retracement by Friday close, watch Monday open (TGIF deferred rule).
- **TGIF timing:** Thursday 1 p.m. ET is the start of the retracement window; can extend through Friday close.
- **Judas swing ratio:** Drop from open = add to open = smart-money accumulation zone; below that is chasing.
- **Body rule on 30s/15s/1m:** Once a body closes above the 50% midpoint of a gap, bodies must stay out of the lower half for continuation bullish.
- **Wick rejection:** If a wick doesn't touch the CE midpoint (consequent encroachment), that's strength; overshoot and still don't touch = very strong.
- **Red folder events:** PPI, CPI, NFP, FOMC are high-impact magnets.
- **Opening gap fill:** Gaps opened at 6 p.m. ET (electronic open) or 9:30 ET (RTH open); if bodies stay above/below, fill is delayed or unlikely near-term.
- **Three index correlation:** If any index (NQ, ES, YM) is not moving in lockstep, use the weak one as short target.

## Glossary candidates

`Suspension Block` — An anchor reference point formed by high, low, higher-high, and a low that breaks below the first low; identifies a prior region of institutional activity and helps project DOL targets when price is in premium.

`TGIF` — Thank God It's Friday; the algorithmic weekly retracement pattern (20–30%) that occurs Thursday 1 p.m. ET or deferred to Monday if not complete by Friday close.

`Judas Swing` — On a bullish range, the measurement of open-to-low distance added to open price, defining the smart-money accumulation zone (not chasing territory).

`Fair Value Gap (FVG)` — An imbalance (unmet price level) often identified by wicks on micro timeframes; inversion FVG is when price trades above the gap, leaving it below as support.

`Consequent Encroachment (CE)` — The midpoint of a wick; used as a reference for institutional acceptance/rejection of a price level.

`Order Flow (Real OOF)` — Institutional price action read purely from candlestick structure (body position in range, wick behavior) without indicators or volume data.

`Equilibrium` — The 50% Fibonacci midpoint of a marked range; a neutral zone offering no directional advantage.

`SMT Divergence / Six Sisters` — The behavior of the three major indices (NQ, ES, YM) when they are not in lockstep; identifies the weak, medium, and strong markets for entry/exit timing.

`Opening Range Gap (NWOG/NDOG)` — A gap formed at the 6 p.m. ET (electronic) or 9:30 ET (RTH) session open; tracked for potential fill or support/resistance function.

`Magnet (News-driven)` — A price level or high/low formed around a red-folder (high-impact) news event (PPI, CPI, NFP, FOMC) that serves as a structural anchor.
