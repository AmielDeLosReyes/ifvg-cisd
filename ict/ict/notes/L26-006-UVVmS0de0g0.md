## Concepts

### Regular Trading Hours (RTH) Opening Range Gap
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The gap between Friday's 4:14 PM ET close (RTH settlement) and Monday's 9:30 AM ET open (RTH resume). This gap is the primary structural reference for intraday trading. Must be graded (divided into 8ths and 4ths with Fibonacci) to identify key levels (octant, quadrant, half, three-quarter closure points).
- **How to identify:** On daily chart with RTH only (not electronic hours): mark the close at 4:14 PM previous Friday; mark the open at 9:30 AM current trading day. The void between is the RTH opening range gap.
- **How to use:** Use Fibonacci to divide the gap into 8 equal points (octants). Lowest octant and lowest quadrant are failure points (if price can't even touch these, it's weak). Use standard deviations of the gap width for downside targets; -1 and -2 SD levels below the gap define key support zones.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opened lower on 09/28 (discount gap). Lowest quadrant and octant levels were identified around 18430 area. Two SD of the gap drop to ~18370 area, aligning with Thursday's daily low at 18370.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:36](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=396) "discount regular trading hours opening range gap," [0:07:26](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=446) "grade that by simply having the fib on it," [0:26:08](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1568) standard deviations explained.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Do not confuse with "opening range" (first 30 minutes of session, 9:30–10:00). RTH opening range gap is overnight gap only, not intraday.

### Standard Deviations of Gap Projections
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Use the width of the RTH opening range gap (high to low) as a unit. Extend one and two standard deviations below the low of the gap to project downside targets. These levels often align with prior daily liquidity (e.g., Thursday's low), making them high-probability targets.
- **How to identify:** Measure gap high to gap low. Subtract that distance once (-1 SD) and twice (-2 SD) from the gap low.
- **How to use:** Expect price to run toward -1 SD first; if that fails, -2 SD becomes the ultimate target. If both align with daily structure (e.g., daily low), conviction is high.
- **Example in this video:** RTH opening range gap low ~18430; gap width ~60 points. -1 SD projects to ~18370; -2 SD projects to ~18310. Thursday's daily low sits at ~18370, confirming the -1 SD as salient.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:08](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1568) "standard deviations and projections," [0:27:43](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1663) "negative one level," [0:28:29](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1709) "two standard deviations."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not taught by other frameworks (volume profile, Elliot Wave, etc.); unique to ICT methodology.

### Macro Times (Spooling Windows)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Algorithmic order clustering at specific times: last 10 minutes of every hour + first 10 minutes of the next hour (e.g., 10:50–11:10 AM ET). Institutions schedule major order flow into these windows to avoid moving the market aggressively. During macro time, price "spools" (elongates, protract, creates volatility) because order flow arrives scheduled, not reactive. Volume doesn't need to be high; one contract can create movement if order flow is timed.
- **How to identify:** Mark 10:50, 11:50, 12:50, 1:50 PM ET, etc., on 1-minute chart.
- **How to use:** Anticipate major price moves into and out of macro times. If first 10 minutes (10:50–11:00) don't show spooling, expect it in the last 10 minutes (11:00–11:10). Use macro time perspective to know when to expect momentum and when to wait.
- **Example in this video:** Market dropped into macro window 10:50–11:10 AM ET, hitting the two-SD target of the RTH opening range gap and Thursday's daily low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:00](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2100) "The market trades down into 1050," [0:35:48](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2148) detailed macro window logic.

### Order Flow (Real Definition vs. Volume Misconception)
- **Kind:** definition (correcting misconception)
- **What ICT says:** Real order flow is NOT the number of buys/sells at a level (that's past execution, not flow). Flow is movement timing: *when* orders are scheduled to arrive on the market (macros, algorithmic clustering). Price movement is caused by order *timing* and the *rate of change*, not by contract count. You only need one contract to create price movement if order flow timing is right.
- **How to identify:** Observe candlestick bodies and wicks; where they close relative to key levels (octant, quadrant, half) indicates institutional intent (whether they're done pushing or continuing).
- **How to use:** Don't count volume; instead, observe body placement. Body closing above/below key level = bullish/bearish intent. Consistent pattern of bodies failing to reach a level = weakness confirmed.
- **Example in this video:** Bodies failing to touch the -2 SD level and the daily low at open signaled weakness (bearish). Later, bodies staying in upper half of wick and below 9:30 open signaled exhaustion of the move upward (time to exit).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Explicitly contradicts volume profile, VSA, footprint analysis, Level 2 data fixation; volume followers are chasing past data, not predicting next data.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) of Daily Wicks
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** On the daily chart, each candlestick has a wick (high-low extremes). The midpoint of that wick is the consequent encroachment. For daily chart support, look for the CE of the prior day's wick (e.g., Thursday's wick CE is a magnet for bounces on Friday and the following session).
- **How to identify:** On daily chart: mark the high and low of a candlestick; divide by 2 to find the midpoint.
- **How to use:** Intraday, if price reaches the daily low, the CE (half-wick) often acts as a secondary target (bounce area or accumulation zone). If body doesn't go below CE, price may consolidate there before continuing.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday's daily low 18370; its wick CE 18456. Intraday, price bounced and accumulated at the wick CE level (showing institutional buying).
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:29](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=149) "midpoint or consequent encroachment," [0:04:09](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=249) CE used as support.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Must be identified on daily chart first; intraday charts then reference these levels.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) Entry Mechanics
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** When a wick extends beyond a gap, grade that wick (high to low) and measure its midpoint. Price trades into that wick's midpoint and bounces. The wick becomes an "inversion FVG" entry zone: buy into the lower half of the wick; expect the bounce to use the gap as a target.
- **How to identify:** Identify a wick that extends into the gap below. Grade the wick (high to low). If price enters the lower half of the wick zone, it's an inversion FVG entry.
- **How to use:** Enter long into the lower half of the inversion wick; stop below wick low; target the gap high or gaps above.
- **Example in this video:** Morning inversion FVG identified in the wick below the opening range. Entry at ~18397; stop below ~18370.
- **Timestamps:** [0:43:01](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2581) "inversion fair value gap," [0:47:55](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2875) entry execution "not sexy, but it fits the criteria."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Differs from standard FVG (which is above price, not below).

### Institutional Referral Entry Drill
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price comes down to a discount zone (lower quadrant, half-wick, or consequent encroachment of a daily chart structure) and bodies accumulate there without closing below, institutions are "drilling" — establishing entries at discounted prices before pushing higher. This accumulation is a buy signal.
- **How to identify:** Price pulls back to a low or support level; candle bodies cluster around the midpoint of that level without closing below; bodies show minor wicks (tight closes).
- **How to use:** When you see this institutional drilling, expect the next push to target higher liquidity. This is a low-risk entry point.
- **Example in this video:** Accumulation at Thursday's wick CE (18456) and at half-wick before final push higher at 11:10 AM macro window.
- **Timestamps:** [0:44:38](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2678) "institutional referral entry drill," [0:49:32](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2972) "institutional order flow entry drill at last Thursday's consequent encouragement."

### Bodies and Wick Placement as Order Flow Signature
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The location where a candle's body closes relative to key levels (octant, half-wick, gap low, etc.) reveals institutional intent. Body below level = weakness. Body above level and closing at upper portion = strength. Bodies clustering around a support = drilling/accumulation. Bodies closing below key support = breakdown imminent.
- **How to identify:** Mark key levels on chart; observe each candlestick's body location relative to those levels.
- **How to use:** Use body placement as confirmation of reversal or continuation; bodies failing to reach expected level = signal to exit or reverse.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- RTH gap (Fri 4:14 PM to Mon 9:30 AM) is primary daily reference.
- Grade gap into octants (1/8) and quadrants (1/4) using Fibonacci.
- Lowest octant and lowest quadrant are failure levels; if bodies can't even touch these, market is weak in that direction.
- Standard deviations: -1 SD and -2 SD of the gap width project downside targets.
- Macro times: 10:50–11:10, 11:50–12:10, 12:50–1:10, 1:50–2:10 PM ET (and other hourly boundaries).
- During macro: expect spooling (price movement) even on low volume; order flow timing is key, not contract count.
- Daily chart low from prior day (e.g., Thursday low) often aligns with -1 SD or -2 SD of gap.
- Daily wick CE (midpoint) is a bounce/accumulation target intraday.
- Bodies must close above/below key levels to confirm direction; bodies stuck in middle = indecision/exhaustion.
- Non-farm payroll weeks are volatile; Monday trading is harder for new traders due to consolidation complexity.
- Narrative (news) typically primes retail opposite to smart money direction.

## Glossary candidates
- **RTH Opening Range Gap** — Gap between previous day's RTH close (4:14 PM) and current day's RTH open (9:30 AM).
- **Octant** — One-eighth division of a gap (using Fibonacci).
- **Quadrant** — One-quarter division of a gap.
- **Standard Deviation (SD)** — Gap width extended as downside projections (-1 SD, -2 SD).
- **Consequent Encroachment (CE)** — Midpoint of a candlestick's wick (high+low)/2; acts as support/resistance.
- **Spooling** — Price movement/elongation caused by algorithmic order clustering at macro times.
- **Macro Time** — Last 10 min of hour + first 10 min of next hour (e.g., 10:50–11:10 AM); algorithmic order window.
- **Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)** — Gap with wick below it; entry into lower half of the wick.
- **Institutional Referral Entry Drill** — Accumulation pattern where bodies cluster at discount prices without closing below.
- **Order Flow (Real)** — Timing and scheduling of institutional orders, not the count of buy/sell contracts.
- **Narrative** — News headlines and retail expectations; often opposite to smart money direction.
