## Concepts

### High Resistance vs Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases: HRLR / LRLR, "high resistance liquidity run conditions")
- **Kind:** definition (re-explained, with visuals) / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - High resistance means the market is *resisting your ability to find efficient, low-risk trades*; it does not mean support/resistance. On the chart (15-minute NQ example, shaded gray) it is a block where every candle's range overlaps the previous candle's range, "a big block of nonsense". It can look like a bull flag; trying to trade it on a 1-minute chart will "cook" you. Treat it like a rattlesnake; learn to recognise it and simply do not touch it.
  - Low resistance is the opposite: price shows inefficiencies (gaps, wicks) and then does something with them. Pattern: small consolidation, inefficiency forms, price trades back down into it, then makes a higher high (or lower low if bearish). It is "porous" price action with "patchwork": areas where a candle's range needs another touch from price (a partial touch counts, e.g. a wick poking a bit above the prior candle's high inside an order block / IOFED).
  - In low resistance with a bearish bias: up-close candles are repelled lower and down-close candles get "eaten into and consumed". You can read this from price alone, no footprint/level 2/3 needed.
  - Larry Williams cycle (credited): markets alternate large ranges to small ranges; small ranges are the precursor to large ranges. In a bearish low-resistance setting, a small retracement inside a SIBI followed by a small candle means large ranges are coming, with short-term lows as targets.
  - Signature to look for when bearish: stretched-out down-close candles ("black limousines" on his color scheme), expanding range size, contracting counter-direction, and unfilled gaps left in the back part of the run (breakaway/continuation/measuring gaps), meaning the market is weak (captions garbled).
- **How to identify:** Overlapping candle ranges with no clean inefficiency to trade from = high resistance. Visible FVGs/volume imbalances that price returns to and then expands from = low resistance.
- **How to use:**
  - In high resistance do not trade the "scale in, add if it pokes outside the bottom of the gap" approach; the odds fall because volatility and chop are high. In low resistance he does that "90% of the time" (scaling in, adding, adding).
  - Use the 15-minute chart as the bellwether to recognise the condition, then go to the 1-minute/sub-1-minute only if conditions are low resistance.
- **Seasonal rhythm he gives (opinion, from experience):** early-year months are the accumulation phase / high resistance; he deliberately measures risk by not participating the first month or two, then dabbles around the first/second Tuesday of February to gauge the tone (do orders come easily? do I feel in sync? are targets met easily?). Participation normally picks up in the second week of February; March should look clearly different. Summer: mostly high resistance with maybe one or two low-resistance days; fall (from the last two weeks of August or early September through the first/second week of December): low resistance; holiday period back to about 50/50.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-minute, Fri Feb 27, 2026: gray-shaded overlapping candles (HRLR) versus a later leg with gaps, a wick, an IOFED/order block, a change in state of delivery and small ranges between gaps (LRLR).

### Volume Imbalance neutralisation rule (daily chart)  (aliases: volume imbalance, VI, "pink/red box")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the gap between two candle *bodies*: one candle's close and the next candle's open, with wicks overlapping it. Annotate them; price trades back and forth through them.
  - He personally stops using a daily volume imbalance once a later daily candle's *body* lays over the entire range (the body must bridge both prices: the first candle's close and the second candle's open). Up or down close does not matter.
  - After that he will not key off it for a new entry or a terminus/target; he may still use it for stop management. It can still be used by price and its gradient levels still matter on a daily chart; it is just no longer given top importance.
  - Toggling the settlement/session setting on the daily chart changes where volume imbalances exist; he picks the setting that shows close-proximity volume imbalances because the market uses them.
- **How to identify:** closing price of candle A vs opening price of candle B (the gap between those prices; wicks may overlap).
- **How to use:** Mark them; use the low (or high) of the daily VI as a draw on liquidity/target (see lunch macro example). Stop using for new entries once a body fully covers it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily, pink/red volume imbalance above price. The Wednesday run took a short-term high and "gave up the ghost"; Friday's buy-side run went to the low of this VI and reversed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:05](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=1025) — what the daily VI is; [0:17:53](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=1073) — neutralised when a body covers the whole range; [1:02:22](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3742) — settlement toggle changes where VIs exist
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Me personally" rule, stated as his practice. Fits his 2026 emphasis on including volume imbalances in gap boundaries.

### Reclaimed FVG vs Inversion FVG  (aliases: reclaimed fair value gap, iFVG, reclaimed BISI)
- **Kind:** refinement / distinction
- **What ICT says:**
  - An inversion FVG forms when price trades through a gap of the opposite character (a down-close-candle gap, SIBI, that price goes above, or an up-close-candle gap that price goes below). A *reclaimed* gap is a bullish BISI that price traded through to the downside (or the CE) and then rides back above: it is not inverted; it changes its character but stays bullish (if the same gap had formed on a down-close candle it would be an inversion).
  - Up-close-candle gaps that price trades down through need time and patience for price to get back above.
  - Once above, bodies should respect the upper half. A wick can poke one or two ticks below; wait to see whether the body gets back in the upper half while the candle forms.
  - Setup logic before 9:00 Fri: an "orange inversion FVG" formed overnight; if price got above it, it could act as a run (discount array) toward the high liquidity above.
- **How to use:** In low resistance conditions he buys/scales/adds into that reclaimed gap; in high resistance conditions he doesn't (see HRLR). He says the approach works in either condition if combined with the narrative and time, but odds are poor in HRLR.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Feb 27, before 9:00 ET: gap at the lows expected to turn into a run up to the buy-side high if price traded above it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:45:40](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2740); [0:46:27](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2787) — reclaimed vs inversion; [0:47:13](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2833) — upper-half bodies, scaling in 90% of the time in LRLR; [0:48:00](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2880) — don't do it in HRLR

### RTH Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG) preparation and 70% midpoint rule  (aliases: opening range gap, RTH settlement, discount/premium gap)
- **Kind:** rule / procedure
- **What ICT says:**
  - Reference price: the previous day's RTH settlement, the 4:14 PM ET close (he also notes you can use the 5:00 PM ET settlement; taught last year). Toggle to the RTH chart session; the gap only appears once the first RTH print at 9:30 occurs.
  - Pre-market routine: note the 4:14 PM settlement (after the close or first thing in the morning); about 30 minutes before the open (around 9:00) compare where price is. If price is well below, label the settlement price RTH ORG high in advance (it is highly unlikely to trade above it before 9:30); you are in for a discount opening range gap (open below the settlement). Above it is a premium gap. He writes "RTH ORG settlement" on his notepad.
  - Once the 9:30 open prints, drag the fib between the open and the settlement price: 50% is the CE.
  - Statistical idea: there is a 70% likelihood price trades to the midpoint of the gap between 9:30 and 10:00. There will be days it never goes there (opens and just drops/goes up). He frames it as a probability inside a narrative, not a guarantee, comparable to his 70% "Tuesday London session makes the weekly high (if bearish) or low (if bullish), else Wednesday" rule.
  - Full gap closure is always possible, but the easier objective is usually the closer liquidity.
  - He calls "too clean" a high that sat one tick above the CE of the gap (nothing else to compare it to on the chart), visible by eye on the price axis without drawing the line.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Feb 27: at about 8:40 price far below Thursday's 4:14 PM settlement, labelled ORG high in advance; the 9:30 open was at a big discount; a high that lined up with the CE (one tick above the CE) printed at about 10:00 (he says he was 2 minutes off in timing), then price entered a messy consolidation.

### Gradient Levels of the RTH Opening Range Gap ("time and price multiplication table")  (aliases: gradient levels, eighths and quarters of the opening gap)
- **Kind:** new coinage / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Grade the opening range gap (9:30 open to the 4:14 PM settlement) with the fib at the eights (octants) and quarters. Every gradient level is a potential PD array: "that's your order block, breaker, IOFED, propulsion block, Reaper, FVG, first resistance FVG." The hard part (which OB, which FVG, which liquidity pool) goes away because you have built a "multiplication table, the schematic based on time and price".
  - Can also be done with a new week opening gap or new day opening gap.
  - He says he hid this for a long time to avoid others repackaging it; he stresses it is still "elementary" in his toolkit.
  - The level the first presented gap sits on gives validation: on Friday a single-candle BISI lay on the 0.375 level, which validated it as a legitimate entry toward the buy-side liquidity / daily volume imbalance.
  - He calls this "anticipating" (knowing where a gap will form) rather than predicting or reacting.
- **How to identify / use:** Draw fib on the RTH ORG; mark 0.125 increments and quarters; look for FVGs/BISIs/SIBIs that sit on those levels; trade those toward the time-and-price objective (see lunch macro).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Feb 27, 11:30 entering lunch: single-candle BISI on 0.375 gradient level; pyramided on cheaper prices; targeted the buy side above and the low of the daily volume imbalance; position closed after the run to that VI low; figures quoted are unclear in the caption (he mentions "just shy of 9 grand").

### Lunch Macro (11:30–1:30) and the "multiplication table"  (aliases: lunch macro, time first then price, 10:00 low objective)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model component
- **What ICT says:**
  - Lunch macro window is 11:30 to 1:30 PM ET (2 hours). 11:30–noon is the start; 1:30–2:00 is the PM session opening range, which sets the stage for 2:00–close; the lunch macro must end at 1:30. (Elsewhere in the transcript a "10:30 to 1:30" macro box is drawn: caption unclear; in L26-113 he allows lunch effects to start as early as 10:30.)
  - If the morning ran up into noon, the lunch macro runs back to the low that formed at 10:00 or the first low after 10:00 (the mirror for a morning decline: the high after 10:00).
  - Think of it as a multiplication table: horizontal axis = time, vertical = price; the algorithm goes back across time to a specific price; "two points of reference where price and time agree". You must start with time.
  - It does not care about your stop size or whether you are in a trade; it takes the liquidity below that low and neighbouring lows.
  - Trending day variant: do not use the very first low after 10:00. Start at 11:30 (first 30 minutes of lunch = "opening range for lunch hours", which starts the look-back) and look back for the most prominent low toward 10:00. The lunch macro is then a turtle soup from the current price down to that low, and he would be done / wait for the PM session.
  - Consolidation day: use the first low after 10:00.
  - Homework: block out 11:30–1:30 on every day of old data and record what forms and where.
- **How to identify:** 10:00 marker, first swing low/high after 10:00 (or most prominent one on a trend day), lunch window box 11:30–1:30.
- **How to use:** Expect the retracement to run that liquidity during lunch; he also expects it to finish right before 1:30.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Fri Feb 27: morning drive up to the low of the daily volume imbalance (taking relative equal highs), then lunch macro dropped to the first low after 10:00 and ran it out right before 1:30 (he jokes "probably random"), followed by a small pool run up and then dull PM price action.

### Weekly profile: TGIF retracement  (aliases: TGIF, Friday retracement, weekly range 40%/60%)
- **Kind:** rule / numbers
- **What ICT says:** On Friday price usually retraces 20–30% of the weekly range. If the week is a consolidation week, expect more: a 40% retracement of the weekly range (highest high to lowest low) back toward the middle/median is the minimum, 60% the extreme. Reason for 40 rather than 20–30: in consolidation the pull back to the middle can be more exaggerated. Retracement measured when price has been moving lower Wednesday–Friday (or Thursday–Friday, or just Friday).
- **How to use:** Don't treat a Friday bullish first FVG as a long entry in a bearish narrative; expect the run to the TGIF level to use the liquidity (relative equal lows are taken first, then up). Context that day: geopolitical uncertainty ahead of Sunday's open ("nobody wants to be in a position"), so the algorithm won't sink or rally price strongly; it goes back to the middle of the range and lets Sunday's open play out.
- **Timestamps:** [0:38:30](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2310) — 20–30% usual, 40% in consolidation; [0:40:13](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2413) — 40% minimum, 60% extreme; [0:42:32](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2552) — why a middle-of-range delivery into the weekend

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 30 minutes (9:30–10:00); 15 minutes is not it; first 60 minutes gives more clarity.
- RTH settlement = 4:14 PM ET (or 5:00 PM ET alternative); RTH ORG CE = 50%; 70% likelihood of trading to CE between 9:30 and 10:00.
- Macro 9:50–10:10 ET (20-minute window).
- Lunch macro 11:30–1:30 ET; 1:30–2:00 PM session opening range; PM session trading 2:00–close.
- TGIF retracement: 20–30% of weekly range normally; 40% minimum / 60% extreme in a consolidation week.
- Tuesday London session of the week creates the weekly high (bearish) or low (bullish) 70% of the time; otherwise Wednesday.
- Daily VI neutralised once a later body covers the full range (close of candle A to open of candle B).
- Gradient levels: eighths and quarters of the RTH ORG; example 0.375.
- Don't trade HRLR; in LRLR scale in and add (90% of the time).
- Charts always on New York local time.
- Seasonal: Jan–Feb funk, participation up second week of Feb, summer mostly HRLR, fall (late Aug/early Sept to early/mid Dec) LRLR, holidays about 50/50.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Do the work yourself: take trades you found and trust ([0:53:32](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3212)). He will not tell you when to buy/sell, set stops, or manage your position (not licensed; wants you to own results) ([0:50:22](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3022)).
- Study approach: start from his newest videos and work backwards ([0:50:22](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3022)); clips cut from his lectures give only fragments.
- Anticipate, do not predict or react: anticipating means you know what is going to happen from the framework; guessing and reacting (waiting for price to prove itself) are not the same ([1:43:45](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=6225)).
- "Time first, then price"; you can trade the chop based on time even if you can't trade every swing ([1:44:32](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=6272)).
- Four-question checklist he gives: what makes you bullish or bearish; what time of day will you trade; what are the characteristics for that time; which PD array will you use, and are you targeting inefficiencies or old highs/lows ([1:46:08](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=6368)).

## Glossary candidates
`HRLR (high resistance liquidity run conditions)` — overlapping, inefficient price action that resists efficient low-risk trades.
`LRLR (low resistance liquidity run conditions)` — price action with inefficiencies that are revisited and then expanded from.
`Patchwork` — price returning to areas where a candle's range needs another touch.
`Gradient levels` — eighths/quarters of the RTH opening range gap used as time-and-price PD levels.
`RTH ORG settlement` — the 4:14 PM ET prior-day settlement used as the opening range gap high or low.
`Multiplication table (time and price)` — his analogy for the algorithm finding where time and price intersect (time on one axis, price on the other).
`Black limousines` — long down-close candles on his chart color scheme (up-close are green).
`TGIF` — Friday retracement of the weekly range.
