## Summary
On a muddy Monday morning (Sept 23, 2024) price spends the whole 9:30–10:35 window stuck inside gaps and wicks, and ICT uses it to teach how to recognise "time distortion" and why not trading is the correct decision. His tells: price keeps spending time inside inefficiencies (FVGs, wicks, the unfilled part of the opening range gap) instead of leaving them; a PD array is touched without a displacement off it; the same gap's consequent encroachment gets hit twice; and the wicks are not adjacent so precise levels cannot be drawn. He sits through the morning, says a profit that day would be luck, and states the Silver Bullet at 10:00 as the better time to use the first presented FVG. Along the way he reviews an earlier short entered from the CE of a wick with an iFVG, the "chicken leg" idea of not needing the exact high/low, and the sequence he wants Caleb to trade (buy side taken, then sell side taken, then a rally using an FVG/iFVG). Capital preservation is framed as the first lesson.

## Concepts

### Time distortion — recognising a held / strangled market  (aliases ICT uses: time distortion, "held", "strangled", muddy, dirty, holding pattern)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price spending too much time inside inefficiencies (any gap, FVG, wick, the unfilled part of an opening range gap) is the signature that the market is being held — it is not permitted to run yet.
  - He wants to see price spend time ABOVE old highs (accumulating new longs) or BELOW old lows (accumulating new shorts), but not inside inefficiencies.
  - Buying and selling pressure exists but price barely moves; it is kept "artificially" in a tight range; when the range is released the move is cleaner and more sustainable, so the afternoon session should be good.
  - Trading ahead of the release gets you beat up ("chopped up"); "the better you recognise when the market is being held, the better your trading".
  - Signs listed: too many wicks that are not adjacent, so he cannot draw a clean gray (precision) level; a PD array that is only touched with no displacement; the market that "looks like a technical day" turning into a holding pattern; a medium-impact news driver at 9:45 (Flash Manufacturing PMI) causing a flurry but no meaningful run to highs or lows followed by a rejection.
  - With muddy morning probabilities he elects to wait until 10:00 (Silver Bullet starts) and use the first presented FVG then.
- **How to identify:** price repeatedly re-entering the same gap; overlapping candles; small ranges; many equal-ish wicks; he says it can look like consolidation inside a range between two obvious highs/lows.
- **How to use:** do not press; stand aside; use 10:00 Silver Bullet; revisit in the afternoon. He tells viewers to screenshot the chart and label it "time distortion and very difficult morning session conditions".
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/15s Monday Sept 23 9:30–10:35 ET: price chops inside last week's gaps and the unfilled lower part of a premium opening range gap; it clears the early highs, then consolidates under and around them; at 10:31 it is still ranging between the same high and low.

### Displacement as the confirmation signature at a PD array  (aliases ICT uses: "tips its hand", signature, "demanding" displacement)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** having a line or rectangle at a PD array is not enough; there has to be displacement off the level — a signature that "tips its hand". Typical order: a run on stops (liquidity) before price trades to the PD array, then trade at the PD, then drop hard or rally away. Then wait for the fair value gap or order block formed by that move to trade back. Demanding this keeps you out of choppy trades.
- **How to identify:** body closes away from the array with a gap; not just a wick or a touch.
- **How to use:** no entry until the displacement; he says that is why he is not in a trade while price touches arrays.
- **Example in this video:** the tiny first FVG of the day (about a quarter point, overlapping an old new-day opening gap) is not a trade unless price shows displacement off it, trading above it and treating it as a discount array.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:46](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=466) displacement off a level is the signature · [0:10:14](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=614) small first FVG overlapping an old NDOG needs displacement · [0:32:29](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1949) demanding displacement after trade to the array saves you from chop
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** consistent with his earlier PD-array teaching; here framed as a no-trade filter.

### Gap time-in-inefficiency rule: hit the CE once, then leave  (aliases ICT uses: consequent encroachment should not be violated, "second time to CE", repeating phenomenon)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (possible mini-model)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After price reaches the consequent encroachment of a gap (here the first presented FVG of the day), it should leave it; a second trip back to the CE is a failure sign: "it should have hit it once and then left" and "you never want to see that."
  - For a bullish read: price can trade down into the upper half, touch the CE and even wick through it, but it is better if the lower portion stays open.
  - For the short he had framed (gray box): if price gets the CE and leaves, the upper half of that gap should not be traded to again, and the CE should not be retraded, until the low (sell-side) is taken. If it does come back, the trade is unreliable. He tells viewers to record this in the journal — "a repeating phenomenon that could be a model in and of itself".
  - Never want price staying inside any gap, inefficiency or wick.
  - An inverted gap (a line/rectangle over an FVG that price has closed through) should act as support when price is above it; price should get back above it and keep the CE intact.
- **How to identify:** count touches of the CE after the first reaction; track whether the target low got traded to before the second touch.
- **How to use:** a second CE visit before the target = skip or exit; it saved him from a short that failed.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m Sept 23: the gray short box in the first FVG traded down but failed when price made a second tag of the CE; he had said it would likely fail because it was spending too much time.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:34](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2914) second attempt at CE is unwelcome · [0:51:00](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3060) CE should not be violated; lower portion open · [0:59:29](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3569) upper half / CE shouldn't be traded to until the low is taken, journal this
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he does not formalize it as a named model; it is presented as an observation worth journaling.

### Low-resistance liquidity run via volume imbalance after a premium retracement  (aliases ICT uses: "low resistance liquidity run", volume imbalance between bodies, purple premium box)
- **Kind:** entry model (framework described, not triggered)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Preferred sequence (before the low gets taken): price breaks below a level and leaves a small gap between candle bodies (volume imbalance); the next candle closes/opens, price trades back up into the volume imbalance and the bottom of the purple shaded premium array, then reprices lower into the low. That is "a very, very low resistance liquidity run setup".
  - He prefers it when the liquidity (the low) has not yet been taken, because it is then "coming back up into a premium"; it still delivered after the low was taken, but is less preferred.
  - Description: high-frequency trading setups you can study, tape read and demo-trade.
- **How to identify:** break of a low with a body-to-body gap, then a retrace into the gap/premium array.
- **How to use:** short from the premium array (volume imbalance + bottom of the purple box) targeting the untaken low.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/15s Sept 23 morning: levels from last Thursday's data (a gray box = Thursday's first presented FVG, purple box = a premium array/gap from Thursday); price took the short-term low first, which removed the preferred setup.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=358) the framework described · [0:07:46](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=466) high-frequency setups to study · [0:08:35](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=515) premium gap already returned to midpoint
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** the term "high/low resistance liquidity run" appears in his canonical list; here he only mentions the low-resistance variant while explaining he did not get his setup.

### Opening range gap — unfilled portion as the draw (premium gap)  (aliases ICT uses: unfilled portion of the opening range gap, mid gap, new day opening gap)

### First presented FVG and Silver Bullet (10:00) on difficult mornings  (aliases ICT uses: first presented fair value gap, Silver Bullet, "wait till 10:00")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** on a muddy morning, "sit on my hands" until 10:00 and use the first presented FVG for the Silver Bullet — probabilities shift in your favor if you wait. The first FVG on the 1m chart formed at 9:32 (caption says "9 Minute chart" but he means the 1-minute chart). On apprehensive days he highlights it in gray rather than coloring a direction, and tracks whether the trade would pan out while deciding how comfortable he would be in it. He names the Silver Bullet entry as "a run on the buy side" off a candle that holds its low while the next candle trades below it (a possible Silver Bullet for a buy-side run).
- **How to identify / use:** gray FVG, wait for 10:00, require displacement (see above).
- **Example in this video:** at 9:32 first 1m FVG; he marks a gray box; at 0:33:37 he wants one 1m candle to hold its low and the next to trade below it as a Silver Bullet for a run on the buy side (not executed).

### Buy side → sell side → FVG/iFVG rally sequence ("Caleb's strongest model") with wick CE entry  (aliases ICT uses: running short-term high then low then rally, inversion fair value gap, CE of the wick, extension)
- **Kind:** entry model (recap of an earlier trade/vignette)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The strongest version of the beginner's model: a short-term high is taken (buy side first), price then trades down and takes out a short-term low (sell side), then rallies using a fair value gap — which can be an inversion FVG.
  - His earlier entry used the consequent encroachment of a wick; entry slightly above that CE and the inversion FVG. He targeted the short-term buy side with a 0.25 extension of the old high (caption unclear: "load High 0.25 extension") and finessed the exit by factoring in the spread because price does not always reach the target.
  - Missed the best entry below the CE with a market order; a limit would have caught it but "it's not likely to always do that for you".
  - Chicken leg analogy: the meat is not at the tips of each bone; you do not need the highest high or the lowest low, just the bulk of the move in your directional bias.
- **How to identify / use:** sequence BSL taken → SSL taken → bullish displacement leaving an FVG/iFVG → long; target the next short-term high, optionally a −0.25 extension; account for spread.
- **Example in this video:** an earlier trade shown on his Twitter vignette (instrument NQ/micro; date not stated in this video; likely the Friday Sept 20 / prior session).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:48](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=948) Caleb's strongest model sequence · [0:16:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=997) CE of wick entry, iFVG, 0.25 extension target · [0:17:27](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1047) chicken leg analogy

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range 9:30–10:00 ET; Silver Bullet starts at 10:00.
- First 1m FVG of the day today formed at 9:32; the 15s first FVG was only about a quarter of a point and not useful.
- A medium-impact news driver at 9:45 (Flash Manufacturing PMI) caused a flurry; he says he never looks at the data.
- Price should spend time above old highs or below old lows, not inside gaps/inefficiencies/wicks.
- Hitting CE once is fine; a second CE tag before the target low is a failure sign; the upper half and CE should not be revisited until the low is taken.
- Needs displacement off a PD array; a run on stops before the array is a typical signature.
- Preferred setup is before the liquidity is taken (premium retracement into a volume imbalance, then repricing to the low).
- A high-probability setup must be "obvious, one-sided": you cannot imagine it dropping when you want to be long.
- Charts stay clean because the levels drawn are the same ones he writes in his daily notes.
- He streams from ~1:45 PM ET until about 3:00 PM (afternoon session).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Sitting still is a position: "I have absolutely zero issues with not putting a trade on because you're all watching" (0:58:40). If it does not offer a sound, easy entry with a logical stop where price should not reach unless wrong, do nothing.
- Knowing why you should not trade is worth more than an entry strategy; avoiding the majority of mistakes (0:57:53).
- Capital preservation is the first lesson; making money when technicals are easy is easy (0:53:01).
- Do not be a gambler who acts on every opportunity; setups must follow the rules and money management (0:56:57).
- If you trade in these conditions you will lose — not a statement about skill (0:53:01); anyone who made money before 10:30 that day got lucky (0:43:41).
- Shows his own errors/idle mornings so students see it is not always perfect (0:23:05); he plans a library of YouTube shorts showcasing each PD array being executed (0:11:03).
- Journal the "CE must not be retraded" phenomenon; label screenshots of the day as time distortion (1:00:17; 1:04:13).
- He said he would force a trade just to prove the point, then declined twice because he would not take a loss just to show one (1:03:25).

## Glossary candidates
`Time distortion` — market held in a range/inside inefficiencies, waiting for release.
`Held / strangled` — price not being allowed to run its liquidity.
`Signature` — displacement off a PD array that confirms intent.
`Low resistance liquidity run` — move to a low after a premium retracement into a volume imbalance.
`Volume imbalance` — body-to-body gap between consecutive candles.
`NDOG` — new day opening gap (mentioned; teaching deferred).
`Chicken leg` — ICT's image for not needing the extreme tips of a move.
