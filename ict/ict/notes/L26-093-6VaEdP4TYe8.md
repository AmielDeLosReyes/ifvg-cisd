## Concepts

### Market Maker Sell Model (MMSM) / Fading ATH
- **Kind:** entry model + example
- **What ICT says:** Identify when smart money (the "fill belly") runs price just short of all-time highs (ATH), fails to break it, then reverses aggressively lower. This setup forms when price rallies into a "suspension block" (tight range at highs) on a bullish close candle, then the body closes inside that block—signaling the run is complete and reversal is coming.
- **How to identify:** Price runs from a breakout point (e.g., Tuesday RTH opening range gap high) all the way up, gets very close to ATH (26,901.75 in this ES example), but fails to take it out on a close. Smart money prints a bullish candle, drawing in late breakout buyers. Then reverses.
- **How to use:** Entry is short after the failed ATH touch. Use a suspension block (the tight range containing the failed run) as a reference zone. Place protective buy limits (partial exit targets) below key resistance (e.g., 26,830, 26,805) to scalp out on the way down. Stop placement sits above the wick of the "premium wick" (the candle that touched consequent encroachment of the high). Scale in on retracements into the SIBI.
- **Example in this video:** ES 1m, April 22, 2026, 8:00–9:30 ET. Price rallied from 26,745 (7:45 ET) to 26,901.75 (just shy of ATH), failed to break it, closed in a tight suspension block. ICT went short off this failure, layered buys at 26,830 (Monday RTH opening range high), 26,805 (sell-side), and lower levels. Took multiple partials as price worked lower, demonstrating when to scale out (when spending "too much time" in a SIBI = loss of directional pressure).

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** definition + identification rule
- **What ICT says:** A tight, narrow candle range that forms at a reversal point—typically where price is held from breaking into new territory. It's the zone the market maker "suspends" the move to prevent a break, drawing in late entries before reversing.
- **How to identify:** Look for the bullish close candle that marks the rejection of ATH. The body of that candle and the range it contains is the suspension block. In this example, the ES block is around 26,880–26,901 (the high of day).
- **How to use:** Use it as a reference for partial targets and stop placement. Bodies staying **below** certain zones within it signal you should hold the short; bodies breaking **above** it signal a failed short setup and require immediate exit.
- **Example in this video:** ES, April 22, 2026. The suspension block is the 20–point range where price rejected the ATH; ICT references "the high of that suspension block" as a first partial target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:43](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=343), [0:15:51](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=951)

### Consequent Encroachment
- **Kind:** rule + entry/exit filter
- **What ICT says:** When a candle wick touches a resistance or fair value gap level but the **body stays below** it, price is showing "consequent encroachment"—respect for the level without a full break. The body **must stay below** the midpoint or low of that wick to keep the trade valid; if the body closes above it, the setup is broken and you exit.
- **How to identify:** After identifying a key wick or FVG high (e.g., premium wick), watch the next candle(s). If the wick touches it but the body stays in the lower half, you have valid encroachment. If the body closes above the midpoint, it's a failure signal.
- **How to use:** On a short setup, keep a mental or charted line at the wick high or midpoint. Any body crossing above it = exit half the position or bail entirely, depending on your stop placement. Use consequent encroachment as a profit target—"take the profit at consequent encroachment of that premium wick."
- **Example in this video:** Multiple examples: the premium wick above the FVG (ICT watches bodies staying below it), the inversion FVG consequent encroachment (bodies must stay **at or below** the line), the Monday RTH opening range gap high (traced as a dotted line; trade accelerates through it = target hit).
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:08](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=728), [0:14:10](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=850), [0:22:26](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=1346)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The wick itself is valid only once. If it gets "touched" intracandle and then price pulls back, that first touch is the candle to watch; future candles are "encroaching" on it, not touching it fresh.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** definition + role in trade
- **What ICT says:** After a bearish move (down run), an inversion FVG forms in the gap left behind. If price retraces back into this zone, it's a resistance zone. Price will either respect the consequent encroachment level (low of the wick at the top of the iFVG) and reverse lower again, or it will fully fill the iFVG (indicating loss of bearish control).
- **How to identify:** After a down move, mark the iFVG formed in the gap. It's an imbalance going upward. Consequent encroachment = the wick that just touched the top of that gap.
- **How to use:** On a short trade, expect price to retrace into the iFVG. Use the consequent encroachment (top wick) as a resistance line—bodies must stay in the lower half of the iFVG, or the setup is in trouble. If price rallies back and fills the entire iFVG, it's a sign smart money has lost control and you should exit.
- **Example in this video:** After the down move from 26,830, an iFVG forms. Price retraces into it; ICT watches consequent encroachment at the premium wick. When price spends multiple candles in the iFVG without exiting down, it signals loss of conviction (the "Jaws music" moment).

### Sell-side Liquidity (SSL) & Buy-side Liquidity (BSL)
- **Kind:** definition + identification
- **What ICT says:** Sell-side liquidity is resting above price where late sellers are holding stops or sell orders. Buy-side liquidity is resting below price where buyers are hiding. Smart money "takes" (sweeps) one side to trigger stops and create panic, then reverses and "leaves" liquidity resting in that zone for the next move.
- **How to identify:** SSL is above the recent high; BSL is below the recent low. Mark them on the chart at round levels or key resistance/support zones. In this trade, SSL sits at 26,830 and 26,805 (marked as "sell side imbalance" after a bearish FVG forms).
- **How to use:** Target the SSL with your profit-taking limit orders. Expect smart money to take one side (BSL first, leaving SSL intact) before reversing. Place partials just at the top of buy-side inefficiency (the zone that absorbed the buy orders when price was higher).
- **Example in this video:** SSL at 26,830 and 26,805 (the high area from Monday RTH open and prior trading). ICT places limit orders to buy partials at these levels, expecting smart money to sweep BSL and leave SSL for the retracement back down.

### Judas Swing / "Phil" (Market Maker Manipulation)
- **Kind:** example + psychology
- **What ICT says:** Refers to erratic, large-candle wicks ("100-point or 100-handle candles come out of nowhere") that appear to manipulate stops before reversing. ICT nicknames this behavior after a trader he knows ("Phil"). Modern examples include Trump tweets or other unpredictable news events that cause spoofing-like action.
- **How to identify:** Sudden large wick printed in one direction (e.g., up 100 points on a 1m chart) that doesn't hold; the next candle reverses or normalizes. Often occurs mid-trade when smart money wants to take stops or shake out weak hands.
- **How to use:** Accept it as normal market behavior; don't let it stop you out. Use "Judas swings" as profit-taking opportunities if they wick into your target zones. Watch for the pattern to understand when it's happening and don't fight it—just manage risk.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple instances where ICT expects wicks and is NOT concerned ("I know Phil's going to be active today"). One example: price wicks up past consequent encroachment but the body stays below; ICT watches bodies to stay below the wick, not the wick itself.

### Equal Highs / Relative Equal Lows
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When price prints two consecutive lows at or very close to the same level, that's a "relative equal low." This level often becomes a support or exit target (limit order level). It's a sign price is trying to hold support or is vulnerable to a drop through it.
- **How to identify:** Chart tool: mark the lows of the last two candles (or recent lower lows). If they're within a few ticks, you have a relative equal low.
- **How to use:** Place limit orders to exit partials at these levels, as they often get swept. Also use them as a reference for stops—if price trades below the relative equal low convincingly, the setup has failed.
- **Example in this video:** ICT refers to "relatively equal lows" (around 26,804–26,805) as his partial exit zone and as a point where, if price won't go through quickly, he expects reversal (loss of momentum).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:51](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=951), [0:39:31](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=2371)

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Entry:** Go short when price fails to break ATH on a close (prints rejection candle in suspension block).
- **Partial exit strategy:** Layer buy limits at multiple levels: (1) high of the suspension block (1/4 position), (2) key SSL zones (26,830, 26,805), (3) relative equal lows (26,804), (4) lower FVG zones.
- **Stop placement:** Above the wick of the premium wick (the candle that just touched consequent encroachment). In this case, stops sat above 26,859 (the inversion FVG high wick). Adjust stop only after taking first partial and confirming directional pressure.
- **Bodies must stay below:** Consequent encroachment wick high, midpoint of premium wick, lower half of inversion FVG. If they don't, immediately exit half or all remaining.
- **Spending "too much time" in SIBI:** If multiple candles (3+) are printed inside a SIBI without accelerating lower, expect reversal. Take remaining contracts off manually.
- **Limit order vs. market order:** If limit order doesn't fill when price touches the level, don't wait—take a market order and peel it off. Unfilled limits near targets = loss of conviction.
- **Equity to risk:** 20 micros on (equivalent to 2 minis). Scaled to 30 micros (3 minis equivalent) at the high.
- **Time filters:** 8:00–9:30 ET window (after NY open push, before lunch).

## Process, risk & psychology

**Risk management:**
- Only trade what you can afford to ride out volatility. Micro contracts allow larger position sizing with lower notional risk.
- Adjust stops only after first partial is taken and conviction is confirmed.
- Accept that "Phil" (market-maker volatility, news-driven wicks) will happen; prepare for it mentally.

**Trade management:**
- Scale in on retracements back into the SIBI (add on the way down).
- Take partials early (at 1/4 of the zone, then at SSL zones) to reduce mental burden and lock in gains.
- If you feel uncomfortable with a trade, **take a manual market order**—"you never go wrong taking profits."
- Don't use limit orders as a crutch if they're not filling. Price often touches limits but fails to fill when smart money is controlling it; when this happens, accept that the move is stalling and exit manually.

**Mindset:**
- Expect speed and distance on good trades. Slowing down = loss of conviction.
- "In a perfect world" it would drop straight and deliver the targets, but real markets involve retests and retracements.
- Admit when you're wrong. When price spent "way too much time" in the SIBI, ICT recognized loss of momentum and held only for documentation/educational purposes. This prevented a larger loss when the trade eventually reversed against him.
- The "Jaws music" moment (when all the telltale signs point to reversal against you) is a valid exit signal.

**Documentation & proof of concept:**
- ICT posts videos sped up on social media (X) with annotations to prove real-time execution (to counter claims that comments are added after the fact).

## Glossary candidates

`SIBI` — Sell-side imbalance / Buy-side inefficiency; a PD array formed when price makes a down move leaving unmet sell orders (imbalance) and untested buy orders (inefficiency) above it. When price retraces into the SIBI, it's a resistance zone.

`Suspension block` — The tight range at a reversal point, usually where price failed to break into new territory (e.g., all-time high). A bullish close candle that ends inside the suspension block is a reversal signal.

`Fill belly` — Market maker / smart money entity that controls price; often referenced sarcastically ("the fill belly that everybody says doesn't exist").

`Phil` — Nickname for erratic market-maker volatility (large wicks, spoofing, news-driven moves). "Phil is online" = expect unusual candle behavior today.

`Consequent encroachment` — When a wick touches a resistance level but the body stays below it (in the lower half of the zone). A sign of respect for resistance without a full break; bodies crossing above the midpoint = failure of the setup.

`Consequent encouragement` — (Variant term; used interchangeably with "consequent encroachment" in this transcript; may refer to when price accelerates through the level, encouraging continuation).

`Equal highs / Relative equal lows` — Two consecutive highs or lows at very similar levels; often used as support/resistance or partial exit targets.

`Premium wick` — The wick that reached furthest into resistance (e.g., above an FVG high). The candle body's position relative to this wick is a key filter (bodies below = valid; bodies above = failure).

`Inversion FVG (iFVG)` — A fair value gap formed by the gap left in an upward retrace; functionally a resistance zone. Opposite of a regular (bearish) FVG.

`RTH opening range gap` — The gap between the previous day's close and the current day's open (9:30 ET for ES). Levels like Monday's RTH high are key liquidity magnets.

`MMSM` — Market Maker Sell Model; a short entry setup based on identifying failed breakouts and ATH rejection.

`Partial` — Scaling out of a position incrementally (e.g., taking 1/4 off at suspension block high, 1/4 at SSL, leaving 1/2 for a full target).
