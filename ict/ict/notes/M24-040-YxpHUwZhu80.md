## Concepts

### News-week protocol (NFP / CPI / PPI / FOMC weeks)  (aliases ICT uses: "nonfarm payroll protocol", "no touch day", "volatility injections", "high-impact news drivers")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - High-impact reports (NFP, CPI, PPI at 8:30 ET; FOMC in the afternoon, around 2:00/2:30, a two-stage delivery) are used as "volatility injections". They give the market maker a pretext for moves that are really manual.
  - The week of the report is hard to trade, and the difficulty starts days before the release, not only on the day.
  - For NFP week (report Friday 8:30): trade the Monday, which he calls easy, and possibly Tuesday. Tuesday is decent until the London session of Wednesday, which is "the last bit of good price action". Wednesday NY session onward (Wed/Thu/Fri) becomes messy and range-bound.
  - New students should not trade Wednesday, Thursday or Friday of NFP week. The pre-market session before 9:00 ET on Wednesday is acceptable. After 9:00, if you have not already had a trade, do nothing. (Early in the video the caption has him saying the difficulty starts "after 11 o'clock" Wednesday. Caption unclear. He later says 9:00.)
  - If the report is on Thursday or Friday, best trading is Monday and/or Tuesday. If the report is on Monday or Tuesday, do nothing at the start of the week and trade the easier "salad days" later in the week.
  - If you have no clear weekly/daily draw, do very little that week. Once the weekly draw has been satisfied (here the weekly BISI target delivered Monday), "it's already done enough" and he does not need another trade.
  - Never be in the market going into the 8:30 release. A single 1-minute candle can travel 250+ handles in NQ on NFP, and it is untradeable.
  - He ignores the raw data. It is "already baked in". What matters is the energy before the release, at the release, and where the market goes right after. Bullish data often produces a drop and the reverse.
  - Schedule it: check the economic calendar (forexfactory.com or an econ-day calendar, both free) a month ahead. Plan when to trade and when to sit still. Medium-impact (orange) events can be traded. The no-trade rule is for high-impact (red) events.
  - He frames "knowing when not to trade" as a skill worth more than a tight stop or a good entry.
- **How to identify:** Check the calendar for red-folder CPI, PPI, NFP, FOMC. Note the weekday of each release. Mark the week as "reduced activity" from the day the week turns choppy through the release.
- **How to use:** Trade Monday/Tuesday on NFP weeks, tape-read or paper trade the rest. Re-engage only after the post-news precision test (see below).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Wed 2024-10-02, NFP on Fri 10-04. An economic number came out at 8:15 ET. Price ranged around the top of a weekly BISI with minor liquidity above and below. ICT sat still, only annotated, and said any trade idea could be upset by manual moves.

### Post-news re-entry test (precision returns)  (aliases: "manual intervention", "sharks in the water", "green light")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a high-impact report, wait a minimum of 15 minutes. Then study 1-minute and sub-1-minute (15s) price delivery.
  - While "manual intervention" is underway, PD arrays are not respected and price stops and turns for no reason. The market makers are manually pushing price. Moves overshoot highs/lows by a lot and do not lay the low or high onto anything precise.
  - A quick test is the consequent encroachment (CE) of fair value gaps. If price eats straight through the whole FVG with no respect for the midpoint, intervention is still on, so sit still.
  - When PD arrays start stopping price "on the tick" again and CE supports price, the algorithm is back in control and precision tools work again. That is the green light.
  - While intervention is underway, "opportunities are cleverly disguised impossibilities". The entry looks ready to pluck, but it bites.
- **How to identify:** Watch the 1m and 15s charts for whether FVG midpoints, order block edges and old levels are being respected to the tick.
- **How to use:** No respect means no entry. Wait until PD arrays are respected consistently, then trade normally.
- **Example in this video:** He tells viewers to watch the 1m and 15s charts around Thursday 8:30 (employment data) and Friday 8:30 (NFP) and see the absence of precision for themselves.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:30](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2550) 15-minute minimum wait, watch 1m/sub-1m · [0:43:18](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2598) CE test · [0:46:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2789) "opportunities are disguised impossibilities" · [0:48:05](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2885) intervention looks like big abrupt moves past levels
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** His "manual vs scripted" framing: normally price is algorithmic (scripted, precise). During news weeks the hand of the market maker moves it.

### Near-term liquidity first, then flip (range-bound week approach)  (aliases: "volatility pinball", "broken wing trick")
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - In news weeks, expect range-bound, listless trading. Do not mindset a "trending day". Price bumps stops on one side, fakes a trend, and returns to the range.
  - Identify the nearest buy-side and sell-side pools (minor liquidity). Wait for one to be taken. Usually the first side taken can simply be flipped and traded to the other.
  - Significant price runs (30, 50+ handles, not 10-20) usually follow liquidity being taken first. Taking the sell-side first, especially with a bullish higher-timeframe bias, makes any discount array more trustworthy.
  - Why: the sell-side raid stops out profitable longs, traps breakout shorts, and gives smart money cheap fills (the sell stops become market sell orders that smart money buys into).
  - If neither side has been taken, he is "indifferent" and sits still.
- **How to identify:** Mark the closest clean highs/lows (minor BSL/SSL). Annotate whichever has been raided.
- **How to use:** After one side is taken, look for PD arrays (FVG, OB) in the direction away from the raid to ride to the other side. Without the raid, do nothing.
- **Example in this video:** Pre-market NQ 15m/5m: relative equal highs were disrupted early, price traded down into the weekly BISI. Minor sell-side sat below a down-close 5m candle and relative equal lows sat beneath. ICT preferred a sweep of the lows before a rally into the highs. He said an FVG forming after a drop below both lows, with price rallying, would have been a long. Price never did that, so no trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:52](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1072) identify pools, wait for one to be taken, flip · [0:19:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1169) significant runs follow liquidity taken · [0:21:56](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1316) volatility pinball · [0:29:20](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1760) smart money buys the sell-stop liquidity
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Long bias was based on the election-year argument (markets propped up) and the bullish long-term trend. He says that is only his lean, not a requirement.

### Minor vs primary BSL/SSL (annotation at 9:00 ET)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Before 9:00 ET, annotate the closest buy-side/sell-side pools as "minor". At 9:00 ET, re-annotate for the opening bell. The clean highs, clean lows or a singular high/low closest to price at that time are the "primary" buy-side and sell-side liquidity.
  - Do the same on 1m, 5m and 15m. Primary pools are not looked for until 9:00, because you wait for the opening bell and then expect a run to one of them.
  - The best trade circumstance is when the higher-timeframe (daily/weekly) draw is already clear.
- **How to identify:** At 9:00 ET find the nearest clean high and low relative to current price. Anything closer or smaller is minor.
- **How to use:** Treat the primary pools as the targets for the 9:30 opening bell and morning session.
- **Example in this video:** At 9:00, primary buy-side was a recent clean high, higher buy-side was farther away, and the lows below were primary sell-side. A minor sell-side sat at about 19924 (caption unclear).

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None.

### Bullish order block, mean threshold and candle bodies
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A down-closed candle on the 5m (order block) is bullish support while price stays above its mean threshold (50% of the candle).
  - If a candle body closes below the mean threshold, the order block is breached and "not a good area to expect price to stop". It does not always mean continuation lower, but lower becomes more likely.
  - The ideal case is a drop into the upper half of the OB with the mean threshold never touched. He never wants to see candle bodies deliver into the lower half of a bullish OB.
- **How to identify:** The mean threshold is the midpoint of the order block candle. Watch for a body close beyond it.
- **How to use:** A body close through the mean threshold invalidates the long idea. He then looks to sell-side.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m at about 8:50 ET (before the open): a down-close candle with price hammering the bottom of the preceding up-close candle. ICT says the pivot idea is bullish while the OB's mean threshold holds.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:52](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1612) mean threshold of 5m OB · [0:27:42](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1662) body into lower half is the warning sign
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The caption says "water block" for order block here.

### Two stacked FVGs (allow for the second one)  (aliases: "Model 2022 logic", "breakaway gap")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price forms two FVGs close together, e.g. one small FVG above a lower one, applying the 2022 Mentorship model means you must allow price to reach into the second (upper) FVG before concluding the entry failed.
  - If you enter the lower one with a stop only just above the candle high, you can get stopped as price wicks up to the upper FVG.
  - Two approaches: (a) enter at the first FVG with a wider stop above the second, or (b) wait for price to trade into the upper FVG and use that as the entry, with a somewhat larger stop. Beginners especially need the extra forgiveness.
  - A third FVG further up is a "breakaway gap". It shows heaviness. Price does not need to go to it. The move stops before it.
  - The ideal case is that candle bodies stay in the lower half of the upper FVG while only the wick pokes the higher gap.
- **How to identify:** Count FVGs along the retracement. The first and second can be reached. The one beyond is a "breakaway" if price shows heaviness before it.
- **How to use:** Decide stop location to cover the second FVG. Check that the body stays in the lower half.
- **Example in this video:** NQ short at about 9:00 to minor sell-side: FVG at the first break lower and a small FVG just above. Price wicked into the upper FVG (a stop-loss based only on the first would have been hit) then dropped. Later at the open, a third FVG appeared higher up, was treated as a breakaway gap, and price did not reach it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:57:26](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3446) two FVGs, allow the second · [0:58:14](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3494) two schools of thought, wider stop · [1:37:53](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5873) breakaway gap · [1:38:40](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5920) stays in lower half
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Ties back to the 2022 model rule that you may need to wait for the second FVG.

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) and reclaimed FVG as a catalyst  (aliases: "reclaimed bearish fair value gap")
- **Kind:** refinement / example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - The weekly BISI (a bullish FVG) is, when price is above it and drops into it, a bullish FVG. If price trades below it and you are bearish, it becomes an inversion FVG, and you want to see price stay heavy at its midpoint or low and not explore the upper part.
  - A weekly sell-side-inefficiency gap sitting below price is a classic bearish FVG. If price trades back up into it, it becomes a "reclaimed" bearish FVG.
  - An inversion FVG is not meant to be a hold-and-run level. It is meant to stop price, let it run, and then a later FVG forms on the other side. In his drill: price dips to a discount level, a later FVG forms after the rally, and that can be the catalyst for a run to buy-side.
  - Using an iFVG as an entry point of interest, you grade where it sits within the dealing range (see below).
- **How to use:** Treat the old FVG as context. Wait for the second-leg FVG after the raid. Do not call a "failure" because price passed through the iFVG.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s/1m: the early small FVG case study that "cancelled" when price traded below its low inside the BISI inefficiency. The next FVG after price dipped and rallied back became a catalyst candidate. After 9:30, price returned up into an iFVG then dropped to the primary sell-side.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:50](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3650) FVG inverts, future FVG as catalyst · [1:03:14](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3794) reclaimed bearish FVG · [1:09:58](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4198) BISI vs inversion classification
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Brief and loosely explained. Use as context, not a full rule set.

### Premium / discount framing: Inception to Terminus  (aliases: "point of interest", "grading the unrealized dealing range", "Terminus" (new coinage use))
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage
- **What ICT says:**
  - Before every trade you must know where price is going (the "Terminus", the liquidity target) and where you are entering (the "Inception" PD array, the point of interest where the move begins).
  - Frame the range from the entry-side Inception to the Terminus. The 50% equilibrium divides the range. For a short, only enter in premium (above equilibrium). Entries below equilibrium are "so close to where it's going to go" and are low probability.
  - Probability falls as your entry moves toward the target, which is how premium/discount ties to entry quality.
  - You can do this without a Fib tool. Visually, the midpoint between an iFVG and the relative equal lows is where the equilibrium sits. FVGs above that midpoint are premium arrays for a short. The ones in the lower half are not.
  - He calls this "grading the unrealized dealing range". The range has not delivered yet, but you can anticipate where setups will form.
  - To pick between several FVGs: ask where each sits relative to the 50% of Inception to Terminus. In his example the chosen FVG was in the upper half and the next one up was a breakaway gap.
- **How to identify:** Draw (or imagine) the range from entry zone to the liquidity target below the relative equal lows. Locate equilibrium. Classify each candidate PD array as premium or discount.
- **How to use:** Short only from premium arrays, long only from discount arrays. Target the Terminus (beyond the liquidity, since the liquidity has to be engaged).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: Inception was a volume imbalance / FVG area after the pre-market rally. Terminus was just below the relative equal lows. Equilibrium divided the two. The volume imbalance and the first FVG sat in the premium half. The market respected them and delivered to the sell-side below.
- **Timestamps:** [1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833) define range of opportunity, target is beyond the liquidity · [1:22:07](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4927) equilibrium, short only in premium · [1:34:41](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5681) grading the unrealized dealing range · [1:35:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5729) pick the right FVG by premium/discount
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Here "Terminus" means the liquidity end of the move. He says the objective is not just to reach the price point but to trade below it, because the liquidity has to be engaged ([1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833)).

### Candle bodies vs wicks (where smart money is read)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The bulk of volume is in candle bodies, and the narrative is determined by the bodies. Smart money reads bodies. Retail ("street money") gets hurt in the wicks. When price wicks into a higher FVG but bodies stay below, that is the signature of respect. Orders resting above the wick do not matter to the algorithm (he mocks the "show me level 2 data" crowd).
- **How to use:** Judge FVG/OB respect by where bodies close, not by wick overshoot.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: price wicked into the higher FVG, bodies remained in the lower half, then dropped.
- **Timestamps:** [1:39:27](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5967) bodies tell the narrative, victims live in wicks
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Ties to the OB mean threshold rule above.

### RTH opening gap midpoint (CE) in the first 30 minutes  (aliases: "opening range gap", "mid gap", "opening gap is the initial bias")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening gap is the initial bias. In the first 30 minutes after the open, "70% of the time" price trades to the midpoint (consequent encroachment) of the gap. If you gap down, the primary focus is up to the midpoint of the gap. He repeats the 70% number and calls it a 70% strike rate.
  - Size filter: an opening range gap of at least 40 handles (NQ) is his minimum interest for the first 30 minutes. A larger gap (e.g. 75 handles) makes him more interested in the day, and he would use most of his available leverage.
  - After the midpoint is hit, it often acts as the turning point to the primary sell-side.
- **How to identify:** Compare the 4:00 PM close (ET) and 9:30 AM open and mark the gap and its 50%. (Defined as the RTH opening gap in other lectures.)
- **How to use:** Gap down means expect a first-30-minute run up to the gap midpoint. Gap up means the reverse (inferred).
- **Example in this video:** NQ Wed 10-02: gap-down open. The midpoint sat at 19965. Price traded up to it in the first 30 minutes, then reversed and dropped to the primary sell-side below.
- **Timestamps:** [1:23:46](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5026) mid gap at 19965, 70% · [1:24:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5073) opening gap as initial bias · [1:25:23](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5123) 40-handle minimum · [1:32:16](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5536) recap, 70% strike rate

### Opening-gap turtle soup (buy stop at the open of the piercing candle)  (aliases: "mechanical turtle soup", "buy on a stop")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Setup: a gap-down open (at least 40 handles), a minor sell-side low already identified, and the opening gap midpoint as the draw for the first 30 minutes.
  - When price pierces below the low, take the down-closed candle that pierced it. Treat it as an order block. Use that candle's opening price as a buy stop.
  - If price never trades back up to that open, you are never filled. If it does, you are kicked in long. Target the opening gap midpoint (CE) and be out ("small high-frequency entry").
  - It is "very, very mechanical". You are buying from flat, and the buy stop is the only order that executes.
  - You can then aim for the next sell-side (the equal lows) after the midpoint.
  - Backtest it. He says there are multiple ways to trade this tape and they are "equally effective".
- **How to identify:** Gap down at 9:30, a minor sell-side low, a down-closed candle piercing it. (The caption wording on which candle to use is ambiguous. Best reading is the piercing candle's own open.)
- **How to use:** Entry: buy stop at the open of the piercing candle. Target: gap midpoint. Stop: not stated in detail in this video.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m after the 9:30 open, gap down: price traded below a minor low with a down candle, rallied up to the midpoint, then fell on toward the equal lows.
- **Timestamps:** [1:25:23](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5123) ORG gap, turtle soup idea · [1:27:02](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5222) open of the piercing candle as buy stop · [1:27:49](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5269) why it is mechanical · [1:28:37](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5317) went to mid gap

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- NFP week: trade Monday (easy) and maybe Tuesday. Tuesday London into Wednesday London is the last good price action. Wed/Thu/Fri NY sessions are messy. Do nothing if you have not made your week yet.
- Wednesday pre-market (before 9:00 ET) is tradeable. After 9:00 ET, no new trades if you have not traded yet (new students).
- Never be positioned into the 8:30 ET release (CPI, PPI, NFP). FOMC is the afternoon, around 2:00/2:30 ET.
- After a report, wait a minimum of 15 minutes. Check 1m and sub-1m for PD arrays being respected to the tick and FVG CE holding.
- NQ has moved 250+ handles in a single 1-minute candle on NFP.
- Significant price runs (30, 50+ handles) usually follow a liquidity raid. 10-20 handles is not significant.
- Opening range gap: at least 40 handles for interest, 75 handles is "really good". Price reaches the gap midpoint about 70% of the time in the first 30 minutes (his figure, stated repeatedly).
- Bullish OB: body close below the 50% mean threshold means breached.
- Short entries must be in premium (above 50% of the Inception to Terminus range). Long entries must be in discount.
- With two FVGs stacked, allow price to reach the second. A third beyond with heaviness is a breakaway gap.
- Use the lowest leverage. If you cannot be consistent with one contract (even micro), more leverage will not help.
- Do not demo/paper "press the button" on tape reading days in these weeks. It feeds you bad data. Tape read and observe instead.
- Practice: overlay RSI/stochastic/CCI/MACD on the chart in these weeks to see how retail would trade it and compare to ICT tools.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Use the calendar to schedule trading and non-trading in advance ([0:40:57](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2457)). Pros avoid being "behind enemy lines" when outgunned.
- Wrong exposure around news can cost you more than your account: it can wear you down mentally so you freeze when the real setup comes ([0:44:54](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2694)). He describes the two bad outcomes: sit out and beat yourself up, or chase price and argue with yourself ([0:45:40](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2740)).

## Glossary candidates
`Volatility injection` — a scheduled news event used as pretext for volatile, manual market-maker moves.
`Manual intervention` — periods around news when price is hand-moved and PD arrays are not respected to the tick.
`Inception (of the move)` — the PD array/point of interest where a price run begins.
`Terminus` — the liquidity target a move is trying to reach (and trade a little beyond).
`Grading the unrealized dealing range` — drawing premium/discount on a range that has not yet delivered.
`Breakaway gap` — a further FVG that price does not need to reach because it shows heaviness before it.
`Reclaimed bearish FVG` — a bearish FVG that price traded back up into.
`Volatility pinball` — range-bound bouncing between nearby stops in news weeks.
`Salad days` — easier trading days after a Monday/Tuesday report.
`Primary vs minor liquidity` — primary pools are the clean highs/lows nearest at 9:00 ET. Minor are the closer, smaller ones.
