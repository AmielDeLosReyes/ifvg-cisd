## Concepts

### Draw on liquidity first, order block second  (aliases: DOL, magnet, bullseye)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** New students always want to know which order block / entry candle to pick — that's the *easiest* part. What matters is what the market is likely to do and where it's going to draw to and why; spend most of your study on that and on reading the tape. Picture a magnet at the target level pulling price (TradingView's magnet/bullseye icon used to mark it).
- **Example in this video:** ES June 2022 daily: daily bearish OB + seasonal tendency + risk-off dollar → draw to the daily relative equal lows, which were tapped on April 29, 2022.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:04](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=4) — recap: bearish OB, seasonal, REL; [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=100) — picking the order block is the easiest thing; [0:06:25](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=385) — magnet analogy for the DOL.

### Imbalance + gap respected by candle bodies  (aliases: permissible price action, bodies vs wicks)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** Wicks poking slightly beyond an imbalance is "permissible price action" you must get used to; what matters is that candle bodies stay inside the imbalance — the wicks reach for liquidity/stops but the bulk of the volume is held inside.
- **Example in this video:** ES hourly: previous day pumped higher, fell just short of a high, declined, paused, then gapped down and rallied back; between one candle's low and the next candle's open there's an imbalance coupled with an actual gap; five or so candles wick just above it while bodies stay inside; then price releases lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:01](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=481) — imbalance coupled with a gap; [0:08:49](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=529) — bodies inside, wicks reach for liquidity.

### Premium/discount relative to old highs/lows  (aliases: short-term premium, short-term discount)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Any time the market trades *above an old high* it is in a short-term premium (it's going into liquidity); any time it trades *below an old low* it's in a discount. Trading above the 8:30 opening price also puts it in a premium on a bearish day. Like overbought/oversold, premium can still go higher and discount can keep going lower — so it must be read through the narrative.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:25](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=625) — 8:30 open traded above = premium, above old high = premium; [0:11:16](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=676) — definition, viewer question answered.

### Narrative vs. market structure  (aliases: narrative, present narrative)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Market structure "is not the answer"; it frames an idea, but the idea must align with the present *narrative*: why the market should go where you think on that particular day, based on the climate, economic calendar events, and the volatility being offered for that session/day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:04](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=724) — narrative defined.

### Power of 3 on a bearish day / breakout traders as counterparty  (aliases: open-rally-distribute, AMD)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Bearish day Power of 3: the open, a rally up (manipulation) creating the high, then the move lower; smart money sells short into buy stops above highs, then offloads (covers) their shorts into sell stops below relative equal lows. Daily REL attract long-term trend-following/breakout traders who sell at market when they break — the perfect counterparty for smart money buying back shorts. Smart money enters near the high above the opening price, exits at an important low, and the market closes near the low of the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:51](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=771) — Power of 3 described; [0:13:40](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=820) — trend followers' breakout sells pair with smart money; [0:14:26](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=866) — enter near high above opening, close near low.

### 9:30 open stop run → displacement → FVG (15m checklist)  (aliases: equity open, 2022 model checklist)
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** At 9:30 the stock market opens (the bell). Checklist on the 15m: Did it take a high out? Did it break below a low? Was it displacement (energetic)? Did it trade back up into the imbalance? If all yes, you can be a seller to get in sync and, as a swing/short-term trader, aim for the old daily relative equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m, April 29, 2022: 9:30 runs a short-term high, breaks lower leaving an imbalance; retrace into it = sell; target = daily REL (blue line), which was reached.

### ES 1m short: FVG entry and stop placement  (aliases: stop above FVG candle)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** On the 5m, relative equal highs sat above the opening price (buy-side liquidity), with other nearby highs too ("too rich" to expect the farther ones). Price punched up through them, broke down, came back once more above the opening price, then "gave up the ghost" and ran for sell-side. On the 1m: run above REH → break of a swing low with displacement → back up into the FVG, above the opening price → short. Stop must be *at minimum* above the candle that creates the FVG (conservative: higher). A big stop reflects volatility; worrying only about max profit while dismissing risk is what gamblers do.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:16](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=916) — 5m REH above opening price; [0:16:03](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=963) — runs them, breaks, aggressive sell-side run; [0:16:59](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1019) — 1m checklist; [0:17:46](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1066) — stop above FVG-creating candle.

### Stacked FVGs: where to enter and where the stop goes  (aliases: two fair value gaps rule)
- **Kind:** rule (restated, reinforcing Ep. 21)
- **What ICT says:** If there is only one FVG, place your entry there and don't expect a deeper retracement. If there is another FVG beyond it, expect price to "stab up into" that one, so your stop must be positioned to weather it — either have deep pockets or use micros. Trying to sell the highest candle / buy the lowest (being "ICT junior") makes you miss moves.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m: after displacement below relative equal lows, a FVG right above, and another one above that; price did extend into the upper one.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:33](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1353) — two FVGs, expect stab into the upper one, micros vs $20/point; [0:23:20](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1400) — rule: one FVG vs two FVGs; [0:24:07](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1447) — don't hunt the exact top/bottom.

### Swing-point hierarchy: long-, intermediate-, short-term highs  (aliases: market structure nesting, fractal)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A swing high followed by a break lower *with an imbalance* qualifies as an intermediate-term high. When two such highs nest (the later one lower than the earlier), the earlier one is a long-term high and the later one an intermediate-term high. A retracement inside that bearish structure forms a short-term high, which should respect the underlying bearish order flow and go lower toward sell stops below the lows. The same pattern (REL → high taken → break with displacement → FVG → retrace with bodies respecting it) repeats smaller and smaller: it's a fractal.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m/1m, April 29, 2022: high run (buy stops taken, breakout longs trapped), REL broken = MSS on 5m during a bearish period; a smaller copy of the move forms below it: REL, high taken, two candles broken, FVG, bodies respecting it, aggressive break lower; then retracement = short-term high under the LTH and ITH.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:46](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1306) — buy stops taken, REL broken, MSS; [0:24:54](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1494) — fractal repeat; [0:25:41](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1541) — two narratives, LTH vs ITH via imbalance; [0:26:27](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1587) — STH inside bearish structure should respect order flow.

### Order block refinement ("calibrate") and midpoint entry  (aliases: top-down OB refinement, mean threshold entry)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He uses the up-close candle in the retracement as a bearish order block — "I'm telling you how to read the tape," not the YouTube model. "Calibrate" = top-down: take the 5m order block, go to the 1m, refine it, and enter as price retraces and hits the *midpoint* of the block. A small counter-signal on the 1m (e.g., an opposing OB/FVG) is subordinate to the higher-timeframe bearish structure. Wicks above the OB are fine as long as candle bodies (the bulk of the volume) stay inside it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: price retraces up into the 5m up-close candle area (shaded zone); on the 1m he enters around the midpoint; price wicks above the OB but bodies stay inside, then breaks lower with displacement → REL → displacement → FVG; price comes back into the FVG and exactly to the bottom of the bearish order block (he notes he says "bullish order block" for the green candle at one point; context = up-close candle acting as bearish OB, caption/misspeak unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:16](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1636) — up-close candle = bearish OB, tape reading; [0:28:13](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1693) — calibrate 5m OB down to 1m; [0:29:00](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1740) — midpoint entry, subordinate to HTF; [0:32:11](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1931) — wick above OB, bodies inside, displacement.

### Pyramiding: largest size first (NQ trade, April 29, 2022)  (aliases: scaling in, building a position, close proximity entry)
- **Kind:** rule / live trade
- **What ICT says:** He builds positions with the maximum size first, then smaller adds at logical, precise areas (3 → 2 → 1 contracts); he never keeps adding one more contract. Built-in equity on the earlier contracts makes heat on the last small add insignificant. A "close proximity entry" = entering right after a level filled when price is still near it (he wasn't watching when the FVG filled).
- **Example in this video:** NQ June 2022: sold 3 contracts at the 1m OB midpoint; sold 2 at the FVG (close-proximity, just after it filled); sold 1 when price retraced back into the bearish OB — the candle low there was 13,334.75 and his entry 13,335.00 (a quarter point into it); heat on that contract up to 13,339.50 (4.5 handles). Covered all 6 at 13,285.00 — set just above the target level in case shallow runs didn't fill. He calls the whole thing a market maker sell model (selling where distribution takes place).
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:58](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1978) — the three entries; [0:33:44](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2024) — entry 13335 vs candle low 13334.75; [0:34:33](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2073) — max size first, 4.5 handles heat; [0:36:07](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2167) — exit all six at 13285; [0:36:59](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2219) — market maker sell model.

### SMT divergence (ES vs NQ)  (aliases: SMT, smart money technique, correlation crack)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** ES and NQ (S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 composites) are closely correlated and generally move in tandem, not always. If you're bearish and one makes a higher high while the other fails to, that shows a willingness to break the correlation: the higher one's move is a stop run and the lagging one is really weak — distribution. It's confirmation without an indicator; an "x-ray view" of real accumulation and distribution.
- **How to identify:** Compare the same swing points on both. On TradingView: compare/add symbol in a new pane below, and set the price source to High to compare high-to-high.
- **Example in this video:** Friday April 29, 2022: ES high at ~2:30 a.m.; after 8:30 ES rallies but makes a *lower* high than the 2:30 high, while NQ makes a *higher* high (vs its ~2:35 high) → bearish SMT, confirming the short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:35](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2555) — ES 2:30 a.m. high vs post-8:30 high, NQ higher; [0:43:24](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2604) — SMT definition, stop run, ES weak; [0:44:12](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2652) — TradingView compare pane with high source; [0:45:00](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2700) — x-ray of accumulation/distribution.

### Fibonacci setup: equilibrium, OTE on bodies, standard-deviation projections  (aliases: OTE, 62/79, standard deviations, calibrate to daily range)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Fib settings (he types his own levels into the style tab): 0.5 = equilibrium (discount vs premium). OTE levels = 62% and 79%. Negative extensions act as standard deviations used to project how far a move will run; calibrated to the daily range they give a good idea of where the low should form. Standard deviation projections mentioned: one, two, and one-half standard deviations. For premium/discount use the range high to low; but for OTE he anchors on candle *bodies* — the lowest open or close and the highest open or close in the swing — because wicks are stop runs; using bodies "gets to the heart of the matter" (he says critics who claim he misuses Fibs don't get this). OTE + FVG together is "what makes it optimal."
- **How to use:** Entry where the 62–79 OTE overlaps a FVG; take profit at a standard-deviation projection; to know "how far below the low can it go," look at the standard deviation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: OTE high-to-low; price trades up into OTE and the FVG = entry; body-anchored OTE puts the retrace right up almost to the 79% level. A one-standard-deviation projection lower is hit precisely, then a retracement — good profit-taking area, also below the low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:46:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2799) — fib style settings, 0.5 equilibrium, standard deviations, calibrate to daily range; [0:47:30](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2850) — OTE 62 and 79; [0:48:17](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2897) — OTE + FVG = optimal; [0:49:05](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2945) — 1/2/0.5 SD projections, bodies (lowest open or close) for OTE; [0:50:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=3039) — 1 SD target hit, take profits.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Exact on-screen settings list isn't read out in full; only 0.5, 62, 79 and the 0.5/1/2 SD projections are spoken.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Above an old high = short-term premium; below an old low = discount.
- 15m checklist after 9:30: took a high? broke a low? displacement? traded back into the imbalance? → sell (mirror for buys).
- FVG stop: at minimum above (below) the candle that creates the FVG; conservative beyond that.
- One FVG → enter there, expect no deeper retrace; two FVGs → expect a stab into the farther one; stop must allow it.
- Afternoon: go back in around 1:30 p.m. ET, after lunch smooths out; expect a retrace against the day's direction to enter.
- Intermediate-term high = swing high followed by a break *with an imbalance*; nested highs form long-term vs intermediate-term; retracement = short-term high.
- OB refinement: 5m OB → 1m, enter at the midpoint; bodies must stay inside the OB, wicks allowed.
- Pyramid largest first: 3 → 2 → 1; never keep adding single contracts.
- NQ trade: entries incl. 13,335.00 (candle low 13,334.75), heat to 13,339.50, cover all 6 at 13,285.00; later long scalp exit ~13,409 (target 13,425), ~$740.
- NQ = $20 per point; micros if account can't weather the stop.
- SMT: ES vs NQ highs; compare with TradingView pane (price source = High).
- Fib: 0.5 equilibrium; OTE 62% / 79% on bodies (lowest/highest open or close); targets at 0.5 / 1 / 2 standard deviations.
- Live streams (May 2022 only): start ~8:00 a.m. ET, may run to 10:30–11:00; no trade calls; chat not read.

## Glossary candidates
`Calibrate (an order block)` — top-down refinement of a higher-TF order block on a lower TF to enter at its midpoint.
`Close proximity entry` — entering just after a level fills while price is still near it.
`Permissible price action` — wicks beyond a PD array while bodies stay inside.
`Narrative` — why price should go to a target today, given climate, calendar events and volatility.
`ICT junior` — ICT's jab at students trying to sell the exact high / buy the exact low.
`SMT divergence` — correlated indices (ES/NQ) failing to confirm each other's high/low; reveals stop run vs weakness.
`Standard deviation (Fib)` — Fib extension levels used to project targets beyond the range.
`Long-/intermediate-/short-term high` — swing hierarchy; an imbalance after the swing upgrades it to intermediate-term.
`Market maker sell model` — selling where smart money distributes, delivering to sell-side liquidity.
