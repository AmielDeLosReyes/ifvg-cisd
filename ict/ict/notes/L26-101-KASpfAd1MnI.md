The point is not to show trades but to teach drills: small, mechanical, demo-only participations (enter into a fair value gap or inversion FVG, fixed stop, 10–15 handle target on one micro) done to build a baseline of experience and a "muscle of indifference" to outcome. Along the way he teaches how to tell low resistance from high resistance liquidity-run conditions (candles sharing prior ranges, "barcode" candles), the "to it and through it" test at relative equal highs, body-versus-wick-midpoint protection rules, a half-leg equilibrium projection after a stop hunt, and the SMT "sick sister" read between MNQ/MES/MYM. ## Concepts

### Drills (practice method for entry models)  (aliases: labs, "little exercises", "leg day", "yellow belt")
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage (a practice protocol, not a trade setup)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Drills are small participations in price action with "no monetary hope of making money and no fear of losing". Paper/demo account only. He explicitly says do NOT copy his drill entries into funded or live accounts; he is "not licensed to give trading advice".
  - Goal: desensitize yourself to entering inside a fair value gap, remove the need to be right, and record a personal baseline (how you felt: scared, nervous, euphoric, frustrated; how price behaved). Being indifferent to the outcome is the target state ("muscle of indifference").
  - Entry model for the drill: wait for the fair value gap to form, enter it, put a stop on, use a "one-for-one model" with one micro (MNQ). Target = a 10-handle minimum, 15 handles maximum run off the FVG. Treated as "low-hanging fruit" on sub-1-minute charts. (Exact stop distance is not stated; "one for one" is later used to mean win/loss symmetry, so read it as roughly 1:1 risk to reward - caption-limited.)
  - Execution chart is 15-second; ranges, liquidity and inefficiencies are all derived from the 1-minute chart.
  - Practice on the hard days, not just clean ones. High-resistance conditions "record things subconsciously" about how price fails to run, which cannot be learned from a book, lecture or mentor.
  - Do drills even when you have no model yet and even when you are already profitable. If you have already made your profit for the day, do not go back to live; go to demo and drill.
  - Minimum 15 minutes per day (analogy: 20 minutes on a treadmill); "leg day" needs to be done only once a week, more is overtraining.
  - Log for every drill: time in trade, drawdown taken, whether a full stop-out still yielded anything first, and how much it yielded. If a trade stops out, note the maximum it yielded before that ("cheerlead yourself in your study journal").
  - Do not read failed drill runs as a failure of you, the model or the concept.
  - Gamify: when the drill target is hit ("your little cookie", "yellow belt") that is your reward; then ask the next-stage question: is price willing to continue/extrapolate beyond the target? That is feedback without risking anything.
  - Series plan: today fair value gaps, tomorrow order blocks, Friday mixed; next drill stream tomorrow 9:25 ET.
- **How to identify:** n/a (a method). The inputs are a visible FVG/iFVG, a draw on liquidity (some old high/low or pool), and a pre-set stop and 10–15 handle target.
- **How to use:** run 2–3 drills per hour for a short session; screenshot and annotate afterwards if you cannot be at the charts; log feelings and outcomes; repeat daily.
- **Example in this video:** about seven drills on MNQ (see the FVG/iFVG, wick-CE and equilibrium-projection entries below). Two or three stopped out or never filled; others yielded 15–22 handles. ICT stresses that "not all of them panned out".
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:20](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=260) — drill premise, don't copy; [0:17:28](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1048) — one micro, 10 min / 15 max handles; [0:28:09](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1689) — definition of drills; [1:28:02](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5282) — target hit as "your cookie", then test continuation; [1:53:31](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6811) — at least 15 minutes a day.

### Fair Value Gap entry (drill entry on 15-second chart)  (aliases: "entering into the gap", FVG with CE stop)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Enter when price trades back into a fresh FVG that is aligned with the intended run to liquidity. For a bullish FVG: price should trade down into the upper half only, keep the bodies out of the lower half, then rip strongly higher. (He is describing the ideal, not a hard filter on every drill.)
  - Consequent encroachment (midpoint) can be used as the stop ("we'll use consequent encroachment as a stop" on a buy-side FVG entry).
  - After entering, a body/candle that fails to run (stalling, "stutter", small wick above a high then nothing) is a warning; consider partials and a stop move.
  - On a stalled long: "there should be no give back at all"; if a higher high forms and it does not accelerate, take some off.
  - Roll the stop to where a stop-out covers commissions ("painless") once the next candle shows the right behaviour.
  - If nothing qualifies, sit still: "If there's nothing in the chart, don't force it."
- **How to identify:** a 3-candle gap on the 15-second chart that sits inside a 1-minute context with a nearby pool (relative equal highs/lows, old high/low) to run to.
- **How to use:** entry in the gap, stop at CE or beyond the high/low (long: CE or below; short: above the high), target 10–15 handles or the nearest small pool.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ, 15-second, about 9:55–10:00 ET. After price runs the sell side and reclaims above the 9:30 open, ICT enters long in a buy-side FVG with stop at CE; paper-trading platform fails to fill the stop/limit, so he closes the trade manually "for fairness" and notes it would have stopped out ([0:30:32](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1832)). Later long at 1:22 into a fresh FVG after a stop hunt of equal lows (see "Equilibrium projection").
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:33](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2193) — buy-side FVG entry, CE stop; [0:39:47](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2387) — short in an FVG, midpoint noted; [1:44:49](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6289) — ideal bullish-FVG behaviour (upper half, bodies out of lower half).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** in a choppy tape he would rather see it run once inside the gap; "too small" a wick/gap above a high is not enough for him to short.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) use on the 15-second chart  (aliases: inversion, "reclaimed bearish fair value gap")
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - A gap that price closes through and respects from the other side becomes an inversion. For a reclaimed bearish FVG, bodies close above it and later come back and the level holds as support (the 9:30 opening price can confluence with it).
  - Key condition: the bodies stay out of the gap. Wicks into it are acceptable; a wick can touch the 9:30 opening price and the level can still be used.
  - If price comes back through and stops you out, that is just one transaction; the body staying in the upper half after a stop-out can keep the iFVG idea alive ("as long as we don't take out these highs").
  - For shorts: stop above the high; entry once a body trades below the imbalance/the high of the relevant candlestick; target the RTH opening range gap CE.
  - He also uses the volume imbalance as a level that can be wicked to alongside CE; want bodies to stay outside the upper half.
- **How to identify:** a prior FVG that price has closed through, then a retrace into that gap/level with bodies holding on the far side.
- **How to use:** enter on the retrace to the iFVG, stop beyond it (above the high for shorts), target nearby liquidity or the opposite side of a nearby gap (10–15 handles).
- **Example in this video:** MNQ, 15-second, about 9:44–9:50 ET: price runs sell-side, reclaims the 9:30 opening price, a bearish FVG gets reclaimed ("it would look like this when drawn on the chart"), price retraces to that level with the bodies staying out; he treats it as support and price runs up with a small gap and a volume imbalance toward the high ([0:26:17](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1577)). A short idea: price breaks below a small imbalance with a body, iFVG for a run back to the RTH ORG CE ([0:33:25](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2005)). Later (ES, observation only) price takes sell side, rallies, uses a portion of a gap as an iFVG and runs to the target ([1:50:20](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6620)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:51](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1311) — reclaimed bearish FVG; [0:26:17](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1577) — iFVG works; [0:30:32](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1832) — iFVG idea after a stop-out; [1:49:34](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6574) — ES iFVG run example.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** at 0:26 he says "volume imbalance silver bullet" - caption unclear; likely mis-hearing, not a Silver Bullet window reference.

### Low Resistance vs High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions  (aliases: "fast and loose", "all lanes open", "barcode / UPC candles", "time distortion")
- **Kind:** definition / filter (the core conceptual lesson of the video)
- **What ICT says:**
  - High resistance (the day of this video, rated 9 out of 10 difficulty): candles keep sharing the previous candle's range, bodies overlap ("fuzzy"), few imbalances are created, price keeps going back and forth inside consolidations, often inside a wick ("time distortion" - wait for it to resolve by leaving the range). He compares the look to a UPC barcode.
  - Low resistance: market is "fast and loose", lots of inefficiencies, open lanes of traffic ("not rush hour"); price runs freely in expansions, gives multiple clean setups per hour, and you do not need to babysit or jam your stop.
  - Why high resistance is dangerous: it can spike through the stacked consolidation in one wick and traverse 50–60 handles to take the liquidity on the other side; also repeated small false runs.
  - Do not trade real money in high resistance conditions; practice in them. Learn to recognize the transition from low-resistance/high-probability to lesser probability.
  - Continuity check: clean price action should show on the 1-minute and on your executable timeframe (e.g. a 15-minute chart with a 1-minute below it). It does not matter that this is a 15-second chart; the cleanliness test applies at every timeframe.
  - Warning signs ("canaries in the coal mine"): a higher high followed immediately by a wilt; a shallow peek above a high then back inside; stutter instead of acceleration; sharing prior range. These force you to babysit positions.
  - If it is "a day where you really would not be wanting to do any live trading with real money," the recommended use is practice. He says he told viewers at the start it would be such a day.
- **How to identify:** look at the last 10–20 candles on your trading timeframe: do bodies overlap with the previous candle? Are there few gaps? Does price keep returning inside a small range? If yes, high resistance.
- **How to use:** skip live trading; use only for demo drills; or demand extra from any setup (see "to it and through it").
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1-minute after 9:30 ET: candles stack into "all back and forth", no FVGs form since the open for the first ~10 minutes ([0:18:18](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1098)); at about 10:30 ET (video 1:07) the 1-minute shows fuzzy overlapping candles under a premium and he refuses to participate in the long ([1:07:14](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4034)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781) — "high resistance liquidity run conditions" as a practice day; [1:10:29](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4229) — fuzzy candles, want a day with inefficiencies; [1:12:04](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4324) — barcode candles, 50–60 handle spike risk; [1:32:04](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5524) — low resistance has no babysitting; [1:32:53](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5573) — "all lanes of traffic open".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he ties this concept explicitly to "if you have a way of measuring when the market is difficult vs easy" and says other methodologies never define what makes their model strong or weak except after the fact.

### Relative Equal Highs/Lows — "to it and through it"  (aliases: REH/REL, stop hunt, false breakout, turtle soup)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price sits just below relative equal highs and has already run (taken) sell-side/short-term lows, the stops of longs have already been trailed under those lows and "enough damage" is done. A move through the equal highs should then accelerate ("to it and through it").
  - A shallow peek above the equal highs, then back into the range, is a false breakout and more indicative of an intraday reversal or a deeper retracement (bulls chased, "dragged across the coals"). If it then rolls over and takes the low, he is "not interested at all for the rest of the day".
  - After going above, you want to see it expand aggressively, not stall.
  - If a high had buy-side above and gets run, and a lower high etc. all keep running without give-back, longs should be developing without stutter.
- **How to identify:** two or more highs within a tick or two (his words: "look how relatively equal they are"), with sell-side already taken below.
- **How to use:** if long, expect continuation immediately after the high is taken; if it fails (small run then back inside), exit/partial and consider the opposite as a stop-hunt reversal; practice false-breakout (turtle soup) shorts here in demo only.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ, about 10:45 ET: price took a stop hunt of equal lows, rallied via an FVG, sat under REH; he outlines both outcomes; price went through the highs and hit the drill target, then continued ([1:24:51](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5091) to [1:28:02](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5282)). Earlier ([1:01:14](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3674)) price takes a higher high but falls back into the range, which he calls wrong behaviour if bullish and possibly a failure swing.
- **Timestamps:** [1:25:37](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5137) — "to it and through it"; [1:26:24](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5184) — false breakout for the day; [0:43:41](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2621) — how to practice becoming a turtle-soup type trader.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he says repeatedly "I could have it wrong"; it is a conditional read, not a prediction.

### Wick midpoint (CE) body-protection rule  (aliases: bodies above wick CE)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price makes a higher high and the prior high was a wick, watch the CE (halfway point) of that wick: price bodies should stay at or above it. Wicks can tap it (preferably not), but a body closing below it is problematic for continuation higher and must be resolved in price action.
  - After a wick pushes down into a CE of a gap, the very next candle should show willingness to reach its own midpoint and close beyond it; otherwise continuation is problematic.
  - Also: when price makes a higher high with a wick and the bodies drop inside the wick range, that's a warning.
- **How to identify:** a wick that made the previous high; mark its 50%.
- **How to use:** hold longs only while bodies stay above the CE; use it as a quick invalidation read and for stop roll/partials.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1-minute at ~10:25 ET (he checks the daily and quotes a draw around "349/355/360", captioned shorthand, probably 26,3xx): a higher high above a prior wick high; ICT says bodies must hold above the wick's 50% for continuation; price then stalls and he declines to participate ([1:04:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3841) to [1:05:38](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3938)).
- **Timestamps:** [1:04:47](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3887); [0:46:58](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2818) — next-candle midpoint rule and break-even-ish stop.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** extends his long-standing CE rule from gaps to wicks.

### Equilibrium projection of a stop-hunt leg (half-leg target)  (aliases: algorithmic price levels, "new thresholds")
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a stop hunt (a leg down that raids equal lows), take the high-to-low range of that leg, mark its equilibrium (50%), then project that same half-range distance above the high of the leg (i.e. high + half the range). If price takes the relative equal highs, it should "at least" reach this level; this is a repeatable target ("by taking half that drop down on that stop hunt, project that higher").
  - It gives algorithmic price levels to reach for; failure to reach it and reversal is weakness and sets the stage for lower prices into about 1:30 PM.
- **How to identify:** a clear leg down that ran sell-side, followed by a rally into REH.
- **How to use:** target for longs after the highs are taken; if price rejects before it, treat as a warning.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1-minute, about 10:45 ET: the drop of the lunch-time stop hunt is measured; price trades up to exactly that level ([1:34:28](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5668)), "so it was able to overcome it", though ICT notes a low-resistance market would not have had the hiccups on the way.
- **Timestamps:** [1:29:36](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5376); [1:30:28](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5428); [1:31:17](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5477).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** shown as a drill target, not yet stated as a general rule beyond "a target that you can continuously do".

### SMT "Sick Sister" (intermarket decoupling)  (aliases: sick sister, decoupling, MES/MYM/MNQ divergence)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Closely correlated markets (ES and Nasdaq) should confirm each other; when one makes a higher high and the other does not, there's a break in correlation. The lagging market is the "sick sister" and the leader "encourages it".
  - It does not need to match the size of the move; a higher high intraday on MES is all that is necessary. When decoupled, he reads it as heavy manipulation.
  - When MES finally makes its higher high and NQ (the leader) then breaks down and uses a 1-minute iFVG, the decoupling goes away for the near term and sell side can be engaged.
  - YM ("MYM") is completely decoupled in the early part.
- **How to identify:** compare MNQ/MES/MYM highs/lows over the same window.
- **How to use:** a decoupled tape plus a premium wick supports false-breakout shorts (small, demo only); when the laggard catches up, expect a retracement.
- **Example in this video:** NQ makes slightly higher highs while ES/YM are lower (~10:05 ET) ([0:41:21](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2481)); later MES catches up with its higher high at ~10:20 ET ([0:56:19](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3379)); on ES he repeats the same drill pattern ([1:40:02](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6002)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:21](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2481); [0:57:07](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3427); [1:40:50](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6050) — definition for newcomers.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he uses "sick sister" for what other lectures call SMT divergence; "sick" is caption-clear here (sometimes auto-captioned as "six sister").

### Premium wick CE short
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** after a large premium wick on the 1-minute chart with a small gap below it, the half of that wick (its CE) is the area to short; stop at the high; target a run below sell side back into the gap (plus one tick).
- **How to use:** entry at wick CE; stop at high; target the gap below.

### SIBI + BISI as Balanced Price Range with rejection block reaction
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says:** a sell-side imbalance/buy-side inefficiency (SIBI) next to a BISI forms a balanced price range; wicks inside show willingness to stave off higher prices; for a short he wants a sharp run below the low, then a dig into the rejection block; the first wick down tapped the CE; the next candle should close below its midpoint; he moved the stop to cover commissions.

### Regular Trading Hours Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG) and 9:30 open as references
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The previous day's RTH close (the 4:14 PM ET candle close/settlement, as he says) and today's 9:30 open define the RTH opening range gap; the 9:30 open is the gap high when it opens above settlement; the settlement can be relabelled as RTH ORG low.
  - The 9:30 opening price acts as a level (reclaim/hold above or below) and its CE is a drill target.
  - First reads at the open: no gaps = sit still.
- **Example in this video:** at 9:30 price opens above the prior settlement; ICT labels the 9:30 open as ORG high and later targets the ORG CE for a short ([0:06:45](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=405); [0:33:25](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2005)).

### Market Maker Buy/Sell Model per 15-minute interval  (aliases: MMBM/MMSM on the 15-second chart)
- **Kind:** new coinage / framing
- **What ICT says:**
  - In 15 one-minute candles (viewed on the 15-second chart) there is a market maker buy or sell model "every single time"; per hour there are about four, in any combination of buy and sell models.
  - You only need a run on liquidity; you do not need to understand the start of the move; you may see it after the fact ("the sell side of the curve delivers on a buy model").
  - You must know the draw: "the target determines" what data matters; if nothing is the target you get "a 100% strike rate hitting nothing".
- **How to use:** each 15 minutes is a chance to practice finding a small pool of liquidity and using an FVG to enter.
- **Timestamps:** [1:53:31](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6811); [1:55:10](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6910).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "don't take my word for it, go look" - an empirical claim, not a rule. A fractal extension of the MMBM/MMSM.

### Timeframe indifference ("price is price")  (aliases: fractal reading)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** the 15-second chart does nothing faster than the 1-minute; a 15-second candle just measures the high/low of a 15-second interval, so the 1-minute shows the same price delivery at a coarser scale. Do not fear the low timeframe; use it as a tool. If you did not know what instrument or timeframe you were looking at, you would still read the same pattern.
- **Timestamps:** [1:43:14](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6194); [1:44:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6241).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he admits he cannot narrate in real time at 15 seconds (it moves too fast), which is why you narrate and review yourself.

### Audible journal (narrating price action)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** if you cannot sit at the charts, screen-record a clean chart with a 15-second and 1-minute window, then replay; when able, record yourself narrating what price should and should not do ("an audible journal"). ## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Drill size: one micro (MNQ), target 10 handles minimum, 15 maximum; "one-for-one" stop/target idea ([0:17:28](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1048)).
- Drill session: stream used 15-second chart for execution, 1-minute for liquidity/ranges; at least 15 minutes a day.
- 9:30 ET open and the prior RTH close (stated 4:14 PM ET) define the RTH opening range gap; the 9:30 open can be the ORG high.
- Lunch period: 11:30–1:30 ET (stated as a 2-hour lunch window); a failed projection implies lower prices into 1:30 PM.
- Daily context quoted: 7 consecutive bullish daily closes; draw on daily relative equal highs / all-time highs; MNQ next upside "26,300s" (continuous contract); daily levels mentioned ~349/355/360 (captioned shorthand for 26,3xx).
- Nothing in the chart = sit still; don't force a trade.
- Wick-high rule: bodies stay at or above the wick's CE for continuation.
- After a stop hunt of lows and a rise to REH, expect at least a half-leg projection above the high.
- Entering on a wick/FVG: next candle should reach its midpoint and close beyond it.
- Stop management: roll the stop to cover commissions once there's movement in your favour.
- Premium wick CE short: stop at the high, target gap below plus one tick.
- Never copy drill entries in funded/live accounts.
- Price high-resistance warning signs: shared candle ranges, barcode look, stutter/wilt after higher high, shallow run above highs.
- Market maker buy/sell model: about four per hour (one per 15 minutes).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Do drills on hard days ("leg day"); a 9/10 hard day for new students is ideal for baseline experience ([0:13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781); [1:21:36](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4896)).
- If you have already made profit, do not trade live; go to demo and drill ([0:05:07](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=307)).
- Be indifferent to outcome: "muscle of indifference"; one transaction is neither a trophy nor a mortal wound ([0:28:56](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1736)).
- Record how you felt (scared, nervous, euphoric, frustrated) and the data: time in trade, drawdown, max yield ([0:19:08](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1148); [0:49:31](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2971)).
- Paper platform glitch: if stops/limits do not fill, close honestly or screenshot what would have happened, and be content with it; unfilled stops and unfilled limits cancel out ("one for one"); do not let it turn into tilt or revenge ([0:30:32](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1832); [1:13:37](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4417)).
- Everyone consistently profitable keeps practising outside the live account; you do not keep an edge sharp by constantly risking money ([1:36:50](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5810)).
- Spectating vs speculating: watching recordings is not enough; you must participate ([1:38:26](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5906)).
- Journal to find your real pressure points; one example he gives is trading to impress or please a partner instead of waiting for your model ([1:47:10](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6430); [1:48:45](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6525)).
- Do not reset demo/funded accounts to erase evidence of weaknesses; flaws are easiest to fix early ([2:07:00](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7620)).
- Do not job-hop between mentors, models, PD-array systems or software; you cannot buy success, and nobody can shorten the learning curve ([2:07:47](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7667); [2:02:16](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7336)).
- Aim your attention: you notice only what you are looking for. Analogy of a stranger's shirt colour in 30 minutes with a $1M prize; 20 photos from different angles of the same person = repeated exposures to your model in live price ([1:56:47](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7007); [1:58:20](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7100)); "hologram" of price action ([1:59:52](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7192)).
- Don't expect a report card on day one; expect to fumble; embrace uncertainty ([1:51:08](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6668); [2:05:24](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7524)).
- Expect a profitable-trader timeline of roughly 18 months before taking money out (he mentions this timeframe in the context of relationships/expectations; not a hard rule) ([1:47:10](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6430)).
- Warning: the market is heavily manipulated and trading may become harder ("big moves happen when you are not in front of the charts, big Sunday-open ranges") ([1:51:56](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6716)).

## Glossary candidates
`DRILLS` — small demo-account participations in an FVG/iFVG entry with a 10–15 handle target, done for conditioning and baseline experience, not for profit.
`LEG DAY` — ICT's name for a deliberately hard/uncomfortable practice session or market day.
`HIGH RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN CONDITIONS` — choppy, overlapping-candle tape with few imbalances where runs fail or spike through consolidations.
`LOW RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN CONDITIONS` — fast, loose, inefficient tape with open lanes where runs to liquidity are clean.
`BARCODE (UPC) CANDLES` — overlapping candles that share prior ranges; sign of high-resistance conditions.
`TIME DISTORTION` — consolidation inside a wick; wait for price to leave it.
`TO IT AND THROUGH IT` — near relative equal highs, price must trade to them and accelerate through, not peek and fall back.
`SICK SISTER` — the lagging correlated market (e.g. MES vs MNQ) that fails to confirm a higher high.
`CANARIES IN THE COAL MINE` — early warning behaviours (wilt after a high, shallow runs, stutter) that mean you will have to babysit the trade.
`MUSCLE OF INDIFFERENCE` — the trained ability to not care about the outcome of any one trade.
`AUDIBLE JOURNAL` — recording yourself narrating price action as it unfolds.
`SIBI / BISI` — ICT's wording: sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency / buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency.
