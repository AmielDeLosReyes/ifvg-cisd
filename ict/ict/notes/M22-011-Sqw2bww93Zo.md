## Summary
ICT reviews a live NQ (March contract) short taken Monday 21 Feb 2022 (Presidents' Day, abbreviated session). He entered at 13,858.25 at 10:33 a.m. ET and held through the 1:00 p.m. holiday halt into the 6:00 p.m. reopen gap, exiting at 13,612.25: 246 handles, $4,920 on one mini. The key clarifications: use the **midnight NY open for the whole daily range and the 8:30 open for the morning session**; and **short-, intermediate- and long-term highs** (Larry Williams' nesting), which explain why he trusted a short at an intermediate-term high. He also covers trading inside the FVG while ignoring wicks, displacement as confirmation, holiday session hours, and gap rebalancing. The behavioral rule: after a big win (or a big move), don't trade the next morning session. "Pushing your edge" dulls it.

## Concepts

### Daily narrative: dealing range, a premium retracement to fill a gap, then a sell-side raid
- **Kind:** example / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the NQ daily, measure the dealing range high to low. 50% = equilibrium.
  - Price went from the lows up into premium, tried to rally above the high and failed, broke back below 50% and left a gap. It came back up into premium to fill the gap.
  - That kind of retracement "lulls traders" into thinking higher. But the target is the sell-side liquidity below the old daily low (his "green line"). All the consolidation just sets up another selling opportunity.
  - It spent three days in the range rebalancing, then "released" downward and attacked the sell side.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March futures daily: a daily FVG (from the previous episodes) traded into, three days of rebalancing, then a sell-off into the SSL below the old low.

### Judas swing at 8:30: sell above the 8:30 open into an imbalance
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - With a bearish bias and the old daily low as the draw, the rally from 8:30 is a fake rally (Judas swing) that lulls in chasers.
  - He wants to be short above the 8:30 opening price.
  - Retail order block traders will say "every down-close candle after a run-up is a bullish order block". It isn't. The real context is the imbalance (FVG) above, and he operates inside that range for the short.
- **How to identify:** Bearish bias, then the 8:30 opening price, then a rally above it into a bearish FVG.
- **How to use:** Short inside the FVG, above the open. Target the SSL below the old daily low.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m, Mon 21 Feb 2022. The 8:30 open was at 13,798.5. Price rallied above it into a 5m FVG.

### Short-, intermediate- and long-term highs/lows (market structure nesting)  (aliases ICT uses: halos, long-term intraday high)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Swing high: a candle with a lower-high candle on its left and a lower-high candle on its right (3 candles).
  - Short-term high (STH): any swing high.
  - Intermediate-term high (ITH): a swing high with lower short-term swing highs on both sides of it.
  - Long-term high (LTH): a high with lower intermediate-term highs on both sides. On a 3m chart this is a "long-term intraday high", which he admits sounds like an oxymoron.
  - Lows are mirrored.
  - Old chart-book habit: one circle (halo) over a short-term high, two for an intermediate-term high, three for a long-term high. This classifies "the nesting effect of the highs and lows".
  - It's "far more than just lower lows and lower highs", and it's not supply and demand or support/resistance.
  - Source: Larry Williams, from his early-90s VHS course (*Future Millionaires Confidential*, title approximate) and the market-structure chapters of *How I Made One Million Dollars Last Year Trading Commodities*. ICT says it's still essential reading (minus the "moon phases" fluff), and he rereads it yearly.
- **How to identify:** Mark every 3-candle swing high. Then promote a swing high to intermediate-term when the swing highs on either side are lower. Then promote to long-term when the intermediate-term highs on either side are lower.
- **How to use:** In a bearish leg with the draw below, short close to the current intermediate-term high. It's a lower high relative to the long-term high, so it's unlikely to be exceeded and is "likely to break" lower. Backtest by classifying swing highs and lows to build trust in the model.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 3m, 21 Feb 2022. An earlier long-term intraday high had lower swing highs on either side, making the next high an intermediate-term high.
  - At 10:33, one candle back showed a high with a lower high on either side: a swing high lower than the ITH, which was lower than the LTH.
  - That nested structure (with the SSL draw below acting as a "magnet") is why he trusted the short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:28](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=628) — long-term, intermediate and short-term highs on a 3m chart · [0:11:17](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=677) — the draw below as a magnet; short near the ITH · [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=822) — swing high definition; chart-book halos (1/2/3) · [0:14:29](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=869) — nesting; Larry Williams book

### Displacement as confirmation and optimal entry near the session high
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After entering he wants to see displacement. When it comes, it's confirmation: "sit back and relax and let it do its thing."
  - He enters up near the session high (the 8:30 run-up). He doesn't wait for a breakout below the low, because that's the optimal entry. You don't need the highest high, but you must understand why price should trade away from your entry level.
  - Missed entry: after the swing high is broken and the displacement comes, if price returns to touch the FVG (the candle high) again, you can short there using the FVG entry technique.

- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**

### Exit management: market vs limit order; take profits into the raid and step aside for the gap fill
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He was watching 13,590/13,586 as an objective but didn't use a limit order. If price ran fast and far lower he didn't want a limit capping his exit, so he left it open and closed manually at 13,612.25.
  - Once price runs into the low (the liquidity), take profits and move to the sidelines. A big gap is likely to come back and rebalance (fill), and it did.
- **Example in this video:** Short 13,858.25, exit 13,612.25 = 246 handles (×4 ticks per handle for tick counters). That's $4,920 on 1 NQ mini.

### After a big move or big win: avoid the next morning session
- **Kind:** rule (risk/psychology)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a big move you don't want to trade the morning session: "just don't do it." (Consistent with the Ep. 9 overnight-run rule.)
  - He goes into a demo account to appease the urge, so he doesn't take the heat off a nice win by replacing it with a loss.
  - New traders feel an impulse to jump back in after a winner for another dopamine hit.
  - You must know when enough is enough. Social-media talk of "push your edge" is wrong: pushing it dulls it. You sharpen your edge by knowing when to get in and when to get out. People who say that are gamblers, likely unprofitable.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Daily range reference: the midnight NY opening price. Morning session reference: the 8:30 a.m. opening price.
- Bearish day: sell above the 8:30 open, inside the FVG. Down-close candles after a run-up aren't automatically bullish order blocks.
- FVG validity: judge by candle bodies; wicks can overshoot.
- Swing high = lower high on each side (3 candles). ITH = lower STHs on each side. LTH = lower ITHs on each side. Halos: 1/2/3 circles.
- Short close to the intermediate-term high when the draw is below.
- Displacement after entry = confirmation.
- U.S. holiday futures hours: stop around 1:00 p.m. NY, resume around 6:00 p.m. NY.
- Take profits into the liquidity raid. Expect a big gap to be refilled.
- After a big move or win: no morning session trading (demo instead).
- Trade: NQ short 13,858.25 (10:33 a.m., 3m) to 13,612.25 = 246 handles = $4,920 per mini. 1 handle = 4 ticks.
- Forward levels mentioned: 13,300; 12,850–12,875.

## Glossary candidates
`Short-term high (STH)` — a 3-candle swing high.
`Intermediate-term high (ITH)` — a swing high flanked by lower short-term highs.
`Long-term high (LTH)` — a high flanked by lower intermediate-term highs; on intraday charts, a "long-term intraday high".
`Halos` — 1, 2 or 3 circles marking short-, intermediate- or long-term swing points.
`Nesting` — the hierarchy of swing points inside larger swing points.
`Green line` — ICT's marked old daily low (the SSL target) in this series.
`Push your edge` — social-media idea ICT rejects; overtrading dulls your edge.
