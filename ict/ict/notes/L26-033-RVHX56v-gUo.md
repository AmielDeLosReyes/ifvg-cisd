## Concepts

### Order Block and State of Delivery Change
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** An order block is a PD array (usually on a higher timeframe like daily) where institutional order flow clustered. When price trades into that block from above (after rejection), it's a Change in State of Delivery (CISD). That entry point is where big money commits to a reversal. Watch for bodies in specific halves and wick behavior to confirm the reversal is underway.
- **How to identify:** (1) Find a fair value gap or imbalance on the daily, (2) price rejects it initially, (3) price comes back and trades INTO it, (4) watch bodies stay in lower half (if bouncing upward) or upper half (if continuing downward) as confirmation. (5) Once bodies show "clean" placement away from the gap's midpoint, that's the CISD.
- **How to use:** Entry point. Go long/short into the order block with tight stops at the far end of the gap. Add on confirmation (bodies doing the right thing). Trail stops above/below minor swings as price moves in your favor.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily has a buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (FVG). Price trades into it at 8:30 ET on the PPI print. The gap is from [top of FVG range] down to [bottom]. Once price enters and stays below the midpoint, bodies remain in lower half → long entry confirmed. Rallies from there to Wednesday's high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:37](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=337) — defining the order block and CISD; [0:06:27](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=387) — entry and stop placement
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The order block is typically a much larger, more stable level than intraday ranges. Trading into it after a failed rejection is high-probability.

### Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency (BSI/SSI) Fair Value Gap
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** A gap where there's no volume (unfilled space) in one direction. On a daily chart, if there's a bullish move (up-close) followed by a gap up at the open, the gap is a buy-side imbalance (buyers moved price higher); if price then rejects this gap on a down-close, that gap becomes a sell-side inefficiency (sellers dominated that session). These are key institutional entry/exit zones.
- **How to identify:** Look for gaps on the daily after strong directional closes. The gap direction and the follow-up close tell you which side (buy/sell) is in control.
- **How to use:** Entry on pullback into the gap. If bullish, enter long on any probe into the gap's lower half. If bearish, enter short on any test of the upper half.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily has a gap from Friday → Monday. Sellers rejected it (down close). That makes it a buy-side imbalance (Friday's move) with sell-side inefficiency (Monday's rejection). When price trades back down into it mid-day on PPI, it's the entry for a long.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:50](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=290); [0:05:37](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=337) — defining the gap structure
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Imbalances and inefficiencies are flip sides of the same gap; the functional role depends on context (what happens after the gap).

### Wick Consequent Encroachment as Daily Projection
- **Kind:** example + technique
- **What ICT says:** A wick on a daily can be measured via Fibonacci to find its midpoint (CE). This midpoint often aligns with intraday support/resistance levels or prior highs/lows. It's a geometric projection that price respects with precision. Can be used to set stops or confirm reversals.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify a significant wick on the daily, (2) Apply Fibonacci from body close to wick extreme, (3) Mark the 0.5 level (CE). (4) Watch if that level aligns with prior swing highs/lows or daily highs from earlier weeks.
- **How to use:** Stop-loss placement. Confirmation level. If price touches wick's CE and bounces, it's rejection; if it closes through, it's a reversal signal.
- **Example in this video:** Large wick on daily; its CE aligns directly on top of Wednesday's daily high. When price pulls back intraday, it stops at that CE level, confirming the bounce. ICT uses this as a stopping point for covering shorts.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:27](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=387) — showing wick measurement; [0:08:03](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=483) — Wednesday's high = wick's CE; [0:08:54](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=534) — stop placement
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This alignment is often "brilliant" (ICT's word) because it's precise; not random, but mechanistic—the algorithm respects geometric levels.

### Relative Equal Highs as Resistance in Daily Context
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** When the daily chart shows two or more highs at approximately the same level, that's a relative equal high. It's a zone where momentum historically failed, so price must "bust through" it with significant follow-through to make a new high. If price just pokes above and closes back down, it's a false breakout. Clearing it cleanly (with wicks/bodies above and no rejection) suggests continuation higher.
- **How to identify:** Zoom out to daily chart; mark the last two swing highs. Are they within ~50–100 points? If yes, that's a REH zone.
- **How to use:** Resistance. On a break above, add longs or short-cover confirmation. On a rejection, short into the level. If body closes above and away, it's bullish breakout signal.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily shows relative equal highs at 30,094. Price needs to break above this to reach Wednesday's high (30,600 area). Once it clears 30,094 cleanly, the path to Wednesday's high opens up. ICT calls 30,600 and 30,975 as additional targets after breaking REH.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:14](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=434) — wick measurement; [0:09:42](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=582) — identifying REH on daily; [0:10:29](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=629) — projecting targets past REH
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** REH is most powerful on daily; less so on lower timeframes. Once cleared, don't expect price to come back into it easily.

### Event-Driven Price Action (News Events and Trend Reversals)
- **Kind:** observation + example
- **What ICT says:** Major economic prints (CPI, PPI, NFP) cause sudden moves that can flip intraday bias. Before the event, price consolidates or exhibits uncertain behavior. At the event release, momentum shifts instantly. The direction is often predictable if you've marked key daily levels beforehand (highs, gaps, octants). Events don't create opportunity randomly—they allow the algorithm to execute pre-planned reversals at key times/prices.
- **How to identify:** (1) Know the economic calendar, (2) Mark daily support/resistance well before the event, (3) Watch how price approaches the event time (calm vs. nervous candles), (4) At the release, expect a directional break from consolidation.
- **How to use:** Plan entries/exits before the event. Don't fade the initial move; ride it into your pre-marked targets. Take partials at milestones (prior highs, FVG midpoints, relative equal highs). Trail the runner if bullish bias is confirmed.
- **Example in this video:** CPI yesterday (Aug 12) and PPI today (Aug 13 at 8:30 ET). Before PPI, NQ was consolidating and failing downside (ICT's initial short bias). At 8:30, the PPI beat bullish sent price higher into pre-marked targets (Wednesday's high, REH, old highs). ICT flipped from short to long and rode the move with partials.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:49](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=49) — pre-event consolidation; [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=243) — the 8:30 move; [0:05:37](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=337) — flipping to long
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Events are catalysts but not the cause of moves; the levels and order flow were already set. Events just trigger the execution.

### Pseudo-Diagonal Trend Line Monitoring
- **Kind:** observation
- **What ICT says:** In consolidation or before directional breaks, price sometimes traces a subtle diagonal (not a strong trend, but a lean). ICT watches for these "pseudo" trend lines and bets against them—they often mark the breakout point. He doesn't emphasize trend lines heavily (as he dislikes them), but he enjoys "seeing them victimized" (broken).
- **How to identify:** Draw a line through two or more candle bodies that lean up or down; watch if price breaks cleanly above/below.
- **How to use:** Breakout confirmation. If price breaks above the pseudo-line with a clean body, expect continuation. This supplements other signals (FVGs, REH, etc.).
- **Example in this video:** Before PPI, NQ showed a pseudo-diagonal structure consolidating downward. The midpoint of that diagonal was a reference. At 8:30, price broke above and never came back—diagonal was "victimized" (broken).

### Event Horizon (Fibonacci 50% of a Multi-Day Range)
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** Measure from one daily high to another (e.g., Tuesday's high to Monday's high), find the midpoint (50% Fibonacci level), and project it forward. That level often acts as a ceiling on strong bullish days or a floor on bearish days. It's a psychological equilibrium point where momentum often fades briefly before continuing.
- **How to identify:** Select two daily highs/lows separated by a few days. Apply Fibonacci; the 0.5 level = event horizon.
- **How to use:** Partial-taking target. Resistance on intraday rallies. If price blows through it cleanly, expect continued momentum. If it stalls there, it might retrace.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's high to Monday's high, 50% = event horizon. Price reaches it intraday, hesitates, then blows through on continued strength. ICT calls this out as a reference level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:03](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=483) — measuring event horizon; [0:08:54](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=534) — price action at/through it
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Works better on intraday charts after a strong directional break. Less reliable on choppy days.

## Glossary candidates
`Event horizon` — (derived from trading term) the 50% Fibonacci level of a multi-day range (e.g., Monday high to Tuesday high). Often acts as a psychological equilibrium; price hesitates or reverses there on strong intraday moves.

`CISD` — Change in State of Delivery; the moment when price enters a PD array (order block) and bodies confirm the new direction, signaling institutional commitment to a reversal.

`Pseudo-diagonal` — a subtle, non-rigorous trend line that leans up/down in consolidation; used more to see when it's "victimized" (broken) than as a primary signal.

`Relative equal high / low` — two or more highs (or lows) at approximately the same price level on a daily chart; represents a zone where momentum historically failed, requiring a clean break with follow-through to signal continuation.

`Wick consequent encroachment` — the 0.5 Fibonacci level of a wick (from body to wick extreme); often aligns with prior daily highs/lows and acts as intraday support/resistance or stop-loss placement.
