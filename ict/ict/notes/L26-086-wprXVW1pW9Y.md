## Summary
ICT dissects his Thursday (May 14, 2026) NQ session at all-time highs to show that time-and-price logic still works when the market is in a one-sided bull run. Time is the first filter: charts on New York local time, the 9:30 open, the 11:30 to 1:30 "New York lunch" (the lunch macro, 2 hours) and the 1:30 to 2:00 PM opening range. Price is then handled with the RTH opening range gap (9:30 open versus the prior 4:15 PM close): its retest acts as discount sensitivity in a bull market, and the gap's height, used as a unit and projected upward in multiples ("standard deviations", about 6.5 to 8), gives a turning point that lands inside the first hour of the lunch window. He shorts at the 8x projection into an inversion FVG, takes profit at minor sell-side liquidity and at "event horizon" (50% of a fib), and explains why, because he is trading against the higher-timeframe bull, he exits early rather than waiting for the exact relative-equal lows. He also warns that the market is in "capitulation" (absurdly one-sided) and that an intermediate-term high is due.

## Concepts

### Time-first framework (New York local time, 9:30, first hour, 11:30 lunch, 1:30–2:00 PM opening range)  (aliases ICT uses: "it's always time first, then price")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Always keep the chart on New York local time. If you are anchored elsewhere you are "off time, late or early", and moves that look like they come from nowhere are just time misreads.
  - Mark 9:30 (the open). The first hour after 9:30 is the first hour's dealing range.
  - 11:30 AM ET starts the New York lunch "without fail" (it can start sooner, he says it "will always start at 11:30"). The New York lunch is a 2-hour period (11:30 to 1:30).
  - 1:30 to 2:00 PM is the opening range of the PM ("4:00 PM") session: all PD arrays in that range are salient for the PM session, the final hour, and the 6:00 PM restart.
  - Price action is read relative to a time grid first; staring at every little candle makes you "lose the forest for the trees", and "the thing that matters most is time".
  - He says algorithmically everything is based on time, then price: "the code, the sequence, the scripting of these markets they follow a sequence."
- **How to identify:** put vertical lines at 9:30, 10:00 (a reference), 11:30, 12:30 (end of the first lunch hour), 1:30 and 2:00 ET.
- **How to use:** use the time lines to decide when to look for a setup (see Lunch Macro) and which swing lows/highs count as targets.

### RTH Opening Range Gap (discount opening range gap) and its retest as discount sensitivity  (aliases: "regular trading hours opening range gap", "discount array", "trampoline")
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The RTH opening range gap is the space between the close of the prior session's last candle (the 4:15 PM ET settle) and today's 9:30 open. A gap lower is a "discount opening range gap": price opened below where it settled the day before, i.e. it is being "offered at a discount".
  - Classic expectation: a premium/discount gap tries to fill; the gap closure happens either way, whether you short it or buy it.
  - But if you trade against the higher-timeframe freight train (here a violent bull run), shorting the fill is not a trade for a new trader.
  - Better use: if price overcomes the gap (a candle goes up through/over it), then the very next candle opens and trades back down to it, and the gap now "acts as a discount array". In a bull market, that first return into the gap is the original first run into the range and should act as discount sensitivity: price uses it like a trampoline, drops in, then runs energetically away.
  - If you miss that move, do not chase candle by candle; anchor with a vertical line at the open so you keep time in view.
- **How to identify:** compare the 4:15 PM ET close of the previous day with the 9:30 open; mark the gap box between them (open as the low, previous close as the high on a gap-down day).
- **How to use:** long on the first retest into the gap after price has overcome it, with the higher-timeframe bias bullish; use the gap's height as the unit of measurement for upward projections (next concept).
- **Example in this video:** NQ opens at 9:30 below the prior close (gap-down). Price immediately overcomes the gap on one candle, the very next candle opens and trades back down into it, and it bounces energetically. ICT also notes he traded the "full mini, not the micro".
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:56](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=116) — defines today's RTH opening range gap and expects upside draw; [0:10:35](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=635) — discount gap versus 4:15 PM settle; [0:13:45](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=825) — retest of the overcome gap acts as a discount array; [0:14:32](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=872) — "trampoline".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** restates and extends his opening-gap teaching: the gap is used (a) as support after being overcome and (b) as a ruler for projections.

### 8:30 micro vs. mini divergence redelivery (news-event stop hunt)  (aliases: "re-deliver", "rerun")
- **Kind:** rule / example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - He posted on X that when a market makes a spike at the 8:30 news event in one contract (micro NQ went down precipitously) but not in the other (mini did not), the market "generally wants to redeliver that": it will go back to repeat that same price run. If the move had been higher, it would repeat that run higher.
  - It does not just overlap the level; it goes through it and operates as a stop hunt.
  - On the day: the micro made a spike low at 8:30; the mini did not. Price later traded lower to take out that low, taking out all the short-term sell-side liquidity, "primed everyone to anticipate it going lower", and then rallied. You can capitalise with either the micro or the mini.
  - He mentions a "bread and butter" setup on a 30-second chart using micros.
- **How to identify:** compare micro and mini NQ around 8:30; a one-sided spike low (or high) in one product that the other lacks marks the level to be revisited.
- **How to use:** expect a revisit and a stop run beyond that spike extreme before the real move; trade the reversal after the sweep.
- **Example in this video:** NQ micro prints a spike low at 8:30; mini does not; at the open price runs lower again, takes the 8:30 low and the short-term sell-side, then extends to the upside through the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:44](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=164) — micro vs mini at 8:30 and redelivery; [0:03:30](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=210) — "goes through it and operates as a stop hunt"; [0:04:17](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=257) — sell-side liquidity taken, then extrapolates to the upside.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** an intermarket-style (instrument-divergence) SMT-like observation inside one index; not a standard named concept, so treat as a coinage-free observation.

### Lunch Macro (11:30 to 1:30 ET): first low to the left of 11:30, setup forms in the first hour  (aliases: "lunch macro", "New York lunch")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 11:30 a 2-hour window begins where "whatever took place in the morning session will roll against that": a retracement back to a low that formed after 10:00 ET.
  - If the morning was one-sided (all buying, here all-time highs), expect the lunch macro to retrace the morning move.
  - Which low: from 11:30 look left toward 10:00 and use the first low you come to (he calls it the best-case lunch macro). Lows right before 10:00 are not used; the proper pick is the first meaningful one after 10:00. Use the very first one when probabilities are against you (deep retracements) or when the market is very strong from the morning.
  - A good lunch-macro low (his pick): relative equal lows that had "a nice run up" first, left the high intact, then a deep retracement, and the biggest run started from there.
  - Timing within the window: the setup optimally forms in the first hour (11:30 to 12:30), especially after one-sided morning price; the delivery can then take place in the second hour.
- **How to identify:** vertical lines at 10:00, 11:30 and 12:30 ET; the first swing low to the left of 11:30 after 10:00; check for relative equal lows.
- **How to use:** in a strong up day, look for the high of the morning to be reached before or at 11:30, expect the retracement in the first hour of the window and the delivery to the low by 1:30.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, 14 May 2026: low at 10:29 AM of 29,595.50 (relative equal lows). The lunch macro delivery came down and took that low at 1:26 PM with a low of 29,595.25, i.e. by one tick, just before 1:30.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:07](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=967) — "That's the lunch macro... going back for a low after 10:00"; [0:19:19](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1159) — use the first low to the left of 11:30; [0:20:53](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1253) — low prices 29,595.50 at 10:29 vs 29,595.25 at 1:26; [0:32:38](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1958) — setup forms in the first hour 11:30–12:30.

### Opening Range Gap "standard deviations": the gap height as a ruler with a time window (Market Alchemy)  (aliases: "multiplication table", "replications / standard deviations", "projection")
- **Kind:** new coinage / entry-timing model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Draw a Fibonacci tool from the opening price (9:30 open, the low of the gap) to the prior close (top of the gap): that height is "one standard deviation". Project it upward multiple times (1, 2, 3 ... 7, 7.5, 8).
  - Think of a multiplication table: the vertical axis is price multiples, the horizontal axis is time. First go up the vertical axis (count the multiples), then when price enters the time window (here 11:30) look horizontally; the right multiple "unlocks like the tumblers in a lock".
  - He estimates the reachable count with a thumb: he holds his thumb out until it spans the gap height, then steps it up the screen; roughly 6.5 to 7.5 deviations.
  - Testing on the day: 6.5 and 7 were already exceeded before 11:30, so they could not be the turning level. 7.5 (caption: "2.5") was reached just before 11:30 (wonderful but not the turn). 8 deviations is the next level: price turned there at the start of the first hour of the lunch window.
  - Earlier teaching restated: when the market is consolidating, measure the opening range gap at 0.25 and 0.50 (graded levels). Everything is "graded" because the market is algorithmic.
  - An independent projection: the 10:00 to 11:30 run measured with a Fibonacci projection also points to the same vicinity.
  - ICT says this level-by-time technique is "not found anywhere else", and that parts are being held back for his written books but he will teach it on the channel.
- **How to identify:** (1) gap box = 9:30 open to prior 4:15 PM close; (2) project multiples upward; (3) the candidate multiple is the one not yet exceeded before 11:30; (4) watch candle bodies at that multiple inside 11:30–12:30.
- **How to use:** as the profit-taking or reversal-short zone on strong up days: level (multiple) plus time (lunch window) together define the turning point, and a short is taken from there (see Inversion FVG short).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 14 May 2026: gap = open to prior 4:15 close; 8 deviations is where price stalls in the first hour of the lunch window; he calls it "a turning point randomly in the first hour of the 2-hour lunch period" and challenges "talk to me about not picking a daily high".
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:28](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=448) — multiplication-table analogy; [0:27:56](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1676) — Fibonacci from the open to the close of the gap; [0:30:16](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1816) — 0.25 and 0.50 grading in consolidations; [0:33:25](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2005) — thumb measurement; [0:35:02](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2102) — test 6.5, 7, 7.5, then 8.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** counts are approximate in the captions (caption unclear around "2.5"/"7.5" and "6.5 to 7.5"). He says you will need to practise daily: it feels cumbersome at first. The Market Alchemy title is not explicitly explained; this section is the main new material.

### Inversion FVG short at the time/price turning point (entry model)  (aliases: "inversion fair value gap", "buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency with a volume imbalance")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - At the 8-deviation level, on the next candle price opened and "didn't go higher than the previous one", only a little wick, then sank. "When it dropped down in there, I already knew we're done" because little time remained before the close of the first lunch hour.
  - Entry structure: a buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (BISI) with a volume imbalance at the top. A candle takes the high and reaches up to the low of that volume imbalance.
  - Why an inversion FVG: the next candle was unwilling to go to the previous candle's high and take it out. That unwillingness plus a drop through = the bullish gap flips into a bearish inversion FVG. "You have to be real quick, Johnny-on-the-spot, or miss it."
  - Fill at the upper portion of the inversion FVG. If the order had not filled there, the next candle's open went right back up to the consequent encroachment of that level, another chance.
  - After the CE tag, nobody wants to buy any higher than that level: "an invitation goes out" for short sellers to run price lower.
  - A second bullish PD array drawn later was run through "as we should have" with a small run up to its high.
- **How to identify:** time (first hour of the lunch window) + price (8x gap-height projection) + a bullish gap that fails to extend and inverts.
- **How to use:** enter short at the upper part of the iFVG (or at the CE retest); stop above (he says the worst that happens is a normal stop-out); first target is the nearest minor sell-side low (first partial), second target event horizon, final target just above/below the relative equal lows (see Event Horizon).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, 14 May 2026 about 11:30–12:00 ET: price reaches the 8x level, one candle fails to exceed the prior high, price drops through the BISI/volume imbalance; short filled at the upper portion of the inversion FVG; the next candle re-tests the CE and the market rolls lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:37:21](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2241) — new candle fails to go higher, "we're done"; [0:38:56](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2336) — BISI with volume imbalance at the top; [0:40:31](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2431) — fill at the upper portion of the iFVG and why it is an iFVG; [0:41:18](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2478) — CE re-test, buyers disappear.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he draws the PD arrays only after the market works through them (he keeps the chart naked until then). He notes this is a trade against the higher-timeframe order flow.

### Event Horizon (50% of the fib drawn between two lows)  (aliases ICT uses: "event horizon")
- **Kind:** rule (target) / definition (consistent with L26-074)
- **What ICT says:** after the first partial at the minor sell-side low, he drew a fib between that low and the next low and "50% is this line... that's event horizon". It is the second take-profit area when trading against higher-timeframe order flow. Price "took the profit below that", i.e. his exit filled at event horizon.
- **How to identify:** fib between two liquidity points (here two lows); 50% line.
- **How to use:** second scale-out level; the final target is just beyond the relative equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** short from the iFVG, partial at minor sell-side, event horizon partial, remaining contracts at the relative equal lows (one tick beyond); price then ran lower without him.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:52](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2572) — fib 50% = event horizon; [0:44:26](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2666) — profit taken at event horizon, then target below the relative equal lows.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** event horizon here is between two lows (the sell-side counterpart of the L26-074 definition, where it is the midpoint between a 1-hour minor BSL pool and an old high).

### Minor vs. primary sell-side liquidity, and exiting early against the higher-timeframe trend
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Minor" sell-side is not the real sell-side he expects price to go for; the primary sell-side is the relative equal lows (the 10:29 low). Price reached the primary sell-side during the lunch macro just before 1:30 and then the market could go higher.
  - He marks minor sell-side lows as profit targets first and places limit exits slightly above the primary low, because price only took the 10:29 low by one tick at 1:26 PM (29,595.25 vs 29,595.50). An order sitting at or just beneath that low would never have filled ("too shallow"); experience shows exits must be "a tick or two" earlier.
  - Reason: trading against a bull market, if he is wrong "it can violently shift against me", so he takes "easy objectives" ("the cookie", the moral victory) rather than the best-case low.
  - He explains that exits normally have "that little overrun": the market goes a bit further without him; that is not a failure of the analysis.
  - Also keep time in mind: do not spend too much time in the trade as the 1:30 end of the lunch macro approaches.
- **How to use:** short against HTF bias: aim for minor sell-side first, take profit, accept being early.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:53](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1253) — the 1-tick overrun; [0:21:40](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1300) — exit placement; [0:24:49](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1489) — minor vs primary sell-side; [0:26:22](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1582) — "little overrun", time risk.

### Trading at all-time highs: capitulation, one-sidedness, and who should short
- **Kind:** rule / context (higher-timeframe narrative)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The market is in "capitulation": extremely one-sided with no meaningful retracement ("the Fonz jumping the shark", absurd levels). Either it keeps going higher or crashes, but "an intermediate-term high forming is warranted". He compares it to gold and silver, where he told people it would be "uncivilized" not to take profits; both then made deep retracements; indices are next.
  - People are primed to think "the market only goes up" until it violently shifts lower. Do not trade without stop losses.
  - A new trader should not short a bull market: "you should be afraid to take the short because you don't have the experience": study it, paper trade it, tape read it. If you must trade against the freight train you must be highly selective.
  - He is not afraid: the worst case is a normal stop-out; the idea that being stopped at all-time highs hurts more than other shorts is a myth that experience dispels.
  - Without a primary bull trend he would have targeted the relative-equal lows directly; against the trend he must be prudent.
  - After the trade, the market is "in slop": do not trade; let the overnight session pass and look for something in the pre-market session (about 7:00 to 9:00 ET).
- **How to use:** with a strong bull trend, favour longs; if shorting, use time (lunch macro) + price (8x) + iFVG, take profits early; avoid trading mid-range slop.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:09](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=729) — shorting against a bull trend requires selectivity; [0:17:43](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1063) — capitulation; [0:22:27](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1347) — who should short; [0:47:38](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2858) — don't trade the slop, wait for pre-market.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Chart on New York local time; mark 9:30, 10:00, 11:30, 12:30, 1:30, 2:00 ET.
- RTH opening range gap = prior 4:15 PM ET close vs 9:30 open.
- 11:30 starts the New York lunch; it is a 2-hour window (11:30–1:30). The setup forms in the first hour (11:30–12:30); delivery can come in the second.
- Use the first low to the left of 11:30 that formed after 10:00 (not lows right before 10:00).
- 1:30–2:00 PM = PM opening range; its PD arrays matter for the PM session, last hour and the 6:00 PM restart.
- Opening range gap unit; projections roughly 6.5–7.5 candidates, 8 was the turning level on the day; consolidations use 0.25 and 0.50.
- Day numbers (NQ): low 29,595.50 at 10:29; low 29,595.25 at 1:26 PM (one tick lower).
- Event horizon = 50% of fib between the two lows used.
- Against the HTF trend: take the first partial at minor sell-side and the next at event horizon; exit before the exact primary low.
- Do not trade without stops. In slop, wait for the next session (pre-market about 7:00–9:00 ET).
- FX aside: while the dollar index is range-stuck, trade pairs without USD (exotics).
- Aside: "Zerocon" (caption unclear) is a timing strategy he promises to teach later in the summer.
