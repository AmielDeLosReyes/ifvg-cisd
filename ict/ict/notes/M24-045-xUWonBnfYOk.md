## Summary
On Wed Oct 16, 2024 the three US stock index averages (NQ, ES, Dow) were moving in different directions in the NY morning session. ICT uses it to teach "decoupling": when the averages are not in agreement, precision/"resolution" drops, probability drops, and the right action is to observe (or wait for them to re-sync later in the day) rather than trade. Live he calls an SMT-type divergence in advance (NQ makes a lower low, ES refuses to; Dow ignored), then a return into the opening range gap and the first presented FVG, using an inversion FVG as the entry area. The second half is about being highly selective, saying "no" more than "yes", when you are ready for real money, and why he trades intraday rather than holding.

## Concepts

### Decoupled Markets (index averages not in agreement)  (aliases: "decoupling", "mixed direction on all three averages")
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage (market-condition filter)
- **What ICT says:**
  - For stock-index trading all three averages (NQ, ES, YM/Dow) should be moving in the same direction. If you trade NQ and want to be long, you want to see support for the idea from ES and Dow as well, "all three bullish".
  - Decoupling is common when no news is due that morning. It makes precision harder; you trade at lower probability. He has traded it, but a new trader/new student should not.
  - Reading price becomes "fuzzy", "out of focus": candles overlap the previous candle's range, a lot of revisiting of the same prices (seen in ES) versus the clean, refined ranges and imbalances in NQ.
  - Choices when decoupled: (1) sit and wait for the averages to come into agreement and move in tandem (sometimes within the same session, sometimes only after the morning, so the afternoon becomes better); (2) worst case they stay decoupled all day and are very problematic, tradable only by very short-term scalpers, which he does not teach.
  - Even when price still delivers to PD arrays and liquidity, that does not make it a high-probability trade; delivery to the sell side happened and still "shouldn't have" been traded.
  - Write in your notes: as soon as you see decoupling, expect an SMT-type divergence later in the session after which they go back in sync.
- **How to identify:** compare the three charts; Dow going one way while ES and NQ do something else; on 10/16 at 9:00 ES dropped while Dow went higher and NQ also dropped (so NQ and ES shared direction briefly but Dow did not).
- **How to use:** Be extra selective; treat these days as observation/teaching; "give yourself permission" to let a trade pan out without you when precision is unlikely; accept some days offer no low-risk entry or no entry at all. If instead all three were in agreement, price returned to the first presented FVG and then showed displacement, "then it's easy": drop to a 15-second chart and take the next FVG in that direction.
- **Example in this video:** NQ/ES/YM 1m, Wed Oct 16, 2024 AM session. Dow was going higher/consolidating ("unruly brat"), NQ weak, ES shrugging off weakness. Opening range gap anemic again. NQ took sell side, never traded back up into its first presented FVG initially, then after the sell side run made a lower low while ES held and returned into the range.

### SMT Divergence between NQ and ES (Dow excluded)  (aliases: "SMT", "unwillingness to go lower", "shrugging off weakness")
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - When NQ makes a lower low and ES fails to make a lower low, that is SMT divergence and signals the bulls' side: price will return up into its range. Called "shrugging off any weakness" = failing to take the sell side.
  - He forecast it before it happened: "if es fails to shrug off ... I'm sorry, if it fails and shrugs off weakness, that means it fails to drop down", then NQ would go back into its range and return to the first presented FVG.
  - He does NOT count the Dow in SMT: the Dow's 30 stocks "are not that impactful overall." If Dow makes a lower low and ES does not, he doesn't consider that SMT. Normally the Dow bends to the will of ES and NQ ("all boats rise in high tide"); when it doesn't, that is the decoupling signal.
  - Conditions for return to agreement: Dow either holds/consolidates or keeps going higher (doesn't lose ground), ES fails to make a lower low, NQ makes the lower low then comes back into its range.
- **How to use:** Used as the catalyst for the long-side (return into range) and to decide where to anchor risk. Also it conditions the iFVG: if ES refuses to go lower, the NQ FVG that forms below becomes an inversion FVG.
- **Example in this video:** NQ took its sell side (a small low), went a little under it to make a lower low, ES stopped above its low ("low here at 9:51" caption unclear), NQ then returned up above the sell side into the opening range gap/first presented FVG.

### Inversion FVG entry: entering before it "proves itself"  (aliases: "iFVG", "inversion fair value gap", "breakaway gap")
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A bearish FVG (formed on the way down, an imbalance in the down leg) becomes an inversion FVG when price trades back up through it while bullish. Here he pointed out the FVG in NQ was bearish and might invert because ES would not go lower.
  - Caveat: if the market is very strong, price can accumulate in/near it and "run right out" without ever coming back down to touch it (it then acts like a breakaway gap). He says he has no rule for knowing when that happens; it's an experience "knack". "I don't know how to teach that" (a limitation as a mentor).
  - His workaround when convicted bullish: enter INSIDE the iFVG as price first touches the candle that enters it (top of the candle/gap), or at the CE of the gap and watch the bodies at that midpoint. First 6 contracts at that level, and a further 4 if price drops to the gap's high (the low of the candle). He would rather do that than wait for price to leave and come back ("last to the dance floor"), because sometimes there is no second chance and you miss both the fill and the pyramid.
  - If very bullish, why wait to buy a retrace after price has moved away? Take the cheap price inside the iFVG.
  - Also wanted the gap above to "stay open" (preferably unfilled) as that fuels a run (breakaway) rather than a pullback.
- **How to identify:** look for the down-leg inefficiency (bearish FVG/volume imbalance) that price subsequently trades above after a sell-side run + SMT; mark the midpoint (CE); watch candle bodies open/close at that midpoint.
- **How to use:** Entry at the first touch of the iFVG's top or at the CE; add on a return to the gap's other edge; objective: first presented FVG and the opening range gap / buy side above; stop below the low (he said no entry for shorts as no way to frame risk).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Oct 16, after the sell side was taken and the lower low printed, price reclaimed back into the range, traded up through the bearish FVG; later he noted a bearish order block (two consecutive up-closed candles) price traded back into, and the bodies landed on the CE. Price never returned to touch the iFVG; the gap stayed open and price broke away higher, so the "second entry" didn't occur. Objective was the first presented FVG, plus the buy side/old highs above.

  - [0:40:46](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2446) — it may just run away; no rule for when
  - [0:45:43](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2743) — "last to the dance floor" argument; midpoint where bodies deliver

### First Presented FVG + Opening Range Gap as objective  (aliases: "first presented fair value gap", "opening range gap", "gap closure")
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** The first FVG presented after the RTH open (here sharing the same price "real estate" as the opening range gap) is a draw; it is "coded" to be returned to. Price delivered to sell side without trading back up into the first presented FVG, then later reached back up to it (after SMT). Opening gap here was again "anemic" (settlement price vs. open drawn on RTH view). He wanted the FVG to remain open so price could go back into the opening range gap and first presented FVG, and buy side above (ES: two highs, one lower than another).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m 9:30–10:30. First FVG formed near the opening range gap area. Price took sell side, made a lower low, returned into the opening range ("two blue lines"), then up to the first presented FVG, with bodies respecting it ("completely random isn't it? no it's coded"). Phil's request to do it before 10:30 is just the time boundary of the stream.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:42](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1122) — anemic opening gap, first FVG forms in same area; [0:20:18](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1218) — conditional scenario; [1:00:20](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3620) — "that's what it's coded to do"; [1:09:56](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4196) — bodies respect the first presented FVG.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Calls the structure a "market maker buy model" and says it does not have to clear the original consolidation, "but you have to know where it's going to draw to."

### Bodies vs. Wicks: CE of wicks, volume imbalance, and body behavior inside FVGs  (aliases: "bodies stay at consequent encroachment", "wicks do the damage")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - In a clean FVG/order block the candle BODIES stay at the consequent encroachment or on one side of it; the wicks are allowed to do the "damage". If bodies stay out of the gap, the level is respected.
  - Volume imbalance: price body stops at the high of the imbalance/at the open, doesn't breach it; when a body starts in an imbalance and leaves without coming back that is weakness.
  - Marking the midpoint (CE) of a gap or wick with a rectangle highlights where to watch bodies.
  - Time-based candles (open/high/low/close) are the "advantage": range bars, Renko, Heikin-Ashi hide the algorithm and hide intervention; "price is the same price on any timeframe."
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m (about 9:45–10:00): bodies of a pair of bearish-order-block candles settle at the CE of the gap; the wick pierces CE ("damage"), then price rotates out of a volume imbalance toward sell side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:38](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=878) — bodies stay at CE or lower; [0:37:35](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2255) — body stops at the volume-imbalance high; [0:46:29](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2789) — bodies close/open at the midpoint; time-based candles advantage.

### Change in State of Delivery (CISD) via the open of consecutive up-closed candles  (aliases: "change in a state of delivery", "order block")
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:** When price has been delivering with two (or more) consecutive up-closed candles (buy-side delivery), use the OPEN of the lowest/first of those up-closed candles as the reference. A move back below that open = change in the state of delivery (price was delivering higher, now delivers lower). He ties it to an order block ("your order block or change in state of delivery").
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m ~9:35–9:40 ET: two consecutive up-close candles; open of the lowest is the CISD line; price returned to it, traded lower, then delivered to the low of a candle with a 1-tick overshoot (levels read as ~20,37x, caption unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:49](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1369) — CISD defined as the opening price of the lowest of the consecutive up candles; [0:23:38](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1418) — perfect delivery to candle low plus one tick.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Plus one tick per spread" — he mentions spread as the reason price trades one tick past the exact level (consistent with the "one tick beyond CE" idea from M24-044).

### Why NQ and not ES; instrument choice  (example only)
- **Kind:** rule/personal preference
- **What ICT says:** He prefers NQ because of volatility and more exaggerated delivery (100 stocks vs 500 in ES); he needs movement; ES is slower, "more well-rounded." ([0:26:03](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1563)–[0:27:39](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1659)).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Trade NQ only when ES and Dow agree (at least don't have to be fighting); if all three in agreement and NQ returns to the first presented FVG and shows displacement, drop to a 15-second chart and take the next FVG.
- Do not enter when no low-risk entry can be framed, even if you know where price will go; delivery to a draw on liquidity "is not enough."
- SMT: NQ lower low + ES no lower low = divergence; exclude the Dow.
- iFVG entry: first 6 contracts at first touch/CE, then +4 at the far edge if it comes; may not get second fill.
- Wait for a first presented FVG and opening range gap objectives before 10:30 on this day (stream ended ~10:30; ran 22 minutes over).
- Mentions 9:00 as the time ES started dropping while Dow rose.
- Risk model: fixed percentage per trade, e.g. 1%; risk percentage never increases; position size scales with equity (see Process).
- Stop loss in every trade and managed ("always there").

## Process, risk & psychology
- Be highly selective on the days/sessions to enter; "all the things going for you" instead of "any old thing" ([0:10:34](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=634)). Journaling reinforces this.
- Bad days make better teaching environments; "nobody wants to talk about these" ([0:13:02](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=782)).
- Observe without participating: watch them paint, discuss with others, send screenshots, but don't be a participant ([0:16:17](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=977)).
- Don't chase a concept you just learned or copy community members' latest success; impatience, no model/protocol, forcing concepts in conditions the author wouldn't use leads to blown accounts, failed combines, funded accounts lost ([0:24:27](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1467), [0:28:27](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1707)).
- First lesson is capital preservation: know in which market environment you'll lose money ([0:53:38](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3218)).
- Use YouTube comments as a personal journal of timestamps ([0:30:51](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1851)).
- Don't set a date to be profitable or quit your job; he kept pushing those deadlines back in the 90s ([1:13:56](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4436)).
- Readiness for live money: you are ready when you can sit in front of a chart without outside influence (including him), see what price will do, know entry and stop, and have no emotional charge around the outcome, not trying to fill an emptiness in your life with trades. Earlier = scar tissue, blown account ([1:14:45](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4485), [1:15:32](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4532), [1:16:21](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4581)). Practice with "no monetary link to price action."
- Say "no" more often than "yes"; saying yes only to high-probability setups gives longevity; losses won't send you into tilt ([1:17:12](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4632), [1:18:00](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4680)).
- Record trades with a stop loss and manage them; screenshots after the fact with no stop are worthless ([1:18:48](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4728)).
- He doesn't take thousands of trades a month; he wants "low resistance liquidity runs": slow ride in, no friction/adversity ([1:19:37](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4777), [1:20:24](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4824)).
- Why intraday: velocity; rolling an account across many setups on lower timeframes rather than holding for months; he dislikes pyramiding new equity in long holds. Fixed % risk (e.g., 1%): as equity grows, contracts/lots grow but percentage risk does not ([1:21:11](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4871), [1:22:00](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4920)). Compares to high-frequency trading firms.
- Early career story (1992): started a month after reading a book, blew half the account on first trade (an orange juice option trade); a lesson in not trading real money too early ([1:16:21](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4581)).
- Note: the transcript contains long rants about educators, copyright strikes, and "Goldman Sachs alumni" saying lower timeframes are noise; the only trading substance there is the claim that price behaves the same on all timeframes and he wants to trade lower timeframes by timing.

## Glossary candidates
`DECOUPLING` — the three US index averages (NQ, ES, Dow) not moving in the same direction; reduces precision.
`SHRUGGING OFF WEAKNESS` — an index (here ES) failing to make a lower low while another (NQ) does; sets up SMT.
`FIRST PRESENTED FVG` — the first FVG that forms after the open; a draw price is "coded" to return to.
`BREAKAWAY GAP` — a gap left open that price never returns to, driving a run away from the PD array.
`PROTRACTION` — as in M24-044, one-sided delivery.
`LOW RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN` — smooth run to liquidity with no friction, the kind of trade he wants.
`HIGH TIDE ANALOGY` — Dow should rise/fall in sympathy with ES and NQ when the move is real.
`MARKET MAKER BUY MODEL` — used here for the rally-from-consolidation structure; doesn't need to clear the original range.
