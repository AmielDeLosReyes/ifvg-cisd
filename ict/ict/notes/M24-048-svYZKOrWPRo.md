## Summary
ICT explains how to anticipate a large-range day. The setup is an inside or small-range day after a large-range day, measured against a 3-day average range. A draw on liquidity (smooth highs or lows left behind) supplies direction. Large-range days are mostly low-resistance liquidity runs, where the open sits near the low of a bullish day and price rushes to its target. He then gives his most explicit statement of how he chooses WHICH fair value gap or order block to trade. He grades the dealing range into equilibrium and quadrants, and a bullish PD array has to sit at or below the lower quadrant, after displacement and a market structure shift, at a macro time. The lecture also covers how to enter (Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill, order block mean threshold, consequent encroachment of an FVG at equilibrium), how to manage stops in low- versus high-resistance conditions, and why to trade pre-market (7–9 AM) on such days. Day-of-week and split-news context are woven in. It ends with a long warning to cut leverage into the 2024 election.

All worked examples are NQ on Friday 2024-10-25. Wed 10/23 was a large down day that made the low of the week. Thu 10/24 was an inside day, a smaller range inside Wednesday's range. Fri 10/25 was the expected large bullish range day. Prices are stated as spoken; some are unclear in the captions (flagged).

## Concepts

### Large-range day anticipation: 3-day average range (and 5-day alternative)  (aliases: "large range day", "range expansion day", "small range day / compression")
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Large range" is measured against the average daily range of the last 3 days. If the current day's range is larger than that average, it is outside the normal 3-day range.
  - A day that closes with a range smaller than the 3-day average is a "small range day". It is "mathematically defined" and very sensitive.
  - Stricter or more sensitive version: use a 5-day average. If daily ranges start printing smaller than the last 5 days, you are close to a large-range expansion day.
  - Compression precedes expansion. He frames it as twisting a metal spring tighter and tighter until it releases (a borrowed image from Larry Williams). The smaller the ranges get, the bigger the likely expansion.
  - The large day usually comes the very next day, or worst case the second day after. The compression gives no direction on its own; you still need higher-timeframe order flow or the draw on liquidity.
  - He says large-range days are about 90% low-resistance liquidity runs (he first misspoke "high"). If the day is not a large range day, you will probably meet high-resistance runs instead, "not all the time but 90%".
- **How to identify:** compute the average range of the last 3 days (optionally 5). If yesterday's range is below that average, or an inside day, flag the next day as a large-range candidate.
- **How to use:** as a volatility filter that says WHEN an expansion is likely. Direction comes from the DOL (see next concept). On such a day he is willing to trade pre-market and hold, expects little retracement, and expects the day's open to be near the low (bullish case).
- **Example in this video:** Wed 10/23 NQ large down day. Thu 10/24 range sat inside Wed's range, with a higher low and lower high. Fri 10/25 was expected to be the large bullish range day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443) 3-day average and what counts as "large"; [0:17:05](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1025) spring analogy; [1:37:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5857) restated as the math for anticipating a large range day; [1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906) 5-day version and the 90% low-resistance figure.
- **Nuance / caveats:** he admits an expansion day inside the last 3 days can skew the average. He notes the smallest-range-in-7-days idea (Toby Crabel, from the book "Street Smarts" by Raschke & Connors, 1995) and Larry Williams' inside-day precursor as influences, and says the 3-day average is his own repeating theme.

### Inside day as precursor  (aliases: "inside day", "range contraction", "spring")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** an inside day is a day whose high is lower than the previous day's high and whose low is higher than the previous day's low. It is a precursor to a large-range day (credited to Larry Williams and the Crabel/Raschke material). It does not give direction.
- **How to identify:** compare today's daily high and low to yesterday's. Both must be inside yesterday's range.
- **How to use:** expect the next trading day (or the one after) to expand. Pair it with a DOL for direction.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday 10/24 high lower than Wednesday's high and Thursday's low higher than Wednesday's low. Thursday's intraday was choppy, back and forth, overlapping ("fuzzy price action").
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:01](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=541) inside day introduced; [0:11:27](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=687) definition and "it doesn't give you direction"; [0:42:09](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=2529) restated.
- **Nuance / caveats:** a small-range day is itself a high-resistance, "fuzzy" environment. Compression means a choppy day now and an explosive day next.

### Direction from smooth highs/lows left behind (draw on liquidity after a sell-off)  (aliases: "smooth highs", "unfinished business", "relative equal highs", "single highs")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a large drop (Wed), retail expects continuation lower, so shorts defend "smooth" relative-equal highs left above the market. Those stops are the target.
  - Therefore the inside day's direction is solved by looking at where price left smooth highs or lows that have not been engaged, and not at the drop itself. His Thursday tweets said "look up, don't go short". He calls the untouched orders above "unfinished business".
  - A single high also counts; it is not always relative equal highs. Equal highs and lows are just what new students spot easily.
  - Prime candidate on the 15-minute chart: "high, lower high, lower high" smoothness above price.
  - He also leans on the higher-timeframe narrative: primary bull market, election year, "we're not trying to pick the top in a primary bull market".
- **How to use:** if bias is bullish, only look for buy-side draws and bullish PD arrays. He is "not interested in shorts".
- **Example in this video:** 15-minute relative equal highs above price, with unfinished business above NQ roughly 20,659.25 (spoken "659 and a quarter", caption unclear) and 20,680. Both buy-side levels were run Friday.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:36](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=156) relative equal highs and the "10 o'clock may use data to go through" idea; [0:15:29](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=929) "unfinished business"; [1:10:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4214) bias bullish means only bullish PD arrays; [1:40:54](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=6054) smooth highs on 15m are the prime candidate.

### Large-range day open near the low (bullish) + session Power of 3  (aliases: "open is the low of the day", "buy below the opening price", "Power of 3 / AMD", "Judas swing")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a bullish large-range day, the open is near the low of the daily range, in the lower quarter of the day's range (an observation he got from Larry Williams' material).
  - He maps a daily candle's open, high, low and close onto sessions. Manipulation is the drop below the opening price (Judas swing). Accumulation happens around and just above the open. Price may or may not come back to touch the open again. Distribution is the profit run to the DOL or a higher-timeframe PD array.
  - His own workaround for buying below the open was to use time: the day starts at midnight, so take the midnight open, let price drop below it, and buy there. In the 1990s he waited for a bullish divergence on the stochastic and a MACD histogram as his filter (not a current teaching).
  - Williams' own method (add about 20% of the average range of the last several days to the open, then buy) hurt ICT; he says it was drawdown he could not weather, not that it fails.
- **How to use:** on days expected to be large and bullish, look for longs below the midnight open (or session open) where smart money accumulates. Bearish days would be the mirror (open near the high).
- **Example in this video:** Fri 10/25 midnight open on NQ. Price dropped under it (the tail/low of the day), traded back up through it, and accumulated around it. The 9:30 morning session open was also the low of the session so far.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:10](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1270) open in the lower quarter; [0:21:58](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1318) session Power of 3; [0:24:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1468) Williams' admitted weakness; [0:26:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1564) midnight open idea; [0:27:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1657) buy below the open.
- **Nuance / caveats:** the 1990s stochastic/MACD filter is storytelling about his past, not a rule he gives now.

### Low-resistance vs high-resistance liquidity runs  (aliases: "high/low resistance liquidity run conditions", "fuzzy price action")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Low-resistance run:** little give and take. Price goes straight to a PD array and "gone in 30 seconds". Down-close candles (in a bullish run) support price higher when price returns to them, and inefficiencies (FVGs) are left partly open instead of being fully closed. Retracements are shallow. Large-range days are about 90% low-resistance.
  - **High-resistance run:** lots of back-and-forth, overlapping ranges, every fair value gap gets closed in, and price spends a lot of time inside inefficiencies. That is the tell that you are in high resistance. Thursday was an example, visible on the 15m, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 minute and sub-minute charts. It can blow accounts (combines) or tilt you.
  - Write the two characteristic lists in your daily journal and have them in front of you at the start of each day.
- **How to use:**
  - **Low resistance:** pay at or below the PD arrays that will hold. Keep stops very tight (he was comfortable adding as price neared his stop). Do not rush partials. Manage with the stop and not with early partials.
  - **High resistance:** don't run the stop to breakeven quickly (let it move) or don't trade. It is the environment where a fast-trailed stop gets hit, and he treats the days as ones to avoid or trade lightly.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday 10/24 as high resistance and Friday 10/25 as low resistance. On Friday, the OB at the lower quadrant held, the FVG that formed left its lower portion open (a breakaway gap), and price ran in a straight line to the NWOG and the 15m smooth highs.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:58](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=2578) large range days deliver as low-resistance runs; [0:51:44](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3104) Thursday as high resistance; [1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744) low-resistance characteristics; [1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793) high-resistance characteristics; [1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128) "signatures of a low-resistance run".
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says nobody else teaches this and he did not know he needed it earlier in his career. The stop advice in the high-resistance context is loosely worded in the captions (lines around 1:19:53 and 1:42:29). See the stop-management concept below.

### Dealing range grading: equilibrium and quadrants  (aliases: "grading a price swing", "lower/upper quadrant", "the quadrant", "PD array matrix premium/discount")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (the lecture's centerpiece)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Define a dealing range from the high of the retracement leg down to the low reference point. He is explicit that this is NOT quarters theory and NOT "quarterly theory". It is grading an actual dealing range or an actual price run he expects to enter or has entered.
  - Equilibrium is the halfway point. Ideal long entries ("lowest risk, highest R multiple yield") are at or below equilibrium. Pyramiding (adding) is limited to equilibrium: adding above it is "tossing good money after" a position.
  - The range is also split into upper and lower quadrants (the 75% and 25% levels, as spoken). When bullish, the PD array he chooses (OB or FVG) must be at or below the lower quadrant, or he will not use it. He says "it must be at or below the quadrant" with emphasis.
  - The measuring tool also shows extensions. He says these (based on the opening range gap and standard deviations) are being held for his book. He customises the fib levels (rounds the OTE level; exact numbers in the captions are unclear, see caveat).
  - He says he can predict where future PD arrays will form by doing this. He claims to know where fair value gaps must form before the dealing range even forms.
- **How to identify:**
  1. Find the swing high in the move and the pre-market/consolidation low reference. Use the bodies of the candles and ignore wicks that were stop runs ("doing damage"). For example, he disregarded a wick spike high as "a little bump" and drew the range from the bodies.
  2. Confirm a shift in market structure bullish (a break of a swing high, defined below) so that retracements into the inefficiency are treated as discount arrays.
  3. Mark equilibrium (50%) and the lower quadrant (25% from the low).
  4. Judge each FVG/OB: is it at or below the lower quadrant (primary), or around equilibrium (secondary)? A PD array whose body is mostly above the quadrant is skipped.
- **How to use:**
  - Primary entries: at or below the lower quadrant.
  - Pyramid: up to equilibrium only.
  - Anything above equilibrium (or the stated cut-off) is used for management purposes, not new entries. At [1:23:51] he says "you're trading ends on the entries here or less" and anything above that is for management.
  - For a short (not shown), invert.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 8:30 AM ET 10/25, range from the highest high of the retracement leg down to the pre-market low-body reference (15-second chart used to define the range). The OB and FVG at 8:30 were at or below the lower quadrant; a later FVG near equilibrium was handled differently (see CE-at-EQ entry below).
- **Timestamps:** [1:05:25](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3925) dealing range and ignoring the stop-run wick; [1:07:01](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4021) "grading a price swing, not quarters theory"; [1:07:49](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4069) equilibrium and where best entries and pyramiding are; [1:09:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4168) quadrants and extensions; [1:11:50](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4310) "must be at or below the quadrant".
- **Nuance / caveats:**
  - At [1:08:41] and [1:09:28] he adjusts the OTE level (spoken "change the 0.75 to 0.725 or 0.79 because I round it" and "I don't do the 78% or 79%", caption unclear).
  - A tension in his own logic: he rejects the later FVG because its body is mostly above the lower quadrant, then says the same area is still tradable when its CE overlaps equilibrium (next concepts). Read it as: lower-quadrant = primary entry; EQ-or-below = adds and secondary entries.

### How ICT picks the FVG / order block (selection checklist)  (aliases: "why that fair value gap", "authorship / fingerprints", "PD arrays the algorithm goes back to")
- **Kind:** rule (the answer the title promises)
- **What ICT says:** it is not "any down-close candle" (order block) and not "any 3-candle gap with a one-candle separation" (FVG). Someone teaching it that way is hurting students. He claims no one else teaches this correctly. A tradable PD array has to pass several filters at once:
  1. Bias/narrative is set and there is a DOL (e.g. smooth highs). In a bullish bias only bullish OBs, bullish breakers, bullish FVGs, and bearish-looking FVGs broken and inverted (iFVG) count.
  2. Time: a macro (8:30 for example) with the algorithm "spooling", and awareness of the economic calendar (here split 8:30/10:00 news) and day of week.
  3. The market first trades into a discount PD array and then must displace: it breaks a short-term/swing high (MSS) and leaves a BISI-type FVG or OB. "Let the algorithm tip its hand" and do not just buy because price is in the zone.
  4. The array must be at or below the lower quadrant of the dealing range (bullish).
  5. For OBs, take the lowest down-close candle's body (not wicks). Two consecutive down closes count as one range. Use the order block mean threshold.
  6. The FVG should be one that leaves a portion open (a breakaway gap) in low-resistance conditions.
- **How to use:** for the shortlist the algorithm "will go right back to". He does not "trade zones" and he does not trade supply and demand: "I don't trade zones". He wants a PD array reference point and not a zone.
- **Example in this video:** at 8:30 on 10/25 price traded into the discount array, displaced up through a short-term high (a 3-candle swing with lower highs each side), then broke a second swing high, leaving an FVG/OB at or below the lower quadrant. A later FVG formed at displacement near equilibrium and was NOT used for the primary entry because most of its body was above the lower quadrant.
- **Timestamps:** [1:03:49](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3829) it's not every down-close candle or every 3-candle gap; [1:10:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4214) only bullish PD arrays when bullish; [1:13:27](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4407) "which OB and FVG I trade is the one the algorithm uses"; [1:18:15](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4695) why a nearby FVG was skipped.
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says he is not giving "the complete treatment to a fair value gap" here, only enough to "divide the waters". He expects more detail in a book.

### Market structure shift used to qualify the array  (aliases: "shift in market structure", "short-term high", "swing high", "displacement")
- **Kind:** definition / entry condition
- **What ICT says:**
  - A short-term/swing high is a candle whose high is lower than the candle before and has a lower high on the next candle (a 3-candle formation).
  - When price breaks above it (candle by candle), that is a market structure shift to bullish. Displacement higher then leaves the inefficiency.
  - He says the setup is very similar to Model 2022 and tells viewers to rewatch those videos.
  - With two swing highs both broken, the break "qualifies it even further". Any retracement into the resulting inefficiency is a discount array.
  - If bullish and you want the stricter version, use the closer short-term high, or the higher one if you want to be really sure.
- **Example in this video:** 8:30 AM 10/25 NQ: price trades above a swing high (shift), displaces higher, then breaks a second swing high, leaving the BISI/OB.
- **Timestamps:** [1:06:12](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3972) shift defined with candle highs; [1:11:03](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4263) two swing highs broken, displacement.
- **Nuance / caveats:** at [0:40:31] for Model 2022 with two inefficiencies in a discount (the two FVGs in the 7–8 AM area), he says to trade it but the stop has to respect the second fair value gap. He calls two PD arrays in agreement "conflation" (jokes he made the word up; he means confluence).

### Order block body, mean threshold and entry rule  (aliases: "bullish order block", "mean threshold", "down close candle supports price")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement / entry rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bullish OB = the down-close candle (or consecutive down closes as one range). Use the body (the lowest down close with a body), not the wick.
  - Half of an OB body is the mean threshold (MT). The term is for candle bodies. "Consequent encroachment" is the term for gaps, inefficiencies and wicks.
  - In the live example, price reacted at the OB's mean threshold. The low of the very next candle matched the MT price to the tick (spoken "42.75", 20,4x2.75, caption unclear; one tick of precision was the point).
  - He wants price to use the MT or higher (up to the OB's high) to trade down into the block and repel. He would never enter expecting a trade to the low of the candle, "I would never enter on a trade like that", even though it sometimes does that.
  - Wicks or tails below the candle body are inside-OB / inside-FVG formations and are expected when order flow is bullish. That is why his entries are on the lowest candle or one candle before or after it (bullish).
- **How to use:** entry at or near the OB mean threshold; very tight stops. Can add when price approaches the stop in a low-resistance run.
- **Example in this video:** the 8:30 OB (two consecutive down closes) near the lower quadrant: price delivers to the MT, reacts to the tick, and rallies into a volume imbalance.
- **Timestamps:** [1:14:15](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4455) OB body, MT vs CE; [1:15:03](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4503) "that's perfection to the tick"; [1:27:50](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5270) never enter assuming the low of the candle; [1:28:40](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5320) entries on the lowest candle or one before/after.
- **Nuance / caveats:** the rant about Elliott Wave, Wyckoff and "buying and selling pressure" is skipped, but the point stands: he claims the exact tick reaction is evidence of an algorithmic price engine.

### Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOFED)  (aliases: "institutional order entry drill", "institutional orderflow entry drill")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - IOFED is an entry model where you expect a fair value gap to be traded into but not fully closed. You expect only a partial entry into the gap (the shaded area).
  - Entry is a very specific price and not a zone: the FVG's upper edge, with an extra tick (spoken "462 and 3/4 plus one tick"), as a limit order.
  - It suits low-resistance run conditions, where an FVG is expected to stay partly open. In such a run, a breakaway gap leaves the lower part open.
  - He ties it to the gap forming below the upper quadrant (the entry area at or below the quadrant, "anything above is for management").
  - The stop can sit just below the high of the lowest candle of the pattern (the bottom of the gap), because in a low-resistance run price should not trade down to the bottom.
  - He says in this environment it is "Flawless" and his stops are ultra tight.
- **How to identify:** in a bullish low-resistance run, a fresh FVG (leaves a gap behind the displacement) at or below the quadrant, with an order block (down-close candles) beneath that should support price.
- **How to use:** limit buy at the FVG's upper edge plus one tick (specific price, not a zone); stop just below the gap's bottom/candle high; add as price nears the stop if conditions remain low resistance.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 10/25 pre-market after the 8:30 displacement: an FVG formed whose upper edge was about 20,462.75 (spoken "462 and 3/4", the thousands digit is inferred from context). The limit at 20,462.75 + 1 tick (about 20,463.00) was the entry. Price traded into the gap partially and left the lower part open as the run went on.
- **Timestamps:** [1:21:29](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4889) IOFED named; [1:22:16](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4936) price plus one tick; [1:24:41](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5081) the partial-open idea; [1:26:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5174) stop placement.
- **Nuance / caveats:** caption unclear in places on exactly which edge the "plus one tick" applies to and where the stop sits. The stop description reads as "just below this candlestick's high" (the bottom edge of the gap). He also says other models are possible, but he follows his protocol, "stay on the right side of the road".

### Consequent encroachment of an FVG overlapping equilibrium  (aliases: "CE at EQ", "convergence of two midpoints")
- **Kind:** refinement / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - A later displacement left an FVG whose CE (its 50%) overlapped the dealing range's equilibrium. That is a convergence of two midpoints.
  - When bullish you can place a limit buy right at the midpoint (CE), trusting the lower half stays open (a breakaway gap). It is a tradable FVG and a good candidate for the institutional entry drill, with only a partial fill expected into the shaded area.
  - It is also the boundary for pyramiding: all adds at or below equilibrium.
- **Example in this video:** after the OB entry, price rallied through the lower quadrant to equilibrium and meandered, then displaced again, leaving an FVG straddling EQ. The earlier question "why not this FVG?" was answered by: most of the body was above the lower quadrant, so it is not a primary entry. Later he says it is still usable because its CE overlaps EQ.
- **Timestamps:** [1:17:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4646) price returns to equilibrium and displaces again; [1:20:40](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4840) limit at the CE; [1:21:29](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4889) convergence of two midpoints.
- **Nuance / caveats:** this slightly softens the lower-quadrant rule. Treat lower-quadrant as the main entry and CE-at-EQ as a secondary or add entry (see dealing range concept).

### Volume imbalance delivery  (aliases: "volume imbalance", "buried body")
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** after the entry price rallied into a volume imbalance, which is missing buy-side and sell-side delivery. The candle "buries a body" inside it. Price then dropped back to a swing high and rallied again, leaving the lower quadrant and trading up to equilibrium.

### Pre-market (7:00–9:00 AM) positioning on expected large-range days  (aliases: "trade before 9:30", "pre-market entries", "pyramid at lunch")
- **Kind:** rule / entry model context
- **What ICT says:**
  - When a large-range day or a one-sided day is likely, he elects to trade pre-market and take no entries after the 9:30 bell. He is typically in between 7:00 and 9:00, holds, has taken multiple partials, and rides the opening-range expansion (9:30–10:00).
  - If the 10:00 AM Silver Bullet then offers a longer hold, he holds through lunch and can pyramid in the lunch macro retracement (re-adding what he took off, plus more, because of unrealized profit).
  - Not every pre-market should be traded. Many days are quiet pre-market and then easy at 9:30. He lists the coalescing factors that justified it that day: inside day, bullish DOL, Friday, split news at 8:30 and 10:00, large opening gap.
  - The Silver Bullet at 10:00 is a baseline/training-wheels model ("pushing my son Cameron into a mold"). There is a silver-bullet-style window every hour and there is more than one way to use his concepts.
- **Example in this video:** midnight open on 10/25; 3:00 AM (London) displacement through a short-term high, a drop to an OB without taking the low, rally again; volume imbalance and inversion FVG clustered; 3:30 AM rips higher; 4:00 AM trades back down into the middle of the consolidation (discount, equilibrium); 7:00 AM AM session starts and price runs the buy side then returns to two inefficiencies (Model 2022 with the stop respecting the second FVG); 8:30 AM macro entry at the lower-quadrant array (see above).

### Macros (timing) as stated in this lecture  (aliases: "macro", "8:30 macro", "lunch macro")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - 8:30 AM ET is "the only 30-minute delineator for a macro". At 8:30 the algorithm starts spooling whether or not there is news. If price does not start running, wait for the 9:30 open.
  - Every other hourly macro runs from 10 minutes before the top of the hour to 10 minutes after it (e.g. :50–:10). The last-hour macro is 3:15–3:45 PM.
  - A pre-market macro around 8:50–9:10 is implied (spoken "8 or 10 minutes after 8", caption unclear).
  - The 10:30–11:30 hour typically forms a higher low of the morning session (in a bullish day) and invites the lunch macro.
  - **Lunch macro = 11:30 AM–1:30 PM ET (New York).** At about 10:30 the algorithm computes the range high and low, defines the midpoint as equilibrium, and below equilibrium looks for sell-side liquidity. The lunch macro "runs on those that are profitable". Two outcomes: it takes the first sell-side pool and then consolidates (no new high or low the rest of the day), or it trades down into trailing stops, triggers the sell side, then accumulates and runs higher into the close of the week.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:38](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1898) lunch 11:30–1:30; [0:32:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1948) lunch macro mechanics; [1:12:38](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4358) macro list; [1:35:11](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5711) lunch high/low as a draw.

### Day of week (Friday) and split economic calendar  (aliases: "split delivery", "manual intervention", "day-of-week phenomenon", "last trading day of the week")
- **Kind:** rule / context
- **What ICT says:**
  - On Friday (last trading day), there is little time, so price rushes at the open to the liquidity. The algorithm "doesn't want to offer the opportunity" for stops to stay. They also do not want positions held over the weekend with gap risk, so they "intervene manually". The 9:30 rip looks like FOMC and is "not algorithmic, that's intervention".
  - A 70% likelihood that the opening gap has its half repriced to in the first 30 minutes is thrown out on a day like this ("not today"). Do not look for the opening gap to fill; wait for it to run to unfinished business. He expected little or no retracement into the opening range gap.
  - Split news (8:30 and 10:00): can create a catapult or whiplash. The algorithm uses the first data as the fake run and the later one as the real run (like FOMC at 2:00 versus the 2:30 press conference). He can filter this out when the bias is already clear. That Friday had core durable goods (8:30, medium impact) and revised consumer sentiment (10:00, medium impact).
  - News drivers are used as a "smoke screen" to mask real intentions.

### Stop and partial management in low-resistance runs  (aliases: "stops and partials", "walk away", "banking 80%")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - In low-resistance run days, do not rush partials. Move the stop to a reasonable level where you can step away, have limit orders at the targets, and go for a walk or workout for 20–30 minutes. You get rewarded for staying in longer. Watching a 15-second chart will scare you out.
  - Trailing the stop to each swing is a high-resistance habit. In high resistance it gets you stopped out (with the wording unclear around [1:42:29]). Relaxing the stop in low resistance avoids premature stop-outs.
  - After the run: a morning-session trader is done. A position/swing trader should have about 80% of the position off by the end of the run; if not "you're doing it wrong". Afterwards see if it wants to continue into the afternoon.
  - If you are flat and were not in the trade, it is fine to sit still and call the week done.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Large range day = range above the last-3-day average. A small-range day = a range below the 3-day average (or 5-day, more sensitive). Inside day: lower high and higher low than the previous day.
- Expansion usually follows within 1 day (worst case 2).
- Large-range days: about 90% low-resistance liquidity runs.
- Bullish large-range day: open in the lower quarter of the daily range (near the low).
- Avoid (if new) the morning session after a large-range day.
- Dealing range: use candle bodies, ignore stop-run wicks. Longs at or below equilibrium (50%); the PD array must be at or below the lower quadrant; adds only at or below equilibrium.
- Bullish MSS: break above a 3-candle swing high (candle with lower highs on both sides) then displacement.
- OB: lowest down-close body; mean threshold = 50% of the body; CE = 50% of a gap/wick; never expect a trade to the OB's low.
- IOFED: limit at the FVG edge plus one tick (e.g. 20,462.75 + 0.25), stop below the gap's bottom; expects a partial gap fill only.
- Macros: 8:30 AM ET (the only 30-minute one); every other hour :50 to :10; last hour 3:15–3:45 PM; lunch 11:30 AM–1:30 PM ET; 10:30–11:30 forms the morning session's higher low (bullish).
- Pre-market entries between 7:00 and 9:00 AM on expected large-range days; Silver Bullet 10:00; wait a few minutes (5–10, at most 15) after 10:00 news before a Silver Bullet.
- NWOG: Friday 4:59 PM ET close to Sunday 6:00 PM ET open; CE is the midpoint.
- Opening gap: about 70% likelihood that its half is repriced to in the first 30 minutes (ignore on Friday low-resistance days).
- Position/swing trader: have about 80% off after a big run.
- Execution timeframes mentioned: 15-minute for liquidity targets, 15-second for defining the range, 5-second for execution.
- Prices (NQ, as spoken, some unclear): unfinished business about 20,659.25 and 20,680; Thursday's target 20,495; OB mean threshold about 20,4x2.75; FVG upper edge about 20,462.75.

## Glossary candidates
`Inside day` — a day whose high and low are both inside the previous day's range; precursor to a large-range day.
`Small range day` — a day whose range is under the 3-day (or 5-day) average range.
`Large range day` — a day whose range exceeds the recent 3-day average; usually a low-resistance run.
`Low-resistance liquidity run` — a run with little retracement where down-close candles hold and FVGs stay partly open.
`High-resistance liquidity run` — a choppy, overlapping run where FVGs get fully closed.
`Fuzzy price action` — ICT's layman term for choppy, back-and-forth price action.
`Unfinished business` — untouched buy-side or sell-side liquidity left resting that the market is expected to take.
`Smooth highs/lows` — relatively equal, clean highs or lows left in the chart, seen as engineered liquidity.
`Grading a price swing` — dividing a dealing range into equilibrium and quadrants to rank PD arrays.
`Lower/upper quadrant` — the 25% and 75% levels of a graded dealing range.
`Mean threshold (MT)` — 50% of an order block's body; contrasted with CE for gaps.
`Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOFED)` — limit entry at an FVG edge plus one tick, expecting only a partial fill into the gap.
`Breakaway gap` — a gap that stays open behind a run (the lower part of an FVG left unfilled).
`Manual intervention` — ICT's term for a sudden, non-algorithmic rip, like the 9:30 or FOMC move.
`Split delivery` — news at both 8:30 and 10:00 on the same day, creating whiplash risk.
`Conflation` — ICT's joking word for two PD arrays agreeing (meaning confluence).
