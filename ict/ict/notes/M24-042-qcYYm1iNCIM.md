## Concepts

### News-day protocol: no positioning into 8:30, wait 15 minutes, no raw data  (aliases: "carnival ride", "treat it like FOMC / CPI / PPI")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NFP, FOMC, CPI and PPI are all treated the same: you do not know where they go, so do not speculate ahead of them.
  - Wait about 15 minutes after the release, minimum. NFP at 8:30 ET means 8:45 ET is the green light to start looking and studying. Then see what inefficiencies are still in play and what smooth areas sit above or below.
  - An order placed right at the 8:30 release will not fill, or fills at the worst time with enormous slippage.
  - Brand-new students (including anyone coming from another teacher, who he treats as brand new): do not trade after Wednesday morning New York time on NFP weeks. Wednesday and Thursday were challenging this week.
  - Journal tip: give yourself written permission to never trade this day. Months later, with a model you have not tinkered with, consistent returns and no emotional swaying, you can trade it after the release plus 15 minutes, normally for the rest of the day.
  - He never reviews the number, jobless claims or the change from last month. He does not look at CPI, PPI or the FOMC rate decision. The market uses these as red herrings to manipulate sentiment. Candles are a seismograph of less-informed traders' sentiment.
  - He admits he does not know how to call NFP in advance, and says he is "generally wrong" on NFP. Short-term use of macro or interest-rate data (long-term carry-trade logic included) is useless intraday.
  - In his career some NFP weeks had very favorable Wednesday/Thursday price action, but that is rare and not the standard.
  - Before 8:30 NFP Friday: hold no bias and no expectation. Let price show you after. "An opportunity in the wrong conditions is a cleverly disguised impossibility."
  - He had no stop-loss he could trust for a long chasing the 8:30 run, so he would not take it even though the payout looks great.
- **How to use:** Mark levels at 8:15, sit through 8:30, resume study at 8:45 ET. Treat trade ideas before that as untradeable.

### Minor vs primary liquidity drill (8:15 ET pre-news annotation)  (aliases: "minor buy side / sell side", "primary buy side / sell side")
- **Kind:** rule / study drill
- **What ICT says:**
  - At about 8:15 ET, on a 15-minute chart, see where price is. Mark the closest buy-side and sell-side pool at that moment. Those are the "minor" pools. Then look a little broader, above and below, for the next logical level of liquidity. Those are the "primary" pools.
  - Minor buy side: here a recent high (after the previous high was already cleared). Minor sell side: the nearest low. He ignores small consolidation lows. Using lows that are lower than earlier lows is what makes the pool the "first minor sell side".
  - Primary: cut through the consolidation to the relative equal lows (primary sell side) or relative equal highs (primary buy side). "Primary is just the next logical level of liquidity below your near close proximity minor."
  - Study drill for every NFP: watch which pool is taken first, then look for the opposite side to be traded to.
  - He promises to explain why this will not work as a trading method. It is a study mechanism and not a signal. (The explanation is not clearly given. Caption unclear.)
  - Primary buy side: if price goes up there it will run through to clear it, because shorts above think they are safe behind "retail resistance".
- **How to identify:** Nearest high/low at 8:15 equals minor. Next clean relative-equal highs/lows equals primary.
- **How to use:** Watch the 8:30 reaction on the 15m or 5m. Record which pool engages first. Wait 15 minutes. Then look for the other side.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15m: minor buy side was tagged within seconds at 8:30, and the first pool taken was the minor buy side. ICT then looked to see whether price would go down to the minor sell side. He viewed the run as a possible accumulation ahead of running the primary buy stops.

### NFP as the disruptor of profitable positions  (aliases: "Boogeyman trading", "pain mechanism")
- **Kind:** definition / narrative rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NFP is used many times to destabilize sentiment and unseat people who are currently profitable, then go the other way. Here the profitable ones were Tuesday's shorts after the drop into the weekly BISI.
  - Their stops would sit at least above the high of the consolidation. A high slightly lower than the previous one makes a high-probability relative equal high, which is a prime candidate to be taken.
  - Large institutions do not trail stops to breakeven after a 10-handle move. They leave core protective stops outside the normal range so they do not need a trade manager babysitting every fluctuation. So the large pool of orders is at the highs, not at the bottom of the nearby inefficiency.
  - He lists the scenarios and says he cannot pick one: (a) run up to knock out the shorts, then lower next week without taking out the lows. (b) Go down and trip shorts, then pump all day into the weekend, then lower next week. (c) Move around in the middle and leave both pools.
  - A move that rises straight from 8:30 with no stop run or candle taking out trailed sell stops is a premium being built ahead of the 9:30 open.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: price dropped to a weekly BISI (blue box, from Monday), then did little. NFP lifted price straight up from 8:30 toward a primary buy side above Tuesday's relative equal highs.

### Efficient delivery, rebalancing an inefficiency, and why liquidity still matters more  (aliases: "SIBI", "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency", "efficient delivery", "balanced price range")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A single candle with a sell-side delivery leaves an inefficiency on the buy side (an "island" between the previous candle's low and the next candle's high). The interval of time between those two prices has not been balanced with price in both directions, so price tends to return to overlap that range. The algorithm "reprices to engage liquidity" and also rebalances inefficiencies. It does not know how many stops are above an old high or below an old low.
  - Once price has traded back up over the single candle and touched its low, the inefficiency has been "efficiently delivered". That is not a balanced price range (BPR).
  - But reaching the top of an inefficiency is not enough as a target. The large pools of orders are at old highs (buy stops) and old lows (sell stops), not at the top of the inefficiency. Price is "offered" there for efficient delivery, and that is not the same as a stop pool.
  - He answers a viewer question: there are no larger pools of liquidity than those above old highs and below old lows. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling a new theory.
  - Time symmetry: the time it took to build the inefficiency is the time you should expect for the return delivery. Here it took two candles instead of one, which is fine.
  - Speed and magnitude: if the inefficiency was created by a quick sudden move, expect the buy-side repricing to be quick and made of large candles. If price instead sputters up and back inside the gap, it is "high resistance" and manual intervention.
  - Management when price sputters up inside an inefficiency (and it is not an NFP day): do not chase or trail the stop up. Lock in cover costs and let it go. Trades like that have many retracements.
- **How to identify:** One candle, or a short run, that traded away leaving a gap with no overlap. Check whether price has since traded back over the whole range.
- **How to use:** Use the gap as context for a draw. Target the higher-liquidity pool above or below and look for speed as confirmation. Slow grinding means do not trust it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m/5m: a sell-side delivery candle formed on Tuesday (red shaded area). From 8:30, price repaid it (overlapping candles on top of it, then through it). ICT said it was now "efficiently delivered" but not a BPR, so a further run to the relative equal highs above was possible. He also recalled that people had thought price would go up just to fill the inefficiency, and he had said that it would go to where the positions are protected.

### Opening range gap: ETH vs RTH, midpoint, and gap-closure scenarios  (aliases: "opening range gap", "mid gap", "gap closure half", "settlement")
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Chart sessions: electronic trading hours (ETH) run 6:00 PM to 5:00 PM New York time. Regular trading hours (RTH) run 9:30 AM to 4:14 PM. The opening range gap is always defined by RTH: previous RTH settlement (4:14 PM) to the 9:30 open. Toggle between the two when planning.
  - Mid-gap (50%, consequent encroachment) is reached about 70% of the time within the first 30 minutes (9:30-10:00). He restates his 40 years of data (32 years watching the markets, bought historical data before he started trading) against a Twitter study that said otherwise.
  - Standard day: the gap is the initial bias. Scalp to the mid gap if you want. At mid gap, decide whether price continues in the gap's direction or fully closes the gap. If it closes a premium gap, use the bottom of the gap as resistance and expect a move down in multiples of the gap size, two to three standard deviations.
  - With a small gap, wait for the first presented FVG.
  - NFP scenario (this day): ETH price was sitting at the top of an enormous potential gap with relative equal highs just above. Possible script: open at 9:30, run up to those highs to accumulate buy stops (street money chases), then smart money sells into the flood of willing buyers and drives down to the mid gap, three-quarters, or full closure. The primary buy side may not appear on an RTH chart, so check ETH.
  - Conditional read: if the open pushes down to the mid gap early and then climbs all the way back up, that is not a turtle soup short setup. It suggests price is going much higher (consolidate, accumulate, higher). He would prefer a small drop first to make traders think lower and then a rally to the obvious buy side.
  - He says the mid-gap move may take longer than 30 minutes on a news day, and he "is not holding it to" the 70% on NFP.
- **How to identify:** Toggle ETH/RTH. Measure the RTH settlement to open, then the 50%. Note buy-side liquidity on ETH that is not visible on RTH.
- **How to use:** First-30-minute bias toward mid gap. After that, decide on full closure vs continuation using higher-timeframe bias and liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 10-04: large gap-up in the making. Price rallied from 8:30, opened, ran, then dropped to the mid gap before 10:00. ICT said "not a tick more" at the half of an FVG, then aggressive delivery to mid gap, the 70% strike rate by 10:00.

### FVG quadrants, body respect, "Mohawk", and the breakaway gap  (aliases: "grading the gap", "burying the bodies", "coloring outside the lines")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Divide a bearish FVG into quadrants (grading). If candle bodies stay out of the upper half (specifically they respect the upper quadrant/CE), that is a bearish signature. In an ideal world the upper half of a bearish gap is not closed in.
  - Wicks outside the gap are normal and allowed. He calls this a "Mohawk" or coloring outside the lines. What counts is where bodies close ("where are they burying the bodies").
  - Draw the FVG rectangle carefully. Include any volume imbalance. When there is a volume imbalance inside what looks like an FVG, there is no body separation between candle 1 and candle 2, but there is a separation between the bodies of candle 2 and candle 3 (candle 2's close is lower than the next candle's open). The rectangle must reflect that so the quadrants are right. This is why it is not "supply and demand": the levels are specific.
  - If price leaves lower after respecting the upper quadrant and then creates another FVG, that new gap is a breakaway gap. It marks the Inception of the run. Next steps: the CE of the wick, then the half of the gap (the 50% of the opening range gap).
  - Breakaway gap definition requires enough context (here: opening-gap draw, prior respect) to set the stage.
- **How to identify:** Check bodies and quadrant of the FVG after retracement. Check for a following FVG after displacement.
- **How to use:** Bearish: bodies not entering the upper half means expect a drop, then the next FVG is your delivery path. Targets: FVG CE, then the mid gap.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 10-04 around 9:40-9:50 ET: price came back into a gap, bodies stopped at or under the upper quadrant (wicked outside, closed below), then it broke lower and left another FVG (breakaway). Price tagged the FVG's CE, then the half-gap before 10:00. ICT pointed to the FVG as it formed and said he was not taking it because it was NFP.

### Trading Asia after a last-hour sell-side raid at the close  (aliases: "market on close", "Asia protocol", "6:50-7:10 macro", "7:50-8:10 macro")
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Preconditions: no medium- or high-impact news and no speeches in the economic calendar between 8:30 and 10:00 of the next New York session. Bullish context is assumed.
  - During the last hour of the cash session, price trades down to take a pool of sell-side (below relative equal lows or a multi-day low) and is held there into the close. That is the setup. (The move below the low and then rallying in the last hour is not the same high-probability case, since then smart money is only using the last hour to accumulate sell stops and hold price.)
  - At 6:00 PM ET (not Friday) the new ETH session starts. Wait until about 6:50-7:10 ET and look for reasons to be long.
  - The 6:50-7:10 macro should start sending price higher if smart money is long. After roughly the first 50 minutes of Asia the algorithm "starts spooling" price. Each short-term discount array is used to accumulate more longs.
  - If the 6:50-7:10 macro is silent, the 7:50-8:10 macro can still deliver, in his opinion a better one. Typically 20-40 handles (he says "anywhere between 25 and 40" and "20 to 40"), not necessarily 100. This holds even if news is due the next morning.
  - Asia is often the low of the day when bullish, or the high of the day when bearish.
  - Working window: Asia 7:00 to 9:00, which can extend to 10:00 (PM ET, inferred from the 6:00 PM ETH open). At 10:00, square up, or take some profit and leave a runner if you expect a daily continuation and there is no news at 8:30-10:00 next day.
  - Best case: Asia runs to midnight, London consolidates (or Asia is the real run and London consolidates), New York gives a second stage. The day can total 500-700 handles from the 6:00 PM ETH open to the NY high of day. He says this is not an everyday event and not every Asia session is good.
  - Validation: study by contrast the last hour of cash trading before the better Asia days. Do not rely on the last two weeks only.
  - Daily-chart filter he mentions: the previous close and next open are often close, bodies near each other. (Brief. Not fully explained.)
- **How to identify:** Check the economic calendar for 8:30-10:00 next day. See whether the last hour of RTH raided a clear sell-side and stayed there into the close.
- **How to use:** Look for longs after 6:50 PM ET, with the macro as the timing window. Manage at 10:00 with partial plus a runner.
- **Example in this video:** A "couple weeks back" he bought into a market-on-close idea in an instrument whose name is unclear in the caption ("radi", caption unclear). Asia kept rising for hours. Viewers emailed asking what happened in Asia.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
