## Summary
ICT reviews a bearish ES (June 2022) day. Before the open he forecast a run below the daily relative equal lows to 4070 and into the daily FVG, producing a large-range down day on an employment-data morning. He uses the day to teach several things: transposing HTF levels to LTF, bearish Power of 3 with the midnight open, the NY lunch "buy-stop clearing" rule, and the ICT bearish breaker, which needs a known DOL or "it's a gamble". He recaps a 9:30 demo short and explains why he cut part of it when price traded through a bearish order block. The core of the video is the stripped-down "one-trick pony" model he teaches his son Caleb. Time comes first (8:30, 9:30, 1:30 PM, 3–4 PM). In each window you look for a liquidity run, then an FVG, then a sell or buy into the FVG toward the opposing liquidity pool, and you take a fixed 5 ES points. He adds rules on how many trades per day and on demo-trading after a winner. He also expands on algorithmic theory ("price levels are useless until time is considered", macros, manual intervention), the daily range as a programmed "game map", and long passages on discipline and fear.

## Concepts

### HTF levels transposed to LTF; keep annotations simple
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Start your annotations on the higher timeframe and keep them simple: the level price will draw to, and the key levels that would change or confirm a bias. Then carry exactly those levels to the lower timeframe. Lower-timeframe price is "subordinate" to HTF levels. Many new students get lost in the timeframes.
- **Example in this video:** ES daily (left) and 1h (right). The daily relative equal lows and the daily FVG below them were drawn on the hourly.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:09](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=9) — daily and 1h side by side, LTF subordinate to HTF; [0:04:58](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=298) — start annotations on HTF, keep it simple.

### Conditional narrative, not "always sweep equal lows and reverse"
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The forecast was conditional. If price went below the daily relative equal lows, it would not "go down to go up". It would keep drawing into the daily FVG below.
  - That implies the volatility would be large enough for a large-range day.
  - Others teaching his concepts (or their own twists on them) claim price always hits relative equal lows and then reverses. "That's not the case." Narrative must be understood, and narrative requires experience, which he is lending.
- **How to use:** When he names an objective (4070), study what sits at that level yourself (old lows, relative equal lows, FVG). He will not spoon-feed it.
- **Example in this video:** ES. Pre-market (before 9:30) DOL was 4070, below the daily relative equal lows. Beyond that, the target was the daily FVG. Price ran through 4070, then to about 4040 (caption says "440"), and closed inside the larger daily FVG.

### FVG and order block on the hourly: candle bodies stay inside the FVG
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**

  - He points out that the candle bodies stayed mostly between the FVG's two levels. Wicks poked out, but "largely the volume is encapsulated" inside the gap.

### Power of 3 (bearish) with the midnight opening price; Judas swing
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a down day, short at or above the NY midnight opening price, as close to it as you can.
  - Bearish Power of 3: accumulate shorts, then manipulate the traders already short (the Judas swing) by running up to a short-term premium in the dealing range, then distribute lower.
  - Hold shorts for the daily range to fulfil. "Submit yourself to time": wait until the last hour, 3:00–4:00 PM, for delivery into the target.
  - In London, price rallied and built a premium ahead of the 8:30 window.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m. Price hovered around the midnight open, and London rallied to build a premium before 8:30. A high formed, short-term SSL was taken, and price retraced up and consolidated (11:00–noon). It took buy-side again during lunch, then broke lower and attacked the SSL below 4070, then ran to about 4040 and closed inside the daily FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:29](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=629) — midnight open plus Power of 3, Judas swing; [0:11:17](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=677) — partial at 4070, wait until 3–4 PM; [0:13:45](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=825) — short at or above midnight open; [0:14:37](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=877) — whole daily range on 5m.

### Pre-conditions for any PM-session trade
- **Kind:** rule / checklist
- **What ICT says:** Before you even consider an afternoon trade, ask what the daily range is trying to do:
  1. expand higher;
  2. expand lower;
  3. it reversed in the AM session, so will it make a counter-trend move;
  4. it is just consolidating because a big news event is due the next day.
  
  This day had employment data, a bearish bias, and a specific one-sided target (4070, then the daily FVG). The framework was "not ambiguous".

### NY lunch hour (bearish): clearing buy stops; smart money "cannibalizing" two order types
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NY lunch is 12:00–1:00 PM NY time.
  - If bearish, the market clears the stops above the highs formed in, and just before, the lunch hour. At about 1:00 the algorithm reprices and runs those buy stops.
  - Large-range days often have a "busy lunch". In a fast market, lunch can form a significant high or low (as just taught in the previous lecture).
  - Why: the algorithm does not want morning shorts to be profitable, so it knocks them out by flooding the market with buyers at a higher price. Smart money sells to that buying.
  - Those buyers are of two kinds: shorts' buy stops, and breakout traders' buy stops. Both are buy-side liquidity, the counterparty smart money needs for its shorts.
  - Aggressive traders can short during lunch on such a directional day. Under the model's rules, wait until after lunch.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m. Highs formed in and before lunch were run at about 1:00 PM. Price then broke lower toward 4070, then 4040, then the daily FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:16](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=976) — lunch is 12–1; [0:17:05](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1025) — bearish lunch rule, reprice at 1:00; [0:17:51](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1071) — fast-market lunch; [0:18:38](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1118) — smart money cannibalizing buy stops.

### ICT Bearish Breaker
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A breaker is a pattern where price runs out a pool of liquidity (here buy-side), then reverses and trades back below the short-term low. When price later trades back up to that zone and the narrative is bearish, it is a short.
  - The reference is the down-closed candle: the lowest one before the up-move that cleared the short-term high.
  - You must know what the market is likely reaching for. "If you leave that out it's not a breaker, it's a gamble", aimless speculation.
  - After it repels price, expect acceleration through the sell-side below. Price should not hold the low to go higher.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m afternoon. The swing low, then a higher high (BSL taken around lunch), then a break back below the swing low. Price returned to the bearish breaker, then fell through 4070 with only a small consolidation and no retracement, down to about 4040 and into the daily FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:13](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1213) — breaker definition, needs a DOL; [0:21:00](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1260) — down-closed candle; [0:21:48](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1308) — expect acceleration through SSL.

### Time and price in algorithmic theory; macro defined; support/resistance as a fallacy
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price is delivered by an algorithm. "Buying/selling pressure" is an excuse.
  - Algorithmic theory is time and price, and it leans heavily on time: time of day, day of week, week of month, month of year, seasonal influences.
  - "Price levels are useless until time is considered." That is why support/resistance is a fallacy: anyone can find a line that worked in hindsight, but picking the right level live, consistently, does not work.
  - The algorithm "works a specific macro": a short sequence of instructions. Seek a level, move to a premium or discount, move suddenly or consolidate, then wait for another time element in the same day, week, or month.
  - The reverse also holds: "time is of no use unless price is at a key PD array". Blending the two gives the precision.
  - Learning time is where students have the largest growth but also the first adversities.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:08](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1928) — algorithm, time and price, time-heavy; [0:32:55](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1975) — time factors; price useless without time; [0:34:29](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2069) — macro definition; [0:36:05](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2165) — time useless unless at a PD array.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He tells his own 1992–94 story: stopped out at "resistance", afraid to re-enter, abandoned a framework that was still valid. Better: keep the framework, admit the entry was wrong, and re-enter with less risk and leverage.

### Daily range as a programmed "game map"; algorithm blind to order volume
- **Kind:** definition (analogy)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The algorithm is not aware of how much volume rests above or below a high or low, and it does not need to be. It just has to take out the short-term high or low.
  - Like Pac-Man's maze or a Fortnite/Call of Duty map, the daily range has programmed boundaries, until manual intervention removes them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:44:11](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2651) — algorithm unaware of volume; [0:45:48](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2748) — daily range limits programmed.

### Manual intervention and the economic calendar
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Every price move is controlled, engineered, and premeditated, either by the AI/algorithm following its code or by manual intervention.
  - Manual intervention (for example FOMC or rate announcements) sends price abruptly to a level well outside what is reasonable for the short-term perspective.
  - Use the economic calendar. In his son's model, engage on days with medium or high-impact news. On days without news you can practise, but not with your normal risk percentage.

### "The model I teach my son" (Caleb's 5-point model)  (aliases: one-trick pony model)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Built to fit his son's personality (short attention span), with a very low points threshold. Index futures were chosen: no spread issues as in forex, a professional and very liquid market, and systematic price delivery.
  - **Time first.** Set the TradingView clock to New York time. Key windows:
    - 8:30 ET: the news embargo lifts, so it is the first time element.
    - 9:30 ET: equities open.
    - 1:30 PM ET: the PM session, after lunch.
    - 3:00–4:00 PM ET: the "market on close macro", which forms a small setup most days, buy or sell.
  - **Setup (bearish example at 8:30):**
    1. At 8:30 price is above the midnight opening price on a bearish day.
    2. It runs a short-term high (buy stops of 1-minute traders), so it is now at a premium.
    3. It then trades lower through a short-term low, leaving an FVG.
    4. It rallies back up into the FVG. Sell short there, targeting the sell-side pool below (old lows or relative equal lows; here 4101 / the 4100 big figure).
    5. Mirror all of this for buys.
  - **Target:** exactly 5 ES points, then out, even if the framework allows 30. Then watch whether price reaches the liquidity pool. Later, he will take a partial at 5 and hold the rest for the pool.
  - **Why 5 points:** it is easy and is the lowest threshold a setup should yield. Less than that is ultra-short-term scalping where commissions kill you. Even on a choppy day, an experienced trader can reasonably get 5 points once in the AM and once in the PM.
  - The DOL must be clear: buy-side above or sell-side below. This is not trading a level to watch it repel.
- **How to use (daily trade-count rules):**
  - Morning: one trade, at either 8:30 or 9:30. If he gets 5 points at 8:30, he does not trade 9:30. If he misses 8:30, he tries 9:30. A loss at 8:30 may be made back at 9:30.
  - Afternoon (1:30): traded live only if he missed the 9:30 setup or took a morning loss. If the morning was profitable, the afternoon is demo or paper only. Not pushing the edge teaches discipline, not greed.
  - Up to three opportunities a day; he looks for just one to yield 5 points.
- **Example in this video:**
  - **8:30:** ES was above the midnight open, ran a short-term high, traded lower through a short-term low creating an FVG, rallied into it, then sold toward the 4100/4101 SSL.
  - **1:30 PM:** buy stops were taken. Price broke down, returned to the bearish breaker, displaced lower, and broke relative equal lows ("this low, this low and this low"), leaving an imbalance. Price traded back up into it: short for 5 points, then watch for 4070 and the daily FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:39:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2360) — time first, 8:30 news embargo, NY clock; [0:46:36](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2796) — 8:30 sequence: BSL run, premium, reprice to 4100 SSL; [0:47:23](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2843) — short-term low broken, FVG, sell short, 5 points; [1:06:24](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=3984) — AM/PM trade-count rules; [1:10:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4220) — 1:30 PM setup; [1:12:44](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4364) — 3–4 PM market-on-close macro.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:**
  - His son reportedly went from an equity low of about $2,810 to about $18,300 in roughly four weeks of live trading (from the first week of May), "about 400%". ICT stresses this came with him co-piloting minute by minute, and that you should not quit your job on it.
  - Earlier he made his son take a trade at every window (8:30, 9:30, PM, sometimes 3–4 PM) as practice.

### 9:30 recap and reading a failing short: multiple SSL levels taken + bearish order block traded through
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 9:30 price first dropped. He tweeted that price would reprice up to 4105, where buy-side liquidity and an FVG sat, and then the real move would come.
  - He shorted 4105 on demo and tweeted "pay the trader" before taking a 2-contract partial below the short-term low. He then moved his stop slightly better than breakeven.
  - Why he knew it was turning: after BSL was taken, price should have been "off to the races". Instead it took sell-side at many levels (old lows, then relative equal lows, then another low, and more) "without moving very much".
  - The last up-close candle (a bearish order block) should have capped price under bearish institutional order flow. It might be touched once more or slightly exceeded, then price should drop aggressively. Price instead traded up into and through it.
  - After taking multiple levels of sell-side and then that candle, the algorithm seeks buy-side.
- **How to use:** When this happens, take a further partial before the stop is reached rather than closing everything in panic. He closed 3 more contracts as price went through the order block. The remaining 3 were stopped out at better than breakeven, so no losing scratch. Pieces taken off can be added back later (pyramiding) if price resumes.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m at 9:30: short from about 4105, partial below the short-term low, then the bearish order block (up-close candle) failed. Price ran above the high and back above the midnight open, consolidated, then broke down in the afternoon.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Set the chart clock to New York local time.
- 8:30 ET: news embargo lifts, the first time element of the morning. 9:30 ET: equities open.
- NY midnight opening price: on a down day, short at or above it. On an up day, buy at or below it.
- NY lunch: 12:00–1:00 PM. Bearish: highs formed in and before lunch get run at about 1:00 PM. Busy lunches go with large-range days.
- PM session entry time: 1:30 PM ET.
- 3:00–4:00 PM: "market on close macro". Also the hour to wait for daily-range delivery to the target.
- PM pre-conditions: is the daily range expanding up or down, reversing counter-trend, or consolidating ahead of next-day news?
- A breaker needs a known DOL. Without one "it's a gamble".
- Son's model: 5 ES points per trade, then out. One morning trade (8:30 or 9:30). An afternoon live trade only if the morning was missed or lost. A profitable morning means demo/paper in the afternoon.
- 5 points is the minimum worthwhile target. Below that, commissions kill you.
- Math: 1 ES contract is $50/point, so 5 points is $250 a day. At once a day that is 25 handles, or $1,250, a week.
- Trade with normal risk only on medium or high-impact news days. On no-news days, practise at reduced risk.
- Bearish order block = up-close candle. Under bearish order flow it should cap price. If price trades through it after multiple SSL levels were taken, expect a BSL run.
- Levels this day (ES Jun-22): 4070 (old lows / DOL below relative equal lows), 4105 (BSL + FVG at 9:30), 4100/4101 (big-figure SSL at 8:30), about 4040 (caption "440"), then the daily FVG. ICT's previous-day loss was 5 points.
- He says he has now taught everything except money management, which is "next week".

## Process, risk & psychology
- After a loss, do not rush to make it back. He took a 5-point loss the previous day, could have made 4x that later, and chose not to, to model behaviour. ([0:03:22](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=202), [0:55:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=3320))
- Do not trade live funds with these concepts while you are still learning them. ([0:04:10](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=250))
- Pick a model that matches your personality: full daily range vs quick small pieces. Do not copy someone else's model because it works for them. ([0:24:59](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1499))
- Kill FOMO early through backtesting. When a setup does not form at your time, move to the sidelines. ([0:28:57](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1737))
- Low-hanging-fruit approach: small fixed goals (5 points, like his old "25 pips a week" on BabyPips) for encouragement. It is not a lifetime cap. ([0:52:10](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=3130))
- Discipline over greed. Knowing you can take setups any time removes the urge to overtrade and the fear that the concepts will stop working. ([1:07:12](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4032))
- Quiet students focused only on results do best. Clout-seekers who try to rebrand the concepts tend to fail. ([1:09:32](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4172))
- Graduation means seeing many valid setups and letting them pass without regret, free of fear and greed. ([1:18:31](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4711))
- Losing trades are like a flat tire: a delay, not the end of the trip. When it gets hard, take a week off and come back fresh. Do not make it "I have to get out of my job now". ([1:20:04](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4804), [1:20:52](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4852))
- Real money creates real feelings (drawdown pain, profit joy) that a demo cannot. That is part of why the son trades small live. ([0:51:24](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=3084))

## Glossary candidates
`Pay the trader` — ICT's cue to take a partial profit.
`Market on close macro` — the 3:00–4:00 PM ET algorithmic window where a small setup forms most days.
`Macro (algorithmic)` — a short sequence of instructions the algorithm runs in a time window: seek a level, move to a premium or discount, move suddenly or consolidate, then wait for the next time element.
`Manual intervention` — a non-algorithmic, abrupt repricing (FOMC, rate decisions) that breaks the daily range's normal "map" limits.
`Algorithmic sell day` — a one-sided down day with no meaningful retracements, where retail S/R concepts fail.
`News embargo (8:30)` — the 8:30 ET release time that starts the morning time sequence for index futures.
`Busy lunch` — on large-range or fast-market days, the 12–1 lunch hour delivers significant moves or highs/lows instead of consolidating.
`Cannibalizing orders` — smart money selling into both shorts' buy stops and breakout buy stops above a short-term high.
