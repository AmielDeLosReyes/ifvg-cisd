## Summary
This is the opening livestream of the 2024 mentorship, taught live (no market replay) as if to his son Caleb, a beginner who will later trade a Topstep funded account. The first part covers mindset: work out what kind of trader you are, don't put a deadline on learning, trade one micro contract and one trade a day, and journal your feelings as well as price. The technical core is a beginner's bias method. Use only the 15m/5m/1m charts and sit down in a 30-minute "pre-market range" window (7:00–7:30, 8:00–8:30 or 9:00–9:30 ET). Look left for relative equal highs or lows. The "smooth" side gets run and the "jagged" side has already been raided. ICT calls this contrast of smooth vs. jagged his "secret weapon" and says it was never taught even to his private mentorship. He also defines the 30-minute opening range vs. the pre-market range, a refinement for spotting relative equal highs/lows that will be swept (left swing slightly beyond the right one), and applies breaker, inversion FVG, CE and a Judas swing at 9:30 live on NQ, which ran to the 17,820.75 relative-equal-high liquidity he marked at the start.

## Concepts

### Time as the first filter (algorithm runs on New York time)  (aliases: "time is the first hallmark", macro)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Price is not moved by buying and selling pressure. It is "coded" to move based on time. Time is the first hallmark of when and why a market produces displacement, whether that is an impulsive run or a retracement. A move happening at a specific time, or within a 20-minute period, is a macro. Every time he mentions is New York local time; the algorithm runs on NY time "period". Unless a setup is grounded in time first, "if it's not the right time of day it is absolutely not going to work", whoever the trader is.
- **How to identify:** Set a NY world clock on your phone. Do all the study inside the specified windows (see Pre-market range / Bias).
- **How to use:** Look for setups only at the key times. The first line he drew on the chart was chosen because of time first.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Aug 5 2024. He drew the relative-equal-high line before the session ran and justified the pick with "the algorithm is going to go based on time".

### Pre-market range vs. opening range (30-minute windows)  (aliases: PMR, opening range, "every hourly candle")
- **Kind:** definition (new framing)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The AM session starts at **7:00 ET**, whether you trade Forex or futures.
  - **7:00–7:30 = pre-market range.** **7:30–8:00 = opening range.**
  - 8:00–8:30 is also used as a pre-market-range window. (By the same hourly logic 8:30–9:00 would be its opening range, but that is implied, not stated.)
  - **9:00–9:30 = pre-market range.** **9:30–10:00 = opening range.** The opening range is the first 30 minutes after the NY opening bell.
  - "There is no 15-minute opening range." The opening range is 30 minutes, and that length is algorithmic.
  - He hints that he can trade "every single hourly candle" this way: first half is pre-market range, second half is opening range.
- **How to identify:** A 30-minute box that starts at the top of the hour (7, 8 or 9). Everything to the left of the box is where you look for relative equal highs or lows.
- **How to use:** It is the framework for the bias exercise below. Don't call the pre-market range the opening range.

### Daily bias from smooth relative equal highs/lows (the "secret weapon")  (aliases: smooth vs. jagged, "where it's smooth it's going", contrasting one side of the market against the other)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule (beginner bias framework)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sit down at a key window: **7:00–7:30, or 8:00–8:30, or 9:00–9:30 ET**. That gives you three opportunities every morning. You don't need the afternoon or London.
  - Look to the left of the window on the **15m, 5m and 1m** charts for **relative equal highs or relative equal lows**. Whichever side has them is your bias, because price will draw to it.
  - **If there are relative equal highs above AND relative equal lows below:** wait for the first one to be disrupted, then work toward the other one. Defer and wait.
  - Each window that passes without the run makes it more likely, not less. Example: the relative equal high formed at 7:00, price didn't go there in 8:00–8:30, so 9:00–9:30 "chances are that's the run".
  - "Key takeaway": the framework starts at 7:00, so look for the relative equal highs to form from 7:00 on, "not before". Relative equal highs/lows from before 7:00 (the London session) may work, but expecting London levels to be run is often wrong, so starting at 7:00 acts as a filter.
  - He calls this a secret weapon he had never given out, even to his private mentorship: "a flamethrower" that "makes every playing field even". The market can't hide it.
- **How to identify:** Smooth means two swing points close together that make a flat, obvious level. Jagged means choppy swings where stops have already been run ("the work has been done").
- **How to use:** Trade toward the smooth side. Use PD arrays (breakers, FVGs, inversion FVGs) as stepping stones toward it. A level or inefficiency visible on all three timeframes is "three timeframes telling you pay attention to me". You don't need to trade every day. Trade when it's obvious, and accept that sometimes you'll miss the move.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Mon Aug 5 2024. After a multi-thousand-handle selloff there was a big NWOG overhead and the market had dropped early, around 7:00. On the 5m, relative equal highs (each high slightly above the next one to its right) put liquidity at **17,820.75**, and the first line he drew was there. Below, the lows were jagged, so they had already been raided. Price formed a bullish breaker, made a 9:30 Judas-swing drop into it, then ran up through intermediate FVGs and took the 17,820.75 highs.
- **Timestamps:** [1:43:12](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6192) — 7:00–7:30 window, relative equal highs/lows formed prior · [1:47:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6479) — "secret weapon": contrast one side of the market against the other · [1:53:31](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6811) — whichever side has equal highs/lows is the bias; if both, wait for the first to break · [2:05:29](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7529) — three windows, likelihood builds; start at 7:00, not before
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:**
  - A higher-timeframe draw is "not necessary". He used only 15/5/1 and time.
  - The level does not have to be hit in that session. That is the risk you take on every trade.
  - Manual intervention (news, geopolitics) can always break it.
  - The wording on the 7:00 window is ambiguous. At 1:43 he says look for relative equal highs/lows "that have happened prior to 7 o'clock". At 2:05 he says start looking for them to form at 7:00, "not before". Most likely the AM framework begins at 7:00 and pre-7:00 (London) levels are the weaker filter (partly caption unclear).
  - He admits relative equal highs/lows aren't new teaching, but says students haven't actually traded this way.

### Relative equal highs/lows: how to tell which ones get swept  (aliases: relative equal highs/lows, "priming", "sympathetic side", failure swing)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Highs:** if the swing high on the left is slightly higher than the one on the right, there is a very high probability price will run above them. The lower right-hand high is what "frames these two as relatively equal".
  - **Lows:** the high-probability pair is a low followed by a slightly higher low (the left one is the deeper one).
  - This is the shape of a failure swing, but he is not trading the failure swing. He is identifying what the algorithm refers back to.
  - The algorithm stores these as an array: "this high at this time, and a higher high prior to it, in close proximity". The proximity filter is "not very much" of a price difference, and he won't disclose the exact number.
  - **Priming:** continually creating, inspiring or manipulating the expectations of a large number of participants, so they expect the high to hold or to break.
  - **Sympathetic side:** when price turns down just under the prior high, shorts relax and treat it as resistance. New shorts put their stops just above those highs, and that is the liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Two swing highs or lows close together. For highs, the right one is slightly lower; for lows, the right one is slightly higher. Smooth and flat-looking.
- **How to use:** Mark them as the draw on liquidity. When one is swept, take a screenshot, fade (lighten) it on the chart, and shift focus to the next PD array or target.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m: relative equal highs with the left one slightly higher left liquidity at 17,820.75. Smaller relative equal highs formed before 9:30 on top of the bullish breaker and were also swept.
- **Timestamps:** [1:14:55](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4495) — the "secret" to a high-probability relative equal high · [1:15:44](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4544) — priming · [1:17:22](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4642) — the relative-equal-lows version, failure swing · [1:18:12](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4692) — the algorithm references them as an array; filter not disclosed
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** It's high probability, not certain, and it doesn't have to happen in the session you're trading.

### Smooth vs. jagged price (shark / still-water analogy)  (aliases: "still waters invite rocks", "shark with no teeth")
- **Kind:** new coinage (principle)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Smooth relative equal levels are like a shark with no teeth, or still water: they invite disruption.
  - Jagged price is where a shark attack has already happened. You can see the wounds, and the "cannibals" have already eaten. Don't trade into a level where the frenzy has already taken place.
  - The market goes to areas of smoothness to disrupt the orders resting above or below them. This holds on every timeframe, from sub-minute charts up to quarterly.
  - His closing rule: "Where it's smooth it's going; where it's jagged it's going to move away from."
- **How to identify:** Compare the two sides of the current range. Flat, clean highs or lows are smooth. Choppy swings with stop runs are jagged.
- **How to use:** Your target side is the smooth side. A jagged run that then rips the other way tells you buy-side liquidity is next (shorts trapped on a breakout).
- **Example in this video:** NQ: the lows had been disrupted and were jagged after the 7:00 drop and the 9:30 Judas swing. The highs at 17,820.75 were smooth, and price went to them.
- **Timestamps:** [1:09:15](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4155) — the two-sharks analogy · [1:10:05](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4205) — the market goes to smoothness on any timeframe · [1:54:18](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6858) — jagged then ripped higher means look for buy stops · [2:14:17](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=8057) — closing rule
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He ties the jaggedness to "fingerprints" left by consistent winners, using the World Series of Poker final-table players as the comparison.

### Draw on liquidity (DOL)  (aliases: draw, "low-hanging fruit")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The draw on liquidity is just an initial reasonable assumption about a directional move that could unfold. It is not the be-all, end-all target or the end point of the run. Price can retrace or even make a lower low first, though he says that is highly unlikely. The New Week Opening Gap (Friday settlement to the Sunday 6:00 pm ET open) is a draw on liquidity, but price doesn't need to reach it. For a short-term bias, use the low-hanging-fruit objective, such as nearby relative equal highs.
- **How to use:** It gives you something to focus on, and a way to judge each candle's behavior as it forms against your expectation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 5: a huge NWOG overhead after a multi-thousand-handle decline. The short-term draw was the relative equal highs instead.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:19](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=2899) — relative equal highs and NWOG as draws · [0:49:08](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=2948) — low-hanging fruit for short-term bias · [1:06:50](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4010) — DOL is not the terminus

### What the algorithm reaches for: old highs/lows and inefficiencies  (aliases: liquidity or inefficiency, 8:30 spooling)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price moves between obvious levels: old highs, old lows and inefficiencies (FVGs). Those are "the only two things you're worried about".
  - At **8:30** the algorithm starts "spooling" for liquidity or inefficiencies. Asked whether it will go for an old high/low or an FVG, he says it goes for both.
  - This happens "every single day" between **8:00 and 8:30**, which he insists on absolutely. That alone, he says, is enough foundation to "make all the money you'd ever want".
  - Beginners aren't trying to predict the day's direction or catch 50-handle runs, only to study and observe.
- **How to use:** Sit down before 8:30, preferably at 8:00. Mark the highs, lows and inefficiencies on the 15m, 5m and 1m.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: just after 8:30 price went below relative equal lows, then traded above the short-term high made at 8:36 and into an FVG.

### Displacement
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Displacement is when the market runs against the pre-session, pre-day, pre-trend or price-swing direction. It is a counter move to what has already been in play, and it is aggressive.
- **Example in this video:** NQ after 8:30: price had been dropping, took liquidity below, then moved aggressively above the short-term high.

### Fair value gap: SIBI/BISI naming, volume imbalance, CE  (aliases: SIBI, BISI, inefficiency, mid gap, consequent encroachment)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A fair value gap made by a down-close candle is a **SIBI** (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency).
  - One made by an up-close candle is a **BISI** (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency).
  - When a volume imbalance sits next to the gap, include it in the gap. Watch the candle bodies being encapsulated by it.
  - When price is above an FVG, that FVG is a discount array. Price trading down to its mid-gap (CE) and holding is the signal to look for the run.
  - FVGs visible on all three timeframes act as "stepping stones" toward the draw.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 1m after 8:30: it took 6 candles from a volume imbalance to run above the short-term high and into the SIBI above.
  - Later, a wick traded down to the mid-gap of an FVG below price (a discount array), and then the run started.
  - A volume imbalance encapsulated in a gap (caption "SIII", unclear whether SIBI or BISI) saw price run exactly to that gap's high.
- **Timestamps:** [1:01:16](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3676) — 6-candle count, SIBI/BISI definitions · [1:25:28](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5128) — include the volume imbalance; bodies encapsulated · [1:30:21](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5421) — trade into mid gap = discount array CE · [1:39:12](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5952) — three timeframes; stepping stones

### Breaker block (bullish), and breaker vs. MSS
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - To know whether you're looking at a breaker or a shift in market structure, you need a higher-timeframe premise and a directional draw (where the market is going).
  - **Bullish breaker:** when price drops through relative equal lows, the whole run of consecutive up-close candles before the drop is technically the breaker. The most sensitive part is the last up-close candle.
  - The candle bodies respect the level even when wicks run through it. Bodies "tell you the narrative".
  - This is not a supply/demand zone. He reads algorithmic candle bodies, not floor-trader perception, and contrasts it with Sam Seiden.
- **How to use:** If the narrative is higher, expect price to come back to the bullish breaker and then reach for the short-term high and inefficiency above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: relative equal lows were taken, the bodies stopped at the range low of the last up-close candle (wicks went through), and the range high is the bullish breaker. Price later came back down into the breaker (and again at the 9:30 Judas swing), then rallied. He calls one bounce a nice scalp of about 20 handles if read on a 15-second chart (exact prices caption unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [1:22:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4979) — breaker vs. MSS needs HTF premise and draw · [1:23:49](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5029) — last up-close candle; bodies vs. wicks · [1:26:16](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5176) — return to the breaker, scalp · [1:57:30](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7050) — recap: breaker identified before it was hit

### Inversion fair value gap
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says:** Once price trades above a short-term high and its inefficiency, he wants that inefficiency to act as support. That makes it an inversion FVG, and from there price reaches for the next high and inefficiency.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: a gap that was overhead was traded through and became an inversion FVG. It sent price higher, price retraced, and then a second leg ("redistribution") took the smooth highs.

### Balanced price delivery vs. runs with separation
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Back-and-forth price, where each candle overlaps the prior highest high and lowest low, is very balanced delivery, with no liquidity or inefficiency inside it. Runs leave separations (gaps). A spike that stops right at the top of a breaker after a balanced creep is meaningful.
- **Timestamps:** [1:25:28](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5128) — balanced, overlapping delivery · [1:55:05](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6905) — creeping up balanced, spike stops at the top of the breaker

### Equilibrium measured from the relative equal lows, not the lowest low
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** For the midpoint of the expected leg, draw a fib from slightly above the relative equal highs (the target; no special logic to the exact price) down to the relative equal lows, not the lowest low. The lowest low is the manipulation. The FVG near that equilibrium is the entry area. An FVG slightly above equilibrium is still usable because the relative equal highs haven't been taken yet.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: the fib from just above the 17,820.75 highs to the relative equal lows put the midpoint close to an FVG. He said "watch this fair value gap" in real time, and price took off to the highs.
- **Timestamps:** [1:59:55](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7195) — the midpoint between where price came from and where it's going · [2:00:43](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7243) — use the relative equal lows, not the lowest low · [2:01:30](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7290) — the called FVG launches the run

### Market standard delivery vs. manual intervention
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Normal algorithmic delivery goes up a little and comes back a little.
  - Manual intervention is FOMC, CPI or NFP-type delivery: one direction, no pause. If you're on the wrong side when it hits, "you're dead". It is often untradeable once it starts ("chasing the wind").
  - Manual intervention (and geopolitical shocks) is the one thing that always makes his model wrong, and nobody can predict it. That is the main reason never to over-leverage.
  - Gaps can jump far beyond your stop, which is why brokers make you sign risk disclosures.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- All times are New York local time. The AM session starts at **7:00 ET** for both Forex and futures.
- Key study windows: **7:00–7:30**, **8:00–8:30** and **9:00–9:30** ET. Three chances every morning. If you can't make 7:00, do the same thing at 8:00 or 9:00. At worst, 7:00–7:45 or 7:30 works with smaller timeframes.
- Pre-market range: 7:00–7:30 and 9:00–9:30 (8:00–8:30 is used the same way). Opening range: 7:30–8:00 and 9:30–10:00. Opening ranges are **30 minutes, never 15**.
- Sit down before 8:30, preferably at 8:00. At 8:30 the algorithm seeks liquidity or inefficiencies, and it goes for both.
- Bias = the side with relative equal highs or lows to the left of the window. If both sides have them, wait for the first to be disrupted, then target the other.
- Look for the relative equal highs/lows to form from 7:00 on, "not before". Pre-7:00 (London) levels are less reliable.
- Relative equal high to be swept: left high slightly higher than the right one. Relative equal low: left low slightly lower, so the right one is a slightly higher low.
- Beginner charts: 15m, 5m and 1m only.
- Bullish breaker: the run of up-close candles before the drop; the last up-close candle is the most sensitive; bodies matter more than wicks.
- Include an adjacent volume imbalance with the FVG. An FVG below price is a discount array, and its mid-gap (CE) is the reaction point.
- Equilibrium fib: from the target (just above the relative equal highs) to the relative equal lows, not the lowest low.
- An FVG made by a down-close candle is a SIBI; one made by an up-close candle is a BISI.
- New Week Opening Gap = Friday settlement to the Sunday 6:00 pm ET open. It is a draw on liquidity.
- Macro = a move at a specific time or within a 20-minute period.
- Event Horizon = the midpoint between two New Week or New Day Opening Gaps.
- Journal the 7:00–11:00 ET block (four hours) daily on the 15m, 5m and 1m.
- Size: one **micro** contract and **one trade per day**. More than one micro on a ~$3,000 funded-account cushion while not consistent is gambling.
- Pyramiding: not until about a year after trading one contract consistently.
- First live trade: flip a coin for direction, lowest leverage, a 15-point stop, and let it happen, just to break the ice.

## Glossary candidates
`Pre-market range` — the first 30 minutes of the hour (7:00–7:30, 8:00–8:30, 9:00–9:30 ET) used as the bias-reading window; not the opening range.
`Opening range` — the 30 minutes after the pre-market range (7:30–8:00; 9:30–10:00 after the NY bell); always 30 minutes, never 15.
`Smooth / jagged` — smooth = clean relative equal highs/lows not yet raided (the target); jagged = choppy swings where stops have already been run (move away from them).
`Priming` — the market repeatedly shaping crowd expectations around a level (e.g., a slightly lower second high) so that stops pile up beyond it.
`Sympathetic side` — the relief and resistance belief shorts feel when price turns just under a prior high, which leads them to place stops just above it.
`Spooling (8:30)` — the algorithm starting to reach for liquidity and/or inefficiencies at 8:30 ET.
`Manual intervention` — news- or event-driven one-way delivery (FOMC, CPI, NFP, geopolitical shocks), as opposed to normal back-and-forth "market standard delivery".
`Balanced price delivery` — overlapping back-and-forth candles with no gaps; holds no liquidity or inefficiency.
`SIBI / BISI` — an FVG made by a down-close candle (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency) / an FVG made by an up-close candle (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency).
`Event Horizon` — the midpoint between two New Week Opening Gaps or New Day Opening Gaps.
`Reaper FVG` — a named FVG variant from the private mentorship, mentioned but not defined.
`Low-hanging fruit` — the nearest obvious liquidity objective, used for short-term bias instead of a distant draw such as a far NWOG.
