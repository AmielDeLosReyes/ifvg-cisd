- **What ICT says:** Between 5:00 AM and 6:00 AM ET (after London close, before pre-session officially starts), price action is low-probability. Price runs can occur, but they're not to be trusted. 6:00 AM is the earliest that higher-probability price runs can materialize. Michael prefers to wait for 6:00 AM before looking for scalps.
- **How to identify:** Mark 5:00 AM and 6:00 AM on your chart; avoid trading 5:00–6:00; watch for setups forming at 6:00+.
- **How to use:** Use 5:00–6:00 AM for observation and setup identification, not execution. At 6:00 AM, if a setup is forming, you can enter with higher conviction.

### Relative Equal Highs (aliases: REH, equal highs, liquidity cluster)
- **Kind:** definition + reference
- **What ICT says:** Two or more highs at the same price level. These form pools of buy-side liquidity (limit orders resting above). When price approaches REH from below, expect a test or break above. Once taken, expect pullback or reversal.
- **How to identify:** Scan recent price action (especially overnight/London session); find two+ highs within a few ticks of each other.
- **How to use:** Use REH as targets for long trades or as stop-out points for shorts. Once REH is taken (break above), prepare for volatility and potential reversal.
- **Example in this video:** "Relative equal highs formed during the London session." Later: "relative equal highs, be mindful that they'll probably utilize that as a next draw on liquidity." Michael uses them as directional markers.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:48](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=48) (REH formed London session) · [0:07:55](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=475) (REH as next draw on liquidity).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** REH is a two-way level; it's both a target and a reversal point depending on direction.

### Buy-Side and Sell-Side Liquidity Sweeps (aliases: sweep sequence, double-sweep, buy then sell)
- **Kind:** pattern + execution framework
- **What ICT says:** When price sweeps buy-side liquidity first, then sell-side second, it's a "buy side taken, sell side taken" sequence. This double-sweep creates inversion fair value gaps and validates the directional move. Smart money uses this sequence to take out both retail longs (at buy-side highs) and retail shorts (at sell-side lows) before executing the true directional move.
- **How to identify:** Watch for price spike above a level (buy-side swept), then pullback and spike below (sell-side swept). Each sweep creates an inefficiency/gap.
- **How to use:** After buy-side is taken and pull back occurs, watch for the sell-side to be taken next. That second sweep validates the direction.
- **Example in this video:** "Convenient how it ran up above taking that buy side. We broke down traded down into that low there clearing out that area. So buy side taken first then sell side."
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:23](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=143) (buy side taken, then sell side swept; sequence).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The sequence is key: buy-side first validates the algorithm is "running" that direction.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap with Close Validation (aliases: iFVG close requirement, body below CE)
- **Kind:** rule + distinction
- **What ICT says:** To confirm an iFVG, you MUST see a close outside the gap. A wick into the gap doesn't count. Example: inversion iFVG between two candles; on retrace, price wicks into upper half but closes below it = iFVG confirmed. Michael emphasizes: "iFVGs require the close. Order block does not require close. Inversion fair value gaps require the close." This is critical distinction.
- **How to identify:** Mark the FVG (low of lower candle to high of higher candle); watch the next retracement; check where it closes.
- **How to use:** Only trade iFVGs once close is outside the gap. Don't guess based on wicks; wait for close.
- **Example in this video:** "We have a candlestick close outside of this buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency which validates this becoming an inversion fair value gap... We want to see it trade down. This is not validating it. It's just wicking down through it."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:56](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=236) (close outside = validates iFVG; wick alone doesn't validate).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Close validation is non-negotiable; it's what separates true iFVGs from guesses.

### Upper Quadrant of Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency (aliases: upper portion, upper half of gap)
- **Kind:** measurement
- **What ICT says:** Divide a gap or inefficiency into quadrants (four 25% sections). The upper quadrant is the top 25% of the gap. When price rallies and stops in the upper quadrant before reversing, it shows weakness; price didn't reach consequent encroachment (the wick's high or top of gap). This is a rejection signal.
- **How to identify:** Mark the gap; divide into 4 equal parts; note which quadrant price reaches.
- **How to use:** If price reaches only the upper quadrant and reverses, expect continuation of the down move (for bearish structure).
- **Example in this video:** "The rally up, we stop in the upper quadrant of this buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency. And then we aggressively leave the range."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:56](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=236) (stop in upper quadrant; aggressively leave range).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Quadrants are finer detail; they help you see where exactly the weakness is.

### Position Scaling and Adding (aliases: layering, position building, adding on weakness)
- **Kind:** risk management + execution
- **What ICT says:** Instead of entering a full position at once, start small (e.g., 2 contracts) at the first signal. As price confirms and moves in your direction, add more. This reduces entry risk and lets you scale in as conviction builds. Example: enter 2 contracts at first iFVG target, add 5 more when price breaks sell-side, add 2 more on next weakness.
- **How to identify:** Define your levels before entry; plan where you'll add.
- **How to use:** Enter small at first signal; add at confirmation levels. This gives you better average fill and reduces drawdown.
- **Example in this video:** "I threw in a small position with just simply two contracts... anticipating... then I added the lines portion of the position I wanted to assume... took off below here. I added them right back on... I added another exit as it went through."
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:44](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=284) (two contracts initial entry, add 5 more, add 2 back).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Layering reduces emotion and improves execution; you're not forcing full position at uncertain price.

### Market Maker Sell Model (MMSM) Structure (aliases: accumulation → reversal → distribution sequence)
- **Kind:** pattern structure
- **What ICT says:** MMSM has a specific sequence: original consolidation → first stage accumulation (sometimes just one stage in fast moves) → smart money reversal → low-risk sell → first stage distribution → second stage distribution → drop below original consolidation. The final drop is the largest, easiest move. Identifying this structure predicts where price will go.
- **How to identify:** Scan the chart; find consolidation, then count stages.
- **How to use:** Once you identify the stages, expect the second stage distribution to be the biggest move. Let it run; don't exit early.
- **Example in this video:** "Original consolidation. First stage accumulation... Smart money reversal. Low risk sell. First stage distribution. Second stage redistribution. And then the biggest move takes place there."
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:32](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=332) (full MMSM structure identified).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** MMSM is a reliable pattern; second stage distribution is the best trade.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) as Entry and Exit Level (aliases: CE level, wick low, deep point)
- **Kind:** precision timing
- **What ICT says:** Consequent encroachment is the wick's deepest point (or top for bullish setups). Use CE as a precise entry point. Example: Michael entered his short "right at consequent encroachment of that little inversion fair value gap." CE levels are where smart money is likely trading; they're high-probability entries.
- **How to identify:** Mark the wick's deep point; that's CE.
- **How to use:** Use CE as your entry zone; stop just beyond it.
- **Example in this video:** "First entry with the two contracts right at consequent encroachment of that little inversion fair value gap."
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:20](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=380) (first entry at CE).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** CE is the most precise entry; use it when you have high conviction.

### Breaker (High-Low-Higher High) (aliases: breaker formation, swing structure)
- **Kind:** pattern
- **What ICT says:** A breaker is a short-term high, a low below it, then a higher high (but still lower than the original short-term high). This structure is a reversal signal. Michael uses it as one of many confluences for direction confirmation.
- **How to identify:** Mark a swing high, then a lower low, then a test back up.
- **How to use:** Breaker formations are reversal filters; use them to confirm turns.
- **Example in this video:** "We have a breaker which is a high, low, higher high."
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:10](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=190) (breaker: high, low, higher high).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Breaker is a component of larger patterns; use with other confluences.

### Terminus Objective (aliases: final target, ultimate target)
- **Kind:** measurement / targeting
- **What ICT says:** The terminus objective is the ultimate, deepest target for a move. It's often a previous swing low or a calculated level far below/above current price. Michael mentions having a "terminus objective" but not showing it initially; later he adds it as an additional exit.
- **How to identify:** Identify the lowest low or highest high the market could theoretically reach (lowest REL, oldest swing low, etc.).
- **How to use:** Use as a final runner target; most traders won't reach it, but it's there if conviction holds.
- **Example in this video:** "Targeting that. Once it went through it... I added another exit." (The terminus objective is the ultimate profit-taking level.)
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:32](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=332) (targeting the terminus objective).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Terminus is not always reached; use it as a "dream target" while taking partials on the way.

- **Self-directed learning:** Michael encourages viewers to pause and annotate the chart themselves before he reveals his analysis. This is active learning.

- **Pajamas trading:** Michael executed this multi-leg, high-precision trade from home, on his phone, before markets opened. The order flow and structure work regardless of location.
- **Partial-taking discipline:** Rather than holding to "the one big target," taking partials at minor sell-side and other confirmations reduces emotional burden and locks gains.
