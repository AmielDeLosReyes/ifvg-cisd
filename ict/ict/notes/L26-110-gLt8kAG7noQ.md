## Concepts

### Opening Range Gap (ORG)
- **Kind:** definition | rule
- **What ICT says:** Yesterday's regular trading hours (RTH) open and close establish the RTH opening range gap for the next day. The gap between 4:14pm ET close and 9:30am ET open forms the structure. If 9:30 open is below the prior close, it's a discount gap (bullish setup likely).
- **How to identify:** Compare 4:14pm ET close to 9:30am ET open; note the high and low of the gap range and subdivide by octants (1/8ths) and quarters.
- **How to use:** Use ORG levels as support/resistance and potential rally/selloff targets. The 50% and 70% retracement levels are common targets.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opened 9:30 at a discount (below prior close); immediately rallied to ~70% of the ORG and failed, indicating weakness. Then traded back into and below the ORG low, confirming further downside potential.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:29](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=209), [0:33:38](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=2018), [0:34:25](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=2065)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On high-uncertainty days (like this one with geopolitical risk), price action is "wild and woolly" and less precise. ORG analysis is still valid but requires more patience to see clean execution.

### Liquidity Sweeps (Buy-Side / Sell-Side)
- **Kind:** rule | entry model
- **What ICT says:** On a bearish sentiment day (engineered by news/geopolitical risk), market makers first run price up to take out shorts' stops (buy-side liquidity), then run it down to take out longs' stops (sell-side liquidity). Order of execution matters: if buy side is taken first on a bearish day, sell side remains and should be the next target.
- **How to identify:** Watch for a sharp move that "traps" a logical group of traders (e.g., shortsellers on a bearish day), then reverse. Mark the short-term highs and lows where stops would logically be placed.
- **How to use:** After buy-side (BSL) is swept on a bearish day, expect sell-side sweep next. Don't trade until both sides are cleared, as traders on both sides are "unseated."
- **Example in this video:** 9:30 price ran higher, sweeping shorts' stops. Then it failed to break above ORG high and reversed lower, setting up the sell-side sweep around the lower quadrant/octant levels. By 9:30am, both BSL and SSL were tagged.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:53](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=833), [0:16:17](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=977), [0:20:29](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1229), [0:35:11](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=2111)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a special case of the market maker buy/sell model applied to opening ranges on sentiment-engineered days.

### Tape Reading & Price Action Interpretation
- **Kind:** rule | definition
- **What ICT says:** Reading the tape means observing which liquidity pools are hit, in what order, and what remains. It's "reading what the market maker is trying to do" by watching what they take out first. The real skill is discerning narrative: geopolitical sentiment → trader positioning → liquidity location → execution order.
- **How to identify:** Chart key price levels (ORG, prior day high/low, BSL, SSL, prior wicks). Watch live candles form and see which levels hold or break.
- **How to use:** Don't trade until you can describe what the market should do next based on what's been taken/left. If you can't place a comfortable stop and target, don't trade.
- **Example in this video:** After observing the ORG and first liquidity sweep, ICT notes "I would have preferred it to be a little bit more animated" (deeper penetration of sell side). He reads the subsequent shallow dip as incomplete and still expects lower prices.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:43](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=163), [0:25:54](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1554), [0:27:27](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1647)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Tape reading requires "2+ months of consistency" of observation without trading. It's an experience-driven skill, not a book skill.

### Sentiment Engineering & Narrative Trading
- **Kind:** rule | definition
- **What ICT says:** Geopolitical news (e.g., threats of military action) creates fear-based sentiment. Retail traders position against that sentiment (bearish bias). Market makers exploit this by taking the opposite side first—running price up to stop out shorts, then down to trap breakout buyers. The narrative is the setup; price action confirms the execution.
- **How to identify:** Watch economic calendar and geopolitical headlines. Note when sentiment is "engineered" bearish or bullish. Then observe if price action goes the opposite direction initially.
- **How to use:** Don't trade with sentiment; trade against it once you confirm the setup. But on extremely high-uncertainty days, the risk may be too high.
- **Example in this video:** Trump posts and war threats made traders expect a downside move. Market ran up first (against sentiment), trapping shorts. By the time sellers entered, they were already trapped.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:27](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=447), [0:08:15](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=495), [0:15:29](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=929), [0:18:02](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1082)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated; this is core to the framework.

### High-Risk / High-Uncertainty Trading Environment
- **Kind:** rule | psychology
- **What ICT says:** On days with major geopolitical or economic risk (e.g., potential military action in <11 hours), price action becomes unpredictable and "wild." The smartest move is to observe only, not trade. "If you make money today, consider it lottery—no skill involved." Risk of blown accounts or emotional losses is extremely high. Best practice: sit on sidelines, read the tape, take the learning without the capital risk.
- **How to identify:** Major headline events (wars, elections, policy shifts), multiple sharp reversals in overnight/early session, low conviction candles, consensus bias in one direction.
- **How to use:** Stay off the charts. Or, if watching live, do so for education only with zero capital at risk. Journal the levels and observations for later review.
- **Example in this video:** April 7 opening with war threat at 8pm ET = high-risk day. ICT repeatedly warns not to trade; he's observing for educational purposes only.

### Entry Protocols & Position Management
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** "I have to have protocols and things that keep you from taking action." Without rules (stops, targets, conviction filters), traders chase price, experience massive drawdowns, and blow accounts. Even on quiet days, if you can't place a comfortable stop or target, don't trade.
- **How to identify:** Before entering, ask: Where is my stop? Where is my target? Can I defend both? If answer is no, don't trade.
- **How to use:** Use ORG levels, liquidity voids, and prior day structure to set stops above/below obvious levels. Set targets at the next PD array or key level.
- **Example in this video:** The shallow sell-side penetration would have offered a poor stop-placement scenario ("Where would your stop loss go if you shorted here?"). So no entry is warranted despite the technical setup.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:50](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1130), [0:24:17](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1457)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### Paper Trading & Learning Without Capital Risk
- **Kind:** psychology | rule
- **What ICT says:** New traders should spend 2+ months reading price action and tape with zero capital at risk (no demo, no paper trading, no prop firm evaluation) before even considering live trading. The goal is to build confidence and skill without emotional/financial loss. Doing this in your own hands—feeling the uncertainty—is the only way to develop genuine anticipatory price action skills.
- **How to identify:** You're ready to move on when you can look at a setup and say, "I'm not ready" and mean it, or when you can predict the next 3–5 candles consistently.
- **How to use:** Keep a journal of levels, narratives, and predictions. Review daily. Don't trade. Just observe.
- **Example in this video:** ICT is doing this himself—live streaming but not trading because he's "getting back in the groove" after vacation.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:05](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=545), [0:22:27](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1347), [0:27:27](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1647), [0:30:24](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1824), [0:37:35](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=2255)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT emphasizes that no teacher can transfer this skill. It must be developed "in your own hands." Self-sabotage and doubt are common obstacles.

### Octant & Quadrant Subdivisions
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Subdivide a gap (ORG, FVG, etc.) into 8ths (octants) and 4ths (quadrants). The lower quadrant (lowest 25%) and lower octants are key support; upper quadrant/octants are resistance. Wicks and bodies often stop at these Fibonacci-like levels.
- **How to identify:** Measure the gap height, divide by 8 and 4, mark the levels on the chart.
- **How to use:** Expect price to find support/resistance at these levels before continuing.
- **Example in this video:** The sell-side wick at 25:54 "touched the lower quadrant or...octant 0.625 (the low of yesterday's RTH opening range gap)." Price body stayed there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:25:54](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1554)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On very muddy days, octant levels become less reliable.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- On days with major geopolitical/economic risk (potential military action, policy shifts), don't trade; observe only.
- Entry requires comfortable stop placement and a clear target. If unsure on either, don't trade.
- ORG subdivisions by quarters (quadrants) and eighths (octants) are key S/R levels.
- On sentiment-engineered bearish days, expect buy-side liquidity taken first (shorts stopped), then sell-side next.
- Spend 2+ months reading price action and tape (zero capital risk) before attempting live trading.
- If you can't describe what should happen next using the levels you see, you're not ready to trade.
- Risk of losing capital on uncertainty days is too high; "if you make money, it's lottery luck."

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Observation without trading:** The best learning comes from chart observation without capital at risk. This builds real anticipatory price action skills and removes emotional bias from decision-making.
- **Emotional management:** New traders place too much emphasis on being right immediately. This leads to forced entries, missed stops, and blown accounts. Build patience first by observing for 2+ months.
- **Self-sabotage:** Many traders unconsciously self-sabotage because they don't "feel like they deserve" success in the market. Awareness of this internal conflict is the first step.
- **Expectation management:** Expecting a setup to happen every single day is like trying to hit the Powerball every day. Realistic expectations come only from experience.
- **Uncertainty tolerance:** Learning to sit with uncertainty—not knowing if the market will go up or down—is the hardest skill to develop and cannot be taught in a book.
- **Consistency threshold:** Two months of consistent (correct) tape reading and prediction is a minimum baseline before attempting any real trading.
- **High-uncertainty environment risk:** On days with geopolitical threats or major economic surprises, volatility is severe. Even experienced traders should reduce risk or sit out.

## Glossary candidates
`RTH (Regular Trading Hours)` — U.S. stock market 9:30am–4:15pm ET
`ORG (Opening Range Gap)` — Gap between prior-day close (4:14pm ET) and current-day 9:30am open
`BSL (Buy-Side Liquidity)` — Price levels where short-sellers have placed stops; a run above these levels stops out shorts
`SSL (Sell-Side Liquidity)` — Price levels where long-buyers have placed stops; a drop below these levels stops out longs
`Octants` — Division of a gap/range into eighths (1/8, 2/8, ..., 8/8)
`Quadrants` — Division of a gap/range into quarters (1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4)
`Sentiment Engineering` — Media/headline-driven positioning that skews trader bias, which market makers then exploit
`Tape Reading` — Live observation of price action and candle formation to infer market maker intent
