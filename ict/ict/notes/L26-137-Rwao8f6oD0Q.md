### First Presented Fair Value Gap in the pre-market window (7:00 to 9:30 ET): validity rules  (aliases ICT uses: first presented FVG, "common gap", imbalance, displacement)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (re-statement of his FVG rules, with the "common gap" distinction)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The time window for the first presented FVG in pre-market is 7:00 AM ET (pre-market start) to the 9:30 open, a 2.5 hour window. His interest is "zeroed into a very specific candle, a very specific time of day".
  - A tiny gap that forms in the first minutes after 7:00 (7:00, 7:01, 7:02 candles) is a "common gap" and is not the first presented FVG, because it does not cause displacement. A critic saying "ICT got it wrong because price went to the first gap after 7:00" is looking at a gap he explicitly excluded.
  - The qualifying gap is the one made by a displacement that takes price above a prior high (a candle that displaces above a high, "not engulfing the previous candle"). That displacement is technically a shift in market structure.
  - He states emphatically that an engulfing candle is not a market structure shift.
  - Three-candle construction: candle number one, candle number two (the one that contains the imbalance in the middle), candle number three (closes the pattern). The imbalance is between candle one and candle three.
  - Stop placement: for a bullish FVG the stop goes below the low of candlestick number one; for a bearish FVG the stop goes above the high of candlestick number one.
  - A candle body must stay inside the FVG. A small wick through an inherent small common gap inside the zone is permissible because the real stop (candle one's low) was never in jeopardy.
  - The gap must displace and be significant; "it can't be just a little common gap".
- **How to identify:** After 7:00, wait for the first gap formed by a displacement leg (breaks above a high or below a low); ignore the micro-gaps in the 7:00 to 7:02 candles; mark candle one (low for bullish, high for bearish).
- **How to use:**
  - Use that gap as the point of entry; stop beyond candle one's extreme; plan a partial in advance; if only one contract, plan how and where you will move the stop (see risk section).
  - Entry price within the FVG: he buys the high or upper quadrant of a bullish gap (see "Entry at the FVG high / upper quadrant" below).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday 12 Jan, pre-market (during the previous day's stream). The 7:00 candle (small wick above), then 7:01/7:02 leave a tiny gap that he does not count. Later, a candle displaces above a high; the gap that forms is the first presented FVG. After the 9:30 open, price trades down into this gap and also into the small common gap inside it, but never breaches the low of candle one. Price then rips higher.

### Inversion FVG used as a launch pad during a pre-9:30 "holding pattern"  (aliases ICT uses: inversion fair value gap, iFVG, SIBI, foothold, holding pattern)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - An inversion FVG (iFVG) is a gap used as the opposite of how it was expected to work when it formed. A SIBI (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency; a down-close candle gap) is normally something to sell into if you are bearish; if price overtakes it, it becomes a foothold to propel price higher. Critics say "it doesn't work", but that is the inversion.
  - If you expect a Judas swing lower, you look at the bearish FVG, wait for it to be overtaken, and then use it as a foothold to push price higher, which is what happened at the 9:30 break time.
  - He taught the idea about two years earlier in "Kit's ICT Concepts trader roundup" (caption unclear): look at your FVGs and invert them.
  - In the live stream he said not to short the iFVG because the market was in a holding pattern: with the daily draw above (the 26,133.5 daily relative equal highs), price was expected to hold until the 9:30 opening range / 10:00, then run bullish through the trendline liquidity.
  - Price had to come down first to absorb liquidity (stops of those buying the apparent trendline breakout) into the first presented FVG, then it releases.
  - The volume imbalance: the iFVG here was a sell-side imbalance with a volume imbalance at the low of it. He recalls that when he first taught volume imbalances to mentorship students he pointed only to the volume imbalance (not the whole range) to teach gradually, because of leakers; here it is part of the bigger iFVG.
  - Anything that trades up into this iFVG and drops is a short-term fluctuation. It is useful for tape reading, but it looked "muddy and dirty" because it was part of the holding pattern.
- **How to identify:** A bearish FVG overtaken by an up-close that holds above it; the volume imbalance at its low is a precise level; check what time of day (holding pattern until 9:30).
- **How to use:** Do not short a pre-9:30 iFVG when daily bias is up; wait for the 9:30 opening range release and buy the retest of the iFVG low / first presented FVG with the stop below candle one.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Monday 12 Jan: the 9:30 candle opens at the RTH opening range low, trades down to the low of the iFVG (with the volume imbalance), then rips through and higher.

### Daily bias, higher-timeframe draw, and "hold for the close" (Larry Williams)  (aliases ICT uses: higher time frame draw on liquidity, daily candle close, trading with the larger move)
- **Kind:** rule / bias
- **What ICT says:**
  - Frame direction from the higher time frame draw: here the daily relative equal highs around 26,133.5 were the draw (discussed about a week earlier). The best intraday moves are framed by daily bias, even though counter-trend shorts can work briefly.
  - Idea credited to Larry Williams: if you day trade, be on side with where the daily candle is likely to close and hold something into the close. Decide before you sit down whether the daily candle should close up, and look for longs, holding at least part of the position.
  - His own timing version: long at the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range or pre-market from 7:00 onward, then off-board something between 10:00 and 11:00 (profit-taking; London close; entering NY lunch).
  - Trading only on the side of the daily candle's expected extension lets you catch the big running moves; trading every minor fluctuation against that bias (e.g., a short from the Asian session BSL FVG) will not.
  - Time and price meet like a multiplication table (liquidity price on one axis, time on the other): the algorithm finds where time and price meet.
- **How to identify:** Daily chart: nearby old high/low or relative equal highs/lows or imbalance above/below price; recent liquidity events (e.g., sell side just run).
- **How to use:** Trade only in the daily-bias direction, partial off 10:00 to 11:00, hold remainder to the close if running.
- **Example in this video:** Daily bullish toward 26,133.5; Monday's session (12 Jan) went long from the first presented FVG at the 9:30 release.

### Core tenet checklist: bias to first presented FVG entry  (aliases ICT uses: "central core tenets", multiple choice, kitchen sink approach)
- **Kind:** entry model / process
- **What ICT says:**
  1. Choose a market on a higher-timeframe premise that leads to a specific price level: an old high or old low (single or relative equal highs/lows) or an imbalance above or below price.
  2. Check what recently happened. If there was a stop hunt (raid on sell side), look for a premium array: the first imbalance that price climbs back through inside the range; then decide whether the draw is a single high or relative equal highs. If the buy side was run recently, do the same in reverse. It is multiple choice: one of the three, not all.
  3. Drop to the lower timeframe and wait for the first presented FVG in the time window he specified (he has taught several). It is a finite reference point, not shifting.
  4. The FVG must displace and be significant (not a common gap); enter there, stop from the first candle (candle one).
  5. Pre-plan a partial; if trading one contract, pre-plan where the stop moves, graduating it to protect, and manage until the end.
  - Warnings: do not pile on everything (inversion breaker, 2022 model, COT, seasonals, structure, MMBM/MMSM). That is a "kitchen sink". There is a tool for bias, a tool for timing entry/exit, a tool that confirms or qualifies.
  - But also: it is not a "one candle" premise. A single candle needs supporting actors (market structure, seasonality, range logic); they do not all have to agree. You must know what you are buying, where it is going, where the stop is, how price works inside the range, and whether it is worth holding once it leaves the range.
  - Students may avoid order blocks (too many down-close candles, uncomfortable) and use only the FVG; that is fine. Example: daily looks bullish after sell side ran, relative equal highs above; only take longs at FVGs.
  - It works in every market (gold, indices, crypto per students; he has not used crypto himself).

### Entry at the FVG high / upper quadrant (not the CE or lower)  (aliases ICT uses: consequent encroachment, upper quadrant, limit order, stop loss size)
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a bullish FVG, buy the high (upper boundary) or the upper quadrant of the gap. Do not try to buy the lowest low of the gap.
  - Price often goes down and touches one tick into the high of the gap and runs away; a resting limit at the CE or lower is the greedy approach (you want a better fill, more contracts, a smaller stop) and misses the trade.
  - Current volatility makes very small stops on every trade hard; tiny stops are possible sometimes but it is a knack/experience matter.
  - Overleveraging to hit a profit goal quickly is the danger.
- **How to identify:** Bullish FVG; the entry at its upper boundary/upper quadrant after the first touch.
- **How to use:** Place the entry at the high of the FVG; stop below candle one's low; size down rather than squeezing the stop.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [1:28:40](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5320) — buy the FVG high or upper quadrant, not the CE or lower
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Refines the common "limit at the CE" approach. He says "just look at my executions" as evidence.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- First presented FVG window: 7:00 to 9:30 ET (a 2.5 hour window); micro-gaps in the first minutes after 7:00 do not count.
- FVG stop: bullish below candle one's low; bearish above candle one's high.
- Bodies must stay inside the FVG; small wick through an inherent common gap is permissible if candle one's extreme is not breached.
- An engulfing candle is not a market structure shift; a displacement above a high is.
- Do not short an iFVG pre-9:30 when the daily draw is higher (holding pattern until the 9:30 opening range).
- Entry on a bullish FVG: its high or upper quadrant; not the CE or lower.
- Asian session killzone is a 2-hour window.
- Daily draw in the example: 26,133.5 (daily relative equal highs); NWOG/previous settlement hit 25,933.5.
- Offboard something between 10:00 and 11:00 (London close, entering NY lunch); hold part into the close if the daily candle should close in your direction.
- 8:30 CPI: 104 handles in one 1-minute candle; no trading the morning of CPI/PPI or afternoon of FOMC rate announcement; start looking for trades about 9:00.
- Never risk 20% of an account; trade one contract on prop firms, ignore the 15 to 30 contract allowance.
- Stop is static and rule-based; only move it a little as the trade works; do not jump straight to breakeven.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Review the previous day's recording the next day after sleep; you will see things you missed and it solidifies the model (0:20:37).
- Backtesting "shows you what real jade is": holding the real thing (old data) in your hands lets you spot fake moves live without help (0:53:34). The Jade Master parable (Ed Seykota's Trader's Window, caption "Sakakota") is used for teachability: do not tell the teacher how you should be taught.
- Slow is fast: do not rush; learn the process, defer expectations; do not put a deadline on mastery (0:48:46, 1:22:19).
- The over-leveraging test (1:31:52): before taking a trade, ask "if I lose on this trade, how eager will I be to take the next one?" If you feel tension, nervousness or unease, you are over-leveraged. Act like a board: CEO/CFO review of the trade size before entry (1:33:30).
- If trade size is right, a full stop-out does not deter the next trade: loss is "operator error", "a cost of business", a "premium for being wrong" and information (1:35:07).
- Signs of over-leveraging: worn out even after winning; urge to post only winners on social media for a dopamine hit; showing profits but not drawdown; chasing an equity number (trading the equity curve) is gambling (1:30:16, 1:36:44).
- The "loser cycle": gamblers who numb out and want to blow the account; never enter it by never over-leveraging (1:32:41).
- Do not leave social media after a win to be rewarded; get off social media (1:40:42).
- Prop firms: trade one contract even if 15 or 30 is allowed; make enough, then move money to a regulated discount broker and withdraw; he calls prop firms "doomed" (rules keep changing); use them in a controlled way (1:42:17).
- Trade simple: wait for time, execute a model/idea, risk a specific static stop, move it gradually, be comfortable holding risk (1:45:31).
- Do not be in news-day trades without knowing risk (see news section).

## Glossary candidates
`FIRST PRESENTED FVG` — the first displacement-created fair value gap in the 7:00 to 9:30 ET pre-market window (or other specified window); tiny common gaps before it do not count.
`COMMON GAP` — a small three-candle gap with no displacement behind it, ignored as a valid FVG.
`HOLDING PATTERN` — pre-9:30 consolidation in which a gap is not traded against the daily bias until the opening range releases it.
`TREND LINE PHANTOM` — ICT's name for the retail ascending support line that actually marks resting sell stops.
`FOOTHOLD` — an overtaken (inverted) FVG that price uses as support to propel in the opposite direction.
`VOLATILITY INJECTION` — high or medium impact news that forces price to reveal its hand.
`SURGICAL STRIKE` — his current style: enter on a specific setup, take the profit and go home, no multi-day holds.
`TIME AND PRICE (MULTIPLICATION TABLE)` — the idea that the algorithm finds the point where a price level and a time meet, like cells in a times table.
`OPERATOR ERROR` — a loss caused by the trader, not the model; accepted as a premium for being wrong.
