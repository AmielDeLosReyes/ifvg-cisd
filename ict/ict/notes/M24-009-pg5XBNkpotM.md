## Concepts

### Premium vs discount: do not chase a rich opening (rule: "too rich, too expensive")  (aliases ICT uses: "long in the tooth", "stretched out", "street money / dumb money chases")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** After a gap-higher open plus an 8:30 impulsive run, price is too expensive to buy in the morning session. He will not buy a breakout above the highs and will not even take a bullish FVG in such conditions because the run is "too long in the tooth" and is more likely to revert to fair value (below: lows, relative equal lows, a gap, a consolidation). If it wants to run without him, it can ("it's a move I'm not a part of"). Retail ("the street", "dumb money") buys momentum and sells weakness, perpetually chasing; a professionally minded speculator buys cheap/discount prices and sells expensive/premium prices. Larger pools of capital will not engage while price rallies off the open; they wait for a return into a pool of liquidity where prices are discounted, which is always below old lows, single or relative equal lows.
- **How to identify:** Large gap between prior RTH settlement and the 9:30 open (he annotated the settlement level and the 9:30 open on the chart), an 8:30 impulsive run, price already pushing multiple highs after the open, a "jagged" (choppy-up) market, and a week-and-a-half of grind higher.
- **How to use:** Write the rule in the journal: nothing forces you to pay too much, and nothing should make you feel you will miss out. Chasing runs "five, six hundred... eight points" is very rare; more likely it is "go up, break out, get people caught long" and then reverse. Sit on hands until a signature forms; take the Silver Bullet at 10:00 instead.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m (with 15m top-left and 5m bottom-left), Thursday 8/15/2024. Gap up open; 9:30 open price annotated (digits partly uncaptioned); 8:30 impulse had created the high; price opened, dipped briefly into a small FVG, then ran to the buy-side above the 8:30 high and kept making marginally higher highs ("one more bump above the high") through about 10:00.

### Trapped longs and where their stops sit (run on liquidity logic)  (aliases: "snare", "holding traders in", "jagged")
- **Kind:** definition / rule of thumb
- **What ICT says:** A one-sided run that keeps printing small wick/body pushes through highs is work to (a) hurt anyone who bought a breakout, (b) get more people long or stop out anyone already short, and (c) build net-long positioning that the larger player can later sell into. Longs trail their stops to just below the latest consolidation/gap/low, so that is where the sell-side liquidity is; relative equal lows from the 8:30 consolidation, the gap, and the consolidation itself all hold stops. Shorts' stops sit above the highs; he expects a final overshoot to clear them ("if I was making the market... I would do that too and then sink it after").
- **How to identify:** Price holds above a gap/low, refuses to rally away convincingly, small candles that do not run; a "three drives"-type pattern higher inside liquidity; each new high is only a few ticks to slightly more.
- **How to use:** Expect a retracement to engage the sell-side. Frame, don't force: "we're looking for signatures to support an idea that we're hunting", not "arm wrestling" the market.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: 8:30 run up created a consolidation with relative equal lows; after the 9:30 open price ran to buy-side above 8:30 high and kept pushing; he sees "one, two, three" drives higher into the liquidity and says the longs' stops are right under the gap/consolidation.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:22](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=682) everyone long trails stops under here · [0:16:14](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=974) three drives pattern higher inside liquidity · [0:22:00](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1320) the hold-above is potentially a trap · [0:25:15](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1515) stops just below the consolidation to protect the move
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "If it doesn't rally and start running away, it's more likely just a snare." Contrast: if price does rally away immediately, he is not in it either.

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) — conditions for a short idea after a premium run  (aliases: "treat it as an inversion fair value gap")
- **Kind:** entry model (framing) / refinement
- **What ICT says:** His morning plan is a sequence he wants to see happen, not a prediction: price breaks below a particular gap (highlighted yellow, sitting below price from the 8:30 run, which he calls a breakaway gap / "institutional order flow entry drill" after a bull flag), comes back up into it and treats it as an inversion FVG (resistance), then sends price down into the sell-side liquidity and into the next gap below. He also wants price to remain above the recent lows and not trade down into the lower gap before the iFVG forms.
- **How to identify:**
  - Price must first close/trade through the gap to the downside; then retest from below.
  - Spikes/wicks above the gap are permissible, but the candle bodies must stay inside the shaded gap ("any spiking up with a wick but the body staying inside of that shaded area validates this as an inversion fair value gap").
  - The upper half (above the gap's consequent encroachment) should remain open/unfilled; if price completely closes the gap by trading over the top of it, the idea is dead.
- **How to use:** He wants bodies inside, upper portion open, then displacement below the CE of the wick, with larger candles accelerating down. Target: first the 9:30 opening price (fair value), then lows / sell-side liquidity, possibly into the afternoon.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m ~10:05–10:25 ET (stream clock 0:42–1:04; he states 9:50 at 0:26 and "10:00 now" at 0:36): while price keeps pressing above, he wants the gap (marked in pink) to be traded into with wicks only, upper half left open; "if it completely rolls over top of that pink area and trades higher, I'm standing still, no bias, no touch, no trade". Then wait for the 11:00–11:30 lunch macro and fade whatever has run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:13](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=733) break below the yellow gap, return as iFVG · [0:43:53](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2633) wick above but body inside validates the iFVG · [0:45:31](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2731) upper half should stay open for a strong conviction · [1:04:54](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3894) quadrants on the gap, upper half open, work done in the lower half
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is an unusually explicit statement of the "wick may enter, body must not" tolerance for an iFVG, together with a "upper half must remain open" quadrant condition. The exact identity of the yellow vs pink gaps is hard to follow from audio alone (caption unclear on which gap he points at in places).

### Gap quadrants / consequent encroachment of a gap or wick  (aliases: "run the quadrants", "midpoint", "consequent encroachment of that wick", "discount wick")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** For a large gap, draw the fib from the candle low to the candle high and watch the quadrants: the 50% is the consequent encroachment (CE). For a bearish idea he wants price to stay mostly in the lower half or underneath, leaving the upper half open, and then trade below the CE of the "wick" (midpoint of the wick/gap) with large candles. A weekly wick is also treated as a gap: NQ was trading in the CE of the weekly gap/wick (level read as about "44450" in captions; digits unclear), and that level could act as an inversion level only if price goes down through it, comes back up and uses it as support.
- **How to identify:** Mark the candle low and candle high forming the gap/wick; mark the 50%.
- **How to use:** Closing below the CE point would "indicate it's most likely going to roll over and then return back into the opening range". No close below and a push above = no bias.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, around 10:12 ET: price touches the CE of the wick again ("there we go, touch the consequent encroachment") and he repeats that the portion above must stay open.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:00](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1980) weekly wick treated as a gap, CE · [0:46:20](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2780) CE of the low, candle's high · [0:47:09](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2829) close below this point means roll over · [0:48:56](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2936) touch CE again, upper portion stays open
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Treating a weekly wick as a gap is a short mention but fits his "wick = gap, use CE" teaching.

### Exhaustion gap (gap that has been traded up into fully and left behind)
- **Kind:** definition (brief) / new usage
- **What ICT says:** If price trades up into a gap and moves away aggressively without leaving part of it open, that gap is "exhausting" — it has done its job and loses relevance as resistance. For the key gap, he wants it traded into only partially; the upper half open means "really heavy" and he would have strong conviction for a return to the 9:30 opening price.
- **How to identify:** Gap that is completely filled with bodies, not just wicks. He notes a tiny "Mohawk" (a small wick) outside the range is fine as long as bodies stop inside.
- **How to use:** Exhaustion = that gap no longer supports the short idea; look at the next higher inefficiency or stand aside.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m ~10:18 ET: "this entire gap closed in here, the bodies are stopping, that one little Mohawk outside... I'm viewing that as an exhaustion; press press press press press, high, trade below the gap I have interest".
- **Timestamps:** [0:43:05](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2585) don't want it to trade up to there, exhaustion gap · [0:44:42](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2682) one gap exhaustion, other stays open in upper half · [0:54:08](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3248) entire gap closed with bodies, viewed as exhaustion
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Audio is ambiguous about which of the two gaps he calls exhaustion at each moment (caption unclear); treat as: gap fully traded through by bodies = exhaustion.

### Opening range, 9:30 opening price as fair value, and the gap-open magnet  (aliases: "opening range gap high", "opening range high", "new day opening gap", "RTH opening gap")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** The opening range is the first 30 minutes, 9:30–10:00 ET (set the chart to New York time so what you learn matches the channel). The 9:30 open price is annotated; on a gap-up open it forms the "opening range gap high" (top edge of the gap between the prior RTH close/settlement and the open) and the opening range high. Many times, even when price runs straight away from the open, it comes back down to touch the opening price, and then either continues higher later or goes lower into the close. "Fair value for the sake of fair value" is that 9:30 open price. A large premium opening does not always mean a complete return to the prior settlement, but watch for signs it will try. He also wants price to be traded back through; touching alone is not enough for him ("I want a little bit more than that").
- **How to identify:** Drop a line at the RTH close (4:00 pm settlement) and at the 9:30 open price (he preps a horizontal line near market before 9:30 and sets it to the actual open once it prints).
- **How to use:** Use the opening price as the first target for a short from a premium; the prior settlement as the deeper magnet; lunch/afternoon as the time for a delayed return if it does not happen before 1:00 pm.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, open at "352" (partial digits; e.g., the opening price line adjusted from "348" to "352" once the open printed); price spent the first hour above it. His expectation for the afternoon: "I believe we're going to see that again today" (return to this gap and the opening price level).
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:46](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=226) annotating the 9:30 open · [0:04:36](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=276) opening range is 9:30–10:00, set platform to NY time · [0:34:51](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2091) price often comes back to touch the opening range high = opening price · [0:39:31](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2371) fair value is the 9:30 opening price · [1:02:30](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3750) expect it in the afternoon if not before 1:00
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Calling the 9:30 price the "opening range high" is his usage on a gap-up open day; don't over-generalize to days with no gap.

### Buy-side liquidity run → immediate delivery (example screenshot for the log)  (aliases: "bread and butter set up", "heat on the FVG")
- **Kind:** example only / process
- **What ICT says:** Early in the session a small gap was traded back into (a volume imbalance between two bodies after the 8:30 pop, plus relative equal lows), which then ran quickly to the pool of liquidity above (relative equal highs). He calls this one of the really easy bread-and-butter setups: a pool of liquidity is visible, price reaches into a gap/breaker and runs to it. You don't need the candle to close for the FVG to respond. A "low–high–low" structure there makes the move a breaker/FVG entry. For Caleb's journal screenshot, annotate: how long it took (about 1 minute), how much "heat" was on the FVG (zero drawdown), and the immediate run to liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Relative equal highs/lows visible; small gap or volume imbalance; low-high-low into breaker.
- **How to use:** Mark the entry PD array, log elapsed time and drawdown ("heat") to the target. Annotate as a screenshot, don't trade it unless it is in the model's time window.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, about 9:31–9:35 ET: price dips into a small gap after the open, then runs above the relative equal highs (buy-side) at the 8:30 high; ICT notes this as a first screenshot with 0 heat and ~1 minute duration.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=423) small gap engaged, easy bread-and-butter setup · [0:09:37](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=577) low-high-low breaker acts as FVG; need not close · [0:10:30](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=630) annotate time taken and heat on the FVG: zero
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Heat" = adverse excursion into/through the entry PD array before the delivery; useful metric when studying setups.

### SMT divergence: Dow is the unreliable leg; prefer ES vs NQ  (aliases: "mixed market", "no participation")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** At the high, NQ made a higher high while YM (Dow) did not, and ES was lagging (checked at 9:50). ICT does not argue SMT on the Dow alone but notes the Dow is "usually the first indication of things starting to crack"; the Dow tends to be lethargic and then suddenly runs, cancelling an SMT, so he prefers to see divergence on ES or NQ against each other. If ES makes a higher high, market breadth/strength is there and NQ should do the same; if NQ doesn't, he won't chase continuation for the session. Specifically: if ES lags, either NQ stays at its highs and ES catches up (so the NQ high gets taken), or NQ breaks down and ES fails to reach the high, in which case he likes the ES gap below.
- **How to identify:** Compare swing highs across NQ/ES/YM at the same time; he wants ES or NQ to diverge. If all three are not in agreement, it is a "mixed market".
- **How to use:** A single index not confirming = reason not to buy; ES not making a high while NQ does and sells = better short signal. He said if the S&P (ES) had been the one lagging, it would have been "really problematic" to be a buyer.
- **Example in this video:** About 9:50 ET: ES lagging NQ; around 10:02–10:05: NQ higher high vs Dow not confirming = "we could be forming an intermediate-term high for Nasdaq".
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:14](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1574) ES lagging NQ, what it implies · [0:37:50](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2270) Dow is another reason not to buy · [0:38:39](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2319) possible intermediate-term high for NQ · [1:03:18](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3798) Dow usually the one that doesn't listen; prefer ES/NQ for SMT

### Stop run overshoot before the reversal  (aliases: "run the high", "10 to 20 handles", "one more gun")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Before the retracement he expects one more run above the high that is large enough to engage stops and trap: not "a tick or two", but "10, 15 handles, maybe 20 handles above it", then peter out, especially when ES is weaker. This gives the "street money" comfort that they were right so they raise stops under the new low (second pool of liquidity) that gets run on the way down.
- **How to identify:** Clean old high/relative equal highs sitting just above; an SMT divergence with ES/YM.
- **How to use:** Don't short the initial poke; wait for the sweep, then the inversion of a gap below/MSS-type confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m about 10:23–10:28 ET: price expected to sweep the high, "it should do it now because we had a gap, it overshot, it should spring up there and clear that high", and does pop on the next candle ("there it is, anyone short here has been canceled out").
- **Timestamps:** [0:58:23](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3503) bump the high once more to give comfort · [0:59:10](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3550) second pool of liquidity · [1:01:43](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3703) 10–20 handles above the high then peter out · [1:04:06](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3846) it pops, shorts canceled
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The handle range is explicitly his rough preference, not a fixed rule.

### Silver Bullet (10:00 hour) and lunch macro as fallback windows  (aliases: "10 o'clock Silver Bullet", "11:00–11:30 lunch macro")
- **Kind:** rule / time
- **What ICT says:** The 10 o'clock Silver Bullet is the main focus for the day; "it's always there, it's never not in the charts". Don't trade the early session (opening range) because it's a time you can get caught offside. If the idea doesn't materialize ("no touch, no trade"), wait until around 11:00–11:30 and fade whatever the market has done until then, using it as a "lunch macro". If the retracement does not happen before 1:00 pm, he expects it in the afternoon.
- **How to identify:** Clock based (10:00–11:00 ET window; lunch macro ~11:00–11:30 ET).
- **How to use:** Stand aside during the first 30 minutes, reassess at 10:00 with the pre-defined levels.
- **Example in this video:** He stays flat all morning; afternoon expectation is a return to the gap and the opening price.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:01](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=781) 10 o'clock Silver Bullet is always there · [0:28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683) 10:00 Silver Bullet main focus · [0:47:09](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2829) wait until 11:00–11:30, lunch macro, fade the run
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Here the lunch macro is used as an 11:00–11:30 fade window on a trend day, not a time-based entry model.

### Election-year / "built-in buy model" bias note  (aliases: seasonal tendency, "election years")
- **Kind:** context (opinion)
- **What ICT says:** In election years the stock market tends to be pumped to flatter the incumbent; markets are also meant to go up over time, so it is easier to go higher than lower and shorting every wobble gets you chopped up. That is why he adopted models where certain things must trade first (e.g., a sell-side engagement) before he takes a short.
- **How to use:** Don't fade strength without the model's criteria; accept it can keep going.
- **Timestamps:** [0:28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683) election-year pump tendency · [0:28:54](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1734) why models require sequence before shorting

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET; use New York time on the chart.
- Do not buy premium after a gap-up open plus 8:30 run; "it has to make sense or I'm not doing it".
- Stand aside during the early session; main focus is the 10:00 Silver Bullet; fallback fade window 11:00–11:30; if no retracement by 1:00 pm, expect it in the afternoon.
- iFVG validity: wick above OK, bodies inside the gap; upper half of the gap left open; a full roll-over above = no bias, no trade.
- A close below the CE point of the key gap/wick indicates a roll-over back into the opening range.
- Expect the final stop run to exceed the high by roughly 10–20 handles, not 1–2 ticks.
- Prefer SMT on ES vs NQ; Dow cancels SMT often; if indices are not in agreement, treat as mixed.
- First-screenshot metrics for the journal: time to deliver (about 1 minute) and "heat" on the FVG (0).
- Future stream schedule: start about 9:15 ET and end by about 11:00 ET, shorter and with less talking (after August).

## Glossary candidates
`Opening range gap high` — top of the gap between prior RTH close and the 9:30 open (the open price on a gap-up day), used as the first return target.
`Heat (on an FVG)` — adverse excursion into/through the entry gap before price delivers to the target; he logs it as part of a setup screenshot.
`Street money / dumb money` — retail participants who buy momentum and short weakness and chase price.
`Exhaustion gap` — a gap fully traded through with bodies and left behind; no longer useful as an inversion level.
`Handles` — NQ points (e.g., "10, 15, 20 handles above the high").
`Mixed market` — indices not agreeing on a high/low (e.g., NQ new high, YM and ES not), reducing his willingness to chase.
`Low–high–low breaker` — breaker structure he mentions as an entry PD array that can behave like an FVG (caption somewhat unclear).
`Institutional order flow entry drill (breakaway gap)` — older ICT entry pattern name used for the 8:30 gap and bull flag (caption unclear).
