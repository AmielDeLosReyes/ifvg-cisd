## Summary
A live "walk forward" session on Thursday Aug 8, 2024 (NQ, 8:30 ET employment data) that applies the previous day's lecture on new day / new week opening gaps (NDOG/NWOG). ICT shows how to label opening gaps cleanly (high, low, consequent encroachment, quadrants), and the core bias rule: right before 8:30, look at which side of price the cluster of the last five NDOGs and the NWOG sit on — price will most likely press into that side, even if those gaps were already traded into. He then drills down to 15-second and 1-second charts to show the 8:30 "spooling" run (~100 handles in 15 seconds), how volume imbalances, small FVGs, BPRs and order blocks inside the opening-gap levels act as entry points and stop references, and a hypothetical OB/volume-imbalance long targeting the CE of an FVG above equilibrium (~70–80 handles). Large portions are about the beginner process: observe and journal price without demo trading for weeks/months, use a hard stop, hide P&L, breathing and self-talk.

## Concepts

### New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) — derivation & labeling  (aliases: new day opening gap, NDOG)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The NDOG for a trading day is formed the evening before: the gap between the 5:00 PM ET close (start of the one-hour break) and the 6:00 PM ET open. E.g. Tuesday's NDOG is created Monday at 6 PM; Wednesday's NDOG is created Tuesday at 6 PM.
  - If the 6 PM open is above the 5 PM close it is an up gap: the 6 PM open is the NDOG high and the 5 PM close is the low (and vice versa for a gap down).
  - Put a Fibonacci on the gap's high and low; the 0.5 level is the consequent encroachment (CE / midpoint).
  - Add 0.25 and 0.75 to your Fib settings to get the quadrant levels.
  - Quadrants are only worth plotting when the range is large. If the NDOG is small (he gives "10 or 15 handles, something less than 20 handles" on NQ) just eyeball the midpoint — the difference between entry and stop is too small to matter.
  - Example of a "significant" range: ~66 handles (caption reads "18,99 to 18,33"; exact prices unclear) — he would plot quadrants.
- **How to identify:** 5 PM ET close vs 6 PM ET open of the prior evening.
- **How to use:** reference levels (high, low, CE, quadrants) for where price gravitates, where bodies stop, where wicks overshoot. See "Opening-gap clustering bias" below.
- **Example in this video:** Before 8:30 on Aug 8, NQ was trading just below Wednesday's NDOG low and above today's (Thursday's) NDOG high — "in between", i.e. no-man's land.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:27](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=507) — price sitting between Wednesday's NDOG low and today's NDOG high; [0:13:17](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=797) — 5 PM close / 6 PM open derivation; [0:14:06](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=846) — CE via Fib, quadrants only for ranges > ~20 handles; [0:15:40](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=940) — add 0.25/0.75 to Fib settings.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Continues Lecture #3 (Aug 7) teaching — he tells viewers to go back and watch that one.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) — labeling  (aliases: new week opening gap, NWOG)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Measure from Friday's close to Sunday's 6 PM ET open; 50% = CE. For a significant NWOG, also plot the quadrants. To keep the chart clean, he measures with a Fib, then replaces it with a single horizontal line typed at the exact price (e.g. NWOG CE ~18,451.75 — caption also says "441.75", unclear) and removes the Fib.
- **How to identify:** Friday close vs Sunday 6 PM open.
- **How to use:** Same as NDOG — one of the clustered reference points that tells you where price will reach.
- **Example in this video:** Early in the week price spiked into the NWOG with wicks ("wicks do the damage"), hit the bottom of the NWOG and accelerated lower into Tuesday's NDOG CE, then Wednesday's NDOG with bodies respecting it, then CE again. On Aug 8 the NWOG was far above price — "it's going to take a real move to get up there".
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:30](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=990) — NWOG measured from Friday close to Sunday open, quadrants; [0:18:54](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1134) — review of price respecting NWOG / NDOG levels earlier in the week.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Keep the **last five NDOGs** and the **last five NWOGs** on your reference layout.

### Opening-gap clustering bias (which side will price press into at 8:30)  (aliases: stacking / clustering of new day opening gaps)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 8:00 ET look for relative equal highs and relative equal lows into 8:30.
  - At 8:30 (with or without news) ask: where is the stacking/clustering of the NDOGs and the NWOG — above or below the current price? Price is most likely to press into the side that has the inefficiencies, **even if they have already been traded to / "filled"**.
  - Gaps are not "used up" once touched — "the algorithm ... calls back to these points of reference based on time and price." They have a "life cycle" of sensitivity.
  - He estimates the direction is right "80% or higher" initially (explicitly "not 100%").
  - Retail breakout traders will try to buy above / sell below the nearby highs/lows — you wait, because between two gaps neither direction is more likely until the 8:30 move.
- **How to identify:** (1) Price location just before 8:30; (2) count NDOGs/NWOG above vs below; (3) context: has price already dropped into a deep discount of the recent range? (4) where have the profitable positions been (the side that has been winning recently is the side that will be squeezed).
- **How to use:** Defines the draw for the 8:30 run and subsequent scalps. If price had dropped into a clean FVG just before 8:30 (e.g. at 8:25), you could have bought into the news anticipating the run; without such a PD array, don't gamble — wait and drop to a 15-second chart to trade the inefficiencies/first order block that support the idea.
- **Example in this video:** Aug 8, NQ: just before 8:30 price was below Wednesday's NDOG and above Thursday's NDOG; Tuesday's NDOG, Wednesday's NDOG and the NWOG all sat above. Price had already dropped into a deep discount of the range from the low up to Wednesday's high, taken relative equal lows inside a BISI, and bounced off the upper quadrant of that BISI. At 8:30 it rallied up into Wednesday's NDOG low, CE, and high, then Tuesday's NDOG — as forecast the day before.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:33](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1233) — clustering is above, so bias is up; [0:26:23](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1583) — 8:00 relative equal highs/lows, 8:30 clustering; [0:27:10](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1630) — gaps already traded through still matter; [0:33:38](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2018) — same signature without news, ~80%+.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Direct application of Lecture #3. Contrasts with the "gap is filled, forget it" view of everyone else in the industry.

### 8:30 spooling / algorithmic repricing  (aliases: spooling, the market spools, buy program / sell program)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 8:30 ET the algorithm starts "spooling" (running) regardless of news; he never looks at the actual report numbers (CPI, PMI, NFP) — "it's all baked in."
  - Medium/high-impact news "loosens it up" so price can reach further outside the normal band of volatility; the magnitude and speed are bigger. No news → price still moves toward the same levels, just less energetically.
  - Spooling analogy: like fishing line shooting off a reel when cast. When the algorithm enters a buy program it spools higher; a sell program, lower.
  - The big 8:30 run is repricing, not buying pressure — check time & sales / volume: few contracts traded during the run. Historical analogy: open-outcry floor traders "pairing orders" (one contract needed to book a price) to stair-step price.
  - Other scheduled times where price spools: 8:30, 10:00, sometimes 9:45, 10:30 (crude oil inventories), plus macros.
- **How to identify:** Economic calendar (Forex Factory is cleaner; he prefers Econoday — more information, free). The calendar is your "TV guide" for when to watch.
- **How to use:** The 8:30 initial run is essentially untradeable unless already positioned before it; the draw is determined by where price is relative to the gap clusters right before the release.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-second chart at 8:30: from 17,986.75 to roughly 18,08x (caption unclear) — "100 handles in just like 15 seconds". On the 1-second chart, the first one-second candle went from 17,986.75 to ~18,037.75 (caption unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:16](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=556) — algorithm spools at 8:30 even without news; [0:36:13](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2173) — 15-sec chart ~100 handles in 15 seconds; [1:19:56](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4796) — fishing reel analogy, buy/sell programs; [1:56:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=6964) — 1-second chart of the 8:30 candle.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Warns funded-account traders using 5–15 contracts on a ~$3,000 max loss that gambling these runs guarantees blowing out.

### Macros (algorithmic time windows)  (aliases: macro)
- **Kind:** rule (restated)
- **What ICT says:** A macro is the last 10 minutes before the top of the hour and the first 10 minutes after the top of the hour (i.e. xx:50–xx:10). The market spools there too, running for liquidity or rushing into an inefficiency. He prefers to trade a bullish MSS setup inside a macro.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1384) — trade inside a macro; [1:21:32](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4892) — macros spool like news times.

### Breaker with an FVG inside (and the potential inversion FVG)  (aliases: breaker, bearish breaker, inversion fair value gap)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** A (bearish) breaker = high, low, then a higher high that blows out the buy-side liquidity. If there is a fair value gap inside the breaker's leg, focus on that FVG rather than only the down-close candles that formed the last turn. That FVG becomes a potential inversion FVG — highlight it and extend it to the right as a PD array price may draw up to.
- **How to identify:** High → low → higher high (BSL run) → FVG within that structure.
- **How to use:** Upside target/draw; area where price can engage/reverse.
- **Example in this video:** Above the FVG he chose (which sat above equilibrium of the range), there was an FVG inside a breaker; he flagged it as the next upside target if price went higher. Later, price did reach it ("there's that Gap I told you about, high, low, higher high").
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:02](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1922) — breaker + FVG rule; [1:21:32](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4892) — inversion FVG above breaker as upside target; [2:06:34](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7594) — reached.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He marks FVGs he views as inversions in orange. A BISI that price has displaced back down through is NOT a buy when retested — "that's not a buy" — just because it was originally a bullish gap.

### Fibonacci = equilibrium only; premium/discount of the most recent range  (aliases: equilibrium, 50%, premium, discount)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** His only use of the Fibonacci is to find the midpoint — that tells him overbought (premium) vs oversold (discount); no indicator needed, and divergence on any indicator is "useless". Measure the most recent large directional range (here the high down to the low right at 8:30). Ask who is holding profit — in a protracted down move it's the shorts, so the pain will go to the shorts. At or below 50% = discount; at or above 50% = premium.
- **How to use:** Choose the FVG target that is above equilibrium (for a bullish draw) as the objective.
- **Example in this video:** He measured the high (Wednesday area) to the low before 8:30; price at 8:30 was in deep discount; the FVG he chose as target sat above equilibrium and inside the breaker.
- **Timestamps:** [1:15:55](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4555) — Fib only for midpoint; [1:16:43](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4603) — who is profitable in the recent range; [1:17:30](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4650) — premium/discount.

### Fair Value Gap — definition, bodies vs wicks, CE  (aliases: SIBI, BISI, sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency, buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - FVG = the space between candle 1's low and candle 3's high (bearish case) on a single pass of price; with sell-side delivery the inefficiency is on the buy side (SIBI). It remains an inefficiency until price trades back into it.
  - "Consequent encroachment" = the midpoint; it's "consequential" that price encroaches into it when it returns.
  - Bodies respect the levels; "the wicks do the damage".
  - The extreme of how far a wick may go against you: look left — a volume imbalance or short-term high nearby; expect at most a tick or so beyond it.
- **How to use:** Bearish example: price returns into a SIBI, wicks up into a nearby volume imbalance (one tick above), fails to reach CE, next candle dives → that's your "institutional order flow entry drill" (sell). Stop above the FVG/wick extreme. Partial at the next level (e.g. Tuesday's NDOG low). If the gap is left open (not refilled) it is a breakaway gap; price likely stays heavy and could offer 15–20+ handles.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-sec chart, after the run up to the FVG CE target (~…250): price couldn't hold at CE (resistance), broke below a low = market structure shift, retraced up into a short-term premium SIBI with bodies respecting it and wicks reaching a small volume imbalance, then traded down to the low of Tuesday's NDOG.
- **Timestamps:** [1:15:06](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4506) — FVG definition; [1:27:12](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5232) — CE as resistance, MSS, retrace to short-term premium; [1:31:15](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5475) — CE meaning, bodies; [1:32:03](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5523) — wick extreme, entry drill, breakaway gap, partial.

### Reclaimed fair value gap  (aliases: reclaimed FVG)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** If you miss the first move away from an FVG, you can trade it when price returns into it again — the market is "offering that again", reclaiming the FVG. A small volume imbalance next to/inside the FVG tells you how far the wick can go. If bodies stay inside the FVG and the wick only reaches the volume imbalance, the setup is still viable.
- **Timestamps:** [1:29:36](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5376) — takeaway on FVG + volume imbalance + reclaimed FVG; [1:30:26](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5426) — bodies staying inside the FVG.

### Volume imbalance & overlapping bodies as PD arrays on the 15-second chart  (aliases: volume imbalance, "one of the PD arrays I supposedly don't have")
- **Kind:** example / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Where two consecutive candle bodies overlap/agree (top of one, low of the next), that level is a PD array. After the 8:30 run, a volume imbalance inside a BISI should hold: price should form support or be unable to trade to the BISI midpoint — or if it does, it must repel immediately. Every little down-close candle must then support price (acting as a bullish order block) until price takes out the next level.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-sec chart after 8:30: price pulled back to Wednesday's NDOG CE; the open of one candle and the low of the next matched exactly (caption "18,81.5", unclear), then a rally leaving a BISI with a small volume imbalance; price traded only to "some random quadrant level", left the volume imbalance open, rallied to the high of Tuesday's NDOG, then pulled back into down-close candles just outside Tuesday's NDOG low. He said if that fails below the candle's low, stand aside until the 9:30 open.
- **Timestamps:** [0:38:37](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2317) — overlapping bodies PD array; [0:39:28](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2368) — volume imbalance inside BISI, must hold; [0:40:19](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2419) — down-close candles must support; stand aside if it fails.

### Order block + volume imbalance + NDOG confluence entry (hypothetical long)  (aliases: bullish order block, rejection block)
- **Kind:** entry model (example)
- **What ICT says:** On a 15-second chart you can use order blocks, small FVGs and volume imbalances as entries as long as the direction is still in play. Any convergence of an NDOG high/low/CE with a down-close candle while bullish → use it as the catalyst to study whether price runs to your objective.
- **How to use:**
  - Entry: the opening price of the order block (down-close candle) / the volume imbalance inside it ("worst case" fill).
  - Stop: at the rejection block — the close of that candle; he expected at most a one-tick wick below because the bodies had already filled the volume imbalance. This is the logic behind his tight stops.
  - Target: CE of the FVG above equilibrium.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15-sec, after ~9:00 ET: price came down, touched the top of Tuesday's NDOG high (two PD arrays — volume imbalance + NDOG high — inside a down-close order block). Hypothetical entry ~…173, target CE ~…250, high reached ~…252 → roughly 70–80 handles.
- **Timestamps:** [1:03:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=3784) — volume imbalance + Tuesday NDOG high inside order block; [1:08:37](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4117) — entry and stop logic (rejection block); [1:12:37](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4357) — 173 → 250/252; [1:23:59](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5039) — convergence of NDOG level + down-close candle as catalyst.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Done after the "major damage" of 8:30 was over; price had already pressed one-sided, so he said he'd wait rather than chase a ~100-handle run. Don't read stalling as reversal/bearish divergence — it is drawing to inefficiencies.

### Balanced Price Range (BPR)  (aliases: balanced price range, balanced price delivery)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:** When price has been offered both up and down through the same range (e.g. between one candle's low and another's high), it is balanced. Analogy: painting a wall with a roller — once an area is painted (balanced), you don't need to go deep into it again; price only returns to the edge. Use quadrants of the BPR to study reactions.
- **Example in this video:** 15-sec chart: a BPR formed from a low that was also Wednesday's NDOG CE up to a high; price later traded back to the up-close closing price/open and rallied. On the 1-second chart, price dropped into a BPR around Wednesday's NDOG CE and didn't go deeper.
- **Timestamps:** [1:23:10](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4990) — BPR on 15-sec chart; [1:56:54](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7014) — FVG at Wednesday NDOG CE and BPR; [1:57:50](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7070) — paint-roller analogy, quadrants.

### Support & resistance critique / old highs as targets  (aliases: liquidity above old highs)
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:** Classical S/R (John Murphy etc.) shows what retail will try to do, which is useful only because it tells you where their stops are. If an old high is resistance, why does price go back to it? To go above it, because shorts' stops are there. Treat old highs/lows as levels "to be attacked". The real S/R is the opening gaps and inefficiencies (NDOG, NWOG, "Event Horizon" — things outside the candles "like dark pools").
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:43](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1483) — S/R too myopic; [0:55:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=3304) — "we're trading old reference points ... to engage and liquidate traders"; [1:26:25](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5185) — outside-the-candles levels; [2:01:48](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7308) — attack the levels where orders sit.

### Walking forward / observation drills (no demo)  (aliases: forward testing, tape reading)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:** Lectures 1–3 covered back-testing; this lecture and Friday's are about walking forward. ## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- He never looks at the actual numbers of news reports (CPI, PMI, NFP); only the time.
- NDOG = 5:00 PM ET close vs 6:00 PM ET open the prior evening; NWOG = Friday close vs Sunday 6 PM open.
- CE = 0.5 of the gap; quadrants = 0.25 and 0.75 (add to Fib settings). Plot quadrants only for large gaps; eyeball the midpoint if the gap is under ~20 NQ handles (e.g. 10–15).
- Keep the last 5 NDOGs and the last 5 NWOGs.
- 8:00 ET: look for relative equal highs/lows; 8:30 ET: look for where NDOGs/NWOG cluster relative to price — price presses into that side (~80%+ in his estimate).
- Gaps remain relevant after being traded into.
- If price expands up hard before 9:30 → wait for the 10:00 Silver Bullet.
- Macro = last 10 minutes before and first 10 minutes after the top of the hour.
- Scheduled spool times: 8:30, 9:45 (sometimes), 10:00, 10:30 (crude oil inventories).
- If the post-8:30 support (down-close candles/volume imbalance) fails below the candle low → stand aside until the 9:30 open.
- Stop for OB entry: at the rejection block (candle close); expect at most ~1 tick of wick beyond a filled volume imbalance.
- A bullish FVG that price has displaced back down through is not a buy on retest.
- Fib is used only for the 50% equilibrium; indicator divergence is useless.
- SMT is a qualifier, not a requirement.
- First 30 minutes after 9:30 = opening range; it can retrace deeply and fake out faders.
- Homework: for the post-8:30 price action, estimate the low-end amount you could reasonably have taken (25 handles? one 10-handle trade with little drawdown? 50 handles?) — find your ideal model (could be 15–20 handles/day, or three 25-handle runs for 75).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Build trust in the concepts by observing for weeks/months without pressing a button; "don't take my word for it — test it" ([0:29:37](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1777)).

## Glossary candidates
`Spooling` — the algorithm rapidly repricing in one direction (buy program / sell program) at key times like 8:30 or in macros.
`Opening-gap clustering` — several NDOGs/NWOG stacked on one side of price; that side is the likely draw.
`Reclaimed FVG` — an FVG price returns to a second time and that still holds (bodies stay inside); a second chance to enter.
`Institutional order flow entry drill` — entry on a return into an FVG that fails to reach CE, with the next candle diving away.
`Breakaway gap` — an FVG left open (not refilled) after the entry drill; price likely continues.
`Rejection block` — used here as the stop reference: the close of the order-block candle.
`Matrix` — ICT's layout of 5/4/3/2/1-minute charts for NQ, ES and YM to spot SMT.
`Event Horizon` — mentioned as a level "outside the candles" alongside NDOG/NWOG (not explained here).
`Walk forward` — real-time observation/logging without trading, as opposed to back-testing.
