## Summary
Episode 9 teaches Power of Three (accumulation, manipulation, distribution) and New York PM session opportunities, using NQ on 14 and 15 Feb 2022. ICT defines the bullish daily profile: open near the low, a false move lower (Judas swing), a rally, a close near the high. He uses the 8:30 opening price as the reference, buying below it in the presence of imbalance, order block and OTE. He gives a precise order block definition: consecutive down-close candles right before a displacement that leaves an imbalance. The most important rule: when equity index futures make a big overnight run, don't chase it and avoid the NY AM session. Wait until after New York lunch (after 1:00 p.m.) for a raid on the lunch or morning lows into a FVG. He walks through a 1m PM long (2:00–2:34 p.m.) with entry, stop, contract math and heat, and explains why a wick through a FVG doesn't invalidate it.

## Concepts

### Daily dealing range: discount plus an indecisive daily candle means expect a retracement
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Choose the daily range: the most recent swing low (which took out a short-term low, digging into SSL) and the swing high. Fib it only for the 50% (equilibrium). Below 50% is discount.
  - On Monday 14 Feb 2022 NQ traded into a deep discount but didn't take the lows further left. Monday closed with an indecisive candle, the body "basically absent" (open ≈ close).
  - In discount with an indecisive close, a retracement may be needed first, even if a lower low is the eventual target.
  - Monday's close left a daily imbalance (FVG). Overnight into Tuesday, price ran up into it. The FVG's lower and upper edges became reference levels on the lower timeframes.
- **Example in this video:** NQ continuous (TradingView). Mon 14 Feb: deep discount, doji-like close, daily FVG. Tue 15 Feb: overnight run into the FVG; the upper end of the FVG was the draw.

### Power of Three (AMD)  (aliases ICT uses: Power 3, accumulation manipulation distribution)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bullish day: the opening price is near the low of the day or session. Price trades lower to make an important low (manipulation), then rallies to create the high and closes near the high.
  - You don't need to predict the close. The aim is to anticipate the fake move at the start.
  - If bullish, the small move lower after the open is what you hunt for a long. If you miss it, try to get long as close to the opening price as possible.
  - Which opening price? He likes 8:30 a.m. ET. Draw it forward in time.
- **How to identify (bullish, checklist he runs at the 8:30 open):** Did price go below the 8:30 opening price? Did it trade into the imbalance? Did it take a short-term low? Did it hit an order block? Was it an optimal trade entry? Each yes adds confluence.
- **How to use:** Buy below the opening price during the manipulation leg, with a target toward the day's draw (e.g. the high end of the daily FVG or relative equal highs).
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Mon 14 Feb 2022. Price was below the 8:30 open, inside the pre-8:30 imbalance, took a short-term low, hit an order block, at OTE: a long. In the afternoon price traded below the opening price again, then rallied and took the relative equal highs toward the upper end of the daily FVG.

### Order block (precise definition)  (aliases ICT uses: bullish order block, complete order block)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Consecutive down-close candles right before a price surge that has an imbalance": that's how you find order blocks.
  - A high-probability order block: your narrative or bias is bullish, you see displacement (price runs quickly higher), and you mark the down-close candles before it and anticipate a return to them.
  - In the 5m example, the imbalance's lowest portion contained 4 consecutive down-close candles, a "complete order block on this time frame", anchored to the daily bullish order block.
  - The best entry combines all three: FVG plus order block plus OTE.
- **How to identify:** a run of consecutive down-close candles, then displacement up leaving a FVG. Mark the down-close candles' range.
- **How to use:** In a bullish narrative, wait for the retracement back into the order block (ideally overlapping the FVG and OTE) and buy.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5m, 14 Feb 2022. After the high formed, price broke down and retraced into the imbalance (the FVG between one candle's low and another's high) that had the 4 down-close candles of the order block. It rallied, sloppily but continuously higher, into the close.

### Don't chase overnight runs; after a big overnight run, avoid the NY AM and trade after lunch
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When there's a big overnight move (he cites London, 2:00–5:00 a.m.) before New York, don't chase it at 8:30 just because it went up overnight. Wait for more information.
  - A consolidation typically follows a big run. Not always: sometimes it keeps running and you'll miss it.
  - With a big overnight range, don't expect much in-and-out precision. Wait for a significant move or you'll get chopped up.
  - **Equity index futures only (NQ, YM, ES), not Forex:** if there's a big overnight run, avoid the New York AM session entirely. Wait for the other side of lunch (after 1:00 p.m. NY time) and anticipate a raid on the New York lunch lows or the New York morning-session lows.
  - New York lunch = noon to 1:00 p.m. NY local time.
  - Everyone wakes up wanting to chase. That leads to losing trades and a chase cycle, and in choppy sideways conditions it's "like a blowtorch on your account".
- **How to use:** After the overnight expansion, mark the AM consolidation lows (especially relative equal lows). In the PM, wait for them to be run into a FVG, then a bullish MSS and FVG entry.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Tue 15 Feb 2022. A huge overnight (London) rally ran into the daily FVG. ICT says he would have missed it and it surprised him. The AM was sloppy and back-and-forth, leaving relative equal lows. Relative equal highs formed before 7:00 a.m. weren't taken. BSL sat around 14,575 and the draw was the upper boundary of the daily FVG. In the PM, NQ dropped, took the sell side and dug into a FVG, with candle bodies respecting the level, then rallied.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:35](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=635) — 15 Feb overnight run, wouldn't have caught it · [0:12:08](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=728) — don't chase; consolidation follows; big overnight range means don't trade much · [0:15:22](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=922) — the rule: avoid the NY session, wait until after 1:00 p.m. · [0:24:16](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1456) — why: everyone chases in the morning

### Engineering liquidity (relative equal lows as stop pools)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Back-and-forth AM price action builds "suspect" relative equal lows with sell stops below them. Anyone long overnight will "jam their stop loss right underneath" once price starts rising.
  - It's even better when the high above hasn't been taken yet.
  - Price runs up, consolidates, creates a low, starts rallying; retail moves stops under that low. That's the setup he wants, the liquidity he expects to be run.
  - After the overnight stops have been run, the market doesn't need to go back down there. It gets "sneakier" and won't give stopped-out traders another chance at the same price.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:33](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=873) — relative equal lows, overnight longs' stops · [0:17:45](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1065) — "that's engineering liquidity" · [0:19:21](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1161) — post-lunch, they won't let stopped traders back in

### PM entry model: lunch/AM lows raided, MSS, FVG (with missed-entry limit at the short-term low)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - From 2:00 p.m. NY time, price trades down into an imbalance and takes the liquidity below relative equal lows (post-lunch), then rallies.
  - Checklist: a swing high is broken. Was it energetic? Yes. Did it leave a FVG? Yes. Price trades back down into the FVG and creates a short-term low. You can buy there.
  - If you missed it and price runs off, you may get a second chance when price comes back to that short-term low, which has sell-side liquidity resting below it. Buy at that low or 1 tick below it on a limit.
  - Stop: at the short-term low below, formed after the FVG (exact reference caption unclear). "That's the rules."
- **How to identify:** PM session (after lunch), a raid on SSL (relative equal lows), an energetic break of a swing high with a FVG, a retrace into the FVG.
- **How to use:** Limit buy at the FVG, or at the short-term low or 1 tick below. Stop below the swing low. Target the BSL or daily FVG high above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, 15 Feb 2022:
  - The short-term low formed at 2:34 p.m. with a low of 14,528.5. The suggested limit was 14,528.25 (or 14,528 even).
  - Risk about 14.25 handles. With 6 micros he says about $85 risked to make about $300, roughly 3.5:1.
  - Heat after entry: about 9 points (he says $54 on 6 micros). The stop was not hit, with about 5 handles to spare.
  - One mini = $20 per handle.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:33](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1113) — PM narrative: stops raided into FVG, rally · [0:20:09](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1209) — swing high broken, energetic, FVG, missed-entry case · [0:20:59](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1259) — limit at the short-term low or −1 tick; 2:34 p.m.; 14,528.5 · [0:21:49](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1309) — stop rule, 14.25 handles risk, 6 micros, about 3.5R

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Daily range: swing high to swing low; Fib only for 50% (equilibrium); below 50% is discount.
- Indecisive daily close in discount: expect a retracement (e.g. into the daily FVG) before continuation.
- Power of Three, bullish: open near the low, false move down, rally, close near the high. Hunt longs below the opening price.
- Opening price reference: 8:30 a.m. ET.
- 8:30 long checklist: below the open? Inside the imbalance? Short-term low taken? Order block hit? OTE?
- Order block = consecutive down-close candles right before displacement that leaves an imbalance.
- Practical close for judging the day's range: 4:30 p.m. ET (bell at 4:00 p.m.).
- Overnight run window referenced: 2:00–5:00 a.m. ET.
- Indices only: a big overnight run means avoiding the NY AM session. Trade after 1:00 p.m. ET, targeting a raid on NY lunch or AM lows.
- NY lunch: 12:00–1:00 p.m. ET.
- PM setup example: 2:00 p.m. raid; short-term low at 2:34 p.m.; limit at the low or 1 tick below.
- Stop below the short-term swing low ("that's the rules"). Example risk 14.25 handles, about 3.5R.
- NQ mini = $20 per handle.
- A wick through a FVG doesn't invalidate it if bodies respect it.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Don't chase. Chasing big overnight moves begins a losing cycle, and choppy conditions burn accounts. ([0:24:16](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1456))
- Sloppy, choppy ranges aren't high probability. If you're a high-frequency trader, reduce frequency and wait for the better setups. ([0:16:56](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1016), [0:17:45](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1065))
- Missing a big overnight move doesn't matter. It gives insight: it tells you to avoid the morning. ([0:23:27](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1407))
- Expect heat. Don't expect entries at the exact low with no drawdown. ([0:22:36](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1356))
- Trust setups and let stops do their job. One loss isn't your career. ([0:25:52](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1552))
- Missed entries happen (phone, grabbing a drink). Look for the second opportunity at the short-term low. ([0:20:09](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1209))

## Glossary candidates
`Power of Three (Power 3)` — accumulation, manipulation, distribution: open, false move against the bias, expansion with the bias, close near the extreme.
`Judas swing` — the false opening move of a session (London or NY) that runs against the day's real direction.
`Pseudo Judas swing` — an initial move against bias that chops instead of reversing cleanly.
`Complete order block` — the full run of consecutive down-close (or up-close) candles before a displacement with imbalance.
`Engineering liquidity` — price action (consolidation, relative equal lows) that invites retail stops to cluster, to be run later.
`NY lunch` — 12:00–1:00 p.m. ET.
`Heat` — adverse excursion against an open position before it works.
