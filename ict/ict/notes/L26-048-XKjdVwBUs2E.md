## Summary
ICT shows how a daily inefficiency from May 5, 2026 (a bullish buy-side-imbalance/sell-side-inefficiency, i.e. BISI, with a volume imbalance at both its top and bottom) can be graded into octant and quadrant levels and projected onto the 1-minute chart. Price opened the session, drove up into the upper volume imbalance (which also lined up with Friday's new day opening gap), and then sold off through the graded levels one after another. He shows his own short entries, stop-outs, re-entries and partials: entries on SIBIs/FVGs sitting on octant levels, partials at the lowest octant/quadrant and the octant below the CE, treating each level like sell-side liquidity. He also shows when the day turns into a "narrow range between octants" (stand aside) and closes with mindset advice for hard, high-volatility days.

## Concepts

### Daily inefficiency (May 5, 2026 BISI) graded into octants/quadrants as the chain of custody  (aliases ICT uses: gradient levels, octant and quadrant levels, daily buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency, suspension block, volume imbalance upper/lower)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The daily chart's bullish BISI from May 5, 2026 has a small volume imbalance at its upper end and one at its lower end (the two ends of the suspension block). The upper one is where price drove up to at the start of the session; the lower one is where price traded to on Friday (CE of that block was traded to on Friday, then price opened Monday and traded up into the upper volume imbalance).
  - He had told a student (on the Sunday X Space, "trader roundup") that "price would likely draw up into that volume imbalance and then sell off", without giving full analysis for the week.
  - The gradient levels are anchored to the daily timeframe: measure from the high of the May 5th inefficiency (top of the upper volume imbalance) to its low (the low = a candle open/close near 28,000.25 per captions) and project; levels: high of the volume imbalance, upper octant, upper quadrant, next octant, CE, octant below CE, lower quadrant, lower octant, low of the May 5th inefficiency.
  - The upper volume-imbalance also coincides with Friday's new day opening gap (NDOG), a confluence.
  - He had a fib mis-anchoring problem on screen (it showed 28,000.75 which shouldn't have been shown) and says "good enough for government work."
- **How to identify:** daily BISI/suspension block with volume imbalances at top and bottom; fib anchored high to low; mark octants, quadrants, CE; overlay NDOG/NWOG if they coincide.
- **How to use:** (1) trade the daily levels down to the 1-minute chart and look for SIBIs/FVGs whose bodies lie on graded levels; (2) take partials at the graded levels; (3) treat the levels like SSL.
- **Example in this video:** Monday (day of the review), NQ(?) 1m: overnight price hits Friday's NDOG; at the open price trades up into the upper volume imbalance of the May 5, 2026 daily BISI; ICT shorts there; price then breaks down through upper octant, upper quadrant, lower octant just above the CE, CE, octant below CE, lower quadrant and finally the daily block's low, with intermittent bounces.

### Short entries on SIBI/FVG whose body sits on an octant level; bodies staying outside the upper half  (aliases ICT uses: cibby/sibby = SIBI, "keeping the body outside", fair value gap in the upper quadrant)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He found an FVG at the upper quadrant of the May 5 BISI: price traded up into it, then broke lower.
  - The next SIBI formed with its body laying right on the lower octant just above the CE; price traded up to it again, giving a "really nice shorting opportunity."
  - The key tell: bodies stay outside the upper half of that SIBI; all bodies stay outside; "that's an indication it's likely to go down a lot." Then price breaks aggressively lower.
  - He wanted to get short with a limit order near the open, was afraid it wouldn't fill, so entered anyway to manage around it; he got stopped out ("as you saw me get stopped out in the black"), re-entered at the upper volume imbalance high, took a partial, got stopped again on the rally and shorted again.
  - He expected the upper volume imbalance to get "really really heavy" (a clean sell-off) but price returned to trade in it more than once.
- **How to identify:** a SIBI (bearish gap) whose body is on a graded level; later candles rally into it but do not close in its upper half.
- **How to use:** short on the retrace into the SIBI at the octant level; stop above the array; expect a sharp decline if bodies stay outside the upper half.
- **Example in this video:** 1m, morning: FVG in the upper quadrant of the daily block - price trades up into it then breaks lower; SIBI at the octant above the CE - price trades up, bodies stay below its midpoint, breaks lower; price continues down to the octant below the CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:18](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=498) — limit order, stop-outs, re-entries; [0:09:56](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=596) — FVG in upper quadrant; [0:10:45](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=645) — SIBI body on the octant, bodies outside the upper half

### Partial profits at gradient levels ("run down equity")  (aliases ICT uses: chasing new equity, trim, partials near gradient levels)
- **Kind:** rule (trade management)
- **What ICT says:**
  - As price approaches the lower graded levels he teaches students to "run down equity": take partials at each key level, "chasing new equity" each time the market gives an opportunity; the key levels being lowest octant, lowest quadrant, octant below the CE, and the CE.
  - Treat each graded level just like sell-side liquidity: once price gets to it or below it, take a partial, or take it as price gets close.
  - It is fine to peel off something and let the low be traded below and then taken out, closing the remainder.
  - He notes entries/partials in this session were all close to gradient levels.
  - Heavy morning: it could easily come back and trade against him, so trimming is wise.
  - "You're never going to get all of it ... be content with just being good enough."
- **How to use:** scale out at graded levels, take the last portion on a sweep of the low, don't wait for the full range.
- **Example in this video:** short from the upper imbalance; partials near the octant below the CE, the CE, the lower octant and the block's low; stop on the remaining position hit once; new entries marked in the lower half; final fill near the low, price goes a bit lower afterwards.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:34](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=694) — run down equity; [0:12:22](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=742) — partials near gradient levels; [0:13:13](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=793) — be content, never get all of it
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Run down equity" is his phrase (captions render it as "run down equity"); keeps the idea of banking at levels ahead of the target.

### Narrow range between octants/quadrants and lunch hour: stand aside  (aliases ICT uses: sit on the sidelines, retail bull flags don't work)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price spends a lot of time between octants and quadrants (a narrow range), "it's usually indicative that you want to sit to the sidelines"; don't try anything fancy and don't squeeze for more.
  - During Monday's New York lunch a retail-looking bull flag failed ("it doesn't seem to work"), price went into the octant below the CE and lower.
  - The "octant below CE" acts like support/resistance: bodies don't touch it; then price breaks lower and flirts below the daily block's low and trades back up inside the lower quadrant.
- **Example in this video:** after the morning drop, price rebuilds a narrow range below the CE; lunch hour churn.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Grade a daily inefficiency into: high of upper volume imbalance, upper octant, upper quadrant, octant, CE, octant below CE, lower quadrant, lower octant, low; use them on the 1-minute chart.
- Daily inefficiency from May 5, 2026: low about 28,000.25 (caption); upper volume imbalance coincided with Friday's NDOG.
- Take partials at the lowest octant, lowest quadrant, octant below CE, and CE; treat each as SSL.
- If bodies stay outside the upper half of a SIBI, the move is likely to continue (go down a lot).
- Spending lots of time between octants/quadrants in a narrow range = sit out.
- Swing stop placement vs the volume imbalance (caption unclear): stop should not be "below" the lower imbalance (see note).

## Glossary candidates
`RUN DOWN EQUITY` — scaling out at successive levels so you are always "chasing new equity" rather than waiting for one final target.
`GRADIENT LEVELS` — octant/quadrant/CE levels of a daily inefficiency used as lower-timeframe reaction points.
`MAY 5TH BISI` — the daily buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency from May 5, 2026 used as this week's key array.
`OCTANT BELOW CE` — the graded level one-eighth below the midpoint, acting as a reaction level.
