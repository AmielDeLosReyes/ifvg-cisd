## Concepts

### TGIF (Thank God It's Friday) & Weekly Retracement
- **Kind:** time-based pattern / weekly structure
- **What ICT says:** TGIF is a pattern where price retraces 30% of the **weekly range** by Friday. The weekly range is calculated from the weekly low to the weekly high of the past week. After retracing to 30% of that range, price often reverses and runs back to the highs (or higher) by week's end. This is a structural pattern that reflects profit-taking and then resumption of the longer-term trend.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Calculate last week's range: high - low.
  2. Multiply by 0.30 to get 30% retracement level.
  3. Find the weekly low and add 30% of the range.
  4. On Friday, watch if price approaches this level.
  5. If it does, expect reversal and rally higher.
- **How to use:** On Fridays, if price has retraced to 30% of the weekly range, be ready for a bullish reversal or resumption of the uptrend. Don't short into 30% retracement on a Friday; instead, expect buyers to step in. Use it as a support level or as a confirmation that the weekly trend remains up.
- **Example in this video:** April 14, 2026 (Tuesday). ICT mentions that price traded down into 30% of last week's weekly range and found its footing, confirming the weekly trend is bullish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:45](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=105)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** TGIF is specifically for Fridays, but the concept of finding support at percentage retracement levels of the weekly range can apply mid-week as well if that level is tested.

### Lunch Macro (10:30–11:10 ET)
- **Kind:** time window + retracement expectation
- **What ICT says:** The lunch macro is not the traditional lunch time (11:30–13:30 ET). Instead, it's a profit-taking / retracement window that begins around 10:30 ET (when the first full hour of trading, 9:30–10:30, is complete). The micro came from FX trading: between 10:00–11:00 ET is London close time, and the midpoint (10:30) marks the start of profit-taking. During lunch macro, expect price to retrace back into the range established since the 9:30 open. In a bullish market, the retracement is shallow (only to intermediate lows). In a bearish market, it can go much deeper (to the lowest low of the day).
- **How to identify:** Mark 10:30–11:10 ET on your chart. Within this window, price will typically pull back. Identify the range since 9:30 (opening price to the high of the run).
- **How to use:** In a bullish market, short during the lunch macro with tight stops, targeting only intermediate lows within the range. Don't expect a deep reversal. Once the target is hit, immediately take profits and prepare to re-enter longs as price recovers. In a bearish market, shorts can target deeper lows. Time your entry to coincide with the lunch macro window (10:30–11:10).
- **Example in this video:** April 14, 2026. Price rallied from 9:30 until 10:30, then the lunch macro window opened (10:30–11:10). A minor pullback occurred at 10:50, but not significant. The main pullback happened slightly later (around 10:50 onward). ICT waited for the right conditions and entered a short trade targeting the buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency within the range.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:07](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=247), [0:05:52](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=352), [0:08:20](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=500), [0:14:40](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=880)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The lunch macro CAN start as early as 10:30, but strong markets may not show a significant retracement. Watch for a pullback attempt, but in very bullish markets, it may be shallow or fail entirely. The macro isn't a guarantee; it's a time-based opportunity window.

### Buy-side Imbalance / Sell-side Inefficiency (BISI)
- **Kind:** PD array + order flow marker
- **What ICT says:** When price makes a significant move in one direction (e.g., bullish push from 9:30–10:30), it leaves behind an imbalance: buy-side imbalance (unmet buyers who tried to enter on the way up but were skipped) and sell-side inefficiency (untested sell orders). When a BISI forms during a bullish run, the buy-side imbalance is in the zone the price covered, and the sell-side inefficiency is below (orders that were never tested during the run). On a retracement, price targeting this BISI is targeting the sell-side inefficiency (the unexploited sellers). This is a high-probability retracement target.
- **How to identify:** After a bullish move, mark the range covered (high to low of the move). Look for tight consolidation or wicks within this range. The BISI is the zone between the high and the consolidation point where sellers are resting (sell-side inefficiency).
- **How to use:** During lunch macro, target the BISI as your first profit-taking level on a retracement. Price will often drop to this zone and bounce back. Once the BISI is "broken" (price closes below the wick low or consequent encroachment), expect the move to continue downside to deeper sell-side pools.
- **Example in this video:** A BISI formed at 26,600+ after the morning run. During the lunch macro, price retraced toward this zone, and ICT shorted into it, targeting the sell-side inefficiency (lower sells) within and below it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:55](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=595), [0:13:04](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=784), [0:20:11](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1211)

### Dealing Range vs. External Range Liquidity
- **Kind:** liquidity structure + targeting framework
- **What ICT says:** The **dealing range** is the range of price from the high of the current move down to a significant low (e.g., the 10:00 low during the morning session). **External range liquidity** is the sell-side liquidity that sits OUTSIDE and BELOW the dealing range—further lows that were formed earlier (yesterday, earlier this week). When price retraces into the dealing range (say, a lunch macro retracement), targeting the low of the dealing range is one target. Targeting external range liquidity is the more aggressive target, going below the dealing range to unexploited sellers.
- **How to identify:** Draw the dealing range (high to low of current session/move). Mark the external range liquidity (previous session's low, earlier week's low, or other old support).
- **How to use:** In lunch macro shorts, first target the low of the dealing range (smaller profit). If price breaks below that, the next target is external range liquidity (larger move). Use the dealing range low as a first partial, then hold for external range.
- **Example in this video:** The dealing range was from the morning high (26,600+) to the 10:00 low. External range liquidity was older support levels below. ICT targeted both, taking a partial at the dealing range low, then holding for the external range liquidity.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:11](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1211), [0:29:57](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1797)

### Bearish Order Block (Activation & Entry)
- **Kind:** entry model + pyramiding point
- **What ICT says:** When price makes a down-close candle after a series of up closes, the **opening price** of that down-close candle activates as a bearish order block. When price later retraces back up and touches that opening price level, it becomes an **entry or pyramiding point** for shorts. You don't enter at the down-close itself; you wait for the retracement back to the open price, then enter as price fails to hold above it.
- **How to identify:** Find a down-close candle at a reversal point (after up closes). Mark its opening price. Later, when price retraces back up and touches that open price, you have a confirmed entry point for shorts.
- **How to use:** Use the bearish order block open price as a trigger for additional short entries (pyramiding). Once price fails to hold above this level and closes below it again, the order block is validated and the downside move accelerates.
- **Example in this video:** A candle closed down after the morning rally. Its opening price became the bearish order block. When price retraced back up during the lunch macro and touched that open price, ICT used it as a pyramiding entry point for adding to his short position.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:21](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1401), [0:25:45](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1545)

### Premium Sensitivity (Bearish Validation)
- **Kind:** rule + order flow confirmation
- **What ICT says:** When trading into a bearish fair value gap (an inversion FVG where we expect prices to fall), the bodies must stay in the **lower half** of the gap. This is premium sensitivity in a bearish context. If bodies enter the upper half, the setup is weakening. The moment a candle closes with a body in the upper half, cancel the bullish interpretation and expect continued downside. This is the "give up the ghost" moment for bullish reversals.
- **How to identify:** After price trades into an inversion FVG on a retracement, watch the bodies. Stay in lower half = valid short; bodies in upper half = invalid.
- **How to use:** Hold shorts as long as bodies stay in the lower half. The moment a body closes in the upper half, be ready to exit or reduce. Use premium sensitivity (bodies in lower half) as confirmation to add to shorts.
- **Example in this video:** Price retraced into an inversion FVG (bearish setup). Bodies stayed in the lower half, confirming premium sensitivity and validating the short. Price then accelerated lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:33](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1353), [0:24:07](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1447)

### Wick as Gap & Consequent Correction
- **Kind:** micro-level targeting / Fibonacci application
- **What ICT says:** A wick that extends significantly can be treated as a gap for Fibonacci purposes. The lower portion of the wick (from wick low to midpoint/consequent correction) becomes the target zone for a retracement. When price trades down and touches the consequent correction of a wick, it's a high-probability support/resistance level.
- **How to identify:** Find a wick that extends significantly (e.g., from a previous high down 50 points). Mark the low and midpoint (consequent correction). This zone (low to midpoint) is the wick-as-gap zone.
- **How to use:** When price dives down and reaches the consequent correction of a wick, expect a pause, reversal, or final target achievement. Use it as a precision exit point or as confirmation that the target has been hit.
- **Example in this video:** A wick extended below a key level during an earlier candle. When price dropped into the lunch macro, it targeted the consequent correction of that wick, which was the final target for the short trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:43](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=643), [0:11:30](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=690), [0:17:48](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1068)

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (Premium Grade)
- **Kind:** high-probability entry / continuation signal
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG that forms from an up-close candle (where a gap is left above the close) is a high-caliber entry when price retraces into it. If bodies stay in the lower half (premium sensitivity), it's the strongest signal. The inversion FVG acts as a magnet for shorts. You don't need to be perfect; just recognize when price trades into it and bodies show bearish intent. This is one of the most obvious FVGs and should never be missed.
- **How to identify:** After an up-close candle, mark the gap left above it. This is the iFVG. When price retraces back down and enters this gap, watch the bodies.
- **How to use:** Short aggressively when price enters an inversion FVG from an up-close candle and bodies stay in the lower half. This is one of the "obvious" high-probability setups that "jumps off the chart."
- **Example in this video:** Multiple inversion FVGs formed; price traded into them and bodies showed premium sensitivity. Each was a clear short setup.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **TGIF level:** Weekly low + (30% × weekly range).
- **Lunch macro window:** 10:30–11:10 ET. Expect a retracement into the range formed since 9:30.
- **First-hour range:** 9:30–10:30 ET. Once complete, macro can trigger retracements.
- **Bullish market lunch macro target:** Intermediate lows only (within the range, not below it).
- **Bearish market lunch macro target:** Lowest low of the day (since 10:00 or earlier).
- **Buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency:** Formed during bullish runs. Target the sell-side (the low of the BISI or below).
- **Dealing range:** High of current move to significant low within the move (e.g., 9:30 high to 10:00 low).
- **External range liquidity:** Sell-side pools below the dealing range (yesterday's low, earlier week's low).
- **Bearish order block:** Opening price of a down-close candle. Re-entry point when price retraces back to this level.
- **Inversion FVG premium condition:** Bodies in lower half only. If bodies enter upper half, cancel bullish and expect downside.
- **Wick low to consequent correction:** Micro-level target for a retracement into the wick-as-gap.
- **Position sizing (this trade):** 10 micros initially, add 10 more at order block, add 10 more at iFVG entry (30 micros total = ~3 minis equivalent).
- **Exit levels:** First partial at dealing range low, second partial at external range liquidity low (consequent correction of wick).

## Process, risk & psychology

**Pre-session setup:**
- Calculate TGIF level for the weekly range (applies more on Fridays, but useful context daily).
- Mark key relative equal highs and lows from the past few days.
- Identify dealing range (if trading before that range forms, anticipate where it will be based on the opening range).

**During morning session (9:30–10:30):**
- Track the range as it forms. Note the high(s) and low(s).
- Watch for a pullback toward the open price as a test (often doesn't happen if market is very bullish).
- Prepare for lunch macro by having targets ready (BISI, internal lows, external liquidity).

**Lunch macro window (10:30–11:10):**
- Expect a pullback/retracement into the morning range.
- In bullish markets, target only intermediate lows (don't go for the lowest low).
- Enter shorts into inversion FVGs or BISI zones.
- Watch bodies: if they show premium sensitivity (lower half), add to the position.
- Scale out: first partial at dealing range low, final partial at external range liquidity.

**Post-macro (11:10 onward):**
- If the short setup worked, the day often resumes the original trend (bullish in this case).
- Prepare for re-entry longs at the low achieved during lunch macro.

**Key discipline:**
- Don't force trades outside of macro time windows.
- Don't go deeper into lows on lunch macro shorts unless the market is bearish (HTF bias).
- Use obvious setups only; if it's not obvious, don't trade it. "If it's not obvious, you're probably forcing a trade."

## Glossary candidates

`TGIF` — Thank God It's Friday; a weekly pattern where price retraces to 30% of the weekly range and then reverses back to the highs. Useful for confirming weekly trend direction.

`Lunch macro` — A 40-minute retracement/profit-taking window (10:30–11:10 ET) that follows the first full hour of trading (9:30–10:30 ET). Inspired by FX London close time (10:00–11:00 ET).

`Buy-side imbalance / Sell-side inefficiency (BISI)` — A PD array formed during a bullish run where buyers were skipped (imbalance) and sellers below were untested (inefficiency). Retracement target in lunch macro.

`Dealing range` — The range from the high of the current move to a significant low within that move (e.g., morning high to 10:00 low).

`External range liquidity` — Sell-side liquidity pools that sit BELOW the dealing range, from previous sessions or earlier in the week.

`Bearish order block` — Opening price of a down-close candle. Acts as a pyramiding entry point when price retraces back to it and fails to hold above.

`Premium sensitivity (bearish)` — Bodies staying in the lower half of a bearish FVG or down-move zone. Indicates continued selling pressure.

`Wick as gap` — Treating a wick as if it were a gap for Fibonacci and target-setting purposes. The wick low to midpoint (consequent correction) becomes a micro-level target.

`Consequent correction` — Midpoint of a wick or gap. Used as a precision exit or support/resistance level.

`Inversion FVG (from up-close)` — A gap left above an up-close candle, which forms an inversion FVG. When price retraces into it with bodies in lower half, it's a premium short setup.

`False breakout` — Concept from Linda Raschke's "Street Smarts": price breaks above a level but reverses sharply. ICT's turtle soup adapts this concept with macro time and two-stage liquidity extraction.

`Macro-aligned setup` — A trade setup that coincides with a macro time window (e.g., 10:30–11:10 lunch macro). Higher probability than off-macro setups.
