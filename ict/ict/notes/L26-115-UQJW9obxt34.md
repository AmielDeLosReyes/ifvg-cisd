## Concepts

### Volume Imbalance (Refined: Wicks Bridging the Gap)
- **Kind:** refinement / definition
- **What ICT says:** A volume imbalance (FVG) exists when bodies do not fill the gap between candles, even if *wicks* extend into that gap and touch both the prior close and current open. The bodies' absence is what defines the imbalance; wicks don't invalidate it. These imbalances are "pertinent to future price action."
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Spot a gap between candle bodies (prior close to current open).
  2. Check if wicks reach into the gap; they may touch both sides but don't fill with bodies.
  3. If bodies stay above or below, it is still a volume imbalance.
  4. Measure the imbalance by the body extremes (not wick extremes).
- **How to use:** Treat the imbalance as a PD array to be respected or filled. Wicks touching the gap are secondary; bodies matter. Use for higher-timeframe bias on lower-timeframe trades.
- **Example in this video:** DXY daily, ~0:04:12: a candle's high is 99.299, low touches 98.something; wicks bridge the prior gap, but bodies define the imbalance. This imbalance is still pertinent to future price. "Even though the wicks are actually bridging the gap between the two, it's still a volume imbalance."
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:04:12](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=252) — "Even though the wicks are actually bridging the gap… it's still a volume imbalance. And those levels are pertinent to future price action."
- **Nuance:** This is a refinement to clarify that volume imbalances are defined by *body placement*, not wick extremes. Many retail traders dismiss imbalances when wicks touch both sides; ICT clarifies this is wrong.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) – Context & Confirmation
- **Kind:** entry model / bias filter
- **What ICT says:** An iFVG is valid when price trades *below* a level (bears in control), then pulls back into the gap but refuses to break above consequent encroachment or lay bodies in the upper half. Use iFVGs as bias anchors on daily charts; apply on lower timeframes for entries. "Until I'm proven wrong in this being traded above," treat it as an inversion FVG and expect lower prices.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Price is already trading below a gap (sell-side imbalance intact).
  2. Price rallies back into the gap but fails to close above the midpoint (consequent encroachment).
  3. No body in the upper half.
  4. This is a confirmed iFVG (bearish bias).
- **How to use:** Use on daily as macro bias. Apply on 1m, 5m, 15m to find entries. Example: if daily shows price respecting an iFVG (not breaking above it), enter shorts on lower timeframe reversals from the gap. Targets: sell-side below the gap.

### Relative Equal Lows (RELs) / Relative Equal Highs (REHs)
- **Kind:** definition / target
- **What ICT says:** A relative equal low is a prior low that price is likely to revisit or break through on a fresh downside move. A relative equal high is the analog for upside. Use them as targets after confirming direction via FVG/iFVG or intraday structures. They are NOT precise levels but "probable" objectives.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Scan the chart for prior swing lows (RELs) or highs (REHs) that price has not recently touched.
  2. If trading lower off an iFVG, anticipate price will reach at least one prior REL.
  3. If breaking above, anticipate prior REH.
- **How to use:** Use as secondary targets. Primary target: consequent encroachment or imbalance rejection. Secondary: the nearest REL/REH. If price blows past REL/REH, expect continuation to the next level.
- **Example in this video:** 
  - Silver (0:18:20–0:19:07): "we would trade down to consequent encroachment. And if we got below that, we would clear up the smooth levels here. And eventually went down into that little volume imbalance there." Price cleared consequent encroachment, then hit sell-side.
  - MNQ (0:27:03): "You take this below, then come back, and then not go in the lower half… then I would expect it to start going higher" once REHs are available. Relative equal highs act as targets.
  - Intraday ES (0:28:36–0:29:24): "took the sell side into the imbalance here, left the sell side there. Rallied up, pushed it higher, left this high intact." Relative equal highs are tracked and expected to be hit.
- **Timestamps:**

- **Nuance:** RELs/REHs are probabilistic, not certain. Price may overshoot or undershoot; use them as "likely" zones, not hard stops.

### Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL) & Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency
- **Kind:** definition / structure
- **What ICT says:** Sell-side liquidity rests below a market high (stops under the high). When price trades below that region without filling the gap, it creates a buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (BISI). This is the imbalance in the gap below the high. It acts as support or reversal point.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Price makes a swing high.
  2. Price gaps down and opens below that high without bodies in between.
  3. That gap below the high is BISI.
  4. The SSL is the concentration of stops just below the original high.
- **How to use:** 
  - If shorting and price drops through BISI, trail stops above it.
  - If long, use BISI as a support level; bounce above it and continue higher.
  - Use on daily charts to identify macro targets; apply on lower timeframes for entries.
- **Example in this video:** 
  - EURUSD, GBPUSD: multiple references to "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency" (BISI).
  - DXY (0:04:59–0:05:47): "if we lose this imbalance and go lower, then you're probably going to see some pressure on dollar index."
  - Intraday ES (0:28:36–0:29:24): "took the sell side into the imbalance here, left the sell side there… If it's going to go below here, this should be used as fuel, kind of like energy to send it lower."

### Market Maker Buy/Sell Model (MMBM/MMSM)
- **Kind:** concept / mechanism
- **What ICT says:** Market makers operate a model where they long below a low (to collect liquidity and profits) or short above a high (same logic). If price "should" go lower but instead breaks up, it may be because the MM buy model is in effect (long positions being taken, shortstop hunting in reverse). Use this to confirm or negate setups. Example: "exactly where a market maker sell model would fail and go the other direction."
- **How to identify:** Observe price not delivering expected direction despite iFVG or other confirmation. If price should go lower but goes up, assume MM buy model.
- **How to use:** If you see MM model in effect (opposite of expected direction), either sit out or reverse your bias. Use it as a warning: "if I'm wrong here, this is where the MM model is likely at play."
- **Example in this video:** GBPUSD (0:13:39): "It's exactly where a market maker sell model would fail and go the other direction if I'm wrong."
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:13:39](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=819) — MM sell model as opposite outcome
- **Nuance:** MM model is speculative; use as a defensive check, not a primary signal.

### Consequent Encroachment (Midpoint / Equilibrium)
- **Kind:** definition (same as L26-114)
- **What ICT says:** Split any gap or wick in half (midpoint = consequent encroachment). If price fails to touch it, that is weakness in that direction. If it respects the midpoint and doesn't close above it when trying to rally into an iFVG, the iFVG is confirmed.
- **How to use:** Use as a filter: if price in an iFVG scenario fails to touch or exceed consequent encroachment of the wick, it confirms bearishness (if iFVG) or bullishness (if bullish FVG).
- **Example in this video:**

## Glossary candidates

`Consequent Encroachment` — The midpoint of a gap or wick. Failure to touch it = weakness in that direction. Critical filter for iFVG validation.

`Buy-Side Imbalance / Sell-Side Inefficiency (BISI)` — The imbalance left below a swing high when price gaps down without filling the gap with bodies. Acts as support or reversal point.

`Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)` — A FVG that price has already traded below (bearish) and may retest; if it fails to break above consequent encroachment, the iFVG is confirmed and lower prices expected.

`Volume Imbalance` — A gap between candle bodies with no bodies in the gap. Wicks may bridge, but bodies do not; still a PD array worthy of respect.

`Relative Equal Low (REL)` — A prior swing low that price is likely to revisit or break through on a corrective move lower.

`Relative Equal High (REH)` — A prior swing high that price is likely to revisit or break through on a corrective move higher.

`Seasonal Tendency` — A recurring directional bias for a currency pair or commodity at a certain time of year (e.g., GBPUSD stronger in spring). Use as a *filter*, not a primary signal.

`SIBI` — Sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency; a volume imbalance where the sell-side liquidity has been taken and bodies sit above the gap.

`Tomfoolery` — Market manipulation by institutions, especially in pre-market (7 am ET) or after-hours sessions, designed to trap retail traders and move positioning.

`Consequent Correction` — The midpoint of a wick that has gapped below it; used as resistance to confirm weakness below.

`Reclaimed Bullish FVG` — A FVG that price trades above, pulls back into, but refuses to stay in the lower half upon re-entry; the FVG now acts as support for continuation higher.

`Low-Volume Trading Period` — Calendar windows (Passion Week, holidays, year-end, etc.) where volume is too thin, spreads are wide, and manipulation risk is high. Best to avoid trading entirely.

`Market Maker Buy/Sell Model` — A hypothetical framework where market makers long below support to collect buy-side liquidity (profit-taking on longs) or short above resistance to collect sell-side liquidity. Used as a *defensive check* to explain why a setup might fail.

`Draw on Liquidity (DOL)` — Price moving to and potentially through prior liquidity levels (swing highs/lows) to hunt stops.

`2030 Agenda` — ICT's reference to a geopolitical/economic roadmap (fuel restrictions, 15-minute cities, ESG, etc.) that he believes is driving commodity and energy policy, thereby making crude oil and other commodities manipulated.
