### Daily context: wicks, volume imbalance between two wicks, and grading the wick  (aliases ICT uses: discount wick, consequent encroachment of the wick, suspension block, discount, premium array)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - A six-to-seven-week big daily range; price dropped between two daily wicks; a volume imbalance sits at the low. He shades the area gray "because I don't want to imply a direction yet."
  - Always grade a wick: put a fib on it and show the 50% (CE). Last Friday's discount wick CE could act simply as a premium array; a close above it on the daily would warrant likely higher prices; the next level is the CE of the other wick.
  - The suspension block below has not fully closed in; wicks are enough "to build an initial idea."
  - Daily volume imbalance down there is what he wants to act like a breakaway gap: if price is not interested in returning to it, the sell-side liquidity there was enough to send price higher.
- **How to identify:** two daily wicks; the volume imbalance/space between their lows; the CE of each wick.
- **How to use:** premium/discount read from the wick CE; look for longs if price is in discount and holds above; use the volume imbalance as the limit of how far a probe can go.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily: drop between two wicks and into discount; volume imbalance at the low; Friday's wick CE as premium array.

### Preparing the RTH opening range gap CE before 9:30 (manual fib)  (aliases ICT uses: RTH ORG, half gap, mid gap, regular trading hours opening range gap CE)
- **Kind:** rule (process) / how-to
- **What ICT says:**
  - With a large gap between Friday's settlement (RTH close, about 30,048.5 per captions) and the pending Monday open, prepare before the bell: put the fib on the exact Friday settlement level, drop it down toward the 9:32/9:33 mark on the time axis (not at 9:30, so you can see the candle open), enable "show all levels," make the CE line black and thick.
  - When the 9:30 opening price prints, drag the fib's other anchor to it; the 50% is the "RTH ORG CE" (label it so you do not lose sight of it).
  - Prefer drawing manually to scripts: scripts can only crunch the data they're given, the platform's data sometimes looks wrong, and doing it by hand keeps you involved ("as the author, if it doesn't jive with me, I won't use it").
  - A huge gap tends to attract an attempt to fill; he thinks the market is predisposed to draw toward the purple Friday-settlement line and toward buy-side near the 30,048.5 level, though it doesn't need to get there.
  - He estimates about 70% likelihood that price rallies to the CE of the RTH ORG around 10:00 ("that's usually the statistics"; caption unclear).
- **How to identify:** RTH ORG = prior-day RTH settlement (last print) to the 9:30 open; CE = 50%.
- **How to use:** CE as the first target for the gap draw, expect to approach it by about 10:00; a probe long uses the stop under the CE of the wick, etc.
- **Example in this video:** Monday 7/13: Friday settlement ~30,048.5; Monday's open lower (opening price implied about 29,714.5 given the 7/14 CE of ~29,881.5, my arithmetic from L26-063); RTH ORG CE annotated.

### Probe trade: one contract, bullish order block, and what failure means  (aliases ICT uses: head fake, probe, paper cut, failure to launch, scratch)
- **Kind:** example / entry model (exploratory)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He only wants "more information from the opening range, first 30 minutes" before committing; with a one-contract paper account ("for compliance reasons") he explores.
  - Trade 1 (long probe): after taking a short-term low he trades into a bullish order block (a down-close candle) with change in state of delivery; he enters long; plan: as long as the close stays above the halfway point (CE) of the wick, he's fine; stop just below that CE and below the low, no partials possible with one contract. A head fake (a 9:30 opening pop then drop) occurs; the long fails.
  - Trade 2 (short): he sells short at the CE of a wick (near a lower low that only had a small shallow wick through it) with a stop just above; the plan: sell-side liquidity is the draw; after taking it, reverse and go long to recover the loss; he wants bodies to stay below half of the wick.
  - Price came right to the event horizon but his limit order did not fill ("classic"); stop moved to break-even; price then dove aggressively; he describes it as "two executions, one scratch, minor little thing, then came back for a run - pretty positive outcome."
  - A body below the level is "much more significant than just doing wicks": he wants the body, not the wick, through the low.
  - Failure of one side tells him what to look for on the opposite side - but "that doesn't mean if you take a loss going long, it's always a sell short."
- **How to use:** take small, defined probes at levels with clear invalidation (stop under the CE/low), and read the failure for information; do not force.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m 9:30-10:10 on 7/13/2026: long at a bullish OB after a short-term low is taken; head-fake opening; stopped; short at the CE of a wick with the stop above; draw = sell-side liquidity/lower low; scratch near break-even; price rallies, then reverses.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=974) — bullish OB, CISD, head fake; [0:17:51](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1071) — stay in upper half of the wick, one contract; [0:20:18](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1218) — short at CE of the wick, draw = sell side; [0:28:13](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1693) — body vs wick; [0:41:02](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2462) — "two executions, one scratch"

### Candle-count rule inside an inefficiency (1-2 healthy, 3-5 failure)  (aliases ICT uses: crowded house, block party, a market in a hurry, no more than five candles)
- **Kind:** rule (new/most-emphasized teaching of the video)
- **What ICT says:**
  - "The only time I'm counting candles is when inside inefficiencies." A market that wants to go somewhere does not spend time inside a gap: it enters with one candle, doesn't leave a body there, and doesn't close the gap or even reach half of its range (CE).
  - Ideal: price displaces within the next candle or two. "Inefficiencies, you want to have one, two at most. Three, the probability starts to shift lower. If you're booking three to five candles inside that inefficiency, you don't want to see that." A "crowded house inside of an inefficiency" is not wanted.
  - "No more than five candles" - at the fifth candle "this is the last ditch. It's got to do it now or it's failing."
  - Example: a bearish gap after the drop: candle one went in, then the second; he says it must displace aggressively lower on this or the next candle or "we go to a different neighborhood" (higher). Spending way too many candles inside = a signal; he contrasts this with retail "support turned resistance."
  - He denies counting candles for the 13th/17th/21st candle type of retail counts.
- **How to identify:** when price retraces into a gap/inefficiency, count candles that trade inside it.
- **How to use:** if bearish, require displacement lower within one or two candles of entering the bearish gap; at three the probability shifts against you; five = failed; use the failure to flip to the opposite expectation (here, back up to the old low).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m after the morning drop: price retraces into a bearish fair value gap and spends five candles in it, fails to displace, and goes back above; it rallies to the old low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:53](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2513) — one candle, then the next; no crowded house; 1-2 at most; [0:42:40](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2560) — market in a hurry doesn't linger; [0:43:27](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2607) — counting candles only inside inefficiencies, no more than five; [0:44:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2654) — last ditch
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** New explicit candle-count thresholds (1-2 good, 3 weaker, 5 max). Related to earlier "how far into the gap" CE rules.

### Do not sell short in an extreme discount; PD failing = information  (aliases ICT uses: sell in premium buy in discount, premium sensitivity, no premium sensitivity)
- **Kind:** rule (reaffirmed core principle)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Selling at the low of the day's range (from RTH high to RTH low = extreme discount) "goes against what I taught in the very first month" of his 2016 private mentorship; "you want to buy in a discount."
  - Because the bearish gap failed to offer premium sensitivity (price showed it was unwilling to go higher but then went higher) and spent too many candles there, it was a failing array; recognizing when a PD array is not confirming is "not failure in the sense that they've changed the algorithm" - it is information.
  - When the sell setup fails, price is likely to draw back up to the old low (buy stops); this wiped out shorts who trailed stops; it then tests whether the lower area works as a discount array to go higher or "wilts through it" and the gap acts as bearish again, sending price to event horizon and then relative equal lows.
  - Advice: listen to the first four videos of the 2016 mentorship; more valuable than any new tool.
- **How to use:** don't sell inside a discount just because a bearish FVG formed; require premium sensitivity; if it fails, expect a return to the old low.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Monday 10:00-10:10: bearish FVG formed after a big drop into a discount; fails after 5 candles; price returns to the old low (range from RTH high down to the lows, "one low, two low, three low," then a full rally back).

### Liquidity void in RTH; RTH has more weight than ETH  (aliases ICT uses: liquidity void, vacuum of price, regular trading hours vs electronic trading hours)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the RTH-only chart, a stretch between one candle's low and another's high with no prints in regular trading hours is a "liquidity void," even if electronic hours traded through it; RTH "has to balance out" ETH "to be an efficiently delivered price."
  - Mismatches create imbalances seen in only one session, but RTH carries more weight because RTH tends to overlap and redeliver to levels ETH has already seen overnight; ETH has been trading since 6:00 p.m. Sunday ET, so those levels haven't been done yet in RTH; hence it is more interesting to see price go up into the gap with no RTH prints.
  - It could drop and never visit, but "more times than not" it adheres to this logic.
- **How to identify:** switch to the RTH chart; look for a price range between candles with no prints in RTH; compare with ETH.
- **How to use:** treat RTH voids as draws/targets (here the opening gap above); weight them over ETH-only inefficiencies.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Monday: RTH gap from Friday's settlement (~30,048.5) down to the open (~29,714.5, implied); price heads up into it later in the day/week.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:25](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2125) — liquidity void with no RTH prints; [0:36:13](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2173) — RTH weighted over ETH; [0:37:00](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2220) — more times than not
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Links to RTH ORG theory (L26-047) and to "liquidity voids" in the canonical list.

### Mondays: manipulation/accumulation day; avoid when new, except NFP-week Monday  (aliases ICT uses: don't trade Mondays, NFP Monday, day-of-week tendencies)
- **Kind:** rule (day-of-week)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Mondays are "the manipulation and accumulation part of the week, the first day of the week"; new traders should avoid them: "predominantly every Monday except for non-farm payroll."
  - NFP-week Monday is good because the rest of the week post-Wednesday-morning is event-driven; the cleanest price action tends to be that Monday; so trade it.
  - If brand new: just study price action on Mondays and accept that you may miss a run.
  - Mondays without news but with big news Tuesday/Wednesday are where traders lose most; it takes a lot more experience: lean on price action; "you don't want to come in with a hard bias"; weigh if-then statements.
  - Experienced students can trade Mondays; the rule is a guardrail "like the yellow line in the street."
- **How to use:** skip Mondays unless NFP-week; if exploring, use small size (one contract).
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:42](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1842) — no hard bias on news-driven weeks; [0:31:29](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1889) — Monday = manipulation and accumulation, avoid; [0:33:04](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1984) — NFP-week Monday is the exception; [0:50:39](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=3039) — Mondays are where you lose most

## Process, risk & psychology
- Start a week from a clean slate; don't impose a preconceived idea ([0:03:19](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=199)).
- The outcome is always the trader's responsibility, even using scripts or automated tools; "operator error" ([0:15:25](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=925), [0:16:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=974)).
- Use one contract (or a micro) while exploring; no reason to fear a loss when risk is small ([0:22:39](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1359), [0:29:06](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1746)).
- Death by a thousand paper cuts: no model + no discipline + small repeated losses bleed an account; being measured prevents it ([0:27:22](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1642)).
- Don't max out contracts on a test day; "going for broke" will hit its target ([0:23:25](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1405), [0:25:47](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1547)).
- Recognize when the odds are stacking against you; preserving capital and mental capital is more important than showing off a trade ([0:45:50](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2750)).
- Contrarian sentiment anecdote: when someone who knows nothing about trading tells you to buy, it's likely the top (Bitcoin story); "buy the rumor, sell the fact" ([0:24:12](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1452), [0:24:59](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1499)).
- A failed trade tells you what to expect on the opposite side, but not automatically a reversal trade ([0:30:42](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1842)).
- Paper trading is shown "for compliance reasons"; he can't advise real money ([0:21:52](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1312)).
- He claims the industry (even AI models) now repeats his vocabulary (FVG, liquidity runs, MSS, macro times); skip as non-instructional ([0:47:26](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2846)).

## Glossary candidates
`LOW-PROBABILITY OPENING RANGE` — an opening range where the odds are not stacked in your favor; explore with minimal size.
`CROWDED HOUSE / BLOCK PARTY` — too many candles (3-5) spending time inside an inefficiency; signals failure.
`LIQUIDITY VOID (RTH)` — price range with no RTH prints even if ETH traded through it.
`PREMIUM SENSITIVITY` — a bearish array's upper half where price should reject (no premium sensitivity = it's failing).
`PAPER CUT` — a small, repeated loss (also "scratch" for break-even).
`ENIGMA` — ICT's phrase for his algorithm blended with price action and time (caption: "my enigma is the algorithm").
