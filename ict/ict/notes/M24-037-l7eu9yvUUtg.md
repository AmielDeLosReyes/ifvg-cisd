## Concepts

### Pre-market / weekly analysis routine (top-down)  (aliases: "soup to nuts breakdown", game plan, bias → narrative → model → time → entry → stop → partial → Terminus)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - He looks at the weekly opening gap only (Sunday ~6 PM open vs Friday settlement), then turns the charts off and lets Asia/London print on their own; he comes in the next morning with no preconceived idea. On the weekend (or Friday) he writes scenarios for a higher/lower/no-gap Sunday open.
  - The questions the analysis must answer: what is my bias, what narrative explains why price should do it, which model, what time of day, how do I enter, where is the stop, when is the first partial, when do I move the stop, when am I content at my Terminus, then stop trading for that session/day.
  - He skips the 4-hour chart when he is only session-trading/scalping up to the daily range; he would use the 4H only if swinging the whole weekly range. Flow used here: weekly → daily → 60 min → 15 min → 5 min → 1 min (15-second chart reserved for later in the week).
  - "If it doesn't jump off the chart at you" it is probably being forced; levels should be obvious. On higher timeframes he does not use a fine-tooth comb.
  - Weekly chart must be consulted once at the start of the week so you are not blind to several-hundred-handle runs; a 15-minute-only trader loses that perspective. Once the levels are annotated on the chart you will not forget them, and it becomes easier to trust a drop to them when price is in the area.
  - Annotate every higher-timeframe line (e.g. "Sept 23 weekly low") so the label shows on the price axis when you drop to lower timeframes; unlabeled lines are distractions. Also label each inefficiency with the timeframe it is anchored to.
  - The "boot camp" week is deliberately stripped down: one instrument, one recipe repeated daily; he admits no model is 100% and uncertainty has to be accepted.
- **How to identify:** n/a (process).
- **How to use:** Do it once per week (weekend) and again each pre-market; screenshot/annotate levels; keep chart on New York time (he stresses all his teaching fails if charts are on local time).
- **Example in this video:** Weekly: this week's candle trades up into premium; the weekly candle sequence leaves a buy-side-only inefficiency (week of Sep 9 high to week of Sep 23 low) which is the target zone; he notes CE and quadrants of the Sept 23 weekly candle. Daily: relative equal lows, consecutive volume imbalances, cleared a high, went into a SIBI, traded back up to the CISD (open of the up-close candle). 60m: big wick = discount wick, CE marked. 15m: relative equal lows over sell-side, plus NQ/ES SMT at Sunday 8 PM.

### Higher-timeframe inefficiencies as draws: weekly BISI/SIBI, volume imbalances, CE of candles  (aliases: weekly inefficiency, "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency", "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency", volume imbalance)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A weekly candle range that "only has buy-side delivery" is an inefficiency that price is reasonably expected to revisit. Here the weekly gap spans the Sept 9 week high down to the Sept 23 week low; he labels it with those dates.
  - He can add CE (midpoint) and quadrants of a candle/inefficiency; the "high of that weekly BISI" sits in the same proximity as the daily/15m relative equal lows, so it adds to the sell-side target zone.
  - When there are consecutive volume imbalances only one candle apart, just box the entire range; the result reads the same as boxing them individually. Because it is a premium array above price, shade it a distinct hue.
  - He does not keep all this on his own chart (it disorients him); he draws it for teaching.
  - Terms in his speech: BISI = "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency", SIBI = "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency".
- **How to identify:** weekly/daily candle ranges (gaps or volume imbalances) unfilled on one side; label with the anchoring timeframe/date.
- **How to use:** Treat the nearest relevant unfilled array in the direction of bias as the draw/target; combine with liquidity at the same price (relative equal lows) for a stronger objective.
- **Example in this video:** NQ weekly BISI (Sept 9 high to Sept 23 low) lies below price; daily chart: two volume imbalances separated by one candle boxed as one premium array overhead; price had cleared a daily high and traded into a SIBI.

### Wick consequent encroachment (discount wick / premium wick)  (aliases: 60m CE, hourly discount wick CE, "mid-wick")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Any big wick on the hourly (or other HTF) chart at a high/low or near market price gets annotated with its CE (midpoint). A wick below current market price is a discount wick; above current price it is a premium wick. He abbreviates on his notepad e.g. "60m CE" with a small "D" for discount.
- **How to identify:** a long wick near a swing low/high or near price; mark the half of the wick.
- **How to use:** A lower-risk, nearer objective on the way to the main target: price may go to the wick CE, perhaps dip a bit below and come back, then continue toward the liquidity beneath. Also usable as a stop reference: if the third candle of an FVG has a wick and the range is small, use the CE of that wick (see FVG concept).
- **Example in this video:** 60m big wick with a swing low; with NQ at 20,525 the wick is below price (discount); its CE is the first objective, then NQ would reach for the sell-side near 19,953 ("worst case" price dips below the CE then comes back, then dives to the sell-side).
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:35](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1115) — marking wicks; [0:19:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1177) — discount wick vs premium wick; [0:54:57](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3297) — anticipated path to CE then sell-side
- **Nuance / caveats:** Level 19,953 is read from captions ("19953"); treat the exact digits as slightly uncertain.

### Premium / discount  (aliases: premium array, discount array, "100% strike rate" claim)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** If you are not viewing price relative to premium and discount you are "doing it wrong". Every profitable short was entered in a premium and moved to a discount; every profitable long left a discount and traded to a premium. He says this has a "100% strike rate" regardless of whose method it is, and calls anyone who says to ignore it a "clown". You must be able to frame a short on the basis of a premium and a long on a discount.
- **How to identify:** relative to the range you expect price to expand from to the target; for a candle/FVG, the part above the middle of the range is premium. In the live example he draws the range from the high of one candle to the low of another and checks which wicks/FVG are above the midline.
- **How to use:** For shorts, take FVG/wick entries above the midpoint of the range being traded; for longs, below it. For the OR gap, once price is below its CE, treat the CE as a premium to sell from.
- **Example in this video:** the 15m inefficiency above price (Sunday 8 PM area) sat in a premium, so short entries in/around it were appropriate; wick tops inside that FVG were all valid short entries.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1177) — "absolutely doing it wrong" statement; [0:20:26](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1226) — 100% strike-rate claim; [0:45:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2711) — why this FVG wick is a premium; [1:02:55](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3775) — FVG must be in a premium relative to the range expanding to the target
- **Nuance / caveats:** Presented as an absolute; treat as emphatic teaching rather than a measured statistic.

### Fair value gap — entry and stop placement  (aliases: inefficiency, FVG, "candle one, two, three")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The inefficiency is always candle number two; the stop, if affordable, goes above candle two (for a short). If the range is small, use the body of candle three, and if candle three has a wick use the CE of that wick.
  - "Always err on the side of the smallest risk": while learning, he wants the smallest risk and smallest leverage and consistency rather than big wins; that "cookie-cutter" is what you multiply later.
  - Not every price run has a propulsion block, institutional order-flow entry drill or breaker, but there will always be a fair value gap; you may get in late in the run, which is fine as long as the FVG is in a premium (short) relative to the range that expands to the target.
  - A return to a PD array must be on a new candle: price must move away, create a new candle, then trade back into the array. Retracing into the array on the same candle that moved away is "never ever ever" an entry.
- **How to identify:** three-candle gap; for entries choose wicks/FVG in the correct half of the dealing range.
- **How to use:** entry inside the FVG (CE/lower half for bearish iFVG, see below), stop above candle 2 or tighter references above; small risk first.
- **Example in this video:** the 15m premium FVG above Sunday's gap (SMT area), and the 1m first FVG at 9:31.
- **Timestamps:** [0:46:04](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2764) — candle two / stop rules; [0:46:52](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2812) — smallest risk and leverage; [1:02:55](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3775) — FVG is always there; [2:06:16](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=7576) — must be a new candle
- **Nuance / caveats:** He mentions entering "if it could be afforded to you" i.e. the candle-two stop may be too wide for small accounts, so the fallbacks (candle three body, wick CE) apply.

### Change in State of Delivery (daily order block reference)  (aliases: CISD, "my order block")
- **Kind:** definition / example only

### Narrative — framing both sides (smart money vs retail)  (aliases: "best case scenario on both sides", cannibalizing retail, confederate)
- **Kind:** definition / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Bias is not enough; you need a narrative for how price will be used on participants: how will sentiment or sudden movement entice traders to buy/chase, unseat them, and let deeper pockets take their seats.
  - Frame the best case for both sides: shorting from the daily FVG down to the sell-side below relative equal lows is not "picking the top"; after a month-plus rally, falling to sweep sell stops below relative equal lows lets smart money cover shorts and accumulate longs, which is constructive for the higher-timeframe bullishness. If smart money sold short in last week's daily FVG, why take profit early when they can take it to the weekly inefficiency and also onboard longs.
  - Ask who is in the trade right now and who would regret being long; put yourself in their shoes when price moves toward an old low/high.
  - Smart money is like a "confederate" in the audience; the algorithm is the price engine that goes to liquidity or reprices to inefficiencies — only two things happen algorithmically.
  - He is not infallible: don't impose your will; have a contingency (if wrong, price may take buy-side first, entice buyers, then break down again; wait for the first FVG value to form and break down).
- **How to use:** your heaviest leverage goes on the trade that fits the narrative; take less leverage on the counter-trend side, "giving yourself permission to be wrong".
- **Example in this video:** narrative that NQ consolidates in a bull-flag like shape to draw in buyers while ES takes buy stops above its equal highs, then sells off toward the 9:31 FVG/ORG then sell-side near 19,953. Retail looks for breakout higher; buy stops above the flag are used by shorts to sell against.

### Fed chair / FOMC days: manual intervention vs algorithmic delivery  (aliases: Powell speech, manual intervention, "back to algorithm delivery")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Algorithmic delivery only does two things: run to liquidity or reprice to inefficiencies. When it is not delivering that way, manual intervention is in play (last week's FOMC, today's Powell speech).
  - Do nothing while the Fed chair talks and for 15 minutes after he stops. Then check whether PD arrays are being respected "to the tick" (price reacts off them or trades into them and leaves right away) — that means algorithmic delivery is back. If not yet, don't trade.
  - Before the speech the market is "fickle" and better left to novices; on days with a testimony in the 9:00–10:00 hour, trade before 8:00 AM; if the speech is in the afternoon, trade only in the morning and the first hour. He calls trading near speeches "the worst time to be in the marketplace".
  - Today's plan: no afternoon trading because of Powell; he expects manual intervention "guaranteed".
- **How to use:** rules say when you can, not "when you might". Don't carve out little exceptions.

### Decoupling — Dow / S&P 500 / NASDAQ (symmetry filter)  (aliases: decoupled market, symmetrical market, "canary in the coal mine", Dow theory)
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule (taught earlier in 2016–2017 core content, per him)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The only element of Dow theory he uses: when bearish or bullish you want all three averages (Dow, S&P 500, NASDAQ) to move in the same direction; this is a "symmetrical market" and the ideal environment. When they do different things the market is decoupled.
  - He credits the idea to a book author whose name is garbled in the captions (caption unclear), not to Dow theory.
  - A decoupled day is a "sit still and wait": you are likely to get "sand kicked in your face" because manual intervention is coming. You can trade decoupled markets, but you probably will not reach your targets.
  - The Dow can be the "canary in the coal mine" (30 stocks, "dirty 30"): when it refuses to do what the other two are doing, that may foretell something.
  - Fed speeches and FOMC cause decoupling; waiting 15 minutes after the speaker lets things re-sync, and if they have not synced, keep waiting.
  - Forex equivalent: a "symmetrical triad" — GBPUSD and EURUSD positively correlated, DXY the opposite; e.g. if DXY is flat, GBPUSD up and EURUSD down means a EURGBP short. Futures are easier because everyone gets the same price, while FX brokers' quotes/spreads differ.
- **How to identify:** compare the three averages around the open; check that highs/lows are being taken together.
- **How to use:** if they disagree, stay out or accept a lower probability; stick to a single instrument so you are not tempted to "pick the right one".

### First-presented FVG → bearish inversion FVG, CE entry, and "reclaimed bullish FVG" correction  (aliases: iFVG, first presented fair value gap, inversion fair value gap)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The model he gives Caleb: the first presented FVG after the 9:30 open on the 1-minute chart (earliest 9:31). If you are bearish, an up-close-candle FVG (BISI) is the one to focus on, because after price trades down through it and away, it becomes a bearish inversion FVG.
  - The retest is expected to hit the CE of that FVG to the tick; the lower half is the entry zone and the upper half should remain untouched. After it has reacted from CE and left it and taken out a low, it is "properly classified" as an iFVG — it need not reach the target.
  - He deliberately corrected himself: if price instead goes back above and into the gap and then comes down into it, it is a reclaimed bullish fair value gap because it is an up-close candle; last week he mislabeled one as an inversion FVG when it should have been called reclaimed bullish FVG (although what he expected price to do was accurate).
  - Using the 9:31 FVG as an iFVG: it should react the first time price returns; a second return (back into it) is not as good.
- **How to identify:** 1m, first FVG after 9:30; in a bearish bias select the up-close-candle gap; wait for displacement below it, then a return to its CE.
- **How to use:** enter at the CE (stacked with NWOG, see below); target relative equal lows; stop back inside/above the gap.
- **Example in this video:** 1m, ~9:31 ET: large first FVG forms; NQ shifts structure, piercing a swing low, trades up into a fair value gap "beautifully" (called in advance), then delivers down to half of the first presented FVG's candle; trades down, away from the orange-shaded 9:31 FVG, returns exactly to its CE "to the tick" and offers a short toward the sell-side beneath the low. Price then trades back up inside the FVG a second time, which he says is weaker.

### Turtle Soup (origin of his stop-hunt thinking)
- **Kind:** definition / history
- **What ICT says:** Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' Turtle Soup (from "Street Smarts") fades the 20-day breakout used by Richard Dennis's turtles — buy the break below the 20-day low, sell the break above the 20-day high, a false-breakout pattern capitalizing on the turtles' low strike rate. He took from it the idea of looking at stop hunts and being on the other side of stopped-out traders, applied to intraday charts; he does not count 20 days, he counts candles/structure. He credits Larry Williams for other ideas (e.g. the currency-futures comparison that preceded SMT).
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:35](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1955) — Turtle Soup explained; [0:34:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2051) — how it became his intraday stop-hunt foundation; [0:30:13](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1813) — 90s currency comparison

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Keep all charts on New York time.
- Pre-market = before 9:00 ET; pre-market session starts 7:00 ET; opening range = 9:30–10:00; first-hour window today = 9:30–10:30; lunch = 11:30–1:30 PM (2 hours).
- High/medium-impact news: wait at least 15 minutes. Fed chair: no trading while speaking and for 15 minutes after, and only resume when PD arrays hit "to the tick".
- If a Fed testimony falls between 9:00 and 10:00, trade before 8:00 AM.
- Today (Mon 30 Sep): Chicago PMI at 9:45 (medium), Powell speaks in the afternoon; trade only 9:30–10:30; no setup, no trade; no afternoon trading.
- FVG: stop above candle two; small range → body of candle three; wick → CE of that wick; always the smallest risk.
- Return to a PD array must come on a new candle, never the same candle.
- Premium for shorts, discount for longs.
- Three averages must agree (Dow/S&P/NASDAQ); disagreement = sit out.
- Seek & destroy: can't trade; HRLR: tradable with wider stop; decoupled: tradable but targets unlikely.
- Annotate every HTF level with its timeframe/date.
- Levels: NQ 20,525 (price at hourly review); sell-side objective near 19,953; 9:31 first 1m FVG.
- Use time-based candles only.
- Stay with one instrument.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Uncertainty has to be accepted; no model is 100% ([0:11:40](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=700)).
- Do not impose your will on the market; the market will humble you, so allow yourself to be wrong ([1:05:23](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3923)); not trading a move is "liberating": "if it keeps on going higher it simply means it's going to do it without me" ([1:28:44](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5324)).
- Smallest risk and smallest leverage while learning; consistency impresses more than big wins ([0:46:52](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2812)).
- Rules should state when you can trade, not when you might; no impulsive pockets of exceptions; if you feel impulsive, turn the charts off; journal what you feel (euphoria, fear, anxiety) while watching price ([1:15:49](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4549), [1:17:24](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4644)).
- Don't anticipate the setup and enter early; anticipate the setup, then pounce when it arrives ([2:13:06](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=7986)).
- Don't treat each next trade as "the pinnacle of your career"; placing too much emphasis on having to do something blows accounts ([2:01:27](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=7287)).
- Knowing when to sit still is an art; you may only realize after the day ends that you should have stopped when it got messy ([2:12:18](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=7938)).
- Old fear of stops being hit; desensitize by repeated buying/selling ([0:37:17](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2237)); his 90s bull-market false confidence and later account blowups from "arm wrestling" the market ([2:30:27](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=9027)).
- Study modularly, an hour or 90 minutes at least one weekend day; rewatching later reveals different things ([0:35:45](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2145)).
- Don't over-complicate: he starts with a few elements to avoid analysis paralysis ([0:27:53](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1673)).

## Glossary candidates
`BISI` — "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency": an up-close-candle FVG (bullish gap).
`SIBI` — "sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency": a down-close-candle FVG (bearish gap).
`CISD` — change in state of delivery; in a bearish context he equates it to the opening price of the up-close candle(s), also his "order block".
`Decoupling` — Dow, S&P and NASDAQ not moving together; a sit-out condition.
`Symmetrical market` — all three averages moving in the same direction.
`Canary in the coal mine` — the Dow diverging first as a warning.
`Manual intervention` — price action not explained by algorithmic delivery (Fed speakers, FOMC).
`Bellwether timeframe` — the 15-minute chart he leans on for most information.
`First presented FVG` — the first 1m FVG after 9:30, used as the entry reference.
`Reclaimed bullish FVG` — an up-close-candle FVG price returns into from above after trading back through it; not an inversion.
`Terminus` — the weekly/session target he stops trading at.
`Volatility pinball` — exercise of repeated buying/selling on the 15-second chart to build trade comfort.
`Hindsight journaling` — annotating screenshots as if you saw the trade coming.
