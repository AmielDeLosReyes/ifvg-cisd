## Concepts

### Weekly Suspension Block (Volume Imbalance)
- **Kind:** definition · price delivery array · range framework
- **What ICT says:** A bullish or bearish fair value gap on the weekly chart, typically created at the open of a new week. It's a "suspension block" because it has volume imbalances (inefficiencies) at both the high and low of its range, creating a "trapped" zone. During the week, price will trade up and down within this zone, eventually breaking out to the downside (if bearish) or upside (if bullish). In this week's example: the weekly suspension block was bullish at the open; price was inside it early week; by week's end, price breaks below it to fulfill the bearish weekly draw. "This suspension block... when we start the week... we're inside the middle of this inefficiency... specifically a suspension block because a volume imbalance at the high and a volume imbalance at the low." (0:04:03)
- **How to identify:** On the weekly chart, mark the opening candle (Sunday–Monday open). Look for an incomplete price delivery (body does not touch high or low, or a gap). This is the suspension block. Mark its high, low, and midpoint.
- **How to use:** Use the suspension block as a weekly framework: (1) Identify the block early Monday; (2) Forecast where weekly price will go (usually the opposite direction of the open); (3) Use daily chart levels (relative equal lows, FVGs) to confirm intra-week targets. (4) On Friday, use TGIF to pinpoint the weekly close.
- **Example in this video:** Weekly chart, July 15–19 (Monday–Friday). Suspension block marked at the open; identified as bullish initially but the block indicated a break below for the week. Lower volume imbalance at the bottom of the block was the draw-on-liquidity target (weekly relative equal lows at 28,512). Price broke out Friday, hitting that target precisely. (0:04:03–0:08:13)

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) / Premium & Discount
- **Kind:** definition · gap · liquidity reference
- **What ICT says:** When a new week starts (Sunday 6 PM ET or Monday pre-market), the settlement price from Friday may differ from the opening price of the week. This creates a gap. If the gap is "premium" (above Friday's close), price will likely retrace back down into that gap mid-week. If "discount" (below Friday's close), price will likely rally back up into it. The gap defines the weekly trading range boundaries. "So, if price is bearish and we went lower, we're trading back up in, it's reasonable. It's reasonable for price to trade up into that on Friday of last week and then open on Monday or Sunday or whatever you want to look at it lower than where we closed on Friday. So, we have a new week opening gap." (0:05:43)
- **How to identify:** On the daily chart, mark the Friday RTH close. Mark the Monday/Sunday opening price. If opening > close, gap is premium. If opening < close, gap is discount.
- **How to use:** Premium gaps typically act as resistance (inversion FVG); price will retrace into them mid-week. Discount gaps act as support; price will rally into them. Use the gap as a weekly target or waypoint.
- **Example in this video:** Monday opens in premium relative to Friday's close, indicating a weekly bearish bias (down move expected). (0:05:43–0:07:25)
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:06:35](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=395) — "If we're bearish this is going to act like an inversion fair value gap."

### TGIF (Thank God It's Friday) — Friday's 20–30% Retracement
- **Kind:** rule · weekly close pattern · target model
- **What ICT says:** On Friday, price will retrace 20–30% of the entire weekly range (measured from the high that forms during the week down to the low that forms during the week). This creates a predictable close zone on Friday afternoon. You can use this to forecast Friday's close in advance. "If we measure the high of the week that formed on Wednesday down to Friday's low, once it pierced this and worked its way back above that low, how far can it retrace? 20 to the 30% of the weekly range. 20% levels here. 30% is here." (0:18:37)
- **How to identify:** (1) On Friday, identify the weekly high (typically formed mid-week). (2) Identify the weekly low (typically hit by Friday or late Thursday). (3) Calculate 20% and 30% of that range above the low. (4) Watch for price to close in that 20–30% zone.
- **How to use:** Use TGIF as your Friday target close forecast. Once price hits the weekly low (draw on liquidity), expect it to retrace into the 20–30% zone and close there. This is one of the highest-probability price actions in the week. Avoid trading below this zone on Friday unless new weekly range-extending moves are happening.
- **Example in this video:** Weekly high on Wednesday, weekly low hit Friday morning. 20% and 30% retracement levels calculated. Friday close landed precisely in the 20–30% zone (0:18:37–0:19:24, 0:37:53–0:38:41).
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:19:24](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=1164) — "You're forecasting where the close of the week is going to be."

### Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions (Easy Days)
- **Kind:** definition · market state · trading environment
- **What ICT says:** Days after major economic news (CPI, PPI, FOMC) has passed, typically Thursday and Friday. Price stretches out in low resistance, meaning fast, directional candles with large ranges that cover significant distance. These are "easy money" days for traders who understand the direction. "Trading will get really nice after the PM session on Wednesday. So, what does that mean? Well, overnight Wednesday into Thursday, it'll be easy trading Thursday session, Friday session." (0:15:23) On these days, position size can be larger, stops tighter, and executions more straightforward.
- **How to use:** On low-resistance days, trust your daily/4H direction bias and trade with confidence. Take larger position size. Expect straight runs without much retracement. On Friday (assuming weekly low is already hit), trade longs into the TGIF retracement zone.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday and Friday (0:15:23–0:16:12). Both showed low resistance—fast expansions lower to the weekly target, then retracements up into TGIF zone.

### High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions (Hard Days)
- **Kind:** definition · market state · trading environment
- **What ICT says:** Days when major economic news (CPI, PPI) is released, typically Monday–Wednesday. These days have choppy, back-and-forth price action with lots of small candles in tight ranges (high resistance). Don't expect easy trades; expect to be stopped out multiple times or forced to sit out. "I told him, I said, you know, Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday is going to be a little bit more difficult for someone that's brand new." (0:10:37) Accept that new traders will struggle on these days and it's normal.
- **How to use:** On high-resistance days, keep size smaller. Accept wider stops. Don't fight the noise. Many professional traders sit out these days entirely. If trading, use only the highest-probability setups (clear daily bias + clear draw on liquidity).

### Pre-Market Session (7:00–9:00 AM ET) Liquidity Runs
- **Kind:** rule · session-specific behavior
- **What ICT says:** The pre-market session (7:00–9:00 AM ET, one hour before regular trading opens at 9:30 ET) is a crucial liquidity-running window. Price will often run overnight highs and lows, taking out relative equal highs/lows from the previous day or previous session(s). This creates fast, directional momentum that carries into 9:30 open. "Relative equal highs and lows... they're going to run them. Even though price existed there and booked overnight, regular trading hours are going to run up there and run down below them relatively sharply." (0:26:36) On Friday especially, the pre-market session is the "rocket fuel" that allows price to reach the weekly low target before the day is over.
- **How to identify:** Mark overnight highs and lows (electronic trading hours 6 PM–9:30 AM). Also mark previous day's relative equal highs/lows. At 7:00 AM, watch for fast runs into these levels.
- **How to use:** Trade into these levels in pre-market. Use them as entries if they align with your daily bias. On Friday, expect the pre-market session to deliver the final push to the weekly low. (0:29:02–0:31:25)
- **Example in this video:** Friday, 7:00 AM start to 9:00 AM close. Price runs down, hits the weekly relative equal low at 28,512, briefly rallies into the 9:30 open (Judas swing), then dumps lower. (0:29:02–0:33:02)

### Electronic Trading Hours vs. Regular Trading Hours (Jigsaw Puzzle Concept)
- **Kind:** rule · session architecture · market structure
- **What ICT says:** Regular trading hours (9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET for NQ) have gaps in price action created by time gaps (4:00 PM Friday to 6:00 PM Sunday open, Sunday 6 PM to Monday 9:30 AM, etc.). Electronic trading hours (6:00 PM–9:30 AM, GlobeEx for NASDAQ) fill in these gaps with price action. The intraday chart is like a jigsaw puzzle: regular trading hours create pieces, electronic trading hours fill in the missing pieces. Understanding both is necessary to see the complete price structure. "It's like a jigsaw puzzle... You have some parts, some pieces... There are pieces missing in here... That's where Electronic Trading Hours fills in those jigsaw puzzle pieces." (0:24:13)
- **How to use:** When analyzing a daily or weekly chart, mentally reconstruct the overnight/electronic trading hours price action by looking at gaps and where price resukes after gaps. This tells you where the real draw-on-liquidity points are and explains intraday price behavior.
- **Example in this video:** 7:00 AM (start of GlobeEx session) to 9:30 AM (RTH open) shows the missing puzzle piece—the pre-market session that delivers the momentum into the 9:30 open. (0:24:13–0:26:36)

### Time as the Most Important Factor
- **Kind:** rule · context
- **What ICT says:** On the last day of the week (Friday), time becomes the dominant constraint. Price must hit its targets within the trading session before the 5:00 PM close. This means price cannot afford to waste time consolidating or retracing; it must use every minute to reach its targets. Session opens (especially pre-market 7:00 AM) become critical as "rocket fuel" to reach these targets before time runs out. "It only has Friday because when we close at 5:00 Eastern time, it's then you go into the weekend." (0:35:27) You can use this to rule out unlikely price moves on Friday—if a move would require too much time, it's not going to happen.
- **How to use:** On Friday, ask: "What can price accomplish between now and 5:00 PM?" Use this to filter out trades that would require more time than available. Example: on Friday afternoon, don't expect price to make multiple round-trips; expect straight runs to targets only.
- **Example in this video:** Friday morning to close. Price had to hit 28,512 and retrace into TGIF before 5:00 PM close. The pre-market 7:00 AM start was critical to allow enough time for the full week's delivery before close. (0:29:02–0:38:41)

### Market Maker Sell Model (MMSM)
- **Kind:** definition · model · distribution pattern
- **What ICT says:** A pattern of price rallying (smart money reversing and going long), distributing (selling to retail buyers), then reversing down. Opposite of the market maker buy model. Marked by (1) rally, (2) consolidation/distribution at higher levels, (3) reversal down. "This is my money reversal, low-risk cell, first stage distribution, second stage distribution, biggest part of the run rate to weekly target." (0:23:25) Identified using body placement, order flow, and levels.
- **How to use:** When you identify the MMSM pattern forming on intraday charts, prepare to short after distribution phase completes. Use the distribution zone as a resistance level; shorts placed above it will reverse down into lower targets.
- **Example in this video:** Intraday chart, 5-minute timeframe. Price rallies and distributes into the sell-side zone, then reverses lower. (0:23:25)
- **Timestamps:**

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- Use the daily chart and weekly chart ONLY to identify the weekly framework. Do not use lower timeframes (1m, 5m, 15m) for big-picture planning; use them only for execution.
- Weekly suspension block defines the range; identify it Monday and it will dictate the entire week.
- If the new week opening gap is premium (above Friday's close) and the suspension block is bullish-looking, the week is likely bearish (reversal down expected).
- CPI/PPI days (typically Monday–Wednesday) are high-resistance; keep size small or sit out.
- Thursday and Friday (after news passes) are low-resistance; use normal or large size, trust your bias.
- TGIF: On Friday, price closes in 20–30% retracement of the weekly high/low range, no exceptions. (Rare exceptions occur on news days or if a new weekly range-extending move happens.)
- Pre-market session (7:00–9:00 AM) runs relative equal highs/lows from previous sessions; this is fastest-moving time of the day and sets momentum for the 9:30 RTH open.
- On Friday, don't expect price to waste time on consolidations; it must use each session open (pre-market 7:00, RTH 9:30) to gain ground toward the weekly low (or weekly high, if bullish week).
- Overnight highs and lows (electronic trading hours) are hunted in regular trading hours; they will be run and taken out.
- Relative equal highs/lows are not traded before the main draw-on-liquidity target on Friday; don't expect price to rally to the 28,512-adjacent highs before hitting the 28,512 low.

## Glossary Candidates

`Weekly Suspension Block` — A bullish or bearish fair value gap at the open of a new week; an inefficiency with volume imbalances at high and low that defines the weekly trading range.

`New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)` — The gap between Friday's close and Sunday/Monday's open; used to set weekly bias (premium = bearish likely; discount = bullish likely).

`TGIF (Thank God It's Friday)` — Friday's predictable close zone, typically 20–30% retracement of the weekly range above the weekly low.

`Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions` — Days after major news passes (Thu/Fri typically); fast, directional candles with large ranges and minimal retracement; "easy money" days.

`High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions` — Days with major economic news (Mon/Tue/Wed typically); choppy, back-and-forth price action; difficult for new traders.

`Pre-Market Session` — 7:00–9:00 AM ET (GlobeEx trading); where overnight relative equal highs/lows are hunted and fastest directional moves occur before 9:30 RTH open.

`Jigsaw Puzzle Concept` — The idea that electronic trading hours fill in gaps left by regular trading hours; understanding both sessions' price action is necessary to see complete market structure.

`Jesus Swing` — A reversal candle at an open (typically 9:30 AM) that wicks up (attempting to reach a level) but closes low, failing to sustain; signals imminent reversal down.

`Market Maker Sell Model (MMSM)` — Pattern of rally → distribution → reversal down; identifies where smart money is exiting long positions and signals imminent downtrend.

`Time as Critical Factor` — On Friday, the time remaining until 5:00 PM close is the dominant constraint; price cannot waste time on consolidations and must use every minute and each session open to reach targets.
