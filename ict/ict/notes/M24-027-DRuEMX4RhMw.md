### Opening range (9:30–10:00 ET) as the algorithmic 30-minute window; standard deviations of it  (aliases: opening range, first 30 minutes, "algorithm's opening range")
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening range is a 30-minute algorithmic period, 9:30 to 10:00 ET. In it the algorithm sets up initial buy side and sell side and then operates off them. 15 minutes of trading is not enough information for the daily range.
  - What to look for in it: initial buy-side and sell-side liquidity, plus any initial PD array (a significant break, or any imbalance: BISI or SIBI, "either one"). That gives context for a possible Silver Bullet trade at 10 o'clock (stated briefly; caption gives no more detail).
  - You can run standard deviations on the opening range: take the first-30-minute highest high and lowest low and project them; he says those projections give intermediate-term and long-term daily/intersession highs and lows ("don't take my word for it, see it for yourself").
  - The first FVG that forms after the open (here on the 1m, with the 9:33 candle) is the first presentation FVG; "the opening range essentially is that first presentation fair value gap".
- **How to identify:** Mark the 9:30 and 10:00 verticals; take the high and low of that window; mark the first 1m FVG.
- **How to use:** Treat the window as information gathering, not a place to bet the farm ("you're not trying to secure the low or high of the day"); then project the range's standard deviations for targets; remove the 10:00 verticals once the method has been communicated.

### Morning-session protocol after a large-range day  (aliases: "keep your fingers out of the mashed potatoes", large range day rule, candlestick range theory)
- **Kind:** rule (core lesson)
- **What ICT says:**
  - When the previous daily candle is a large-range candle (bullish or bearish), the next morning session through lunch is high resistance: price "marks time" (barcoding/painting) for the first 30 minutes, chops traders, and setups are "more prone to fail". He says he has followed this since 1995 and calls it one candlestick range theory; "I never deviate".
  - For beginners the protocol is: do not trade the morning session after a large-range day (that means all the way through lunch). Write it in your journal; "you have permission to sit still".
  - The crowd that watched yesterday's big move wants to be on the same side; smart money lays a "landmine field" in the morning to punish exactly that side (novices follow signals, chat rooms, news).
  - Advanced students may have trades available, but only if they know their model; students like his son should not.
  - The richest traders have written plans of inactivity (planned stillness); trade only when probability is in your favor; recognize the condition, turn charts off, walk away, and return for the next session.
  - If you did trade that morning (even demo) and got lucky, you will mistake luck for skill and repeat it with real money.
- **How to identify:** Daily chart shows a monster candle yesterday (here NQ's large up-close day of Sept 11).
- **How to use:** Skip the AM; come back at about 1:15–1:30 PM ET and use the PM routine below.
- **Example in this video:** The whole session: NQ opens near unchanged from yesterday's close, drops, rallies in small bull flags, then sweeps the low again, exactly the chop he predicts.

### Initial low of the day as a trap (Judas swing as deferred market delivery)  (aliases: Judas swing, turtle soup, "breadcrumbs", engineered sentiment)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - On a large-range up day, after the open smart money lets early buyers in with little bull flags ("breadcrumbs"), then drops price below the initial low of the day. Longs have their stops below that low; supply/demand traders, Fibonacci retracement buyers, harmonic traders, moving-average crossovers and oversold indicators all buy or stop there. The initial low is "always a suspect low".
  - The drop takes out the sell stops, which lets smart money accumulate longs against that liquidity. It also discourages the crowd (they flip to short), which is the same as a sell stop being hit.
  - You can use it as a turtle soup or as a scalp: trade down into the low.
  - He calls the pattern "your Judas swing... a deferred market delivery": initial drop to a short-term low, rally taking out a short-term swing high (breakout traders buy; their only logical stop is the initial low), then the low is taken.
  - "Not buying and selling pressure, but buying and selling liquidity": what matters is where the orders sit.
  - Keep the bigger structure in view: if the high-timeframe narrative is bullish, the sweep is accumulation; the opposite (sweep highs, then lower) if bearish. When short-term highs are taken in a bearish narrative, it is smart money building shorts.
- **How to identify:** Initial low forms within first 30 minutes of a day after a big up-close daily candle; bull flags; stops obviously below.
- **How to use:** Do not buy the first bounce. Wait for the low to be swept, with price reaching into a PD array (see hourly BISI below).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 2024-09-12: a drop to a short-term low after the open; rally to the upper end of the opening range gap; then price sweeps the initial low again into the lower quadrant of the 1-hour BISI, "anyone that's long just had their bread toasted"; the low-of-day sell side is taken at about 0:26 and again later.

### Grading an hourly BISI by quadrants (25/50/75): trade to the CE or lower quadrant, not the full low  (aliases: BISI = "buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency", blue box, consequent encroachment, lower quadrant)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (central technical teaching)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He marks a 1-hour BISI (the blue shaded area) defined by one candle's high to another's low, with a big candle passing through it. He drops a fib on that gap at 25%, 50% and 75%: the "upper quadrant", the CE (50%) and the "lower quadrant". "The mechanism it's calculating on is this high, this low and these levels."
  - Core rule: you do not want to see an inefficiency fully filled. If price wants to go higher, it will not touch the low boundary. Smart money looks for the lower quadrant or halfway point; ideally you see sensitivity in the upper half; if price goes below the CE, it must respect the lower quadrant and leave the lower boundary intact.
  - He earlier (0:06:07) repeats his 2024 teaching: when an inefficiency is bullish, don't expect complete overlap of the single candle that makes the inefficiency; expect sensitivity in the upper half.
  - He says liquidity voids being filled completely is not what he teaches (he contrasts with other teachers); price can trade all the way to the low, but that is not what he looks for.
  - A swing low is only interesting to him if it forms at a meaningful level: here, the swing low forms at the high / upper quadrant of the hourly BISI, so the liquidity below it is the interest. Swing high at the top of the opening range gap similarly has sell side beneath it.
  - Narrative: a Twitter poll: drop at the open into the hourly BISI, then work toward a smooth relative-equal-highs level at about 19,695 (4–5 highs from last week that "just did not get through"; he doesn't require it be reached that day; a logical draw because everyone forgot it).
- **How to identify:** On the 1H chart: candle high vs candle low gap; fib 25/50/75 over it; mark where short-term lows/highs form relative to the quadrants.
- **How to use:** Entries/targets:
  - Levels of interest: the CE and the lower quadrant, and any smaller inefficiency between those two prices.
  - Scale in: one contract at the CE, one at the lower quadrant. Stop at least 2–3 ticks below the BISI's low (the full inefficiency low), because it can touch the low even if you don't want it to.
  - Don't buy just because price is cheap and at the level; wait until it "gives enough information" (not full confirmation, "just a little bit more"), because price could still drop to the low of the BISI.
  - Prop firms may forbid averaging in / adding to a losing trade; in that case wait for the signature described below instead of scaling.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 2024-09-12: hourly BISI and a 5-minute BISI below the opening range gap; price opens and drops into the 5m BISI's upper half (sensitivity), rallies to the opening range gap high, drops again into the 1H BISI lower quadrant (sweeping the initial low of day), then rallies; bodies respect the lower quadrant.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:04:17](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=257) — BISI on hourly, quadrants on it
  - [0:06:07](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=367) — 2024 teaching: sensitivity in the upper half, no complete overlap
  - [0:39:29](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=2369) — why this swing low matters (forms at the high of the BISI)

### Immediate rebalance, vacuum block and order block sequence (worked example)  (aliases: immediate rebalance, vacuum block, order block theory, opening price, change in state of delivery)
- **Kind:** definition + example (several new/applied terms)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price in the lower half of the gap: when price trades to the CE and returns up into the old low without reaching the midpoint of that smaller imbalance, it is weak (the most sensitive points are in the lower half), so it should go on to the next level, the lower quadrant of the 1H BISI.
  - Immediate rebalance: a candle opens and trades straight to the prior candle's low (here a discount wick that rebalances the gap formed by the open); annotate it. He counts three things at once: immediate rebalance + discount wick CE + nearness to the BISI's CE, then price hits the lower quadrant and runs slightly past it.
  - After the lower quadrant is swept, price trades higher: the candle's opening price plus a volume imbalance are two PD arrays. The series of down-closed candles is an order block ("order block theory, there's a chapter coming out"); the opening price of the order block is the change in the state of delivery.
  - Vacuum block (as used here): a separation between one candle's close and the next candle's open; there is no price between the two except the wick. The wick trades down to the prior close — that's the immediate rebalance, because the wick is the gap.
  - Price then delivers up to the first (highest) of the down-closed candles, the high of the whole order block; the market opens, trades into the order block and sends price higher to a swing high at the high end of the opening range gap, taking buy side.
  - Rule on timing: because the lowest desired level (BISI's lower quadrant) had already been traded to and slightly breached, the bounce from there is the valid reaction.
- **How to identify:** Series of down-closed candles at the low; the first up move's opening price; a close-to-open separation (vacuum); wick filling the separation.
- **How to use:** Entry is the order block / opening price after the sweep of the lower quadrant, with the stop under the sweep low (his stop guidance above); target the ORG high / swing high above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ ~10:35–10:45 ET (video 1:05–1:09): price hits the lower quadrant of the 1H BISI, spikes slightly past it, rallies; a candle opens with a gap above the prior close, wicks back down to that close (immediate rebalance), then rallies to the top of the down-closed series (order block high) and on to the opening range gap high.
- **Timestamps:**

  - [1:07:33](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=4053) — order block, opening price, change in state of delivery

### Two-layer buy-side / sell-side routine for the PM session (starting 1:30 PM ET)  (aliases: PM session, afternoon session protocol)
- **Kind:** rule / entry framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - Don't need to trade the morning session at all; the PM session is "always" the easiest session for index futures because the day's range is already printed. You won't get the lion's share, but it is ripe for consistent setups. After a large-range day expect the better trades from 1:30 PM ET into the last hour.
  - Routine: come back around 1:15–1:30 PM ET. On the 15-minute chart note the highest high and lowest low of the day; drop to 5m (or 1m) and mark the next lower high and next higher low inside that range. That gives two layers of buy side (day high and the lower high) and two layers of sell side (day low and the higher low).
  - Before 1:30, write down in the journal (with the time) which side you think price will gravitate to; then see what happens as the PM session unfolds into about 2:00 PM. Chances are it takes one of them, not both.
  - Same method for London: use the previous session; previous day's high/low and the high/low inside it formed up to 2:00 AM ET.
  - Last hour: ICT says price prices in the extreme high/low of the day with big expansion candles (an open near the low and a close at the high); then in the last ~45 minutes it comes off the high/low into settlement; four macros in the last hour (five if you count the 2:00 bleeding into 3:00). He pushes back on someone who said no new high/low gets made in the last hour (the caption's "that is absolute" is unclear).
  - If the AM kept the low of day intact and consolidated, it is "all positive for the afternoon". He also expects an eventual reach for the 19,695 relative equal highs.
- **How to identify:** 15m day high/low; 5m/1m nearest lower high / higher low inside.
- **How to use:** Predict side, journal it, trade once price shows structure toward it; he says the same process is what he does every afternoon ("never changes").

### Multi-timeframe monitoring and building the narrative from other traders' failure
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:**
  - He cycles his eyes through daily, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, plus a matrix of 5/4/3/2/1-minute charts and another matrix of 45s/30s/15s/5s charts.
  - For each timeframe he asks how traders on that timeframe would interpret it and where their stop would sit; the goal is to see where less-informed participants "fail today" and where he can see high-probability delivery to a specific level.
  - Don't over-read retail patterns (pennants, double/triple touches, trend lines); the question is why price should go up: buyers need a discount price, which the algorithm affords.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:47](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1787), [0:30:34](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1834), [0:32:11](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1931)

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET (30-minute algorithmic window); 15 minutes is not enough. Previous settlement = 4:15 PM ET.
- Look for half the opening range gap to fill in the first 30 minutes. Small ORG (about 15 handles) = muddy, uninteresting; shrinking gap pre-market = expect a muddy AM.
- After a large-range daily candle: do not trade the next morning session through lunch (beginners); return at about 1:15–1:30 PM ET.
- Inefficiency grading: fib 25/50/75; trade to CE / lower quadrant, not the full low; scale in 1 contract at CE and 1 at the lower quadrant; stop at least 2–3 ticks below the inefficiency low.
- Don't buy simply because price is at a cheap level; wait for "a little bit more" information.
- Initial low of the day on a large-range day is always a suspect low (trap); expect it to be swept.
- PM routine: day high/low plus inner lower high / higher low (two layers each side); journal the predicted side with a time stamp; PM unfolds toward 2:00 PM; last hour has four macros (five counting the 2:00–3:00 bleed); last ~45 minutes comes off the extreme into settlement.
- Daily draw referenced: smooth relative equal highs near 19,695 (4–5 highs) on NQ.
- Fall 2024: expects high volatility into year-end ("fun but dangerous"): lower leverage per trade, but trade frequency on lower timeframes can be higher.

## Glossary candidates
`BISI / SIBI` — buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency / sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (bullish / bearish FVG) as used by ICT.
`Quadrants (upper / lower)` — the 25% and 75% marks of a gap or inefficiency drawn with a fib; with the 50% CE they are the working levels.
`First presentation FVG` — the first FVG that forms after the 9:30 open (e.g. at the 9:33 candle on the 1m).
`Immediate rebalance` — a candle that opens away from the prior close and wicks back to fill the separation at once.
`Vacuum block` — a close-to-open separation between two candles with no price between them except the wick.
`Change in state of delivery` — the opening price of the order block's candle series, where delivery flips.
`Deferred market delivery` — his description of a Judas swing that unfolds in steps instead of one fake move.
`Barcoding` — price marking time with sideways back-and-forth candles, typical of the first 30 minutes after a large-range day.
`Suspect low` — the initial low of the day on a large-range up day, expected to be swept as a trap.
`Planned stillness` — a written plan of not trading in unfavourable conditions.
