## Concepts

### Parameter-Based Entries (Dual Scenario Setup)
- **Kind:** rule · entry model · risk management
- **What ICT says:** When an economic news event (CPI, PPI, NFP) is about to occur, you don't know which direction the market will move. Rather than guessing, set up two possible parameter zones and wait for price to tell you which one is active. Example: "Either drops down here to go up to new week opening gap, and that's the run for CPI, or it goes up there to drop back down into here." (0:11:19–0:12:07) Whichever zone price enters first becomes the active direction for the trade.
- **How to identify:** On the daily/hourly chart, identify the key PD arrays and supports/resistances surrounding the current price. Mark the two most likely paths price could take after the news. Set one parameter above, one below.
- **How to use:** Do not trade before the news based on guesses. Wait for the 8:30 release. Watch which zone price enters. Once it's clear, build a position in that direction. Use the other zone as your invalidation level.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday, 8:30 AM PPI release. ICT sets up two parameters: either price drops into a lower FVG zone or rallies into the new week opening gap. Price initially runs up (new week opening gap chosen), so ICT shorts on the way down from there. (0:01:49–0:04:16, 0:11:19–0:12:07)

### Staging (Creating Short-Term Highs to Take Buy Stops)
- **Kind:** definition · order flow concept · market structure
- **What ICT says:** Market makers create successive short-term highs on the way up, each higher than the previous. These are "stages" that trigger buy-stop orders (traders who want to short wait above each high and place buy stops there to enter long positions, assuming a breakout). Smart money sells into each successive buy-stop zone, taking them out, then the market reverses. "This is staging. Meaning that they'll run it up, create a short-term high, and then that liquidity gets taken, and it becomes counterparty for shorts." (0:04:48) Each short-term high becomes a potential reversal point.
- **How to identify:** On intraday charts, look for a series of ascending candles where each new candle high is slightly above the previous. Each peak represents a stage where buy-stops are clustered.
- **How to use:** When you see staging, do not go long into the new highs. Instead, prepare to short. Buy-stops will be taken out by smart money selling, triggering the reversal. Use the highest stage reached as a resistance level and a short entry (on the reversal below it).
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday morning: price rallies with successive higher highs creating stages. Traders place buy-stops above each high. Smart money accumulates shorts, then reverses price down below all the stages. (0:04:48–0:05:51)
- **Timestamps:**

  - [0:05:51](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=351) — "With the expectation that the market's not going to breach the halfway point... they're accumulating short positions."

### Building Positions in Lower Half of Range (Bearish Bias)
- **Kind:** rule · position management
- **What ICT says:** When you're bearish and shorting, build your position in the lower half of a key range (e.g., below the midpoint/consequent encroachment of a PD array). This placement protects you from bodies breaking into the upper half (which would signal bullish reversal). "I want to be selling short in the lower half of this cuz it's going to protect and deny any trespass with the bodies in the upper half." (0:13:46) Bodies in the lower half confirm continuation downward.
- **How to use:** Identify the midpoint/consequent encroachment of your target range. Place your shorts below that line and add to them as price stays below. If price creeps above, take profits and re-evaluate.
- **Example in this video:** New week opening gap range from ~29,570 to lower level. Midpoint around 29,570. ICT builds shorts between the lower quadrant and upper octant of this range, staying well below midpoint. (0:13:46–0:14:32)

### Quadrant & Octant Zones as Pyramiding Targets
- **Kind:** rule · entry refinement
- **What ICT says:** Use the quadrant and octant levels of daily/weekly ranges as precise zones to add to positions. Each level is a target to add more contracts. "I'm treating it basically as a premium PD array, and I'm using the order flow ideas... building my position in between the lower quadrant and the upper octant." (0:13:46) Within this zone, pyramid (add) at each octant or quadrant sub-level.
- **How to use:** Mark the quadrants (25% and 75%) and octants (other quarter lines) of a key daily range. As price moves within your bias, add contracts at each level. This allows you to scale in gradually rather than all at once.
- **Example in this video:** ICT adds contracts at multiple levels between the lower quadrant and upper octant zone of the new week opening gap range. (0:14:32–0:15:22)

### Taking Partials for Psychology / "Paying the Trader"
- **Kind:** rule · position management · psychology
- **What ICT says:** When you feel concern creeping in during a trade—worry that it might reverse—take off a small partial (1–2 contracts out of many). This gives you a "reward" (realized profit), eases psychological burden, and lets you hold the remaining contracts without fear. "Just take one of your contracts off. If you feel weird about something... Soon as you start feeling that, the only remedy is either completely bail or take something off... That little tiny little cookie, that reward." (0:18:31) ICT calls this "paying the trader"—giving yourself small wins to stay in the trade.
- **How to use:** When doubt creeps in, don't close the entire trade. Take off 1–3 contracts (depending on size). Lock in some profit. This psychological relief allows you to stay with the main move and capture larger gains.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple times during the day, ICT takes partials to satisfy doubts, then continues holding remaining contracts. (0:18:31, referenced multiple times from 0:17:44–0:19:18)
- **Timestamps:**

### Bearish Suspension Block (Revisited)

### Limit Order Placement Below Key Levels
- **Kind:** rule · order placement
- **What ICT says:** Place limit orders just below key PD arrays (FVGs, relative equal lows, sell-side pools) rather than at the exact level. This gives your order a slightly better chance of fill if price spikes through the level. "I had my limit order right in here... half of that would have afforded me the final exit." (0:19:18)
- **How to use:** When scaling out, place limit orders 2–5 ticks below your actual target level, not on it. This improves fills without requiring manual execution.

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- Never guess direction on CPI/PPI/NFP days; use parameter-based entries (two possible scenarios) and wait for price to tell you the direction.
- Staging is a warning signal: when market is creating successive higher highs, do NOT chase them; prepare to short instead.
- Only trade the news after it's released; don't trade in anticipation of it.
- Build shorts in the lower half of a range (below midpoint); build longs in the upper half (above midpoint).
- Pyramid within quadrant/octant zones; add at each level, not all at once.
- Take partials when doubt creeps in; this psychological relief allows you to hold the remaining position with confidence.
- Limit orders below key levels get better fills than orders at the exact level.
- Precision takes 5–10 years of focused effort; don't expect to execute like this within a year or two.
- Personal responsibility and self-control are non-negotiable for profitable trading. Without them, no amount of education will help.

## Glossary Candidates

`Parameter-Based Entry` — When news drivers create uncertain direction, set up two possible parameter zones and trade whichever zone price enters first, rather than guessing the direction.

`Staging` — Creating successive short-term highs to trigger buy-stop orders at each level; once bought, smart money reverses, leaving buyers trapped. A warning to not chase upward moves.

`Pyramiding` — Adding to a position gradually at multiple price levels (quadrants, octants, key levels) rather than entering all at once.

`Paying the Trader` — Taking a small partial profit when doubt/concern arises during a trade; gives psychological relief and allows holding remaining contracts.

`Quadrant/Octant Pyramiding Zones` — The divided quarters of a range (25%, 50%, 75% lines) used as specific add-on levels when building positions.

`Limit Order Below Key Levels` — Placing exit limit orders 2–5 ticks below target levels (not at them) to improve fill probability while still achieving your target.

`Bearish Suspension Block` — A suspension block with initial bearish bias; once broken below, it acts as resistance above (inversion).

`Personal Responsibility & Discipline` — Non-negotiable traits for profitable trading; without them, no education helps. Requires planning strategy in advance and adhering to it even when uncomfortable.

`Craft Mastery Timeline` — Achieving precision trading execution requires 5–10 years of focused, deliberate practice. There is no shortcut.
