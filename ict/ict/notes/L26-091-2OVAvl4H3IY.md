### Three Drives Pattern (Three Indians Pattern)
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** A classic pattern where price creates three successive highs, each above the prior buy-side liquidity pool. After the third high is made (especially if above prior day's high or a major resistance), the pattern is complete and a breakdown is expected. This is not ICT's invention; it's a well-known pattern documented in "Street Smarts" books (called "three Indians pattern" there, though ICT prefers "three drives"). When price is extremely overbought (no indicator needed), the three drives pattern is a high-confidence short setup.
- **How to identify:** Mark three consecutive higher highs; identify the buy-side liquidity pool that each high exceeds. Count: high 1, high 2, high 3. Once high 3 is confirmed, expect reversal.
- **How to use:** After confirming three drives, short at or near high 3 and target the sell-side liquidity pool(s) below. On all-time highs, add further confirmation by checking daily bias and prior day's high structure.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24, 2026. Price had been trending higher, creating multiple highs. Thursday's high (high 2 or 3) was traded above Wednesday's daily high, confirming the three drives pattern. ICT shorted at Thursday's high with target at relative equal lows below, which worked beautifully.

### Shorting Previous Day's High (Daily High as Resistance)
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price trades above the prior day's daily high (especially on consecutive days), it is a prime area to short. The reason: it attracts breakout traders and bull-flag traders expecting continuation; they place buy stops above the daily high. Smart money runs these stops, then drops. This is a structural setup that works across market conditions (bull or bear market); it is not specific to all-time highs. Targeting sell-side liquidity pools below the daily high is the natural target.
- **How to identify:** Pull the prior day's daily high from the chart; watch when price trades above it. If price is above it and showing weakness (bodies not advancing, pullbacks forming), it is ripe for shorting.
- **How to use:** Short entries at or just below the prior day's daily high. Use sell-side liquidity pools below as targets. Confirm entry with PD array structure (fair value gaps, order blocks, relative equal lows that align below the daily high).
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24. Wednesday's daily high was at a specific level. Price traded above it on Thursday morning, triggering retail breakout buys. ICT shorted at that level and targeted the sell-side pool(s) below (ultimately hitting the relative equal lows near 27,069 / 26,805 range across the week).

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) at 50% Midpoint as Structural Reference
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When a volume imbalance (fair value gap) forms, its midpoint (50% level) can be used as a reference point during the trade. If price is expected to go lower, and a FVG high is at the midpoint, that midpoint becomes a resistance level. Bodies respecting the lower half of the gap confirm bearish order flow.
- **How to identify:** Mark the FVG high and low; calculate the midpoint (50%). Track whether price respects or violates this midpoint.
- **How to use:** Use the FVG midpoint as a reference for "is the structure holding?" If bodies cannot touch the midpoint on rallies, bearish bias is confirmed. If bodies touch the midpoint but stay in the lower half, the move lower is likely to continue.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24. A FVG was marked; its midpoint was used as a reference to confirm that bodies were staying in the lower half, supporting the short thesis.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:46](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=526) — FVG midpoint identified · [0:15:10](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=910) — fairness and context of FVG interpretation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The midpoint is not a hard stop/target; it is a reference for assessing order flow strength.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) Confirmation via Body Placement
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When a bullish fair value gap is breached and becomes an inversion FVG, bodies must stay in the lower half to confirm bearish intention. Specifically: if a body touches the consequent encroachment (CE) and stays in the lower half, that is textbook confirmation. If a body penetrates the midpoint or upper half, the iFVG is being violated (weakens the bearish case).
- **How to identify:** Mark iFVG; observe the body placement on each candle as price approaches and tests it.
- **How to use:** Only take the short if bodies confirm by staying in the lower half. If a body closes above the midpoint or touches the upper half, re-evaluate or exit the position.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24. An iFVG was formed from a bullish FVG; bodies tested it and stayed in the lower half, confirming the breakdown. Bodies later "shared the same space" (consolidated without advancing upper), which ICT interpreted as a signal to take partials.

### Sell-Side Liquidity Pool (SSLP) / Engineering Liquidity
- **Kind:** definition + psychology
- **What ICT says:** When price leaves large wicks (especially down and back up), retail traders using candlestick analysis see those wicks as "bullish accumulation" and protection for their shorts' stop-losses. Smart money capitalizes on this: they use those wicks as areas where they know retail will have stops placed. They then "engineer liquidity" at these levels, raiding them briefly before continuing in the original direction. The wicks are not bullish; they are bait.
- **How to identify:** Look for a down wick with a body that closes well above it (creating a large wick to the downside). Mark that wick; expect price to revisit it and run the stops clustered above the wick.
- **How to use:** The wick area is a sell-side liquidity pool. Use it as a reference: price will likely be attracted to it on retracements. Do not mistake it for support; it is a raid zone. Build your short target accounts for price to attack these wicks and then continue lower.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24–Thursday. A sell-side liquidity pool (from wicks) was identified; price returned to it during the short move, briefly rallied into it (causing rallies against the short), then broke lower. ICT uses this knowledge to trail stops and manage partials.

### Body Consolidation ("Sharing the Same Space") as Signal to Take Partials
- **Kind:** refinement / execution
- **What ICT says:** During a downtrend on a short, if candlestick bodies start "sharing the same space" (i.e., the high of one body is near the high of the previous body, no new lows being made), it is a warning signal. It suggests order flow is waning. When this happens, especially as you approach a target, take partials off. This is because the consolidation may precede a reversal; you do not want to be caught holding through a sharp rally.
- **How to identify:** Watch the bodies as price approaches your target. If highs are not advancing lower (bodies are repeating the same high range), mark it.
- **How to use:** Use this as a discretionary trigger to take partial profit. Rule of thumb: as bodies consolidate, take 20-50% off depending on proximity to target. This locks in profit before a potential reversal.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24–Thursday. Price was moving lower beautifully, but as it approached the target, bodies started sharing the same space (consolidating horizontally). ICT began taking partials off at each new low, preserving profit while allowing the position to run if it continued.

### Partial Profit-Taking: "Running Down Equity"
- **Kind:** rule + execution
- **What ICT says:** On short positions approaching targets, take a small partial every time a new lower low is made. This is called "running down equity." For example, if short 10 lots and aiming for a target 100 handles lower, take 1 lot off at new lows, then at the next new low, take another lot. By the time you hit the target, you've taken off 5–6 lots, leaving 4–5 to run. This ensures you lock in profits while still capturing best-case scenarios.
- **How to identify:** Track new lows as price moves lower; each new lower low is an opportunity.
- **How to use:** Mechanical approach: short entry = 10 lots. New low 1: close 1 lot. New low 2: close 1 lot. And so on. At target: close the remainder.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 24–Thursday. ICT entered the short and took partials at new lower lows; by the time price hit the target level (26,805), most of the position was already closed, and ICT missed the final 100+ handles but was content because profits were locked in before the final leg.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:08](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1388) — "running down equity" / taking partials
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** There is never an unprofitable partial profit; always take them.

## Glossary candidates
`Three drives pattern` — three successive higher highs (often above prior day's high or major resistance), signaling exhaustion and imminent reversal; called "three Indians pattern" in some literature.
`Shorting the daily high` — targeting a short entry at or near the prior day's daily high, capitalizing on retail breakout buys and subsequent smart money raid of those stops.
`Engineering liquidity` — smart money's practice of briefly raiding stop-loss clusters (at wicks/resistance areas) before continuing in the original direction; creates the illusion of false breaks.
`Sell-side liquidity pool (SSLP)` — clusters of stop-losses or retail buy interest above a swing low; bait for market makers to raid before continuing lower.
`Terminus target` — the ultimate price level targeted on a trade; often posted in advance on X for accountability.
`654 Club` — inside joke: the last three digits of a futures price target (e.g., 27,654); shorthand for specific target levels.
`Running down equity` — taking small partials at each new low in a downtrend to lock profits while allowing the position to run.
`Body consolidation / Sharing the same space` — when candlestick bodies fail to advance (highs repeat without new lows being made); signals waning order flow and opportunity to take partials.
`Opening Range Gap (ORG) Consequent Encroachment` — the first body to breach the ORG high/low; a major structural reference for the entire day.
`Fair value gap midpoint` — the 50% level of a volume imbalance; used to assess whether bodies are respecting the lower or upper half and confirm order flow direction.
