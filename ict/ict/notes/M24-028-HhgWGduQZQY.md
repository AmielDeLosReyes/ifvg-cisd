## Summary
First day of "entries week," where ICT teaches his son (Caleb) and the audience exactly how to pick and trade a Fair Value Gap on a lower time frame (1m / 15s / 1s NQ). He defines the three candle roles in an FVG (candle 1 = conservative stop, candle 2 = the framing/"trigger" candle and aggressive stop, candle 3 = the entry candle), the exact entry price (candle 3's edge plus/minus one tick, never a "zone"), and how to grade the gap by halves/quadrants so you know whether it is likely to hold. He explains why a bearish gap formed straight off a Judas-swing open (in the lower half of the drop) was NOT a short, how failed gaps become inversion FVGs, how wicks are graded exactly like FVGs, and why the opening range gap midpoint (CE) is the first draw (70% within 30 minutes). He ends with a live paper-trade on a 1-minute bullish FVG (low draw down, hit first target in about 10 minutes) and a drill protocol: one micro contract, demo, journal time-to-target and draw down, at least 15-20 handles of range from entry to first target.

## Concepts

### Fair Value Gap — three-candle anatomy and candle roles  (aliases ICT uses: "inefficiency", "sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (SIBI) / buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (BISI)", "the framing candle")
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A valid FVG is always three candles. Candle 2 (the middle one) is the displacement/"trigger" candle. It is the candle you watch to see whether the gap stays open.
  - Candle 1 and candle 3 are the outer candles. The gap is the space between candle 1's extreme and candle 3's extreme that candle 2 delivered through, with no overlap.
  - Candle 3 is the entry candle. Candle 2 "frames" the gap: it is the area price should come back and revisit.
  - A gap is not a zone. There are specific price levels: the high/low of the outer candles, the midpoint (CE), and the quadrants.
  - Once candle 4 opens, you have a valid reason to anticipate a return into the gap. It may never come back; if price keeps going, let it.
  - A valid gap shows certain "signatures." ICT looks for all of them; a gap with only a few can still be used for drills, but a bad result in a drill is not a failure (see Process).
- **How to identify:**
  - Bullish: candle 1 moves and delivers price, candle 2 delivers price higher ("that's key"), candle 3 trades higher than candle 2 and candle 3's low does not touch or overlap candle 1's high. The gap runs from candle 1's high to candle 3's low.
  - Bearish: mirror image. Candle 3's high is the bottom of the gap and candle 1's low is the top.
  - Draw the gap including any volume imbalance (see separate concept).
- **How to use:**
  - Stop loss (bearish): aggressive/max-leverage stop is one tick above candle 2's high. Conservative stop for a one-contract trader who does not want to be shaken out is candle 1's high (mirror for bullish: candle 2's low = aggressive, candle 1's low = conservative).
  - Entry (bearish): candle 3's high, or one tick below it if "100% certain." Entry (bullish): candle 3's low plus one tick.
  - See "FVG entry model" and "Quadrant grading" below.
- **Example in this video:** Hypothetical hand-drawn candles first (bearish, then reversed to bullish, ~0:18–0:45), then repeated on the live NQ 1m chart on 9/16/2024 (see live trade concept).
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:01](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1021) three candles make an FVG; [0:18:38](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1118) candle 2 vs candle 1 stop; [0:25:38](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1538) candle roles recap; [0:40:11](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2411) bullish version.

### FVG entry model — candle 3's edge ±1 tick, limit order, and not a "zone"  (aliases: "low-hanging fruit entry", "ICT entry")
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Entry for a bearish FVG: candle 3's high, minus one tick (a limit sell just below it if you are very sure price will drop). Entry for a bullish FVG: candle 3's low plus one tick.
  - ICT often places entries just ahead of the level ("a little bit ahead of a fair value gap or a bearish order block") so he gets filled. He does not like missing entries but is not worried about missing a trade: "81 ways" into the same trade.
  - He insists this is why ICT is not "supply and demand": you do not "trade a zone," you trade specific levels (candle 3's edge, CE, quadrant, candle 1/2 extreme). Shading a gap on a chart is only a visual.
  - Learners should first practice the easy objective entry (candle 3's edge ±1 tick) with one contract. Practice with market orders first so you learn timing and anticipatory price reading. The professional and more efficient way is limit orders. Exits should be limits too; entries on limits, or on rare occasions buying/selling on a stop.
  - Market orders are really only for getting out of something to protect money.
- **How to identify:** n/a (see FVG anatomy).
- **How to use:** Put the limit one tick inside the gap edge on candle 3; stop per candle 2 / candle 1; add only at defined deeper levels (see pyramiding). Once price leaves the gap and runs, trail/reduce the stop; ICT says it "should not go back inside the fair value gap."
- **Example in this video:** Live bullish 1m FVG: ideal entry = candle 3's low 19,339.25 + 1 tick = 19,339.50; ICT's actual market fill was 19,342.00 (about 2.5 handles worse, about $50 per mini), see live trade concept.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:32](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1232) entry on candle 3's high or one tick below; [0:28:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1727) get filled, not zones; [0:35:16](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2116) easiest objective entry, market orders first; [0:36:05](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2165) limits are more professional.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** His market-order advice is for the learning stage; he later says he himself market-orders in when he missed a limit (see missed entries). Slight tension with "market orders only to exit"; resolved as: market-in only to capture a level you intended to be filled at.

### Quadrant grading / Consequent Encroachment of the FVG  (aliases: CE, midpoint, "upper/lower quadrant", "grading your inefficiencies")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Always grade an inefficiency: mark the midpoint (CE, the 50% level) plus the upper and lower quadrant levels (the 25%/75% marks). A Fibonacci or a rectangle with a midline gives the same thing.
  - Bullish gap: the ideal is that price only drops into the upper half (from candle 3's low down to CE) and leaves the lower half untouched. That indicates the gap is "extremely bullish" and you are on side; you shouldn't worry about the stop, it is just there to do its job.
  - Bearish gap: ideal entries form in the lower half (from candle 3's high up to CE) and the upper half (CE up to candle 1's low) stays open. It then means a very strong probability that you are right.
  - Price should spend little time in the entry half: ideally one candle, at most two, enters it, and then the run starts.
  - Wicks (not bodies) may poke through the far side of the gap; bodies should not close beyond it.
  - If candle 4 drops into the gap right after it forms and immediately starts running, that is a clue the other half will stay open and the move is on a fast track.
  - The more price moves away from the gap, the less trepidation about the stop below the gap low.
  - The CE level is "your most important level" once you have drawn the gap with its volume imbalance.
- **How to identify:** Draw FIB/rectangle from candle 1's extreme to candle 3's extreme; mark 25/50/75%.
- **How to use:** Buy in the upper half (bullish), sell in the lower half (bearish). Anything beyond CE is a weaker location: size down to one or two contracts. Use the state of the far half as the confidence gauge.
- **Example in this video:** Bullish FVG that morning on NQ 1m: price dropped only into the upper half (about 3.75 handles of draw down from a ~19,405–19,445 fill, caption figures garbled), reached just short of the upper-quadrant level and never touched CE, then rallied; ICT: "did it leave the lower half open? yes... that means it's bullish."
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585) CE and the half you want left open; [0:43:27](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2607) bullish: buy midpoint-to-low, lower half untouched; [0:53:58](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3238) best buys are in the upper half; [2:02:42](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7362) "you have to always grade your inefficiencies."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Mean threshold" is used for the 50% of an order-block candle; CE is used for the 50% of a gap/wick (see separate concept).

### Volume imbalance included in the gap drawing  (aliases: "volume imbalance")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When drawing the gap, do not use only the wick-to-wick range. If there is a volume imbalance (a separation between the bodies of consecutive candles, where the bodies do not touch) next to the gap, include it so that the drawn range, and therefore the CE, is correct. "You can't just use the wick... that's not valid."
- **How to identify:** Look for a body-to-body separation adjacent to the wick-defined gap; extend the drawn gap through it, back to candle 1.
- **How to use:** Compute the CE and quadrants from the full range including the volume imbalance; he says this gives a better determination of the CE level.
- **Example in this video:** Hypothetical diagrams at ~0:24 and ~0:42; also referenced as the "volume imbalance" the morning's low traded into at 0:14.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:00](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1440) wick-only drawing is invalid; [0:42:40](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2560) always include the small separation.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Captions are garbled here ("(caption unclear)" on exactly which two candles the body separation sits between); his statement is that the box must extend through any volume imbalance.

### Balanced Price Range (BPR)
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** Two opposing gaps that share the same price space (one offering a sell, one offering a buy) form a balanced price range; he calls it that when a bearish and bullish gap overlap. If price has gone down into one and the other holds as support, price can rally ("treat it as a discount array").
- **Example in this video:** On the morning chart, a small gap and a gap right after it isolated the price action in a BPR; he preferred it not to fill ([0:24:00](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1440)). Later, while expecting a rally to the opening range gap CE, he says two candles "shared the same space," so it is a BPR and price should find support in it and attack the buy side ([1:18:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4694)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:00](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1440), [1:18:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4694).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Mentioned in passing, not defined formally in this video.

### Scaling in / pyramiding into an FVG (demonstration only)  (aliases: adding at quadrants)
- **Kind:** entry model (advanced; explicitly not for beginners)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Illustrative bearish plan: sell 6 contracts at candle 3's high minus one tick; add 4 more when price tags the lower quadrant of the gap; if price reaches CE or goes into the upper half (deep premium), add about 2 more as long as it keeps tapping there and bodies stay inside candle 1's low.
  - Bullish mirror: add at the midpoint, at the lower quadrant, and again if price tags the low of the gap, because the stop sits below candle 2's low.
  - Wicks through the far edge of the gap are acceptable; closing bodies beyond it are not.
  - He repeatedly says: do not pyramid or build big positions when learning; "just try to trade one contract."
- **How to use:** Size down as price goes to less ideal levels (beyond CE); "anything less than that I'm going to do one or two contracts."
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1775) 6 / 4 / 2 contract illustration; [0:35:16](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2116) "do not try to pyramid" (when learning).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Contract counts are illustrative of what he does, not a recommendation.

### Missed entries — market in at the better price  (aliases: "missing the limit")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** If you miss the limit (distracted, stepped away) and price is now below your bullish entry (above your bearish entry), execute at market right away: price is offering a better price than the one you were willing to pay, so you are not "chasing." If it touches the upper quadrant or CE, buy more; anything beyond that, keep to one or two contracts, because you do not want to see price spend time in the lower (far) half.
- **How to use:** Treat the better price as a gift, but keep the same stop (below candle 2's low) and sizing discipline.
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:20](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2900) "I'm just Marketing in"; [0:49:06](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2946) why that is not chasing.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The next video (#29) is devoted to managing missed entries.

### Failed FVG becomes an inversion FVG; taking the loss as "VIP information"  (aliases: iFVG, "orange hue", reclaimed FVG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you are stopped out of an FVG trade, you are probably watching an inversion FVG form: a gap that fails and now acts in the opposite direction. You can then trade the opposite direction with half the size you lost on and make the loss back and more.
  - Losing is "paying a premium for information"; you get better view of the next price run ("VIP seating").
  - A failed bearish gap (price trades above it) can be retraced into its upper half only, then it acts as a discount array and sends price higher; a failed bullish gap that price trades down through becomes resistance (he colored it orange to show that it will "not be treated as a buying opportunity").
  - Reclaimed: when price later rallies back above an inverted gap, it can act as a support again, a "stepping stone" toward the minor buy side. He calls the gap-turned-resistance-then-reclaimed a "reclaimed fair value gap" ("easy bread and butter type setup" for his son).
- **How to identify:** The close (body) beyond the gap on the failing side; he warns that wicks into a gap are not rejection if the bodies are respecting the gap.
- **How to use:** Treat the failing-gap level as the next entry area. Do not fear stops, because a stop being hit tells you where the next trade is.
- **Example in this video:** On 9/16 NQ, a gap that had been bullish was traded down through and then served as resistance (price sold off from it); later price rallied back above it and he wanted it to act as support (inversion again) on a pullback into it ([1:22:29](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4949), [1:23:17](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4997)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:52](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1972) stop-out = premium for information; [0:52:18](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3138) stop out means inversion FVG, trade back with half size; [1:07:01](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4021) orange colour = inversion; [1:22:29](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4949) reclaimed FVG.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Sequence of which specific gap flipped how many times is hard to follow from captions; the principle is clear.

### Choosing the right FVG — context at the open, the first presented FVG, and why a short was not valid
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Which FVG to use among several is "trial and error in the beginning," and the question he gets most. The week is meant to supply the nuts and bolts.
  - He wants to "let them have" the first minutes of trading after 9:30: no prediction, watch for the first presented FVG, "let the market shake out its initial excitement."
  - Why the bearish gap at the open was not a short: (1) the market had already gapped lower from Friday's settlement (a deep discount to start with); (2) at 9:30 it sold straight down with no pullbacks (Judas swing: "you never want to chase something like that"); (3) the only gap formed was a single candle in the lower half of the range from the 9:30 open down to the swing low, with no other inefficiency, order block or short-term high above to sell from; (4) price rallied into it and printed little wicks/dojis that retail reads as rejection while the bodies respected the gap, i.e., accumulation. "Retail logic": inside an inefficiency wick = rejection, therefore short. ICT: bait.
  - The gap in the lower half of the drop is "fake"; he would rather see price trade higher out of it and pull back only into its upper half.
  - If price keeps going down through it, a bearish FVG that formed in the lower half of an already deep drop should not be sold. If instead an inefficiency was all overlapping candles and the last candle then created the gap with space above, it could be tradable ("examples later").
  - The first FVG on a 1m chart cannot include the 9:30 candle as its basis; it can appear from the 9:31 candle on. He realised mid-stream that a gap he had called the first was not (caption partly unclear). Video #29 clarifies: the earliest possible FVG has its middle candle (candle 2) at 9:31, since the 9:30 open candle can only be candle 1.
- **How to identify:** After the first swing low forms (a candle with a higher low to its left and right), at that candle's close go back through the swing for inefficiencies, order blocks, short-term highs where buy side would rest. If there is none (a single straight-line drop), the move is characteristic of a Judas swing.
- **How to use:** Do not chase. Frame a long for the draw to high of day / opening price / ORG CE; entry from the first FVG that forms after the swing low, per the entry model.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Mon 9/16/2024: opens below Friday's settlement (big opening gap down), 9:30 opens and drops in a straight line (Judas), forms a swing low, a lone bearish FVG sits in the lower half of that drop, price rallies into it and prints small wicks in it, then rallies to the minor buy side and lower quadrant of the ORG; later makes a new high (above initial HOD) and then a lower high near 19,463.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:37](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=877) first look; [1:01:19](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3679) why not a short; [1:03:42](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3822) FVG in lower half of the drop; [1:14:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4496) 9:30 candle can't be the first gap.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Contrasts with "retail logic" of selling every rejection wick; reiterates that a down-close imbalance after a Judas move is a bait.

### Wicks and candle gaps graded like FVGs; premium vs discount array  (aliases: "wick = inefficiency", "PD arrays work with wicks")
- **Kind:** refinement / new coinage-ish
- **What ICT says:** Anything that acts like an inefficiency, including a long wick, is graded the same way. A wick above market price is a premium array; a wick below price is a discount array (he doesn't use the word "tail"). For a bullish wick, the upper half should keep price from trading into the lower half. The wick's midpoint is its CE.
- **How to use:** If the next candle trades down to a wick's CE and sends price higher, that is a discount array doing its job. If the wick-CE is traded through, the setup is weakened. He uses the wick on candle 1 as an "extra credit" layer: see the next concept.
- **Timestamps:** [0:55:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3335) wicks work the same as FVGs; [1:06:13](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3973) CE of a wick as a reason to expect higher prices.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Said he gets no benefit from renaming; it is the same PD-array logic.

### Candle 1 wick as a second layer — tighter stop ("extra credit")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** If candle 1 has a wick adjacent to the gap, that wick is itself an inefficiency: two layers of gaps. The stop can be placed one tick below the wick's CE instead of below candle 2's low. After price runs, you can move the stop just below that, then once price has left the high, near the lower quadrant of the FVG. This takes months of observation to trust: "you can't trust it the first few times."
- **Example in this video:** The live 1m bullish FVG (~11:00 ET): ICT points at the wick on candle 1 of that gap as the "extra credit" layer (2:01:03 onward), with its CE as an alternative stop reference; his actual draw down was about 1.75 handles.
- **Timestamps:** [2:00:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7214) extra credit; [2:01:53](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7313) stop one tick below wick CE.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says this is an advanced refinement shown to private students first.

### Opening range gap CE ("mid gap") — first draw of the day, 70% within 30 minutes  (aliases: RTH opening range gap, "consequent encroachment of the opening range gap", ORG midpoint)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Draw a Fibonacci from the previous day's settlement price to the first opening tick at 9:30 (RTH). The 50% is the ORG CE; mark the lower/upper quadrants too.
  - About 70% of the time the midpoint is touched by 10:00 (within the first 30 minutes). "Not 100%": in the mentorship it has failed "one or two times," and it gets hit eventually.
  - It is "my always first draw." Even when price doesn't reach it, the CE is the bias anchor: you expect price to try for it, and the targets before it are the high of day, the opening price (a static price level, not a zone), buy-side liquidity (smooth/equal highs), and the lower quadrant of the ORG.
- **How to use:** Use it as a draw, a first partial or a Terminus. Choosing the easiest first partial (e.g., the lower-quadrant or CE of the gap, or the first FVG) is fine.
- **Example in this video:** 9/16/2024 NQ: gap opening lower; price rallies to the lower quadrant of the ORG but not CE by 10:00; he says "that need not be filled today." The level stays a key level for the session.
- **Timestamps:** [0:45:53](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2753) 70% rule; [0:46:41](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2801) half the gap hit by 10:00; [0:58:46](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3526) how to draw the FIB; [1:15:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4547) "my always first draw."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Captions at 0:45 say "7%"; clearly 70%. Appears consistent with earlier mentorship teaching on opening range gaps.

### Judas swing at the 9:30 open after a gap
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says:** If the market opens at 9:30 and goes straight in the direction of the gap (here, lower), with no swing structure to trade, that is a Judas swing; do not chase it, "let it run out of steam." It will run back over the top of the move. Characteristic: a single straight shot with no inefficiencies/order blocks/short-term highs to trade from.
- **Timestamps:** [1:02:08](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3728); [1:03:42](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3822).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Also frames the "trap": retail short-sellers chase and place stops above smooth highs.

### Primary vs minor buy-side/sell-side liquidity; swing low definition; bodied swing high
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A swing low is the lowest candle with a higher low to its left and a higher low to its right; the swing is confirmed at the close that completes it. At that point go back for inefficiencies, order blocks, short-term highs.
  - A minor sell-side is the initial low; once taken out, the new low is the primary sell-side ("a much more meaningful event"). Similarly, a high that takes out the initial high of the day is primary buy-side. Relative equal highs and old highs are targets.
  - He prefers to see a bodied swing high be run (a stop hunt) rather than a tiny wick high.
  - After price fails to get ORG CE and leaves a lower high, short stops rest above it: his note was 19,463 (to be carried as a key level into lunch and the afternoon). He writes raw numbers down on a notepad and plans how they might be used in the next session (lunch macro, afternoon).
- **Timestamps:** [1:02:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3776) swing low defined; [1:19:04](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4744) primary buy side; [1:20:40](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4840) minor vs primary; [1:24:59](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5099) bodied swing high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Minor" and "primary" are by reference to the day's initial range.

### Mean threshold of an order block (down-close candle) and "reclaimed order block"  (aliases: OB mean threshold, MT)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** For a bullish down-close candle (order block), no candle should close below its mean threshold (midpoint); it can stab through or even wick to the candle's low in theory, "it's not the best." If the midpoint is traded to/through, that indicates weakness and a likely run lower. Used to frame the next displacement; if it fails, you sit still or reassess/go the other direction, "that's part of using the PD arrays." A bullish order block that is violated and then recovered can become a reclaimed (bullish) order block that runs for the buy side.
- **Timestamps:** [1:31:44](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5504) no close below mean threshold; [1:40:43](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6043) reclaimed order block.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says he is "trying to trade the fair value gap today" and only describes the order block logic for framing.

### Minimum range rule — 15 to 20 handles from entry to first target; first partial and Terminus
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Only take a framed setup if the range from entry to the first objective is at least about 15 handles for his son, ideally 20 handles (80 ticks on NQ); if it cannot make 15, 20 should still be visible for the target. He dislikes 10-handle trades: 10 handles is static price movement, you can be right on direction and still get stopped, and the risk model in NQ does not work.
  - Start the clock at the time of entry. Targets: first partial = the easiest liquidity or inefficiency; Terminus = where you are done for the day.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- FVG = three candles; candle 2 is the trigger/framing candle; candle 3 is the entry candle.
- Stop: candle 2 extreme (aggressive/max leverage) or candle 1 extreme (conservative, one-contract trader).
- Entry: candle 3's high minus 1 tick (short) / candle 3's low plus 1 tick (long).
- Wicks through the far edge are OK; bodies shouldn't close beyond it.
- Best bullish setups only drop into the upper half; best bearish only into the lower half; one or at most two candles in the entry half.
- Draw gaps including any volume imbalance; never just the wick.
- ORG CE: draw from previous settlement to 9:30 open; 70% touched by 10:00 (about 30 minutes).
- Minimum range: 15-20 handles (80 ticks = 20 handles on NQ); avoid 10-handle trades.
- Macro windows noted: 9:50–10:10; 10:50–11:10 ET.
- Contracts: NQ mini $20/point; MNQ $2/point; ES micro $5/point. Roll: Sept → Dec contract (NQZ2024) decided by volume numbers on barchart.com, not by waiting for a streamer.
- News: 8:30 medium-impact news ignored; he trusts price over data ("the data is always fluffed; they'll say it was priced in").
- Drills: one micro contract, demo, at least a month ("no time limit"); first partial only, then add Terminus after 2-3 weeks.
- Entries on limit orders (professional), exits on limit orders; market orders for learning, getting out, or capturing a missed limit.
- Short-term lows are not shorting opportunities after a Judas-swing drop when the only gap is in the lower half.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Drills are practice, not tests; "if you get an adverse result don't beat yourself up" ([0:12:08](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=728)).
- Use one micro contract in a demo; the goal is process and desensitization, not money. Funded accounts / larger size make you fearful of the outcome ([0:37:41](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2261)).
- No pressure to trade every day; don't feel you must "catch up" with others ([0:39:17](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2357)). Trade your own model and pace ([1:02:08](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3728)).
- Journal each drill: time of entry, risk (candle 2 stop), target, whether stopped and how long it took, draw-down in dollars, time to target ([1:42:26](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6146)). Log time and draw down in seconds: "journaling... logging all the data" ([2:05:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7556)).
- Do it boring: "if you have that feeling of excitement you're doing it wrong"; emotional excitement prevents clear focus ([1:52:10](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6730)).
- Do not copy his trades, do not size up because of them, and do not tag/share wins with him. His own paper account is for teaching, not a signal service ([1:54:36](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6876)).
- Only trade obvious setups; if it was not obvious you can't be mad that you missed it ([1:29:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5354)). Time distortion (chop) means sit and wait.
- Write numbers on a notepad away from the charts and think through how they may be used in the next session (lunch macro, afternoon).
- Don't trust retail "rejection" wicks inside inefficiencies; don't follow people who "can only talk in hindsight."
- Session plan: take one or two setups and close; "get in, get his and go home."

## Glossary candidates
`SIBI / BISI` — sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (bearish FVG) / buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (bullish FVG).
`Candle 2 (trigger / framing candle)` — the middle candle of a FVG; its extreme is the aggressive stop.
`Quadrant levels` — the 25%/75% levels inside a gap, used to grade where price should enter.
`Low-hanging fruit entry` — the simplest entry: candle 3's edge ±1 tick on one contract.
`Mean threshold` — midpoint of an order block candle (50%).
`Reclaimed FVG / reclaimed order block` — a gap/block that flipped and then was recovered so it acts as support/resistance in the original direction.
`Terminus` — the final target of a trade (after a first partial).
`Handle` — one full point/price unit in NQ (4 ticks; $20 on the mini, $2 on the micro).
`Time distortion` — chop/sideways delivery that makes traders believe the market is random.
`Primary / minor buy side & sell side` — liquidity ranked by whether taking it out breaks the day's initial high/low.
