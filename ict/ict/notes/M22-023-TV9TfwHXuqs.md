## Concepts

### FOMC / news-event delivery pattern  (aliases: FOMC, knee-jerk reaction, NFP-style legs, 2:00 / 2:30 FOMC)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:** FOMC behaves like non-farm payroll: a knee-jerk initial leg, then another leg, then a fake-out leg, *then* the real setup comes. He waits a few minutes for the "initial shakeout" before outlining anything. The announcement is at 2:00 p.m.; price drops into the level, and usually by around 2:30 the initial leg that "prices in" the event (high or low) is finished and the real move starts. A trade taken ahead of the FOMC announcement is a "gambler's setup" and he doesn't count it.
- **How to use:** Mark where the initial leg should terminate (a discount FVG below 50% of the range, near sell-side liquidity), wait for price to deliver into it after 2:00, then look for the reversal and the run on the opposite liquidity (here Tuesday's high).
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: after 2:00 the market drops; ICT marks the blue zone as the end of the bears' race; price drops into it shortly after 2:30, turns, and runs Tuesday's high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:06](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=6) — waiting for FOMC initial shakeout, "end of the road" zone; [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=100) — FOMC like NFP: leg, leg, fake-out leg, then real setup; [0:11:10](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=670) — 2:00 drop, initial leg finished ~2:30, then rally; [0:14:23](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=863) — pre-FOMC trade = gambler's setup.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "This isn't an invitation" to trade FOMC or NFP — extremely risky; great to study in hindsight or watch live as a case study of liquidity runs.

### Stacked FVGs — expect the stab into the second  (aliases: initial imbalance, second imbalance)
- **Kind:** rule (applied again)
- **What ICT says:** With an initial imbalance and a second one beyond it, always imagine price could stab into the second. He'd rather see price come down into the target first; a move too far back up would "upset" the higher-timeframe FVG not yet traded into.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:06](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=6) — initial imbalance, could stab into the second; [0:02:27](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=147) — doesn't want to upset the untraded FVG.

### Short entries into the drop: FVG as resistance, stop above last up-close candle  (aliases: bearish order block, stop above most recent green candle)
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** With sell-side resting below a recent low, you could short with the stop right above the most recent green (up-close) candle to the left. If price revisits the bottom of a FVG and bumps it like support-turned-resistance, that's a short; stop again at the high of the most recent green candle. A bearish order block above can be hit and price resumes lower. An order block (blue line) in the path can cause a bounce first.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ during the post-2:00 drop: shorts aim at the "bullseye" just below a short-term low (sell-side pool), then the blue shaded zone. Sequence: liquidity run, drop, hits an order block (blue line), bounces into the FVG, which sets up the decline into the highlighted zone.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:27](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=147) — short with stop above most recent green candle; [0:03:16](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=196) — FVG bottom bumped like resistance = short; [0:13:35](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=815) — recap of the drop sequence.

### "Running" vs "Sweeping" liquidity  (aliases: run on liquidity, sweep, expansion vs reversal)
- **Kind:** definition (vocabulary)
- **What ICT says:** He picks these words deliberately. *Sweeping* a high (or low) = price goes just a little above (below) it — a shallow run — and reverses back into the range. *Running* a high (old high or relative equal highs) = price goes right over the top of it and continues without looking back (expansion).
- **Example in this video:** Before/at FOMC price made a shallow move above Tuesday's high (red line) = a sweep, then came down. After the drop into the blue zone, price ran Tuesday's high "much more meaningfully" — straight through and higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=243) — "running the Tuesday high, not sweeping"; [0:04:50](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=290) — sweep = shallow run and reverse, run = right over top and doesn't look back; [0:05:38](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=338) — sweep and reverse into range vs expansion move.

### Discount FVG below 50% of the range as the turn point ("checkered flag")  (aliases: end of the road for bears, blue shaded area, bullseye)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** Measure the range from the high formed at 2:00 p.m. (14:00 on chart) down to the low; below 50% (discount) sits the turquoise/blue FVG — that's where the drop should end before the real move higher. Candle bodies respecting that zone is "really handsome price delivery." A slight dip below it is fine; he's not worried about that much accuracy. If wrong, price might only reach the smaller "bullseye" below a short-term low and rally from there.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: price trades down into the blue zone, bodies respect it, dips slightly below, then turns higher; after it takes out the swing high around 13,150 and crosses back above the larger FVG, the reversal is confirmed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:50](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=290) — "checkered flag" = end of race for bears; [0:07:16](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=436) — bodies respecting the blue shaded area; [0:08:02](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=482) — turn, swing high ~13,150 taken, back above the larger FVG; [0:09:37](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=577) — 50% of the range, FVG below it is the target.

### Fibonacci expansion target — wicks vs bodies  (aliases: Fib projection, extreme of range, body-to-body)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Normally he anchors Fibs on the highest open/close and lowest open/close in the swing (bodies) when not much volatility is expected. On FOMC, volatility is high, so the extremes of the range must be considered: use the wicks. For upside objectives draw from the low up to the high (he first drew it backwards). The wick-based expansion gives objective one and objective two (the "extreme target for today"); the body-to-body version "makes more sense" for the target he had outlined earlier.
- **How to use:** After the reversal (swing high taken and price back above the FVG), project the range from the 2:00 p.m. high to the post-2:30 low upward to get the target.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ target 13,437.00 — hit ("there she is") after ~2:30; price then pulled back into imbalances and continued higher even after the target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:02](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=482) — 2:00 high to post-2:30 low expanded to 13,437.00; [0:11:58](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=718) — bodies normally, wicks on FOMC because of volatility; [0:12:47](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=767) — low-to-high objectives 1 and 2, body-to-body comparison.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Answers the recurring "why does ICT use wicks sometimes and bodies other times" question: volatility expectation decides it. Refines Ep. 22, where he anchored OTE on bodies.

### Bullish order block on the reversal & continuation imbalances  (aliases: down-close candles, institutional order flow)
- **Kind:** example / rule
- **What ICT says:** After the turn, a retrace into an order block made of two down-close candles is a buy; as price runs Tuesday's high, retracements into small imbalances are buys. In a bullish delivery of institutional order flow, down-close candles should support price as it goes up.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:10](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=910) — buy at OB of two down-close candles, imbalance buys; [0:15:59](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=959) — down-close candles supporting price in institutional order flow.

### Eight setups in one move — choose the one that fits your model  (aliases: unique setup selection)
- **Kind:** rule (teaching philosophy)
- **What ICT says:** He counts eight tradeable setups in the whole sequence (excluding the pre-FOMC gamble): (1) the drop's FVG short, (2) the return to the FVG short, closing the short below the low, a long scalp up into resting liquidity above a high, a short from the FVG into the outlined objective, a buy at the order block after the turn, buys on retracements into small imbalances during the run of Tuesday's high, and continuation after the target. You aren't meant to take all eight; each could be the specific setup that fits a student's unique model. The highest form of analysis is knowing where price will draw *before* it turns.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:37](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=577) — eight trades in this move; [0:10:23](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=623) — you don't all need the same setup; [0:14:23](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=863) — enumerating the trades; [0:15:10](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=910) — later buys.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The exact count/order of the eight is narrated loosely over the chart; listing above is reconstructed from his narration.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- FOMC announcement 2:00 p.m. ET; initial leg typically complete around 2:30 p.m.; real setup comes after the knee-jerk/fake-out legs.
- Don't trade ahead of the FOMC announcement (gambler's setup).
- Sweep = shallow run beyond a high/low and reversal; Run = through it and continuation.
- Two stacked imbalances: expect a possible stab into the second.
- Short stop: above the most recent up-close (green) candle to the left.
- Range 2:00 p.m. high → low: the FVG below 50% (discount) = where the drop should end.
- Confirmation of reversal: swing high (~13,150) taken and price back above the larger FVG.
- Fib anchoring: bodies (highest/lowest open or close) normally; wicks on high-volatility events like FOMC.
- Fib expansion target on MNQ: 13,437.00 (hit).
- FOMC/NFP are extremely risky to trade; study them.

## Glossary candidates
`Sweep` — shallow move just beyond a high/low followed by reversal back into the range.
`Run (on liquidity)` — price goes through an old high/low and continues (expansion).
`Checkered flag` — ICT's marker for the "end of the race," where the counter move should terminate.
`Bullseye` — marked liquidity target (e.g., just below a short-term low).
`Initial shakeout` — the first knee-jerk FOMC/NFP move to wait out.
`Gambler's setup` — a trade taken ahead of a high-impact announcement.
