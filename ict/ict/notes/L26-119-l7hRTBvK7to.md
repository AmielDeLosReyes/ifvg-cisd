## Concepts

### Immediate Rebalance
- **Kind:** definition / model (ICT's proprietary)
- **What ICT says:** When a discount wick forms (a gap between two candlesticks), the algorithm rushes to deliver price back to the previous day's high or a key level immediately, rather than waiting for a full fair-value-gap correction. The mechanics: price opens, trades down to the consequent encroachment of a discount wick, bounces hard, and delivers directly to the target without intermediate steps. "It's the strongest algorithmic price delivery PD array I've discovered and coined... it's mine and you can't find anywhere else." The trade executes same-day or the next day; if day-two fails, it becomes a "complex reversal" signaling lower prices. ICT taught this extensively in his 2016-2017 paid mentorship but now shares it publicly.
- **How to identify:** Look for a wick that is lower than the prior one; price opens, trades down to the consequent encroachment (midpoint) of that wick, bounces sharply, and runs directly to the previous high or target.
- **How to use:** When you see immediate rebalance form, anticipate a directional run that day or the next. If day-two fails (trades below immediate rebalance), expect a complex reversal and lower prices.
- **Example in this video:** Dollar index March 12-13, 2026: wick formed, price opened, traded down to consequent encroachment, bounced, and delivered right to 104.54—"to the tick." This triggered bearish forex pairs (EUR, GBP) to drop sharply as the dollar firmed.

### Complex Reversal (Day-Two Failure of Immediate Rebalance)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** If immediate rebalance forms but price fails to run up on day-two (instead trades below the immediate rebalance level), it signals a "complex reversal." The market will not protect the upside; instead, it will turn lower. This is a high-conviction sell signal.
- **How to identify:** Day-one: immediate rebalance forms and runs. Day-two: price opens and trades below the immediate rebalance level rather than protecting it.
- **How to use:** As a strong bearish signal; reverse to short bias.
- **Example in this video:** Dollar index: if the bounce doesn't hold on day-two, expect lower prices.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:30](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=810)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "If day two fails, and it trades below immediate rebalance, that's a complex reversal. It will not run and protect the upside. It'll go the other direction."

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (Applied to Price Entry)
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:** When an FVG is treated as an inversion FVG, price must close below the midpoint (consequent encroachment) of that gap to validate the bearish bias. Example: USD/JPY and EUR/USD formed iFVGs; bodies stayed below consequent encroachment, signaling continuation lower. In the micro-Nasdaq intraday trade, the iFVG served as a target for shorts.
- **How to identify:** Fair value gap exists; price trades below midpoint with bodies confirming; next break lower enters at that level.
- **Example in this video:** EUR/USD: inversion FVG with consequent encroachment; price broke down through rejection block and delivered into the sell-side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:17](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=857), [0:31:45](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=1905)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Visual confirmation (body placement) is key; bodies must close below consequent encroachment to validate.

### Rejection Block
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick that shows rejection of price movement at a key level; body closes below the level, wick extends above it. When price trades through a rejection block, it often accelerates in the opposite direction, confirming bearish or bullish bias.
- **Example in this video:** EUR/USD: rejection block at a key level; price broke below it and continued lower into the sell-side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:03](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=903)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rejection blocks provide visual confirmation of directional intent.

### First Percentage Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)
- **Kind:** entry model / reference level
- **What ICT says:** The first FVG that forms after a key event (e.g., morning opening, post-FOMC). In the micro-Nasdaq example, the FPFVG from Thursday serves as a reference level; price rallies back into it but fails to close above it (bodies reject), validating bearish bias. Later, when price breaks back down, the FPFVG becomes the inversion FVG target.
- **How to identify:** First gap in the morning or after a key event.
- **How to use:** As a reference level; bodies should reject if bearish; once rejected, expect return into it as inversion FVG target.

### Order Block (At Bottom of Inversion FVG)
- **Kind:** entry model / stop placement
- **What ICT says:** An order block at the bottom of an inversion FVG is a "big brick wall." When price rally attempts to retrace back into the iFVG, the order block acts as a hard stop. ICT placed his stop loss just above an order block in the micro-Nasdaq trade; price came close but held above it. "If you didn't write that down, you missed a beautiful piece of information right there."
- **How to identify:** The low of the inversion FVG formation (the order block level).
- **How to use:** Stop-loss placement; set stops just above the order block when shorting into the inversion FVG.
- **Example in this video:** Micro-Nasdaq trade: order block at the bottom of the iFVG; stop loss set there; price spike up close to it but held.

### Swing High (Three-Candle Pattern)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** When price shows High-Higher High-Lower High pattern over three consecutive candlesticks, it forms a swing high. This is often followed by immediate displacement lower. In the micro-Nasdaq example, this pattern preceded the sharp drop. "Look at the bodies, not the wicks. See the bodies? High. Higher high. Lower high. There's your swing high. And then displacement lower."
- **How to identify:** Watch three consecutive candlesticks; compare highs; if pattern is H-HH-LH, that's a swing high. Bodies must validate it.
- **How to use:** As a reversal signal; expect lower prices after the third candle closes.
- **Example in this video:** Micro-Nasdaq 1m: multiple swing highs formed before the main dump lower. Price action used this pattern to set up lower moves.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Focus on bodies, not wicks; wicks can be misleading.

### Discount/Premium Wick
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A discount wick is one that is lower in range than prior wicks; a premium wick is higher. When trading at a lower level relative to a discount wick above, that wick acts as premium (even though it's labeled "discount wick") because the range has changed. ICT teaches grading wicks by drawing fibs and identifying midpoint (consequent encroachment).
- **How to identify:** Grade the wick with fib; note the midpoint and octant/quadrant levels.
- **How to use:** As target levels; if price is below a discount wick, that wick's midpoint can be a target for a bounce.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil daily: discount wick graded with fib; consequent encroachment is the first target for continuation higher. ES and NQ: similar wick grading to identify midpoint targets.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:15](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=1095), [0:23:51](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=1431), [0:26:13](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=1573)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The label "discount" or "premium" is relative to current price position; always re-evaluate as price moves.

### Suspension Block
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick that closes with a small body and a wick extending in the opposite direction of intended move, showing suspension of momentum. In the micro-Nasdaq trade, ICT identified a suspension block-like pattern (small body, wick below) as a signal to add to the short.
- **How to identify:** Small body, wick in opposition, at a key level.
- **Example in this video:** Micro-Nasdaq 1m: suspension block pattern used to add to short position.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:26](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=2006)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Similar to a pin bar; indicates indecision followed by direction.

### Displacement (Sharp Move Lower After Pattern)
- **Kind:** entry model / outcome
- **What ICT says:** After a swing high or suspension block forms, price often experiences "displacement" lower—a sharp, aggressive move. This is the money move. In the micro-Nasdaq example, displacement occurred immediately after the swing high pattern, delivering price down into the target levels.
- **How to identify:** Follows a reversal pattern (swing high, suspension, etc.); bodies accelerate and show strength in one direction.
- **Example in this video:** Micro-Nasdaq: after swing high, immediate displacement lower; sharp drop into multiple targets.

## Glossary candidates

`IMMEDIATE REBALANCE` — Market rushes to deliver price back to a prior level immediately, without waiting for FVG correction. Happens same-day or next-day; failure on day-two signals complex reversal.

`COMPLEX REVERSAL` — Immediate rebalance fails on day-two; price trades below the rebalance level, signaling lower prices to follow.

`SWING HIGH` — Three-candle pattern showing High-Higher High-Lower High; often followed by displacement lower.

`DISPLACEMENT` — Sharp, aggressive move in one direction following a reversal pattern; the money move after pattern confirmation.

`ORDER BLOCK` — The foundational level at the bottom of an inversion FVG; acts as a wall for stop-loss placement and reversal reference.

`FIRST PERCENTAGE FAIR VALUE GAP` — First FVG after opening or key event; serves as reference level and later as inversion FVG target.

`DISCOUNT WICK` — A wick lower than prior wicks; grade with fib to identify consequent encroachment and targets.

`PAPER TRADING / DEMO` — ICT's distinction: public teaching on paper accounts (not real money, not investment advice); private trades shown only when signed into live brokerage accounts.

`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — Candlestick with small body and wick in opposition; shows momentum stall; often precedes displacement.
