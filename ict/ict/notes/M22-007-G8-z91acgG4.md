## Summary
Episode 7 (recorded Tuesday 8 Feb 2022) covers daily bias and "consolidation hurdles", meaning what to do when the daily chart sits around equilibrium and the bias is unclear. ICT shows how to read bias from the daily chart: where price is drawing next, which old high or old low. He also shows how to size leverage with or against that bias. When bias is unclear, he falls back to intraday liquidity pools referenced from the 8:30 a.m. ET vertical line. The main lesson is intermarket. He timed an NQ long using an ES fair value gap and a Dow/NQ/ES divergence, which he calls "cracks in correlation" (SMT divergence in later terms), even though the NQ chart showed no FVG. He also explains putting "leader" orders (single test contracts) in at the open to get a read on price. A long mindset section covers imperfection, losses as the cost of information, and realistic expectations.

## Concepts

### Daily bias (reading the draw from the daily chart)  (aliases ICT uses: bias, "where is price going to draw")
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Daily bias means using the daily chart to judge whether the next daily candle is likely bullish or bearish. Ask where price will draw next: up to an old high or down to an old low. Keep that bias until the target is reached.
  - Bearish bias means expecting down-close candles each day and hunting intraday shorts. You can still take a long, but with far less leverage.
  - A bearish bias doesn't mean every day closes down. In his NQ example the bearish leg from the high to the old low had only 2 up-close daily candles. When the algorithm is "spooling" (expanding directionally), some days will still close up, and you may lose trying to short them. That's no big deal.
  - What people "really" want when they ask about daily bias is to catch the exact high and low of every day and never take a loss. He calls that impossible. Bias is a "quick down and dirty" way to frame the day, not a perfect forecast.
  - "Daily bias is not an everyday bias that's going to work in your favor." Some days the expected direction doesn't deliver and you have to adjust.
- **How to identify:** daily market structure shift, then a retracement into a daily FVG, then expect delivery to the next old low (or old high when bullish).
- **How to use:** Trade with the bias at your maximum (non-overleveraged) size. Trade against it, if at all, with reduced size. Hold the bias until the draw is reached; after that, expect a reversal, retracement or consolidation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily (continuous contract on TradingView). Price ran BSL above relative equal highs, then broke below a swing low (MSS). It traded back up into a FVG, then delivered down to the old lows. ICT notes a gap near the low-to-high midpoint. With the bias bearish, each day from that high to the low was approached expecting shorts.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He openly says that even he sometimes has no clear read on bias, and then has to "demand more price action" from the 9:30 open.

### Dealing range, premium/discount and consolidation at equilibrium ("consolidation hurdles")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Once sell-side liquidity below the range low has been taken, price is likely to retrace back inside the range (the swing high to the swing low).
  - In the example price went back up into premium (above equilibrium), then down into discount (bounced off the old low), then consolidated around equilibrium.
  - When price hangs around equilibrium of the daily range, bias is very hard to read, even for him. That doesn't mean no trading. It means:
    - relying on intraday charts,
    - looking for liquidity pools,
    - trading intraday volatility (runs on old highs and old lows),
    - being nimble, a "surgical strike": take your handles and get out, don't overstay.
- **How to identify:** On the daily chart, mark the high to the low of the most recent swing after the liquidity raid and use a Fib only for the midpoint (equilibrium). If price is oscillating around 50%, you're in a consolidation hurdle.
- **How to use:** Lower your expectations, take intraday liquidity targets only, and use smaller objectives.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily after the sell-side raid. Price retraced into premium, then discount, then chopped around equilibrium. ICT preferred a move lower for Tuesday 8 Feb 2022 (a sweep of the lows) but it didn't deliver.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=110) — premium, discount, then consolidation around equilibrium · [0:10:37](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=637) — what to do when bias is unclear
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He uses the Fib only to find the midpoint, saying "no OTE, no indicators" in this mentorship.

### Leverage scaled to bias (risk per trade)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Trading with the bias means using your maximum allowed leverage without over-leveraging. His example: an account that allows at most 10 contracts. He uses a fixed % risk; he likes 3.5% and his maximum is 4.5%.
  - Trading counter to the bias means dialing leverage back.
  - Students should risk less than 1%, about 0.5% or 0.25%. Don't put high risk behind a move you don't understand: it creates "toxic thinking", bad habits and fear of executing.
  - Beginners should be "indifferent to the results". Use a demo with very low leverage, or reduce leverage further.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:11](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=251) — 10-contract example, 3.5% · [0:04:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=298) — students: 0.5% or 0.25% · [0:09:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=590) — max 4.5%, prefers 3.5%

### 8:30 a.m. reference line ("crosshairs") and looking left for liquidity
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Draw a vertical line at 8:30 a.m. New York time. That's the point you start looking back to the left from.
  - Mark the first low and the first high to the left of 8:30. If a swing point sits inside another candle's range, use the extreme (the candle that holds the outermost price), because that's where the liquidity rests.
  - Relative equal highs further left are buy-side liquidity. Retail traders see them as "strong resistance" because price failed there twice, and the market then runs right through it.
  - Use a first target (the nearest liquidity) and a longer-term intraday target further left.
  - "Algorithms run on time and price, not price and time." Refer to time first (8:30), then find the first low to its left.
- **How to identify:** Use the 15m chart. Draw the 8:30 vertical, find the first swing low and high to the left, then look further left for relative equal highs and lows.
- **How to use:** Expect a raid on the liquidity on one side, then a run to the opposing pool.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15m on 8 Feb 2022. Price traded below the first low to the left of 8:30, reversed, and took out the short-term high (relative equal highs further left). ICT took that trade and exited at that high instead of holding for the longer-term target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:24](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=684) — 8:30 crosshairs, first low, use the extreme · [0:12:14](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=734) — relative equal highs as "resistance" retail sees · [0:28:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1700) — time first, then price

### Targeting: range midpoint plus liquidity
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** He exited at the short-term high rather than holding for the longer-term one. The midpoint of the larger range (high down to the low) was just above that short-term high, and there was a small imbalance there. In this mentorship he looks for "about the midpoint and some kind of liquidity" (buy stops above it) as the objective.
- **How to use:** Take profit where the range midpoint lines up with resting liquidity. Don't hold for the extended target just to show off a perfect trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:02](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=782) — why he didn't hold: midpoint plus imbalance plus buy stops
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He deliberately doesn't hold for big runs on the public account, so he doesn't present unrealistic results.

### Narrative over pattern-matching
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Seeing a low taken out and expecting higher prices (or the reverse) isn't enough. There has to be greater context: why should the market do it? Students coming from "SMC" lack narrative, which leads to analysis paralysis or false confidence. Educators who only talk about the left side of the chart and can't trade the hard right edge give false security.

### 2022 Mentorship entry model (raid, MSS, FVG), timed on a correlated index
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sequence: price trades below an old low (SSL taken), then displacement and a bullish market structure shift (breaks a swing high). Look back through the up-leg for a FVG and enter on the retrace into it.
  - If price instead keeps going lower after your entry, "that's what your stop is for."
  - For homework, check the 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute charts. He used the 2-minute chart that day.
- **How to identify:** old low, then a run below it, then a displacement up through a swing high, then the FVG in that leg.
- **How to use:** Stop just below the low candle of the move (about 1 tick below). Target the short-term high or BSL.
- **Example in this video:**
  - NQ 2m:
    - Price trades below the old low formed before 8:30. The 10:04 candle is the low and the stop goes about 1 tick under it.
    - It rallies but doesn't break the short-term high, dips below the old low again but not below the 10:04 low, then turns higher.
    - NQ showed no FVG at entry time. ICT went long at 14,505.5, inside/near a bullish order block.
    - He exited at 14,622.75, above the 14,620 high: about 117 handles on 1 NQ mini.
  - ES 2m:
    - Price traded below the old low (SSL taken) with displacement down, then displacement up.
    - It broke a swing high (bullish MSS) and left a FVG.
    - The 10:36 candle traded down into that FVG, which was the long signal in ES.
    - ES then ran to the short-term high around 4495/4496 ("44.95 level"), swept it, accumulated more, and ran higher through the longer-term old high.
  - He used the timing of the ES FVG entry (10:36) to buy NQ: "trading what's not in the chart."
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925) — 2m chart, 10:04 low candle, stop · [0:18:34](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1114) — ES displacement, MSS, FVG, 10:36 long · [0:20:07](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1207) — NQ long at 14,505 near an order block · [0:37:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2278) — exit 14,622.75, 117 handles
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says he "promised not to teach order blocks much" in this mentorship but still references one for the NQ fill. The ES/NQ FVG timing trick is the point: you don't need the FVG in your own instrument if a correlated one prints it.

### SMT divergence ("cracks in correlation"), confirmation only  (aliases ICT uses: cracks in correlation, divergence, fingerprint of the algorithm)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Stack the Dow (top), NASDAQ (middle) and S&P (bottom) futures. They normally move in tandem.
  - When NQ and ES trade below their old low but the Dow refuses to make the lower low, at a key time and level, that crack in correlation confirms accumulation of longs. The bearish version is the mirror: the Dow fails to make a higher high while ES and NQ do.
  - It must confirm an idea you already have. "You're already looking for a reason to go higher" (old low, raid, MSS). By itself it means nothing.
  - He blew accounts in the 1990s trading these divergences alone, without narrative.
  - The Dow holding up is not "buyers coming in". It's an unwillingness to deliver lower, and ICT calls this a macro: something inside the algorithm that prevents or enables delivery of price.
  - Experienced traders may not need it. For developing students, these intermarket signals are "the best indicators you're ever going to have": no moving averages, stochastics or Fibonacci needed.
- **How to identify:** At a known liquidity level (old low) and time window, one index fails to take the level that the other two take.
- **How to use:** Use it to confirm a long (or short) setup that's already anticipated at a liquidity raid. Then execute with the FVG/MSS logic in your chosen instrument.
- **Example in this video:** 8 Feb 2022. During the decline NQ and ES both traded below the old low; YM did not make the lower low. ICT used this, plus the ES FVG, to go long NQ around 10:36.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:34](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1354) — Dow/NQ/ES stacked, Dow refuses the lower low · [0:23:26](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1406) — it confirms an already-anticipated idea · [0:25:57](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1557) — blew accounts trading divergence alone · [0:28:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1700) — "this is a macro": algorithm prevents/enables delivery
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The term "SMT" isn't used here. "Macro" here means an algorithmic mechanism that withholds or allows delivery. That's a different, earlier usage from the later meaning of a macro as a specific clock-time window (e.g. 9:50–10:10).

### Turtle soup (ICT's version)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A fake break below an old low that then rallies, or a fake break above an old high that then drops. ICT calls this turtle soup. It's his own simplified interpretation, not the pattern from Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' *Street Smarts* (which he recommends as a book). He looks for runs above old highs to set up shorts, and the mirror for longs.
- **Timestamps:** [0:25:09](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1509)

### Intermarket relationships among the indices
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** ES, NQ and YM "generally move in the same direction". He trades NQ for its volatility but watches ES and YM on multiple monitors. The setup forms every day, but it may not be in your asset. "All boats rise in high tide." Read the environment as a whole, not just your own instrument.

### Leader orders / test contracts at the open  (aliases ICT uses: "leaders", "tossing a contract in", "premium for Intel")
- **Kind:** refinement (execution technique)
- **What ICT says:**
  - When he has no read (e.g. a sloppy 9:30 open, price in mid-range on the daily), he puts in a single contract, long or short, just to feel how price delivers around it.
  - Watch whether price moves easily away from the order, moves lethargically, or runs straight against it. If the test order is "ran over", your bias probably isn't aligned.
  - Small losses on these are "premium payments" for information. They aren't skill errors.
  - He learned this from a floor trader and George Angell (S&P floor traders). He also does it in Forex.
  - A demo account can't give this feedback loop because nothing is at risk.
  - He's not trying to pick a spot when he tosses these in, and he doesn't do it every day.
  - The 9:30 open is characteristically sloppy. Many people say don't trade it; he doesn't mind it.
- **Example in this video:** 8 Feb 2022. Two leader orders plus a short around the open lost money (one cost about $750). The 10:36 long, 14,505.5 to 14,622.75, made $2,345 on 1 NQ mini, which covered the leaders and netted about $1,100 for the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:47](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1847) — sloppy 9:30 open, putting orders in to get a feel · [0:31:33](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1893) — George Angell / floor-trader origin · [0:33:54](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2034) — reading price against the order · [0:54:05](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=3245) — trade P&L breakdown
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says many of his students don't do this and don't think it's worth it. It's a trade-off tied to his goal of capturing 100+ handle moves rather than scalping.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Daily bias: decide whether price draws to an old high or an old low, and keep that bias until the draw is reached.
- With bias: use max leverage (example 10 contracts). Against bias: dial leverage back.
- ICT's own risk: likes 3.5%, maximum 4.5%. Students: under 1%, ideally 0.5% or 0.25%.
- Daily price around equilibrium means bias is unclear. Trade intraday liquidity pools, nimble, take handles and leave.
- 8:30 a.m. ET vertical line: look left for the first low and first high. Use the candle extreme, not an inside low.
- Algorithms run on time, then price: reference time first.
- Stop about 1 tick below the low candle of the reversal (the 10:04 candle in the example).
- Check the 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute charts (homework). He used the 2m.
- Divergence between YM/ES/NQ only confirms an already-anticipated setup. Never trade it alone.
- Target: range midpoint plus liquidity (buy stops) plus imbalance.
- 1 NQ mini needed about $21k+ in his account; one session can swing ±$5,000.
- His live account: 1 NQ mini contract only; P&L about $13,926, about 51% in two weeks and one day (Feb 2022).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Embrace imperfection: you won't enter, exit, size or pick the asset perfectly. Being right is not the same as being profitable. ([0:06:36](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=396))
- Embrace the "gray area". A small string of losses doesn't diminish a model built on sound logic. ([0:09:02](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=542))
- Fear of being wrong shows up as wanting to trade without stops. You must accept stops and losses. ([0:29:59](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1799))
- Losing trades aren't all errors. Some are paid information (leaders). A statement without chart context tells you nothing; the trader could just be lucky. ([0:49:19](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2959))
- His personal goal is no losing days, fixing intraday drawdown before the close. He explicitly says this should NOT be a new student's goal. ([0:42:54](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2574), [0:56:29](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=3389))
- Trade for money, not to impress anyone. Emotional highs lead to painful lows. Start with modest goals, like covering a car note or half your rent. ([0:45:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2720))
- Realistic returns: he polled whether 20% a month is respectable. Beware "$60k in one trade" marketing. ([0:44:33](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2673))
- It takes far more development time than students think. He believes about a year of study could get someone trading like this, but promises nothing. ([0:13:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=830), [0:54:53](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=3293))
- Demo for beginners with tiny leverage. Stay indifferent to outcomes early. ([0:04:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=298))
- Not knowing is honesty, not a lack of skill. ([0:45:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2720))

## Glossary candidates
`Consolidation hurdle` — the daily chart chopping around equilibrium of its dealing range, leaving no clear daily bias; handled with intraday liquidity trading.
`8:30 crosshairs` — vertical line at 8:30 a.m. ET from which you look left for the first swing high and low (liquidity references).
`Leader (order)` — a single test contract put in, often near the open, to feel how price delivers around it; its loss is a "premium for Intel".
`Cracks in correlation` — one index refusing to make the new high or low that its correlated indices make (later called SMT divergence).
`Macro (2022 Ep.7 usage)` — something in the algorithm that prevents or enables delivery of price (e.g. the Dow not delivering to a lower low).
`Spooling` — the algorithm expanding price directionally in one direction over several days.
`Turtle soup (ICT)` — a false run above an old high or below an old low that reverses.
`Trading what's not in the chart` — taking an entry in one index using the setup or timing that appears in a correlated index.
