## Concepts

### Decoupling (aliases: lack of agreement, divergence, three averages not in sync)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Decoupling occurs when NQ (Nasdaq), ES (S&P 500), and YM (Dow) do not move in agreement. One might be up while another consolidates or declines. This breaks the algorithmic precision of price action and makes trading harder and riskier. Probability drops; you lose clean structure.
- **How to identify:** Watch all three in real-time. If NQ rallies but ES stalls and YM drops, you have decoupling. No clean bias; price action is choppy.
- **How to use:** When decoupled, tighten stops, reduce size, or stop trading that session. Wait for agreement to return. Do not try to force trades in decoupled markets.
- **Example in this video:** Friday (Good Friday) CPI miss; NQ tried higher, ES hesitated, YM declined. ICT closed interest and stepped away.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:26](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=386) watches for decoupling; [0:07:17](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=437) identifies it; [0:22:39](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=1359) explains decoupling effect on precision.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Decoupling often precedes major moves or reversals; it's a warning flag, not a reason to panic, but definitely a reason to reduce risk.

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (aliases: first FVG, first inefficiency, first gap post-open)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The gap visible on the chart (candlestick low to next candlestick high) is not the real gap. Look at the wick to the left of that gap. The wick itself is the real inefficiency. The first presented FVG is the first one that forms after the opening bell, and it's your key reference for the session bias.
- **How to identify:** 1st candle opens at 9:30, 2nd candle opens; look for separation in bodies. The prior wick rejection is the real level.
- **How to use:** If bodies fail to touch inside the first presented FVG, that's bullish continuation. If bodies breach it or lay in the lower half, bearishness resumes.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: first presented FVG after 9:30 open; bodies stayed above it on retracements, confirming bullish bias short-term; when bodies fell below it, it turned bearish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:06](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=726) explains wick vs. visual gap; [0:18:02](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=1082) applies to live price action.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Most traders look at the visual gap and miss the wick. The wick is the true PD array.

### Rejection Block (aliases: wicked-high candle, failed-run candle, wick rejection)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A rejection block forms when a candle wicks up (or down) but closes below (or above) that wick, creating a wick extension that rejects a price level. This is a powerful signal that the level is held; price will not easily penetrate it on the next attempt.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candlestick that extends far with a wick but closes in the body far from the wick extreme. The wick is the rejection block.
- **How to use:** Use as a key level for entries and stops. Price will often respect the wick rejection and reverse. If price gets above this wick rejection, the level is breached and price can continue higher.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: rejection block forms where price tries higher but closes lower; bodies cannot stay above this wick rejection short-term, confirming bearish tone.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=774) shows rejection block formation; [0:15:28](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=928) uses it as a key level.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated; foundational concept.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (aliases: inversion FVG, bullish inversion, gap formation in wrong direction)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG forms when price is bullish but creates a gap on the downside (or vice versa). It's called an "inversion" because the gap is inverted relative to the direction of the trend. When price trades back above an inversion FVG, it signals continuation of the original trend.
- **How to identify:** In an uptrend, a candle gaps down (body separation, wick reaching down). That's an inversion FVG.
- **How to use:** When price trades back above an inversion FVG, expect continuation higher. This is a continuation pattern.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: inversion FVG formed on a pullback; when price retraced into it and broke above, it was a green light for continuation.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=774) shows inversion FVG formation.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Inversions are not as common as regular FVGs; when they form, they are strong signals.

### Consequent Encroachment / Midpoint Draw (aliases: half gap, 50% level, mid-level draw)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint of the opening range gap is the consequent encroachment; price draws to this level in 70% of first-hour trades. This is the lowest-probability miss in ICT's methodology. If you're watching the market at 9:30, the consequent encroachment is your first target to watch.
- **How to identify:** (Settlement price at 4:15 PM + Opening price at 9:30 AM) ÷ 2 = Consequent Encroachment.
- **How to use:** This is your first-hour bias. Premium gap → target consequent encroachment on downside. Discount gap → target upside. Once price reaches it, look for the next level or consider taking profit.
- **Example in this video:** Friday: CPI weak; opening range gap consequent encroachment was the draw for the first hour.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:26](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=386) sets up consequent encroachment; [0:10:05](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=605) shows price reaching it.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On decoupled days, this level may be delayed or missed; don't force it.

### Sell-Side Liquidity (aliases: short stops, sell orders waiting, liquidity pool)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Sell-side liquidity is a cluster of sell orders or old resistance levels where traders have stop losses (shorts). When price reaches sell-side, it runs through it quickly (often called a liquidity sweep or raid). Identify sell-side using old highs, prior swing highs, and FVG boundaries.
- **How to identify:** Look at prior session's highs, swing highs, tops of consolidations. These are sell-side pools. Also, the upper boundary of an FVG is sell-side.
- **How to use:** When price approaches sell-side, expect a potential run through it (liquidity sweep) or a stall/reversal. If you're short and price is approaching sell-side below you, tighten stops.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: multiple sell-side pools identified at prior highs and FVG boundaries; when price reaches them, it either sweeps through or reverses.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:07](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=487) identifies sell-side; [0:13:45](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=825) references liquidity sweeps as myth vs. reality.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "No such thing as liquidity sweeps" is a myth; ICT just proved one live on tape.

### Buy-Side Liquidity (aliases: buy stops, long stops, liquidity at lows)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Buy-side liquidity is a cluster of buy orders or old lows where long stops are. When price drives down and hits buy-side, it may be swept (longs stopped out) or bounce. Identify buy-side using old lows, prior swing lows, and FVG boundaries.
- **How to identify:** Look at prior session's lows, swing lows, bottoms of consolidations. These are buy-side pools. Also, the lower boundary of an FVG is buy-side.
- **How to use:** When price approaches buy-side, expect potential interaction (bounce or sweep). If you're long, watch buy-side below as support.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ: buy-side at opening range gap low; price drawn to this level repeatedly.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:05](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=605) watches for buy-side interaction.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Buy-side and sell-side are always contextual; use both to frame your tape reading.

### SMT Divergence Failure Swing (aliases: SMT, structural shift, Smart Money Trap)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A failure swing in the Smart Money Technique occurs when price makes a swing high or low but then fails to make a new swing in the expected direction. This divergence is a trap or reversal signal. When the three averages (NQ, ES, YM) create a divergence in their swings, market structure is uncertain and precision drops.
- **How to identify:** Swing high, then lower high (failure). Or swing low, then higher low (failure). Look for it across NQ, ES, YM separately.
- **How to use:** When SMT divergence appears, reduce size and precision. Do not trade with high conviction until agreement returns.
- **Example in this video:** Friday: ES and YM made failure swings (could not follow NQ higher), confirming decoupling.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:17](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=437) identifies SMT divergence in real-time; [0:09:00](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=540) watches ES/YM divergence vs. NQ.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** SMT divergence is a warning sign; it often precedes major directional shifts.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **70% rule applies to opening range gap.** Consequent encroachment is drawn in first hour 70% of the time.
- **Three averages must agree.** When NQ, ES, YM diverge, don't trade or trade smaller. Decoupling kills precision.
- **Rejection blocks must be respected.** If wick rejection fails to be retested and breached, price likely reverses.
- **Bodies cannot lay in lower half of FVG when bullish.** If bullish, bodies must stay in upper half or outside; if they lay in lower half, bull thesis is broken.
- **First presented FVG is the first to watch.** Not subsequent gaps; the very first gap post-open defines short-term bias.
- **Consequent encroachment of opening range gap is the midpoint.** No need to overthink targets beyond this for the first hour.
- **Sell-side and buy-side must be identified before the move reaches them.** This is forward-looking; know where these pools are at session start.
- **Wick, not visual gap, is the real inefficiency.** The gap you see on the chart is secondary to the wick rejection that created it.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Session setup:** Identify opening range gap, consequent encroachment, first-presented FVG, and sell-side / buy-side pools at session open (9:30).
- **Tape reading:** Watch each candlestick form in real-time. As soon as a candle closes, note what it did relative to your key levels. Does the body respect the boundary? Did it breach it?
- **Decision point:** If decoupling appears early (within first 15 minutes), reduce size immediately or step away. Do not fight decoupled markets.
- **Risk management:** Tight stops at key levels. If a rejection block is penetrated with a body close above, move stop and give room. If wick rejection holds, protect that level tightly.
- **Exiting trades early:** When precision is lost (due to decoupling, news, or failed setup), exit gracefully and move to sidelines. This is not a loss; it's smart risk management.
- **Friday bias:** Fridays (especially near holidays) have low participation, wider spreads, and more chop. Reduce expectations and size accordingly.

## Glossary candidates

`DECOUPLING` — When NQ, ES, and YM do not move in sync; one rises while another stalls or falls, breaking clean market structure.

`FIRST PRESENTED FAIR VALUE GAP` — The initial gap that forms in the first few candles after 9:30 open; the most reliable reference for session bias.

`REJECTION BLOCK` — A wick that extends far from the body close, signaling price rejection at that level; often acts as a key support/resistance.

`INVERSION FAIR VALUE GAP` — A gap formed in the opposite direction of the trend (down gap during uptrend, up gap during downtrend); signals continuation when price re-enters from opposite side.

`SELL-SIDE LIQUIDITY` — Concentration of sell orders or old resistance levels; price approaching sell-side may be swept through or reverse.

`BUY-SIDE LIQUIDITY` — Concentration of buy orders or old support levels; price approaching buy-side may bounce or be swept.

`SMT DIVERGENCE` — Failure of one or more averages (NQ, ES, YM) to make a new swing in the expected direction; indicates market structure uncertainty.

`CONSEQUENT ENCROACHMENT` — The 50% midpoint of the opening range gap; price targets this level in 70% of first-hour trades.

`WICK` — The part of a candlestick extending beyond the body; represents price rejection and often holds more significance than the body for key level identification.
