## Summary
"Market efficiency paradigm and institutional order flow." ICT frames the market as smart money cannibalizing uninformed retail liquidity, with time (of day) as the most important element, and explains why price returns to fair value gaps (paint-roller analogy: one-sided delivery must be re-offered in the other direction). He gives the precise three-candle ICT FVG definition (candle 1/2/3, boundaries, limit entry just beyond candle 3, stop above candle 1 or 2), and ties it to the market structure shift: after a run on liquidity, an energetic displacement that closes beyond a short-term swing point defines a displacement high/low range — the FVG must form inside that range or there is no trade. Worked example: NQ, February 3, 2022 (8:30 employment data day), using the first 15m swing high left of 8:30, a 9:30 fake-out, then MSS + FVG short on the 1m (his paper trade: 2 NQ minis short 14,792.50, partial 14,675, exit 14,647). He closes by defining internal range liquidity (FVG in discount of the range) vs external range liquidity (stops beyond the range lows) as the partial and final targets.

## Concepts
### Market efficiency paradigm (smart money vs uninformed retail)  (aliases ICT uses: "market efficiency paradigm", "informed/smart money", "speculative uninformed money")
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Two camps: a small circle of informed/smart money and a large collective of speculative uninformed money, with constant inflow of new money and outflow as accounts blow up. Smart money uses no secret indicators (no stochastics/RSI); it looks at time and price, and time is the most crucial element (time of day builds the likelihood of volatility and of short-term reversals). Smart money "cannibalizes" retail, which is typically wrong on bias and on stop placement. Smart money's question is the narrative: is the daily range going higher, and how — e.g., lower first to sucker shorts and run sell stops so they can acquire longs, then rally into the afternoon/close. Using retail liquidity this way is the market efficiency paradigm.
- **How to identify:** n/a.
- **How to use:** "We look to enter longs where retail sells; shorts where retail buys." "We anticipate price seeking opposing liquidity." Don't trade patterns for their own sake, indicator readings, or momentum.
- **Example in this video:** He notes the e-mini markets' 4:00 PM algorithmic run "today" was relentless (one of the times he gave earlier).
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:02](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2) — enter longs where retail sells; [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=100) — price seeks opposing liquidity; two camps; [0:03:16](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=196) — time and price, time most important; [0:06:24](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=384) — narrative: lower first to sucker shorts, then rally.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Trend is your friend" fails at the end of a trend; every rule has an exception — that's experience.

### Retail stop-placement conundrum
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Retail shorts either use no stop or don't know where to put it above an old high: too shallow and it's hit prematurely; too high and price spikes through to reach it, then reverses in their direction after a big loss. This is the trap smart money exploits.
- **How to identify:** Old highs/lows with obvious stops just beyond.
- **How to use:** Anticipate runs on those stops before the real move.
- **Example in this video:** n/a.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:52](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=292); [0:05:38](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=338).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Bearish ICT Fair Value Gap (SIBI) — exact definition  (aliases ICT uses: "bearish ICT fair value gap", "institutional order flow pattern")
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** Three-candle formation, preferably after a run above an old high. Candle 1's low is traded below by candle 2; candle 3 makes an extended low below candle 2 but its high does NOT trade back up to candle 1's low. The space between candle 1's low and candle 3's high was traded only by candle 2 — only sell side was offered there, buy side was not efficiently offered. Paint roller analogy: rolling down leaves porous pockets; you roll back up over the same area to fix it — the algorithm returns to the area for efficient delivery. When price trades back into it and you're bearish, that's a short signal. Optimal formations come after a run into buy-side liquidity (above a single high or multiple highs such as a double top); nothing before that candle matters except that an old high was run. The FVG is smart money's "saving grace" if they missed selling above the high.
- **How to identify:** Label candles 1, 2, 3. Gap resides in candle 2. Upper boundary = low of candle 1. Lower boundary = high of candle 3.
- **How to use:** Easiest entry: sell limit just above candle 3's high (one tick, or one to two ticks/handle, "whatever you believe is ideal"; one tick removes guesswork). Stop above candle 1's high or above candle 2's high (whichever you can afford); while learning, use the wider stop. If the risk is too much, don't take the trade.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3 2022 1m (see worked example).
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=672) — three-candle rules; [0:12:47](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=767) — paint roller analogy; [0:15:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=912) — optimal after run into BSL; [0:16:46](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1006) — boundaries, limit entry, stop.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Experienced students know how to reduce the stop; he deliberately teaches the wide stop to protect beginners. He insists it not be renamed or repackaged.

### Bullish ICT Fair Value Gap (BISI)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Mirror image: three candles; low of the FVG = candle 1's high; high of the FVG = candle 3's low; gap formed by candle 2. Expected after a run below an old low or multiple lows (sell-side liquidity), then a break above a short-term high closing above it with energetic displacement.
- **How to identify:** As above.
- **How to use:** Buy the retrace into it (limit just below candle 3's low); stop below candle 1/2.
- **Example in this video:** Used as the first target in the NQ short (a bullish FVG in discount).
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:18](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1338); [0:23:06](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1386).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Market Structure Shift with displacement; displacement high/low range  (aliases ICT uses: "displacement high", "displacement low", "trade idea being birthed")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Bearish MSS: price trades higher, retraces a little, runs above an old high (or the initial short-term high), then breaks below the short-term low — the "new trade idea is born" (still no entry). "Quick" = displacement: energetic, a real willingness to go lower, preferably a big beefy candle that closes below the low; a wick through and back is not convincing (it might go higher and make another high). The high that ran liquidity = displacement high; the low/close beyond the short-term low = displacement low. The FVG to trade must form in between these. No FVG in that range → no trade: wait or check another market (one of them will have it every trading day). Applies on all timeframes. Bullish is reversed.
- **How to identify:** Liquidity run → energetic close beyond short-term swing → look for FVG within displacement high-to-low range.
- **How to use:** Entry at the FVG per the rules above.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3 1m (below).
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:21](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1101) — bearish MSS concept; [0:19:08](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1148) — quick = displacement, prefer close below; [0:19:55](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1195) — displacement high/low; FVG hunted between; [0:21:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1289) — no FVG = no trade; forms every trading day.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Refines Ep. 3 (where the MSS candle didn't need to close beyond the high): here a close beyond with displacement is preferred/more significant.

### 9:30 open: first run often opposite the real move
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** If nothing happened by 9:30, don't assume you missed it: equities open at 9:30 and get busy/volatile; usually (not always) the first run at 9:30 is opposite to the real move.
- **How to identify:** Initial 9:30 move against your daily bias, often building relative equal lows/highs and inducing traders.
- **How to use:** Wait for that move to run its liquidity and for the MSS in your bias direction.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3: 9:30 volatility creates a low then another low (sell stops building), inducing longs, while the highs above (his interest) stay intact; then the real setup forms.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:36](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1956); [0:33:23](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2003).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** none.

### Top-down execution: 15m → 5/4/3/2/1m

- **How to use:** The same FVG may show on 4m, 2m and 1m with the same entry concept.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3: 4m shows the FVG after the swing low break (entry at candle high, stop above candle 1/swing high); 2m similar; 1m gives three entry chances into the FVG.

### Worked example: NQ short, Feb 3, 2022
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says / what price did:** NQ March 2022 (NQH2022). Bias bearish for the day because price had already gone up a lot on the daily chart. 15m: line at the first swing high left of 8:30. On replay from 8:30: an early run above a short-term high left an FVG that sold off — a scalper could take that, but for the daily range he wanted the 15m high. 9:30: volatile, a low and another low (sell stops building), highs intact. A shallow initial poke above the 15m high line: candles overlap, no FVG, no swing low taken — nothing. Then a higher high above the 15m line; price breaks below the nearest swing low; candles 1-2-3 between the highest high and that break leave a bearish FVG. Entry: limit one tick above candle 3's high; stop above candle 2 or candle 1 (swing high). Price returns into the FVG three times (third nearly fills it), each feeling like it will keep rising (a bold green candle). Targets: the 50% of the move (entry-swing range) with a bullish FVG below in discount = first partial; relative equal lows with sell stops below = next target. About 60 handles in roughly 11 one-minute candles (over $1,000 per mini). A retracement then shakes out traders who trailed stops too tightly before the lows are taken; target around 14,680. From ~14,800 to ~14,680 ≈ 120 handles. His paper trade: short 2 NQ minis at 14,792.50, first partial at 14,675, limit exit at 14,647.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:36](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1956) — early FVG vs the 15m high; [0:34:18](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2058) — initial poke has no FVG; [0:35:06](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2106) — swing low broken, candles 1-2-3; [0:38:14](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2294) — targets: FVG in discount, equal lows; [0:43:24](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2604) — fills 14,792.50 / 14,675 / 14,647.

### Trailing stops: don't move stop until an intermediate-term low is taken
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** After the first partial, leave the stop where it is (use the partial to quench the desire to roll the stop); beginners trailing aggressively get stopped right before the big move. Only once a significant intermediate-term low (on the 1m) is taken out — which is also a full target — may you roll the stop down if holding on.
- **How to identify:** Intermediate-term swing low on the execution timeframe.
- **How to use:** Partial at the first target; hold stop at original until intermediate-term low taken.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3: pullback after the first partial, then lows taken.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:15](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2415) — move designed to shake out tight trailers; [0:41:01](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2461) — don't roll stop until intermediate-term low taken.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** First mention in the series of intermediate-term swings for stop management.

### Internal vs external range liquidity (IRL / ERL) for partials
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Take the price run (low to high) and put a Fib on it: 50% is equilibrium; below is discount. An FVG in the discount, inside the range, is internal range liquidity — first target for partials. The stops below the range's lows are external range liquidity — the final target. "Partials internal range, external range your trade."
- **How to identify:** Range low to range high; FVG inside below 50%; old lows beyond the range.
- **How to use:** Scale out at IRL, take the rest at ERL.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Feb 3: bullish FVG below 50% = IRL partial at 14,675; sell stops under equal lows = ERL exit at 14,647.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1769) — exit strategy using external range liquidity; [0:45:00](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2700) — FVG in discount = IRL; [0:45:49](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2749) — stops below lows = ERL.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Extends Ep. 3's IRL definition with its counterpart ERL.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Bearish FVG: candle 1 low (top) to candle 3 high (bottom); bullish FVG: candle 1 high (bottom) to candle 3 low (top).
- Entry: limit 1 tick beyond candle 3's extreme; stop beyond candle 2 or candle 1 (wider while learning).
- MSS requires: liquidity run → energetic displacement, preferably closing beyond the short-term swing → FVG inside the displacement range. No FVG = no trade.
- The pattern forms every trading day, long and short, in some market.
- 8:30 news; look left of 8:30 on the 15m for the first swing high/low.
- First run at 9:30 is usually opposite the real move.
- Partials at IRL (FVG in discount/premium), final at ERL (stops beyond range).
- Don't trail the stop until an intermediate-term swing is taken.
- MNQ: $2 per handle, $0.50 per tick; NQ $20 per handle.
- TradingView NQH2022 data is delayed unless subscribed (he pays about $4/month; may relate to his professional account — check yourself).

## Glossary candidates
`Market efficiency paradigm` — viewing price as smart money using uninformed retail liquidity; efficient delivery of price to opposing liquidity.
`Institutional order flow` — ICT's term for the FVG pattern as the visible footprint of order flow.
`Displacement high / displacement low` — the extremes of the energetic move that shifts structure; the FVG must form between them.
`Candle 1 / 2 / 3` — labels for the FVG's three candles; the gap is in candle 2.
`Saving grace` — the FVG as smart money's re-entry if they missed the high/low.
`Internal range liquidity (IRL)` — FVG/stops inside the current range (partials).
`External range liquidity (ERL)` — stops beyond the current range's highs/lows (final target).
