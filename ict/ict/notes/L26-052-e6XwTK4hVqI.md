## Concepts

### High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions (HRLRC)
- **Kind:** observation + market condition
- **What ICT says:** When the market opens significantly higher (large premium gap) and price is trapped between multiple buy-side liquidity pools (relative equal highs above), it's in a "high resistance liquidity run condition." In this condition, upside continuation is difficult; sellers are defending at multiple levels. Shorts are less attractive because there's little room to run downward. When price gets stuck oscillating between the opening price and these buy-side pools, it's best to step aside and wait for clarity.
- **How to identify:** (1) Large premium gap at open, (2) Multiple relative equal highs above current price, (3) Price oscillating up/down without breaking cleanly in either direction, (4) Bodies struggling to move in any direction (whippy candles).
- **How to use:** Risk management. Recognize the condition and avoid trading it. Profits will be small if you catch any move; risk is high because price will whipsaw. Best to wait for the condition to resolve.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opens ~450 handles premium. Multiple REH zones above. All day, price bounces between the opening price and these REH zones without committing. Trading in this condition yields small profits and frequent stops.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:00](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=0) — noting large premium gap; [0:07:11](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=431) — difficulty navigating; [0:11:57](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=717) — "very difficult time to do anything of significance"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** HRLRC is not a reversal signal; it's a "wait and see" condition. Many traders force trades here and lose money.

### Simplified Trading Model for Beginners (Half-Gap Rule)
- **Kind:** teaching technique
- **What ICT says:** (Demonstrated with Caleb) A beginner trader should start with a very simple model: (1) Look at the opening range gap (9:30 open to 4:14 previous close), (2) Identify the direction (gap up = look for shorts to half-gap; gap down = look for longs to half-gap), (3) Wait for a PD array to be traded to (hourly BISI, relative equal lows, etc.), (4) Enter short/long into that array, with target = half-gap (consequent encroachment of ORG). This removes decision-making and focus on simple mechanics.
- **How to identify:** Mark the opening range gap, calculate consequent encroachment (midpoint).
- **How to use:** Entry signal. When a PD array is traded to and direction aligns with gap direction, enter. Target = gap midpoint.
- **Example in this video:** ORG gap = 450 handles. Half-gap (CE) = 225 handles below open (approx). Caleb aims to short into the hourly BISI, target half-gap. Simple, mechanical, no overthinking.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:47](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=47) — explaining the model; [0:03:12](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=192) — executing shorts to half-gap target
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This model requires a "respectable amount of range" (30–40 handles minimum) to yield reasonable profits. If gap is too small, pass.

### Hourly Buy-Side Imbalance Sell-Side Inefficiency as Key PD Array
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** A BISI from the 1-hour chart (e.g., Wednesday noon ET) that aligns with a swing low on the hourly is a powerful reference point. It combines both gap structure (BISI) and swing structure (old low), making it more significant than a pure gap. Use this as an entry zone.
- **How to identify:** Look at 1-hour chart; find an up-close candle followed by a gap down. That gap is the BISI. Check if it aligns with a swing low on the daily/weekly. Double confirmation = strong level.
- **How to use:** Entry zone. When price trades into this BISI, enter in the direction of the model (short if model is bearish, long if bullish).
- **Example in this video:** Wednesday noon 1-hour BISI aligns with an old swing low. ICT enters shorts into this level multiple times, treating it as the mechanical entry zone per Caleb's model.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:36](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=96) — identifying the hourly BISI; [0:02:22](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=142) — explaining why it matters
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Hourly BISI carries more weight than intraday gaps; it persists across days.

### Displacement and First Presented FVG Selection Rules
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** The first presented FVG is NOT always at 9:31 ET. If there's a displacement (a large gap down below a swing low, or a large move below a level), the first FVG might form later. The rule: use the candle that creates the displacement as the anchor for the first presented FVG, not the 9:30 candle. This requires visual inspection and understanding of displacement.
- **How to identify:** (1) Note the 9:30 candle, (2) Check if a later candle creates a larger gap below a swing low (displacement), (3) If yes, that later candle's FVG is the "true" first presented FVG for modeling purposes.
- **How to use:** Projection and target planning. Use the correct first presented FVG, not the 9:30 gap, as a reference level for the day.
- **Example in this video:** The 9:30 candle shows a gap, but a later candle creates a larger displacement below the swing low. That later candle's FVG is used as the first presented FVG, not the 9:30 one.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:21](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=621) — identifying true first presented FVG; [0:12:43](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=763) — confirming the concept
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a detail often overlooked; it requires practice to identify displacement correctly.

### Inversion FVG Transitions in Choppy Markets
- **Kind:** observation
- **What ICT says:** In choppy, high-resistance conditions, bearish FVGs may fail to deliver lower and instead invert. When an FVG that was supposed to be bearish becomes an inversion (price trades above it), the market is showing it wants to trade higher. Transitions from bearish → inversion → bullish are common in HRLRC.
- **Example in this video:** An FVG that was supposed to facilitate selling instead allows price to trade into it from above, transitioning to inversion, then eventually trading above the gap entirely, become bullish. This transition confirms the failed bearish hypothesis.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:32](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=572) — explaining inversion potential; [0:11:10](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=670) — inversion transitions happening
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Inversion transitions are common when the market is in HRLRC; don't force a bearish view if inversions are clearly forming.

### 1-Hour Buy-Side Imbalance Sell-Side Inefficiency Resistance in Choppy Conditions
- **Kind:** observation
- **What ICT says:** When price reaches the hourly BISI's upper half or consequent encroachment and stalls there (instead of breaking above), it's a sign of resistance. Price may oscillate around this level for hours without committing upward.
- **Example in this video:** Price rallies up, reaches the 1-hour BISI's consequent encroachment, touches the high, then fails to break above. Instead, it spends time oscillating around the midpoint. This is not a breakout; it's resistance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:10](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=670) — price at BISI resistance; [0:11:57](https://youtu.ye/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=717) — stalling and whipsaw
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** When price stalls at a resistance like this, don't trade; wait for a clear breakout or breakdown.

### Participation Discipline: When NOT to Trade
- **Kind:** rule + philosophy
- **What ICT says:** In choppy, HRLRC conditions with small ranges and multiple false breakouts, it's better to NOT participate. Close the charts, step away, let the condition resolve. The few handles you might capture aren't worth the stress and whipsaws. This is a key risk-management principle: knowing when to sit out is as important as knowing when to enter.
- **Example in this video:** ICT trades in and out multiple times, each with small profits or stops. By mid-day, he decides to close the charts and spend time with family. He explicitly says this type of choppy action isn't worth participating in.

- **What ICT says:** The gap between Monday's 4:14 PM close and Tuesday's 9:30 open is a reference level for overnight trading and the next day's opening. If price comes back down into this gap overnight, it suggests the market is consolidating and may continue in a direction aligned with that gap tomorrow.

- **Profit expectation in good conditions:** 10–15 handles minimum (per mini contract = $300).
- **High Resistance Liquidity Run Condition:** Multiple REH above, price oscillating, no follow-through = don't trade.
- **Hourly BISI + swing low alignment:** Strong entry zone.
- **Inversion FVG transitions:** Common in choppy markets; don't force bearish if inversions forming.
- **Participation discipline:** If conditions are whippy and ranges small, sit out.
- **Overnight ORG:** Reference for consolidation and next-day bias.

## Glossary candidates
`High resistance liquidity run condition (HRLRC)` — a market state where price opens significantly higher (large premium gap) and is trapped between multiple buy-side liquidity pools (REH), making upside continuation difficult. Price oscillates without direction; trading is choppy and low-profit.

`Half-gap model` — a simplified beginner entry model: (1) open gap (ORG), (2) wait for PD array, (3) target = gap midpoint (CE). Requires 30–40 handle minimum range to be viable.

`Displacement-based first presented FVG` — the first presented FVG is not always at 9:30; if a later candle creates a larger gap below a swing low (displacement), that candle's FVG is the "true" first presented FVG.

`Inversion transition` — when an FVG that was bearish (should send price lower) instead allows price to trade above it (inversion), indicating the market wants to go higher. Transitions happen in HRLRC.

`Participation discipline` — knowing when NOT to trade; in HRLRC with small ranges, it's better to sit out and wait for clarity than grind small profits and endure whipsaws.

`Overnight ORG` — the opening range gap from the previous day's 4:14 PM close to the next day's 9:30 open (in electronic hours), used as a reference for overnight consolidation and next-day bias.
