## Concepts

### Relative Equal Highs and Lows as Liquidity Pools
- **Kind:** definition + rule + example
- **What ICT says:** (Refresher) When two or more highs (or lows) form at the same level, that's a relative equal high/low. Each represents a pool of liquidity (buy-side above a high, sell-side below a low). The market will target these liquidity pools. If it initially fails to reach them, that failure is itself a signal—it means that direction isn't being favored; look for a reversal instead.
- **How to identify:** Mark all swing highs/lows from the current session and prior days; identify any at approximately the same price level.
- **How to use:** Bias and target planning. If trying to go long, expect it to reach the relative equal high. If it fails before getting there, flip the bias to short and target the relative equal lows. Conversely for shorts.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opens up; ICT expects it to run to relative equal highs. Price immediately sends it lower instead (failure of his bias). He pivots: now expects a run to relative equal lows, which it hits by midday. Multiple REL zones are marked: confirms the eventual target.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** If initial move fails before hitting a level, don't force it—wait for the market to deliver the alternate setup.

### Retracement Measurement via Wick Fibonacci
- **Kind:** technique + example
- **What ICT says:** When price makes a high and then retraces lower, measure the wick from the high down to the low of that retracement leg. Apply Fibonacci from high to low of the wick. The 0.618 (golden ratio) level often projects how far the next leg can travel downward. It's a "secret sauce" for predicting downside targets before price gets there.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify a high and low of a retracement, (2) Apply Fibonacci from high to low (wick range), (3) Extend downward using the ratio.
- **How to use:** Downside projection. Know how far you expect price to go lower before placing shorts or sizing your position.
- **Example in this video:** NQ high to retracement low = X points. Applied 0.618 ratio projects downside target. "Boom, there you go," ICT says—price hits that projected level with precision. Even the bodies (which tend to be stronger than wicks) respect it; wicks exceed it but bodies stop short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:43](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=103) — demonstrating retracement measurement and projection
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The bodies/wicks distinction is key—wicks can exceed the projection, but the "real" order flow (bodies) respects it.

### Gray Pole (Wick Pattern Signal)
- **Kind:** new coinage + observation
- **What ICT says:** When you have consecutive wicks in a specific pattern (usually two or more wicks measured and graded by Fibonacci), their converging midpoints (0.5 levels) can form what ICT calls a "gray pole." This is a zone where price might gap or move quickly because liquidity is concentrated but "unnoticed" by retail traders who use other methods. It's a hidden liquidity cluster.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify two or more significant wicks, (2) Apply Fibonacci to each wick (high to low), (3) Mark the 0.5 levels (CE of each wick), (4) If they align or are very close, you have a gray pole.
- **How to use:** Breakout/gap prediction. When price reaches a gray pole, expect a directional break through it (either up or down, depending on context). Use it as a stop-loss target or profit-taking level.
- **Example in this video:** NQ shows two wicks graded separately. Their 0.5 levels align, forming a "gray pole." ICT shows it as a "beautiful gray pole." When price approaches it, it breaks decisively (gaps through). This is the moment ICT enters heavily ("I was in there accumulating and building position").
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:47](https://youtu.ye/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=347) — identifying and explaining gray pole; [0:06:36](https://youtu.ye/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=396) — price action at the gray pole
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is ICT's coinage; highly specific to his framework. Requires practice to spot.

### Change in State of Delivery (CISD) via Opening Price Selection
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** When identifying a key level or reversal, sometimes the opening price of a candle is more significant than its close because that opening matches or aligns with prior significant levels (e.g., relative equal lows, liquidity pools). Using the opening of a down-close candle instead of another consecutive up-close shows that the down-close is where "true" delivery changed. It's a technical choice, not arbitrary.
- **How to identify:** (1) Look at the opening of recent candles, (2) Compare them to prior support/resistance, (3) If an opening aligns with a significant level, it's a CISD candidate.
- **How to use:** Confirmation and entry point selection. If you're building a position, using the opening of a candle that changed direction (vs. the close) can be more precise.
- **Example in this video:** ICT selects a down-close candle's opening (not the close) as the CISD because that opening aligns with the relative equal lows he identified. This is why he picks *that* candle instead of consecutive up-closes. It shows where state of delivery truly changed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:32](https://youtu.ye/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=152) — explaining candle selection for CISD
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not all down-closes are equally valid; pick the one whose opening aligns with key levels.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap Entry and Bounce
- **Kind:** example
- **What ICT says:** (Demonstrated) When price trades down into a bearish FVG and you're short, if price bounces back up into that FVG, it becomes an iFVG (inversion). You can add shorts into the iFVG on the rally. If it breaks back out above, you can stop out. If it closes back inside the iFVG lower half, you're still valid.
- **Example in this video:** NQ trades down and creates an iFVG. On a bounce, ICT considers adding. Price does bounce back up into the inversion, he adds (building position). It comes back down; he's then in profit. Later it pulls back up to relative equal highs—more opportunity to scale out or add.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:32](https://youtu.ye/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=152) — adding into iFVG on bounce
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Timing the add is crucial; do it on the pullback/bounce, not on the initial break.

### Session Type Prediction Based on Pre-Market Behavior
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** (Related to prior lesson) If the pre-market (7–9 AM) is consolidating (boxy, choppy) instead of trending, expect the main session (9:30+) to trend more decisively. If the pre-market is trending, expect the main session to consolidate. Today's pre-market was choppy (consolidating), so the main session should trend—and indeed, it did trend down.
- **How to identify:** Observe pre-market range and direction. Is it making one directional move, or is it choppy/rangy?
- **How to use:** Session bias. If pre-market is choppy, plan for a directional day. Increase position size, be ready for more decisive moves.
- **Example in this video:** Pre-market 7–9 AM was inside a range, consolidating. ICT predicts "one directional movement" in the main session. Price indeed trends down most of the morning, hitting the relative equal lows he projected.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- **Initial bias:** Look for REL highs on a break above; if failed, reverse bias to REL lows.
- **Retracement measurement:** Use wick Fibonacci 0.618 ratio to project downside targets; bodies typically respect this level.
- **Gray pole:** Consecutive wick 0.5 levels converging → hidden liquidity, directional break likely.
- **CISD:** Use the opening of a candle that changes state (not arbitrary close) when it aligns with key levels.
- **Pre-market consolidation → main session trending.** Pre-market trending → main session choppy.
- **Economic calendar:** CPI, PPI, other high-impact events → increased volatility, larger ranges, faster moves.

## Glossary candidates
`Gray pole` — (ICT coinage) a zone where consecutive wick Fibonacci 0.5 levels (consequent encroachment of two or more wicks) align or converge, creating a hidden liquidity cluster. Often precedes directional breaks or gaps because retail traders using other methods don't identify it.

`Retracement measurement` — using Fibonacci (typically 0.618 ratio) from a swing high to a retracement low to project how far the next downleg can extend. Bodies typically respect this projection; wicks may exceed it.

`CISD via opening` — selecting the opening price of a candle that changes state of delivery (reversal candle) when that opening aligns with key levels (support, REL, liquidity). More precise than using the close.

`Session type prediction` — inferring whether the main 9:30+ session will trend or consolidate based on the pre-market 7–9 AM behavior. Consolidating pre-market typically means trending main session, and vice versa.
