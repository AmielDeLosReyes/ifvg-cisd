## Concepts

### Volume Imbalance and Sell-Side Imbalance / Buy-Side Inefficiency (aliases: SIBI, sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency, volume gap)
- **Kind:** definition + identification tool
- **What ICT says:** When a candle opens significantly lower (or higher) than the previous candle's close, that gap is a volume imbalance—an inefficiency. If a candle opens below the prior close and shows sell-side imbalance (sellers willing at lower prices) paired with buy-side inefficiency (no buyers at those lower prices), it creates a gap. This inefficiency is a target for reversal or draw.
- **How to identify:** Look at the prior candle's close and the current candle's open; if they don't align, there's a volume imbalance. Use the "toggle" feature (zoom in/out) to see gaps more clearly.
- **How to use:** These gaps act as targets. If price opens down into a sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency, expect price to eventually fill or bounce from it. Use it as a reversal zone or draw target.
- **Example in this video:** NQ had a volume imbalance at the open; this was noted as sell-side imbalance with buy-side inefficiency. Market later returned to fill/test this gap.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Use toggle (zoom in/out) to make gaps visible; they're institutional reference points.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (aliases: iFVG, bullish/bearish inversion, bodies respect one side)
- **Kind:** definition + order flow rule
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG is a gap where bodies trade on one side and wicks reach the other, but bodies never commit to the wick's side. Example: gap up, bodies in lower half, wicks touch upper half but no body reaches it = bearish inversion FVG. It's expected to reverse. Key: watch where bodies trade vs. where wicks reach.
- **How to identify:** Mark a gap; then observe: do bodies commit to one side while wicks reach the opposite? If bodies never reach consequent encroachment on the wick's side, it's an inversion.
- **How to use:** Inversion FVGs are rejection signals. If price failed to commit beyond CE, expect reversal. Use them as bounce targets and as confirmation of directional bias on retracements.
- **Example in this video:** Dollar rallied into an inversion FVG; bodies stayed in lower half, wicks reached high but no body commitment. This was bearish, and price later reversed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:24](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=144) (rally through CE of inversion FVG, kind of inversion FVG) · [0:05:36](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=336) (bodies using inefficiency, but bodies never came back to CE; bearish).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The key is body commitment; wicks are noise; bodies are order flow.

### Algorithmic Price Delivery vs. Buying/Selling Pressure (aliases: algorithmic execution, price delivery, order flow)
- **Kind:** conceptual framework
- **What ICT says:** Retail traders think "buying pressure = price up, selling pressure = price down." This is backwards. The market is zero-sum: for every buyer, there's a seller. What matters is WHERE the algorithm places price—whether it's sweeping liquidity (stops, limit orders) or drawing into inefficiencies. The algorithm is not creating supply/demand; it's routing price to specific institutional liquidity zones. Recognize this and read price action accordingly.
- **How to identify:** Instead of asking "is there buying or selling pressure?", ask "where is the algorithm routing price?" Use order flow rules (body placement, CE commits, displacement) to read intent.
- **How to use:** Use algorithmic framework for prediction. If bodies show weakness at a level, expect continuation down (algorithmic delivery lower). If bodies are strong and reach CE, expect continuation up.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: when price failed to get bodies above CE on an up move, Michael noted "Algorithmically, this is weak" = expect reversal down. Later, price delivered down as the algorithm routed to lower inefficiencies.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:31](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=151) (net sum zero game; it's not buying/selling pressure, it's the algorithm moving price to higher prices) · [0:05:36](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=336) (bodies never reached CE; is that bullish or bearish based on Michael's order flow rules? Bearish—algorithmic) · [0:11:07](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=667) (failed to get CE with a body; is that bullish or bearish based on algorithmic order flow? Bearish).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Flip the narrative: it's not supply/demand; it's institutional routing of price to liquidity.

### Draw on Liquidity (aliases: draw, short-term draw, best-case target, near-term draw)
- **Kind:** rule + targeting
- **What ICT says:** A draw is when price moves toward an identified inefficiency or liquidity pool with institutional conviction (sustained move, clean structure). Michael identifies two draws per setup: near-term draw (lower-probability, closer target) and best-case scenario (higher-probability if momentum continues, further away). Use these to define targets and partial profit zones.
- **How to identify:** Identify inefficiencies at multiple levels; the nearest one is near-term draw; the furthest is best case.
- **How to use:** Set targets at draws. If price is moving toward a draw and showing strength, expect it to reach the draw. Use near-term as first target; best case as runner target.
- **Example in this video:** Euro Dollar: near-term draw was a minor sell-side liquidity pool; best-case scenario was a deeper inefficiency. Michael would take partial at near-term, hold runner to best case.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:13](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=193) (best-case scenario short-term, near-term draw; minor sell-side liquidity pool) · [0:06:24](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=384) (draw down here; idea to share, not hard and fast).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Draws don't always complete in the timeframe you expect; they may take days or require additional structure to materialize.

### Minor Sell-Side Liquidity Pool (aliases: minor SSL, secondary target)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Within a daily range or between major inefficiencies, there may be a smaller pool of sell-side liquidity (resting sell orders at an intermediate level). This is a minor target—a bounce point or partial profit zone, not the ultimate target. Use it as a first-take profit zone; then run the rest toward best-case (the major inefficiency).
- **How to identify:** Scan the daily chart between the opening range and a lower major inefficiency; if there's a swing high or clustering of small inefficiencies, that's a minor SSL.
- **How to use:** Treat minor SSL as your first target for shorts. Take bulk of position here; hold runners for best case. If minor SSL is taken out, expect a continuation to major inefficiency.
- **Example in this video:** Euro Dollar had a minor sell-side liquidity pool at an intermediate level; Michael noted "That would be enough for me if I was a bearish forex trader" to take considerable profit there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:02](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=242) (minor sell-side liquidity pool; take considerable profit there).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Minor zones are stepping stones; they're not final targets, just profitable exit points.

### Displacement (aliases: strong move, aggressive break, breakout)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A displacement is a strong, directional move that breaks through structure with conviction. When bodies are committed and showing aggression (not wicking back into inefficiencies), you have a displacement. Displacements predict continuation; the direction of the displacement tells you where price will go next.
- **How to identify:** Look for candles with minimal wicks and bodies committed to one direction; that's a displacement.
- **How to use:** When you see a displacement, expect the direction to continue. Displacements often precede the largest moves.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil had "a strong displacement below these relative equal lows"—bodies broke below and continued; no reversal; this signaled lower prices ahead.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:24](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=384) (strong displacement below relative equal lows).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Displacements are conviction moves; they're high-probability continuations of the direction.

### Event Horizon (aliases: midpoint, old low to next target)
- **Kind:** measurement tool
- **What ICT says:** Event Horizon is the midpoint between two price levels. When used in daily analysis, it's often the midpoint between an old low and the next target/inefficiency below. This midpoint is a pause zone or bounce point; price often pauses here before continuing to the next target.
- **How to identify:** Find an old low and a target below it; calculate the midpoint price.
- **How to use:** Use as an intermediate pause/bounce zone. If you're short and price is at Event Horizon, expect a pause or bounce before continuation to the next target.
- **Example in this video:** Bitcoin: Michael used old low to next target; Event Horizon is the midpoint between them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:50](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=1190) (event horizon concept: old low down to next target or pool of liquidity).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Event Horizon is a technical level, less critical than institutional liquidity levels.

### Judas Swing (aliases: sucker run, trap rally, news-driven spike)
- **Kind:** definition + psychology
- **What ICT says:** A Judas swing is a rally or spike in price that lures retail traders to buy ("come here, Judas!") before the market reverses sharply. Often triggered by news or a brief algorithmic spike. The move is designed to trap long positions. Recognize it and fade it (go short into the spike).
- **How to identify:** Watch for spikes higher that fail to stay above previous highs; bodies don't reach upper inefficiencies; gaps higher on open but then reverse lower.
- **How to use:** Fade Judas swings. When you see a news-driven spike or a brief rally that fails to sustain, short into it with conviction. These often deliver large moves down.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: Trump announced military action; oil gapped higher (Judas swing to trap bulls). Michael tweeted "Fade the Cheeto"—fade the Judas swing. Price indeed reversed lower and delivered a $20,000 per contract move down.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:53](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=713) (Trump announcement; market tried to go higher; Michael faded it; strong sell-off) · [0:12:41](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=761) (last-ditch attempt to get people thinking it's going up; they're trying to trap traders).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Judas swings are often news-driven; the underlying order flow is bearish despite the spike.

### Intermarket Relationships (aliases: inverse correlation, Dollar vs. Commodities)
- **Kind:** macro context
- **What ICT says:** Different markets move in opposite directions based on macro conditions. Example: US Dollar Index often moves inverse to commodities (oil, bitcoin). When Dollar rallies, commodities weaken (and vice versa). Use intermarket relationships to confirm directional bias and to avoid trading against correlated moves.
- **How to identify:** Track DXY (Dollar Index), EUR/USD, GBP/USD, Crude Oil, Bitcoin simultaneously. Note which pairs are correlated or inverse.
- **How to use:** If you're bullish on DXY, expect commodities down. If you're short crude oil, expect DXY strong. Use this as a bias confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** Michael noted that Euro Dollar was in a retracement to the daily bullish inversion FVG (bearish euro = bullish dollar). Pound Dollar was also weakening (dollar strong). Crude Oil was plunging (dollar strong = commodity weak). All moves were consistent with a bullish dollar bias that Michael predicted on June 7.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Macro relationships are general rules; geopolitical events can override them temporarily, but order flow eventually reasserts the correlation.

### Relative Equal Lows (aliases: REL, equal lows, old low reference)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Two or more swing lows at approximately the same price level. These are institutional reference points—clusters of stop losses and limit orders. When price breaks below RELs, it signals weakness; the sweep is a "liquidity grab" before continuation.
- **How to identify:** Scan recent price action (5–20 candles back on daily); find two or more lows within a few ticks of each other.
- **How to use:** RELs are targets for shorts. When you're short and price approaches a REL, expect a test or sweep. Use as a partial-profit zone or a confirmation of directional continuation.
- **Example in this video:** Crude Oil had relative equal lows; price broke below them with a displacement, signaling institutional participation and lower targets ahead.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:24](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=384) (strong displacement below relative equal lows).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** RELs are liquidity clusters; sweeps through them often accelerate the move.

### Futures Contract Months (aliases: contract symbols, H, M, U, Z)
- **Kind:** reference information
- **What ICT says:** Index futures have four main contract months: March (H), June (M), September (U), December (Z). Always note which contract you're analyzing; the continuous contract (rolling between months) can show different behavior than individual month contracts. For NQ in mid-June, June contract (M) is expiring soon; September (U) becomes the primary contract.
- **How to identify:** Check the futures ticker; note the symbol (ZNU for Sept NQ, ZNM for June NQ, etc.).
- **How to use:** Use the continuous contract for long-term bias; use individual months when near expiration to avoid roll gaps. Michael checks if the June (M) contract made a higher high vs. the September (U) contract to assess continuity of rallies.
- **Example in this video:** NQ June (M) contract vs. September (U) contract; Michael noted that neither made higher highs compared to the recent low, so the continuous contract showed the rally potential.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:38](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=998) (NQ front month contract September, symbol U) · [0:16:38](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=998) (contract symbols: H=March, M=June, U=September, Z=December) · [0:17:26](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=1046) (June contract not higher high; September contract not higher high; continuous shows rally potential).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Always check both the month and the continuous contract; don't rely on one without the other.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Body placement determines algorithmic intent: bodies above CE = bullish; bodies below CE = bearish.
- Inversion FVG: bodies on one side, wicks on other = rejection; expect reversal.
- Draw on liquidity: near-term draw first, then best-case scenario for runners.
- Minor sell-side liquidity: take considerable profit here; hold runners for major inefficiencies.
- Displacement: continuation signal; expect direction to continue.
- Judas swings: news-driven spikes are traps; fade them with confidence.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Watch for Judas swings:** News-driven rallies (especially from geopolitical announcements) are often trap moves. Fade them by shorting into the spike with conviction.
- **Use daily for bias, intraday for execution:** Michael finalized his daily analysis on June 7; by June 18, all predictions had materialized across multiple markets. Lesson: daily timeframe is where high-probability setups form; intraday is where you execute them.
- **Patience on draws:** Draws may take days to play out. Don't get impatient if a target doesn't hit within hours. Check overnight (Asia, London) for continuation.
- **Partial profits at minor zones:** Take bulk of profit at near-term/minor targets; hold runners for best-case. This removes emotion and locks in gains while staying exposed to larger moves.
- **Check multiple markets:** Don't over-focus on one. NQ, ES, crude, crypto, forex—all moved inline with macro bias. Trader who reads one market gets one perspective; trader who reads multiple gets macro confirmation.
- **Simplify:** Michael covered four markets efficiently by focusing on daily inefficiencies and order flow. No intraday chop discussed; just the high-probability zones.
