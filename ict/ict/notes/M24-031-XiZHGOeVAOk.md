## Summary
ICT trades the December NQ micro and mini side by side on a day that opens with a several-hundred-handle opening range gap up (Sept 19, 2024), and uses it to teach a deliberately small, rule-bound "laboratory" routine for a beginner: use the micro contract, aim for just $75–$100 per day on one contract, and use the first 1-minute FVG of the day, the opening range gap midpoint, and a 20-handle minimum run as the whole framework. The key market lesson is that after a huge gap higher, price first drops to make an initial high of the day, then runs that high to trap breakout buyers (stops above = buy stops), then retraces deeper toward the gap's upper quadrant/midpoint. He lays out a binary "if / then / else" decision tree from two data points (prior-day settlement and the 9:30 open) and says to sit still if neither scenario is present. The rest is process and psychology: journal how it feels to hold or not hold, pass on setups that offer less than 20 handles, scale slowly, and do not chase big funded-account payouts. The final stretch re-states how macros, OTE and PD arrays combine, and that ICT does not trade classic support/resistance, trend lines, Gann, Elliott, or "zones".

## Concepts

### Opening Range Gap (RTH) — huge gap handling, quadrants and midpoint draw  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap, "mid gap", upper quadrant level, ORG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The opening range gap is the space between the prior day's settlement/last RTH print (he says "414", i.e. 4:14 PM ET close, caption) and the 9:30 ET opening print. Two data points, two "arrays".
  - If today opens ABOVE yesterday's settlement, the bias/initial draw is LOWER toward the midpoint of that gap (50%, "mid gap" = consequent encroachment of the gap). It does not need to get there that day or that morning; it is only the bias/framework.
  - He claims an approximately 70% tendency ("built-in 70% advantage", "70% strike rate") for price to gravitate to the middle of the opening range gap within the first 30 minutes of the session (he says "first 10... sorry, first 30 minutes").
  - He draws quadrants of the gap: the upper quadrant level (75% of the gap measured from the gap low to the opening high) is the first logical reaching point on a drop, then the mid gap (50%).
  - A very large gap (several hundred handles) changes the plan: it "gives the market a chance to give the public a reason to chase" — price is likely to make a drop first (initial high of day), then run above that initial high to hit buy stops, then drop for the deeper retracement. With a small gap (he says 40–60 handles) the first fair-value-gap short would already have been an entry for him; with this gap he waits.
- **How to identify:** compare the 9:30 open with the prior settlement; draw the gap box; mark 75% / 50% (and 25%); mark the initial high of the day made during the 9:30–10:00 opening range.
- **How to use:** use the gap midpoint as the first draw; do not demand that it trades there. Use the 75% level as the interim/partial level (he describes it as a logical place for a partial, "if you were in a day that didn't have this multiple-100-handle opening gap you could take this as a partial and then aim for the upper quadrant level").
- **Example in this video:** NQ December (micro and mini), 15-second and 1-minute charts, Sept 19, 2024. Open gapped up several hundred handles. Mid gap stated as 19844.5 on the micro and 19843.5 on the mini (about one full handle apart because of the two contracts). Price opened, dropped initially, then ran the initial high (he marks a 19926 level at one point), then retraced.

### Initial high of day trap / buy-stop run after a huge gap up  (aliases ICT uses: "amazing trap for retail", running the initial high, stop run of the initial high)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a huge gap higher, expect price to make an initial high, wind down (creating an oversold look to indicators/harmonic traders), then run up and take out that initial high. Breakout buyers who buy above it are "trapped long" and the move above the high is to gather the buy stops/orders so smart money can sell into them and drive price lower toward opposing liquidity (sell-side).
  - Retail likes breakouts and confirmation; buying the highest high gives no reasonable stop and no retracement room.
  - The patient alternative: wait for the initial high to be taken out; a 15-second chart still offers plenty of FVGs and micro-structure turtle soups; you do not have to rush.
  - The old high is "not resistance" — he does not sell at the high; he expects price to go up there, spend time, accumulate orders and then drop. "Targeting" a level is different from treating it as support/resistance.
- **How to identify:** huge opening gap, 9:30–10:00 first push to an initial high, a pullback, then a run through that high; buy stops above.
- **How to use:** do not buy the breakout; wait for the stop run, then use the first FVG / iFVG for shorts targeting the gap midpoint or the sell-side below.
- **Example in this video:** After the first drop, price traded above the initial high (twice made a higher high intraday), the area between the initial high and the new high is shaded as a balanced price range (see below). Buy-side liquidity was cleared and then price reverted toward the first presented FVG/sell-side lows.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:02](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=602) retail breakout trap explained · [0:13:22](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=802) initial high taken, "we're not rushing" · [0:52:33](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3153) second higher high = stop run · [1:00:43](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3643) high is not resistance, it absorbs buy stops
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says that when it becomes a balanced price range the high "will absolutely be just like" a support/resistance book would say (resistance broken turns support), but only in the sense that the BPR pocket forms after the sweep; the remark is jokey and the exact meaning is caption-unclear.

### First presented Fair Value Gap of the day (1-minute) as focus  (aliases ICT uses: first FVG, "the first one that can't hide", initial fair value gap)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - To answer "which FVG do I use?" he removes the choice: use the very first FVG presented on the 1-minute chart after the 9:30 open. Candle-by-candle check from 9:30 (no gap, no gap, ...); the first one that is a true gap is "first presented FVG".
  - Mechanically: wait for the 9:31 candle to close; at 9:32, if the 9:30–9:31–9:32 three-candle pattern leaves a gap around the 9:31 candle, that is the first FVG of the day. That gives the zone where the first setup should form "at or near".
  - He later adds that other time-based FVGs matter too, but this first one is "the first that can't hide" and is enough to start.
  - Keep the FVG gray at first (so no bias is baked in), extend it to the right.
  - Bias off this FVG: the initial focus is middle of the gap (the opening range gap midpoint) — "I'm making it binary".
- **How to identify:** 1m chart from 9:30; three-candle pattern where candle 1's high and candle 3's low (for bullish) do not overlap; the first one after the open.
- **How to use:** use it as the frame for the setup; either a retrace into its lower half / CE (the "model 2022" style entry) or, once price trades back through and below it, as an inversion FVG for shorts.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m Sept 19, 2024: first FVG formed after the 9:30 open; price traded to its CE, rose to fill a smaller FVG above, then dropped below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:26](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=746) candle-by-candle search for the first gap · [1:07:08](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4028) why first FVG · [1:09:31](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4171) 9:31 candle / 9:32 first FVG rule
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Simplifies which FVG to pick. A candle that overlaps (e.g. a later candle's high touching the earlier candle's low) means NO FVG there; he says he cannot use that one.

### Model 2022 two-FVG logic (stacked FVGs)  (aliases ICT uses: "model 2022", "initial fair value gap model 2022")
- **Kind:** entry model (brief application)
- **What ICT says:** if there is an FVG and another smaller FVG just above it, always expect price to go up into the second, higher one. He calls the first entry into the lower FVG followed by the stab into the higher FVG "the model 2022 entry". He tried to time the low of the first FVG and notes that the actual entry was even better because it was outside (below) the FVG, i.e. forgiving.
- **How to identify:** two FVGs stacked, a larger lower one and a smaller one above.
- **How to use:** buy the retrace into the lower FVG, target the upper FVG.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s/1m: price dipped to the lower of the two FVGs (mid / lower half), then "stabbed" into the upper FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:20:59](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1259) two FVGs, "this is model 2022 stuff" · [0:35:19](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2119) he recaps that he pre-called the trap and the 2022 entry
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** only referred to, not re-taught; the reader should cross-reference the 2022 mentorship notes.

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) short after a trap  (aliases ICT uses: "treat it as an inversion fair value gap", work the lower half / CE)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After price drops below the first FVG (the bullish one) it can be treated as an inversion FVG; shorts can be taken from the lower half / consequent encroachment of the iFVG, aiming at sell-side below.
  - Condition for the 15s entry: price must leave the iFVG and stop reaching for premium. Allow ONE candle to touch back into it. After that, the next displacement lower that creates an FVG is the one he would sell on, targeting the range/liquidity (here the minor sell-side low).
  - If the candle that touches back means no clean gap exists (candle 3's high overlaps candle 1's low), that FVG is unusable — "I can't use that one now"; wait for another displacement lower that does leave a gap. Without a clean one, no trade — "can I take a losing trade here? No".
  - Look for bodies staying below the CE of the iFVG; he watches that the bodies do not lay on top of the mid gap.
- **How to identify:** a bullish FVG that price has closed below; retrace into its lower half/CE; rejection.
- **How to use:** entry on a fresh FVG made by the displacement after the touch; target a minor sell-side liquidity at least 20 handles away; risk on a micro can be wider.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s: after the second run higher, price falls below the first FVG; one candle touches back; the next displacement lower leaves no gap because of an overlap so he stands aside; later another one forms.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:30](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=930) either CE of the first FVG then up, or work lower half as iFVG · [0:37:44](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2264) reaction off iFVG CE · [0:38:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2312) conditions for the short · [0:43:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2612) cannot take it without a fresh FVG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The "touch once with one candle" language is a refinement of the iFVG entry; it is stated for 15s execution.

### 20-handle minimum run filter  (aliases ICT uses: "20 handle run potential", "frame a little box with 20 handles")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - For this NQ lesson, a setup is only valid if it offers at least 20 handles (points) to a logical target. First require a directional bias, then find a PD array (FVG, order block, any PD array) from which price can run at least 20 handles.
  - If the available range is 18 handles, he does not take it and the student should feel good about the discipline — "white glove approach".
  - Daily practice: after the session, frame a 20-handle box on the chart and look back to see where entries from an FVG would have yielded 20 handles and whether a logical target (e.g., filling a gap) was within reach.
- **How to identify:** measure entry-to-target; target examples: the low of the minor sell-side pool, upper quadrant of the opening range gap, the next FVG.
- **How to use:** a filter, not a target in itself; with a micro NQ, 20 handles is about $40 per contract at $2/point (arithmetic, not stated) — he instead frames the goal as $75–$100.
- **Example in this video:** the minor sell-side low was just about 20 handles from the proposed entry; the caption gives "20,10 even" as the 20-handle target level (caption unclear).
- **Timestamps:** [0:28:58](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1738) 20 handle run introduced · [0:39:21](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2361) if it doesn't afford 20 handles, pass · [0:46:50](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2810) frame a 20-handle box after the fact
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This 20-handle threshold is specific to this beginner exercise on NQ ("if you watched what I was teaching on the other day").

### Fibonacci external projections (negative extensions) for the run beyond a dealing range  (aliases ICT uses: negative 0.25, negative 0.5, one standard deviation / measured move)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Anchor the Fibonacci on the swing high (highest candle) to the low of the dealing range. For external liquidity beyond the range, the first stepping stone is -0.25. If it makes a measured run, the target is -1 (one "standard deviation" = the range projected below the low). Half of that is -0.5.
  - "How far can we reasonably expect it to go below this low?" — use these levels.
  - He notes -1 here lines up with the upper quadrant of the opening range gap; confluence between Fibonacci and another PD array is what matters.
- **How to identify:** a clear high-to-low leg; project the same distance beyond the low.
- **How to use:** targets/partials beyond a low; with a huge gap, continuation beyond is possible.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s: fib from the highest high down to the low; price traded to -0.25 and bodies stayed around -0.5 area; measured move projected to near the upper quadrant level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:54:08](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3248) dealing range fib anchored high to low · [0:54:55](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3295) -0.25, -0.5, -1 · [0:55:46](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3346) half the run = $75–$100 open profit
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Matches his earlier "-0.25 / -0.5 / -1 (std dev)" projection teaching; here the level is linked to the opening gap quadrant.

### Balanced Price Range from a run into real orders
- **Kind:** example only / refinement
- **What ICT says:** the shaded area between the initial high and the stop-run high is treated as a balanced price range because price ran into real buy orders (breakout buyers) that smart money sold to. It is "not contrived", not a random area — it is where the orders were. Below it: sell-side pools and the first presented FVG.
- **How to identify:** overlapping up and down moves around a stop run (caption description loose).
- **How to use:** price should gravitate toward opposing liquidity (sell-side) because breakout buyers are trapped.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s: price swept the initial high, sold off from there; he highlights the pocket and extends it right.
- **Timestamps:** [0:53:21](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3201) BPR defined from the run · [1:02:17](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3737) pocket becomes BPR
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** definition here is looser than the strict "overlapping bearish and bullish FVG" version — treat as his usage in this video.

### Binary if/then/else decision tree (algorithmic framework)  (aliases ICT uses: "arrays", "data points", "if then else")
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Build trading as programming: define where the data comes from (arrays) → where the first setup will form each day (the first 30 minutes after 9:30) → what the first data point is (settlement vs 9:30 open) → conditions that trigger an action.
  - If the open is above settlement: bias lower to the gap midpoint; wait for 9:31 to close; if at 9:32 an FVG exists, that's the focus; then wait for a setup that meets the 20-handle filter. If neither scenario is present, sit still.
  - "I'm making it binary."
- **How to identify / use:** a checklist before the open each morning.
- **Example in this video:** the whole session.

### Macros / "time and price theory", Fibonacci OTE and PD-array confluence  (aliases ICT uses: macros, 20-minute intervals, "loaded deal", 62–79% retracement)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - His "time and price theory" is macros: 20-minute windows every hour — the last 10 minutes before the top of the hour and the first 10 minutes after it (i.e. xx:50–xx:10). Time when the market should start running for liquidity or inefficiency; he sets an expectation to look for a setup every hour.
  - A retracement into 62–79% of a leg is only a 3/4-probability thing by itself; combine it with a PD array agreeing and being inside a macro window and it becomes "a loaded deal" — it may not run 100 handles but will run back to the top of the framed range.
  - The same applies to indicators: bullish divergence on stochastics/RSI/MACD works only when it coincides with an order block or FVG inside a macro.
  - An order block is two specific price levels, not a zone: the first is the initial entry for smart money, the second is where they can add if offered.
  - Fibonacci only gives measurements of a range and of the area beyond; it matters when it agrees with another PD array.
- **How to identify / use:** check the clock (xx:50–xx:10), the OTE retracement (62–79%) and a PD array overlapping it.
- **Example in this video:** none (spoken principle).
- **Timestamps:** [1:13:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4412) macros = time and price theory · [1:16:48](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4608) order block = two price levels · [1:20:00](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4800) 62–79% + PD array + macro · [1:20:47](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4847) indicator divergence in a macro

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET. Opening range gap = prior settlement ("414", i.e. 4:14 PM ET, caption) to 9:30 open.
- Open above settlement → bias for a draw down to gap midpoint (50%); about 70% tendency to reach it in the first 30 minutes (his figure, not verified).
- Huge gap (several hundred handles): expect initial high, then a run of that high, then a deeper retracement; do not chase breakouts. Small gap (40–60 handles) → first FVG would be an entry.
- First 1-minute FVG: candle 9:31 closes, at 9:32 check the gap; keep it gray; extend right.
- Minimum 20-handle potential or no trade.
- Execution timeframes: 15-second, 1-minute and 5-minute for high-frequency entries; one screen only on stream.
- Daily goal for a beginner: $75–$100 on one micro contract, then stop for the day.
- Fib projections beyond a dealing range: -0.25 first, -0.5, -1 (measured move).
- Macros: last 10 minutes of the hour and first 10 of the next hour.
- Retracement 62–79% + PD array + macro window = "loaded" setup.
- Contract: December NQ replaced September on Tuesday of that week; the Aug 27–29, 2024 60-minute relative equal highs were the earlier focus.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Low-risk, low-yield "laboratory experiments": one micro contract, $75–$100/day, stop when achieved; do it for a couple of weeks while only backtesting/tape reading, then 2–3 weeks later press the button on one micro (1:23:11).
- Journal how it feels: take screenshots, record whether you feel regret, fear, excitement when holding or when price moves toward your stop; test holding past the $75–$100 target as an experiment to learn (0:23:23).
- A stopped-out or break-even trade in an experiment is not a loss; moving the stop to cover commissions removes drawdown (0:48:26).
- "No emotional response to price ever" if you have a framework; do not beat yourself up, acknowledge, journal improvements (0:30:36).
- Funded accounts: do not size up to chase a big payout; risk $50 or less to make $100 (2:1), do it for weeks, then try two contracts; he criticizes stacking 10–20 accounts with big contracts (0:31:23–0:32:56).
- Take the first opportunity at the plan; sitting still when neither scenario appears is a trade decision; passing on a setup that fails your filter is a milestone (0:47:38).
- Every day, review the chart after the fact with a 20-handle box to learn what was available.
- Slow is smooth, smooth is fast (1:03:52).
- Market-in at first (no limit orders) to feel entries (0:33:43).
- He says he has 1,400+ handles in the last 2¾ weeks (self-reported claim).
- Large parts after about 1:08 are rants aimed at critics and a "predator/prey" speech; no transferable rules beyond those listed above.

## Glossary candidates
`Opening range gap` — space between prior settlement and the 9:30 open, midpoint used as the first draw.
`Mid gap` — 50% (consequent encroachment) of the opening range gap.
`Upper quadrant level` — 75% of the opening range gap (first stepping stone when dropping from a gap up).
`First presented FVG` — the first FVG on the 1-minute chart after 9:30; the one that "can't hide".
`Initial high (of day)` — first high in the 9:30–10:00 opening range, the one that gets run to trap breakout buyers.
`Handle` — one point on NQ.
`Laboratory experiment` — ICT's term for tiny-size, rule-bound practice trades.
`Loaded deal` — OTE retracement + PD array + macro window all aligned.
