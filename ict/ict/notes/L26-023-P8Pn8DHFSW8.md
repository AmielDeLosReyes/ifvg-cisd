## Summary
ICT demonstrates how to identify and short the daily high using first presented fair value gaps from the previous Friday, combined with opening range gap analysis and order flow mechanics. He executes a short entry near the intraday high and manages it to the opening range gap midpoint, teaching how price will gravitate to predictable levels based on PD array logic. ## Concepts

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (aliases: first presented fair gap, first fair gap, FPFVG)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + example
- **What ICT says:** The earliest fair value gap that can form in a session—occurs at 9:31 ET on NQ (the second 1m candle). It's identifiable before the day even begins by looking at the previous Friday's first presented gap. Must track both Monday and Friday first presented gaps from the last two weeks. These gaps are "Easter eggs" embedded in markets and repeat with high probability; they often signal the daily high or low.
- **How to identify:** Look at open and close of 9:31 candlestick; if no volume imbalance in upper half, examine lower half. The gap is the space between the high of one candle and the low of the next (or vice versa).
- **How to use:** Use these gaps as reference levels for predicting where price will trade intraday. Watch whether bodies close above/below consequent encroachment (midpoint) to determine bullish/bearish bias. If bodies fail to touch the upper half and stay in lower half, that's bearish. Use the gap to identify session reversals or continuation points.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Friday Aug 21, 9:31 ET: first presented gap is 426.00 (open/close both 426.00, so use wick). Projected forward to Tuesday Aug 23. Twice during the morning (8:18 ET and again ~9:00 ET), price rallied into this gap's upper half but bodies couldn't touch consequent encroachment—bearish signal. Later in the session, when price trades into the upper half again, it fails to close there, confirming the gap as weak resistance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:38](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=818) — identifying Friday's first gap; [0:16:03](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=963) — projecting it to today; [0:28:15](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1695) — bodies respecting consequent encroachment
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT claims this is original methodology not taught by Wyckoff, Elliott Wave, or other price action traders. The logic applies to stocks, forex, bonds, commodities (he's used it across all instruments). Keep only the last two weeks' Friday and Monday first gaps on charts—don't clutter; use separate workspaces for PD array tracking.

### Opening Range Gap (aliases: RTH opening range gap, ORG)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + example
- **What ICT says:** Gap between the previous day's 4:14 PM ET close (end of RTH) and the 9:30 ET open of the next day. Premium gap = opens higher; discount gap = opens lower. Divide the gap into octants and consequent encroachment (midpoint). 70% probability of trading to the midpoint (consequent encroachment) within first 30 minutes (by 10:00 ET).
- **How to identify:** Find the close price of previous RTH day and the open at 9:30 ET next day. Measure the difference. Use fib from high to low of gap; mark octants (upper quadrant, upper percentile, consequent encroachment, lower percentile, lower quadrant).
- **How to use:** In a premium gap (opens higher), expect price to retrace to at least the gap midpoint as low-hanging fruit. Don't demand full gap closure. For intraday: take first partial at the low formed before the gap, then manage for midgap. Entry signals: watch how bodies behave relative to octants.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 23, opens at 9:30 ET at higher level than Aug 22 close (premium gap). Consequent encroachment (gap midpoint) = yesterday's 4:14 PM close. Price is expected to trade down to this level by 10:00 ET. ICT shorts into the gap's octants; targets the midpoint; later pushes for new day opening gap.
- **Timestamps:** [0:37:50](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2270) — defining ORG; [0:41:02](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2462) — 70% rule and octants; [0:50:47](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3047) — naked chart with fib on gap
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't demand full gap closure; that's greedy. Two partials (one at prior day's low, one at gap midpoint) are often sufficient for a profitable session.

### New Week Opening Gap (aliases: NWOG, new week gap)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** Difference between Friday's 4:14 PM ET close (end of RTH) and Sunday 6 PM ET open (start of new week in electronic markets). Project this gap across entire week. Keep last five weeks on chart but only mark gaps relevant to current price action.
- **How to identify:** Track Friday close to Sunday 6 PM open difference.
- **How to use:** Price often respects new week opening gap in early morning rally/decline. If price runs above it early, it signals strong directional bias. Mark it as a level not to be taken for granted.
- **Example in this video:** NQ rallies into new week opening gap early morning; doesn't reject it, suggesting bullish pressure. Later when bearish, price respects this level during retracements.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:42](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2562) — identifying NWOG; [0:44:17](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2657) — how it guides price
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Keep workspaces separate for new week gaps only; don't clutter trading chart.

### New Day Opening Gap (aliases: NDOG)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Similar logic to new week opening gap but from previous day 4:14 PM to current day 6 PM ET (restart). Use this as floor/ceiling depending on bias.
- **How to identify:** Previous RTH close to 6 PM open differential.
- **How to use:** Stop-loss placement. Target zone during reversals.
- **Example in this video:** NQ short trade stopped out just above new day opening gap low; ICT wanted to put limit just above this level to get full gap closure.
- **Timestamps:** [0:58:00](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3480) — stop loss at NDOG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### Consequent Encroachment (aliases: CE, consequent encouragement, 0.5 level)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint (50% Fibonacci) of a PD array (fair value gap, opening range gap, wick). Bodies stopping below CE while wicks can exceed it indicate bearish pressure. Bodies in upper half of PDA = bullish; lower half = bearish. It's the "threshold" between bullish and bearish order flow.
- **How to identify:** Apply Fibonacci from high to low (or low to high) of the imbalance; 0.5 level = CE. Visual: draw horizontal line at midpoint.
- **How to use:** Order flow filter. If bodies can't reach CE, that's rejection of that zone. Watch where closes land relative to CE to confirm direction.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple instances where bodies stop short of consequent encroachment of Friday's first gap, signaling bearish bias. At [0:32:12](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1932): wick goes above, but body closes way underneath—bearish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:03](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=963); [0:32:12](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1932); [0:46:45](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2805)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Wicks are allowed to exceed CE; only body closure matters for order flow assessment.

### Relative Equal Highs (aliases: REH, relative equal lows)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When price makes two (or more) highs at approximately the same level. This level represents buy-side liquidity above, which traders will target. If price pokes above and rejects, that's a short entry (turtle soup, false breakout).
- **How to identify:** Look for two highs within same session separated by at least one candlestick. Mark them on chart.
- **How to use:** Likely to trade up into REH; watch for wick or body rejection above. Short-entry zone if bodies fail to close above REH.
- **Example in this video:** Two highs in early morning (before 8:18 ET) at approximately same level. Buy side liquidity sits above. When price trades above late morning at ~10:20 ET, it pokes the REH level but doesn't close above it—short signal.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:23](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1403) — identifying REH
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't expect it to be blown out without first showing rejection.

### Order Flow and Body Placement (aliases: bullish/bearish order flow)
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** Where the body closes within a candle is the truth. Don't use complex volume profile or value area—just look at open, high, low, close. If body is in upper half of PDA and candle closes there, it's bullish. If body is in lower half or middle, it's bearish. The wick can go anywhere, but body placement is the real order flow signal.
- **How to identify:** Draw horizontal line at midpoint of PDA; observe where each candle's body (not wick) closes.
- **How to use:** Confirm direction bias. Entry/exit filter.
- **Example in this video:** [0:32:01](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1921) — multiple candles trade into Friday's FVG upper half but bodies fail to close there, staying in lower half = bearish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:32:01](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1921); [0:46:45](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2805)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This logic is ICT's claim to uniqueness; not found in Wyckoff or Elliott Wave books.

### Wick Behavior and Imbalance Measurement
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** Wicks can be treated as gaps/imbalances just like bodies. Measure wick from body low to wick high as an imbalance, apply Fibonacci, and find consequent encroachment. Wicks are allowed to exceed PDAs, so when a wick penetrates above a gap and the body closes below, that's a strong bearish signal.
- **How to identify:** Identify the wick; measure from body close (or low) to wick extreme; grade the 0.5 level.
- **How to use:** Stop placements, target zones. If price climbs via wick alone but body rejects, short it.
- **Example in this video:** Early morning wick at ~6:00 AM ET penetrates Friday's 9:31 FVG, but body closes below it—bearish setup confirmed. Later shorts placed in premium of wicks, betting on body rejection.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:36](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1836) — treating wick as gap; [0:33:01](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1981) — measuring wick imbalance
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Nobody has looked at wicks as imbalances before ICT; this is unique to his framework.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (aliases: iFVG, reversal FVG)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** When a gap that was bullish (buy-side efficiency) gets traded into from above, it becomes a bearish inversion gap on the way down. First utilization = buy-side efficiency; second = inversion. The bodies behavior inside the iFVG (upper/lower half) still applies.
- **How to identify:** Track when a prior bull gap becomes a reversal point. Watch for close below the wick of that gap.
- **How to use:** Confirm continuation lower. If bodies stay in upper half, iFVG is weak; if bodies are in lower half and close below wick, iFVG is strong and price will continue lower.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's first FVG transitions from buy-side (first utilization) to iFVG (second utilization) when price trades into it from the short side. The structure repeats on smaller ranges throughout the day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:49:11](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2951) — iFVG recognition; [0:53:59](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3239) — iFVG behavior and continuation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A "failed" FVG is valuable as an iFVG; don't see it as a loss. ICT says this gives him "an out" for every scenario, which critics mock, but he argues it's the reality of how markets work.

### Session Phases and Consolidation/Expansion (aliases: four-phase market, consolidation vs expansion)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** If London (overnight) trended up, the next session (7–9 AM pre-market, 9:30+ RTH) will likely consolidate or reverse—rarely do three straight sessions trend the same direction. After consolidation, expect expansion. Consolidation looks like a tight range; expansion looks like a breakout move.
- **How to identify:** Compare overnight range size to pre-market range to early RTH move.
- **How to use:** Bias for consolidation after overnight trend; bias for expansion after consolidation.
- **Example in this video:** Overnight trended higher; 7–9 AM consolidated; 9:30 ET expanded lower (bearish).
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:14](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1454) — four-phase teaching reference; [0:28:15](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1695) — consolidation example
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rarely see three sessions trend the same way; two back-to-back is possible but third is unusual.

### Identifying Daily High/Low via Fibonacci Measurements
- **Kind:** example + technique
- **What ICT says:** Anchor a Fibonacci to the low of a swing (using bodies, not wicks), then look at the negative 0.5 level. When price comes down from a wick and the body aligns with that negative 0.5 level, that's often the day's high (or low if measuring upward). It's a precise geometric signal.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify swing low (body close), (2) Apply Fibonacci from that low upward, (3) Watch for price reaching negative 0.5 on the wick and aligning on body at that level on the next pullback.
- **How to use:** Confirm that a high/low is in place. Short into highs with confidence once this alignment appears. Enter short with first add when wick confirms, add second position if bodies align.
- **Example in this video:** NQ swing low around 29,316 (Aug 23 AM). Fibonacci up from there shows negative 0.5 at ~29,413. Wick runs to 29,414.75 (exact!), bodies pull back to align with that level—daily high confirmed. ICT shorts from there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:50:47](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3047) — naked chart with Fib; [0:53:59](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3239) — measuring bodies; [0:54:46](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3286) — confirmation and entry
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This requires meticulous charting; don't expect to find it on every single session, but when it aligns, it's very high-probability.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- **First presented fair gaps:** Track last two weeks' Friday and Monday 9:31 gaps; project them forward all week.
- **Opening range gap probability:** 70% likelihood of trading to the gap midpoint (consequent encroachment) by 10:00 ET.
- **Session phases:** Rarely three consecutive sessions trend the same direction; consolidation typically follows a trend.
- **Daily high/low:** When wick reaches negative 0.5 Fibonacci level (from swing low) and body aligns on pullback, high/low is likely confirmed.
- **Partial profit targets (gap-driven short entries):** (1) Prior day/swing low, (2) Opening range gap midpoint, (3) New day opening gap low.
- **Stop-loss placement:** Just above wick of entry confirmation or just above short wick.
- **Jackson Hole Symposium:** Expect erratic, manipulated price action; don't make firm predictions during this event.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Chart management:** Use separate workspaces for different gap types (new week gaps, ORG, etc.) to avoid clutter. Only mark salient levels relevant to current price bias. A clean chart lets you focus on candle-by-candle tape reading (how bodies and wicks form).
- **Live trading on 1-minute:** Faster timeframe (15 seconds) is harder but proves you know what you're doing. 1m doesn't allow replaying—must analyze and act instantly.
- **Entries and fills:** Don't copy the trader directly. Learn the logic, find your own setups, use position sizing and stops that fit your account. Chasing the same entries at the same price (especially with 10,000 other people) means you don't get filled—someone else does.
- **Risk management:** Don't overlever. A micro contract (or even 1 contract) pulling 15–20 handles a couple times per week can cover monthly groceries. Build confidence slowly; don't rush to larger size.
- **The danger of self-doubt:** ICT notes he sometimes overthinks exits, second-guessing himself after feeling "led" to put a limit order at a specific level. He frames this as the conflict between experience/faith and flesh. Traders will experience the same—learn to trust your logic and rules; emotional second-guessing costs money.
- **Mindset on criticism:** ICT spends significant time defending his methodology against claims of rebranding or fraud. His argument: the logic works repeatedly, executions prove it, and critics offer no counter-demonstrations. His advice: ignore trolls; study the concepts, backtest, and verify yourself over 4 weeks.
- **Journaling:** Keep key levels on a notepad even if not all are drawn (avoids chart clutter but ensures you remember the backdrop).

## Glossary candidates
`FPFVG` — first presented fair value gap; the earliest and most significant gap of a session (9:31 ET for NQ), tracked from the previous Friday's gap; said to predict session highs/lows with high probability.

`ORG` — opening range gap; difference between previous day RTH close (4:14 PM ET) and current day 9:30 ET open; divided into octants; 70% probability of trading to midpoint (CE) by 10:00 ET.

`NWOG` — new week opening gap; difference between Friday RTH close and Sunday 6 PM electronic open; projected across the entire week.

`NDOG` — new day opening gap; difference between previous day RTH close and current day 6 PM electronic open (market restart).

`CE` or `consequent encroachment` — the 50% Fibonacci level (midpoint) of any PD array; separates bullish (upper half) from bearish (lower half) order flow based on body placement.

`REH` — relative equal high; two or more highs at approximately the same price level within a session; represents buy-side liquidity above; likely to be tested intraday.

`iFVG` — inversion fair value gap; a bullish gap (buy-side efficiency) that becomes a reversal point when traded into from above; second utilization of the gap structure.

`Wick imbalance` — treating a wick (from body extreme to wick extreme) as a measurable gap; can be graded with Fibonacci and used for precision stop-loss and target placement.

`Turtle soup` — (in ICT's frame) false breakout above relative equal highs; short entry into rejected poke above resistance.

`Octants` — eight divisions of a PD array (from high to low: upper quadrant, upper percentile, upper octant, CE, lower octant, lower percentile, lower quadrant, and the below-gap zone). Used for level-of-precision entries and profit targets.

`Tape reading` — observing how each individual 1-minute candle forms (wick placement, body closure, sequence) to predict the next candle's behavior; requires live observation, not market replay.

`Order flow` — the direction and force of buying/selling pressure, inferred from body placement within PDAs and candle-sequence patterns.
