### Daily Volume Imbalance as the target ("Terminus Daily V.I.")  (aliases: "daily volume imbalance", "orange box/area", "Terminus" (named model, caption))

- **How to identify:** mark the daily volume imbalance (gap between candle bodies on the daily chart left by a big down day). The target is its low, then its CE.
- **How to use:** buy relative equal lows below price when a higher-timeframe imbalance above is the draw; target the low of the daily VI first (partials), and keep a runner for its deeper part; once it's reached, the short-side thesis becomes eligible.

### Inversion FVG + bullish FVG nesting as discount sensitivity  (aliases: "discount sensitivity", "two qualified areas")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Two qualifying areas stacked — a bullish FVG and a bullish inversion FVG — give "discount sensitivity": price should react from them.
  - After the entry, he does not want price to return and touch the high of the inversion FVG again ("I want to see it run, take out that high, without touching the orange area again" — here the orange area is the zone he is working from; caption mixes the iFVG with the daily VI shade).
  - A "mohawk": a wick that dips a little outside the iFVG and reverses sharply back above the upper half; the body should end inside the iFVG ("look where the body is").
  - The next candle "has to do a lot of work to go higher and not lay a body below the previous candle's wick low" — a body below that wick low is "problematic".
- **How to identify:** small bullish FVG + bullish iFVG at the same low, a sharp rejection wick, bodies inside/above the iFVG.
- **How to use:** hold the long while bodies stay above the previous candle's wick low; a body below it shows the wick's control is lost.
- **Example in this video:** after the entry, a candle wicks slightly outside the iFVG then reverses up outside the upper half; the next candle must run and cannot lay a body below that wick's low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:01](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=241) — "mohawk" wick and body inside the iFVG; [0:04:49](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=289) — no body below the previous wick low; [0:15:14](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=914) — "nested areas of two qualified areas for discount sensitivity".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** reinforces the iFVG as a support PD array; adds the wick-low body rule as a hold condition.

### Consequent Encroachment of a wick as a barrier / next PD array  (aliases: "consequent encroachment of that wick", "barrier or boundary")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - He places the first stop-trail "just at the consequent encroachment of that wick". Reason: if price is not going for the sell side below, it will go for inefficiencies; if there is no inefficiency below the bullish iFVG he has noted (blue bullish FVG), "then the next PD array is this wick" — i.e. the wick itself is the next PD array and its CE is the boundary.
  - "Consequent encroachment is a barrier or boundary for price action." No gimmicks, no volume or order-count data: it is "very simple, very visual".
  - He wants an energetic run above the CE of that wick into the orange area (the daily VI) for the first payout.
- **How to identify:** take a long rejection wick on the retest candle; its 50% (CE) is the reference level.
- **How to use:** place the initial risk-reduction stop at the CE of the wick; trail the final runners behind it.
- **Example in this video:** after the mohawk, ICT moves the stop to the CE of that wick ("take some of that risk away") and holds the rest for the run into the daily VI.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:40](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=340) — why the wick CE is chosen; [0:06:30](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=390) — "barrier or boundary for price action".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** a wick as a PD array in its own right is a notable extension: if there's no FVG below, the wick's CE is the next support.

### Scaling out a 20-lot with layered limits and stop to the daily VI low
- **Kind:** entry/management model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Entry: 20 contracts into the nested area (accepting a wick slightly outside).
  - First plan: "15 leave five on for a runner" (take 15 off, keep 5), then he layers the exits instead of one price: sells one at the low of the daily VI, drops limit orders above ("stagger one right there"), and keeps four contracts working as "immediate rebounds on this wick".
  - Once price clears that candle's high, he moves the stop to the low of the daily volume imbalance: with a large portion taken above that low, the last two contracts are "locked in" — they can only stop out at the daily VI low, giving "two runners" with no risk of loss.
  - He says it is fine for the final contracts to be forced out at the daily VI low: "no sense of giving back good money".
  - He keeps a limit and a stop on the remaining runner rather than closing it "in case it does run away a little bit higher"; the aim is the "555–560 vicinity" and he'll peel it off there.
  - Frustration beat: price gets close to the limit order and says "not yet" — he keeps limits at target rather than chasing.
- **How to use:** layered limits above the first target; stop trails to the level where the realised partials guarantee profit; leave runners with a defined stop.
- **Example in this video:** 20 NQ contracts; touch of the daily VI low at ~9:05 into the video; scale-outs through 9:54–13:32; stop raised to the daily VI low (orange shaded area); final runner targeted at ~555–560 (caption).
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:18](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=438) — "15 leave five on for a runner"; [0:09:54](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=594) — layering sells; [0:11:51](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=711) — stop to the low of the daily VI locks in the final two contracts; [0:14:20](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=860) — keep limit + stop on the runner.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Entry: relative equal lows (sell stops) with a nested bullish FVG + bullish iFVG; 20 contracts into it.
- First objective: clear the high, then the low of the daily volume imbalance; "just a little bit inside" is enough.
- Hold condition: no body below the previous candle's wick low; do not want price to touch the iFVG high again before running.
- Stop: CE of the key wick, later the low of the daily volume imbalance (locks in final contracts).
- Scaling: take most contracts off above the daily VI low; final runners with stop at the daily VI low.
- Targets: low of the daily VI; CE of the daily VI measured "29 560 ish" (caption); runner aim "555–560".
- A fast return to fill the imbalance below the entry is "less bullish".
- Short bias is not allowed until price has traded up into the daily VI.
