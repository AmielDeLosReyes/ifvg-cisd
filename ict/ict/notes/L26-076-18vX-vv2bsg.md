## Concepts

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) Validation Across Timeframes (aliases: multi-timeframe confirmation, context-based iFVG)
- **Kind:** rule + validation method
- **What ICT says:** Don't call something an iFVG without context and multi-timeframe confirmation. An iFVG is only valid if: (1) price trades through it with a close outside it, (2) on the retrace into it, bodies fail to reach consequent encroachment, (3) the next candle gives immediate feedback (direction confirmed). To remove ambiguity, watch the hourly chart for first utilization (breakout of the FVG), then drop to 1m to watch for iFVG validation (bodies reject CE). Only then is it a confirmed iFVG.
- **How to identify:** On hourly: identify where price first breaks the gap (this is "first utilization"). On 1m: watch next retracement; bodies must fail to reach CE. Multiple closes and body rejections = true iFVG.
- **How to use:** Once iFVG is validated across timeframes, trade it with high conviction. Expect sharp reversals from validated iFVGs.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1-minute iFVG was validated when price traded above it, failed to leave a body above, and closed below it. Hourly chart showed first utilization (break above the gap). This multi-timeframe context gave high conviction for the short.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Context is everything; a price structure looks like an iFVG until bodies prove otherwise.

### New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) / New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) (aliases: opening gap, weekly gap, settlement gap)
- **Kind:** definition + targeting tool
- **What ICT says:** NDOG is the gap between RTH close (4:59 ET previous day) and RTH open (9:30 ET next day). NWOG is the gap between week close and week open. Michael uses these as reference levels and as targets for reversals. When price approaches these gaps, it often acts as inversion FVGs (weak commitment).
- **How to identify:** Mark the settlement price (4:59 ET close) and the next day's RTH open (9:30 ET); the gap between them is NDOG. Mark Friday close and Monday open for NWOG.
- **How to use:** Use NDOG/NWOG as targets and bounce zones. On the 5m or 1m chart, use the gradient to show the NDOG levels.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: Michael graded the new week opening gap (settlement price to next week open). On the 5-minute chart, price utilized the gap and launched lower; it was a target zone.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:19](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=199) (new week opening gap: high and last week settlement price) · [0:04:08](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=248) (shows how NWOG is graded on 5m chart; used as launching price level).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** NDOG/NWOG act as institutional reference points; use them as targets or bounce zones, not as hard support/resistance.

### Breaker with Nested Fair Value Gaps (iFVG nested in breaker) (aliases: Reaper, nested pattern)
- **Kind:** pattern + high-probability setup
- **What ICT says:** When you have a breaker (short-term high, low, higher high but lower than original) AND you nest an iFVG (buy-side imbalance sell-side efficiency) inside it, AND you have a second iFVG opposing the first, you have a variant of the Reaper pattern. This is extremely high-probability. Michael notes: "When you have that and it's part of a breaker, that is a variant of a reaper."
- **How to identify:** Find a breaker structure; identify two nested iFVGs within it (one bearish, one bullish, opposing each other).
- **How to use:** When you spot this, expect sharp moves through both iFVGs in sequence. First iFVG acts as a bounce zone; second iFVG is the target. Use both for entries and targets.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m chart had a breaker with nested iFVGs; Michael shorted the first iFVG retrace, then expected price to drop to the second iFVG (which it did).
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:17](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=1037) (when you have two nested fair value gaps that's part of a breaker; that is a variant of a reaper).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Reaper is one of Michael's named patterns; the nested structure makes it extremely high-probability.

### Buy-Side Bounce / Sell-Side Efficiency (aliases: bearish inefficiency, bull imbalance structure)
- **Kind:** definition + direction indicator
- **What ICT says:** When you see a buy-side bounce with sell-side efficiency (buyers can bounce price up, but sellers show efficiency/willingness at higher prices), it's bearish. This inefficiency acts as an iFVG when price comes back to it. The presence of this structure signals weakness despite the bounce.
- **How to identify:** Look for a bounce higher (buy-side bounce) that fails to sustain; sellers show no commitment at higher levels (sell-side efficiency). Mark it as an inefficiency.
- **How to use:** Treat buy-side bounce/sell-side efficiency structures as reversal zones. When price retraces into them, expect bearish continuation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: "We have this sell-side bounce buy-side efficiency which is bearish. We want to see price utilize that."
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:28](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=988) (sell-side bounce buy-side efficiency, which is bearish).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The language can be confusing; focus on the outcome: is price showing willingness above, or is it weak? That's the signal.

### First Utilization / First Presentation of Fair Value Gap (aliases: first break, initial FVG use)
- **Kind:** pattern + timing indicator
- **What ICT says:** The first utilization is when price first trades through/beyond a fair value gap after it forms. This is often on a higher timeframe (hourly or daily). Once first utilization happens, watch the lower timeframes for the iFVG behavior on the retracement. First utilization confirms the FVG is real institutional reference.
- **How to identify:** On hourly/daily, mark where price first breaks above or below the FVG (this is first utilization). Then drop to lower timeframes to watch the retracement.
- **How to use:** First utilization is confirmation that the FVG is on the institutional radar. After first utilization, the retrace into the FVG will likely show iFVG behavior.
- **Example in this video:** NQ hourly: price first utilized (broke above) the FVG. Then on 1m, the retrace showed bodies failing to reach CE = validated iFVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:26](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=686) (lower quadrant hourly positive bounce sell-side efficiency; turn inversion fair gap).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** First utilization is the "green light" for trading the iFVG on lower timeframes.

### Volume Bounce and Volume Imbalance (aliases: volume gap, micro-imbalance)
- **Kind:** definition + identification
- **What ICT says:** A volume bounce is when a candle's open is higher than the previous candle's close (or vice versa), creating a small gap/imbalance. This micro-inefficiency shows up in structure. Example: close 905, next open 905.5 = 0.5 point imbalance. These tiny gaps are where smart money enters and exits.
- **How to identify:** Compare candle closes and next candle opens; any difference is a volume bounce/imbalance.
- **How to use:** Use volume bounces as entry points. When you see a clear volume bounce, the next candle's direction often confirms the trend.
- **Example in this video:** NQ hourly: close 905, next open 905.5 (volume bounce). This was part of the hourly iFVG structure Michael was watching.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:26](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=686) (close 905.5, next candle open.5; there's a volume bounce).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Volume bounces are microscopic; they're easy to miss if you don't zoom in.

### Nested Inefficiencies on Multiple Timeframes (aliases: nested FVGs, multi-timeframe structure)
- **Kind:** setup structure + high-probability pattern
- **What ICT says:** When an inefficiency on the daily/hourly level contains smaller inefficiencies on the 15m/1m level, you have nested structures. The outer inefficiency is the major target; the inner ones are bounce zones. Michael uses nested structures to define precise entries and exits. Example: daily iFVG contains an hourly iFVG, which contains 1m iFVGs.
- **How to identify:** Zoom from daily to 1m; identify inefficiencies at each timeframe; note how they nest.
- **How to use:** Use the outermost (daily) inefficiency as the long-term target. Use inner (hourly/1m) inefficiencies for entries and partial exits. This gives you precision and confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** NQ had a daily inefficiency; within it, an hourly iFVG; within that, 1m iFVGs. Michael used each layer to refine entry and management.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Nested structures are the holy grail of trading; they offer precision and high probability.

### Quadrants (aliases: 25% levels, quadrant breakdown)
- **Kind:** measurement tool
- **What ICT says:** Divide a range into four equal parts (quadrants = 25% each). Price often pauses or reverses at quadrant boundaries. When price reaches the lower quadrant of an inefficiency and shows weakness there, expect reversal or consolidation.
- **How to identify:** Mark the high and low of an inefficiency or range; divide into 4 parts; note each quadrant.
- **How to use:** Use quadrant levels as interim targets and reversal zones. If price reaches the lower quadrant and shows iFVG behavior, don't expect it to go further.
- **Example in this video:** NQ hourly had a lower quadrant mark; Michael was watching to see if price reached it with weakness = confirmation of bearish bias.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:22](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=742) (lower quadrant hourly positive bounce; sell-side efficiency turn inversion fair value gap).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Quadrants are mathematical subdivisions; they align with algorithmic price levels.

### Risk Management in High Volatility (aliases: position sizing, small risk big play, pack small play big)
- **Kind:** risk management principle
- **What ICT says:** In high-volatility environments (like June 2026 markets), traditional position sizing is dangerous. Instead, "pack small, play big": use very tight stops (small risk per trade), but let winners run with patience. This way, even if you're wrong, the loss is small; when you're right, the gain is huge because you're not exiting early. Example: Happy Meal worth of drawdown ($7 range) vs. thousands of handles of profit.
- **How to identify:** Calculate your account size and maximum loss tolerance; size positions so your stop-loss risk is 0.5–1% of account.
- **How to use:** On high-volatility days, use stops 3–5 ticks away (vs. 10 ticks normally). Hold winners longer because they're likely to run 50–100+ ticks.
- **Example in this video:** Michael took a short with tight stop (near entry), but let it run for 1600+ handles. If he'd been oversized, the tight stop would have been hit multiple times; instead, he stayed in and captured the move.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:06](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=846) (fill at one tick below; couple bucks drawdown, like a Happy Meal's worth) · [0:21:20](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=1280) (pack small, play big: keep risk small, let trades run, exercise patience).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In normal markets, you can take on 2–3% risk; in volatility >1000 handles/day, cut that to 0.5% or less.
