### Time Distortion & Multiframe Reconciliation
- **Kind:** definition | rule | refinement

### Premium Gap vs. Discount Gap & Opening Range Gap (ORG) Targets
- **Kind:** definition | rule | entry model
- **What ICT says:** Premium gap = open above prior close. Discount gap = open below prior close. On a premium gap day, the first protocol is to expect price to trade back into the ORG (back towards or below the opening price, seeking the low of the gap). On a discount gap, expect price to extend higher first, then consolidate. For premium gaps: first target is the opening price itself (9:30); second target is the midpoint (consequent encouragement); full gap closure is the best-case scenario but often won't occur (use a "low-hanging fruit objective"). Time window: 9:30 AM to 10:00 AM (first 30 minutes). Probability: ~70% of the time, at least to the midpoint.
- **How to identify:** Compare prior day's close (4:14 PM ET) to 9:30 AM ET open price. Gap = the difference. Subdivide by octants and quadrants. Mark as "premium" (higher) or "discount" (lower).
- **How to use:** Use ORG levels as natural S/R. Expect partial or full gap retracement within 30 minutes. Stops above (for shorts) or below (for longs) the ORG extremes. Targets at the midpoint or at full closure depending on market structure and time of day.
- **Example in this video:** NQ opened at a premium (above prior close). ICT expected downside penetration to the opening price or the midpoint within the first 30 minutes. This was achieved by ~9:50 AM.
- **Timestamps:** [1:23:28](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4808), [1:25:06](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4906), [1:25:55](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4955), [1:26:49](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5009), [1:27:35](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5055)

### Order Flow Logic: Bodies Refusing to Touch PD Array Levels
- **Kind:** definition | rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:** When a candlestick body fails to touch the high or midpoint of a PD array (FVG, inversion FVG, wick inefficiency) even though the wick reaches it, this is a "signature" or "big neon sign" of bearish order flow. Wicks can "color outside the lines," but bodies signal intent. If bodies refuse a level = no conviction = weakness = likely to drop. This is NOT supply-and-demand myth; it's institutional algorithm behavior. "If bodies can't even touch the midpoint, I'm looking for it to go lower."
- **How to identify:** Draw a PD array (especially inversion FVG). Watch the candlesticks: if wicks touch the upper boundary but closes stay in the lower half or midrange, bodies are avoiding that level. Multiple candles with this pattern = stronger signal.
- **How to use:** Bodies refusing to touch = bearish; expect a continuation move lower. Conversely, if bodies are consistently closing above the midpoint of a range, that's bullish. Use this to filter entries and set stops. If you're considering a long into an inversion FVG and bodies won't touch the high, don't go long.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5-minute inversion FVG: bodies consistently failed to reach the high end even though wicks did. This signaled that the move up was weak and downside was likely. Later, price dropped to the opening range gap target and closed below it.

### Wicks as Inefficiencies in PD Array Context
- **Kind:** refinement | definition
- **What ICT says:** A wick (especially a one-sided wick) is itself an inefficiency. When a wick is in close proximity to an FVG or imbalance, the wick takes precedence in price's hierarchy of attention. When stop-loss placement or entry logic points to an FVG, always verify: is there a wick nearby? If yes, price may defer to the wick over the FVG. The wick range (high to low) is often where price will work inside before breaking further.
- **How to identify:** Mark wicks separately from candlestick bodies. Note wick-body separation (premium or discount). Use wick high/low as micro-S/R.
- **How to use:** Stop placement: if shorting into an FVG gap, place stop above the nearby wick, not just above the FVG. Entry bias: if price is oscillating within a wick range, it may not break the adjacent FVG immediately. Wait for a candle that closes outside the wick and the FVG.
- **Example in this video:** EUR/USD: "When the area in close proximity the wick is going to be the more salient PD array. It's going to be the one that price is going to factor in more importance, more sensitivity." YM: "This wick takes precedence over that FVG right here."

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** definition | refinement | entry model
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG is formed when price rallies into a region where it previously had a discount (FVG below), or sells into a region where it previously had a premium (FVG above). The iFVG is the inverse of the prior gap. When a price pulls back into an iFVG after a rally, the bodies not touching the high of the iFVG indicate weakness in the uptrend and forecast a reversal. Conversely, sellers being stopped at the low of an iFVG suggest strength in the downtrend.
- **How to identify:** After a significant move, check if price is retracing into a region that previously had a one-sided gap (now inverted in structure).
- **How to use:** Use iFVG levels to set stops and targets. Entries: if going short into an iFVG rally and bodies won't touch the iFVG midpoint/high, that's a bearish signal for a short. Targets: the iFVG low or the prior FVG below it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 5-minute inversion FVG: price rallied into an iFVG, bodies refused to touch the high, and it subsequently dropped to test the opening range gap and prior FVG lows.
- **Timestamps:** [1:20:16](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4816), [1:23:28](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4808), [1:38:58](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5938)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The iFVG is a reversal signal ONLY if accompanied by bearish order flow signatures (bodies refusing key levels).

### Volume Imbalance (as distinct from FVG)
- **Kind:** definition | refinement
- **What ICT says:** A volume imbalance is a gap between two candlesticks where there is no overlap in the high/low range. Unlike an FVG (which has a 1–2 candle gap), a volume imbalance can span multiple candles or be a large single-candle gap. Price often revisits and "uses" these imbalances as targets or levels. They work similarly to FVGs in terms of order flow logic.
- **How to identify:** Look for candlesticks with no overlap in range (daily charts often show these clearly). Mark the gap.
- **How to use:** Price targets; support/resistance. When price approaches a volume imbalance, expect to see similar order flow signatures (bodies avoiding, wicks testing, reversals).
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil, Gold, Bitcoin: ICT noted "volume imbalances right there" and stated that traders should keep them on their charts. Also EURUSD and GBPUSD futures daily charts had volume imbalances in daily gaps.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:56](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=236), [0:25:57](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=1557), [1:12:46](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4766), [2:05:48](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=7548)

### Rejecting Higher Timeframe Inefficiencies
- **Kind:** rule | refinement
- **What ICT says:** When price moves into a higher-timeframe inefficiency (e.g., weekly or daily FVG) but wicks out without closing in the upper half, that's a rejection. Bodies not reaching into the range = rejection = likely reversal. This is a strong signal for a counter-directional move.
- **How to identify:** Mark daily/weekly PD arrays. Watch how price interacts: if wicks reach but closes don't, rejection.
- **How to use:** After rejecting a higher-timeframe level, expect a reversion or at least a pullback. Targets: the opposite inefficiency or prior support/resistance.
- **Example in this video:** NQ bodies failing to touch the high of the 5-minute inversion FVG = rejection of the upside, forecasting a drop.

### Fibonacci Octants & Quadrants
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Subdivide a range (gap, FVG, wick) into eighths (octants) and quarters (quadrants). The 0.625 octant (5th octant) is often significant. Upper quadrant and octants are resistance; lower quadrant and octants are support. Bodies stopping at these Fib levels indicate algorithmic targeting.
- **How to identify:** Measure the range, divide by 8 or 4, mark the levels. Use a Fib tool on your chart.
- **How to use:** Set stops, targets, and micro-S/R at these levels. Expect bodies to respect them more than support-resistance orthodoxy suggests.
- **Example in this video:** "The octants? This is Forex, by the way. So, it trades down in there, but it's overshooting the actual imbalance" [implying octant levels within the imbalance]. "Look at the bodies respecting that right in here too."

## Glossary candidates
`OHLC` — Open, High, Low, Close; the only data ICT uses for analysis
`Premium gap` — Opening higher than prior day's close (bullish setup; expect reversal)
`Discount gap` — Opening lower than prior day's close (bearish setup; expect extension or consolidation)
`ORG (Opening Range Gap)` — The range between prior-day close and 9:30 AM open
`Consequent encouragement` — The midpoint of an ORG or range; ~70% of the time price gravitates here in first 30 min
`iFVG (Inversion Fair Value Gap)` — A gap formed by price reversing into a region where it previously had a one-sided gap
`Volume imbalance` — A gap between candles with no overlap in range; less common than FVG but works similarly
`Phil` — Algorithmic or manual manipulation; sudden localized moves with no body conviction
`Time distortion` — Choppy 1-minute action explained by a higher-timeframe inefficiency; resolve by moving to 5-minute or higher
`Coloring outside the lines` — Wicks extending beyond a PD array's boundaries (permissible); bodies are what matters
`Octant` — 1/8th of a range (Fib subdivision); 5th octant (0.625) is often significant
`Quadrant` — 1/4th of a range
`Event-driven` — Market reacting to geopolitical, political, or economic catalysts; volatility elevated, manipulation likely
`Contango reversal` — Nearby contract > next month contract; signals supply concerns (reverse of normal carrying charge)
