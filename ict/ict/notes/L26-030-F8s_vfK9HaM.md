## Concepts

### Trade management: fund the trade, partial early, trail the rest  (aliases: "trade management 101", "pay myself a hassle/harassment fee", "take the risk out of the trade")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The target (here NWOG high, 30,169.75) is only the best-case scenario. If you have no way of getting paid before price reaches the terminus (the last bit of what you expect to see), it is very hard to be profitable early in a career.
  - Once price has moved in your favor, take a partial so the position is "funded" and the trade cannot become a net loser in theory. The exception he gives is a gap against you that stops the rest out above entry.
  - Once a portion of the trade is locked in as profit, do not let the remaining unrealized profit erode. His analogy: you would not work Monday to Wednesday and then do Thursday and Friday for free.
  - Each time he pays himself he also tightens the stop on the remainder (the "hassle fee"). The stop goes to just above a structural level (a candle high plus 1 tick, then the wick's CE, then a candle high that has already "done its job").
  - Partials always add to the positive column of equity, so nobody can argue against them. He calls "no partials, that's stupid" the view of someone who is not consistently profitable.
  - Even if the last contracts get stopped out and price then runs to target, the trade was still profitable. A stop-out on the final piece is "a good stop out" if it is above a level that would invalidate the idea.
- **How to identify:** the situation is a trade in profit that is approaching a big magnet level (NWOG), or price action that is no longer supporting the idea (heavy retracement, "not organic" spikes).
- **How to use:**
  - Take one contract off when price runs into discount and shows "too much discount sensitivity", to fund the position (about 0:15:32).
  - Use a buy stop above a recent high (30,213) as the trailing exit for one contract. If it fills, that contract is a partial, and the remaining contracts keep their own wider stop.
  - Set a resting buy limit (30,179.75) just above the target as the second exit. Whichever of the two fills first, cancel the other. This is a manual one-cancels-the-other, because fluctuations could fill both and remove the chance of reaching the 30,169.75 terminus.
  - When one contract remains, move the stop in steps (see timestamps).
- **Example in this video:** short on NQ-type price (instrument not stated) from near the 8:30 ET Monday open. Stop initially just above the CE of a SIBI. About 3 contracts total (inferred): one peeled off for funding, one exited by trailing buy stop (he says this "secured $215 more"), one remaining. Final contract stop moved in several steps: high + 1 tick (about 0:31:04), then to the CE of a wick (about 0:49:53), then above a candle high at about 30,196.25 that had already spent two wicks above the BISI (about 0:52:39).

### Right is not required for profitability  (aliases: "removing the need to be right", "correct is not the game")
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:**
  - Profitability means getting in while price moves in one direction and finding ways to get out in that direction, without needing the exact terminus you called.
  - Critics focus on "he said it would go there and it didn't" and ignore the entry logic, wick support and partials.
  - A trade can be "fruitful" even if the target is never reached. Seeing price reach the target proves you were right, but it is not what pays you.
  - Every trade is not guaranteed, and you should not demand that price reaches your target.
- **How to use:** build the exit plan so that profit is banked before the last leg, which is the purpose of the partials above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:07](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2107), [0:37:33](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2253), [0:41:27](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2487)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says nobody else teaches "all or nothing is wrong"; he positions partials as a core of his method.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) as a premium array  (aliases ICT uses: "inversion", "first utilization", "give up the ghost")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The reference gap is a BISI (caption: "buy side of balance, sell side efficiency") that was acting as a bullish PD array. For a short he wants it to fail as support.
  - The wanted behavior: price goes through the gap, or goes through and then comes back up, and respects it from below as a premium array that supports continuation lower. "First utilization" means the first reaction is as buy-side delivery; he does not want price to use it that way. He wants to see it be used as an inversion.
  - Validation is on closing bodies: price must close below the gap (below the candle's high / the gap's boundary), not merely wick through. Wicking down through it without leaving a body below it keeps it "still potentially in play", but not yet validated.
  - If bodies stay out of the upper half of the gap, that is good; any body in the upper half is a warning.
  - He says the gap need not spend any time there. He only does not want it used as first-utilization support.
- **How to identify:** a BISI that price trades down through, followed by a retest from below that is rejected, with bodies closing below its low. He also watches the CE and the opening level of the same candle ("the opening of that same candle, same value").
- **How to use:** short on the retest of the inverted gap. The stop sits just above the candle high that has already rejected twice (see trade management).
- **Example in this video:** the BISI is at 30,196.25 and above, in a retracement area. He wants a close below 30,196.25 (the candle's high / the next candle's open) to confirm the roll-over toward the NWOG. A small orange inversion zone at about 30,188–30,184 (caption garbled) is where he wishes he had taken most of the position off.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:58](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=418) — first description of the BISI-to-inversion idea; [0:23:45](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1425) — "if market is bullish we should see willingness to go higher; I want it used as inversion"; [0:49:06](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2946) — wick through it but no body below, still in play
- **Nuance / caveats:** he notes that to a new trader a BISI looks like an obvious long, but "not necessarily": you have to know the narrative and whether he is even positioned that way (about 0:36:46). Critics who see an FVG that fails and say "ICT's concept doesn't work" ignore the context.

### Bodies and closes versus opens and wicks  (aliases: "stronger on the close, not the open")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - A move is stronger when a candle closes beyond a level than when it merely opens beyond it. "The open is just not as reliable for continuation."
  - For continuation lower he wanted a body to close below the midpoint (CE) of the gap or candle. A close below that is "usually indicative of a pretty high probability continuation".
  - A close below the body CE and below the midpoint of an earlier gap makes it highly likely price reaches lower at least to the relative equal lows.
  - Wicks are used for precision, e.g. he measures from the wick low to the CE of that wick and says anywhere in this zone you can add.
  - He draws a midpoint line on the volume imbalances and grades price: "as long as it keeps the bodies in the lower half, I'm okay with that."
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:56](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=176), [0:16:20](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=980), [0:22:08](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1328)

### Relative equal lows as the first target of the move  (aliases: "taking out these lows")
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** after a close below the SIBI's body CE and the midpoint of the gap, likelihood is high that price reaches lower, at least taking out the relative equal lows he has marked. How price behaves when it takes those lows is what he then grades. A body closing below the lower wick area is the signal to follow through and to pull the stop down inside the wick-low-to-wick-CE zone.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:56](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=176), [0:04:34](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=274)

### Volume imbalance and SIBI used for precision entry, stop and management  (aliases: "cibby" = SIBI in the captions; "institutional order entry drill" — caption unclear whether "drill" or "draw")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Stop for the initial short goes just above the consequent encroachment (CE) of the SIBI; bodies should stay under it. He says he could make the stop a lot larger, but this "accomplishes the method".
  - A small volume imbalance near the entry candle should act as a premium array.
  - "Institutional order entry drill" (caption unclear): the entry point is one tick above the high of the candle to the left of a gap or volume imbalance, with the stop just above. In this video he cites a candle high at 30,251. He claims nobody else teaches the logic of precise entry, stop placement and stop management around imbalances.
  - He later added into the wick: his fill sat in the lower half of a wick, so the wick (and candle of entry) was treated as a premium array.
  - He calls a stack of "two volume imbalance" inside a SIBI a "suspension block" (caption: likely "suspension block", definition not given beyond that).
- **How to use:** the entry is a limit at or just above the volume imbalance or CE; stops above CE / candle highs; adding is done inside the wick portions (wick low to wick CE).
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:20](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=20), [0:09:45](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=585), [0:45:49](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2749)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says he would have gone short on the candlestick at the open but could not because his recording software was rendering the earlier video.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) as a draw and Monday framework  (aliases ICT uses: "Sunday's opening", "terminus draw", "black hole")
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NWOG is a "big black hole" that can pull price toward it, but price need not reach it. So know where you can get out before the terminus.
  - Monday framework for experienced traders: if price opens and rallies away from the NWOG, and never trades back to it during Sunday evening (Asia) or London, and there is no runaway price action higher or lower, then on Monday it is "very easy" to aim for a run to the NWOG. He calls it a "no-brainer type thing".
  - In this trade the target was the high of the NWOG (30,169.75) and he had also posted that idea publicly before the trade.
- **How to identify:** NWOG not yet traded back to in Sunday evening / Asia / London, price displaced away from it, no runaway trend.
- **How to use:** short or long toward the gap (direction opposite the move away), take partials before the gap, and treat the gap high / low as the terminus.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:50](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1130), [0:19:38](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1178), [0:20:27](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1227)

### Posted levels create liquidity (influence)  (aliases ICT uses: "pool of liquidity to be sacrificed")
- **Kind:** new coinage / commentary
- **What ICT says:** ICT claims that because so many people watch the levels he posts, those levels become a large pool of liquidity that others can run. People who act on his levels and lose faith in him are the ones being targeted. He compares it to seeing a wallet across the street on the way to a destination: price may take the easy liquidity first and then continue. He says this is "not ego, just market dynamics". He sees "not organic" price action around his levels.
- **How to use:** for the student, the point is to expect that a widely shared level may be run first. That is why stops and partial management matter, and why you should have your own analysis.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:53](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1913), [0:33:30](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2010), [0:34:20](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=2060)
- **Nuance / caveats:** this is opinion; treat it as context, not a testable rule.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Initial stop for the short: just above the CE of the SIBI; bodies must stay under it (about 0:00:20).
- A close below the CE of the body and the midpoint of the earlier gap makes a run on the relative equal lows likely (0:02:56).
- Prefer closes to opens; for continuation lower, want a body close below the midpoint of the level (0:22:08).
- Keep bodies in the lower half of the imbalance as a condition (0:16:20); no bodies in the upper half of the inversion zone (0:50:41).
- Entry-drill reference: one tick above the high of the candle to the left (cited candle high 30,251) (0:09:45).
- Prices in this trade: buy stop 30,213; resting limit 30,179.75; NWOG high 30,169.75; candle high/open 30,196.25; orange inversion zone about 30,188–30,184 (caption garbled).
- Whichever of the buy stop or limit fills first, cancel the other (0:26:07).
- Partial exits: one at funding limit (above CE of prior Thursday 7–10 p.m. ET range), one by trailing buy stop; stop on the remainder is moved down in steps to structure.
- Do not choose the intermediate low as the target; focus on the NWOG high (0:27:53).
- Monday: if NWOG untouched through Asia and London with no runaway, aim for the NWOG (0:19:38).
- "If it's causing you to have an altered state … close the trade" (0:47:27).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Trade management 101 is navigating the trade and your emotions, and not allowing the market to take back all unrealized profit (about 0:27:53–0:28:40).
- Watching him manage an idea that is not working is, in his view, one of the best ways to learn, versus staring and praying. You need your own process and protocol; "nobody is doing it for you" (about 0:29:28–0:30:16).
- A trade where you have funded the position "should not be permitted to turn into a losing trade" (about 0:21:14).
- With one contract left, he says it can be torturous because he no longer cares about the outcome and is spending mental capital on something he has already been paid for. Sometimes he has felt like killing the trade. He estimates odds as about 60/40 against reaching the target at that point, and says the odds improve if price closes below the inversion FVG (0:45:00, 0:48:16).
- If the trade gives you anxiety, palpitations, or you snap at family, close it. No trade is worth an altered state. Walk away, and come back at the end of the day rather than in 10 minutes (0:47:27).
- Be an independent thinker: "if I'm influencing your trade you are following a man, not a model". Your own read of price is the only thing that matters because the responsibility is yours (0:51:50–0:52:39).
- Hindsight sizing comment: he would have used 6 contracts on the entry, 4 on the entry drill, plus 2 added, and held 3 (0:39:13).
- Do not draw conclusions from one stopped trade, or decide your approach "no longer works" (0:54:16).
- A pre-market video was posted before the trade as proof of the analysis and not recorded with market replay (0:05:22–0:06:10); this is context for his critics, not a trading lesson.

## Glossary candidates
`BISI` — buyside imbalance, sellside inefficiency (bullish FVG); captions render it "buy side of balance sell side efficiency".
`SIBI ("cibby")` — sellside imbalance, buyside inefficiency (bearish FVG).
`Inversion FVG (first utilization)` — a gap that was used one way first (e.g., as bullish support) and then fails and is used the opposite way; here a BISI used as a premium array for a short.
`Institutional order entry drill` — (caption unclear) entry placed one tick above the high of the candle to the left of a gap/imbalance.
`Suspension block` — (caption) used for a pair of volume imbalances within a SIBI; definition not given.
`Terminus draw` — the final target level price is expected to reach (here the NWOG high).
`Hassle / harassment fee` — his term for the profit banked by tightening the stop while you sit through a drawdown.
`Octant (.875)` — an eighth subdivision of a dealing range, used as a graded level.
