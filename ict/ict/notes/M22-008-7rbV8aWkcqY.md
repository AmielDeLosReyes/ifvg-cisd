## Summary
Episode 8, "Applying institutional order flow to Forex markets", is a short lesson. It shows that the 2022 mentorship model (liquidity raid, market structure shift, FVG entry) works the same on Forex, demonstrated on EURJPY for 10 Feb 2022. ICT frames the daily bias (relative equal lows raided, two bullish structure breaks, a daily FVG retest, old highs as the draw). He then drills down 15m, 5m, 3m inside the New York killzone (7:00–10:00 a.m. NY time) for the entry. He shows how to project a target with a Fib standard deviation (−1) when there's no obvious liquidity to aim for. He gives the London-session window (2:00–5:00 a.m. NY time). He also explains checking a cross pair's bias by looking at its two component currencies (euro futures strong, yen weak).

## Concepts

### Daily bias on a Forex daily chart (HTF application of the model)
- **Kind:** example only / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The model applies to "higher time frame" daily charts too.
  - On the daily chart: relative equal lows get run (SSL taken), then price rallies.
  - Two points of market structure break to the upside: a first swing high taken, a drop back, then a second swing high taken.
  - In the up-leg, find the FVG(s). Price trades down into one and is sent higher.
  - The old high above is buy-side liquidity, a likely draw on liquidity "whether it trades there or not".
  - Horseshoes analogy: you aim at the post every throw even though you don't hit it every time.
  - Even a failure swing short of the old high is tradeable if there's enough range from the open to the high to "take a stab at it".
- **How to identify:** SSL raid (relative equal lows) on the daily, then a bullish MSS (sequential swing highs broken), then a retrace into the daily FVG, with an old high as the target.
- **How to use:** Expect the next daily candle (here 10 Feb 2022) to trade higher, then go intraday to execute.
- **Example in this video:** EURJPY daily (Forex.com data feed on TradingView; he avoids another feed). Relative equal lows raided, rally, two bullish structure breaks, retrace into a daily FVG, then higher toward the old highs. The 10 Feb 2022 candle was expected to be bullish after the consolidation.

### Entry model: SSL raid, MSS, FVG with top-down drill (5m to 3m to 1m)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the 5m chart within the killzone, price trades down, takes out a swing low, then punches above a swing high.
  - Isolate the leg from that low to that high and study it on each lower timeframe (5m, 3m, 1m).
  - On the 3m: did it take a swing low? Yes. Did it take a swing high? Yes. That makes it a "valid condition" to look for a trade.
  - Go back through the price leg for FVG(s). There were two. Price "could stab down" into the lower one, so be mindful, but try to get the entry in on the first one.
- **How to identify:** Inside 7–10 a.m. NY: sell-side swing low taken, then a displacement through a swing high, then FVG(s) in that leg.
- **How to use:** Enter on the retrace into the FVG. Be aware a deeper FVG may get tagged instead.
- **Example in this video:** EURJPY 5m/3m, 10 Feb 2022 NY session: the low was swept, the swing high broken, the 3m FVG(s) retested, then the rally.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:41](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=341) — 5m: swing low taken, punch above swing high · [0:06:27](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=387) — top-down 5m to 1m, 3m conditions · [0:07:13](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=433) — valid condition, two FVGs, entry

### Fib standard deviation (−1) projection for targets in expansion  (aliases ICT uses: standard deviation negative one)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When the setup forms in the middle of a range, with no nearby old high or low to target ("if we don't have a range to trade inside of"), project how far an expansion can go.
  - Anchor a Fibonacci on the previous swing: the low to the high of the price leg right after price leaves the consolidation (before the discount retracement). Then use the −1 standard deviation as the target.
  - His Fib settings are in the "OTE primer" video on the channel.
- **How to identify:** The swing that broke structure before the consolidation and pullback. The pullback creates the discount, and then the retracement rally.
- **How to use:** The −1 extension of that swing is the expansion target. Here it was hit "right to the point".
- **Example in this video:** EURJPY. The −1 SD projection landed at about 133.15/133.153, which matched the high of the move (133.153), and price reacted from there.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:00](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=480) — no range to target, use the swing leg · [0:08:46](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=526) — anchor Fib, −1 SD, 133.153
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A harder-timeframe old high exists further left, but the SD projection is the "real easy way" when nothing is close.

### Cross-pair bias from its component currencies (intermarket)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - To frame a cross (e.g. EURJPY), check each component on its own. Look at 6E (euro futures, front-month continuous) on the daily: clearly rising. Look at the yen: weak.
  - Strong euro plus weak yen means EURJPY higher. This supports a bullish bias.
  - It's a "real simple way" to frame exotic crosses. Then apply the time of day and wait for the pattern.
  - He also gives the reason simply as "because the euro dollar is going higher".

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Forex data on TradingView: use the Forex.com feed.
- Charts must be set to New York local time.
- NY killzone for Forex: 7:00–10:00 a.m. NY time. London killzone: 2:00–5:00 a.m. NY time. Prefer these two; Asia and London close are possible but not preferred.
- Mark midnight New York time on the 15m chart.
- Valid setup condition on the entry timeframe: a swing low taken AND a swing high taken (for longs), then a FVG in that leg.
- Top-down: 5m, then 3m, then 1m on the same leg.
- Target without nearby liquidity: Fib on the prior swing, −1 standard deviation.
- Cross-pair bias: compare the component currencies (e.g. 6E vs yen).

## Process, risk & psychology
- The model is the same across markets. You don't need subscriptions or other courses. Apply time of day and wait for the pattern. ([0:12:42](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=762))
- He trades only pairs he likes. He dislikes yen pairs and has other Forex markets to "scratch the itch". ([0:09:34](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=574))

## Glossary candidates
`Standard deviation (−1)` — Fibonacci extension of a prior swing used to project an expansion target when no obvious liquidity is near.
`NY ICT killzone (Forex)` — 7:00–10:00 a.m. New York time.
`London killzone (Forex)` — 2:00–5:00 a.m. New York time.
`Valid condition` — on the entry timeframe, both a swing low and a swing high (in sequence) have been taken in the leg you're trading.
`Failure swing` — price falls short of the old high or low it's drawing toward; still tradeable if there's enough range.
