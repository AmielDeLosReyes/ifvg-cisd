ICT spends the first half addressing a commenter who claimed to lose money after his "I'm watching this" community post, and lays down the responsibility rules: everything is demo/paper trading, "study / observe / watch" is never a trade signal. He then reviews his community-post idea (NQ/ES relative equal highs above, a small FVG below that he hoped price would drop into at the 9:30 Judas swing) and proves the 2022 model never actually triggered — price traded below an old low but produced no displacement back above it and melted straight through the FVG. The key teaching is "where do setups form": two framings of the same entry model — (1) a run above relative equal highs/old high, then displacement through the nearest short-term low, then the FVG; (2) a run up into an old FVG, using the FVG's low as the "line" that is raided. A valid model's main value is keeping you out of bad trades.

## Concepts

### 2022 Mentorship model — where setups form (bearish version via relative equal highs)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:** Setup forms after price runs above an old high or relative equal highs. As soon as price trades above the relative equal highs, scan back through the run-up and find the **nearest short-term low** — that is the trigger. It is not enough that price goes above the highs and simply drifts back down below them; price must go below that short-term low **with displacement** ("energetic move"). That displacement leg is "the foundation of the setup." Only then go into that leg and look for a fair value gap to short into.
- **How to identify:** (1) relative equal highs / old high level; (2) price runs through it; (3) identify the nearest short-term low formed during the run-up; (4) displacement takes out that short-term low = market structure shift; (5) FVG inside the displacement leg.
- **How to use:** Sell when price retraces up into the FVG in the displacement leg. Until the short-term low is taken out, "there's no trade there, there's no setup there." Reverse everything for longs (run below relative equal lows/old low, displacement above nearest short-term high, buy the FVG).
- **Example in this video:** Diagram drawn: green line = relative equal highs; price approaches, small correction, approaches again, then runs through; trigger = nearest short-term low in that run; displacement through it; FVG in the leg.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:41](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1901) — "where do setups form" journal subtitle; [0:33:16](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1996) — nearest short-term low is the trigger; [0:34:03](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2043) — no displacement through the low = no setup.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He calls this the filter that removes low-probability trades — the displacement requirement is what separates a real shift from a simple dip back below the highs.

### 2022 Mentorship model — second entry framing: raid into an old FVG
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Same logic as relative equal highs, but the "level" being raided is an old fair value gap above price (bearish case). Price trades up into the old FVG; it does **not** have to completely close the gap — trading anywhere between its low and high is good. Simplify it: treat the **low of the fair value gap** exactly like the relative equal highs. Once price trades above the FVG's low into it, go back through that up-leg, find the trigger (short-term low); when it is taken out = the market structure shift; the move down is the displacement leg; then look for a (new) FVG in that leg to sell.
- **How to identify:** old opposing FVG overhead; price trades into it (partial fill OK); nearest short-term low of the run-up taken out with displacement; new FVG formed.
- **How to use:** Short the new FVG in the displacement leg. For longs: old FVG below price, price trades down into it, treat the FVG's high as the "relative equal lows," wait for displacement above the nearest short-term high, buy the new FVG.
- **Example in this video:** The bullish version is what he hoped for at the 9:30 open: drop into the small gap he marked with a blue arrow, then displacement up, then buy an FVG targeting a run to 4303 (level quoted; likely ES — caption context unclear which index).
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:37](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2137) — old FVG version, doesn't have to fully close; [0:36:24](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2184) — FVG low treated as the relative-equal-highs line; "those are the two entry strategies for this model".
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He states these are "the two entry strategies for this model" — i.e., the model's sweep can be of liquidity (equal highs/lows) or of an opposing PD array (old FVG).

### Model as a filter (no setup = no trade)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** A model with sound logic should keep you out of bad trades — not always, but sometimes when a big move goes the other way that you didn't expect. If the structure doesn't form, nothing is lost; it's a missed move, not a losing trade. "It doesn't mean that the model's broke."
- **How to identify:** Missing ingredient (e.g., no displacement after the stop run) = no entry.
- **How to use:** Don't buy just because price reached your point of interest. Wait for all elements.
- **Example in this video:** May 5, 2022. 15m chart: price traded down into the shaded FVG, the candle stopped right at an old candle high, and the next candle "gave up the ghost." Initial criteria met (traded below an old low → sell stops run), but on 15m and 5m there was **no displacement back above the old low** — price consolidated, returned to the low and "melted" through it, and through the FVG. Anyone who bought "is not using what I'm teaching." Market then made a large move lower (the post-FOMC selloff) — a missed move, not a loss.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:33](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1053) — model validity keeps you out of bad trades; [0:22:14](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1334) — 15m: traded below old low (initial criteria); [0:23:01](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1381) — 5m: no displacement → no trade; [0:37:11](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2231) — "market did not provide the structure."

### FVG as "where the trade might form" + time element (9:30 Judas swing)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The FVG he flagged is only the place in price where the pattern *might* form — "by itself it's just price." You must first incorporate time, algorithmically: at the 9:30 equity open you expect volatility. With price focused on highs overhead and the market already dropping into a discount ahead of 9:30, he expected a drop (to get traders thinking lower) then a rally — a Judas swing at 9:30 — "not just go down indiscriminately"; it has to do something specific (drop into that FVG, then displace).
- **How to identify:** HTF draw above; price in discount pre-9:30; a nearby FVG below; 9:30 volatility.
- **How to use:** Wait for the drop into the FVG at 9:30, then require displacement and a new FVG before buying.
- **Example in this video:** Blue arrow in the community post marking the small gap below price; he expected a 9:30 drop into it, displacement, FVG long, run to 4303. Never formed.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Setup requires: liquidity run (relative equal highs/old high, or raid into an old FVG) → displacement through the nearest short-term low/high → FVG in the displacement leg → entry on retrace into FVG.
- Price trading back below the swept highs is NOT enough; it must take out the short-term low with displacement.
- An old FVG doesn't need to be fully closed; trading anywhere within it qualifies. Treat the FVG's near edge (low for bearish, high for bullish) as the liquidity "line."
- A PD array (FVG) is only where a trade *might* form; time (9:30 volatility) must be incorporated first.
- "Study this / observe this / watch this" is never trade advice — everything is demo/paper.
- None of the viewers should trade this model with live funds yet (content not complete).

## Glossary candidates
`Smooth criminal highs` — ICT's nickname for clean relative equal highs (liquidity) used here on NQ/ES 15m; not further defined in this video.
`Trigger (short-term low/high)` — the nearest short-term swing inside the run to liquidity whose displacement break confirms the setup.
`Displacement leg` — the energetic move through the trigger; "the foundation of the setup," where the entry FVG is sought.
`Judas swing` — the 9:30 false move (here, an expected drop) before the real move toward the draw.
