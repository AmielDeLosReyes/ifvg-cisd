## Concepts

### Seek and Destroy Model
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Market maker pattern of taking out buy-side and sell-side liquidity in alternating runs. Price runs up to take out buy-side stops (above prior highs), then reverses to take out sell-side stops (below prior lows), then reverses again upward. This back-and-forth pattern — low, higher high, lower low, higher high, etc. — is the "seek and destroy" where institutions systematically collect both long and short stop-losses. Occurs frequently on event-driven weeks (Jackson Hole, FOMC, NFP).
- **How to identify:** Mark the highs and lows on a 1-minute chart; look for alternating pattern where each swing extends beyond the prior swing. Example: Low → Higher high → Lower low (below first low) → Higher high (above second high).
- **How to use:** Anticipate the pattern; expect multiple "second runs" (reversals to take out the other side's stops). Don't panic on reversals; they're part of the pattern. Hold through reversals if your original thesis (direction) is intact on larger timeframe.
- **Example in this video:** Price ran low (took out sell-side), then higher high (took out buy-side), then lower low (took out new sell-side), then higher high (took out new buy-side) — classic seek and destroy on an event-driven day.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664) "second runs," [0:11:56](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=716) "that is the seek and destroy model. That's it."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** More pronounced during event-driven weeks (Jackson Hole, FOMC, NFP); less common in quiet weeks.

### Discount Wick as Target / Reference Level
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The prior day's wick (high to low), especially if it's a "discount wick" (below the body, indicating weakness), serves as an intraday target on the following day. Price often runs to touch the upper half or midpoint of the prior day's discount wick before continuing or reversing. This wick acts like a magnet or friction level.
- **How to identify:** Look at the prior day's candlestick; if it has a significant wick (body doesn't fill entire range), mark the wick high and low; note if it's below the body (discount wick).
- **How to use:** On the following day, anticipate price to gravitate toward the upper half or midpoint of the prior wick. Use as a target zone or reversal level.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday's 9:55 AM ET discount wick was targeted on Friday; price ran down to touch its upper half and midpoint, then reversed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:24](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=144) "Thursday's 9:55 a.m. Eastern time discount wick," [0:03:12](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=192) "Look where the body stopped."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Upper half or midpoint is target, not full wick range.

### Displacement Lower (SIBI Entry Mechanics)
- **Kind:** entry model (for longs after downside displacement)
- **What ICT says:** When a candlestick opens lower and closes lower (down-close candle), it creates "displacement lower" = sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency (SIBI). This candlestick is NOT the FPFVG; it's a separate inefficiency. If price later trades down to the low of this displacement and reverses back up into the inefficiency zone, that becomes an inversion FVG long entry opportunity.
- **How to identify:** Identify a down-close candle (close below open); mark its range (high to low); this is the displacement lower.
- **How to use:** When price trades down into the low of this displacement and bounces back up, enter long into the inefficiency zone (between the wick low and body close). Stop below the wick low. Target upward.
- **Example in this video:** 9:30 AM ET showed a displacement lower; later, price pulled back into it, qualifying it as an inversion FVG entry for longs.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:44](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=284) "displacement lower... sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency," [0:04:44](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=284) "potential inversion fair value gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't confuse displacement lower with FPFVG; displacement is a separate inefficiency.

### Fibonacci Projections (Half-Wick as Target)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Identify a range (wick high to wick low); use Fibonacci to measure the distance. The midpoint (50% level) or other Fibonacci ratios (like 0.618, 0.382) act as target levels for price retracements and continuations. Especially useful for identifying where price is likely to stall or reverse.
- **How to identify:** Mark a wick or range; measure high to low; apply Fibonacci tool; identify 50%, 61.8%, 38.2% levels.
- **How to use:** Use these levels as micro-targets for exits, stops, or trend confirmation. Bodies respecting these levels = confirmation of direction.
- **Example in this video:** Fibonacci levels of prior wicks were used to identify exact price targets (e.g., 29,757.25 as next target, then 830s as extension target).
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:24](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=144) "grab our fib and anchor it to that range," [0:03:59](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=239) "29,757 and a quarter uh that would be the next upside draw."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Multiple Fibonacci levels on same chart require discipline to avoid clutter; prioritize key levels only.

### Trader Development Three-Stage Model
- **Kind:** rule (process)
- **What ICT says:** New traders should develop through three stages:
  1. **Stage 1: Observation & Logging (1+ months)** — Study price action, screenshot key levels, annotate, journalize. Do NOT trade (not even demo). Focus on seeing repeating patterns.
  2. **Stage 2: Demo/Paper Trading (minimum 2–3 months)** — Trade using only the patterns you observed. Use no leverage or minimal leverage. Measure like a scientist; don't chase money.
  3. **Stage 3: Live Trading** — Only when you're no longer excited or scared about outcomes; when you know the process repeats; when you're certain you're not impulsively chasing.
  Never rush through these stages; they cannot be skipped. Each stage builds courage and conviction through repetition.
- **How to identify:** Self-assess: Are you still impulsive? Still chasing? Still afraid? If yes, you're not ready for the next stage.
- **How to use:** Follow the progression rigorously. Use observation phase to understand price action mechanics, not to find trade opportunities. Spend time in demo before committing real money.
- **Example in this video:** ICT emphasizes avoiding demo trading during Jackson Hole week; instead, use it for observation of the seek-and-destroy model.
- **Timestamps:** [0:23:47](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=1427) "It's a matter of collecting information, data," [0:24:33](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=1473) "Minimum, 3 months... demo or paper trading," [0:25:20](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=1520) "you're probably going to be ready to trade... no longer excited or scared."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** These stages are not negotiable; many traders skip them and blow accounts.

### Trader Independence & Mentality
- **Kind:** rule (psychology)
- **What ICT says:** As a trader, you owe no one your results or schedule. Don't feel obligated to trade every day (even Mondays or Fridays) just because content creators, live streamers, or social media expect it. The strongest traders close profitable weeks early (take 3-day weekends) rather than chasing more money. Trading every day when you're already profitable is greed, not discipline. Conversely, don't skip trading just to please followers. Make decisions based on YOUR risk/reward, not external pressure.

### Inversion FVG on Multi-Day Structures
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Inversion FVG logic isn't limited to intraday charts. When reviewing multi-day price action, an inefficiency (gap or imbalance) formed on a prior day can become an inversion FVG target on the current day. Example: If Friday shows a gap down (displacement lower), price on Monday may run down into that Friday gap zone and reverse back up — that Friday gap becomes a multi-day inversion FVG.
- **How to identify:** Review prior day(s) for inefficiencies (gaps, imbalances); identify the range (high to low); mark that range.
- **How to use:** On the current day, if price reaches the low of that prior inefficiency, anticipate a reversal back into the inefficiency zone for long entries. Stop below; target above.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's inefficiency (displacement lower at 9:30 AM) was used as a multi-day reference; price action throughout the day interacted with this level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:44](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=284) "inversion fair value gap... trades below it... goes like one or two ticks below it, that's fine."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Extend these levels forward; they retain validity across multiple days.
