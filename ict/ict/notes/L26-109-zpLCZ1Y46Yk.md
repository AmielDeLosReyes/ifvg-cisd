## Concepts

### Event-Driven Markets (aliases: geopolitical volatility, news-driven, fundamentally unstable)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Event-driven markets are those where major news (war, leadership changes, policy shifts) temporarily overrides technical analysis. Algorithms are paused; manual intervention increases; price moves become unpredictable. You cannot apply normal tape reading rules because the rules are suspended by events. The best traders do not fight event-driven markets; they sit out.
- **How to identify:** Watch for geopolitical headlines, major policy announcements, and wars/conflicts. When these are active, expect chop, reversals, and 400-handle candles. Markets will also be illiquid (wider spreads).
- **How to use:** When event-driven, reduce size by 50%+ or step away entirely. Accept that you will be wrong more often. Do not demand normal profitability; be grateful if you break even. Consider shifting to education (tape reading) instead of trading.
- **Example in this video:** ICT references Trump's threats to attack Iran, then a sudden ceasefire announcement, causing crude oil to gap down 18+ dollars in minutes. This is event-driven. Normal tape reading rules don't apply.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT is explicit: even a 34-year veteran cannot "consistently correct" in event-driven conditions. This is humbling and real.

### Manual Intervention (aliases: human intervention, direct influence, not algorithmic, hedge fund positioning)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Smart money and governments place large bets ahead of announcements, then use the announcements to trigger their favorable moves. This is "manual intervention"—a human decision to execute a large order at a strategic time, not an algorithm following price action rules. The evidence: a move happens right after a headline, not because the headline changed long-term fair value but because someone was ready to execute.
- **How to identify:** A major headline drops, and price moves 500+ handles in minutes. That's manual intervention, not algorithmic movement. Also, look for moves during off-hours (Asian session, pre-market) that are disproportionate to the news.
- **How to use:** Expect these moves during major events. Use wide stops. Do not hold without stops. If a manual intervention move is against you, size is critical; if your position size is too large, you're wiped out before you can react.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil gapping down 18+ dollars (9+ points per contract) within minutes after ceasefire announced. This isn't supply/demand; this is a pre-positioned short being executed.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:43](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=523) discusses manual intervention on crude oil; [0:20:47](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1247) explains manual intervention in crypto/equities too.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** ICT is suggesting this is insider trading or at minimum, pre-positioning before announcements. This is a serious allegation but not directly actionable for individual traders.

### Rug Pull (aliases: reverse, bait-and-switch, false breakout into trap)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A rug pull is when price makes a strong move in one direction, stops retail traders into that direction (triggering their alarms), then violently reverses and takes the other side. Example: price rallies hard on war fear, stops longs above a key level, then news drops that ceasefire is likely, and shorts are in control. The rally was the rug pull; retail thought it was the main move.
- **How to identify:** Price makes a strong move; you see clips online of traders bragging about their wins; then suddenly price reverses violently. That was a rug pull.
- **How to use:** Don't fall for the initial move. Wait for confirmation that the move is sustained. Use stops to protect yourself if you're caught.
- **Example in this video:** War fear rallies in equities; Trump threatens attacks; retail chases the rally. Then news of ceasefire; rally reversed. The initial rally was the rug pull.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:43](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=523) references the rug pull structure implicitly.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rug pulls are more obvious in crypto; in equities/futures, they're subtler but same concept.

### Volatility Expansion (aliases: large range candles, speed of moves, magnitude increase)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Event-driven markets show extreme volatility: 1-minute candles spanning 400+ handles, 5-minute candles spanning 1,200+ handles. This is not normal. Normal markets have tight, predictable ranges. Event-driven markets break those assumptions. You must respect this by using tight stops and small size.
- **How to identify:** Watch the range of individual 1-minute candles. If you see a 400-handle 1-minute, that's expansion. If you see multiple 1-minute candles with 100+ handle ranges, that's expansion.
- **How to use:** Respect it. If your position size assumes normal volatility (20–40 handle stops), you'll be wiped out by a 400-handle candle. Reduce size or use wider stops (but then you can't take as many trades).
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1-minute candle: opened 24,600, high 24,748, low 24,360. That's 388 handles in 60 seconds. This is extreme.
- **Timestamps:** [0:39:10](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=2350) shows the 400-handle 1-minute candle; [0:42:21](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=2541) repeats this to emphasize risk.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a wake-up call. If you trade without respecting this volatility, you're taking unnecessary risk.

### Education vs. Live Trading Stance (aliases: responsibility teaching, stepping away from live, shifting focus)
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:** ICT decides to step away from live trading and focus only on education because he has influence over many traders. - **How to identify:** Is your mentor live trading during volatile markets and encouraging you to follow? That's irresponsible. Is your mentor telling you to sit out and practice tape reading? That's responsible.
- **How to use:** Apply this to your own trading: when conditions are unpredictable, don't trade. Teach yourself; practice; wait for clarity. Responsibility to yourself and your capital comes first.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (aliases: inverted FVG, gap in trend-opposite direction)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** An inversion FVG is a gap that forms in the opposite direction of the trend. When price trades back into and above an inversion FVG, it confirms continuation of the original trend. This is a strong continuation signal.
- **How to identify:** In an uptrend, a down-gapped candle creates an inversion FVG. In a downtrend, an up-gapped candle creates one.
- **How to use:** When price re-enters and closes above an inversion FVG (in an uptrend) or below one (in a downtrend), expect continuation.
- **Example in this video:** Implied in USD/JPY and EUR/USD reviews; gaps form, then price re-enters them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:28:48](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1728) references inversion FVG in EUR/USD context.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In event-driven markets, these setups are less reliable.

### Premium / Discount Zones (aliases: overbought/oversold, supply/demand zones, high/low price zones)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Price trades in zones: premium (above fair value, resistance-heavy), equilibrium (fair value), and discount (below fair value, support-heavy). Identify these on daily charts using suspension blocks and opening range gaps. Use them to frame intraday trades.
- **How to identify:** Is price above the midpoint of the daily range? Premium. Below? Discount. At midpoint? Equilibrium.
- **How to use:** Premium zones: look for sells, reversals, short entries. Discount zones: look for buys, bounces, long entries. Equilibrium: expect consolidation.
- **Example in this video:** Currency pairs are analyzed by identifying where they trade relative to daily ranges.
- **Timestamps:** Multiple references throughout (not timestamped individually).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** None stated.

### Consequent Encroachment on Wicks (aliases: wick midpoint, wick rejection level, consequent curvature)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When a candlestick forms a large wick (rejection), the consequent encroachment (midpoint) of that wick becomes a key level. If price breaks above that wick midpoint, the wick rejection is breached and bearishness is broken. If price stays below it, the wick rejection holds.
- **How to identify:** Measure the wick high to wick low (or wick to body). Find the 50% point.
- **How to use:** This is a precision level for stops and partial exits. If you're short and price breaks above wick consequent encroachment, the setup is broken; exit.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ tape reading: consequent curvature of wicks used to judge whether bearish tone persists.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:58](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=958) uses this concept; [0:21:33](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1293) applies it to risk management.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a sub-level of detail; use when you've mastered the main levels.

### Relative Equal Highs / Lows (aliases: equality levels, matched highs/lows)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When two swing highs (or lows) are at nearly the same level, they form a relative equal high (or low). These are key breakout/breakdown levels. A break of relative equal highs is a strong bullish signal; a break of relative equal lows is bearish.
- **How to identify:** Plot daily swings. When two swing highs or lows align, mark them as relative equal.
- **How to use:** Relative equal highs act as resistance until broken; then they act as support. Relative equal lows act as support until broken; then they act as resistance.
- **Example in this video:** Pound/Dollar and EUR/USD analyzed using relative equal highs as targets.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:10](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1870) shows relative equal highs on cable (GBP/USD).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In event-driven markets, these levels may be blown through.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Do not over-leverage in event-driven markets.** Position size must be 50% or less of normal.
- **Use wide stops.** Event-driven means 400-handle candles; if your stop is 20 handles, you're wiped out in one candle.
- **Do not hold without stops.** Ever. Not in normal markets, definitely not in event-driven.
- **Manual intervention is not random.** Even when being "played," price goes to an algorithmic level, not a guess. But timing is unpredictable.
- **Geopolitical events override technical analysis.** When at risk, technicals are secondary; risk management (stops, size) is primary.
- **One-minute candle ranges can exceed 400 handles.** Plan for it.
- **Off-hours gaps (Asian session, pre-market) can be extreme.** Don't assume overnight gap-fill happens intraday.
- **Decoupling (NQ, ES, YM not in sync) is common in event-driven markets.** Expect chop and poor precision.
- **Take your profit when available.** In event-driven markets, don't hold for "the big run." Take small wins and move on.
- **Stay out if you're not comfortable with the risk.** This is not weakness; it's wisdom.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Session setup during event-driven conditions:** Same as normal (identify gap, daily suspension block, levels), but adjust expectations. Don't expect 70% accuracy; 50% is acceptable.
- **Risk management:** Use 50% of normal size. Use 2x normal stop distance. Use tighter profit targets (take 50% profits early, let the rest run with a wider stop).
- **Emotional regulation:** Events trigger fear and greed. Price up on war fears triggers buying (greed). Price down on ceasefire triggers covering (fear). These emotional moves benefit early-knowing parties and hurt retail. Don't be emotional; be robotic.
- **Tape reading priority:** Shift focus from trading to tape reading. Watch price action without capital at risk. Learn how price responds to events. Build pattern recognition for the next time events occur.
- **Moral responsibility:** If you're influencing others (mentor, coach), disclose the risks. Don't sugarcoat event-driven conditions. Either sit out or make clients smaller/stop losses mandatory.
- **Recovery mindset:** After an event-driven period, prices normalize. Use that opportunity to rebuild any losses through careful, normal trading.

## Glossary candidates

`EVENT-DRIVEN MARKETS` — Markets where geopolitical events, policy announcements, or major news temporarily override algorithmic order flow; technical precision is reduced.

`MANUAL INTERVENTION` — Direct execution of large orders by smart money/governments at strategic times around events; not algorithmic, but predictable to those with prior knowledge.

`RUG PULL` — A move that triggers retail traders in one direction, then reverses violently, trapping those traders; often related to event-driven news releases.

`VOLATILITY EXPANSION` — Increase in candlestick ranges due to fear/euphoria; 1-minute candles may span 400+ handles during event-driven periods.

`CONSEQUENT ENCROACHMENT ON WICKS` — The 50% midpoint of a candlestick wick; used to judge if wick rejection holds or is breached.

`RELATIVE EQUAL HIGHS/LOWS` — Two swing highs or lows at similar price levels; act as key breakout/breakdown points.

`PREMIUM/DISCOUNT/EQUILIBRIUM ZONES` — Price above, below, or at the midpoint of a daily range; used to frame directional bias.

`INVERSION FVG` — A gap formed opposite the trend direction; when price re-enters it from the opposite side, signals continuation.

`LIQUIDITY ILLIQUIDITY` — Event-driven markets show wider spreads and less depth; fills may be worse than expected.

`INSIDER TRADING / PRE-POSITIONING` — Implied when large moves happen right after events; suggests advance knowledge of the announcement.
