## Concepts

### SMT Divergence (Smart Money Trend Divergence)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** SMT divergence occurs when correlated indices move in opposite directions at key levels. Example: Nasdaq makes a lower high while S&P makes a higher high at the same time (relative strength divergence). This indicates the weakest index will pull the broader market lower; strong ones hide the distribution happening underneath.
- **How to identify:** On daily line charts (plotting high or low, not close), overlay correlated pairs (NQ vs ES vs YM). Mark significant highs/lows at the same time period. If one can't keep up = divergence. NQ failing to make new highs while ES makes them = bearish for Nasdaq.
- **How to use:** When weakest index (Nasdaq) shows distribution (lower high), expect pullback to be MORE aggressive in that index. Use divergence as bias confirmation. Don't expect normal correlated moves; expect weakness to cascade.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq failed to make higher highs in late January while S&P did. This = distribution. Expect Nasdaq to pull down harder than S&P in any selloff. Also, S&P rejected higher highs last night (March 8) while Dow tried.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:18](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=198) — explaining how to set up the charts; [0:04:06](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=246) — showing the divergence visually; [0:11:05](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=665) — connecting divergence to bearish distribution
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a FORM of relative strength analysis, not a standalone indicator. Use it with PD arrays and order flow, not alone. SMT is a filter, not a signal.

### Macro Times (Algorithmic Time Windows)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** Markets move on scheduled algorithmic time windows. Examples: 8:50–10:10 macro (opening algos), 2:50–3:10 macro (lunch end), 3:15–3:45 macro (last hour spool / market on close setup). Last hour has multiple macros: 2:50–3:10, 3:15–3:45, then market on close (MOC) at 3:45–4:00. These are NOT indicators; they're algorithmic activity triggers.
- **How to identify:** Note the times on your charts. Watch for acceleration or volatility spikes at these windows. Compare across days—pattern should repeat.
- **How to use:** Anticipate moves within these windows. Don't fight them; expect liquidity runs during macro times. If a macro is supposed to create a move and nothing happens, market may be in consolidation or manipulation.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil March delivery: opened at ~10:40 p.m. ET Sunday, ran to near inefficiency high, sold into evening macro (10:20–11:00 p.m.), then ripped down at 3:15 p.m. macro on Monday. Lowest candle at 3:39 p.m., confirming the last-hour macro window.

### Propulsion Block & Order Block Theory
- **Kind:** definition / entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:** An order block is a candlestick or series representing institutional activity. A propulsion block is the LAST down-close candlestick in a series; when price trades above its open, it "propels" upward (like rocket fuel). Minimum criteria: price never breaches mean threshold (50% of block range) on retrace—if it does, block is invalid. Once breached on opening basis, expect extrapolation.
- **How to identify:** Find a down-close candlestick (or series). Check if it's the "last" (is the next candle up-close or outside the block range?). Measure the range and mark mean threshold.
- **How to use:** Entry: when price breaks above the opening of a propulsion block. Stop loss: just below the block (or mean threshold, depending on risk tolerance). Target: trace to next unmet buy-side liquidity. Expect rapid extrapolation.
- **Example in this video:** ES March 09: series of down-close candles = order blocks. Last one = propulsion block. When price broke above its open, it "shot all the way" up (his words). Two propulsion blocks in series = "double rocket fuel."
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:00](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1620) — defining propulsion block; [0:27:47](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1667) — showing the two-candlestick propulsion setup and extrapolation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He references "Month 4 content of the 2016 playlist" (his mentorship videos, now free) for deep order block teaching. Emphasizes: mean threshold is MINIMUM; block can fail if breached on close. Common mistake: calling any down-close candle an "order block" without confirming the last-in-series rule and mean threshold hold.

### Turtle Soup (Failed Breakout Reversal)
- **Kind:** entry model / example only
- **What ICT says:** Turtle soup = price trades below a recent low (fails to break out) and immediately reverses. Classic manipulation setup: run a low to stop out bears, then reverse. Not a buy signal itself; rather, context for where NOT to be short and WHERE to go long (back above failed low).
- **How to identify:** Recent swing low broken = turtle soup potential. Watch for immediate reversal back above that low.
- **How to use:** Do NOT short below the low when it's being tested (likely to fail). If it fails, trade long above it. Stop loss: below the low just tested. Target: buy-side liquidity or next key level.
- **Example in this video:** ES on March 09: traded below an earlier low (looked like continuation trade), but immediately bounced back up = turtle soup environment. Not shorting opportunity; a long opportunity using the failed break as confirmation.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:47](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1667) — "In other words, go below it, outside of it, and trade back up into it" = turtle soup definition
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Common in low-volatility or consolidation ranges where algos shake out weak positions before the real move.

### Change in State of Delivery (CISD)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** CISD is when price action shifts from consolidation/ranging to a trending move, or from up to down. Identified by candlestick pattern: a change in the candlestick's character (from up-close to down-close or vice versa) that signals the end of a prior trend or the start of a new one.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candlestick that closes opposite the prior candlestick's direction. For example: several up-closes, then a down-close with body below previous range = CISD.
- **How to use:** CISD can be an exit trigger (trend ended) or entry confirmation (new trend starting). Once CISD occurs, check if price is respecting new levels; if bodies are above/below previous inefficiencies, use those as targets.
- **Example in this video:** Not deeply explored in this video, but referenced in passing during ES review.
- **Timestamps:** Mentioned but not detailed with specific timestamp.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** CISD is subtle; wicks don't count, only bodies. One down-close doesn't mean the trend reversed forever; wait for confirmation through subsequent candles respecting new structure.

### Market Maker Buy/Sell Model (MMBM/MMSM)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Market makers use a buy model (accumulation at lower prices) and sell model (distribution at higher prices) to drive order flow. When Nasdaq shows unwillingness to make higher highs = market maker is in sell mode (distribution). Understanding the model = understanding what price must do next.
- **How to identify:** Cross-asset divergence (like SMT) + candle rejection patterns = evidence of sell/buy model in play.
- **How to use:** If market maker is selling (shown by distribution/SMT divergence), expect pullback and lower prices. If buying (NQ leading), expect higher prices. Trade WITH the model, not against it.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq failing to make new highs while others do = market maker is selling Nasdaq; expect Nasdaq lower.
- **Timestamps:** Not explicitly detailed with timestamp; embedded in broader SMT discussion.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is not a mechanical rule; it's an observation of algorithmic behavior. Multiple factors (news, geopolitical events) can override.

## Glossary candidates

`SMT Divergence` — When correlated indices show relative strength differences (e.g., NQ makes lower high, ES makes higher high at same time); indicates the weaker index will lead pullback and show more distribution.  
`Macro Time` — Algorithmic activity window (e.g., 3:15–3:45 p.m. ET); market often accelerates or executes final moves within these times.  
`Propulsion Block` — The last down-close candlestick in a series; when price breaks above its opening, it triggers rapid upside extrapolation ("rocket fuel").  
`Mean Threshold` — The 50% midpoint of an order block range; breaching this on close invalidates the block as a support.  
`Turtle Soup` — A failed breakout below a recent low that reverses sharply upward; signals a long opportunity above the failed low, not a short.  
`CISD (Change in State of Delivery)` — Shift in candlestick pattern signaling trend end or reversal (e.g., up-close candles followed by down-close).  
`Relative Strength` — Comparison of two correlated instruments to identify which is leading (strong) and which is lagging (weak); lagging index expected to pull market in its direction.
