## Summary
On a Monday that opened almost unchanged from Friday's settlement, ICT shows the protocol for a weak opening range gap. When the open is within about 30–40 handles of the previous settlement, do nothing until 10:00, because the algorithm will do what it does in the first 30 minutes and early opinions tend to be wrong. While waiting he teaches several things. The first is the "immediate rebalance", a one-candle tell of coming speed. The second is how a 15-minute buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (BISI) that price closes through becomes an inversion FVG. The third is that candle bodies, not wicks, say whether anything has changed. The fourth is that nested NDOG/NWOG act as a "black hole" magnet for price. No trade is taken, and he calls that a win: no loss, no commission, and patience gained. The long second half argues that stop-loss discipline, one micro contract, at most one trade a day, a boring rule-based process, not model-hopping, and self-knowledge of one's emotional triggers matter more than any setup. He also corrects a YouTuber's CISD diagram.

## Concepts

### Opening Range Gap (RTH) — small / "anemic" gap protocol  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap high/low, "previous settlement", "little unchanged", discount gap / lower gap opening)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the RTH chart, the opening range gap (ORG) is the space between the previous day's regular-hours settlement and the 9:30 ET open. The previous settlement (Friday's 4:14 pm ET RTH price here) is the ORG high when the open is lower, and the 9:30 opening price is the ORG low. On Friday the market keeps trading electronically after the RTH close for another 46 minutes. On other days it closes and reopens an hour later at 6:00 pm ET.
  - Before 9:30 he labels the ORG low "opening range gap low / settlement" and fixes the label when the real open prints. Toggling between RTH and electronic (ETH) hours lets you see the gap.
  - "Little unchanged" means the 9:30 open is less than about 30–40 handles from the previous settlement. If the market cannot muster a bigger difference at the opening bell, he sits on his hands until 10:00.
  - The first question for a morning session is whether the market is opening significantly higher or lower than the previous settlement. Larger gaps "invite larger volatility and a lot more protraction". A very small gap signals a market reluctant to run.
  - Scale reference: a 20-handle move is 19,732 to about 19,752 on the chart (the caption says "7,52", so 19,752 is inferred). The ORG that day was not much bigger than that.
- **How to identify:** compare the previous RTH settlement price (4:14 pm ET candle) with the 9:30 open on the RTH chart. If the difference is under about 30–40 handles, it is a weak gap.
- **How to use:**
  - Protocol for a weak gap: sit still, wait until 10:00, wait for the Silver Bullet or something more obvious, and give the algorithm the full first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00). "I don't care if there's a fair value gap." Ignore anything that shows up before then.
  - Don't elect a liquidity target or take a trade early. He says that over about 25 years of using ORGs he has been wrong when he forced it.
  - Conditional he gave: had the open been a higher gap, at or above the 8/21 NDOG, he would have looked for a drop into the 8/25 NWOG (using its consequent encroachment) and then a long up into the buy side above.
  - Do not treat the ORG high as the CE. The CE is the midpoint between the ORG low and high, which is a different price.
- **Example in this video:** Mon 8/26/2024 (stream starts about 9:31 ET). Pre-open, price had a higher-gap setup but erased it. On the 2-minute chart it hammered the CE of the 8/25 NWOG and Friday's settlement, then dropped and displaced below relative equal lows (sell side disturbed). It had already taken a buy-side level and was seeking minor short-term buy side above. At 9:30 it opened with a small discount (lower) gap just under settlement. Price traded back above the ORG high (settlement), stayed inside the 8/25 NWOG, failed hard near the 8/21 NDOG, and returned to the southern end of the ORG. He sat out all of 9:30–10:00.

### New Day / New Week Opening Gaps (NDOG / NWOG) as nested magnets — "Caleb's model" draw  (aliases ICT uses: "black hole", draw on liquidity, layered/nested gaps)
- **Kind:** rule / entry model (draw only; entry is an FVG or iFVG)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Caleb's draw on liquidity is NDOGs and NWOGs. When several are layered or nested together they act "like a big black hole" and a very large magnet that "sucks price in". Retail tools (support/resistance lines, pitchforks) will not show this.
  - The model chain he lays out: OTE (his long-running flagship), then the 2022 model (his daughter's), then the Silver Bullet (Cameron's), and now NDOG/NWOG plus an FVG, "first presentation", as Caleb's model.

- **How to identify:** mark each NDOG/NWOG on the chart and extend it right. Look for clusters where two or more overlap or sit close together below (or above) price.
- **How to use:** target the nested cluster as the draw once a valid entry appears. A "real nice setup": price trades up into an FVG or iFVG, rejects, and then runs down into the nested gaps. Use the CE of a gap as the reaction level.
- **Example in this video:** the 8/23 NDOG and the 8/18 NWOG were nested below price, with the 8/21 NDOG and 8/25 NWOG above. After the 10:00 hour price dropped into the nested 8/23 NDOG / 8/18 NWOG. ### Immediate Rebalance  (aliases ICT uses: "one directional and an immediate rebalance")
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - It is a single candle with a large range lower and a lower close, clearly bigger than the surrounding ranges, followed by a candle that opens and trades all the way back up to touch the prior candle's low. That low is the level that would have bounded a bearish FVG. The down candle's whole range is re-delivered with buy side, so the gap never forms.
  - The rebalance must happen on the candle immediately after the big down candle. If the first candle does not do it, the next one still counts. After that it is not valid.
  - It works on all timeframes.
  - In a bearish context it is a strong continuation tell, "tipping its hand" like a poker player flashing a full house. It promotes speed and large range, and it is not a reversal.
  - He describes only the bearish case, and says "it matters not if it goes higher or lower" (caption unclear, probably meaning the mirror image is equivalent).
- **How to identify (checklist):**
  1. A low forms.
  2. One candle prints a large down-close range, larger than recent candles.
  3. The next candle (at most the following one) opens and trades back up to the prior candle's low.
- **How to use:** if you are short from higher, or want to short or add (pyramid), anticipate this when the large down candle closes. Watch where the next candle opens and expect it to run back to the prior candle's low, then expect speed to the downside. He says it "might be your model" in itself. Retail chat reads it as a reversal (candlestick logic, "Steve Nison") and gets it backwards.
- **Example in this video:** 1-minute chart just before and around 10:00 ET (video about 0:26–0:31). Price made a low, printed a big down-close candle below the prior candle's low, and the next candle opened and ran back up to that low. Price then took out the low, "boom". He used it as the example of an observation to screenshot and annotate.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:27:38](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1658) — walk-through of the candle sequence.
  - [0:28:25](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1705) — "fair value gaps are not a renaming of imbalance": the superior form is the gap that does not fill in.
  - [0:32:30](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1950) — definition: single big candle, lower close, must be larger than prior ranges.
  - [0:33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998) — one-or-two-candle window and how to use it.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** the candle-count window is stated slightly inconsistently. At 0:31:40 it is "valid if it does it on the next candle, but after that it's not", and at 0:33:18 he allows the candle after the first. Treat it as one or two candles. The one example is only partly clear in the captions, so the diagram should be redrawn from the description above.

### Fair Value Gap — context matters; the "not filling in" form  (aliases ICT uses: inefficiency, BISI/SIBI, volume imbalance)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**

  - He criticizes a YouTuber who calls FVGs a renaming of an imbalance. The FVG he cares about is a specific signature where price does not fill in. "The highest order of trading them" is to see them not close in, which he says he proved with real money in live sessions.
  - On Friday he gray-shaded a gap because he wanted to see whether price displaced down from it. It then traded down and went through the gap, so he treated it as an inversion FVG.
  - Viewers who said he was wrong were incorrect, in his account. His usage: the bottom of the gap acts as resistance (premium) and the upper half is left open. (The captions of this explanation are partly garbled.)
- **How to use:** don't trade every three-candle gap. Ask what price is doing around it and whether price respects it or only wicks into it.
- **Timestamps:**

### Inversion FVG (iFVG) on a 15-minute BISI + lower-timeframe entry  (aliases ICT uses: "inversion fair value", BISI / "buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency", "institutional order flow entry drill")
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A 15-minute BISI (a big up-close 15-minute candle's gap) sits below price as a discount array. When price finally "snaps through" it (closes through), it becomes an inversion FVG.
  - Use the CE (midpoint) as the key level. The setup is bearish as long as price does not return above that midpoint. It may wick to or through it, but it is better if price only trades back up into the lower half and falls from there. You never want to see price go back into the upper half.
  - Ideal path: price leaves, comes back up, bumps a wick just below the CE, and leaves.
  - Entry on the lower timeframe: on a 15/30/45-second chart (he is on 1-minute), after price leaves the iFVG and displaces away, take the very next FVG that forms on those charts, which he calls the "institutional orderflow entry drill". Then target the next sell-side liquidity or inefficiency.
  - A sell-side target in this case was the nested NDOG/NWOG cluster.
- **How to identify:**
  1. A larger discount array (15-minute BISI) exists below.
  2. Price closes through it (bodies, not just a wick).
  3. Price returns to the high or the CE of the shaded area.
  4. Price stays below the midpoint.
- **How to use:** short from the lower half or CE of the iFVG after a lower-timeframe FVG forms. Stop and size are not given here beyond his general rules. The target is the next sell-side liquidity or a nested NDOG/NWOG.
- **Example in this video:** about 10:05–10:15 ET. Price traded down through two relative equal lows but had not closed through the whole 15-minute BISI. Bounces back up to the high of that gap had bodies not closing in it ("did not close out of it"), so it was "nothing yet". Then price closed through the BISI and he watched it return to the gap for a rejection (iFVG behavior). It later fell toward the nested 8/23 NDOG / 8/18 NWOG and the old low 19,591.75, and accelerated lower. There was no 1-minute FVG at 10:00 to enter from, so there was no trade for Caleb. He said a better setup would have been a rally into a small 1-minute FVG and a drop from it.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:34:05](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2045) — the 15-minute BISI not fully closed, bodies still show nothing.
  - [0:37:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2239) — price "snapped through"; the CE (half) rule.
  - [0:38:56](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2336) — return to the high of the gap without a close in it; "neon sign".
  - [0:50:14](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=3014) — iFVG rules and the 15/30/45-second entry drill.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he repeatedly says not to try to pick the bottom ("stinky fingers") while the bodies still say lower. He also says that if price fails even to retrace to the halfway point of a small SIBI, it never reaches the iFVG and likely runs down aggressively with large, quick ranges (0:39:44).

### Bodies versus wicks — what the candle closes say
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Wicks into a level prove little. The candle bodies (closes) show whether the narrative has changed. "Look where the bodies are."
  - A wick into the 15-minute BISI or iFVG without a body closing in it means nothing has changed, so don't try to catch the turn.
  - Momentum and delivery being lower means any bounce is only a return to premium. A bullish body or wick alone is not a reversal.
- **How to use:** require closes (bodies) through a level to change your read. Otherwise treat wicks as noise, and sit still.
- **Timestamps:**

### Retail logic that misleads: three drives, divergence, hidden divergence
- **Kind:** definition / example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - Three consecutive lows (three drives, "three Indians" in Street Smarts) look like a reversal pattern to retail traders. The bodies said nothing had changed.
  - Standard momentum divergence (RSI/CCI/MACD/stochastic) would have shown bullish divergence at the lows. He lost money on it in 1992–94 and says price doesn't respect it.
  - Trend-following (type 2) divergence, a higher indicator high against a lower price high, is a "hidden divergence". He credits Nick Vanise for it (name as captioned, unclear) and says it is the useful form.
  - "My stuff makes your retail stuff work."

### Model selection and not model-hopping  (aliases: Enigma, "right-now model")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Pick one model and don't swap it because another would have worked today. "I'm only interested in price if it shows me what I'm looking for."
  - ICT himself has different models for different conditions: choppy/listless markets, trending markets (three or four), reversals, MMBM/MMSM, and intraday volatility. The last is Enigma, which needs no bias, no long-term draws and no PD arrays.
  - A model that gives no setup today is not worse than one that did. Wanting your model in every day, session or instant is the problem.
  - The "right-now model" is whatever you grab to justify getting in, and it is the one that blows accounts.
  - Don't reach for OBs or OTE just because the assigned model has no setup.
  - A model that doesn't fire gives a win: no commission, no loss, practiced patience.
  - He notes it would be easy to show off setups outside Caleb's model and collect praise, but that would not teach Caleb anything (1:13:41).

### Stop-loss philosophy and Caleb's risk rules  (aliases: risk management, one contract, one trade a day)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Never trade without a stop-loss. The stop is a real structural stop (not "a two-handle stop-loss"), placed where you are definitely wrong.
  - A stop defines the moment you are wrong and pays for the lesson. Size so the stop does not hurt: a loss must not shake trust in the model or stop you from taking another trade.
  - People who avoid stops keep blowing funded accounts.
  - Caleb's rules: one contract only, micro first. At most one trade a day. One winning trade for the day, you're done. One losing trade, you're done. Turn the charts off.
  - Funded-account plan: take roughly $5,000 out of the funded account, then move to a live account at AMP Global (the broker he names). Trade a micro and build slowly toward one standard lot, only when trading at 1.5x the full exchange margin. A standard lot on a $300 account is reckless.
  - Fees: commissions are "piranha". Example: $1,000 made on $1,200 of commissions is a loss. Fewer trades means lower costs. One good trade a week puts you ahead of 99% of retail. Four trades a month keeps commissions minimal.
  - The "shield first" idea: learn to survive with small risk before using the sword.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Weak-open rule: open within about 30–40 handles of the previous settlement means wait until 10:00, then use the full 9:30–10:00 opening range as observation only.
- Scale: 20 handles is roughly 19,732 to 19,752.
- Previous settlement is the 4:14 pm ET RTH price. After the Friday settlement the market trades 46 more minutes, then closes. On other days it reopens at 6:00 pm ET.
- AM session is before noon ET. Review the noon close.
- Immediate rebalance: big down-close candle, larger than recent ranges, then the next candle (at most the one after) opens and trades back to the prior candle's low.
- iFVG: bearish while price stays below the CE (midpoint); a wick through it is acceptable but returning to the upper half is not. Entry is the next lower-timeframe FVG (15/30/45-second) after displacement away. The target is the next sell side or a nested NDOG/NWOG.
- CISD / order block: for the bearish case, reference the highest succession of down-close candles, not the lowest one.
- Caleb: one contract, micro first; one trade a day; win or lose, done for the day; stop must be structural; withdraw about $5,000 from the funded account, then trade micro at AMP Global; go to a standard lot only at 1.5x full exchange margin.
- One trade a week beats 99% of retail; four trades a month keeps commissions low.
- Friday PM exercise: ICT once allowed himself only a time-based trade on a Friday between 2:00 and 4:00 pm (ET implied).
- Coin-flip challenge: trade the first return to the ORG, risk 0.25% or less per trade, stop at the other end of the opening range.
- Silver Bullet: "always there"; he says it has not changed.

## Glossary candidates
`ORG (opening range gap)` — the gap between the previous RTH settlement and the 9:30 ET open; settlement is the high when the open is lower.
`Previous settlement` — the prior session's RTH closing price (4:14 pm ET candle).
`Little unchanged` — an open within about 30–40 handles of the previous settlement; wait until 10:00.
`Handle` — a full-point unit of index price (a 20-handle move = 19,732 to 19,752).
`Immediate rebalance` — a large down candle followed at once by a candle trading back up to the prior candle's low, closing the would-be FVG; a speed tell.
`BISI / SIBI` — buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency (and its mirror), ICT's name for an FVG by direction.
`iFVG (inversion fair value gap)` — an FVG that price closes through and that then acts as the opposite-side level; keep price below its CE (midpoint) for a bearish read.
`Institutional orderflow entry drill` — his phrase for the lower-timeframe FVG entry after price leaves an iFVG.
`Nested gaps` — NDOG/NWOG layered at nearby prices, acting as a stronger magnet.
`Bodies` — candle closes, as opposed to wicks, used as evidence that a level was accepted or rejected.
`Right-now model` — the improvised justification for a trade that is not your real model.
`Point of indication` — a magician's term for a tell; used for a trader's visible discomfort.
`Scar tissue` — his term for unresolved emotional history that drives impulsive trading.
`Enigma` — one of his named models; buys and sells intraday volatility without bias, long-term draws or PD arrays.
