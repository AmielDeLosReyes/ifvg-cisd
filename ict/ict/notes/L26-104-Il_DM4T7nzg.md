## Concepts

### Opening Range Gap (RTH Opening Range Gap / New Week Opening Gap)  (aliases: NWOG, gap closure)
- **Kind:** definition + entry context
- **What ICT says:** Mark the gap formed at 9:30 ET (RTH open) or at Sunday 6 p.m. ET (electronic/new-week open). The high and low of that gap are reference levels. A "full gap closure" means price trades all the way back through the gap and out the other side (below the low, if it opened with a premium gap above prior close). Consequent encroachment of that gap (the midpoint) is often a first test. Bodies closing above or below that midpoint tell you whether the move is committed.
- **How to identify:** Find the bar at 9:30 or 6 p.m. ET. Mark the high and low. Measure the midpoint. Track whether bodies breach and hold above/below it.
- **How to use:** If looking for bullish, you want to see bodies stay above the gap's midpoint and then full gap closure + continuation above the prior close (RTH close from previous Friday, 4:14 p.m. ET). If full gap closure doesn't happen with strength, that's a warning sign. Gaps can serve as support/resistance for future retracements.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Sunday opening: large premium gap below Friday close (around 18,340 vs prior 18,530). Monday 9:30 opened even lower. Throughout the morning, price tested the consequent encroachment (gap midpoint), then bodies moved above it. Full gap closure (return to Friday's 4:14 p.m. close level of 18,400+) and continuation above it was the bullish outcome being watched.

- **Nuance / caveats:** Gaps formed in uncertain/event-driven openings (geopolitical, war, peace news) have lower probability early on; bodies' behavior (reluctance, hesitation) is the real signal, not gap location alone. "Reluctance" (price not doing what it "should") is a canary-in-the-coal-mine warning.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) / CE Midpoint  (aliases: wick midpoint, inversion wick CE)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** The exact midpoint of a wick (the gap between the body and the extreme of the wick). When bodies trade back to that midpoint and close **at or just above it** (not into the lower half), that shows institutional rejection of the lower price. The CE midpoint is graded as "strong" (body away from midpoint) or "reluctant" (body touching midpoint).
- **How to identify:** Wick visible. Measure/eyeball midpoint. Watch where body closes relative to it on the next candle(s).
- **How to use:** If bodies close above CE, that's strength. If bodies touch CE and struggle away, that's reluctance/warning. On micro-timeframes (30s, 15s), CE rejection (not touching) on repeated tests = very strong bullish setup.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: early candles dropped into a gap with wick. CE midpoint of that wick was referenced. When price came back down to it and the body closed just above CE (not into lower half), that showed "agreement" = continued bullish. When bodies touched CE and bounced without hesitation, that was strength. Repeated on 15s: bodies respecting CE midpoint and bouncing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:14](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=74) consequent encroachment above upper quad of opening gap. [0:12:13](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=733) wick midpoint rule: if bodies stay below it, strong rejection. [0:13:02](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=782) watch midpoint for acceleration signal. [0:20:06](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=1206) reluctance at CE = warning (any other day it would have already taken sell side).
- **Nuance / caveats:** CE is not a hard support; it's a reference point where you read body behavior. "Reluctance" at CE (not taking out the level when it "should") is the canary—a sign price is planning to go the other way.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) / Inversion FVG / Reclaimed FVG  (aliases: imbalance, inversion, reclaimed bullish/bearish)
- **Kind:** definition + entry model + rule
- **What ICT says:** A FVG is a gap not covered by candlestick bodies. On 1m, 30s, 15s, a FVG below price (sell-side gap) can become an "inversion FVG" when price trades back into it. If bodies subsequently close above the midpoint of that inversion FVG, it "reclaims" as a bullish FVG (buy signal, further upside likely). If bodies close below, it "reclaims" as bearish FVG (sell signal, further downside likely). Entries are taken at the **next FVG after confirmation**, not at the retracement FVG itself.
- **How to identify:** Price gap (wick-defined). Body positions relative to that gap. Subsequent candlestick's body position (upper vs lower half of prior gap).
- **How to use:** When a potential reclaimed bullish FVG forms (bodies above midpoint of inversion gap), screenshot it. Drop to lower timeframe (1m to 30s, 30s to 15s). Wait for the next FVG to form on that lower timeframe. Once bodies confirm they're rejecting the lower half of that new FVG, that's your entry. Target is typically the next minor buy/sell side level. Stop below the low of the entry candle.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m dropped into a gap (discount FVG), bodies stayed above midpoint. Price rallied. At the top, a new gap formed (premium FVG, inversion). Bodies closed above that inversion's midpoint = reclaimed bullish. Drop to 15s: bodies confirm they're above lower halves of subsequent gaps = continuation up. Entry at a confirmed new FVG on 15s when it shows no willingness to go lower, then accelerates up. Target the prior short-term high or next minor buy side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:13](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=373) full gap closure expectation. [0:14:49](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=889) inversion FVG becomes reclaimed bullish if closed above. [0:35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126) bread-and-butter entry: inversion FVG, bodies above midpoint, next FVG entry. [0:48:59](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2939) reclaimed bearish FVG criteria: bodies below midpoint on closing basis. [0:50:44](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=3044) 15s chart shows FVG formation in proximity to 1m reference levels.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Entry is **not at the inversion retracement**; it's at the **next new FVG** after the inversion reclaim is confirmed. Many traders enter too early (at the inversion) and get whipsawed.

### Stop Hunt  (aliases: stop run, liquidation run)
- **Kind:** rule + market maker behavior
- **What ICT says:** When price rapidly dips below a key support level (where retail traders' stops are placed), takes them out, then immediately reverses back up (often higher than before), that's a stop hunt. Market makers/institutions are "hunting" for retail stop orders. The subsequent reversal (after stops are cleared) shows their real intent is to go the opposite direction. This happens in sync with algorithmic models firing once the algorithmic condition is met.
- **How to identify:** Price drops sharply, wicks below a key low (often below a support zone or FVG), then body closes far above that low on the next candle. The wick is much longer than the body.
- **How to use:** When you see a stop hunt (bodies didn't go lower, just the wick), don't assume the move is over. Expect the opposite direction. Follow the same model, watch for the next FVG on the lower timeframe, and anticipate a run in the opposite direction. Don't be fooled into shorting after the hunt.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: bodies reluctant to go lower, then a sharp wick down (stop hunt likely), followed by a powerful rally. ICT warns: don't assume this is an inversion; watch the bodies stay above subsequent levels (which they did) and re-affirm bullishness. Another run down (potential stop hunt) then reversal and run to higher minor buy side. This is "the same model, just in a different location."

- **Nuance / caveats:** Stop hunts are not "rigged" against you in a moral sense; they're algorithmic efficiency: once the algorithmic condition is met (stop cleared), the dormant model fires up and hunts longs.

### One-Sidedness / High-Probability Setup  (aliases: obvious direction, strong setup, conviction)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** High-probability setups are "one-sided"—they show obvious, undeniable directional bias. The bodies line up, the wicks reject, the levels cascade in your favor with minimal retracement noise. Low-probability setups feel 50/50; you have to frame logic for both sides. When you see one-sidedness, that's where you should engage. When you see both sides feeling equally likely, sit still.
- **How to identify:** Candlestick patterns: do bodies all close in the upper half? Do wicks all reject the same level? Does price run with minimal pullback? Or do bodies jump around, wicks are long, pullbacks are deep? One-sided looks clean; 50/50 looks messy.
- **How to use:** Only take setups when one-sidedness is clear. If you can't definitively pick a direction in the first few candles, walk away for that move. This is not FOMO; it's discipline. The next one-sided move is 5 minutes away. Wait.
- **Example in this video:** Early Monday: opening was low-probability (event-driven, geopolitical uncertainty, reluctance visible). ICT didn't trade. Then mid-morning: bodies started lining up above CE, wicks rejecting lower halves, no deep pullbacks, cascading higher. That's when the "bread and butter" setup appeared—clean, one-sided, obvious.

- **Nuance / caveats:** "One-sided" doesn't mean straight line; it means the trend is so obvious that wicks/consolidations are minor noise.

### Anticipatory Price Reading (Sniper vs. Reactive)  (aliases: prediction, foresight, not reacting)
- **Kind:** skill + mindset
- **What ICT says:** Most traders react: they wait for price to move, then jump in. ICT teaches anticipation: you set your "crosshairs" ahead of where price is going. Like a sniper accounting for wind, distance, target speed—you're shooting ahead, not at the bird. This requires knowing what "should" happen, then watching to see if price does it. When it doesn't (canary dies), you know to expect the opposite.
- **How to identify:** Recognizing patterns before they complete. Expecting a wick rejection before the wick appears. Knowing "if it takes out that low, I'm expecting a run to that target."
- **How to use:** Build a model (FVG sequence, body positions, gap rules). Before price moves, say "if bodies stay above this CE, I expect acceleration to that minor buy side." Watch. If price does what you predicted, confidence rises. If it doesn't (canary warning), expect reversal. This is tape reading in the moment.
- **Example in this video:** ICT saying, "If we get above this level, this won't act as an inversion; it'll act as a bullish FVG" before the body even closed. Then watching price confirm exactly that. Or: "This reluctance tells me they're not done; they're going higher" before the reversal happened. Pre-announcing what should happen, then proving it (or proving it won't).
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:13](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2413) sniper analogy: anticipate, don't react. [0:44:58](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2698) warning signs (canary) when price doesn't do what should happen. [0:52:35](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=3155) anticipate before they form; don't wait for chart to show it after the fact.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Anticipation requires you to get wrong sometimes. The skill is improving prediction rate over time (70%+), not achieving 100%.

### Reticular Activating System (RAS) / Pattern Recognition  (aliases: seeing repeating signatures, confidence building)
- **Kind:** psychology
- **What ICT says:** When you buy a car, you suddenly see that car everywhere—not because it's more common, but because you're now tuned to notice it (RAS activated). Same with price action: once you activate your RAS for a FVG sequence or body rejection pattern, you start seeing it everywhere, and your confidence grows. You begin to **anticipate** it before it forms.
- **How to identify:** Exposing yourself to price action repeatedly. After 10 times seeing a body close above a CE and then rally, your brain automatically expects the 11th time.
- **How to use:** This is why tape reading (not trading) is valuable: repeated exposure builds RAS. Once RAS is activated, you recognize setups in real time, and entries become obvious (not guessing).
- **Example in this video:** ICT's students who've watched streams develop the ability to spot FVG sequences and body rejections immediately, without thinking. His own prediction of "reluctance here tells me they want higher" happens because his RAS has seen it 1,000 times.

- **Nuance / caveats:** This is not mystical; it's neurobiology. Repeated exposure + conscious study = pattern recognition.

### Bread and Butter Setup  (aliases: simple entry, foundational setup)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** A basic, repeatable entry model that doesn't require all the bells and whistles. Simple: inversion FVG (bodies above midpoint), next new FVG forms, bodies reject the lower half of that FVG, take entry at the break of the low (or at the body close above midpoint), target the next minor buy/sell side or prior short-term high. Stop is tight (below entry candle low or at CE midpoint).
- **How to identify:** After an inversion FVG reclaims as bullish, drop to lower timeframe. Wait for the next gap to form. Watch body positions.
- **How to use:** Entry point: when body closes above the gap's midpoint on lower timeframe, you're in. Tight stop (2–5 handles on NQ). Target: nearest minor buy side or prior short-term high.
- **Example in this video:** At [0:35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126), ICT marks: inversion FVG (bodies above its midpoint) = draw to minor buy side (prior high). Entry at the next FVG's midpoint break. Stop below the prior low. Target: event horizon (split between two prior highs) or next minor buy side.

- **Nuance / caveats:** Bread and butter doesn't mean "always works"; it means the logic is clear and repeatable. You'll miss some, and some will stop out, but the win rate is high.

### Event Horizon  (aliases: midpoint split, equilibrium target)
- **Kind:** target method
- **What ICT says:** When you have two key levels (e.g., high of inversion FVG and high of new week opening gap), split the difference (midpoint). This is often a reversal zone or partial-profit target. It comes from the idea that "octave" or "gradient" levels have algorithmic significance.
- **How to identify:** Mark two highs or two lows. Measure the midpoint between them. Apply Fib if needed.
- **How to use:** As a partial profit target or a place to watch for reversal/congestion.
- **Example in this video:** High of new week opening gap + inversion FVG high, split in half = event horizon. Price stops there or reverses. Use as a target on bread and butter entries.
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126) split difference = event horizon.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Not guaranteed; just probabilistic.

### Volume Imbalance / Empty Bar  (aliases: gap, imbalance)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick (usually on opening) that has no overlap with the prior candlestick's range. This creates a gap. The term "volume imbalance" refers to the fact that no trades occurred at those price levels. It's ripe for a retracement or a run-away in the direction of the gap.
- **How to identify:** Look for candlesticks that don't touch the prior bar's range (gap up or gap down).
- **How to use:** As a reference for measuring FVGs and as a potential retracement target or breakout zone.
- **Example in this video:** Opening candles at 9:30 had volume imbalance from the prior close (no trades at those levels due to overnight gap). Tracked throughout the morning as a retracement target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:53](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=533) volume imbalance in opening candles.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Volume imbalance alone isn't an entry; it's just a gap to reference.

### Rejection Block  (aliases: rejection zone)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A level where price shows rejection or slowdown; it's a prior high that is being tested but not overcome. It can also be a level identified by FVG structure or consequent encroachment where bodies are rejected.
- **How to identify:** Price runs to a prior high, then reverses or consolidates (bodies fail to go above it).
- **How to use:** As a reversal zone or as a place to take profits. If price takes out a rejection block, that shows strength (no longer rejected).
- **Example in this video:** Various prior highs marked as "rejection blocks"; when price failed to take them out, ICT watched for reversal into minor buy sides.

### Tape Reading Journal / Notepad Method  (aliases: study journal, anticipation logging)
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:** Use a physical notepad (not on the chart) to log your **real-time anticipations** as price unfolds. Write timestamps, abbreviated terms (FVG, CE, minor buy side, etc.), and your prediction of what should happen next. You're not annotating the chart in real-time; you're jotting notes while watching. After the price run completes, go back to the chart and annotate it with your notes. Then screenshot it. Finally, add to your **study journal** (separate doc) your emotional/psychological state: Did you feel confident? Uncertain? Did you expect it to work?
- **How to identify:** This is a personal practice; no chart tells you how to do it. It's discipline.
- **How to use:** The notepad creates a record of your predictions vs. reality. Over time, you see which predictions you get right and which are wrong. Emotional notes help you understand your psychology during success vs. failure. When you're in drawdown, you go back to your study journal and read how you felt during winning periods to rebuild confidence.
- **Example in this video:** ICT says, "Write down 10:28 on your notepad, anticipated run to buy side, happened." Then after, annotate the chart with that entry point and screenshot. ### Canary in the Coal Mine (Warning Sign)  (aliases: dying canary, price reluctance)
- **Kind:** rule / psychology
- **What ICT says:** Coal miners used to bring canaries down into mines; if the canary died, oxygen was too low and miners had to evacuate. Same principle in trading: when price does something it "shouldn't" (shows reluctance, doesn't take out a level it should, wicks don't touch a CE it should), that's your canary dying. It's telling you the environment has changed; stop what you're doing and get out of that line of thinking. Expect the opposite direction.
- **How to identify:** Recognize what "should" happen in your model. When price doesn't do it, that's the canary dying.
- **How to use:** When the canary dies (price shows reluctance), pivot your expectation. Don't defend your original thesis; pivot to the opposite side and wait for confirmation of the new thesis.
- **Example in this video:** Early Monday, price showed reluctance to go lower (didn't take out sell side when it "should" have). That was the canary. ICT recognized it and pivoted to expect a run higher (which happened). He didn't get frustrated; he just flipped the switch.

- **Nuance / caveats:** This requires you to know what "should" happen. If you don't have a model, you can't recognize when it's violated.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Opening probability:** Event-driven openings (geopolitical, war, peace news) = low probability early on; wait for "one-sidedness" to show before engaging.
- **Body rule on gaps:** If a gap is filled (body closes inside prior range) on the next candle, that gap doesn't become a robust FVG reference.
- **Body-in-upper-half rule:** Bodies closing above the 50% midpoint of a gap = bullish; bodies staying out of lower half on subsequent candles = continued bullish. One body touching lower half = warning.
- **Wick rejection:** Wicks that don't touch a CE midpoint = strength; long wicks that touch are permissible if bodies stay above.
- **Entry only at next FVG:** Don't enter at the inversion FVG retracement; wait for the next new FVG and enter there (confirmed).
- **Bread and butter components:** Inversion FVG (bodies above), next new FVG entry, tight stop (below entry low), target minor buy side or prior short-term high.
- **Stop hunt pattern:** Wick below support, body closes far above = expect reversal, not continuation lower.
- **Wednesday–Friday rule:** Real entries begin Wednesday; Monday/Tuesday are observation days (for most weeks).
- **One-sidedness threshold:** If you can't obviously pick a direction in first 3–5 candles, walk away.
- **Reluctance warning:** If price "should" take out a level but doesn't, expect reversal in opposite direction.
- **High-probability strike rate target:** 70% or higher for models worth trading.
- **Event Horizon:** Midpoint between two key levels = potential partial target or reversal zone.

## Glossary candidates

`Anticipatory Price Reading` — Predicting what price should do next (like a sniper accounting for wind), then watching to see if it does. Not reacting after the fact.

`Bread and Butter Setup` — A foundational entry: inversion FVG + bodies above midpoint, next new FVG, bodies reject lower half, entry at the break, tight stop, target minor buy side.

`Canary in the Coal Mine` — A warning sign when price violates expected behavior (shows reluctance when it shouldn't, fails to take out a level it should). Signals reversal or thesis change.

`Event Horizon` — The midpoint between two key price levels (e.g., inversion FVG high and opening gap high); often a reversal zone or partial profit target.

`One-Sidedness` — High-probability setup showing obvious, undeniable directional bias (bodies aligned, wicks rejecting cleanly, minimal retracement noise). 50/50 ambiguous setups should be avoided.

`Reclaimed Fair Value Gap` — An inversion FVG that "reclaims" its original nature based on where bodies close: if above midpoint, it becomes bullish (reclaimed bullish FVG); if below, it becomes bearish (reclaimed bearish FVG).

`Reluctance` — When price fails to do what it "should" (doesn't take out a stop hunt low, doesn't fill a gap, doesn't break through a CE). A warning that direction is about to reverse.

`Stop Hunt` — A rapid dip below key support (taking out retail stops) followed by a powerful reversal (and usually continued move in the opposite direction). Algorithmic efficiency: once stops are cleared, the dormant model fires.

`Tape Reading Journal` — A real-time notepad log of anticipations (timestamps, setup names, predictions) during price action, without trading risk. After the run, annotate the chart and journal your emotional state. Builds RAS and self-awareness.

`Volume Imbalance` — A candlestick with no overlap with the prior candlestick's range (a gap); indicates no trades occurred at those price levels. Used as a reference for FVG structure.

`Wick Rejection at Consequent Encroachment` — When a wick approaches or touches the CE midpoint but the next body closes above it (rejecting the lower level). Shows strength; repeated across timeframes = very strong.
