## Concepts

### First Utilization of a PD Array
- **Kind:** definition · price delivery concept
- **What ICT says:** When a price delivery array (FVG, suspension block, order block, etc.) first forms, it has a "first utilization"—an initial purpose or bias. For example, a suspension block created during a bullish move has an initial bullish utilization; if price comes down into it and the bodies cannot touch the upper quadrant, it shows the block's bullish purpose is being rejected and may now act bearish (inversion). Once price closes and clearly goes through/below it, the first utilization is confirmed, and the array gets recalibrated. "First utilization is when a PD array forms... That validates this as a bearish breaker." (0:21:33)
- **How to identify:** Identify when an array forms and its initial direction bias. Then watch for the first major move through/away from it. If bodies and closes confirm that move, first utilization is validated.
- **How to use:** Use first utilization to set initial bias. But if the array is invalidated (price goes the opposite direction), recalibrate it—it now has a new utilization. Example: a bullish suspension block that gets pierced below becomes a bearish inversion.
- **Example in this video:** The daily suspension block formed bullishly on Wednesday. By Friday, it's being traded into and below, invalidating its bullish first utilization and confirming a bearish recalibration. (0:04:01–0:08:13, 0:21:33)

### Quadrant & Octant Levels (Detailed)
- **Kind:** definition · price level · order flow tool
- **What ICT says:** When dividing an inefficiency or range into four equal quarters, the dividing lines are quadrants. More specifically, the upper and lower quartile lines are "quadrants"; the 25% and 75% lines are "octants." ICT uses these extensively on daily charts as reference points for entries and exits. For example, "the lower quadrant of the daily suspension block" is the 25% line above the block's low. Price will often stall or reverse at these lines because they divide the inefficiency zone. "That octant this quadrant. Okay? That's what's being carried over into the daily chart." (0:14:28–0:15:18)
- **How to identify:** Mark the high and low of an inefficiency. Divide into quarters. The 25% line above the low = lower quadrant; the 75% line above the low = upper quadrant.
- **How to use:** Use quadrant lines as refinement entry points. For a short, enter at or just below the lower quadrant if it aligns with daily bias. Use upper quadrant as a resistance level; if price wicks into it but bodies can't touch it, that's bearish (rejection).
- **Example in this video:** Daily suspension block marked with quadrant lines. The 9:30 opening candle reaches exactly the lower quadrant of the block and is shorted there. Multiple intraday moves refine around these quadrant/octant levels. (0:04:01–0:15:18, 0:24:13–0:25:31)

### Hangman PD Array (New Trading Pattern)
- **Kind:** definition · price pattern · PD array
- **What ICT says:** A specific candlestick pattern where the body closes above (or near) a key level (like consequent encroachment), but the wick extends below that level and then rejects back up, leaving a small body and longer wick. Named after the hangman's noose—traders going long get trapped as the wick pulls their stops, then the market reverses and leaves them "hanging." The pattern signals likely downward continuation. "That's the hangman. Anyone that was going long here, they get stung right there. So, they're getting dropped and the noose is around their neck, and then you're gone." (0:27:09–0:28:45)
- **How to identify:** Look for a candle where: (1) Body closes above a key level (e.g., consequent encroachment), (2) Wick extends well below that level, (3) The wick is notably longer than the body, (4) The next candle confirms by breaking lower again.
- **How to use:** When you see a hangman pattern, expect continuation downward after the wick rejects. Do not go long into the body close; instead, prepare for shorts below the wick low. Use it as a confirmation pattern.
- **Example in this video:** Marked around 0:27:09–0:28:45. Body closes near consequent encroachment level, wick extends below, then rejection and downside follow. This pattern appears at the convergence of the 1-hour FVG boundary.

### Lunch Macro (11:30 AM Pullback)
- **Kind:** rule · macro timing · intraday pattern
- **What ICT says:** Around 11:30 AM ET, price typically experiences a pullback (retracement) into the 10:00 AM (first hour close) low. This is a consistent intraday pattern due to algorithmic profit-taking and rebalancing. "When we get closer to the 11:30 time... this is where the lunch macro tends to have a strong effect. And you'll see the pullback into a higher low that formed at 10:00." (0:17:39)
- **How to identify:** Mark the 10:00 AM low on the 1-minute or 5-minute chart. Around 11:30 AM, watch for a retracement/pull back into that level or near it.
- **How to use:** Use lunch macro as a profit-taking point or as a retracement target. If you're short, you can take partials into the 10:00 low at lunch. If you're planning to re-enter longs after a short trade, the lunch macro low is a target.

### Time Distortion
- **Kind:** definition · candlestick characteristic
- **What ICT says:** A period where multiple candles form within a tight range, not covering much price distance. Called "time distortion" because time passes but price doesn't move much. Typically occurs near support/resistance levels or before directional breaks. "Spent a little bit of time in here. This is time distortion." (0:20:45, 0:30:18)
- **How to identify:** Look for a series of small-range candles that don't advance the price much despite forming multiple candles.
- **How to use:** When you see time distortion, expect a breakout soon. Don't expect more movement until the breakout occurs. Use the distortion period to tighten stops or take partials if you're already in a trade.
- **Example in this video:** Marked around 0:20:45 and again around 0:30:18.

### Invalidation & Recalibration of Inefficiencies
- **Kind:** rule · price behavior
- **What ICT says:** When a PD array (FVG, suspension block, etc.) is initially bullish but price closes and trades below it, the array is "invalidated" as bullish and must be recalibrated—it now acts as a bearish inversion. Recalibration requires a clear close below the array. "How do you get back in sync with it? Well, you got to give it a chance to take out buy side here. And then once we close below it again, that resets first utilization... That resets first utilization." (0:21:33–0:22:20)
- **How to use:** When an array you thought was bullish gets invalidated, flip your bias. Don't try to fight it; accept the recalibration and trade the new bias. Recalibration gives you a second chance at the trade in the opposite direction.
- **Example in this video:** Daily suspension block is initially bullish (created Wednesday). By Friday, it's being broken below, which recalibrates it from bullish to bearish. Once confirmed, it becomes a bearish inversion FVG. (0:21:33–0:22:20)

### Open and Close as Most Important Prices
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Of all the candlestick prices (open, high, low, close), the open and close are the most important because they define the body and the narrative of order flow. "The open and close is the most important two prices you're ever going to have." (0:31:52) Wicks are noise; bodies show volume and real order flow.
- **How to use:** When analyzing candlesticks, focus on where the body opens and closes relative to key levels. Ignore wicks; they're manipulation. The body close tells you the true direction intent.
- **Example in this video:** Throughout the trade, ICT emphasizes where bodies close relative to consequent encroachment levels, not where wicks go.

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- Suspension blocks define daily and weekly ranges; use quadrant and octant divisions to refine entries.
- First utilization of a PD array must be confirmed by a close beyond the array; a wick alone doesn't confirm.
- Once first utilization is confirmed, don't trade against it; recalibrate and use it as the new bias.
- Quadrant and octant levels are precise entry zones on the 1-minute and 5-minute charts for pyramiding.
- Lunch macro (11:30 AM) pulls back into the 10:00 AM low; use this to take partials or re-enter.
- Bodies close to consequent encroachment levels signal continuation; bodies unable to touch consequent encroachment signal rejection/reversal.
- Hangman pattern (body above level, wick below, rejection) signals downward continuation.
- Use a pre-trade notepad with quadrant/octant levels marked for reference during live trading; don't over-complicate it.
- If you miss the initial entry (at the quadrant level), wait for a retracement and re-entry at a lower level; don't chase the move.
- Turtle soup patterns (false breakouts of old highs) can trap long traders; recognize them and use them as shorts.
- Chat sentiment (on live streams) is often contrarian to price movement; when chat is animated and bullish, price is likely bearish (and vice versa).

## Process, Risk & Psychology

**Pre-Trade Preparation:**
- Mark all key levels (daily suspension block, quadrants, octants, relative equal highs/lows) on a notepad before the session starts. This allows for quick reference without slowing down execution.
- Don't show your notepad or all your levels publicly; keep some analysis private. It helps maintain your own trading edge and prevents students from trying to copy your exact levels instead of developing their own logic.

**Execution Philosophy:**
- Catch entries at quadrant/octant levels when they align with daily bias.
- Pyramid into lower levels as price confirms the direction. Don't try to get all your position in at once.
- Take partials at obvious intermediate targets (relative equal lows, FVG boundaries).
- If you miss the initial entry, don't FOMO in at a worse price; wait for a retracement and re-entry.
- Move in silence: execute without constant live-stream chatter. Let your actions speak. ICT references doing silent execution videos where he just performs trades without explanation.

**Psychology & Chat Management:**
- Ignore live-stream chat sentiment. Chat is usually wrong and acts as a contrarian indicator. When chat is animated/bullish, price is likely to reverse down.
- Do not let student/follower suggestions change your bias. Manage your own trade, not theirs. (ICT refused to tell a student to reverse positions, even when the student was offside, because managing other traders' positions builds codependency.)
- Expect some missed entries due to life/schedule. It's not a loss; it's just opportunity missed. Don't chase worse entries.

**Market Precision:**
- The market is algorithmic and hits quadrant/octant levels with precision. When you see price hit these levels exactly as you planned, it should reinforce that the market is not random but pre-programmed (for smart money). Use this as motivation to study and apply the framework.

## Glossary Candidates

`First Utilization` — The initial purpose or directional bias of a PD array when it forms (bullish or bearish). Must be confirmed by a close beyond the array; once confirmed, it can be recalibrated if invalidated.

`Quadrant / Octant (detailed)` — Quarter-point divisions within a range or inefficiency. Quadrants divide the range into four equal parts (25%, 50%, 75%); octants are the 25% and 75% lines specifically. Used as precise entry and reversal points.

`Hangman` — A candlestick pattern where the body closes above a key level (like consequent encroachment) but the wick extends well below and is rejected. Signals upcoming downside continuation. Named after the metaphor of traders being trapped and "hung."

`Lunch Macro` — A consistent 11:30 AM ET pullback/retracement that targets the 10:00 AM low. Used for profit-taking or retracement entries.

`Time Distortion` — A period of multiple small-range candles that don't advance price much. Typically precedes a directional breakout.

`Recalibration` — When an array's initial utilization is invalidated (price closes the opposite way), the array is recalibrated to serve the new bias. Requires confirmation via close before the new utilization is valid.

`Turtle Soup` — A false breakout pattern where price breaks above a resistance/old high, trapping long traders, then reverses and "soups" them.

`Mohawk` — A pattern with a sharp spike up followed by a flat/consolidated body; appears visually like a mohawk hairdo (not deeply explained in this video).

`Chat Sentiment Contrarian Indicator` — Live-stream chat sentiment often opposes actual price movement; use chat enthusiasm as a signal that you're on the right side of the market if you disagree with chat.
