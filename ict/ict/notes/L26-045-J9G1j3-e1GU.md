## Summary
ICT reviews a long he took on the morning of the video (futures, price around 27,665 on the daily; instrument implied NQ but not named). The daily chart gives a bullish "suspension block" (a daily candle with volume imbalances at both ends) whose consequent encroachment price trades below. At the 9:30 RTH open price makes a tiny move up holding relative equal highs, then drops into a deep discount below that daily array: he reads this as a Market Maker Buy Model, buys around the midpoint of a wick left in the engineered sell-side liquidity, and targets yesterday's 9:30 RTH opening price, which turned out to be the high of the regular trading hours session. The point: you can buy the session low and know in advance where the session high will be, if the daily PD array, the time of day, and the intraday model agree. A personal-emergency interruption cut his management short (not relevant to the lesson).

## Concepts

### Suspension block (daily PD array with volume imbalance at both ends)  (aliases ICT uses: bullish discount array, "lower BISI / sell-side inefficiency" (caption garbled), bullish order block)
- **Kind:** new coinage (caption partly unclear) / example only
- **What ICT says:**
  - A down-close daily candle that has a gap to its left was already used earlier in the week to set up a bullish order block; price opening below / changing state of delivery on the daily chart means it will not simply reach the zone and stop - it digs underneath it a little first.
  - He calls the structure a "suspension block": a volume imbalance at the top end and a volume imbalance at the low end, with its midpoint being the consequent encroachment (CE) of the inefficiency.
  - It is a bullish discount array in a deep discount relative to where price had been higher up.
  - When price trades below the CE, expect "a bounce ... some kind of a pop ... some kind of a reaction" because it is a daily-chart array.
  - Even if price keeps rolling lower, it is "still worthwhile to hunt something" in that vicinity, especially if time of day agrees.
- **How to identify:** daily down-close candle with gap to its left (used as bullish OB), imbalances at both the top and low end of the candle body/range area; shade the full range and mark the midpoint (CE).
- **How to use:** treat the price zone at/below the CE as an area to hunt long entries on the lower timeframe (1m), not to buy blindly on the daily; confluence with time of day (the 9:30 open) is what he wants.
- **Example in this video:** daily NQ(?) chart; the key level mentioned is about 27,665.5 "or less". Price opens the 9:30 session and trades down into the shaded gray range, below the CE, and further below the low of the suspension block.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:00](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=0) — daily chart, OB with gap to the left; [0:00:46](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=46) — suspension block defined, CE reaction expected; [0:01:37](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=97) — 27,665.5 or lower is worth hunting
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Suspension block" is not a term in the canonical list; treat as a new coinage. Captions render the gap type garbled ("lower by side balance sell side inefficiency" = BISI, a bullish gap, I believe). He had flagged before the week started that price would go up (to a high) and then come down to this area.

### Octants / quadrants of the daily array for depth of discount  (aliases ICT uses: octant, lower quadrant, deep discount)
- **Kind:** refinement / example only
- **What ICT says:** He measures the daily suspension block in finer slices than halves: the CE is the 50%, the lower quadrant and a lower octant sit beneath it. Price then trades to "a lower octant than the low of the daily suspension block", i.e. below the whole array. He likes this ("one, two, three"): market probes in steps, "getting people to chase it, go lower."
- **How to use:** the deeper below the CE / the array low that price trades, the deeper the discount for longs; he is hunting the lowest zone, not the first touch.
- **Example in this video:** 1m chart: price touches the CE of the daily array, continues into the lower quadrant, then to a lower octant beneath the array's low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:26](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=146) — octant and lower quadrant at the daily CE; [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=195) — "one, two, three" probing
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Octant levels are only named, no percentages given.

### Market Maker Buy Model (intraday, from the 9:30 open)  (aliases ICT uses: market maker buy model, smart money reversal, first-stage accumulation, second-stage reaccumulation)
- **Kind:** entry model (example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Right from the gate at 9:30, price makes a tiny move up leaving relative equal highs, then drops. When it drops like that, "what do you think it's forming?" - a Market Maker Buy Model.
  - The drop goes into the daily PD array (CE and below) and leaves a minor buy-side liquidity pool above plus the engineered sell-side liquidity below.
  - Two things then happen: (1) price accumulates all the engineered SSL; (2) a wick is laid down.
  - After the low, he describes the delivery as "smart money reversal, low risk buy, first stage accumulation, second stage reaccumulation" running all the way to the target.
- **How to identify:** 9:30 open -> small up-move with relative equal highs -> sell-off into a daily bullish PD array (below CE, even below the array) leaving SSL -> wick -> reversal.
- **How to use:** the buy is taken at the low around the wick (see next concept); target is a pre-planned RTH-session objective (prior-day 9:30 open here).
- **Example in this video:** 1m, 9:30 open of the video's day: tiny rally holding equal highs, sell-off into the lower octant of the daily suspension block, wick, then rally in stages to yesterday's 9:30 open.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:26](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=146) — "It's a market maker buy model"; [0:04:02](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=242) — smart money reversal, accumulation / reaccumulation to the terminus
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says seeing price print retail "continuation patterns like a bear flag" on the way down is extra odds for the reversal (see below). He also calls the MMBM something he teaches "as price action" - no time-window or rule given for how long stage one lasts.

### Entry at the midpoint of the wick (and aggressive entry below it)  (aliases ICT uses: "perfect entry", aggressive entry)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the low is formed and a wick is laid down, he wants to get in "around that wick."
  - He tried to time the entry as price dipped below the midpoint of that wick: "anything below that or at that level is perfect entry."
  - He also placed an extra aggressive entry below the wick to make sure he had a position in addition to the main one; total position was three contracts, built up there.
- **How to identify:** the wick left by the sell-off into the engineered SSL; draw its midline.
- **How to use:** buy at or below the wick's midpoint; add an aggressive entry lower; only three contracts were on (he intended to build more but was interrupted).
- **Example in this video:** 1m: the entry fill is shown below the wick; ICT notes "it's probably random" that the print landed exactly there, but that was the plan.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=195) — wants in at the wick; [0:05:35](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=335) — midline of the wick = perfect entry; [0:07:16](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=436) — executions drawn below the wick, 3 contracts

### Retail continuation patterns (bear flag) as a trap that adds odds  (aliases ICT uses: "bubble gum I chew up")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** when price prints what retail sees as a bear flag (a bearish continuation pattern) in the area where he wants to buy, that "goes further in the odds of probability" for the reversal because it draws in sellers (stops/entries) that smart money feeds on.
- **How to use:** read an obvious retail bearish pattern into an array/liquidity area as confirmation of engineered sell-side liquidity rather than a reason to short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=195) — bear-flag comment
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** One-sentence aside only; no quantification.

### Inversion FVG with "Mohawk" wick; bodies must not close outside the gap  (aliases ICT uses: inversion fair value gap, mohawk)
- **Kind:** refinement / new coinage ("mohawk", caption could be unclear)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the market rallied above a bearish gap and came back down, a wick went through the gap, and this inverted the gap (iFVG used as support).
  - He pre-annotated the chart ("I want to show you without having to talk") with what would happen at that iFVG.
  - A small wick in the iFVG he calls a "mohawk": "look where the bodies are - the bodies can't even lay down outside of this inversion fair value gap. That's bullish."
  - Because bodies hold inside / above the iFVG, he stays with the idea that price goes higher.
- **How to identify:** a bearish FVG that has been traded through to the upside and is then revisited from above; candle bodies stay within or above the gap while only wicks poke the boundary.
- **How to use:** confirmation that the long remains valid and to hold toward the next objective.
- **Example in this video:** 1m: price rallies from the low, trades up through a bearish FVG, comes back down, wicks through it ("mohawk"), bodies stay above; then the next leg higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:04](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=484) — iFVG, mohawk, bodies stay inside
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Same body-vs-wick logic as the SIBI/iFVG behaviour taught in other 2026 lectures (e.g. bodies must keep closing the right way).

### Target = prior day's 9:30 RTH opening price; terminus of the RTH session  (aliases ICT uses: terminus for the trade, 9:30 opening price for regular trading hours)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - His objective for the regular-trading-hours session was yesterday's 9:30 RTH opening price; he calls it his "terminus."
  - He moved the target on the chart from the prior-day 9:30 price to the next partial; the partials: first partial just under a minor buy-side level (smooth edges in the move), second partial next, final contract at the 9:30 price.
  - The 9:30 price then got hit, and that level ended up being the high of the regular trading hours session.
  - Underlying objectives in the same area: old areas of a prior sell-off over a premium array, bearish fair value gaps (used like bearish order blocks) in the original consolidation; price draws toward the original consolidation and to the level just above it.
- **How to use:** pre-define the session objective from the previous day's RTH open (and nearby bearish FVG/old consolidation) and scale out toward it; partial at each liquidity pool or minor BSL.
- **Example in this video:** NQ(?) 1m: long from the lower-octant wick; first partial at a minor BSL "right underneath my cursor"; second at a minor buy-side above; remaining contracts targeted at yesterday's 9:30 price - he left before the final part and screenshotted that the level got hit and was the RTH high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:16](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=436) — where he was aiming; [0:08:53](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=533) — partials; [0:09:41](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=581) — 9:30 price hit = RTH high
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He frames this as "man buying the low and knowing exactly where the high's going to be," and says he does this on weekly ranges, daily charts, session highs and lows too. Reference to "something a little bit different over here" is left unexplained.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Key level: about 27,665.5 or lower on the daily suspension block area is where to hunt for a pop (caption number may be imperfect).
- A daily-chart PD array gives a reaction even if price continues lower; hunt entries near/below the CE rather than blindly buying the first touch.
- 9:30 open then a tiny up-move with relative equal highs then a drop = read it as a Market Maker Buy Model forming.
- Enter at or below the midpoint of the wick left in the SSL; aggressive extra entry below the wick.
- Target: yesterday's 9:30 RTH open; partials at minor buy-side pools.
- iFVG is respected when bodies do not close outside of it.
- Position size used: 3 contracts (he intended to build more).

## Glossary candidates
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — ICT's name here for a daily PD array with a volume imbalance at both its top and low end; the CE is its midpoint.
`OCTANT` — an eighth-sized slice of a range/array; the lower octant is the deepest discount slice.
`MOHAWK` — his name for a small spiking wick into an inversion FVG while bodies stay out of/inside the gap (caption unclear).
`TERMINUS` — the final target of a trade/session (here yesterday's 9:30 RTH open).
`SMART MONEY REVERSAL` — the turn off the engineered SSL low in the MMBM, a low-risk buy.
