## Summary
ICT answers the title question with a procedure: when you do not know which side to focus on, wait. Submit to the first half hour of the opening range, look for the first FVG, let any 9:45 or 10:00 news pass, and if you still don't trust direction, wait for the first hour of trading to finish. Then mark the highest high and lowest low from 9:30 to 10:30, lay gradient levels over it, and focus on the half (upper if bullish, lower if bearish) in line with your daily draw, here the consequent encroachment of a daily suspension block. He reviews the daily and hourly context (volume imbalances, suspension block, relative equal highs, new week opening gap), then shows demo executions on the March contract using the Market Maker Buy Model, an inversion FVG and a bearish order block.

## Concepts

### Engaging the market with no initial bias: wait for the first hour range  (aliases: first hour's range, 9:30-10:30 range, submitting to the opening range)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:** If you don't know which side to focus on and are waiting for more information, submit to the first half hour of the opening range, look for the first FVG, and let the market consume 9:45 or 10:00 news. If you are reluctant to do anything until the first hour trades (also when you "don't trust the direction"), it is usually because the market rips off in one direction. After the first hour (9:30 to 10:30), mark the highest high and lowest low of that range and grade it with a fib: "all of your key PD arrays are going to form in close proximity to one of these gradient levels". The 50% level of that range is the pivot: if bullish, the upper half is sensitive, so focus on it and ignore the lower half; if the market stops you out and goes back below equilibrium into the lower half, look for the lower quadrant or a complete roll-over to the low of the first hour. Also usable when markets run one-sided, when he waits for 9:45/10:00 news, or when he has a hard-line bias.
- **How to identify:** First-hour range = highest high to lowest low between 9:30 and 10:30 ET; 50 percent = equilibrium; gradient levels at the fib quarters/eighths.
- **How to use:** Once the range is set, trade PD arrays that sit on the gradient levels in the half that agrees with your higher-timeframe draw. Here he was bullish because price looked headed for the consequent encroachment of the daily suspension block, so he used the upper half of the first-hour range.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m March contract: he highlighted the first-hour high and low, then zoomed into the upper half. Price almost reached the first-hour high, then broke a low and went to the next gradient level; just above that level was a small gap, a swing low sat on the level, and further left an inefficiency, a valid candidate for a high-probability discount BISI. Price traded to its CE with bodies staying in the upper half, rallied, dropped back to touch the gradient level, rallied and eventually took out the buy-side above the relative equal highs.

### Market Maker Buy Model read on a 1-minute chart
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** After the walkthrough he removes the box and asks you to read the same action as a market maker buy model: original consolidation, first stage of the sell programme ("distribution", as phrased), second stage down into a discount BISI, then a rally and an inversion FVG; he extends the iFVG forward. Within the upper half of the high-to-low range, it stays at equilibrium: accumulation, second-stage reaccumulation, then it rips higher attacking the buy-side; the bodies converge around the same level (ignore the wick that was marked as highest high at the time).
- **Example in this video:** 1m March contract. A bullish model: price falls into the discount BISI, the bodies of the following candles bunch together, then price takes the buy-side above the relative equal highs. His long used the original stop below the low and he sat through some heat before price ripped above the bodies and he exited.

### Inversion FVG + CISD + bearish order block (demo executions)
- **Kind:** entry model (applied example)
- **What ICT says:** Price traded up toward an inversion FVG ("I was watching to see if it was going to use that"); it went below it, producing a change in state of delivery marked by three consecutive up-close candles, which he treats as the bearish order block, and the opening price of that sequence as the entry reference. Price traded up until it hit that level quickly and pulled away; he entered as it pulled away (some slippage), then added three more times, including when price traded back up into it, and exited at the CE of the BISI below, exactly to the tick, with a limit order. If instead price had traded above the order block he would have stopped out, then looked long using the market maker buy model.

### Volume imbalance toggle and daily suspension block with CE
- **Kind:** rule / application
- **What ICT says:** On the daily, he toggles the Settings option until the most exaggerated volume imbalances appear and uses those; "the easiest thing in terms of rules" (though not always). Long-term on the daily: buy-side up here, sell-side below. The daily suspension block is what price drove into; the reasonable draw is up to its halfway (CE). If price gets above the half and trades outside it, he anticipates the block acting as an inversion FVG and a challenge to the relative equal highs. As long as price stays below the CE of the block, it hammers around inside the range between the high and the low, which he finds disenchanting because he needs it to leave the range to take "significant live fund trades".

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Unknown bias: wait for the first half hour of the opening range, the first FVG and the 9:45/10:00 news.
- Still unsure: wait for the first hour (9:30-10:30 ET), mark its high and low, fib it; trade the half that agrees with your daily draw.
- Upper half sensitive when bullish; if price breaks into the lower half (below equilibrium), look for the lower quadrant and a roll-over to the first-hour low.
- Key PD arrays form near the gradient levels of the first-hour range.
- Daily: use the toggle that shows the most exaggerated volume imbalances.
- Above the CE of the daily suspension block -> it may act as an inversion FVG and challenge the relative equal highs; below it -> range.
- Stops in this volatility are wider than people want; tight stops are hard to pull off.

## Glossary candidates
`FIRST HOUR RANGE` — highest high and lowest low from 9:30 to 10:30 ET, graded with gradient levels; the half agreeing with your draw is focus.
`SUSPENSION BLOCK` — daily structure of two volume imbalances; CE used as a draw (see L26-131).
`BISI` — buyside imbalance, sellside inefficiency (bullish FVG).
`INVERSION FVG` — a gap that, once traded through, acts in the opposite direction.
