## Concepts

### Silver Bullet (10:00 Macro / First Hour Close)
- **Kind:** rule · time-based entry
- **What ICT says:** The 10:00 AM ET close (first hour of regular trading hours is 9:30–10:00 ET). This close is a key reference for early-session direction and is often a turning point or acceleration point. "Give it till 10:00 and if nothing's obvious then simply submit yourself to the 10:00 hour silver bullet which is the first of everybody." (0:01:44)
- **How to identify:** Mark the 10:00 ET candle close on the 1-minute chart. Observe price action and momentum at that time.
- **How to use:** Use the 10:00 close as a bias confirmation. If price closes above opening range high, it's likely bullish; if below opening range low, likely bearish. Use it as a reference point for entries after the first hour of trading. On slower news days, the 10:00 print becomes even more important as a direction signal.

### Seasonal Tendencies (July to October Energy Rally)
- **Kind:** rule · macro context · cyclical pattern
- **What ICT says:** Every year, starting the first week of July and running through October, crude oil and energy commodity prices tend to rally. This is because large industrial companies (heating oil suppliers, natural gas distributors) must stock up on supplies *before* the cold winter months arrive. They buy at lower "summer" prices and accumulate inventory so they can meet customer demand in winter. "Large companies have to buy that stuff before the cold months get here... they're buying it at technically what it's considered the lower prices." Seasonal tendencies work because they reflect real supply/demand fundamentals in commodities (unlike stocks/futures, which are purely "hot potato" games where traders just chase higher prices). However, event-driven markets (wars, geopolitical tension, sanctions) can override seasonal patterns. (0:06:45–0:24:19)
- **How to identify:** Look at 5–10 years of historical crude oil (or energy) charts. Mark each occurrence of the first week of July. Observe whether a rally occurs into October. Calculate win-rate: how many years does the pattern hold?
- **How to use:** Use seasonal tendencies as a *bias tool*, not a guaranteed trade setup. If the seasonal tendency suggests prices should go up, and price is showing weakness instead, that weakness is bearish (prices should be going up and aren't, so when they eventually do, it'll likely be sharp). If seasonal tendencies align with technical levels (e.g., a daily open or structural low), confidence increases. Example: "If I'm watching crude and the first week of July should seasonally rally into October, and price is instead declining, that's very strong bearish indication—prices should be going up." (0:13:14)
- **Example in this video:** 
  - 2024 example (0:12:25): First week of July through October, rally occurred on schedule.
  - 2023 example (0:14:00): First week of July, then rally into end of September/early October.
  - 2020 example (0:17:09): First week of July, drop into August, then rally into October (deferred, but pattern held).
  - 2020 (COVID crash example, 0:17:56): Crude went negative (below $0/barrel). This broke the seasonal pattern entirely and caused destabilization for several years. Eventually recovered to $100+ range, but the shock disrupted the pattern temporarily.
  - Current year (implicit): First week of July 2026 into mid-session showed a drop despite seasonal expectation; attributed to absence of geopolitical conflict. (0:12:25)

### Event-Driven Markets Override Seasonal Tendencies
- **Kind:** rule · macro context
- **What ICT says:** Geopolitical events (wars, sanctions, military conflict, supply disruptions) can completely override seasonal patterns. In 2024–2026, crude oil experienced supply disruptions and geopolitical tensions, which caused the seasonal pattern to break. "If things start to heat up... if they drop a tactical nuke... this is going straight up above $200 a barrel." (0:08:24) The 2020 COVID crash (crude went negative) is the extreme example of event-driven market disruption. (0:17:56)
- **How to use:** When trading commodities with seasonal bias, monitor geopolitical news. If major events occur (wars, sanctions, supply disruptions), expect the seasonal pattern to break or reverse. Use this as a filter: "Is the seasonal tendency holding? If not, why not?" If the "why" is a major event, the event take precedence over seasonal bias.
- **Example in this video:** 
  - 2024–2026: Military tensions and geopolitical conflict between Middle East powers. These events caused crude to rally when it might have otherwise drifted lower per seasonal tendency alone. "We have a wartime scenario and we have the two major straits in that area... it's going to cause prices to go up." (0:19:33)

### Fair Value Gap as Retracement & Recovery Tool
- **Kind:** definition · PD array · retracement magnet
- **What ICT says:** When an FVG is created (price gaps up on a candle, leaving an inefficiency), price typically retraces back into it. This is passive tool use: you identify FVGs and expect price to return to them during consolidations or retracements. "Whatever fair value gap that lays in the week... we can use that and interpret that as a potential bullish inversion fair gap to make a much more meaningful run above here." (0:04:16)
- **How to identify:** Mark FVGs on the daily, 4H, and 1H charts; note them as potential retracement magnets.
- **How to use:** In bullish scenarios, use FVGs below price as "buy the dip" zones. In bearish scenarios, use FVGs above price as "sell the rally" zones. Inversion FVGs (created on bullish gaps up) become targets for bullish reversals—price retraces into them, uses them as springboards for further upside.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil daily chart, around 0:06:45. An FVG is created; price trades into it, bounces, then uses the FVG as a floor for further rallies.
- **Timestamps:**
  - [0:04:16](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=256) — "Whatever fair value gap that lays in the week... we can use that as a potential bullish inversion fair gap."

## Rules, Checklists & Numbers Stated

- On low-news days (no economic drivers), trading is choppy and directionless until the 10:00 silver bullet (first hour close); use that as a decision point.
- Seasonal tendencies work because commodities have real supply/demand fundamentals; stocks/futures are "hot potato" games and don't have real supply/demand.
- July → October crude oil rally has multi-year data supporting it; betting on the absence of the rally (shorting despite seasonal bias) is speculative.
- Event-driven markets (wars, geopolitical conflict) override seasonal patterns.
- For crude oil specifically, monitor the Strait of Hormuz and other major shipping lanes; blockades or conflict there will spike prices regardless of seasonal factors.
- Use technical levels (FVGs, breaker blocks, order blocks) in conjunction with seasonal bias for higher-confidence setups.

## Process, Risk & Psychology

**Forecasting vs. Trading:**
- ICT emphasizes that forecasting is "mumbo jumbo." He's outlining what *could* happen (bearish scenario for NQ, bullish scenario for crude) but acknowledging uncertainty. "I don't have really hard bias one way or the other. So it's kind of like a wait and see what we get." (0:01:44)
- Trading involves waiting for confirmed structure before committing capital, not betting on forecasts.

**Seasonal Tendencies as Educational/Confidence Tools:**
- Understanding *why* a seasonal tendency exists (heating oil supply build-up in summer) builds conviction. New traders often dismiss seasonal tendencies as unreliable because they don't understand the mechanism. "Listening to people that don't really have a whole lot of experience... they're parting [copying] them and they don't know what they're doing either, it can trip you up." (0:16:22)
- Use seasonal tendencies to filter out bad ideas: "If crude should be rallying into October per seasonal tendency and it's instead falling, that's bearish." This filters out trades that go against the seasonal grain.

**Risk Management in Event-Driven Markets:**
- Crude oil (and other commodity markets affected by geopolitical risk) can gap dramatically on news. Position sizing should be smaller when event risk is high. "The volatility in it already caused options to be overvalued... I don't really want to say the word safe... but it's a very event driven market right now." (0:20:22)
- ICT personally does not take large positions in crude currently because of geopolitical volatility and expensive option premiums.

**FUD (Fear, Uncertainty, Doubt) Warning:**
- On Bitcoin, ICT warns against listening to social media doomers who say "You're stupid if you sell your Bitcoin" or "You're stupid if you don't hold." This is emotional manipulation, not trading logic. "You waited and more lifeboats are leaving... You're left holding the bag and everybody else got out." (0:22:45)
- Apply logic: take profits when technical levels are hit, don't hold for emotional reasons.

## Glossary Candidates

`Silver Bullet` — The 10:00 AM ET candle close (end of first hour of regular trading). Used as a direction reference and entry confirmation, especially on low-news days.

`Seasonal Tendency` — A recurring multi-year pattern in commodity prices tied to real supply/demand cycles (e.g., crude oil rallies July–October when companies build inventory for winter). Distinguished from random patterns; works because fundamentals support it.

`Event-Driven Market` — A market where geopolitical, economic, or supply-chain events dominate price action over technical or seasonal patterns. Examples: war, sanctions, supply disruptions.

`Market Maker Buy Model (MMBM)` — A pattern of price going down to accumulation level, consolidating, then reversing up as smart money reverses and enters longs; a higher-probability bullish entry pattern.

`Inversion Fair Value Gap` — An FVG created on a bullish gap-up; price retraces into it and uses it as a springboard for further upside.
