# L26-037 — How To Frame Sessions In High Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions

## Summary
ICT explains how he framed Monday's NQ session. Because CPI is Wednesday and PPI is Thursday, he expects the volatility to be held back, so Monday and Tuesday regular trading hours are likely to be range-bound or "high resistance liquidity run" conditions, while electronic trading hours (London and pre-market up to 9:30) are where trending happens. He shows exactly which levels he graded: Friday's final-hour RTH dealing range (fib from its low to its high), the first 30 minutes of the pre-market session (7:00–7:30 ET), the midnight opening price (29,878.25), the new week opening gap, and a BISI that validated as an inversion FVG because it sat on graded levels. He then reviews the morning, where he says a short from a wick would have worked, and explains why he deliberately engages high-resistance days: to show students what harder conditions look like and how to manage them without forcing trades.

## Concepts

### High Resistance Liquidity Run conditions: framing the week by the economic calendar  (aliases ICT uses: "range-bound", "held during regular trading hours")
- **Kind:** rule / context filter
- **What ICT says:**
  - Check the economic calendar for the week. CPI is Wednesday and PPI Thursday (both high-impact "red folder"); Friday's items (consumer sentiment and one more) are medium impact.
  - Because the big volatility injections are on Wednesday and Thursday, there is no reason to expect wildly trending, extrapolated moves on Monday and Tuesday. Volatility is being "held" for the numbers. The one-sided price delivery is more likely on the news days.
  - Caveat: electronic trading hours (London session and the pre-market leading to the 9:30 opening bell) tend to trend in such weeks. Regular trading hours then tend to range, i.e. high-resistance liquidity run conditions.
  - Do not assume you cannot trade it, and one session of the day (AM or PM) may be permitted to trend briefly. Anticipating it helps you avoid forcing trades.
  - When taking trades "look for the best-case scenario, but don't demand it."
- **How to identify:** a week where the heavy news is Wednesday/Thursday and Monday/Tuesday have no equivalent catalysts.
- **How to use:** lower expectations for runaway RTH moves on Monday and Tuesday; use ETH (London / pre-market) for the trend leg; take smaller, faster targets in RTH and don't force breakouts or trending models.
- **Example in this video:** Monday: price consolidated overnight around the midnight open, made relative equal highs in London, drifted lower into the pre-market (the trend leg), and then spent RTH in a range.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:35](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=95), [0:02:21](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=141), [0:03:10](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=190), [0:04:44](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=284)
- **Nuance / caveats:** this extends the "High/low resistance liquidity run" idea to a weekly framing driven by news placement. He says new traders get hurt when they try to force a breakout or trending model on such days.

### Prior Friday's final-hour RTH dealing range as Monday reference
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** he takes the final hour of regular trading hours on Friday (the low and high of that hour; the lowest low is around 4:00 p.m. ET "or thereabouts") as a dealing range. He draws the fib from the low up to the high; a candle laid right on the 0.75 level of that range was a suspension block (caption), so that level mattered. These two reference points (the last-hour low and high) serve as liquidity targets and as grading levels for where turning points may form on Monday. A "key discount array from last Friday" is what price eventually reached in the PM session.
- **How to use:** include Friday's final-hour range on the chart on Monday; treat its low as sell-side liquidity and its graded levels as places to expect reaction.
- **Example in this video:** Monday morning price made a low in the session from a "green box" graded from Friday, and later sold into Friday's final-hour low during the afternoon.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:00](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=0), [0:00:46](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=46), [0:01:35](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=95), [0:09:36](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=576)

### Grading a dealing range with fib levels and carrying it forward  (aliases: quadrants, octants, "gradient levels")
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** measure highest high to lowest low of the defined window, anchor the fib on the low (for an up leg) and the high; quadrant and octant levels then tell you where a PD array should form. If a BISI/FVG touches one of these levels it is "valid", and the range can be carried forward through the entire first hour of trading. A PD array on a graded level is a higher-probability one.
- **How to identify:** the array's boundary or candle touches the same graded levels as the range (e.g. "touching this level and this level").
- **How to use:** carry the graded range into the first hour; use the validated array as the setup (see next concept).
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:09](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=429), [0:07:57](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=477)

### Pre-market first-30-minute range (7:00–7:30 ET) and the 9:30–10:00 opening range
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - He highlights the first 30 minutes of the pre-market session hours, from 7:00 to 7:30 a.m. ET, to see if a range forms. Monday: a high formed, price traded down to make the lowest low since 7:00, then traded back up to almost 3/4 (if not 80%) of the 7:00–7:30 range. He graded the levels from that swing's low to high.
  - The opening range for regular trading hours is the first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00 ET), which is the dealing range; "it's not 5 minutes, it's not 15 minutes, it's 30 minutes."
  - On a day that is not CPI/PPI he could use the whole first hour instead of the first 30 minutes. On a news-week day like this, he uses the first 30 minutes.
- **How to use:** build the fib from the 7:00–7:30 range and carry it through the first hour; look for PD arrays (BISI/SIBI) on its graded levels.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday, 7:00–7:30 ET range; a BISI touching two graded levels.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:19](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=379), [0:07:09](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=429), [0:14:22](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=862)
- **Nuance / caveats:** his "first 30 minutes" window for pre-market (7:00–7:30) is the specific range used here; he does not say it is a universal rule for other times.

### BISI that validates and inverts (iFVG) on graded levels, with midnight open and a macro
- **Kind:** entry model (worked example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A BISI sitting on the graded levels is "valid". Price opened, traded up into it, hit the upper quadrant level, and closed there. The next candle was anticipated to be lower. A candle low / opening price / volume imbalance that is bullish but then gets traded below and validated by closing lower becomes an inversion FVG, "because it's on this level and this level".
  - Price then sold off into the NWOG, rallied back to Monday's midnight opening price and the inversion FVG again during a macro, "the last minute of the macro from 7:50 to 8:10", and then sold off hard, blowing through sell-side and moving to the final-hour RTH low from Friday.
  - Midnight opening price: price gravitates back to it. He says you must have it toggled and annotated on your chart in any market. Monday's midnight open was 29,878.25.
- **How to use:** short the retest of the inversion FVG into the midnight open / macro; target the NWOG and the session low liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, Monday 10 Aug 2026 (inferred), 1-minute-ish: relative equal highs form in London; midnight open 29,878.25; pre-market sell-off; BISI at graded levels inverts; price pulls up to midnight open plus the inversion FVG in the last minute of the 7:50–8:10 macro; then sells off to Friday's final-hour low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:57](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=477), [0:08:48](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=528)
- **Nuance / caveats:** the 7:50–8:10 macro is as the captions give it; compare other lectures for his standard macro list (the 8:50–9:10 and 9:50–10:10 macros are the ones more often quoted).

### Why one low and not another: session and range liquidity list
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** the lows he trusts are chosen from session highs and lows, daily highs and lows, the London lunch range high/low (caption: "London lunch"), the previous week's high/low, and the highest high / lowest low of the last three days. Friday's final-hour RTH low was one of those key liquidity pools. He says he has taught this "ad nauseam" and will not answer every repeated question.

### High-resistance trade review: short from a wick, partial below relative equal lows  (aliases: "inversion FVG", "discount relative to last Friday's internals")
- **Kind:** example only (hypothetical entry)
- **What ICT says:**
  - In the afternoon price rallied to the midnight opening price and the area of bullish FVGs; he had said that, because price was below them, they would behave as inversion FVGs and get "messy". It did get messy and broke lower (he was stopped out in his own trade; he was away from the charts for the later rally).
  - He says he would have sold on the candle that was trying to get to the middle of a wick; that area becomes an inversion FVG; it broke lower and came back to the sell-side pool; he would have partialed below the relative equal lows and trailed the rest, then price went back to the NWOG.
  - A close below halfway of the wick meant the low was likely to be taken, which it was.
  - Later price went into an area where he would not want to participate. A midnight-open touch at a key octant, 0.375 ("0375" in the caption), was a sell, followed by a sell-off into Friday's discount array.
- **How to use:** the same wick-midpoint logic as in other lectures: bodies closing below the wick's CE favor continuation to the low; partial under relative equal lows; trail the stop.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:00](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=720), [0:12:48](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=768), [0:13:35](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=815), [0:15:09](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=909)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he describes it as a trade he "would have" taken, not an actual fill; he also states he got stopped out on an earlier attempt.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Week framing: CPI Wednesday and PPI Thursday → Monday/Tuesday RTH likely range-bound; ETH (London / pre-market) can trend.
- Look for best case but don't demand it.
- Prior Friday final-hour RTH range (low around 4:00 p.m. ET) is a reference; fib low-to-high; a candle on the 0.75 level mattered.
- Pre-market first 30 minutes: 7:00–7:30 ET. RTH opening range: 9:30–10:00 ET (30 minutes, not 5 or 15).
- Non-news day: use the whole first hour; news week: first 30 minutes.
- Midnight opening price on that day: 29,878.25. Always mark it.
- Macro cited: 7:50–8:10 ET (as captioned).
- Octant level cited: 0.375 where price took the midnight open and reversed.
- Validated BISI requires touching graded levels; carry the graded range through the first hour.
- Bodies closing below the midpoint of a wick = likely run of the low.
- Reasons for liquidity picks: session highs/lows, daily, London lunch, previous week, last three days.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Don't force trades; if you have never traded real money you will want to force "even $50", so treat backtesting, forward testing and demo as real, because if you cannot do it there you won't with real money (0:05:32–0:06:19).
- He deliberately participates in high-resistance days after the easy part of the move so students see harder conditions: trades won't always be perfect, you will sometimes see him stopped out, and he shows ways to secure unrealized profit and manage the psychology when your trade isn't "real easy" (0:14:22–0:15:57).
- Study the content in the order he released it, at your own pace; he says 99.9% of questions get resolved that way and he will not answer repeated questions or emails (0:10:24–0:11:11).

## Glossary candidates
`High resistance liquidity run` — conditions where the delivery is choppy or range-bound, typically RTH when the real volatility is saved for later news.
`Electronic trading hours (ETH)` — the overnight/London/pre-market period before the 9:30 RTH open; the trending part of the day in this framing.
`Midnight opening price` — the 12:00 a.m. New York price; a magnet for retracements.
`Suspension block` — (caption) candle laid on a graded level (here the 0.75) that makes that level important; caption may also read "dispassion block" later (caption unclear).
`Final-hour RTH dealing range` — the high/low of the last regular trading hour of the prior day, used as Monday liquidity/grading reference.
