## Concepts

### Macro Time Entry (9:50–10:10 AM ET)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The window between 9:50 and 10:10 AM ET (last 10 minutes of the 9 AM hour + first 10 minutes of the 10 AM hour) is a macro time boundary. During volatile weeks (Jackson Hole, FOMC), major institutional order clustering occurs at this boundary. Entering trades at this window increases conviction and probability of success because algorithmic order flow is scheduled and forced.
- **How to identify:** Mark 9:50 and 10:10 AM on 1-minute chart; observe price action at these times; identify when lows/highs are being taken out at this boundary.
- **How to use:** Anticipate large moves around macro windows. If you're already positioned before the macro window, expect acceleration into and through the window. Use this window for pyramiding (adding to position).
- **Example in this video:** Entry was executed in the middle of the 9:50–10:10 macro window; subsequent pyramiding added during this same window.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Macro window execution increases volatility and stop-run risk; expect whipsaws.

### Three-PD-Array Defensive Framework
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When entering a trade, identify three independent PD arrays that support/defend the position:
  1. **Primary level** — Direct entry reference (inversion FVG high/low, order block boundary, etc.)
  2. **Secondary level** — Wick consequent encroachment (midpoint of prior wick; next friction level)
  3. **Tertiary level** — Volume imbalance or order block low (additional support if primary breaks)
  These three levels form a "stack" of defense. Stop loss is placed just below the tertiary level. If price violates all three, the trade thesis is negated.
- **How to identify:** Identify the entry level; then scan for the next two support levels downward (or upward for shorts).
- **How to use:** Place stops below the tertiary level (the lowest defense). As price moves in your favor and confirms the trend, raise stops to protect profits.
- **Example in this video:** Three PD arrays stacked: (1) inversion FVG low, (2) wick consequent encroachment, (3) volume imbalance high. Stop was placed below all three.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:03](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=243) "three levels being like my three PD arrays that supports the trade," [0:07:25](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=445) "several wicks and an order block down close candle supporting my trade."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** All three must be identified before entry; don't add them after the fact.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (Multiple Uses)
- **Kind:** entry model (advanced)
- **What ICT says:** On volatile days (seek-and-destroy), multiple inversion FVGs may form as price oscillates. Each oscillation creates an inversion FVG zone where the previous wick becomes the reversal target. ICT may enter multiple contracts into different inversion FVG zones on the same day, pyramiding the position as each zone confirms.
- **How to identify:** After price makes a run up and wicks, the wick becomes an inversion FVG target for the next downside run. When price dips back into that zone, it's an entry point for the next leg up.
- **How to use:** Identify inversion FVG zones at different price levels; enter partial position into each zone as confirmed. This is pyramiding into range-bound volatility.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple inversion FVGs were identified and used; ICT entered, took partials at targets, and re-entered into new inversion FVG zones on subsequent pullbacks.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:39](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=99) "wick... premium wick that's just above me," [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=195) "I want to see it now stay either in the upper half."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Requires active monitoring; set limit orders for pyramiding; do not chase.

### Wick Consequent Encroachment (Dynamic Stop Level)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint of any wick acts as a dynamic support/resistance level. As the trade progresses and new wicks form, their midpoints become the next friction levels. When raising stops, move them to just below the midpoint of the wick that supports the current position. This ensures maximum profit protection without giving up gains.
- **How to identify:** After each candlestick, note the wick high and low; calculate midpoint; mark it on chart.
- **How to use:** Use wick CE as stops and as retracement targets. Raise stop to wick CE when position is confirmed and moves in your favor.
- **Example in this video:** As the long position moved higher and new wicks formed, ICT raised the stop to just below the consequent encroachment of the most recent supporting wick.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:27](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=147) "showing basically the wick. That's good encouragement," [0:06:33](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=393) "Just the low of the wick that I measured consequent encroachment."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Dynamic; must update as new wicks form.

### SIBI (Sell-Side Imbalance, Buy-Side Inefficiency)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A candlestick with a down-close (close below open) creates a "sell-side imbalance" — the gap between open and close. This indicates selling pressure. However, the wick below that close (the "buy-side inefficiency") represents price that the market didn't stay at; it's an inefficiency that price may fill on subsequent bounces. Multiple SIBIs on a seek-and-destroy day represent successive "tests" of support.
- **How to identify:** Identify down-close candles; mark the open-to-close gap (the imbalance); note the wick low below (the inefficiency).
- **How to use:** For longs: SIBI zones become targets for upside runs. When price pulls back into a SIBI from above, it bounces upward (testing that inefficiency from below). For pyramid entries, target the upper part of the SIBI zone for limit orders.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple SIBIs were identified; price was expected to bounce and fill the upper portions of these zones.
- **Timestamps:** [0:02:27](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=147) "volume imbalance," [0:09:02](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=542) "another sibi," [0:04:54](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=294) "I want to see it now stay... in the upper half."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Imbalance is the gap; inefficiency is the wick. Don't confuse the two.

### Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When a volume imbalance or SIBI zone is only partially filled (price dips into lower portion but doesn't fill the entire gap), this indicates institutional buying at discount — they're accumulating without fully committing. This "partly unfilled" condition is bullish and signals continuation upward is likely.
- **How to identify:** Mark a SIBI or volume imbalance; observe price movement: if it dips into lower portion but bounces before filling upper portion, it's an institutional entry drill.
- **How to use:** When you see this pattern, expect strong upside continuation. It's a green light to hold position or pyramid.
- **Example in this video:** "Institutional order flow entry drill" was called out multiple times as price kept partly-unfilled gaps and bounced higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:29](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=929) "institutional order flow entry drill," [0:25:33](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1533) "You just want to give an opportunity for institutional order flow entry drill."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Opposite: fully-filled gap = weakness.

### Buy-Side Imbalance, Sell-Side Efficiency
- **Kind:** definition (inverse of SIBI)
- **What ICT says:** An up-close candlestick (close above open) creates a "buy-side imbalance" — gap between open and close. The wick above that close (the "sell-side efficiency") represents price that the market didn't sustain; it's inefficiency that may be filled on subsequent pullbacks. Identifying where buy-side imbalances exist helps predict pullback targets.
- **How to identify:** Mark up-close candles; note the open-to-close gap (imbalance); note the wick high above (efficiency).
- **How to use:** For longs: these areas are where you expect minimal pullback (they're resistant to filling due to sell-side efficiency). For shorts: they're targets to test and fill.
- **Example in this video:** Buy-side imbalances from prior days (August 4th NQ) were referenced as structural support; failing to reach these levels was bullish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:10](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1330) "August 4th, 2026 NQ buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency," [0:24:24](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1464) "buy side imbalance sell side efficiency."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Opposite of SIBI; bullish vs. bearish implication.

### Cork Pattern (Consolidation Before Breakout)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Before breaking through to a new high, price often consolidates into a tight, seemingly odd-shaped price block — the "cork pattern." This is not a classic bull flag or bear flag; it's a sloppy, tight block of price action that resists moving higher (or lower) for a few candles. Once it "pops the cork" (breaks out of the consolidation), it runs quickly to the next target. This pattern is characteristic of seek-and-destroy days.
- **How to identify:** Look for a tight, rectangular-ish block of price action just below (for longs) or above (for shorts) a key target level.
- **How to use:** Patience is critical. When you see a cork pattern forming, know that breakout is coming. Don't exit the trade out of impatience. The consolidation is setup for the next big move.
- **Example in this video:** Cork pattern formed around the intraday high target; ICT identified it and advised patience: "Like a champagne bottle being popped."
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:49](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1189) "This looks like it's starting to cork," [0:20:36](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1236) "weird little block of price action," [0:23:01](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1381) "cork... sloppy little block."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Requires patience and conviction in the setup; many traders exit prematurely.

### Discount Sensitivity Zone
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The upper half of a wick (or the zone between the wick low and the wick midpoint) is the "discount sensitivity" area. When price trades in this zone on a pullback, it's still in a discount area. Trading in the discount zone (rather than chasing higher) is lower-risk. Expect price to bounce from this zone.
- **How to identify:** Identify a wick; divide into two halves (lower = deep discount, upper = shallow discount/sensitivity).
- **How to use:** For pullback entries, buy the upper half of the discount zone (discount sensitivity area), not the lower half (deep discount where reversals are uncertain).
- **Example in this video:** The upper half of prior wicks was referenced as the ideal zone for maintaining bullish position without over-risking.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:15](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=195) "use the high of it for discount sensitivity," [0:11:25](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=685) "the discount array... upper half of the wick."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Context-dependent; in strong uptrends, discount sensitivity area is preferred for entries.

### Limit Orders for Pyramiding
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Rather than manually chasing price higher for pyramid entries, use limit orders placed at anticipated resistance/target levels. When price reaches the limit order price, the order automatically fills, adding to the position without requiring manual intervention or emotional decision-making. This is especially valuable on volatile days where multiple pyramids are planned.
- **How to identify:** Identify the next target level (top of next inversion FVG, relative equal high, etc.); place a limit order slightly below that level to increase fill probability.
- **How to use:** Pre-place limit orders before entering the trade; let the market fill them as price runs. Reduces need to stare at the screen for every pyramid add.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple limit orders were placed for pyramid contracts; ICT watched them execute rather than manually chasing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:14](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=494) "I'll use a limit order here," [0:13:04](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=784) "put a limit order in there for contracts."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Risk of limit orders not filling if price gaps over them; must balance fill probability with target precision.
