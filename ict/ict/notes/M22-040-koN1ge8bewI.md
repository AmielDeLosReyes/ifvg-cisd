## Summary
ICT reviews USD/CAD on 21 June 2022, a day with a high-impact Canadian core retail sales release at 8:30. He defines a swing high as a 3-bar pattern and shows a daily swing high at an old high. He explains how price rallied into a premium (manipulation) ahead of the news, then displaced at 8:30 into the model short. He uses an "elongated" FVG to explain entry inside the premium of the imbalance, and teaches London close (10–11 AM NY) as the time to take about 80% off. He then gives his simplified "keys to daily bias": you do not need a bias every day. Find the likely weekly expansion, go top-down to an FVG, and trade only high- or medium-impact news days inside killzones. An ES (Sept 2022) long shows the same model: an early pre-9:30 setup from a bullish order block plus FVG in a discount, three hourly targets hit, and a 10-second-chart entry at 3741.75. He links the two markets through risk-on/risk-off (lower USDCAD, so a weaker dollar, so higher ES). He closes on pair selection by economic calendar and SMT (EURUSD vs GBPUSD, AUD vs NZD).

## Concepts

### Swing high (3-bar definition)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A swing high is three candles: a high with a lower high on the candle to the left and a lower high on the candle to the right. It is not a Williams fractal and not five candles. "If you're waiting for five candles you missed the boat."
- **How to use:** A daily swing high that forms right after price runs an old high means expect lower the next day.
- **Example in this video:** USDCAD daily. Last week price bumped just above an old short-term high (visible clearly on the 1h), then printed a lower-high candle the next day, forming the swing high. ICT expected lower prices after that.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:08](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=248) — old high run, 3-candle swing high; [0:04:57](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=297) — not a 5-bar fractal.

### Bullish order block as an impediment to a down move (daily)
- **Kind:** example only / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the way down, a down-closed candle (a bullish order block) on the daily can slow the move. He anchored a dashed line at that candle's opening price.
  - He was not saying price would go into it to go higher. Only that it is the "impediment" to one big down candle clearing the level below.
  - Price dug into about half its body, and the daily candle closed there (21 June 2022).

### News-driver manipulation into a premium, then displacement at 8:30 (forex model)
- **Kind:** entry model / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Ahead of the 8:30 high-impact news, price rallies. "This is all manipulation." It runs into an imbalance and above a short-term high (BSL), building a premium from about 5:00 AM NY.
  - Only high (red) or medium (orange) impact events interest him; he says yellow does not (he seems to call orange "yellow", caption unclear). Calendar source: forexfactory.com.
  - The ICT New York killzone for forex runs 7:00–10:00 AM NY.
  - A small break of a low ("toe in the water") is not enough. Wait for meaningful, energetic displacement through the short-term low. That is the MSS. Here the displacement candle was exactly the 8:30 release.
  - A pre-news FVG short was possible but riskier, because the 8:30 release could spike price up into the area above. The conservative way is to wait for the news, then for the setup. Trusting pre-news continuation "takes a little bit more experience". Practise these on demo in real time instead of forecasting how far the release will push.
  - After the displacement, define risk for a possible return to the higher FVG(s) with the volatility.
- **How to identify:**
  1. Run of the short-term high (BSL) in the killzone.
  2. Energetic displacement through a short-term low at or after the news.
  3. An FVG left behind.
  4. Opposing liquidity (relative equal lows / SSL) below as the target.
- **Example in this video:** USDCAD 15m and 5m, 21 June 2022. From 5 AM NY price built a premium. At about 7:00 AM a short-term high formed, then was run (BSL taken). A first drop was too weak. At 8:30 (core retail sales high-impact, retail sales medium-impact) the displacement candle broke the short-term low, leaving an FVG. Price then delivered into the FVG below (a discount array, past equilibrium of the high–low range) and then into the relative-equal-lows SSL from the morning.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:19](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=439) — pre-news rally is manipulation into FVG and BSL; [0:08:09](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=489) — high/medium impact only; NY killzone 7–10 AM for forex; [0:08:57](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=537) — meaningful displacement, MSS on the news candle; [0:09:43](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=583) — wait for the news (conservative) vs pre-news entry.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** His tweet at 8:29 AM asked students to note SSL and BSL on the 5m and 15m and "study if it wants to run sell side before buy side or rebounds to a discount".

### Elongated FVG and entering in the premium of the imbalance; stop placement
- **Kind:** refinement / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - When a sell-side delivery is long, one-sided, and imbalanced, with "not enough buy-side delivery", the algorithm will want to come back up and overlap it. That gives buyers a chance and rebalances the leg.
  - Forex has no central volume measure.
  - Under the model rules, the stop goes above the FVG / the level it could revisit, wherever you enter between the low and that high.
  - To refine the entry, fib the range high to low and sell at or above the 50% (equilibrium), in the premium.
  - About 12 pips of risk is fine because "it's all relative". Forex scales your size to the stop; futures lock you into contract size (or over-leverage).
- **How to use:** Short in the premium of the displacement leg. Stop above the FVG / swing high. First partial at the first discount array, then target SSL.
- **Example in this video:** USDCAD 4m. Entry area about 1.2944–1.2947 (caption "129.44 / 129.47"). Candle bodies respected the premium high of the imbalance. A small false rally took buy-side once more, then the real move ran into the FVG below (partials), then below a low of engineered SSL and into the SSL pool.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=774) — 4m chart, elongated imbalance; [0:13:42](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=822) — one-sided delivery must rebalance; stop placement; [0:14:30](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=870) — 12 pips is relative, forex scalable; [0:15:18](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=918) — 50% equilibrium, sell in premium; [0:16:04](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=964) — entry 1.2944–1.2947.

### Partials at the first discount array
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Between the high and the low is equilibrium. When short, find a discount array below it (an FVG), take a partial there, and target the sell-side pool. Partials matter because price may come all the way back up and revisit the FVG, and you would lose the chance to bank something at your first discount array.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:07](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=727) — equilibrium, discount array, partial; [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=774) — why partials.

### London close (10:00–11:00 AM NY): take about 80% off
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If you are in a directional trade, have about 80% of it off between 10:00 and 11:00 AM NY. That hour usually (not always) creates the opposite end of the daily range. If the session high is in, the low is likely to form 10–11. "That's algorithmic."
  - For CAD on crude oil inventory days, crude news can skew this, so he widens the window to 10:00–noon.
  - He has always taught 10–11 as the "London close profit-taking hour".
- **Example in this video:** USDCAD rolled over during 10–11 after the 8:30 short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:50](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1010) — 10–11 London close, 80% off; [0:17:38](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1058) — crude oil days, 10–noon.

### Daily bias simplified ("keys to daily bias")
- **Kind:** rule / checklist
- **What ICT says:**
  - Having a bias every day is unrealistic. If he decides a daily bias before the market trades, invariably he will be wrong sometimes. There is no cookie-cutter ABC; the rest is experience.
  - Instead, determine the likely weekly expansion from the weekly chart. Is it gravitating to:
    - an old high to run above it;
    - an old low to run below it;
    - an imbalance above or below to revisit;
    - or is it likely not to move, because this market has no news that week while others do (then expect a lacklustre market)?
  - He is not trying to catch the whole weekly range, buy the weekly low, or predict the weekly close. He only wants the direction of the bulk of the volatility ("volume" in forex means interest, energy, activity). "Who's in the crosshairs?" For example, if longs have been making money, is there a low to run to stop them out?
  - Go top-down: weekly, daily, 4h, 1h, 15m, then 5m. Then scale through 5/4/3/2/1m to whichever shows a clear FVG under the model rules.
  - Focus only on days with high or medium-impact calendar events. Check the calendar ahead (even next month): which day and time.
  - On that news day, in your killzone, in the direction of the expected weekly expansion, look for: a run up into an FVG or above a short-term high (stops), then a breakdown with displacement, then an FVG to short (mirror for longs).
  - If the move happens before the event, it is a missed opportunity. Use another pair.
- **How to use:** This gives a developing student "permission not to know the daily bias outside of these rules". Plan the trade: calendar, time of day, killzone, and the weekly expansion direction.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:12](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1152) — daily bias intro; [0:20:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1247) — everyday bias unrealistic; weekly expansion; [0:21:37](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1297) — weekly targets (old high/low, imbalance, no news); [0:24:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1487) — top-down to 5–1m, high/medium news days only; [0:25:34](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1534) — news-day setup in killzones.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He presents this as the simplification of all his earlier daily-bias lessons. "There's nothing else I'm holding back."

### FOMC whipsaw: ignore the two-sided wick range
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When price whipsaws both sides (sell-side then buy-side taken), it is usually FOMC or a rate announcement. Ignore those wicks. The "real range" is the body-defined swing; the manipulation is done and "not going to hurt you".
- **Example in this video:** ES hourly. Relative equal lows were taken by a slightly lower low. Then a two-sided FOMC whipsaw, a breakdown, a new low, consolidation (Monday holiday), and a drift higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:24](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1824) — whipsaw both sides; [0:31:12](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1872) — ignore wicks, real range.

### Premium FVG as a target (and skipping an FVG that sits on equilibrium)
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take the swing high-to-low range and find equilibrium.
  - He was not interested in an FVG overlapping the 50% level. He wanted an FVG fully in the premium as the draw, as the "first low-hanging fruit", even if the market later goes up to go down.
  - Candle bodies stayed within the FVG in the premium.
  - The hourly FVG's lower and upper levels became the intraday targets.
- **Example in this video:** ES (ESU2022) hourly. The premium FVG above equilibrium was the draw. Price reached the low of the hourly FVG, then the high of it, during the NY AM session.

### London forms the high or low of day (~70%); consecutive down-closed candles as one order block
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - London creates the high or the low of the day "most of the time, 70% of time", if your directional bias is correct.
  - The last three down-closed candles before a rally count as one (consecutive) bullish order block.
- **Example in this video:** ES 15m. From the NY midnight open price went down (not up) into an FVG and the bullish order block of the last three down-closed candles during London. It rallied energetically (a short-term bullish MSS), made a new FVG, ran buy-side, and left relative equal highs ahead of NY. Then it dropped into the FVG and the last down-closed candle (a bullish order block), a buy area ahead of the 9:30 open. ICT trusted the London low as the likely daily low.

### NY AM session for index futures (8:30–11:00); early setup before 9:30
- **Kind:** rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - For index futures, the NY AM session runs 8:30–11:00 AM.
  - There is a hierarchy: price trades down into HTF, then lower-TF, then lower-TF down-closed candles (order blocks) and FVGs, all within a bullish bias. FVGs get closed and price goes higher. You need no indicators or moving averages; "the candlestick is telling you everything".
  - When manipulation is already done before 9:30, you do not need to wait for the 9:30 volatility. Price had made a low, then a lower low (SSL taken, a stop run) into an order block, reacted, shifted structure, and closed the only FVG. Then it was not likely to take that low out.
- **How to use:** Buy at the down-closed candle plus FVG in the short-term discount (below 50% of the low-to-high leg). First target is the BSL (relative equal highs) as the first partial. Pyramid as it runs. Then target the hourly FVG low and high.
- **Example in this video:** ES 5m and 3m:
  1. Low, then a lower low into a bullish OB (SSL run).
  2. Rally gives a short-term MSS.
  3. Retrace into the FVG plus last down-closed candle in a discount.
  4. Consolidation at 9:30.
  5. Run to three targets, all hit: BSL relative equal highs, the low of the hourly FVG, and the high of the hourly FVG.
  
  An FVG was overshot slightly ("that's fine").
- **Timestamps:** [0:35:08](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2108) — 8:30–11 session; hourly FVG levels as targets; [0:35:57](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2157) — hierarchy of down-closed candles; no indicators; [0:38:20](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2300) — why the low will not be taken; buy at OB+FVG; [0:39:09](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2349) — no need to wait for 9:30.

### Intermarket risk-on / risk-off using the pair with the news driver
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Look at USDCAD's first-named currency (USD). If USDCAD is expected to go down into a discount or run SSL, the dollar is weaker. That is risk-on, so ES, NQ, and foreign currencies rally more easily.
  - Because USDCAD had the high-impact news, he did not need the DXY. He went straight to USDCAD to frame the morning bias for ES.
  - The markets are not "correlated". It is only risk-on vs risk-off.
  - Risk-on: foreign currencies, index futures, and stocks up; dollar down. Risk-off: dollar up, everything else down.
- **Example in this video:** At the 9:30 period, USDCAD traded down into its discount while ES rallied.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2447) — USDCAD bearish means risk-on; [0:41:35](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2495) — no need for DXY; [0:44:46](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2686) — risk-on/off definitions.

### SMT for pair selection in forex
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Pick the market by the economic calendar.
  - If EUR has the high-impact news, compare EURUSD and GBPUSD. If both fell before the release but GBPUSD failed to make a lower low (risk-on), buy GBPUSD, the relative strength leader, even though the news is on EUR, because they move in sympathy.
  - The same applies to AUDUSD vs NZDUSD: buy the one making the higher low when risk-on.
  - On this day he did not check other pairs. He stayed only on USDCAD because the calendar put forex volatility there.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Swing high = 3 candles (lower high on each side). Do not wait for 5.
- Trade only around high- or medium-impact news (forexfactory calendar).
- ICT NY killzone for forex: 7:00–10:00 AM NY. NY AM session for index futures: 8:30–11:00 AM.
- London close 10:00–11:00 AM NY: have about 80% of a directional trade off. It usually forms the opposite end of the daily range. On crude-oil inventory days for CAD, use 10:00–noon.
- London forms the high or low of day about 70% of the time, if the directional bias is correct.
- The displacement must be energetic. A "toe in the water" break of a low is not an MSS.
- Short in the premium (above 50%) of the displacement leg's range. Stop above the FVG / high that could be revisited.
- Ignore two-sided FOMC whipsaw wicks. Use the real range.
- An FVG overlapping equilibrium is not the target. Prefer one fully in the premium or discount.
- Several consecutive down-closed candles can form one bullish order block.
- Daily bias: find the weekly expansion direction (old high/low or imbalance), go top-down to a 5–1m FVG, and only on high/medium-news days in killzones.
- Afternoon continuation window: 1:30–4:00 PM NY.
- Levels: USDCAD short entry about 1.2944–1.2947, about 12 pips of risk. ES (ESU2022) long fill 3741.75. Date 21 June 2022 (US holiday on Monday the 20th).

## Process, risk & psychology
- Do not be the "everyday trader". Trading every day leads to more losses. He blew accounts at 20–22 from that "fevered pace". ([0:27:10](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1630), [0:28:00](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1680))
- "Plan your trade and trade your plan" made concrete: calendar event, time of day, killzone, weekly expansion. ([0:28:48](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1728))
- Backlog and journal every day, marking up charts. Over time you will see the best setups cluster around calendar events. "They don't give out gold medals for overtrading but they do blow accounts." ([0:29:37](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1777))
- Use demo to watch news-driven moves form in real time before trusting pre-news entries. ([0:09:43](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=583))
- Forex position size scales to the stop distance. In futures, if the stop is too wide for one contract you either skip the trade or are over-leveraged. ([0:14:30](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=870))

## Glossary candidates
`Swing high (ICT)` — a 3-bar pattern: a high flanked by lower highs on each side.
`London close` — 10:00–11:00 AM NY, the profit-taking hour that often forms the opposite extreme of the daily range.
`Weekly expansion` — the expected direction of the week's main volatility (toward an old high/low or imbalance). It is the starting point for daily bias.
`Elongated imbalance` — a long, one-sided displacement leg with little opposing delivery, expected to be partly rebalanced. Enter in its premium or discount half.
`Consecutive order block` — several back-to-back down-closed (bullish) or up-closed (bearish) candles treated as one order block.
`Risk-on / risk-off` — dollar down with stocks, indices, and foreign currencies up / dollar up with the rest down. Used for intermarket bias, not correlation.
`Real range` — the range after ignoring two-sided manipulation wicks (for example FOMC whipsaw).
`Toe in the water` — a weak, marginal break of a low that does not count as displacement or an MSS.
