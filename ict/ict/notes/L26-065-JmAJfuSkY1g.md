## Concepts

### Order Block (aliases: OB, bearish order block, bullish order block)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + entry model
- **What ICT says:** The open of the bearish order block is the last up-close candle among consecutive larger candles. Until price closes below it, changes in state of delivery don't apply. Only when price breaches below it does the level become salient and pertinent to price action decision-making. Specifically mentioned: 29,282.50 level on daily NQ chart on 07/08.
- **How to identify:** Scan left for the largest consecutive up-close candles (last one is the OB); mark the open of that final candle.
- **How to use:** Once breached with a close below, the order block validates directional continuation. When trading near it on lower timeframes (1m), wicking up into it while inside other confluences (macro, breaker) suggests rejection and further downside.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily, 29,282.50 level (bearish order block open). Price tested it on 1m timeframe at ~10:05 ET while inside the breaker formation; wicked up into it and then fell through, continuing the sell-model decline to 29,250 area.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:50](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=50) (OB defined) · [0:05:42](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=342) (daily 29,282.50 level) · [0:13:36](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=816) (daily OB being wicked up to on 1m) · [0:18:27](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1107) (confluence: bearish OB + macro + breaker).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Opening of the candle is irrelevant unless price gets below it. The order block is the *last* up-close candle if it's the largest; consecutive candle size is the deciding factor.

### Consequent Encroachment (aliases: CE, wick grading)
- **Kind:** refinement + rule
- **What ICT says:** When price leaves a wick, that wick—specifically its extension into the next level—must be "graded" or considered. If price closes below the consequent encroachment (the wick's deep point), it signals continuation. If it doesn't close below, price may retrace, consolidate, or reverse. This applies to daily wicks on lower timeframes (1m) and to inefficiency wicks.
- **How to identify:** Look at the wick on any candle; draw a line at the wick's lowest point (for bearish setups, the wick extends down).
- **How to use:** Use as a rejection filter: if price wicks up into consequent encroachment but fails to close above it (or vice versa for bullish CEs), expect reversal or consolidation. For entries, position stops just outside the CE to avoid wick whipsaws.
- **Example in this video:** NDOG consequent encroachment (the wick extension of the new day opening gap); price fails to leave a body at or above it multiple times, signaling no bullish intent. Also: inversion FVG wick CE at ~29,270; price wicks up but body stays in lower octants, indicating weakness and bearish pressure continuing.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:40](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=100) (wick grading concept) · [0:08:50](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=530) (NDOG CE definition) · [0:09:37](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=577) (bearish action when failing to leave body above NDOG CE) · [0:15:11](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=911) (inversion FVG CE: wick can't touch it).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Consequent encroachment of a wick is not support/resistance; it's an inefficiency marker. If not breached, it's weakness, not strength.

### Market Maker Sell Model (aliases: MMSM, sell model)
- **Kind:** entry model + rule
- **What ICT says:** Original consolidation → leaves consolidation → comes back to test consolidation → first stage reaccumulation → second stage reaccumulation → smart money reversal → low-risk sell → first stage distribution → second stage distribution → drop below original consolidation to take lows. When bullish, the model will NOT take out the original consolidation; it will stop in deep discount but outside the sell-side liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Scan for consolidation (ranging price); identify the break and rejection back into it; count two reaccumulation stages, then reversal candle, then two distribution stages.
- **How to use:** The second stage distribution is the largest, easiest, longest-magnitude move. Enter near reversal with targets at sell-side liquidity or relative equal lows below consolidation. Stop above the breaker (high-low-higher high structure).
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m on 07/08, 9:30 ET open started a MMSM. Original consolidation near NDOG; price rallied into NDOG, failed to break above NDOG CE, retraced, then executed second stage distribution to take lows at ~29,250 and relative equal lows lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:17](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=197) (MMSM structure) · [0:18:27](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1107) (MMSM in action: second stage distribution) · [0:20:51](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1251) (MMSM variation: when bullish, doesn't take original consolidation).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** When bullish, MMSM stops in deep discount, not at original consolidation. Must confirm with other confluences before entry.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) and Inversion FVG (aliases: iFVG, bullish/bearish inefficiency)
- **Kind:** definition + rule + entry model
- **What ICT says:** A fair value gap is a sell-side or buy-side inefficiency—the difference between the close of one candle and the open of a later candle, leaving imbalanced price. An inversion FVG is one where the body stays on one side (bullish or bearish). Volume imbalance: one tick difference in open/close (e.g., open 252.75, close 252.50 = 1-tick imbalance). When price trades into an FVG unwilling to lay a body on the opposite side of consequent encroachment, it's weak; when it closes below/above, it's strong directional continuation.
- **How to identify:** Look for gaps in price action; mark the high of the lower candle to the low of the higher candle (sell-side) or vice versa (buy-side).
- **How to use:** Entries can target FVG retracements. Exits can be placed at FVG bodies. If price enters an FVG and fails to commit (body on wrong side), expect reversal.
- **Example in this video:** Bearish inversion iFVG after the 10:00 ET rally into NDOG; price enters it unwilling to close above consequent encroachment, signaling bearish continuation. Second example: bullish iFVG on the 29,250 retest; price enters and commits with body above, launching the next rally phase.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:24](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=624) (FVG/iFVG definition: 1-tick imbalance) · [0:11:13](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=673) (inversion iFVG: unwilling to commit) · [0:12:00](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=720) (inversion iFVG now being respected, role reversal).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Inversion FVG role-play: if it doesn't propel price in one direction (e.g., up), it may reverse and pull down.

### Bearish Breaker (aliases: breaker, high-low-higher high)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** A bearish breaker is a short-term high, a low below it, and a higher high (but lower than the original short-term high). Must use the body that reaches lowest, not wicks. Breaker placement is critical for stop loss above it and for confluence with other sell signals.
- **How to identify:** Scan for a small swing high; then lower low; then test back up but not above the first high. Mark the body of the candle making the highest of that higher high.
- **How to use:** Place sell stops just above the breaker body (not the wick). Enter shorts into the breaker if macro, OB, or FVG confluences support it. Breaker failure (close above) = trend reversal.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, ~10:05–10:12 ET, a bearish breaker formed (short-term high, low, higher high below it). Sell entry was placed at this breaker with stops above the breaker body. Breaker wicked up to daily OB and failed, confirming the short.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:46](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=766) (breaker definition: short-term high, low, higher high) · [0:24:01](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1441) (entry off breaker, finessing stop placement).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't use wicks for breaker identification; use bodies only. Breaker is a 1m-level structure; it must align with higher-timeframe confluences.

### New Day Opening Gap (aliases: NDOG, opening gap)
- **Kind:** definition + rule
- **What ICT says:** The difference between settlement price (RTH close at 4:59 ET previous day) and RTH open at 9:30 ET next day (actually 6:00 PM ET previous day to 9:30 ET next day in NQ's continuous session). NDOG has consequent encroachment (the wick extension). If price fails to leave a body at or above NDOG CE, that's bearish. If it does and closes above, that's bullish.
- **How to identify:** Mark previous day's RTH close and current day's 6:00 PM open (electronic open); also mark 9:30 RTH open. NDOG CE is the wick into one of these.
- **How to use:** NDOG acts as a bearish or bullish bias filter. Failed body above NDOG CE = no bullish intent. Successful close above = bullish intent. Also used as PD array discount or premium source for targets.
- **Example in this video:** NQ on 07/08, NDOG was set from 07/07 4:59 ET close to 07/08 6:00 PM ET open. Price rallied into NDOG at 9:30 open but failed to leave a body above NDOG CE, signaling bearish.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:14](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=434) (NDOG defined) · [0:08:50](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=530) (NDOG CE: doesn't leave body above = bearish) · [0:09:37](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=577) (part of MMSM, NDOG failure = selloff).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** NDOG is a higher-timeframe filter; failure on 1m doesn't mean rejection of entire NDOG; must watch for closes and body placement.

### Opening Range Gap (aliases: ORG, RTH opening range gap)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The difference between RTH open at 9:30 ET and RTH close at 4:59 ET the same day. This is a daily structure. 70% likelihood of trading to the 50% midpoint (consequent encroachment) within 9:30-10:00 ET.
- **How to identify:** Mark 9:30 open and 4:59 close on daily; plot Fibonacci levels; focus on 50% midpoint.
- **How to use:** Targets, support in deep discounts, entry confirmation when price holds or rejects at ORG levels.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 07/08, ORG from 9:30 open. Price ran into new day opening gap at 10:00, failed, then dropped. Later, rallied back and used ORG 50% as support.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:02](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=962) (ORG definition, contrasted with NDOG) · [0:16:53](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1013) (ORG 50% = 70% likelihood by 10:00 ET).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** NDOG is part of ORG; ORG is the 9:30–4:59 structure.

### Macro (aliases: macros, algorithmic time window, 10:50–11:10, Silver Bullet)
- **Kind:** rule + timing
- **What ICT says:** Macros are algorithmic time windows when institutional order flow has a higher likelihood of executing. 10:50–11:10 ET is cited in this video as a macro. It's a known timing confluence that can validate a turn (especially at second stage distribution).
- **How to identify:** Memorize key macros: 10:50–11:10 ET (often cited for NY AM); also 8:30 ET, 2:00 PM ET, and others.
- **How to use:** Use as a confluence filter; if you're near a turn and macro is active, higher conviction. Do NOT trade macro alone.
- **Example in this video:** At 10:50 ET, price was inside bearish breaker, MMSM, NDOG rejection, and daily OB—all validated by the 10:50–11:10 macro turn; selloff accelerated.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:36](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=816) (macro timing inside multiple confluences) · [0:21:37](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1297) (macro inside bearish OB: huge confluence).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Macros are one of the "supposedly don't do anything" tools, but they do; they're algorithmic timing, not magic.

### Volume Imbalance and Inefficiency
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** A 1-tick difference between candle open and close (e.g., open 252.75, close 252.50) is a micro-volume imbalance and therefore an inefficiency (fair value gap). These micro-imbalances are part of the larger PD array framework.
- **How to identify:** Compare open and close of individual candles; 1-tick (or small) difference = imbalance.
- **How to use:** These micro-imbalances can act as rejections; price unwilling to fully commit above or below them may reverse or consolidate.
- **Example in this video:** Within the bearish iFVG at 10:00, price open 252.75, close 252.50 = 1-tick imbalance, part of the inefficiency definition.
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:24](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=624) (1-tick imbalance in FVG).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Micro-imbalances are beneath most retail awareness; ICT emphasizes they're real inefficiencies.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Order block is only salient after price closes below it; opening price of the OB candle is irrelevant unless breached.
- Breaker placement must use body that reaches lowest, not wicks.
- New day opening gap: if price fails to leave a body at or above NDOG CE, it's bearish; if close above, it's bullish.
- Opening range gap 50% midpoint has ~70% probability of trading into it within 9:30–10:00 ET.
- Macro time 10:50–11:10 ET validates turns when confluences align.
- Second stage distribution is "the largest easiest price run in the longest magnitude."
- When MMSM is bullish, it will NOT take out original consolidation; it stops in deep discount outside sell-side liquidity.
- Fair value gap consequent encroachment: if price doesn't close beyond it, it's weak; if it does, it's strong continuation.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Stop loss placement:** Position stops just above the breaker body, not above wicks. Wicks are noise; bodies are structure.
- **Entry refinement:** Wait for micro-confluences (e.g., stop just outside breaker, enter on body closure) to avoid early fills.
- **Partial exits:** Michael took a partial at the "Event Horizon" level (the midpoint between two relative equal lows), reducing risk before the full target.
- **Missed trades:** Michael acknowledged missing an overnight move due to sleeping; the London open liquidated stops and he would have "been all over this" if awake. Lesson: trades aren't hindsight; review charts after sleep to understand what happened.
- **Chart management:** Don't load every annotation on your live chart; turn off daily levels while scalping 1m; otherwise clutter obscures structure. "It's not Netflix and chill around here."
- **Learning vs. observing:** Simply watching Michael's charts without replicating on your own charts is *observing*, not learning. Rebuild the analysis step-by-step on your own data.

## Glossary candidates
`Order block` — the open of the last large up-close candle(s); acts as a key level once breached on the downside.
`Consequent encroachment` — the wick extension beyond a candle's body; graded for rejection signals.
`Market Maker Sell Model` — a 7-candle/stage structure of accumulation, reversal, and distribution, culminating in a sharp sell-off.
`Fair Value Gap` — an imbalance between candle open/close, marked as buy-side or sell-side inefficiency; volume imbalances are part of this.
`Inversion FVG` — a fair value gap where price enters but the body stays on one side (bullish or bearish), signaling rejection.
`Bearish Breaker` — a short-term high, low, higher high (but lower than first high); used as a stop-loss reference and entry confluence.
`New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)` — the gap between previous day's RTH close and next day's RTH open; has consequent encroachment.
`Opening Range Gap (ORG)` — the daily range from 9:30 RTH open to 4:59 RTH close; 50% midpoint has high probability trade.
`Macro` — algorithmic time window (e.g., 10:50–11:10 ET) when institutional order flow has higher likelihood of executing turns.
`Relative equal lows` — two or more lows at approximately the same price level, often used as partial targets.
