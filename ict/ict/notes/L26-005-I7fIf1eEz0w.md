## Concepts

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The first FVG that forms after session open, marking a specific time milestone. At 9:33 AM ET (33 minutes after 9 AM open), the first gap forms and becomes a reference tool for later intraday moves.
- **How to identify:** Mark the time of session open (9:00 AM or 9:30 AM ET); identify the first candlestick gap after that timestamp.
- **How to use:** Use as a breakout trigger and to measure subsequent moves; price often uses it for continuation or rejection tests.
- **Example in this video:** First presented FVG at 9:33 AM ET; price used it to break into liquidity, tested the midpoint consequent encroachment, and used lower quadrant as a discount sensitivity area.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:15](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=255) "33 minutes after 9:00 a.m. Eastern time, this is your first gap that forms," [0:05:54](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=354) "midpoint consequent encroachment of first presented fair value gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Only the very first gap after open qualifies; not every gap thereafter.

### Opening Range Gap (Magnetic Attractor)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The high and low of the opening range (here, 9:30–11:00 AM for RTH); acts as a magnet attracting price repeatedly. Even if price fails to reach it on the first attempt, it will gravitate back to it.
- **How to identify:** Mark the opening range: for RTH open, use 9:30 ET and the first 1–2 hours of trading.
- **How to use:** Expect price to revisit opening range levels multiple times during the day; use as support, resistance, and target zones.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's RTH opening range gap low at ~18430; price drifted lower during NY lunch, then gravitated back up to the opening price (9:30 ET opening, anchored 30,708.75 on ES basis).
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:28](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=208) "today's regular trading hours opening range gap," [0:06:42](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=402) "So, while we didn't get to it here, look what it did. It gravitated right back up into it there."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Acts as a magnet even if initially missed; will be revisited intra-day.

### Session-by-Session Price Behavior Framework
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Define market behavior by session:
  - **London session (2–5 AM ET):** Often trends (directional).
  - **Morning session (7–9 AM, then 9:30–11 AM):** May consolidate, then reverse, then consolidate again, then break.
  - **NY lunch (11:30 AM–1:30 PM):** May drift in one direction (often lower), testing new lows, then rallying back.
- **How to identify:** Note the timestamp and session name from market calendars.
- **How to use:** Anticipate the type of move each session typically delivers; don't expect sustained trends during consolidation sessions.
- **Example in this video:** London (2–5 AM): trended. 7–9 AM: consolidation. 9:30–11 AM: reversal then consolidation then broke lower. 11:30–1:30 PM: drifted lower, tried again, rally back.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:01](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=61) starts session breakdown, [0:02:40](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=160) "entering into the 2-hour New York lunch."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not rigid; event-driven or gap-driven days may violate typical session patterns.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) as Extension Target
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The CE of prior gaps (especially opening range gaps) acts as an upside target during rallies. For example, the CE of Monday's RTH opening range gap is an extension target that price may aim for.
- **How to identify:** Identify a prior gap; mark its wick extremes (the consequent encroachment point).
- **How to use:** Use as an intraday rally target; expect price to gravitate toward it if conditions are bullish.
- **Example in this video:** Monday's RTH opening range gap CE (midpoint) is identified as a likely target if price continues higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:28](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=208) "I'd like to see it trade to consequent encroachment," [0:07:30](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=450) "Monday's regular trading hours opening range gap consequent encroachment in the midpoint."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** CE is aspirational (what ICT "likes to see"), not guaranteed; price must prove capacity to get there.

### Discount Sensitivity (Lower Quadrant of Opening Range)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The lower half of the opening range gap can act as a discount sensitivity area. When price trades into the lower quadrant, it signals weakness but also provides a zone where buyers may step in.
- **How to identify:** Identify the opening range gap; divide it into quarters; mark the lower quadrant (lower 50%).
- **How to use:** Price testing the lower quadrant of an opening range is a discount opportunity for long entries (if other conditions align); it's a friction zone.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday's RTH opening range gap's lower quadrant; price pulled back into it and used it as a support friction zone before rallying.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:54](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=354) "lower quadrant of today or Tuesday's regular trading hours opening range gap" used as discount sensitivity.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not a guaranteed bounce point; context (overall trend, liquidity above) must confirm.

### Liquidity Pools from Highs and Lows
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Each new high and each prior low holds resting liquidity. When price reaches a new high, that high becomes an anchor point for future buy-side liquidity; old lows hold sell-side liquidity.
- **How to identify:** Mark every swing high and every swing low on the chart.
- **How to use:** Use these as target zones; when price runs upward, look for highs ahead as resistance; when price runs downward, look for old lows as support.
- **Example in this video:** London session lows hold liquidity; Tuesday morning highs hold liquidity; these are identified as next salient levels.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:28](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=208) "buy side there" at opening range high, [0:07:30](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=450) "both of those are pools of liquidity that I think are going to be salient."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not every high/low holds liquidity; only those that represent swing turning points.

## Glossary candidates
- **First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)** — The first FVG to form after a session open (e.g., 9:33 AM on a 9:00 AM or 9:30 AM open).
- **Opening Range Gap** — High and low of the first 1–2 hours of a session; acts as a magnetic attractor throughout the day.
- **Consequent Encroachment (CE)** — Wick extreme or top/bottom of a gap; used as extension target or support/resistance.
- **Discount Sensitivity** — The lower half of an opening range gap; friction zone where buyers often step in.
- **Magnet Effect** — Price tendency to gravitate back to opening range levels repeatedly during a session, even if first attempt fails.
- **Liquidity Pool** — Resting buy-side (at highs) or sell-side (at lows) orders that price seeks to fill.
- **RTH** — Regular Trading Hours (9:30 ET to 4:00 PM ET for US equity futures).
