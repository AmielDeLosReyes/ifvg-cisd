### FOMC Day Trading Protocol
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** On FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee) days, expect choppy, manipulated price action before the 2:30 PM announcement and conference call. Trade only in the first hour (up to ~10:30 AM ET) or wait until after 4:00 PM ET when the true move begins (at 6:00 PM RTH open). Avoid mid-day trading when money is sitting on sidelines. Be neutral; do not predict the Fed's decision. If you do participate, treat it as observation only (tape reading / demo), not real money.
- **How to identify:** FOMC is announced ahead of time (typically monthly). Pre-announcement price action is jagged, with many reverses; no strong directional move until after the announcement.
- **How to use:** Trade micro-moves and observe only. Use the time to build tape-reading skill, not accumulate risk. If you see a clean setup, take it, but keep size small or trade demo. Post-FOMC (after 4:00 PM) will provide the real trading opportunity; wait for that if unsure.

### Regular Trading Hours Opening Range Gap (ORG) and Quadrants / Octants
- **Kind:** definition + use
- **What ICT says:** When US markets open at 9:30 AM ET, price often gaps relative to yesterday's close. This gap is the "opening range gap" (ORG). The gap divides into four quadrants (25% sections). Expect price to eventually fill the gap and test at least the lower quadrant (if gapped down) or upper quadrant (if gapped up). If price gaps down, expect initial trade up to ~25% of the gap (lower quadrant), then expect it to drop further below the gap (gap-and-go).
- **How to identify:** Mark the previous close, mark the open; draw the gap high/low. Divide into four equal segments (quadrants). Identify the octant (1/8) levels within.
- **How to use:** If gap down: expect run to lower quadrant, then run lower (below the gap). If gap up: expect run to upper quadrant, then expect potential reversal or continued run. On FOMC: use the gap as a reference structure; price may oscillate within the gap range, testing quadrants multiple times.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 29, 2026. Gap down at 9:30 (Friday's close was higher). Price traded up into the lower quadrant (did not reach 25% of gap), then came back down and was testing both lower and upper quadrants as market makers whipsawed retail.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=278) — opening range gap explained · [0:06:13](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=373) — octant and quadrant levels identified · [0:17:53](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=1073) — Friday's ORG as reference
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On FOMC days, the gap may not follow typical rules; be flexible. Upper octant and lower octant are the 1/8 marks; trading within these is precision price action.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) as Resistance / Foothold
- **Kind:** definition + example
- **What ICT says:** When a bullish FVG (gap on an up move, filled as support) is later approached from above, it becomes an inversion FVG (acts as resistance/ceiling). Price will respect the high of that iFVG and the consequent encroachment; bodies cannot penetrate the lower half without signaling weakness. An iFVG can also act as a "foothold" when approached from below (on a recovery), allowing price to spring higher.
- **How to identify:** Mark the original FVG; watch whether price later approaches from above (resistance) or below (foothold). Track whether bodies can touch/cross the consequent encroachment.
- **How to use:** Use iFVG high as a stop-loss level if going short (if it breaks, stop is hit). Use iFVG low as a foothold entry if going long (body touches low, then springs up). If bodies cannot return to CE on rallies, iFVG is being respected as strong resistance (bearish).
- **Example in this video:** NQ morning. A prior bullish FVG became an iFVG that bodies kept respecting on the way down; no bodies returned to touch the CE, indicating weakness and likelihood of continuation lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=518) — iFVG identified and used · [0:32:50](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=1970) — SIBI (BISI/SIBI) as inversion FVG · [0:42:17](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2537) — CE behavior and inversion
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On choppy days, iFVGs may be violated; expect price to test them multiple times before deciding to break them.

### Rock Climber Analogy for Price Action
- **Kind:** new coinage / metaphor
- **What ICT says:** Reading price action is like climbing a rock face. You look ahead and map out where the footholds (price support levels, order blocks, iFVG lows) and handholds (resistance levels, wicks you can "grab" to pull up) are. You do not try to climb the sheer vertical face; you use inefficiencies and structure to guide your path. This analogy teaches you to identify meaningful levels and not force moves where price is not offering purchase.
- **How to identify:** When looking at a chart, ask: "If I were climbing this, where would I place my feet (support) and hands (resistance)?" Order blocks, FVGs, wicks, and relative equal highs/lows are your climbing route.
- **How to use:** Use this mindset when reading tape. Do not demand price go a specific way; observe where it is likely to have "traction" (support for further movement). Identify the next foothold above/below and target it. Once price grabs that foothold (body touches and closes above/below), the next handhold becomes visible, and you can anticipate the next leg of the move.
- **Example in this video:** NQ morning. Price was climbing from the lower quadrant of the ORG; each SIBI, each wick, each order block was a foothold to climb higher toward the relative equal highs above.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:10](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=1810) — rock climber analogy introduced · [0:37:31](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2251) — footholds and handholds explained
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This analogy removes force and desperation; it teaches patience and observation.

### Relative Equal Highs / Lows as Liquidity Targets
- **Kind:** example only
- **What ICT says:** After a range-bound or choppy day, relative equal highs (prior swing highs repeated at similar price) are magnets on price. Market makers target them to run stops and accumulate / distribute. On FOMC mornings, these are used as reference levels for potential break-outs once the Fed announcement clears.
- **How to identify:** Mark intraday swing highs/lows; if multiple are within a few ticks, that level is a relative equal high/low.
- **How to use:** Target relative equal highs on up moves; target relative equal lows on down moves. These are confirmed liquidity removal points.
- **Example in this video:** NQ April 29. Relative equal highs from the open were at the target level; if price were to close above the upper quadrant and accelerate, it would head for those highs.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On choppy days, be flexible; price may test relative equal highs multiple times before breaking through.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE) as Boundary
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The CE is the first candle body that enters a PD array (FVG, order block, SIBI). It is a hard boundary. If price cannot bring bodies back to touch the CE on a retracement, it signals strength (bull move) or weakness (bear move, depending on direction). Track whether bodies are respecting or violating the CE.
- **How to identify:** Find the PD array high/low; identify the first body to enter; that extreme is the CE.
- **How to use:** Place stop loss just outside the CE. If CE is breached with the body (not just a wick), structure is broken and re-evaluate. Track CE behavior: repeated failures to return = strong directional move.
- **Example in this video:** NQ. iFVG had a CE; on retracements, bodies could not get back to the CE line, signaling weakness and likelihood of lower prices.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=518) — consequent encroachment identified · [0:37:31](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2251) — CE respecting and strength/weakness signals
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A wick touching CE and reversing is different from a body closing beyond CE; bodies are the true signal.

### Tape Reading as Meditative Practice & Confidence Building
- **Kind:** psychology / process
- **What ICT says:** Tape reading (observing candlesticks forming in real-time without trading) is a form of meditation and confidence-building. It conditions the mind to observe without stress, pattern-recognize, and retain information. Do not make it traumatic (force outcomes); make it fun, relaxing, with soothing music. This is how you develop subconscious pattern recognition.
- **How to identify:** When tape reading, observe without trade execution. Ask: "Is price doing what I expected? If not, why not?" Record the observation in a journal.
- **How to use:** Spend 1–2 hours daily doing tape reading without any trades. Learn to recognize PD arrays, order blocks, SIBI patterns, and relative equal highs/lows as they form. Build a model by repetition and observation, not by forcing trades.
- **Example in this video:** FOMC morning was ideal for tape reading; choppy, no clear direction, yet full of learning. ICT tape-read the whole thing, identifying footholds and analyzing why price was stuck (market makers had no reason to push hard pre-announcement).

### CFD vs Futures Equivalence
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** CFD markets (US 100, US 500) are not identical to futures (MNQ, ES, NQ) but they have corresponding structure. Time and price are slightly different (CFD fills may be off by a tick or two), but the logic of price action (FVGs, order blocks, relative equal highs/lows) is identical. If you cannot trade futures (e.g., non-US traders), you can trade CFD equivalents by transposing the levels from the futures chart to the CFD chart using time and price alignment.
- **How to identify:** Pull up both charts (futures and CFD) side-by-side; note when the same candle forms on both; measure levels on the futures chart; transfer those levels to the CFD chart.
- **How to use:** If trading CFDs, use the levels identified on the futures chart as reference. The logic is the same; execution mechanics are the same.
- **Example in this video:** ICT showed MNQ and US 100 side-by-side; price action was nearly identical. The inversion FVG, SIBI, and targets all aligned between the two.

### Live Data vs Market Replay
- **Kind:** rule / technical
- **What ICT says:** Never use market replay when teaching tape reading or analyzing live execution. Market replay changes the visual display: execution boxes disappear, date/time stamps vanish, the clean border disappears. This makes it harder to verify the time of execution and candle formation. ICT does not use market replay for this reason.
- **How to identify:** In TradingView, market replay mode hides execution details and changes the UI. Live mode keeps all UI elements visible.
- **How to use:** Always use live data. If analyzing a past session, use recorded video of live data, not market replay.
- **Example in this video:** ICT toggled market replay on/off to show the difference; when on, the execution boxes disappeared. This is why he uses live data only.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Always verify you're using live mode when watching price action in real-time or analyzing recordings.

### Change in State of Delivery (CISD)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** A change in state of delivery occurs when the nature of price movement shifts. For example, when a larger, protracted candle is followed by a smaller candle, that boundary between them is a CISD. It marks a shift in the rate of delivery or urgency of the move.
- **How to identify:** Look at candle sizes; when a large candle (fast delivery) is followed by a smaller candle (slower delivery), or vice versa, mark that boundary.
- **How to use:** CISD can mark the top of an order block or a transition point. Use it as a reference level for entries / stops.
- **Example in this video:** NQ price action: ICT identified a CISD between a large candle and subsequent smaller ones, marking a potential shift in momentum.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Neutral bias on uncertain days:** Do not come in with a hard bias on FOMC days. Observe, tape-read, and let price action guide you.
- **Tape reading as foundational:** 1–2 hours daily tape reading builds pattern recognition faster than trading. It is meditative and confidence-building.
- **Enjoying the journey:** Make tape reading fun (soothing music, relaxed state). If you make it traumatic or forced, you will not retain patterns. Subconscious learning happens in calm, focused states.
- **Personal responsibility:** "Error on the operator." When a trade goes wrong, own it 100%. Do not blame the market or look for external reasons. This develops maturity and control.
- **Indifference to outcomes:** The goal is to develop indifference to whether each individual trade works or fails. This comes from repeated exposure (tape reading) and conditioning. Once indifferent, you can execute with conviction without fear.
- **Avoiding arrogance:** When calls work out (as they did on NQ April 29), do not get "sugar rushed" or arrogant. ICT explicitly warns against this; it leads to overconfidence and poor decision-making.
- **Model development:** Your first model may not be your career-maker. But you must start somewhere. Repeated observation reveals patterns that become your personal model. This takes time and experience; do not rush it.
- **Journal honesty:** Log every observation, every tape-read session, every trade. Be honest but not self-critical. You are building a feedback loop, not a punishment journal.
- **Flexibility on choppy days:** When price action is choppy (like FOMC), do not force setups. Observe, adapt, and take only the cleanest micro-trades if you participate at all.

## Glossary candidates
`Opening Range Gap (ORG)` — the gap created at the 9:30 AM ET market open relative to the previous day's close; divided into quadrants and octants for precision targeting.
`Quadrant / Octant` — divisions of the opening range gap (quadrants = 25% sections; octants = 12.5% sections).
`Consequent Encroachment (CE)` — the first candlestick body to enter a PD array; a hard boundary that signals strength/weakness if violated.
`Tape reading` — observing candlesticks forming in real-time without taking trades; a meditative practice that builds pattern recognition and confidence.
`Rock climber analogy` — a mindset approach to price action: identify footholds (support), handholds (resistance), and map a logical path up or down the price structure.
`Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)` — a previously-filled FVG that now acts as resistance (if approached from above) or a foothold (if approached from below).
`Change in State of Delivery (CISD)` — a shift in the rate/urgency of price movement, marked by a change in candle size or directional conviction.
`Gap-and-go` — when price gaps and immediately moves in the direction of the gap, signaling strong directional bias (e.g., gap down + quick move lower = gap-and-go down).
`Footholds / Handholds` — price action levels (order blocks, FVGs, wicks) that support further movement upward (footholds) or downward (handholds).
