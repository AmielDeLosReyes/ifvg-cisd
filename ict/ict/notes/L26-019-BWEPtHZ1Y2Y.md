## Summary
This is ICT's starting-point lecture for new students: how to prepare for a trading week and how to build a chart that tells you where price is likely to go before any entry is considered. He covers the economic-calendar filter and which days a beginner should and should not trade (NFP week rules), then marks previous-month highs/lows, grades a daily BISI/SIBI range in eighths, labels liquidity, and sets up layouts so the chart stays uncluttered. He then watches NQ live from ~8:40 to 10:30 ET without taking real trades, showing step by step how he arrives at a draw on liquidity, where a partial belongs ("event horizon"), how wicks and iFVGs act as mile markers, how the 20-minute hourly macro and the 9:30 RTH opening range gap shape expectations, and how to learn by observation and journaling rather than by trading real or funded money. The recurring message: know what you are looking for, why it should be there, and what would invalidate it.

## Concepts

### Weekly preparation and economic calendar filter  (aliases: "news drivers", weekend prep)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**

  - Use the Forex Factory calendar (he blocks ads by zooming). Toggle only the currency relevant to what you trade; for NQ/futures that is USD only. If trading a forex pair, toggle the currencies of that pair.
  - Impact filter: low impact (yellow) and gray (speeches etc.) are not a concern; the orange (medium) and red (high) folder events are "where the big waves of interference with normal price delivery occur". Note the day and the time of each one.

  - He says he does not care about the actual numbers because the data is later revised (he considers it unreliable); what matters is that the 8:30 report creates volatility.
- **How to use:** see the next two concepts for the trade/no-trade rules.

### NFP-week rules for new students and the "Monday" clarification  (aliases: day-of-week tendencies)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - NFP is the first Friday of the month at 8:30. The volatility is "like a flame" and new traders are the moths.
  - Teach students to avoid trading Thursday and Friday of NFP week, and on Wednesday to be done by 11:00 ET. Monday through Wednesday-until-11:00 is the "safe spot" for finding setups.
  - Monday of an NFP week: be in your seat and expect to find a setup, because traders will want to take their profit early in the week to avoid Thursday/Friday. "Every Monday of non-farm payroll [week]" is a day to be drilled in.
  - Any other Monday is "potluck": it can be good or not, depends on experience. A new trader should not be demanding a setup every Monday.
  - He explicitly rejects the rumor that "ICT says never trade Mondays"; the above is the real statement. He, and experienced students, can find a Monday setup every week, but he is being practical for beginners.
  - Thursday of NFP week is a day a new student should be studying price, not engaging it. A student not yet ready for even a demo account should not touch Thursday/Friday.
  - Why patience matters early: habits formed at the start tend to stick; rushing and being "toxic" in reaction when wrong (which is normal at the beginning) can lead to talking yourself out of trading altogether.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=191) Monday of NFP week · [0:06:25](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=385) avoid Thu/Fri, Wed until 11:00 · [0:07:12](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=432) the "never trade Mondays" myth

### Marking previous-month high / low / close on every timeframe  (aliases: monthly high/low, HTF levels)
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the monthly chart note the previous month's high, low and where it settled (the monthly close is only known after the Friday-session end of the month; he says the month closes at 5:59 pm ET). Label them (e.g., "July monthly high", "July monthly low"). Very high timeframe labels: bold, black, right-justified.
  - Set each line's visibility to show on every timeframe: every minute setting (and the seconds tab if you trade sub-1-minute), hourly, daily, weekly. Then no matter what lower timeframe you drop to, the macro levels are visible and you do not need to flip between monthly and weekly charts.
  - Copy a line by hovering until the end handle appears, hold Ctrl, and drag; then double-click to rename (July became August).
  - He does not bother to mark the monthly open: a monthly open eventually gets "baked in" to a new week opening gap (NWOG), and NWOG is what he tracks.
  - A buy-side liquidity label is unnecessary on a monthly high ("you already know that is the high of the month") but he labels the monthly low as sell-side liquidity.
- **How to use:** these are the first levels of the draw-on-liquidity map; a monthly high implies resting buy stops above it (breakout buyers, and swing shorts with stops above).
- **Timestamps:** [0:10:53](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=653) previous month high/low · [0:13:21](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=801) show on all timeframes · [0:15:49](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=949) why no monthly open
- **Nuance / caveats:** Contradicts nothing, but explicitly deprioritizes the monthly open versus NWOG.

### Chart layouts / workspaces and labeling conventions
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Keep a "naked" working layout (the one he teaches and executes from) plus separate saved layouts for specific lists: new week opening gaps, new day opening gaps, first-presented FVGs, and a layout with only RTH opening range gaps and a running tally. Toggle between layouts instead of putting everything on one chart (clutter and overload).
  - Anything you only need once, write down the numbers on a notepad.
  - Right-justify labels for higher-timeframe levels. If a label is left-justified on a daily chart, on a 15-minute chart it sits far off-screen and you see a price on the axis but do not know what it is.
  - Lines anchored to the right and extended to the right; otherwise they disappear as new data prints. Save the layout after editing.
  - For intraday or minor levels, place the label just above where it is anchored (not right-justified) so you can find it by compressing the chart (drag the time axis).
  - Colors: buy-side liquidity in blue, sell-side liquidity in deep red, always the same so viewers know what he is highlighting even without understanding his language. Tiny liquidity levels can be drawn as short trend lines.
  - Hierarchy of line thickness: higher-timeframe-anchored lines should be thicker, but not the very boldest (daily-anchored: one notch below the thickest; weekly: same logic) so they stand out among lower-timeframe noise.
  - Add the context to the label (e.g., "daily SIBI") so you keep things framed.
  - Do not show the high/low or midpoint tool lines he doesn't use; each extra line is something to babysit or explain.
- **Timestamps:** [0:18:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1091) layouts · [0:26:14](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1574) right-justify labels · [0:33:09](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1989) line thickness by timeframe

### Dealing range, premium/discount and equilibrium on the daily chart
- **Kind:** definition / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - First order of business on the daily: where are we relative to the most recent range? Anchor range lines to the highest high so they are visible on the daily (he dislikes overhang).
  - Example: the July high-to-low range's equilibrium is the midpoint; price (Aug 31) is in the upper half of July's range, but in the middle of August's range, slightly in discount (just below that midpoint).
  - Look for where liquidity sits in close proximity to current price.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Sept contract (NQU2026) daily: July monthly high above, August low below; price oscillating around the August midpoint, inside a daily SIBI/BISI zone (below).

### Grading a daily PD array in eighths (quadrants and octants)  (aliases: fib grading, "octant")
- **Kind:** refinement / new coinage (octant)
- **What ICT says:**
  - He shades the daily "buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency" (BISI; caption "buy side of balance sell side efficiency") zone gray, from a candle's low down to the low of a volume imbalance, and grades it with fib levels in eight equal parts.
  - A quadrant is a quarter of the range; an octant is half of a quadrant (12.5%). The first line from the top is the 0.125 level (upper octant); the next is 0.25 (first quadrant line); 0.5 is halfway.
  - He turns off the extension and does not show the midpoint line while building it (to avoid student confusion about extra lines); the midpoint line colored black for contrast, the other levels dark but not black.
  - Price tends to use these levels as stepping stones: it traded sideways, then used the daily gray box's upper octant.
- **Example in this video:** 0.125 level = 29,451 ("the first line you would come to"); 0.5 midpoint = 29,211.25; the 0.25 (lower quadrant) line = 29,371 (derived: caption calls it "the lower quadrant at 29,371"). Price came down to the 0.125 and 0.25 lines the same morning.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:57](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1677) shading and grading · [0:45:46](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2746) the 0.125 and 0.5 numbers · [0:53:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3195) "upper octant" as a target
- **Nuance / caveats:** If price closes above the top of the daily array, that changes the whole interest in dropping into it (stated as the invalidation).

### Suspension block  (aliases: SIBI with volume imbalance at its high and low)
- **Kind:** definition (as ICT describes it here; caption quality limited)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (caption "cibby") that has a volume imbalance (two bodies not touching) at its high and its low is a "suspension block".
  - It sits above price here and acts as a premium array; he shades it red and gives the lines a red hue.
  - He uses the volume imbalance at its top as a place a short would have been taken; price trading into the lower half of it was "the lower half of this suspension block".
- **How to identify:** a daily SIBI whose boundaries include volume imbalances (gaps between bodies) at both ends. (Exact geometry not fully clear from captions.)
- **Example in this video:** the red daily SIBI/suspension block above market, with the gray BISI zone below; price is between them, "waiting and seeing" whether it will use the high as a high or stay in the upper half.

### RTH Opening Range Gap (previous settlement) and the half-gap statistic  (aliases: RTH OG, regular trading hours opening range gap)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Before the 9:30 open, draw a line at the previous regular-trading-hours settlement (Friday's close here), labeled "RTH opening range gap settlement". Keep chart time on New York time.
  - At 9:30: if the open is above that price, the line becomes "RTH OG low"; if the open is below it, the line becomes "RTH OG high". So the word "settlement" turns into low or high depending on the opening price.
  - If price opens below the settlement it is a discount gap (premium gap if it opens above); the gap generally tries to fill.
  - Stated statistic: if there is a gap, half the gap gets filled by 10:00 AM about 70% of the time (the first 30 minutes after the open).
  - Plan: draw a fib from the open to the settlement; half gap is the 50% point of that range. Alternative path: price first runs the nearby sell-side (a minor low), then tries half gap, and a trader might leave a runner for full closure.
  - Keep a template/workspace that holds only RTH opening range gaps and a running tally.
- **Example in this video:** Friday's RTH settlement was about 29,487.5 ("487 half"); at ~8:39 price was ~29,420 (caption says "47 handles higher" — digits unclear). At 9:30 price opened below settlement (discount gap) and rallied immediately through the gap, past full closure ("gap closure and more"), heading to the high of the daily BISI box.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:46](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2506) annotation rule · [1:36:58](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5818) 70% half-gap by 10:00 · [1:42:51](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=6171) the 9:30 move
- **Nuance / caveats:** Two draws "in contention" for the opening range (9:30–10:00) were: the minor sell-side low and the RTH OG high. A new student will not know which one; the job is to observe which one price goes to at 9:30.

### Draw on liquidity — step-by-step mapping  (aliases: DOL)
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:**
  - "Flesh out the chart" with no entries: know what you are looking for, why it should be there, which levels are important, and what would change it. This is "the first part of what you are supposed to learn".
  - Layers used: monthly high/low, daily range equilibrium, daily BISI/SIBI zone graded in eighths, relative equal highs on the hourly, minor sell-side lows, RTH OG settlement.
  - Draw hierarchy: price moves from the nearest key level to the next (e.g., from the daily gray box's upper octant down toward the 0.25 line, then a minor sell side). He wants to see the candles "book" with interest to keep going lower, not just reach the level.
  - The more PD arrays and levels you can anchor to the daily chart, the more probable the setup; "probable" does not mean guaranteed or no losing trades; it means never feeling confused. If your read is wrong, that is "a transactional operating error" and the operator owns it.
- **Example in this video:** at 8:39 price sits inside the daily gray zone after a 8:09 buy-side raid (relative equal highs taken on Friday at 10:00, then a drop). He labels the lowest low as sell-side liquidity (deep red, potential draw). He tracks whether price will reach the 0.25 level (29,371) and then the minor sell side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:53:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3195) · [0:54:05](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3245) "how to arrive at draw on liquidity step by step" · [0:56:43](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3403) why setups are reliable only if you know why price should go there

### Big prominent wicks: consequent encroachment as a "mile marker"  (aliases: wick CE, premium sensitivity)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Whenever a big, prominent wick forms, grade it in half (its consequent encroachment) and measure what price does around that halfway point. "Wicks are wonderful little mile markers for measuring continuation."
  - If short: you want to see bodies stay below the wick's midpoint; the upper half is "premium sensitivity" and, preferably, price does not trade there at all. A wick that price has moved below (even if the wick itself is the lower portion of an individual candle) is a premium array relative to where price is now.
  - A close above the midpoint is a concern, not a reason to bail out completely; it means more information to manage.
  - The more candles and distance price gains away from where an entry would have been, the more it builds an "onside narrative" (encouragement versus fear).
- **Example in this video:** after a lower low in the macro, he grades the big wick above it, watches the body stay below, and later notes price took the buy side briefly but the body "stayed inside" (not a close above).
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2895) · [0:58:20](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3500) wicks as mile markers · [0:59:08](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3548) premium sensitivity

### Macro (hourly 20-minute window)  (aliases: algorithmic time window)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Every hour: the last 10 minutes of the hour ending and the first 10 minutes of the new hour (a 20-minute window, e.g., 8:50–9:10, 9:50–10:10). In the video the 8:50–9:10 macro is marked between two vertical lines.
  - A macro gives no directional information at all. It only gives a time at which price should start "spooling": a decisive run toward inefficiency or liquidity, "like a micro start of a new race". You want to see it animate in the direction your analysis expects.
  - If price consolidates through the macro (as seen here), that is problematic for the idea. In recent years he has observed that the consolidation is often followed by a burst right after the 10-minute mark of the new hour (e.g., 10 minutes after 9, 10, 11, 12); he calls this a recent observation, not something he saw in prior decades. Reasons he offers: manipulation, news-driven volatility spikes.
  - If a move expected in the macro does not arrive, that is cause for concern; lower your stop.
- **Example in this video:** 8:50–9:10 macro on NQ 1m: price fell ~half an hour, ranged inside the macro, made a new low around 9:00 (a ~55–60 handle drop from the volume imbalance), consolidated, then took the highs and animated upward after 9:10 (hypothetical stop out).
- **Timestamps:** [1:02:46](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3766) macro defined · [1:05:12](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3912) no directional info · [1:29:37](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5377) consolidation then burst
- **Nuance / caveats:** The "last 10 minutes of the hour + first 10 of the next" definition is stated in his own words here; the "burst after consolidation" idea is explicitly labeled a recent personal observation.

### Event horizon (partial-profit level)  (aliases: halfway to the draw)
- **Kind:** new coinage / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Grade the distance between the most recent low (price just made) and the next draw-level (e.g., a daily quadrant line). The midpoint of that is the "event horizon" and the place to take a partial. "How can I make sure I take something away and reward myself for participation?"
  - For a beginner on a demo or paper trade: if you hold three micros, take one off at that level; the partial "pays" the transaction and removes the need to be right about the final target.
  - Rationale: price has been moving continuously one way for about half an hour with no significant run on the short-term opposite liquidity, so halfway is a reasonable place.
- **Example in this video:** hypothetical short from the volume imbalance in the daily SIBI above; low taken; draw level 29,371; partial at the halfway point (event horizon). After that, as price retraced, he watched the wick midpoint and rolled the stop down.
- **Timestamps:** [1:08:22](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4102) · [1:09:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4151) "event horizon" · [1:10:00](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4200) three micros · [1:27:06](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5226) answering "partials are stupid"
- **Nuance / caveats:** He counters people who say taking partials is stupid: here two partials plus a lowered stop paid roughly 30 handles (stop lowered to 29,412 locks ~30 handles from the hypothetical entry) and later "32 more handles" (caption).

### Trade management: partial, then roll the stop to fund the position
- **Kind:** rule / management
- **What ICT says:**
  - Once a partial is taken, roll the stop down (for a short) to just above the nearest short-term high; if stopped out, the trade has been "funded" by the partial and you were paid for your time.
  - Managing the psychology of holding is tied to reward and consequence: we are motivated by reward and fearful of consequence (what if I held too long, didn't take profit). A stop lowered after a partial removes the need to be right about the final target.
  - If you leave the stop at the original level and ignore what price is doing, you only have a small unrealized profit and live in fear ("a horror movie"); instead, take a shot at the target, then protect the exit ("Top Gun").
  - If a retracement closes above the wick/array that "should never" be closed above, that is a concern: partially exit or cancel the transaction; but the decision is about information, not panic.
  - He teaches students not to depend on being right; they should aim for consistent execution of a model and measure its statistical behavior in their own hands.
- **Example in this video:** hypothetical short from the volume imbalance; partial near event horizon; stop moved to ~29,412; price retraced into a minor buy-side run and stopped the hypothetical remainder with a profit.
- **Timestamps:** [1:11:36](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4296) · [1:12:25](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4345) reward vs consequence · [1:26:17](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5177) stop at 29,412 locks ~30 handles · [1:31:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5475) Top Gun analogy

### Inversion FVG and "immediate rebalance" used as feedback  (aliases: iFVG, immediate rebalance, discount/premium sensitivity)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Watch whether a PD array "continuously supports the idea of the transaction you're in or watching". A bearish FVG (formed in a bearish market) is a premium array: price should respect its lower half as premium sensitivity and push lower; if instead price trades above it, comes back down into it and respects it from above, it has inverted and now acts as a discount array (bullish iFVG), and the low beneath it becomes an intermediate-term low (possibly the session/day/week low).
  - If price closes above the gap but cannot hold, and price then trades through it, that gap has lost its sensitivity.
  - Two PD arrays nested together (an FVG's consequent encroachment lying on a wick's CE) give "immediate intel" on the strength or continuity of a run.
  - Immediate rebalance: after a big up-close candle that cleared the RTH settlement high, if the very next candle (or the one after) trades back to the high of that candle with no volume imbalance or FVG left behind, that is one of his strongest PD arrays because the feedback is immediate. He says it is consistent and that nothing beats it: narrative from market structure plus a clear draw plus an unhideable footprint.
  - Journal it: whenever you see an immediate rebalance, record it.
- **How to use:** In the hypothetical 9:30 short: after the big up-close candle that cleared the RTH settlement, watch whether the next candle reaches the high of that up-close candle; if it trades there, sell short; the stop one tick above the mean threshold (50%) of the up-close candle (caption unclear on exactly which candle); targets the sell side below; take 50–65% off below the low with a partial halfway between that low and the next level, and leave the rest for the sell side.
- **Example in this video:** at 9:30 price opened at a discount and rose through the RTH OG high, with a bearish FVG on the 1m acting as sensitivity; price then dropped, took the low, closed back above the wick, wicked and rejected. The immediate-rebalance candle, then the sell-side delivery ("look how fast it was").
- **Timestamps:** [1:16:05](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4565) iFVG discussion · [1:18:30](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4710) nested PD arrays · [1:56:55](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=7015) immediate rebalance · [1:57:44](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=7064) stop, size
- **Nuance / caveats:** He also says if the up-gap is too large in points for your size, use a micro; if a micro can't pay you, "you're a gambler". Some of the candle references are ambiguous in captions.

### Short / intermediate / long-term highs and lows
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - Significant runs form at long-term or intermediate-term highs and lows, not short-term ones. He labels a short-term low, short-term high, an intermediate-term high, etc. on the chart. Part of the skill is knowing how they should form, at what time (inside the macro) and halfway between targets.
  - A low formed by an inverted FVG getting respected after the macro can be an intermediate-term low (and the session/day/week low).
  - Stop hunts for people trailing stops below lows are expected after a free run: "How is it that they get a free ride and no adversity? It's not likely."

### Learning method: observation lab / tape reading and journaling
- **Kind:** process / psychology
- **What ICT says:**
  - Do this every day with no risk: map the chart, state expectations ("it should do this, shouldn't do that"), then watch how price books; this is how you build a skill set. It is not copy-trading: "watching trades live in front of you is entertainment".
  - Screenshot the inflection points in his videos (e.g., the candle that closed and the next that opened), type your own notes on why price should have behaved that way, and keep them in your journal.
  - For people with day jobs: screen-record a 1-minute chart all day (he mentions Ctrl+Alt+R on Windows, unsure), then in the evening replay it, pause and state what should happen, unpause; do it for the first hour (9:30–10:30).
  - "Not market replay" — he is using the live recording approach.
  - Don't take funded-account challenges or demo-trade with pressure early: "That's all gambling"; no one gives a final exam on day one. You need enough experience before you can measure performance.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Weekly prep: Saturday evening or Sunday afternoon; calendar filtered to your currency; note red/orange folders and their times.
- NFP week for new students: avoid Thursday and Friday; Wednesday finish by 11:00 ET; Monday of NFP week = be at the screen for a setup; other Mondays are hit or miss.
- 10:00 news: wait 30 minutes after the 9:30 open, let news hit, work in its wake.
- NFP/jobs report at 8:30, first Friday of the month.
- Monthly levels: previous month's high/low/close labeled; visible on every timeframe; labels right-justified; bold black for very high timeframe.
- Daily range graded in eighths: 0.125 (upper octant), 0.25 (quadrant), 0.5 (equilibrium); levels cited 29,451 / 29,371 / 29,211.25.
- RTH OG: settlement line pre-open; at 9:30 relabel "RTH OG high" if open below, "RTH OG low" if open above; half the gap fills by 10:00 about 70% of the time.
- Macro: last 10 minutes of the hour + first 10 minutes of the next (20 minutes); gives timing, not direction.
- Opening range: 9:30–10:00.
- Partials: halfway between the most recent low and the next draw ("event horizon"); beginner example: 1 of 3 micros; hypothetical 50–65% off below the low.
- Stop: just above the nearest short-term high after a partial; in the immediate rebalance case, one tick above the 50% (mean threshold) of the up-close candle.
- Handles quoted: about 55–60 handles from the volume imbalance; ~30 handles locked at stop 29,412; "32 more handles".

## Process, risk & psychology
- Patience and discipline early; early habits stick; being wrong at first is normal and should not push you out.
- You are not aiming to be right; aim for consistently following a model and measure its probabilities yourself ([1:12:25](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4345)).
- Probable setups require knowing why price should go there; mistakes are the operator's own errors ([0:57:32](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3452)).
- Take a partial to pay yourself; manage the stop to avoid fear-based holding ([1:30:26](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5426)).
- Risk: if your size makes a stop too big, drop to micros; if you can't accept micro profits you are gambling ([1:57:44](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=7064)).
- Secondary income is a skill that takes time; not promised to everyone ([1:27:54](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5274)).
- Journal the observation points and annotate them yourself.

## Glossary candidates
`BISI` — buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (caption "buy side of balance sell side efficiency"); a bullish FVG-type array.
`SIBI` — sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (captions "cibby", "civvy"); bearish FVG-type array.
`Suspension block` — a SIBI with volume imbalances at its high and low (as described here).
`Octant` — one eighth (12.5%) of a graded range; a quadrant is a quarter.
`Event horizon` — midpoint between the latest low and the next draw level; the partial-taking zone.
`RTH opening range gap settlement` — previous regular-trading-hours close line, relabeled high/low at 9:30.
`Immediate rebalance` — price trades back into the up-close candle's high on the very next candle with no imbalance left.
`Macro` — hourly algorithmic window: last 10 min of an hour plus first 10 min of the next.
`Mean threshold` — 50% level of a candle/array.
`Transactional operating error` — a mistake by the operator, to be owned.
`Premium sensitivity` — a wick or gap whose upper half should not be traded through in a bearish run.
