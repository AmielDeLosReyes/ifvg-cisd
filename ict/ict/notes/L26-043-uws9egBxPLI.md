### Octant Qualification of PD Arrays
- **Kind:** rule + example
- **What ICT says:** A PD array (gap, order block, SIBI, BISI, etc.) is only valid/relevant if it sits at a specific octant of the pre-market dealing range. If a gap sits at the lower octant, consequent encroachment, or upper quadrant, that's why it matters. If it sits randomly between octants, it has less significance. The octant anchors the gap to the algorithmic grid, proving the market is following the grid, not random price action.
- **How to identify:** (1) Identify the gap, (2) Measure where it sits relative to the octants you've marked, (3) If it aligns with an octant, it's qualified.
- **How to use:** Entry/exit filter. Only trade gaps that are octant-qualified. Ignore gaps that fall between octants.
- **Example in this video:** "This fair value gap is valid because it's right here on the lowest octant of that dealing range." Later: "This SIBI touches the consequent encroachment level"—both are octant-qualified, so both are traded.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=672) — identifying octant placement; [0:13:37](https://youtu.ye/uws9egBxPLI?t=817) — qualifying gaps by octant; [0:16:01](https://youtu.ye/uws9egBxPLI?t=961) — SIBI at octant = institutional entry

### Volume Imbalance Validity Rules
- **Kind:** rule + refinement
- **What ICT says:** When identifying a SIBI or BISI (sell-side imbalance / buy-side imbalance), the candlestick that creates the volume imbalance must be selected carefully. If there's a volume imbalance at the high (one side has no volume), use that candle to anchor the gap. If there's a volume imbalance at the low, use the other candle. This detail matters because it determines whether the gap is valid for entry/exit.
- **How to identify:** Look at consecutive candles; if one has an unfilled space at its high/low, mark that candle as the anchor.
- **How to use:** Gap validity. Only use the "correct" anchoring candle; using the wrong one invalidates the setup.
- **Example in this video:** ICT shows a BISI and notes: "There's no volume imbalance at the low, so we don't need to worry about picking a different candle. This one here, it is." This precise selection makes the gap tradable.

### Measuring Gap (0.5 Fibonacci Projection as Daily Target)
- **Kind:** technique + example
- **What ICT says:** When you have a range (pre-market high to low), measure it with Fibonacci. The 0.5 level is the halfway point. If price exceeds the 0.5 level on the way up (or down), it's likely to continue to the opposite extreme (prior high/low or a new high). The 0.5 acts as a measuring gap: once breached, it signals continuation.
- **How to identify:** Apply Fib from pre-market low to high; mark the 0.5 level.
- **How to use:** Continuation confirmation. If price breaks above 0.5 while rallying, it will likely reach or exceed the prior high. Use this to set daily targets.

### Counting Bodies, Not Wicks (Order Flow Truth)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** When identifying swing highs/lows or confirming direction, count the bodies of consecutive candlesticks, not the wicks. Bodies represent real order flow; wicks are noise/tests. If you count a series of candles with wicks up but bodies in lower half, that's bearish (bodies tell the truth; wicks lie).
- **Example in this video:** "Count the bodies, not the wicks. Every one of these is a swing high." Shows a sequence of candles; the bodies form the swing pattern, not the wicks.

## Glossary candidates
`Time-price grid` — (ICT coinage) a coordinate system where time (7:00, 8:30, 8:44, etc.) is the horizontal axis and price octants (divisions of the pre-market range) are the vertical axis. Price action references this grid repeatedly; intersections often align with key levels.

`Octant qualification` — a PD array (gap, order block) is only relevant if it sits at a specific octant (division) of the pre-market dealing range. Arrays between octants have lower significance.

`Measuring gap` — a gap (usually at 0.5 Fibonacci) that, once breached, signals continuation to the opposite extreme. Used to project daily highs/lows.

`Volume imbalance validity` — the rule that a SIBI or BISI is valid only if anchored to the correct candlestick (the one where the imbalance occurs). Using the wrong candle invalidates the gap.

`Macro time` — algorithmic window (e.g., 8:50–9:10 ET) when reversals or momentum shifts are historically likely. Also used for news times (8:30 ET).

`Micro contract` — throwing a small position (1 contract, or a micro) into a level to gather real-time order flow feedback before scaling into a full position. Used for intelligence, not primary trading.

`Tossing in` — entering a position (usually small) as a test or for market intelligence, with the expectation of adding if confirmed or exiting if wrong.

`Grid intersection` — the point where a specific time (from the time-price grid) meets a specific octant (price level). Price action tends to align with these intersections.
