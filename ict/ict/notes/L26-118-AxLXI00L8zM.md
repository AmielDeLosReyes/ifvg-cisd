## Concepts

### Contract Rollover (Rollover Week)
- **Kind:** definition / process
- **What ICT says:** During rollover from one contract month to another (e.g., March to June), there is an artificial influx of buying and selling as traders exit the old front month and enter the new one. This participation is "net sum zero" (no real buying or selling pressure), but algorithms and manual intervention can paint a fake narrative of price direction. Volume imbalances and PD arrays bleed over from old contract to new. During rollover, keep position sizes light, expect to be wrong, and don't try to aggressively trade.
- **How to identify:** When a contract month is approaching expiration (e.g., March delivery ending), rollover occurs; trading activity on the new front month (June) begins.
- **How to use:** During rollover weeks, let volume be very light; synchronize with the new contract rather than trying to build equity. Watch PD arrays and price action for context, but don't assume normal price momentum. After rollover week ends, the contract becomes "pristine" and normal trading resumes.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March-to-June rollover on March 17, 2026; price ran straight up from market open, leaving relative equal lows. ICT collected levels and tape-read but did not actively trade.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:37](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=97), [0:12:05](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=725), [0:22:22](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1342), [0:23:09](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1389)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rollover creates artifacts (false price signals); similar dynamics occur after FOMC or nonfarm payroll. During these events, prioritize staying synchronized over profit-taking.

### High Probability vs. Low Probability Setup
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** A high-probability setup is when price action is "so obvious, absolutely obvious" that it will go one direction—the order flow is clean, bodies validate bias, PD arrays are being respected. You can call "Here is the level I'm looking for. This is what I'm doing." A low-probability scenario is when you can determine both sides of the marketplace; if both directions are equally possible, it's low probability. During low probability, sit still and maintain interest in levels without taking action.
- **How to identify:** Check if the price action respects the PD arrays, if bodies are validating the bias (bullish or bearish), if there is a clean signature. If you can make a case for both bullish and bearish, it's low probability.
- **How to use:** In low probability, don't trade. Wait for a higher-conviction setup or clear feedback. Sit still. This is a core discipline: "Knowing when not to do something is important."
- **Example in this video:** March 17, 2026 pre-10:00 AM: ICT was in low probability—price could run to the gap high or drop into first-presented FVG. Both were possible; therefore, no trade. Sat still until the economic news (pending home sales at 10:00 ET) provided clarity.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Knowing when not to do something is important. Like I could do a whole 50 to 60 video series... but that's the playlist that keeps you from becoming insolvent."

### First Presented Fair Value Gap (FPFVG)
- **Kind:** entry model / definition
- **What ICT says:** The first FVG that forms after the opening of a session (e.g., at 9:32 on March 17). It's the gap between two candlesticks in the first moments of the open. Use it as a target if price is above it (expect it to drop into it) or as a reference for inversion mechanics if price trades above it. Discount sensitivity exists in the upper half; bodies should stay out of the upper half to maintain bearish bias.
- **How to identify:** Look at the opening candle and the first few candles; identify the gap (close-to-open separation).
- **How to use:** If price is above FPFVG and you're bearish, target into it. If price enters the upper half, watch body placement; if bodies stay below consequent encroachment of that FVG, continuation lower is likely.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m on March 17: opened at 9:30, first presented FVG was identified at 9:32. Price traded above it initially, then dipped into the upper half. When bodies failed to go into the upper half and stayed compressed, it signaled intention to break back down into FPFVG. "About 25 handles as a nice little bread and butter setup from here down into first presented fair value gap."
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:01](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1561), [0:30:24](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1824), [0:40:11](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=2411)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** On a session open, the FPFVG is often the first target; treat it as a high-probability delivery point if the bias is validated.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (Revisited)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** When price trades above a sell-side FVG (upper half), consider it as a potential inversion FVG if price later wants to trade back down. It requires specific mechanics: bodies must NOT close in the upper half (discount sensitivity zone); if it does go up there, it must demonstrate reluctance and lack of conviction. Then, on a break lower, the first touch is an entry.
- **How to identify:** Price goes into upper half of FVG with weak body closes; next drop lower is iFVG.
- **Example in this video:** If NQ trades above and bumps the high of FPFVG, it can still be an iFVG setup for lower prices if bodies refuse to close above consequent encroachment.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:59](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1919), [0:40:58](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=2458)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The distinction between "just touching upper half" and "invalidating the bias" depends on body placement and momentum—if bodies are weak, it can still be bearish.

### Relative Equal Lows / Highs
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Two price lows (or highs) that are very close to each other (within a "handle" or two), indicating a high-probability point for a raid (move in the opposite direction) or at least a significant level. On March 17, the market opened at 9:30, ran higher, then left a low at 24,962.00 and another low at 24,964.25—"relatively equal lows." This made the level a "high probability candidate for a raid or at least in the very minimum consider this a relative equal low because the low to the right of this one is slightly higher."
- **How to identify:** Compare two recent lows; if they're within a few ticks of each other, they're relatively equal.
- **How to use:** As a point of interest for a reversal or as a level to watch for stops.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March 17: relative equal lows at 24,962 and 24,964.25.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:54](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=774)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** "Relative equal" (within a few ticks) is different from an "exact" equal; used to identify high-probability reversal or raid levels.

### Tape Reading (Visual Order Flow)
- **Kind:** process / tool
- **What ICT says:** Watch candlestick bodies and wicks in real-time; observe willingness (or reluctance) of price to move in a direction. If bodies are small and compressed near consequent encroachment, it shows reluctance. If there's a large wick above a small body, it shows rejection. Use notes/levels to track price and identify when it's respecting or rejecting a target.
- **How to identify:** Observe real-time candles; bodies tell the story—size, placement, color (close relative to open).
- **How to use:** Combine tape reading with pre-identified levels; watch if price is respecting them or painting a fake move. Can be done with a notepad and quote screen (without chart).
- **Example in this video:** Throughout the stream, ICT watched bodies and wicks; when price bumped the high of FPFVG, he noted body size and placement to assess conviction.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:18](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=438), [0:39:22](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=2362)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Can trade with notes and price quote alone (without charts) by tape reading; ICT does this when shopping with his wife.

### Opening Range Gap (RTH)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The gap (or lack thereof) between the close of the previous session and the open of the regular trading hours session. On March 17, there was an RTH opening range gap with buy-side and sell-side liquidity defined by the gap's low and high.
- **How to identify:** Compare the prior close to the current open; if there's a gap, mark it and identify buy-side (low) and sell-side (high) liquidity pools.
- **How to use:** As targets for price; buyers often seek to fill the gap or access the sell-side.
- **Example in this video:** NQ March 17 RTH opening range gap; sell-side is the high of the gap, minor buy-side is slightly below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:29](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=389), [0:44:13](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=2653)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** During rollover, these gaps bleed over from the prior contract; watch for artificial price runs filling them.

### Volume Imbalance vs. Inefficiency (Toggle Settings)
- **Kind:** definition / tool
- **What ICT says:** There are two settings on trading software to identify imbalances: one shows volume imbalance, one shows inefficiency. They can produce different results based on how they calculate. ICT uses the volume imbalance setting (not the inefficiency setting) for his analysis. Watch which setting reveals the imbalance you want to focus on.
- **How to identify:** Toggle the settings on your charting software; watch which one highlights the imbalance you see in price action.
- **How to use:** Use the setting that most clearly shows the level you're tracking; consistency in application matters.
- **Example in this video:** Showed the toggle on daily NQ; volume imbalance setting revealed the level of interest; inefficiency setting was ignored.

- **When to sit still:** When low probability (both sides of market are possible); when ahead of major economic news; when between significant levels and no clear setup exists.

## Process, risk & psychology

- **Selective trade-taking:** ICT is not aggressively trading during rollover. He is "collecting levels," "getting in sync," and "tape reading," not trying to build equity. This is disciplined restraint.
- **Learning environment:** For brand-new traders, a low-leverage, low-consequence environment (paper trading, demo) is critical. Attaching emotional stimuli (money made/lost) early prevents clear learning.
- **Avoiding impulse entry:** ICT repeatedly warns not to trade just because he mentions a level. "You're impulsively jumping on something that I'm saying I have interest in." Many traders hear a level and immediately enter; this is a common mistake.
- **Synchronization, not aggressiveness:** After vacation, illness, or major events, ICT returns to a "synchronization mode"—watch levels, don't push trades. This is prudent risk management.
- **Knowing what not to do:** The most important skill is knowing when not to trade. "That's the playlist that keeps you from becoming insolvent."
- **Wife/life interruption:** ICT ends the stream early because his wife wants to go out. This shows life balance and the reality of his trading—it's not 24/7 obsession. Also a humorous touch showing he respects his personal commitments.

## Glossary candidates

`ROLLOVER` — The transition from one contract month to the next; creates artificial price action due to mass participation shifts; net sum zero buying/selling but painted as directional movement.

`HIGH PROBABILITY` — A setup where price direction is obvious, order flow is clean, and bodies are validating bias; you can confidently call a level.

`LOW PROBABILITY` — A setup where both bullish and bearish scenarios are equally possible; requires sitting still and maintaining interest without trading.

`FIRST PRESENTED FAIR VALUE GAP (FPFVG)` — The first FVG that forms after the session open; often the first target for price if the bias is validated.

`TAPE READING` — Reading candlestick bodies and wicks in real-time to assess willingness/reluctance of price to move; can be done with notes and price quote alone.

`RELATIVE EQUAL LOWS/HIGHS` — Two price lows or highs very close to each other (within a few ticks); indicates a high-probability reversal or raid level.

`SYNCHRONIZATION` — Getting in sync with price action after an interruption (vacation, illness, rollover event) by watching levels and building context without aggressive trade-taking.

`DISCOUNT SENSITIVITY` — The upper half of a fair value gap where buyers show strength; bodies must stay below it to maintain bearish bias.
