## Summary
ICT shows how to trade the same ideas on CFDs (US100/US500/US30) as on NQ futures: mark the prior 4:14 PM ET settlement and the 9:30 ET open yourself, include volume imbalances inside every FVG on CFD charts, and use the same macro times. The market walk-through is on Friday Sept 20, 2024: a tiny (about 20 handle) discount opening range gap, Friday, big range the day before, and an open INSIDE Thursday's first presented FVG. He teaches that Thursday's first 9:30–10:00 FVG should be extended to the right and used as a reference for about three days; that sideways back-and-forth before 9:50 is "time distortion", not indecision; and that the 9:50–10:10 macro is when price "spools" toward untraded sell-side liquidity. He then trades short on the micro from the high of yesterday's first FVG, targeting just below relative equal lows, and re-enters/exit using the consequent encroachment (midpoint) of a long lower wick. A long argument against reading order flow, DOM, level 2 and volume profile: price is algorithmically delivered, not pushed by buyers/sellers.

## Concepts

### Opening range gap — "discount" gap, size thresholds, and CFD bridging  (aliases ICT uses: opening range gap, ORG, settlement price (4:14 PM ET) to 9:30 open)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Futures: the gap is between the prior day's settlement price at 4:14 PM ET (he also says "4:15 or 4:14") and today's 9:30 AM ET opening price. Futures still trade 4:15–5:00 (he says another 45–46 min, "closes at 5:00") and overnight, but the RTH-chart view (regular trading hours) shows the clean gap.
  - If yesterday's 4:14 settlement is higher than today's 9:30 open, it is a DISCOUNT opening range gap (today opens below the prior close). The settlement line is the high of the ORG and the 9:30 open is the low.
  - A small ORG is about 20 handles and is "limp/anemic"; he does not like less than 40 handles. With under 40 handles he uses other tools for the morning or opts out until 10:00 and then trades a Silver Bullet (short mention).
  - You can anticipate the gap near 9:29 (electronic hours): see whether the open will be above or below settlement and guess whether it will be wide.
  - The opening range is the first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00 ET), an algorithmic interval ("anything less than this they're making up stuff"). He says he will teach more uses in the 2024 mentorship and the book, and won't reveal everything before then.
  - For CFD traders: CFDs trade around the clock with no settlement break and no 4:15–6:00 gap, so there is no gap function; bridge it by marking the 4:14 PM ET candle's close (that is the "settlement") and the 9:30 open on the CFD chart. The same two price points give the same information.
- **How to identify:** RTH chart; find the 4:14 PM ET close, the 9:30 open; draw two bold horizontal lines extended; label premium or discount.
- **How to use:** gap size determines morning approach (small gap = harder morning, pay attention to time and yesterday's levels); the previous day's gap and whether it was traded to its midpoint matter.
- **Example in this video:** NQ December micro, Thu Sep 19 settlement (caption reads "2,55", i.e. about 20,055) vs Fri Sep 20 open ("20,035 even", caption) = 20 handle discount gap. Yesterday's gap "barely made its way toward the upper quadrant" and never traded to its midpoint/CE, leaving a vacuum of price below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:09](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=429) prior 4:14 settlement candle shown · [0:08:00](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=480) discount opening range gap · [0:09:38](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=578) opening range 9:30–10:00 · [0:20:11](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1211) under 40 handles → other tools or wait for 10:00 Silver Bullet · [1:04:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3866) CFD chart: mark 4:14 and 9:30

### First presented FVG (9:30–10:00) from the PRIOR day, extended to the right  (aliases ICT uses: "yesterday's first presented fair value gap", orange box, extend right, life cycle about 3 days)
- **Kind:** rule / new coinage-like refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first FVG that forms between 9:30 and 10:00 on the 1-minute chart must involve at least the 9:31 candle; the 9:30 candle cannot be used for the first FVG presentation. Today's formed at 9:34; Thursday's formed around the 9:38 candle (he points at a candle at 9:38).
  - Extend each day's first presented FVG forward (chart settings: "extend right"); its useful life cycle is about 3 days, sometimes weeks if not yet traded back into. The algorithm refers back to it, unless it is a trending day.
  - Journal them: for each of the last three days, did that day's first FVG influence price today?
  - Today's own first FVG may not matter yet: the market opened inside Thursday's FVG and so uses that larger gap first; today's small gap may be useful "next week instead".
  - Its levels are very specific prices (high, upper quadrant, middle/CE, lower quadrant, low), "not a zone". When bearish, he wants price not to go back into the upper half of the gap.
- **How to identify:** 1m chart after 9:30; first FVG that includes the 9:31 candle or later and is complete before 10:00.
- **How to use:** as a reference range for later days (opens inside it = it matters); short from its high or CE when bearish; used as an iFVG if price trades back up into it after going below.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday Sept 19's first FVG shown as an orange box extended into Friday; Friday opened inside it. Overnight London/Asia trades also worked around that box.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:12](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=672) first FVG forms at 9:34, can't use the 9:30 candle · [0:17:48](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1068) extend yesterday's first FVG, life cycle ~3 days · [0:21:46](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1306) how to set "extend right" · [0:23:21](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1401) why you must extend it · [1:02:47](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3767) later in the day price returns to the first FVG

### Time distortion and the 9:50–10:10 macro  (aliases ICT uses: time distortion, macro, "spool", algorithmic window)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Back-and-forth consolidation before a macro is NOT lack of buyers or sellers and not a balance between them; it is "time distortion" — price waiting for a specific time. Here 9:50–10:10 AM is the algorithmic window when the market "spools" (starts to run).
  - A macro is a short list of directives (a script) that the delivery algorithm runs: it looks back through the previous session (overnight, yesterday afternoon, London, Asia), finds liquidity pools not yet traded to, and then delivers price there. It does not know how many contracts rest there, only that the low/high has not been traded.
  - Macro times repeat: the last 10 minutes before the top of each hour for 20 minutes total (here 9:50 for 20 minutes).
  - He says he does not require macro times when volatility is high; but when factors stack against you (small gap, Friday, end of week), stick to time.
  - "First the time, then what price around that time."
- **How to identify:** clock 9:50–10:10 ET (and other hourly :50–:10 windows); preceding choppy consolidation.
- **How to use:** expect the move toward untraded opposing liquidity once the window opens; wait for it rather than breaking out early.
- **Example in this video:** Friday Sept 20: at 9:50 price began to move lower toward the relative equal lows on the micro NQ.
- **Timestamps:** [0:13:41](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=821) time distortion waiting for 9:50 · [0:33:02](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1982) it's scripted; repeats 10 minutes before each hour, 20-minute duration · [0:37:52](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2272) what a macro is · [0:45:07](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2707) macro = short program script heading to the pools
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** this is a more mechanical description of macros (looking back through the prior session) than in earlier lectures.

### Day-context stack: Friday (TGIF), small gap, big prior range, open inside yesterday's gap  (aliases ICT uses: TGIF, "things stacking against you", weekly range retracement)
- **Kind:** rule / bias
- **What ICT says:**
  - TGIF: on Fridays, look for a pullback of about 20–30% of the weekly range (the highest high to lowest low of the week) — "not that it needs to do that", but he looks for it. He says he will explain how to measure it in Caleb's channel video.
  - Factors that made the morning hard for a newer trader: small 20-handle gap, a large-range day yesterday, Friday end of week, opening inside yesterday's first FVG, and yesterday's gap largely untraded (vacuum below). All combine to favor a lower move after the 9:50 macro and make a clean morning setup harder.
  - His morning tweet said "focus on two sell-side liquidity pools" (the relative equal lows below), so direction was known beforehand.
  - Beginner rules vs advanced: "don't trade Mondays" and "don't trade high-impact news" are guidance for new students; those who know what they are doing can trade news after the report prints and then look for the inefficiencies or liquidity left behind.
- **How to identify / use:** build a checklist of day-of-week, gap size, prior range, where the open sits relative to prior FVGs; combine into a narrative before 9:30.
- **Example in this video:** all of the above for Sep 20, 2024.

### Buy-side raid at the open → trap, then drop to the opening range gap low  (aliases ICT uses: knock out shorts, trap buyers above old high, rake them across the coals)
- **Kind:** example / pattern
- **What ICT says:** at 9:30 the market rallied and took out short-term buy-side liquidity, stopping out anyone short and trapping buyers who bought the break above the old high; price then traded back toward sell-side (sell stops below). The pattern played out as: open → swing up to the high of the ORG (yesterday's 4:14 settlement) → trades through it for more liquidity → back down through the opening price (low of the ORG) → one more pass for buy-side → drops again toward the ORG low.
- **How to identify:** pre-session high wicked at the open, followed by failure to continue.
- **How to use:** do not buy breakouts; look to short from an uptick into a PD array after the raid.
- **Example in this video:** micro NQ 1m, 9:30–9:50 ET Sept 20.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:23](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=923) the raid and trap · [0:26:35](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1595) open → high → open low → buy side again
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** same theme as the 031 breakout-buyer trap.

### Short entry from the high of yesterday's first FVG (sell on an uptick) with breakaway gap  (aliases ICT uses: breakaway gap, sell into strength, uptick)
- **Kind:** entry model (applied) / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - He wants price to trade down through today's own first FVG at the macro; he removed the volume imbalance (body-to-body gap) from today's FVG so it only shows candle low to candle high (wick-to-wick), because the volume imbalance is the "least impactful". Reading the volume imbalance as a breakaway gap: price leaving with a gap because the market is being re-priced lower at that time.
  - Once price is below, he waits for it to come back UP into yesterday's first presented FVG (orange box) and sells short on that rise: entry at the high of that gap (it looks random but is the high of the reference FVG), with time (macro) and the breakaway gap on his side. If it hits the CE of yesterday's FVG and leaves, that is the same story.
  - Rationale he gives: large funds and HFT cannot short on a down-tick; they must wait for an uptick (some have filters needing a percentage/time threshold) — so "selling short on an uptick" is smart money behavior (his claim; not verified).
  - Target: just below the relative equal lows (sell-side) he flagged on Twitter; he exits at a tick below them.
  - Entry was a market order only because he is using market replay/paper; with limit orders you can get very tight entries and meaningful stops, which he will teach in this mentorship.
- **How to identify:** bearish bias; yesterday's first FVG overhead; price makes a lower low then retraces up into the box.
- **How to use:** short at the box high or CE; stop above the box high (not stated numerically); target the equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** micro NQ 1m, Fri Sep 20: price drops through today's 9:34 FVG at 9:50, rallies into Thursday's first FVG (touching its CE), then he shorts at the top of that FVG and covers just under the relative equal lows. (Prices not clearly stated in captions.)
- **Timestamps:** [0:47:33](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2853) levels of yesterday's FVG are specific prices · [0:50:01](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3001) sell on uptick, not downtick · [0:51:37](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3097) CE tag then entry at top of the gap · [0:55:37](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3337) exit under the relative equal lows

### Consequent encroachment of a wick ("discount wick") as entry and target  (aliases ICT uses: CE of a wick, discount wick)
- **Kind:** refinement / new technique
- **What ICT says:**
  - Measure a candle's body to its low (the lower wick) — the midpoint of that wick is its consequent encroachment. If bearish, you can enter short at that price; the same kind of level can be used as the target. Anchor the measurement to something that makes sense (body end to wick extreme); a random line floating in space is meaningless.
  - He calls it "a discount wick" (the wick in the discount of the candle).
- **How to identify:** the long lowest wick candle in the consolidation before the drop.
- **How to use:** short at the wick's CE when bearish; take profit at another discount wick's CE.
- **Example in this video:** micro NQ 1m: CE of the long low wick = 19974.25 (caption "19974 point2"); entry short there; target the CE of a lower wick at 19930.50 or lower; exit filled at 19933.75 ("slipped a little"); then price rallied back up into yesterday's first FVG and cleared the high of the day later.
- **Timestamps:** [1:00:22](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3622) measuring body to low · [1:01:10](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3670) CE price 19974.25 · [1:01:59](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3719) target CE 19930.50, exit 19933.75
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** he says the same wick-CE entry appeared in the CFD chart too (midpoint of the wick, get short, it drops). New refinement; verify the exact definition of "discount wick" in other videos.

### Volume imbalance inside the FVG — include it on CFDs  (aliases ICT uses: volume imbalance, "separation between the bodies")
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the small separation between one candle's body (close) and the next candle's open — body to body — not wick to wick.
  - On futures he sometimes strips it out to focus on wicks (as above). On CFDs always include volume imbalances in the FVG, because broker pricing varies and without them you won't have a true depiction of the zone the algorithm refers to.
  - He says this idea of the volume imbalance is not in other books.
- **How to identify:** open of candle N+1 differs from close of candle N.
- **How to use:** CFD charts: draw FVG including the volume imbalance.
- **Example in this video:** US100 CFD 1m: first gap at 9:30–10:00 with volume imbalances at two places; mark 9:50 macro, 9:51 begin lower, target sell-side/relative equal lows; same wick-CE short.
- **Timestamps:** [1:06:05](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3965) always include volume imbalance for CFDs · [1:06:52](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=4012) definition (body to body) and 9:50 macro marked · [1:07:40](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=4060) "all you have to do is use the times"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** broker feeds differ, so the CFD "FVG" is not identical to futures.

### Algorithmic price delivery vs order flow / DOM / level 2  (aliases ICT uses: scripted market, price engine, repricing, dealer vs market maker)
- **Kind:** definition / mindset (concept framing)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price is delivered by an algorithm (a price engine) that offers prices according to a script; buying and selling pressure does not determine direction. Chop/consolidation is the algorithm cycling through its directives, not indecision.
  - Time and sales, volume profile, depth of market (level 2), the "book map" are used after the fact to justify moves; the ladders can be spoofed ("not all real orders"), a mirage. A large pool of orders on one side does not mean price goes there.
  - Support/resistance, old highs and indicators do not matter if the algorithm is repricing lower; it simply keeps offering lower prices until it reaches the liquidity pool (resting sell stops).
  - Opposing liquidity: if buy-side was taken, find the nearest obvious sell-side pool that has not been traded to.
  - The market is not free; because it is scripted it repeats at the same times each day.
  - Dealer vs market maker: a dealer trades the same price feed as everyone else; it doesn't make price. He says price is "way above you".
  - He uses the GameStop/AMC/Bitcoin runs as examples of price being delivered in big premium skips when it is time to trap participants (and mentions NFP/CPI/PPI as similar news drivers).
- **How to use:** don't use order-flow tools for direction; use time + liquidity + PD arrays. He adds that he wished he had such tools only in the beginning because he did not know liquidity.
- **Example in this video:** the Sept 20 drop through "supports" and old highs to the relative equal lows.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1886) algorithm not buying/selling pressure · [0:35:25](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2125) DOM / level 2 are after-the-fact, spoofed · [0:42:40](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2560) lower prices offered regardless of buyers · [0:48:21](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2901) opposing liquidity and looking back through prior session
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** this is ICT's philosophical stance and conflicts with order-flow teachings; treat as stated opinion.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Futures settlement: 4:14 PM ET (also "4:15"); RTH open 9:30 ET; opening range 9:30–10:00; first FVG must use the 9:31 candle or later; extend it right, about 3-day life.
- Macro: 9:50–10:10 ET (last 10 min before the hour, 20 min long); recurs hourly.
- Small ORG about 20 handles = anemic; prefers 40+ handles; otherwise other tools or wait for 10:00 and trade Silver Bullet.
- TGIF: look for 20–30% retracement of the weekly range on Fridays.
- NQ tick math: mini NQ 1 handle (point) = 4 ticks = $20; micro = $2 per handle (per four ticks); e.g. 20,001.0 to 20,011.0 is 10 handles.
- Always include volume imbalances in FVGs on CFD charts.
- CFD equivalents: US100 = NQ, US500 = ES (S&P), US30 = YM (Dow).
- Include yesterday's first FVG, TGIF, large range prior day, and where the open sits relative to prior FVG in the daily narrative.
- Caption-derived levels: settlement about 20,055, open 20,035; wick CE 19974.25; target 19930.50; fill 19933.75.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Funded-account challenges: if you are not trading micro size you are gambling and over-leveraged; the max loss given is small, but without skill you will draw the account down and pay reset fees. Use a "shoestring" approach with micros; everyone can do it (opinion) (0:05:30–0:06:20).
- Practise with market orders in paper to feel what it is like to engage price; limit orders for tight entries will be taught later (0:54:49).
- Trail stops in the demo were for teaching Caleb where a beginner's stop would be (0:59:34).
- Don't wait for someone to tell you you're ready for real money; you'll know when paper is boring and consistent (0:56:24). Be wary of live-stream influencers with affiliate funded-account links; you copy without knowing why the trade was taken (0:57:11).
- Journal the first-FVG reference points for the last 3 days; note whether they influenced today (0:24:59).
- Reacting means you didn't anticipate; the concepts exist to let you predict (0:24:12).
- CFD users: you can follow the lessons; use the time first, then price (1:07:40). Homework to come each night "like university" (1:08:30).

## Glossary candidates
`Discount opening range gap` — today's 9:30 open below yesterday's 4:14 PM ET settlement.
`Time distortion` — price consolidating/oscillating while waiting for a macro time.
`Spool` — the market starting to run during a macro.
`Breakaway gap` — gap (volume imbalance) left as price breaks away at macro time being re-priced (loosely used).
`Discount wick` — lower wick of a candle; its midpoint is a CE entry/target level.
`Volume imbalance` — body-to-body gap between consecutive candles (close of one to open of next).
`TGIF` — Friday retracement expectation of 20–30% of the weekly range.
`Price engine / algorithm` — ICT's name for the delivery mechanism that seeks liquidity.
