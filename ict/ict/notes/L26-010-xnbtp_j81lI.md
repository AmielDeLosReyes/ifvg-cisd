## Summary
Part 5 of ICT's "how new students should start" series is about how to backtest properly. He recaps the three pillars from part 4 (7:00–9:00 a.m. pre-market range, the 9:30–10:00 opening range and its consequent encroachment, and the 10:00 Silver Bullet hour) and then walks one NQ 1-minute day through them as an example of a backtest annotation. The method: record screen video (or save annotated charts), log precise price notes, trace every candle, define a hypothetical entry, stop and partials, and record drawdown, time against you and risk. He insists on writing only constructive, confident observations to build "pseudo experience," says never to use market replay, and recommends six weeks back and six weeks forward minimum. The applied content includes the discount opening range gap (69 handles) whose mid gap was hit 5 minutes after the open, with -1.0 fib projections hitting the AM-session high and low, and the "first presented fair value gap with displacement" in the 10:00 hour combined with the lunch macro.

## Concepts

### Backtesting properly (ICT's method): recorded price, hypothetical trades, no market replay
- **Kind:** rule | process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Do NOT use market replay. It shows you a candle's open and then its close; you miss the "breath and lifespan" of each candle: how it opens, the flurry, whether it makes a higher high or lower low while still active, where it settles. The logic applies at any timeframe (1m to weekly); "1-minute chart's not moving any faster than the weekly chart." Replay-based mentors pause and describe, which he calls shameful.
  - Instead, watch recorded price action (live or delayed data both work, because you are studying price that has already happened). On Windows: hold Alt + Windows key and tap R to start a screen recording (microphone on) and record observations in your own voice; avoid a screensaver coming on. Free TradingView real-time data is not necessary; he says real-time data is cheap ("$7 on TradingView") and he will not pay for students' data feeds.
  - Backtesting = pattern recognition and characteristic observations; "all setups are not equal ... they rhyme" and don't have an identical fingerprint but share key characteristics. Record small deviations from what you expect.
  - Minimum: 6 weeks of back data and then 6 weeks forward (live/recorded) — "I promise you your ability to see price action will be so far ahead." Repetition conditions the subconscious so you can read the tape live.
  - Define "backtesting" as: a hypothetical entry, a hypothetical stop, and how price delivered. Don't just take a picture after the fact; trace over every individual candlestick (e.g., a candle that opened and traded right back up to the previous candle's low = "immediate rebalance"; a retrace that stops "shy of the close" is only respecting the wick). Use empty chart space with A/B/C legend markers for longer explanations.
  - Add price notes (TradingView "price note") to anchor exact prices of key highs/lows/opens/closes; increase font size. Be precise: his analogy is a cash business, where missing $2.25 of a 562.25 vs "560-ish" matters because "it's your business." Precision in logging is a precondition for precision in trading.
  - Log charts as annotated static pictures or electronically; don't worry about a naked chart if you annotate. Review everything on the weekend.
- **How to use:** Record each day's 7–9 pre-market range, opening range, 10:00 hour, annotate hypothetical entries, stops and targets as if real; compute risk, drawdown and heat.
- **Example in this video:** NQ (September 2026 delivery contract per the caption) 1-minute day, logged with pre-market range, RTH gap and 10:00 hour annotations (below).

### Pre-market range (7:00–9:00 a.m. ET): grading and projecting it  (aliases: "pillar 1", "pre-market session hours")
- **Kind:** rule | definition (recap of part 4 plus how to chart it)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Pillar 1: the pre-market session hours, 7:00 to 9:00 a.m. ET. Mark the highest high and lowest low and the relative equal highs and lows inside that range; determine its phase (consolidation, trending/expansion) and how that usually impacts the morning session.
  - Draw a Fib across the 7–9 range to grade it, then stop the lines at 9:00 a.m. (he teaches his son to do so), and project them into the day, so that when you grade the opening range (9:30–10:00) you can see whether any opening range levels agree with the pre-market levels; agreement makes them stronger key levels for the PD array framework.
  - The algorithm "will absolutely refer back" to relative equal highs and lows inside the 7–9 range.
- **How to use:** The pre-market high/low is a reference for sells/buys, qualifier for a fair value gap, and a draw.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: at 9:30 price rallied away from the pre-market high, created a high, then sold off from a premium bearish FVG for over 100 handles (he later says 94 handles from the pre-market key level); price retraced into the CE of the bearish FVG and the pre-market session high (they coincided), then moved lower to clear relative equal lows from pre-market at 29,370.50 (caption value). The prior premium high acts as discount when price later trades above it. He stresses writing a confident observation ("I was pleased to see how the pre-market session high was used to qualify a key level for the (caption: 11:09) a.m. FVG").
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:45](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=585) — fib across 7–9 and stop lines at 9:00; [0:15:14](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=914) — premium high flipping to discount; [0:16:01](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=961) — pre-market high qualifies the FVG; [0:16:50](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1010) — relative equal lows 7–9 as target.

### Hypothetical trade framing on a bearish FVG (entry at CE or lower half, stop at the high + 1 tick)
- **Kind:** entry model | refinement (backtest convention)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Realistic hypothetical entry: consequent encroachment of the bearish FVG, or one tick below it (never "I would have sold the exact high"). Characteristic of a bearish FVG: if order flow is bearish the BODY should stay below the high (wicks can occur above, "they can do the damage"). Entering in the lower half is where "premium sensitivity" exists. If price goes higher into the upper half you can note an add/pyramid opportunity (one more contract).
  - Stop: the high of the candle plus one tick (record the high of any candle that wicks above). He wants stops logged as real hard stops, not labeled "hypothetical." Entry at or one tick before the level is "permissible" since you need to be filled.
  - Target: the pre-market buy-side/premium high area (the point where you calculate the hypothetical return).
  - You will be forced to size small (micros) when the stop is wide; build realistic expectations: "you have no skill yet, no experience yet"; don't fantasize about quitting your job in 3 weeks.
  - He does not think in reward-to-risk because his concepts are "already built in 3:1, 5:1" (setups generate that by default).
  - Record: total risk, the drawdown/heat the idea incurred, how many minutes and how many points price moved against you before working. This conditions you to know that wanting to be in does not mean it moves in your favor immediately; market replay never conditions you to live in the drawdown.
  - Partials: hypothetically two micros; take one off at the low-hanging fruit (relative equal lows / the 10:00-hour low), and over time condition yourself to hold for longer protractions toward the pre-market (7–9) relative equal lows.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m: relative equal highs made, then price up into a bearish FVG; hypothetical short at CE (-1 tick), stop at the candle high +1 tick, target back down into the pre-market buy-side/premium region; sell reference quoted as 29,562.25 for what NOT to claim as an exact top.
- **Timestamps:** [0:17:38](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1058), [0:18:26](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1106) — CE entry and body-below-high rule; [0:19:13](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1153) — pyramid, stop +1 tick, target; [0:41:28](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2488) — record risk and drawdown; [0:43:04](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2584) — partials on two micros.

### Positive, confident journaling ("love letters to yourself") to build pseudo experience
- **Kind:** rule (psychology/journaling)
- **What ICT says:** In your annotations write only constructive praise and confident observation ("I love how ...", "I was pleased to see ..."), never second-guessing, anger, or dollar losses. The subconscious wants comfort; writing it as if you knew builds confidence and speeds up experience ("pseudo experience" that converts into usable experience). Even when you start trading real money and lose, record what you should have seen, as if you did it that way; do not log "I lost $25,000" or "I blew the account." Your journal is private; you can express excitement at first-time observations without fear of being judged. "You have to parent that inner child that eventually will grow up to be an adult trader." Fill the chart's empty space with details every day for weeks.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:22](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=682), [0:12:08](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=728), [0:23:13](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1393), [0:23:59](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1439).
- **Nuance / caveats:** This is his stated recommendation (and a controversial one); it conflicts with the usual "journal your mistakes" practice, so flag it as his view for the book.

### RTH Opening Range Gap: discount/premium definition, mid gap, and -1.0 fib projections
- **Kind:** definition | rule | refinement (new: standard-deviation projections)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Use the regular trading hours (RTH) chart, not electronic. Reference points: the previous day's RTH closing price (final print 4:14 p.m. ET on the 1-minute chart) and the 9:30 a.m. opening price. Between them is the opening range gap. Opening lower than the settlement = DISCOUNT opening range gap; opening higher = PREMIUM opening range gap. Where it opens at 9:30 determines the type.
  - Watch how the consequent encroachment (mid gap) is traded to after the open.
  - New: Fib standard-deviation projections off the gap. Draw the fib from the open to the settlement and add -1.0: the level hits the high of the AM session; draw from the settlement to the open and add -1.0: it hit the AM session low "to the tick" (also a further "duplicated down" projection of the 69-handle range delivered the lunch-macro lower low — caption unclear).
  - RTH relative equal highs and lows (smooth areas formed in the last 15–20 minutes of the previous day's trading, "previous market on close macro") must be observed in RTH view because the gap will refer back to them; it doesn't matter if electronic-hours price (London/Asia) already traded through them.
  - Record the range from low to target and what it offered; assume you took a trade idea there.
- **How to identify:** Chart set to RTH; previous day last 1m candle 4:14 p.m. close vs 9:30 open.
- **How to use:** Expect the CE (mid gap) to be traded to; project -1.0 fibs from the gap for AM-session extremes; use RTH-smooth highs/lows as references; log the gap size in handles and the time the CE was delivered.
- **Example in this video:** NQ: discount opening range gap of 69 handles. At 9:30 first candle wicked but did not take out the low; a second attempt, then price rallied to the RTH CE (mid gap) just 5 minutes after 9:30. The AM session high was nailed by the -1.0 projection; the AM session low (caption: 29,386.25, at about 11:12 a.m. ET) was nailed initially by the -1.0 projection from settlement to open, then swept by a later lower low; price subsequently worked inside the gap with a lunch-macro draw toward 10:00-hour lows and delivered to a "perfect standard deviation" (69 handles) lower — "to a tick."
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:47](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1487) — RTH, 4:14 p.m. settlement; [0:25:33](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1533) — discount vs premium gap; [0:26:22](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1582) — 69-handle gap, CE hit within 5 minutes; [0:27:11](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1631) — -1.0 projection hits AM high; [0:27:57](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1677) — -1.0 hits AM low; [0:28:45](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1725) — RTH smooth highs/lows.

He says doing this trains "turtle soup" (pattern recognition of stop runs).

### First Presented FVG (10:00 hour / Silver Bullet) and First Presented FVG with Displacement  (aliases: "first instance of FVG", "reflection FVG")
- **Kind:** definition | refinement | entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - Silver Bullet 10:00 hour: always mark 10:00 on the chart, but not as a vertical line across the whole chart, so you can see whether the 10:00 candle forms an imbalance.
  - Definition: the first presented FVG is "by definition the very first fair value gap, absolutely the very first one." It was taught the day before this video (classification and rule). Highlight it. But "there has to be something in here to make us want to be short": by itself it is not enough. You are "blending conceptual ideas to build a very strong narrative."
  - Narrative blend in the example: (1) first FVG at 10:01 (a SIBI created by a down-close candle; he marks the gap between one candle's high and a later candle's low); (2) the lunch macro: the market will seek a low that forms during the 10:00 hour — so shorting right on the candle that formed that low isn't wise; (3) a relative equal low next to the 10:00 low; (4) price retraced up, cleared minor buy side, tagged the gap and did not close outside it while closing below its midpoint = "wonderful shorting opportunity."
  - Upgrade: First presented FVG WITH DISPLACEMENT: a later up-close candle (BISI) that also takes out highs, i.e., displaces higher, leaving a range. He says it "takes greater command over price delivery than a simple initial formation" and "trumps" the first one. This BISI candle is also a "reflection fair value gap" in addition to being a bearish inversion FVG of the first gap (the first gap was from a down-close candle).
  - Two shorting uses: (a) with price up there, short toward the rejection block (the lowest down-close candle) and the low formed in the 10:00 hour — you don't need that low to be taken because it is formed within the 10:00 hour structure, the pullback target of the lunch macro; (b) a second opportunity when price returns to relative equal highs (minor buy side) and creates a second FVG that is lower than the first, with wicks not outside it and bodies not closing at the midpoint (trading in the lower half) — order flow bearish.
  - Entry: anywhere in the lower half of that FVG ("the most realistic for you"). Stop: the high of the last candle whose OPEN is outside the buy-side imbalance/sell-side inefficiency (not just a wick), +1 tick, and include the volume imbalance (two buy-side bodies) in that frame. That makes the stop wide, forcing micros.
  - Targets: first partial at the 10:00-hour low/relative equal lows (low-hanging fruit, lunch macro); a longer hold toward the 7–9 a.m. pre-market relative equal lows. Hypothetical: two micros, one off at the first target.
  - The dealing range / opening range is 30 minutes (9:30–10:00); "I don't care how many people tell you it's a 5-minute or 15-minute opening range ... they're all wrong."
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, 10:00 hour (day unspecified; caption): first FVG at 10:01 (SIBI); price rallied, made a BISI with displacement above highs, returned to the first gap, closed below its midpoint, sold off — "NQ used the first presented FVG to sell off for over 175 handles."

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Never use market replay; study recorded price (live or delayed) and watch candles form in full.
- Backtest 6 weeks back and 6 weeks forward minimum.
- Pre-market session range = 7:00–9:00 a.m. ET; draw a fib on its high/low, stop lines at 9:00, project into the day.
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 (30 minutes); dealing range 30 minutes; not 5 or 15.
- RTH settlement = 4:14 p.m. ET last 1-minute print; discount gap = open below; premium gap = open above.
- Opening range gap -1.0 fib projections (open→settlement and settlement→open) mark AM session extremes.
- Mark 10:00 with a short tick or small marker, not a full-height vertical line.
- Hypothetical short: entry at CE or one tick below (bodies below the FVG high); stop at the candle high +1 tick; stop frame includes the volume imbalance; partials at relative equal lows; record risk, drawdown, minutes against.
- Record price notes with exact prices (open/high/low/close); fill the chart space with annotations.
- First presented FVG alone is not enough; need narrative (lunch macro, relative equal lows/highs, 10:00-hour low) and ideally displacement.

## Glossary candidates
`Pseudo experience` — practice built by annotating hindsight charts confidently so it converts to usable live experience.
`Immediate rebalance` — candle opens and trades right back to the prior candle's low/high.
`Discount / premium opening range gap` — RTH open below / above the previous 4:14 p.m. settlement.
`Mid gap` — consequent encroachment of the opening range gap.
`-1.0 projection` — fib standard-deviation extension (one gap range beyond the gap) used to target AM session extremes.
`First presented FVG` — the very first FVG after the reference time (here 10:00).
`First presented FVG with displacement` — a later FVG with displacement that outranks the plain first FVG.
`Reflection FVG` — (caption/usage unclear) an FVG that reflects/inverts the first gap; here a BISI that is also a bearish inversion FVG.
`Previous market-on-close macro` — last 15–20 minutes of the previous RTH session where smooth RTH highs/lows form.
