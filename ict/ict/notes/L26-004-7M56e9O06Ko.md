## Concepts

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Entry on an iFVG anchored to Tuesday's opening range cap. The gap runs from a specific high to a specific low.
- **How to identify:** Formed within the structure of the Asian session; identified as a gap with defined anchor point to prior session opening range.
- **How to use:** Go long from the iFVG entry; expect price to run into liquidity pools above; use stop loss below the gap candle.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Asian session, iFVG goes long, price runs from ~18450 area into multiple liquidity pools above, eventually being stopped near 18720.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:09](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=9) entry, [0:09:28](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=568) first partial hit, [0:31:07](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1867) stop out.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** The iFVG must be anchored to a clear structural reference (here, Tuesday's opening range cap) to be valid.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Used as a defensive support level; price should not go below the consequent encroachment (wick level) of prior gaps or structures. Used to verify viability of the trade; if price violates this, consider it a sign of deeper retracement.
- **How to identify:** Look at the wick extremes of prior candlesticks/gaps and their consequent encroachment levels.
- **How to use:** Place stop loss below the CE level; if price violates it, the trade is likely negated or needs restructuring.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple references to "don't want to see it go below the wick consequent encroachment" at ~18515 area; this acts as the final line of defense.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:16](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=916) explains CE concept, [0:24:01](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1441) details defensive use.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** CE is part of a 3-PD array defensive framework, not for entries, only for trade viability checks.

### Double Tap Exit Strategy (Head Shot & Center Mass)
- **Kind:** entry model (exit management)
- **What ICT says:** Take profits at two tiers: head shot (best case target, full hit) and center mass (second tier, chest hit). Metaphor from shooting range: aim for the best target but take what you can get.
- **How to identify:** Identify multiple liquidity pools ahead; the highest is the head shot, the middle tier is center mass.
- **How to use:** Exit partial positions at each level to lock in profit and fund remaining runners; this reduces need to hit the ultimate target.
- **Example in this video:** First partial taken above 709 level (center mass), head shot targeted around Monday's opening range cap, then additional partials at event horizon midpoint (~725 area).
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:10](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=310) introduces double tap, [0:23:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1392) detailed metaphor.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a partition of larger trade, not required to hit single terminus.

### Order Block (Defensive)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Part of the defensive PD array; the gap candle's order block (candle #2 in gap sequence) is used for stop loss placement. Don't place stop below the entire gap, but use the order block to bracket risk tightly.
- **How to identify:** The second candle in a fair value gap sequence.
- **How to use:** Stop loss is placed just below the order block candle's low, not the entire gap range.
- **Example in this video:** Stop is placed below candle #2 of the entry gap at the start; later tightened as profit accumulates.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:11](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=671) mentions "my stop is below the gap candle, number two candle in the gap."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Differentiates between gap range and order block placement for tighter risk management.

### Volume Imbalance
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Gaps in candles showing imbalance; used to confirm continuation and measure next liquidity targets. Creates a "ladder" leading to higher prices as price respects each imbalance without filling them.
- **How to identify:** Visual gaps on the chart where no trading occurred between candles.
- **How to use:** Expect price to run over/respect volume imbalances; they don't require level 2 data or footprint analysis.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple volume imbalances identified as price runs upward; each is respected or targeted as the "ladder" unfolds.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:48](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=768) "everything becomes just like a little ladder, a step leading up to higher prices."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Volume imbalance does not require fancy tools; simple candlestick observation suffices.

### Event Horizon (Measurement Method)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** When two pools of liquidity exist, measure the midpoint between them (event horizon); price can be taken off there as a partial, staggering exits.
- **How to identify:** Identify two clear liquidity pools; measure the distance between them.
- **How to use:** Exit partials at the event horizon to fund the position without requiring the final target.
- **Example in this video:** Event horizon measured between upper and lower liquidity pools; partials taken near midpoint (~725 area).
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:16](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=916) explains event horizon concept, [0:26:27](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1587) execution "if we can get five contracts off there."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Allows staggered profit-taking without single dependency on terminus target.

### Stop Loss Placement Rule
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Never place stop so wide it covers excessive risk; use the order block (candle #2) of the gap. Must follow rules even if stop is tight; if stopped out, accept it — the rules protect you in the long run.
- **How to identify:** Mark the second candle low in the gap sequence.
- **How to use:** Tight stop below order block candle; raise stop as profit accumulates and trade proves viable.
- **Example in this video:** Initial stop below order block; raised multiple times as partials are taken and CE/support holds.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:11](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=671), [0:26:27](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1587) (raise stop after each partial).
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Accept stop-outs; they prove the framework works by limiting damage when conditions fail.

### Three-PD Array Defensive Framework
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** Three price delivery (PD) arrays used defensively: (1) CE of entry gap, (2) Monday's opening range gap low, (3) buy-side liquidity / sell-side efficiency. If price violates all three levels, the trade is no longer viable and stop is triggered appropriately.
- **How to identify:** Identify three structural support levels on the chart.
- **How to use:** Use these to frame the risk/reward of the position; stop loss placed to respect all three. If price breaches all three, the trade's thesis is negated.
- **Example in this video:** Three defenses stacked at ~18440-18515 area; trade stopped out when price finally violates all three.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:01](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1441) details the three PDAs, [0:31:07](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1867) final stop out "below it."
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a defensive framework, not for pyramiding new entries; it's for trade viability only.

### Partial Exit / Position Pyramiding
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Add to position on dips into support; take partials at targets. Example: add 3 contracts here, remove 3 there. This funds the position so you don't need the final target to be profitable.
- **How to identify:** Track entry price, support levels (CE, order blocks, volume imbalances), and exit zones.
- **How to use:** Pyramid into weakness; distribute exits at multiple levels (head shot, center mass, event horizon, etc.). Never require single target.
- **Example in this video:** Initial entry, added 3 more contracts at support, partial off at ~709, another at center mass, more at event horizon; final runner left for ultimate target.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=252) adds 3 more, [0:09:28](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=568) first partial, [0:23:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1392) discusses rationale.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Must respect support when adding; don't pyramid into weakness if price is breaking structure.

### Opening Range Gap (Monday's RTH Open)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** The high and low of the opening range from Monday's regular trading hours (RTH); used as reference level for support and target zones.
- **How to identify:** Identify the first candle(s) of Monday's regular trading hours (9:30 ET) and mark the high/low.
- **How to use:** Price running into these levels often encounters liquidity; use as target zones and support levels.
- **Example in this video:** Monday's RTH opening range gap low (~18530 area) used as target on the way up; also as support on pullbacks.
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:57](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=57) "Monday's record trading hours open range gap cap," [0:13:37](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=817) "low of Monday's regular trading hours opening range gap low."

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Entry on iFVG anchored to prior session opening range structure.
- Stop loss always below order block candle (candle #2 in the gap).
- When adding to position: respect support levels; don't add into breakdown.
- Partial exits: take one off at head (best target), one at center mass (second tier), and possibly one at event horizon (midpoint).
- Three defensive PD arrays must hold for trade to remain viable; violate all three = stop triggered.
- Asian session is subdued, event-driven only (not much volatility usually).
- Open above/below candlestick bodies must stay open on the "correct" side (bullish = upper half).
- Raise stop loss as profit accumulates to lock in gains.
- Never demand single terminus; stagger exits to reduce dependency.

## Process, risk & psychology
- **Execution discipline:** Show live execution, not market replay, to prove the concept works without hindsight bias. Execution management proves it's not fake.
- **Accepting stop-outs:** If stopped out after following rules, that's okay. Rules protect you over time. Proper risk framing (3 defensive levels) shows how much damage was avoided.
- **Staggering profits:** "Give myself a steak dinner" (remove risk/lock profit) early rather than betting everything on final target.
- **Patience on pullbacks:** Don't panic when price retraces to support (CE, order block, etc.); give it a chance to respond.
- **Tape reading:** No special tools required; simple candlestick order flow (bodies, wicks, volume gaps) is sufficient.
- **Risk per trade:** Example given: 12 cars (12 contracts) risking $525 for potential $3,000 return = good risk/reward.

## Glossary candidates
- **iFVG** — Inversion Fair Value Gap; gap anchored to prior session opening range, used as entry signal.
- **Consequent Encroachment (CE)** — Wick extreme of prior candlestick/gap; used as defensive support level.
- **Order Block** — Second candle in a fair value gap sequence; used for tight stop loss placement.
- **Volume Imbalance** — Gap in candlestick sequence indicating imbalance; expected to hold/be used as continuation target.
- **Event Horizon** — Measurement of midpoint between two liquidity pools; used for intermediate partial exits.
- **PD Array** — Price delivery array; structural support/resistance level used for trading or defense.
- **RTH** — Regular Trading Hours; typically 9:30 ET open for US markets.
- **Head Shot / Center Mass** — Exit targets; head shot is best case (full target), center mass is second tier (partial profit-take).
