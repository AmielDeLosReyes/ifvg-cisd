## Concepts

### Continuous contract analysis (versus front-month NQ, and CFD equivalents)
- **Kind:** rule / process
- **What ICT says:**
  - His analysis always begins on a continuous contract chart (he actually starts on a monthly chart). It gives a field of reference for key highs, lows, inefficiencies and arrays that may not exist in the front-month chart because of the rollover.
  - He then checks whether the daily front-month contract (here the September NQ contract) agrees with the levels derived from the continuous chart.
  - Far-dated contracts (e.g. March 2027, December) have no influence on the composite continuous chart.
  - CFD traders (or anyone who cannot trade US futures) should do the same: use the continuous contract for the high/low context and then match the closest correlated highs/lows on their weekly chart (US100 for NQ, US500 for S&P).
  - He says everything he shows works in forex, gold, commodities and bonds. He focuses on one market so he can teach economically.
- **How to identify / use:** (1) weekly continuous chart, (2) mark the previous month's high/low and previous week's high/low, (3) check them on the front-month contract, (4) go to daily, then 15-minute.
- **Example in this video:** the weekly continuous NASDAQ composite chart with July's monthly high and low marked, then the same levels on the September NQ contract.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:55](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=115) — continuous contract vs. front month; [0:04:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=267) — CFD traders; [0:11:41](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=701) — same levels on the September contract
- **Nuance / caveats:** he refers viewers to earlier 2026 lectures where he covers things only visible on a continuous chart.

### Weekly bias: proximity to previous week/month high vs. low  (aliases: "path of least resistance", "draw on liquidity")
- **Kind:** rule / entry model (bias method)
- **What ICT says:**
  - After the weekly close, ask where price opens relative to the previous week's high/previous month's high and previous week's low/previous month's low. Price is more likely to go to the nearer, easier side. He opens the week "right here", far closer to the previous week and previous month highs than the lows, so those highs are the likely draw. This is "the path of least resistance".
  - He adds the primary trend: he sees a primary bullish market ("propped up"), so he takes the bullish order flow at face value until it is proven otherwise.
  - Even if the market later crashes, he says he does not know what it will do. He waits to see where it opens on Sunday, lets Sunday trade, and watches the opening range on Monday.
  - A modest range (previous week high/low) is a big deal when you do it every week, he says in answer to people calling it small.
  - His weekend routine: right after the Friday close he does the analysis on a notepad (sometimes waits until the family is asleep, or does it early Saturday morning) and shares what is on that pad.
- **How to identify:** open (Sunday/Monday) is much closer to one of the previous-week or previous-month extremes, with the primary trend pointing the same way.
- **How to use:** set the weekly draw on the nearer liquidity pool, look for PD arrays that support it (below), and expect him to be indifferent to the far side.
- **Example in this video:** the week of 10–14 Aug 2026 in NQ: open was near the previous week's high and previous month's (July) high; the draw on liquidity was higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:07:41](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=461) — "where is it likelier to go if we open here"; [0:08:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=507) — path of least resistance; [0:12:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=747)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he insists he does not predict the exact number; he names the level he is interested in.

### Liquidity hierarchy: which highs/lows matter  (aliases: static vs dynamic ranges, session highs/lows, equilibrium)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Pools to track: Asian session high/low, London session high/low, NY AM session high/low, the 2-hour NY lunch window high/low (he cites a 2-hour window), previous day's high/low, the last three days' high/low (this one is a dynamic range that continuously changes), previous week's high/low, previous month's high/low.
  - Static levels are session-oriented highs/lows plus previous day/week/month. The last-three-day range is dynamic.
  - Always note the midpoint (equilibrium) of each range.
  - The higher the time frame of the level, the more likely price will draw to it, because a daily-level old high carries a lot of institutional sponsorship between price and that level ("rocket fuel").
  - He checks his notes, e.g. "previous Monday's Asian session buy side liquidity pool", to make sure he has the right level.
  - Using a 15-minute chart for the week: he calls the 15-minute a "bellwether" time frame; it shows the whole week and every session level.
- **Example in this video:** the 29,984 level (relative equal highs) was Monday's London session buy-side liquidity; Monday's NY AM session buy-side liquidity and Tuesday's AM session buy-side liquidity were other pools he named for the Wednesday–Thursday run.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:57](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1617), [0:28:35](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1715), [0:31:50](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1910), [0:32:38](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1958)

### Relative equal highs/lows: when to expect they will be taken or ignored  (aliases: "guarded buy side", "chaff")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Relative equal highs/lows are not automatically targets and not automatically shorts. He says many students short into relative equal highs and get run.
  - Early in the week, he named the relative equal highs at 29,984 (Monday London buy-side) as his interest and the relative equal lows below as a possible sweep, with nothing lower named. Price swept the lows while remaining bullish and without reaching the daily BISI. He treated that as strong bullish order flow.
  - On the Friday: with relative equal highs just above, he wants evidence they will be guarded. He uses the macro 9:50–10:10 and the first-hour dealing range / close of the opening range (30 minutes long) to decide. If buy-side is being "guarded" during the macro, you will not get your best fill; price might still take those highs, sweep, then go lower.
  - He prefers not to take on losses and wait for a clean read rather than "live through the losses".
- **How to use:** don't short into relative equal highs until bodies show failure (see Rejection Block and iFVG below), and don't "get precise" early. If you are not sure the pool is guarded, wait one more candle.
- **Timestamps:** [0:29:23](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1763), [1:04:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3877), [1:05:24](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3924), [1:13:26](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4406)

### Reading a daily BISI for bullish order flow (upper half, CE, "glad-handing")  (aliases: "bullish order flow", "unfinished business")
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - In a bullish market, smart money wants to buy in the upper half of a BISI (from the candle low of the upper side down to the CE/midpoint). Runs that start in the upper half and run higher confirm bullish order flow.
  - "Glad-handing" (his word for pairing of orders so that business can be booked at that price): if price cannot even trade down to the CE of the gap, that is very bullish, "especially" when no bodies were buried below the CE. A bullish market should leave inefficiencies open, so there is no unfinished business below.
  - He says the evidence is only in the open, high, low and close of the candles. You do not need level 2, depth of market, DOM or footprint (if they agree, fine).
  - Last week's retracement did not touch the CE of the daily BISI. On Tuesday of the current week price again failed to trade to the BISI's high (the low of the candle that forms the gap). He counted these as two confirmations that bullish order flow remained intact.
- **How to identify:** a daily BISI where later retracements stop above the CE and bodies stay out of the lower half.
- **How to use:** treat repeated failure to reach the CE as continuation fuel; keep the bullish draw.
- **Example in this video:** daily NQ BISI formed the week before; the retracement low during the week stopped short of the gap's CE; price then rallied via a bullish breaker toward 29,984 and beyond.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:43](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1183), [0:20:32](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1232), [0:21:19](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1279), [0:22:56](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1376), [0:30:12](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1812)
- **Nuance / caveats:** he says he was the first to teach the idea that a bullish market should leave inefficiencies open; the "bodies are the real volume" idea appears again at 1:19:01.

### Premium wick / wick CE (grading wicks like gaps)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** a wick above where price is currently trading and above the prior close is a premium array and "treated just like a gap". Grade the wick with its consequent encroachment: price trades up to the wick, and if bodies stay below the CE of the wick, it is respected. If the market is below it (e.g. Wednesday's trading and close below the wick), a rally into it is into a premium array. He lists it among the candidates after taking previous week's high: the smoothness of price (a "too smooth" run) is something the market dislikes, and it makes it jagged, so a run goes through candles ("cut through the chaff") before it reaches the next high.
- **How to use (Friday example):** after the short entry, he watches a wick; if bearish there should be "no bodies buried in the upper half of that wick". Price spread its wings twice but closed below, treating the wick as a premium array. Stop sits at its CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:14:50](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=890), [0:16:29](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=989), [1:15:02](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4502), [1:15:48](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4548)

### Rejection block  (aliases: "candle's close that can't be exceeded")
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** a rejection block here is a prior high zone where price goes up to the candle and cannot lay a body (close) above it. On the daily chart on Monday price opened below a candle's close that acted as a rejection block, which created an area saying price was not willing to go above it. He taught the PD arrays in his month 4 mentorship content. If price fails to close above it that is a "check in the box" for failure, then the question is whether price comes back and overlaps the BISI, turning it into an inversion FVG.
- **Example in this video:** Monday opened below the close of the earlier candle; Thursday (large range, PPI day) had not yet closed above the rejection block when he made his call. On Friday, the intraday rejection block is next to the relative equal highs and a BISI below it.
- **Timestamps:** [0:22:08](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1328), [1:09:30](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4170)
- **Nuance / caveats:** captions are loose here ("candlestick's close" vs "highs up close"); take it as the rejection block marked by a candle's high area that bodies cannot close above (caption unclear).

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) after a rejection block
- **Kind:** refinement / entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - On Friday, after failing at the rejection block, price traded above a BISI (overlapping it). He wants the market to come back and respect it as an inversion: a candle that opens, trades up, cannot touch the high of the gap, and closes lower and outside of it is "bearish" in his order-flow reading.
  - He waited for a second test. "Because I'm up against relative equal highs, I just want to have a little bit more behind me." The next candle traded up to it, gave up the ghost, and came down. Then he was comfortable.
  - Bodies are "the real volume", so he does not need the wick to take the high. The wick is simply a gap/inefficiency being delivered to.
  - If an inversion FVG takes out (closes through) a rejection block, then the relative equal highs above are unlikely to be taken.
- **How to use:** the confirmation is a close below the inversion gap, then a retest that fails.
- **Timestamps:** [1:10:17](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4217), [1:13:26](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4406), [1:19:01](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4741), [1:19:48](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4788)

### Silver Bullet-style short with suspension block and wick CE (Friday live example)  (aliases: "Model 2022", "suspension block", "change in state of delivery = order block theory")
- **Kind:** entry model (worked example)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Sequence on Friday 14 Aug: 9:30 opening price; price rallies and forms a high in the first 30 minutes; it pulls back to the 9:30 open and closes below it (bodies below the opening price, "lower high, lower high"). He notes a high-then-low sequence where a low is taken, and says "Model 2022".
  - He calls it silver bullet time and says this is "the classic ICT silver bullet", responding to claims that it stopped working.
  - Entry zone: a small FVG that he labels a suspension block, plus the longest wick to the left. He wants to trade as close as possible to about half of that wick (its CE), even if he does not get the gap itself. The stop must go at least to the CE of the wick / gap above.
  - Because the stop requirement was fairly large, he used only 2 contracts. He says normally he uses larger entries.
  - Result: price rolled over; a body sat at the CE of the bearish suspension block; the low from the open was taken, then dropped. The next candle created a wick with its CE respected; no bodies above the CE; price ultimately hit the low formed at about 20% of the weekly range (TGIF).
  - A second reading of CISD: he says the "change in the state of delivery ... is my order block theory". A trade from an order block / CISD one minute after the macro is fine (see Macro concept). It could also be pyramided.
- **How to use:** entry at the CE of the suspension block/wick, stop above its CE, 2 contracts, exit just before the TGIF target (half of 20–30% = 25%). Executions were 2 in, 2 out.
- **Example in this video:** NQ, 1-minute, Friday 14 Aug 2026: 9:30 open, rally during the opening range to the relative equal highs/week high zone, rejection block failure, BISI turned inversion, short around 10:10–10:15 ET (inferred: "one minute after the macro" ending 10:10), out near the 25% retracement level of the weekly range.
- **Timestamps:** [1:10:17](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4217) — "now it's also silver bullet time"; [1:14:15](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4455) — 2 contracts, bearish suspension block body at CE; [1:22:14](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4934) — proof of fills posted on X; [1:24:38](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=5078) — order block / CISD entry outside the macro

### TGIF — Friday retracement of 20–30% of the weekly range  (aliases ICT uses: "thank God it's Friday", "extraction point")
- **Kind:** rule / entry model (Friday profile)
- **What ICT says:**
  - In bullish weeks, the market usually trades off its high during the week and settles around 20–30% of the weekly range (measured down from the week's high). In bearish weeks the reverse. Price "comes off its extreme" on Fridays.
  - Use the 15-minute chart as the bellwether chart for TGIF reference points; no need to go higher.
  - He halves the 20–30% zone by using the 0.25 level of a Fibonacci (quadrants and octants applied to the week's range). He is personally interested in about 25%.
  - 40% is possible but extreme; given recent volatility, if it does 40% it will probably go deeper, and he does not hold for it. 20% is reasonable and tradable, 30% possible.
  - He also stresses the idea of an extraction point: the "theatre of war" runs from Sunday 6:00 p.m. ET to Friday 5:00 p.m. ET and you need to know where smart money exits. The Friday low-hanging-fruit objective is the TGIF zone, not the previous month's high (the previous month's high merely lines up with it; it is not support/resistance).
  - On the Friday, the week's high had already been taken at 9:00 a.m. ET (the high of the week), then price broke down.
- **How to use:** after a bullish week extends to a new high on Friday morning, expect a retrace; plan partial exits at several levels (see Targets). Do not need to enter at the exact weekly high; avoid being precise at first and take the "big piece of meat in the middle".
- **Example in this video:** NQ weekly range (week high labeled '948', digits unclear in the captions, to the week's low below the relative equal lows swept earlier). Price took the high at about 9:00 a.m. ET Friday, rallied/consolidated, then retraced to the 20% level and then toward 25–30%, which also lined up with the previous month's high and previous week's high.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:24](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=2484) — TGIF rule introduced; [0:42:12](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=2532) — 0.25 level; [0:59:48](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3588); [1:20:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4837) — 20/30/40% discussion
- **Nuance / caveats:** the Friday consumer-sentiment news was medium impact and "a nothing burger" after CPI and PPI.

### Weekly profiles and mapping with the economic calendar  (aliases: "mapping", "consolidation midweek rally", 2017 mentorship weekly profile schematics)
- **Kind:** entry model / bias framework
- **What ICT says:**
  - He builds weekly profiles (schematics of how a bullish or bearish week will deliver across days) before price prints, then maps which days the high-impact news falls on. In this week: CPI Wednesday 8:30 ET, PPI Thursday 8:30 ET, medium-impact consumer sentiment Friday.
  - Result: Monday and Tuesday have reasonable swings you can trade; Wednesday at 8:30 "the carnival ride begins" and continues into Thursday (PPI); Friday is a nothing burger.

### Macro 9:50–10:10 and the opening range  (aliases: "Power of 3 range high/low", "first hour dealing range")
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** the macro 9:50–10:10 ET is important because it falls in the first hour's dealing range and is the close of the opening range, which is 30 minutes long (9:30–10:00). Per his Power of 3 concept, the range high/low are formed between 9:30 and 10:00. During the macro, price may run the highs and sweep, then go lower. If buy-side is being guarded in the macro, you will not get the best fill; wait. Answering "do you only trade inside the macro?": no. When the macro is delivering in close proximity to a PD array you can trade inside it; he entered one minute after, and an order block / CISD entry outside the macro is fine.

### Targets and partials: scale out along several levels, size by stop
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** with 10 contracts he could have taken something off below the first low, at the midpoint, below another low, and so on, leaving a runner. He used 2 contracts only because the stop requirement was larger. Exit "right before" the objective for a low-hanging fruit target. Don't try to be precise early; get the big piece in the middle. For TGIF he aimed for half of 20–30% (25%). He did not want to hold for the previous month's high even though he shared it publicly, because outside interference (headline-driven moves) can reverse it.

### Which half of a PD array is "the meat"  (aliases: "the drumstick")
- **Kind:** refinement (analogy)
- **What ICT says:** a bearish PD array is entered in the lower half of the array (price rises into the lower half, consumes it, then goes lower). A bullish array is the opposite: price drops into the upper half and eats it. Use as the practical test for which half to expect orders in. He applies the same rule to a BISI (upper half = buyers) and to a premium wick (price should not bury bodies in the upper half of the wick when bearish).
- **Timestamps:** [1:16:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4597), [1:17:23](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4643)
- **Nuance / caveats:** the caption wording of the analogy is garbled; the above is my reading of it and matches the BISI upper-half rule at 0:20:32.

### Model selection by market condition; avoiding problem areas
- **Kind:** rule / mindset
- **What ICT says:** not every model works every day because conditions must be ripe. Reversal models are not what he reaches for in a continuation environment (though he may use one on a lower time frame, e.g. 15-minute/5-minute/15-second chart, when bullish and waiting for a reversal at a low). He matches a model to market conditions based on the weekly profile, like a dentist choosing an instrument. Do not trade if you do not know how your model will form today; backtest until you can see it happening in the future. His goal is not to "live through the losses": he doesn't teach "lose 60% and still win". Instead he identified what lost him real money and built logic that tells you what the chart shows before that trap.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Weekly workflow: continuous weekly → previous month high/low → previous week high/low → check front-month → daily PD arrays → 15-minute for sessions and intraday levels.
- 15-minute = the "bellwether" chart for the week and for TGIF reference.
- Session liquidity list: Asian, London, NY AM, NY lunch (2-hour window), previous day, last 3 days, previous week, previous month; always mark midpoints.
- Higher timeframe pool = stronger draw.
- Week runs Sunday 6:00 p.m. ET to Friday 5:00 p.m. ET.
- CPI Wednesday 8:30 ET; PPI Thursday 8:30 ET (this week); Friday consumer sentiment medium impact.
- Macro 9:50–10:10 ET; opening range 9:30–10:00 (30 minutes); first hour dealing range.
- TGIF: 20–30% of weekly range retracement from the high in a bullish week; 25% = halfway; 40% is extreme and not held.
- On bullish week: price took the week's high at 9:00 a.m. ET Friday.
- Tweeted levels: Monday London BSL at 29,984 (his first tweet had a typo, 29,894, which he corrected himself in the same thread).
- Entry: only 2 contracts because the stop to the CE of the wick/gap was relatively far.
- Rejection block: bodies cannot close above it.
- Bearish premium array: no bodies buried in the upper half of the wick.
- Rule for wicks: a wick that sits above current price and above the prior close is graded like a gap with a CE.
- Do not trade support/resistance; know which PD array and which liquidity pool.

## Glossary candidates
`Continuous contract` — back-adjusted/composite futures chart used for key highs, lows and inefficiencies independent of the front-month rollover.
`Bellwether time frame` — the 15-minute chart he uses to see the whole week and every session level.
`Glad-handing` — (his word) pairing of orders that lets business be booked at a price; used to describe whether price can trade to a gap's CE.
`TGIF` — Friday retracement from the weekly extreme into 20–30% of the weekly range.
`Extraction point` — the place where smart money leaves the market (here, the TGIF zone on Friday).
`Weekly profile / mapping` — pre-drawn schematics of how a week will deliver, matched to the economic calendar.
`Suspension block` — (caption) small FVG treated as a block for entry; defined only implicitly.
`Rejection block` — zone where bodies cannot close beyond a prior candle level, marking failure.
`Premium wick` — an upper wick above current price/prior close that behaves like a premium array and is graded with its CE.
`Primary bullish market` — his term for the persisting bullish bias he reads on the higher time frame.
