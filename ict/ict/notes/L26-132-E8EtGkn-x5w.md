## Concepts

### Higher-timeframe premise from the RTH Opening Range Gap  (aliases: key level, session open/close)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Every trade needs a higher-timeframe premise: something on a higher time frame that leads to why the market should go up or down. The RTH opening range gap (previous RTH close to the 9:30 open) counts as a higher time frame key level, "you can't get any more higher time frame than session closings and session opens". The high and low of the gap are defined just by delineation; no labels are needed. Such levels act like new week opening gaps and new day opening gaps. He says 24-hour trading, if it happens, would make things easier (the gap would simply be defined differently, other tools exist).
- **How to identify:** Compare today's 9:30 open with the previous RTH close (4:14 pm ET print). With ETH shown continuously you do not see that gap (the only thing missing on ETH is the 5-6 pm hour gap), so toggle to RTH or use annotations.
- **How to use:** Anchor on the old gap and expect price to return to it (he had said on Feb 4 he felt price would come back up there). Fib/gradient levels inside it matter: price traded into its upper half, then quickly failed.
- **Example in this video:** Feb 4 (Wednesday) RTH ORG: 25,440 and 25,385. Five days later, overnight, price traded up into it, into the upper half between the two levels (a bull flag everyone likes to trade), then "gave up the ghost" and went lower; at 7:00 it was in the upper half; then the pre-market ran a buy-side and reversed.

### Pre-market session bias: 7:00-9:30 ET two-sided liquidity
- **Kind:** rule / model
- **What ICT says:** At 7:00 ET the pre-market begins and the market starts seeking liquidity before the 9:30 bell in a more uniform way; then the real run of the day ensues. For a brand-new trader: mark two definitive liquidity levels (e.g. relative equal highs above, relative equal lows below). It is not imperative to know which will be hit first; whichever hits first, the opposing side is traded to next. When price runs one side at 7:00, that should act as a false breakout, so you look for signatures supporting the other side as the next draw. This is the session bias for the 7:00-9:30 window (he describes it as about "2 and 1/2 hours" and mentions hour-and-a-half chunks).
- **How to identify:** Look back over the London session for relative equal highs and lows. Choose the obvious, smooth ones; if the market gives a perfect relative equal low, anchor just that and ignore messier candidates.
- **How to use:** If buy-side is raided first at 7:00, treat the relative equal lows as the draw; confirm with a lower time frame inefficiency showing willingness to drop, and with price failing to reach the upper half of the old ORG or its low. Even "whichever" logic works for a total beginner who has no bias.
- **Example in this video:** Overnight price traded to the upper half of Feb 4's ORG; at 7:00 a run on a small buy-side pool above (highs lower to the right = a prime BSL candidate), then reversal into consolidation, then lower toward overnight relative equal lows (the red line) while never retesting the lower ORG level.

### Buy-side and sell-side liquidity as stops of the other side  (restated for beginners)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Highs/relative equal highs hold buy stops above them: anyone short places a protective buy stop there. Lows hold the protective sell stops of longs: that is sell-side liquidity. Liquidity is "the lifeblood of any market"; he looks for where the market will draw before volume bars print, whereas volume/footprint data are hindsight.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:32](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1172) — BSL above highs, SSL below lows; [0:24:30](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1470) — liquidity as fuel and anchor points

### Counterparty theory: why smart money shorts old highs
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** When price takes out buy-side, smart money sells short into it, using the resting buy orders (breakout buyers and short stop-outs) as counterparties; they keep throwing sell orders to pair off as many as they can. They then need a lower price to get out: the most advantageous places are old lows and relative equal lows close by, where retail longs have their stops (sell stops that become market sells) and sell at a lower price. The lower target can also be a sell-side discount inefficiency. Every setup uses this counterparty logic. Retail does not spoof; retail traders pull orders and close before their full stop is hit, so depth-of-market numbers may not be there when price arrives.
- **How to use:** "Find the counterparty": you don't need to be right initially about direction; know the liquidity, the inefficiency in between, and the next draw. A trader long who dodged a stop by not being taken out but never banked profit will watch price wilt; when price is near the sell side, the setup is easy.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:45](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1665) — buy side taken, who sells short; [0:28:33](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1713) — where they get out (old lows); [0:36:35](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2195) — retail doesn't spoof; [0:39:47](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2387) — handshake with existing interest, counterparty theory

### Institutional order flow entry drill on a small-time-frame FVG (SIBI short)  (aliases: sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency, cookie cutter)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** Once the draw is known (here the relative equal lows), drop to the 15-second or 5-second chart. Find a SIBI (the captions read "sell side of bounce by side efficiency" = sellside imbalance, buyside inefficiency) left during the move down. Price must trade up into it, and the upper half must be respected: wicks may enter, but "we don't want to see a body left in it". A weak market will not close the gap in one pass; it trades up into it and rejects. That is the highest-probability FVG: "it needs to show a willingness to stay open". Re-entries are allowed; he shows two separate entries into the same type of gap and a 5-second repeat of the 15-second orange box.
- **How to identify:** The FVG is small, formed within a run toward a defined draw, and the lower half is where bodies stay. He demands a defined dynamic price range (the FVG high to the target low) with enough range to yield 20 handles.
- **How to use:** Enter short as price trades up into it; stop above candle number one's high plus one tick; target one tick below the sell-side pool. If stopped out and price comes back to the lower half, re-enter with the stop again above candle one's high plus 1 tick.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 15s/5s at about 9:30-9:45 ET after the 7:00 buy-side raid: price consolidated, a SIBI formed during the drop; price traded up into it, respected the upper half (wick only), traded lower, came back up trading just shy of the next inefficiency, then broke lower toward the overnight relative equal lows.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:56](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1856) — institutional order flow entry drill; [0:31:43](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1903) — weak market won't close the gap; [0:33:20](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2000) — 15s order block CISD, wicks allowed; [0:43:01](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2581) — valid short: body stayed in the lower half
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He says this is the "cookie cutter": where is the market going to draw to? He calls it Robin's-cup-winning stuff and says the same skill works on any asset class or time frame.

### Stop and re-entry rules for FVG entries
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Selling inside a SIBI: the stop goes above the high of candle number one (the first candle of the three-candle pattern), plus one tick. Buying inside a bullish inefficiency: stop below candle number one's low, one tick. If you are stopped out and price later returns to the lower half of the same gap (for shorts), that is another entry with the stop again at candle one plus one tick. When a trade looks about to get stopped but pulls away, that is stop-loss management that he will not teach.

### Target: one tick below the sell-side liquidity pool; minimum 20 handles
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** For a beginner, don't get greedy: aim to exit one tick below the level you think is the sell-side pool (or one tick short of the target). Expect more protraction beyond it, but the beginner objective is "just below the line". Determine how much range there is; he wants 20 handles (or 20 pips in forex). On a micro that is about $2 per point (20 handles = $40, "the biggest 40 bucks you'll ever make" the first time, in real money).

### Immediate rebalance entry technique
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:** An immediate rebalance: a candle opens leaving a gap, and the very next candle comes right back and closes the gap in one pass. This is the strongest sign that the filling candle either wilts and goes directly in the direction you expect, or the very next candle does. It is "immediate feedback". He uses it as the entry in a no-clean-setup spot after a consolidation at 9:30: a candle closes down, the next candle opens, comes back up closing the gap at once, then the bottom falls out and price runs lower into sell-side delivery, ending at the RTH ORG high (the unfulfilled side) as a micro scalp.
- **How to use:** Look for runs that are not complete, find small gaps and wait for price to trade up into them; that puts you in the direction of the incomplete run.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 7:00 ET = start of pre-market; 9:30 ET = opening bell; opening ORG gap uses 4:14 pm ET prior close.
- Whichever side of liquidity is taken first, the opposing side is the next draw.
- For short entries in a SIBI: stop above candle one's high + 1 tick; for longs: below candle one's low - 1 tick.
- Upper half of a bearish FVG must be respected: wicks yes, bodies no.
- Target one tick below the SSL pool; minimum ~20 handles (or 20 pips).
- Micro: $2 per point ($0.50 per tick, four ticks per handle); mini: $20 per point ($5 per tick).
- Risk: never more than 1% per trade; technically one quarter to one half of 1% for the first two years.
- Scaling: start with one micro; add one micro for each +$2,500 in equity; remove one contract for each -$2,500 drawdown.
- Use just one micro on demo; collect examples in a journal; tape read before you demo trade.
- Check lows: choose the smoothest relative equal lows/highs and anchor only those.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Remove mysticism: small repeatable steps beat big promises; setting realistic expectations ("lower the expectation... Olympic level performer is not necessary").
- Risk tolerance is paramount: if you cannot sit through a trade with $15-25k risk you will not take a half-million-dollar trade in one pull.
- One micro reduces the stress to binary: did it work or not; you gather data and weather uncertainty.
- Beginners will wrestle with "am I right?"; stop-outs are scary at first; "learning curve you have to submit to".
- Don't chase trade-sizing hype ("30, 50, 100 lots on minis is ridiculous" in this volatility).
- Retail pulling orders before the stop vs smart money order handling.
- Children-on-a-road-trip analogy: impatience about arriving; experience gives the perspective.
