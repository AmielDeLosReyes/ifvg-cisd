## Summary
ICT lectures on NQ (September 2026 mini contract) over one trading week and names the logic "chain of custody of price": how one specific PD array/candlestick, formed at a specific time and price, causes price to stop or pass, and then hand off to the next PD array, as an algorithm - not buying/selling pressure, order counts or volume. On the daily chart he uses a suspension block, two "discount wicks" whose consequent encroachment (CE) price must trade through with bodies, and a daily SIBI from the prior Thursday as a premium array. On the 1-minute chart he collects each day's RTH opening range gap CE, Monday's first presented FVG, and the graded levels of those arrays, noting how price reacts at them. The second half walks through his Thursday trade: an implied (not yet delivered) dealing range anchored on the body-high of a SIBI candle (never a wick) down to Monday's relative equal lows (28,701), with each PD array along the way validated by sitting on a graded level and by "no bodies in the upper half." He closes with how to study this: collect data daily, backtest, practice in hindsight, and keep a notepad of calls.

## Concepts

### Chain of custody of price  (aliases ICT uses: chain of custody and control of price, one PD array to the next, algorithm "source code")
- **Kind:** new coinage / definition (framing concept for the whole lecture)
- **What ICT says:**
  - We study "the chain of custody from one PD array to the next," not buying/selling pressure, not how many orders or volume increases/decreases at a price. "None of that means anything."
  - It is the same mechanism inside any defined dealing range, whether already delivered or not yet delivered.
  - It is "my algorithm doing exactly how it's coded to do. Every little fluctuation has a purpose." If markets were not algorithmic, these things would not keep repeating.
  - It tells you "how one PD array, one specific candlestick, and how it's formed at what time and price" makes price not move higher ("you shall not pass") or go past.
  - Differences from retail S/R: grading a specific inefficiency "removes all the ambiguity of which high, which low would I use"; 50 traders drawing trend lines in a room will not agree; his rectangles look similar but "the logic is unique to me only."
  - He claims no one else teaches the specific logic: bodies not allowed in the upper half of a bearish PD array, CE/octant validation, unrealized/implied dealing range anchoring.
- **How to identify:** a sequence of dated PD arrays (daily wick CE, daily SIBI, volume imbalance, RTH ORG CE levels, first presented FVG) with each next level predicted before price gets there.
- **How to use:** (1) establish bias/draw on liquidity; (2) pre-draw the levels you will watch (written on a notepad / annotated before the move); (3) validate each PD array by graded-level contact and body behavior; (4) trade from the validated array toward the next level.
- **Example in this video:** The week's chain: premium array (last Thursday's daily SIBI, pink) -> up into it -> sell off to the higher volume imbalance of the daily suspension block (purple, the draw) -> next: CE of the suspension block. Intraday Thursday chain: SIBI high -> octants -> measuring gap at 50% -> iFVG -> 28,701.

### Daily wick CE ("discount wick") and body-through rule  (aliases ICT uses: lowest stretched-out wick, consequent encroachment of a wick, discount wick, premium sensitivity)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the daily chart there are two discount wicks (a higher and a lower one), the lowest stretched candlestick wick in the move. Price must trade down below the wick's midpoint (CE) and close below to confirm; trading through the CE on its own "may or may not go lower"; he wants a body through relative equal lows, not just a wick ("insignificant ... with just the wick").
  - Friday's delivery gave a pronounced body run below the relative equal lows.
  - For a bearish stance: the premium (upper) half of a wick is the "premium sensitivity" side - bodies should stay heavy in the lower half; if price goes into the upper half it must show strong rejection.
  - He had called "we're going lower" on Wednesday night's and Thursday's commentary.
  - Rule of halves: if bullish, a revisited inefficiency/wick holds the upper half (price trades down to at least half); if bearish, price goes down to at least half and lower.
- **How to identify:** locate the lowest, most stretched wick on the daily chart; mark its midpoint; note the later candle that closes below it with a body.
- **How to use:** once price closes below the wick CE with a body, expect further lower prices toward the next PD array below (volume imbalance, suspension block CE); re-test of the CE from below should fail.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily: price trades down through the CE of the daily wick and closes below on Friday; next the market opens, trades up into a daily fair value gap and trades lower; then trades into the higher discount-wick CE; target then the daily volume imbalance.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:19](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=259) — wick CE, close below; [0:05:53](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=353) — wick-only run insufficient, want bodies; [0:07:30](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=450) — premium sensitivity of wick upper half

### Suspension block, volume imbalance as draw, premium array down to discount array  (aliases ICT uses: SIBI/BISI suspension block, higher volume imbalance, purple box, pink box)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The daily buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (BISI) that is a "suspension block" has a volume imbalance on its high end and one on its low end; the purple volume imbalance was the draw on liquidity last week and this week; the pink box on the lower-timeframe charts is last Thursday's daily SIBI (a premium array).
  - "Think of it like this: premium array down to discount array." If price goes below the volume imbalance, it gravitates to the CE (halfway) of the suspension block; the CE is the next target.
  - Deep discount: below last Friday's low and below the relative equal lows with bodies.
- **How to use:** draw on liquidity = the next array down; target the CE of the suspension block if the volume imbalance is breached.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Thursday: price rallies up into the pink daily SIBI (premium) and the premium side is rejected; it sells to the purple volume imbalance (~28,891.25 minor buy-side noted there), turns there at least twice; later closes into the next imbalance region; ICT notes it did not take the whole suspension block in one candle.

  - Also carry the CE of the two daily discount wicks and the graded levels of last Thursday's daily SIBI onto the 1-minute chart.
  - He shows each day's RTH ORG: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday (measured close to open, CE), Thursday, Friday; price repeatedly reacts at these CEs; once price trades through one, that CE acts as a premium array.

  - Convergence: on Thursday the CE levels of Monday, Tuesday and Thursday sat in close proximity, giving a cluster; the highest and lowest of the cluster define a range; above the cluster the market is more likely to sell off, below it to rally. With a bearish bias, anything above Thursday/Tuesday/Monday first-instance FVG and the upper half of the higher discount wick is "a premium market."
- **How to identify:** RTH ORG per day = 9:30 open vs prior settlement; use the CE; first FVG after 9:30 on Monday.
- **How to use:** build a map of CEs; when several cluster, treat as one decision zone; with a bearish bias, sell rallies into the cluster/premium.

### Implied (unrealized) dealing range anchored on the body-high, not the wick  (aliases ICT uses: unrealized dealing range, implied dealing range, inception of the price run, finite level)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (the key lesson)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Thursday's range: anchor at the high of a SIBI candle (the body high of the down-close candle) and measure down to Monday's relative equal lows (= Tuesday's electronic-trading-hours sell-side liquidity) at 28,701. The draw is that finite level.
  - Why not the wick high: "the wick is a gap that's allowed to do the damage"; "the bodies tell you the real narrative"; anchoring to the candle body high defines "a range that has yet to be delivered."
  - If you do this anchoring wrong, "everything I'm about to outline will not work for you" - he predicts viewers will anchor to wrong points over the weekend and say it doesn't work.
  - When ranges are unrealized you are only looking at a target/draw; knowing where the price run begins ("the inception of the price run") must be learned from backtesting and collecting information.
  - Known from the moment the SIBI-forming candles close and the next opens: the draw is Monday's relative equal lows and the anchor is the SIBI candle's high.
  - Then "you have to go to a very specific PD array where it makes sense on a higher time frame": the CE of the daily wick (pink line).
- **How to identify:** (1) find a SIBI/down-close candle at the start of the run from a premium PD array; (2) anchor on its body high; (3) anchor the other end at the finite old low/high (relative equal lows) that is the draw; (4) grade the range into octants/quadrants/CE.
- **How to use:** short only from PD arrays that touch the graded levels of this range; targets: the draw (28,701), partials at graded levels; expect no retracement beyond the 50% once below it.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Thursday: premium wick at the CE of the daily wick; SIBI body anchored from its high down to 28,701 (the Monday relative equal lows); gradient levels listed: high, then next lines down to the draw. The SIBI laid on the upper octant; the SIBI's lower boundary (high of the third candle) was 28,941 even "to the tick."
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:18](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1638) — anchor the body high not wick; [0:28:08](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1688) — implied dealing range, inception from backtesting; [0:31:18](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1878) — no projection off a wick; most important part; [0:34:29](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2069) — SIBI on upper octant, you know the draw
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Contradicts anchoring from wick extremes (e.g. earlier events that used full wick range); here bodies define volume of the move.

### "No bodies in the upper half" rule (premium sensitivity) for bearish PD arrays; wick that fails to reach CE is bearish  (aliases ICT uses: premium sensitivity, body in the upper half, keeps the body outside)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - If bearish and price trades back up into a SIBI/bearish order block/fair value gap, it must not leave a body in the upper half of that PD array. Bodies stay below/outside: "bodes well for holding."
  - If the wick cannot even touch the CE of an inefficiency, "that's bearish... it can't even deliver a midpoint."
  - The same logic applies on every PD array in the sequence, and he says it applies to wicks (candle with a wick sitting on a gradient level: bodies not allowed in its upper half, from close to midpoint).
  - "I deny it going above" - the wick is respected: he asks why a given wick is used and not another: because it touches a graded level; the first wick utilizing that level counts; later wicks that do not touch the line are only "candles with wicks."
  - Premium/discount lens: when bearish and in a premium array, any market structure shift lower after being in premium should see lower prices delivered.
- **How to identify:** SIBI/bearish OB/FVG sitting on an octant/quadrant; check subsequent candles' bodies relative to the upper half or the gap's CE.
- **How to use:** sell the retrace into such an array (or add/pyramid) while bodies remain out of the upper half; failing to reach the CE is bearish confirmation; patience ("submit yourself to time"); if nervous take one partial off.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday: the first SIBI on the upper octant; the following candle opens, trades up and falls short without touching the OB; price breaks lower; a wick laying on Monday's RTH ORG CE holds with no bodies in its upper half; a later SIBI at the .375 level has a wick that cannot touch its CE.
- **Timestamps:** [0:28:57](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1737) — no bodies in upper half of SIBI; [0:36:07](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2167) — "cannot leave a body in the upper half"; [0:39:19](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2359) — wick on Monday RTH ORG CE, no bodies in upper half; [0:44:09](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2649) — wick fails to touch CE = bearish
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Same family as earlier "bodies must stay out of the upper half" in L26-045/048; here stated as an explicit rule applying to wicks too.

### Bearish order block validated by a graded level and by CISD  (aliases ICT uses: bearish order block, change in the state of delivery, premium entry pyramiding)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - The PD array has to touch or lay on a graded level (here the upper quadrant) to be "perfect."
  - Bearish OB = an up-close candle at the start of the delivery lower. As soon as a candle trades down through its opening price - it need not close below - that validates it as a change in the state of delivery.
  - Any move back up into it is where you can sell short or add, "pyramiding" premium entries for a larger position down.
  - Price breaks lower after trading up into the bearish OB.
- **How to identify:** up-close candle whose range touches the upper quadrant of the implied dealing range; next candle trades down through its open.
- **How to use:** short on retraces into the OB, add on further retraces; hold toward the draw.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday after the upper-octant SIBI: candle opens, trades up, falls short of the array; then CISD through the open of the up-close candle; price later reaches the OB again and breaks lower.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:54](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2214) — OB must touch the upper quadrant, CISD through the open validates; [0:37:44](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2264) — add and pyramid
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Fits the definition of an OB as "up-close candle before the down delivery"; the new rule is that it must sit on a graded level.

### Measuring gap at the 50% of the implied dealing range  (aliases ICT uses: measuring gap, midpoint, halfway point)
- **Kind:** refinement / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - The 50% level of the range (bearish OB high to the 28,701 draw); a fair value gap that forms exactly there and does not close in is a "measuring gap" (he says he did not invent the term; he is the only one telling you where it will form before it forms).
  - Implication: price should accelerate; little retracement; it can consolidate but retracements should not be seen: "drop lower and speed up, or drop, consolidate, and drop harder."
  - Below the halfway point: consolidate and drop hard or no retracement at all; "we're just not anticipating price having some wild retracements."
- **How to identify:** FVG forming right at the 50% of the graded implied range, with no overlap/closing afterwards.
- **How to use:** hold the short; do not expect a deep retracement; take partials at the next graded levels.
- **Example in this video:** NQ Thursday 1m: FVG at the 50% line forms and never trades back into; price drops aggressively; next candle lays on the lower octant (.375 level).
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:46](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2506) — 50 level of the range; [0:42:33](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2553) — measuring gap not closed, expect acceleration; [0:44:56](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2696) — below halfway: consolidate and drop, no wild retracements
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In L26-047 he used "breakaway gap" for a gap at the CE of the RTH ORG; "measuring gap" is the traditional term for the same position (gap at the midpoint of a move).

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Chain of custody: no volume/order-count/buying-selling-pressure logic; study handoff between PD arrays.
- Daily wick CE: price should trade through CE and close below with a body; wick-only runs are insufficient.
- Bearish: bodies stay in the lower half; if price goes into the upper half of a wick/SIBI/FVG, it must show strong rejection; no bodies in the upper half.
- Wick failing to touch the CE of an inefficiency = bearish.
- Anchor an unrealized dealing range at the body high (not the wick) of the SIBI candle and the finite draw (relative equal lows); no projection off a wick.
- A PD array must touch/lay on a graded level (octant/quadrant) to be valid; CISD = candle trades down through the up-close OB's open (need not close below).
- Measuring gap at the 50%: not closed -> acceleration, no retracement; below 50%: consolidate and drop or just drop.
- Daily RTH ORG CE and Monday's first presented FVG: extend through the week.
- 4x timeframe multiples: 15m -> 1h -> 4h -> 8h.

## Glossary candidates
`CHAIN OF CUSTODY (OF PRICE)` — how price is handed from one specific PD array/candle to the next along graded levels.
`IMPLIED / UNREALIZED DEALING RANGE` — a range whose far end (the draw) is a finite level not yet reached; anchor the near end on the candle body, not the wick.
`PREMIUM SENSITIVITY` — a bearish array's upper half where price should show strong rejection (no bodies).
`MEASURING GAP` — an FVG forming at the 50% of the implied range and not closed; signals acceleration.
`FIRST INSTANCE / FIRST PRESENTED FVG` — the first gap after the 9:30 open; Monday's is carried through the week.
`DISCOUNT WICK` — a stretched lower wick whose CE is a daily key level.
`MOHAWK` — small wick spike that pokes at a gap boundary while bodies stay inside.
`PYRAMIDING` — adding to shorts on successive retraces into a validated OB.
`BELLWETHER TIME FRAME` — the 15-minute chart (multiples of four lead to 1h, 4h, 8h).
