## Summary
ICT shows a first-two-months-of-learning style session: instead of demanding a perfect setup, he reads price against a layered map of key levels and records hypothetical entries, partials and stops, accepting several small stop-outs. The map blends higher-timeframe arrays (a big daily BISI graded in octants/quadrants, prior days' first presented FVGs) with the 9:30 RTH opening range gap (settlement vs open, graded with fib levels). A huge overnight drop of roughly 400-500 handles leaves a very large discount gap above, so he leans long and wants a "low hanging fruit" objective, found where a daily graded level and an opening-range-gap graded level coincide (29,212). Along the way he shows precise wick-midpoint and close rules for confirmation, how an iFVG is not valid until a body closes through, how a one-tick volume imbalance acts as support, and how to manage with cost-covering stops and partials. The point for new students is to learn the feel of chop and stop-outs by watching and journaling, not to chase moves.

## Concepts

### RTH Opening Range Gap (settlement vs 9:30 open) — large-gap handling  (aliases: ORG, "opening range gap", overnight gap, RTH OG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (re-application of the concept from [[L26-019]])
- **What ICT says:**
  - The gap runs from the prior day's regular-trading-hours settlement (captioned "4:14 p.m. Eastern", cash session end; the transcript garbles it as "9:14 p.m." on the open side) to the 9:30 ET open. Here Monday's settlement was about 29,511.5 (caption "0.9511 and a half") and price opened far below, a discount gap of "400 handles or more".
  - He grades the gap with fib levels from the 9:30 open up to the settlement: lines are counted off from the open ("one, two, three, four") with the fourth being the gap's midpoint / consequent encroachment. Normally price trades to the gap midpoint "with 70% likelihood by 10:00 a.m."; on this day it had not happened yet, so he treated it as "deferred delivery" and said price could skip it entirely.
  - On days with a huge gap you "always" should at least give price an attempt to trade into the gap. It may be moot (the market can be too weak and keep selling), but if you try and get stopped out that is okay, because a stop-out is usually followed by an easier setup.
  - Because of the size of the gap and "a lot of open air space up there", he says he is "heavier-handed on the longs": long entries are more likely to yield an exaggerated run in your favor than shorts on a day with that big a gap. He says this does not mean it cannot go lower.
  - The ORG and the 9:30 open line are drawn on the chart so that opening-range-gap levels can be compared directly with the daily array levels.
  - Alternative scenario he voiced pre-open: price could first run higher to sweep the early buy side (the stops of breakout sellers), then drop to try the lower octant of the daily array and bounce.
- **How to identify:** prior RTH settlement line; 9:30 open; measure open-to-settlement; midpoint (CE) = target price is statistically drawn to by 10:00.
- **How to use:** a gap above price after a big overnight drop is a bias toward long entries; use ORG fib levels as objectives; if the gap closure does not get a chance to start by mid-morning, do not fade it blindly ("I don't want to fade that on a day like today").
- **Example in this video:** NQ, open below Monday's ~29,511.5 settlement after an overnight run of about 500 handles lower; hypothetical longs from ~29,0xx–29,13x with the buy-side pools (initial intraday high, equal/"smooth" highs) and 29,212 as objectives; ORG midpoint not reached in the first hour.
- **Timestamps:** [0:01:50](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=110) settlement and gap size · [0:26:32](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1592) always attempt the gap · [0:40:34](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2434) why heavier on longs · [0:56:42](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3402) gap midpoint, 70% by 10:00

### Confluence of daily array grading with ORG levels → "low hanging fruit" objective  (aliases: layered key levels, octant / quadrant grading)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - If there is an inefficiency on the daily timeframe (here the gray BISI box from Part 1) and you grade it with fib levels (quadrants = 25%, octants = 12.5%), and one of those levels agrees exactly with a level on the opening range gap grading, that level becomes an obvious objective: "two levels lining right up at 29,212".
  - He cares more about reaching that shared level than about reaching the gap midpoint, even though the midpoint is also likely, because of NFP week, a day after significant consolidation, and a big overnight run lower: "I want to have a low hanging fruit objective because it's part of a consolidation profile day."
  - Once at that level: partial there, then watch whether price shows "unction" (interest) to keep going or gives up and breaks lower.
  - At the end he stresses you do not need order-flow tools or "inside the candle" data: open, high, low, close and time form the whole story ("you can read that book by its cover").
- **How to identify:** daily BISI graded in 8 parts; ORG graded from open to settlement; look for a price where one level from each lines up. He describes it as the "lowest octant / lower quadrant" of the ORG coinciding with the daily array's level; caption mapping of which octant is which is unclear. 29,212 is the same number as the 50% line of the daily BISI in [[L26-019]] (29,211.25) and sits inside the upper half of that array per this video.
- **How to use:** target = the shared level; stop = below the structure of the entry; first partial at or just before it; trail the remainder to the next level (here Wednesday Aug 26's first presented FVG and the octant inside it).
- **Example in this video:** hypothetical longs from 29,067.5 onward targeting 29,212; the runner partial was filled at 29,213 (~1:05:51) and the last contract went to the octant within Wed Aug 26's first presented FVG.
- **Timestamps:** [0:54:17](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3257) two things layer at same level · [0:57:30](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3450) why 29,212 is the objective · [1:05:51](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3951) partial at 213 · [1:22:23](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4943) "no-brainer", read the candle

### Wick consequent encroachment: close confirmation rules  (aliases: wick CE, "dash line", mile marker)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - When price stutters inside an array it "needs to close above this wick's halfway point (consequent encroachment)". A close above the dashed midpoint line is confirmation that price is likely to clear the buy-side above.
  - If a wick sits just above and a candle fails to close above its CE, price needs to build a close above it with the next candle or the very next one after: "That wick needs to be completely overtaken by this candle or the very next. Preferably this one. If it's this candle, that means it's really strong."
  - For longs, bodies should stay in the upper half of the wick's range; a down-close candle sitting in that zone is watched for rejection. Mirrors the short-side logic from Part 1 (bodies stay below the wick midpoint).
  - A wick high of a candle that is taken out (price closes above it) is also read as proof price can reach the pools above.
- **How to use:** the CE of a recent prominent wick is a checkpoint; failure to close above (long) tells you to tighten risk rather than hope.
- **Example in this video:** at ~9:35-9:40 price wicks up to a prior wick, rejects; he waits for a body close above the wick; the next candle closes above the wick and above the high (the intraday high at "43"), then runs to the 29,118.5 buy-side level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:42](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1002) close above the CE dash line · [0:18:54](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1134) not yet confirmed · [0:19:46](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1186) overtaken by this candle or the next · [0:21:25](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1285) confirmed

### Inversion FVG — validity requires a close; not-yet-inverted as information  (aliases: iFVG)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A first presented FVG formed on a down close is treated as potential iFVG: "we don't want to see it behave as a premium array to sell off and go lower". He wants price to go above it, come back down into it, treat it as a foothold (support) and run into the buy-side pools above.
  - It becomes an inversion fair value gap only if price closes below it after having taken out a high: "the close below is what warrants it". Price moving (wicking) below the gap on a candle or two without a close below does not count.
  - If it does not invert, "it didn't tell us it wants to go lower based on the rules I got", which supports favoring the long side.
  - Also noted pre-trade: if a candle's low is left partly open (price does not completely close back into that candle's low), the next candle could open up, trade down and use that void as a "foothold" to launch higher.
- **How to use:** use the iFVG/no-iFVG status as the tiebreaker for direction; an unconfirmed iFVG keeps the "mode" you were in.
- **Example in this video:** shaded orange block from the first presented FVG; price traded below it twice without a close below; he stayed long at 29,132.
- **Timestamps:** [0:15:40](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=940) orange gap as potential iFVG · [0:43:26](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2606) "becomes a potential iFVG if we close below it" · [0:44:48](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2688) "close below is what warrants it" · [0:45:39](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2739) stays long
- **Nuance / caveats:** Reinforces "close, not wick" as the inversion criterion, a point readers who already know iFVGs should double-check in their own definition.

### Volume imbalance (one-tick) as support; keep it open  (aliases: "buy side of balance outside efficiency", institutional order flow entry drill)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the space between one candle's close and the next candle's open. Example: close 29,133.25, next open 29,133.50, a one-tick (0.25) difference, still a real volume imbalance.
  - If order flow is bullish the imbalance should stay open and not be filled; price only "dips its toe" into the zone (a partial, institutional reference gap closure) and moves on. He does not want a full closure.
  - Price that did not trade down to the candle's close produced "this nice little reaction": the leftover void is where buyers defend.
  - He treats such an entry as an "institutional order flow entry drill": get long as it touches that open space.
  - This array layers with the opening range gap's 9:30 open level, giving two reasons at the same level.
- **How to use:** entry/hold zone for longs; stop just below the candle low.
- **Example in this video:** the 29,133.25 close / 29,133.50 open pair; long at 29,132, stop about 21 handles below, two minis; a partial at ~29,135 (cost-covering stop).
- **Timestamps:** [0:48:10](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2890) bounce on a buy-side balance array · [0:48:57](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2937) don't want full closure · [0:51:04](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3064) one-tick imbalance explained

### Retracement range re-delivery (measured projection) for partials and stop trailing  (aliases: "range redelivering the same range"; ICT calls it an "advanced topic")
- **Kind:** refinement / new idea
- **What ICT says:**
  - Take the small rally-then-pullback range (high to the pullback low). Project that same range upward from the new high: if price trades up to that projected level it is "this range redelivering that same range".
  - Rules he gives: if price trades to the projected level, bring the stop to just below the low of the pullback; if it does not, stay where you are (stop at that candle's low). The projected line is also used as the partial level (e.g. "partial just below this line, which is simply this range of retracement projected above").
  - Why: from that level a deeper retracement is more likely; do not give back more than that. Worst case: you raise the stop, price spikes to the level and then immediately reverses in one candle: "rare, but it can happen".
- **Timestamps:** [1:09:30](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4170) projection explained · [1:10:18](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4218) deeper retracement risk · [1:12:51](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4371) used for partial
- **Nuance / caveats:** He eyeballs the lines ("I'm just eyeballing it"); no exact number given.

### Trade management used in the session: cover-cost stops, partials, trailing under candle lows
- **Kind:** rule / management
- **What ICT says (as practiced):**
  - Enter small to "test the water"; initial stop just below the structure low (e.g. long at 29,067.5, stop 29,050 = 17.5 pts; a long near 29,132 with ~21-handle stop; the shorts used a 130-ish stop, ~35 handles, with two then four minis).
  - Move the stop to "cover costs" (entry plus a few points for commissions; e.g. up to 29,070 or 29,135) as soon as the trade is working or at the first partial.
  - Partials at buy-side levels (e.g. 29,118.5 ≈ 49-51 handles on a first partial); after a partial, trail the stop to just below the low of the current candle or the hypothetical "29,100" (locking about 32.5 handles).
  - Roll the stop up only when a candle closes with the body in the upper half of a key wick, or when price reaches a defined partial level.
  - Do not hold a stop that is tied to an invalid reference: when a reference line is no longer valid, delete it and place the stop under the new reference.
  - Final runners are exited at named levels (Wed Aug 26 first presented FVG low; then the octant inside it).
  - Day tally (his): one losing transaction (about 20 handles) against about 50 gained at the 1:03 mark, net about 125-130 handles on the day by that point, with more partials after.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- 70% of the time the ORG midpoint (CE) is traded to by 10:00 AM.
- Big gap (400+ handles): always try the gap at least once; being stopped out is acceptable and often precedes an easier setup.
- No candle bodies in the lower half of the first presented FVG/wick you are long from; wicks in are acceptable.
- Wick CE rule: for longs, close above the wick's midpoint; the wick must be overtaken by that candle or the very next.
- iFVG requires a close (body) beyond the gap; trading beyond it without a close does not invert it.
- 10:00 ET medium-impact news: expect whipsaw; do not set a tight stop right before it.
- 10:30 ET marks one hour of dealing range; more movement, gap more likely to stay open.
- Micro = $2/pt ($0.50/tick), mini = $20/pt ($5/tick), 4 ticks per point.
- Hypothetical NQ numbers (approximate, captions unclear): long 29,067.5 stop 29,050 to 29,070; partial 29,118.5; 29,100 trail; 29,212/29,213 objective; long 29,132; octant of Wed Aug 26 FPFVG last draw.

## Process, risk & psychology
- "Pantomiming a new student": the first two months should be tape reading and journaling, not demo trading. Say the hypothetical entry, stop and partial aloud and write them down ([0:32:18](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1938): "Just watching ain't enough", keep a notepad; [1:13:46](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4426)).
- Do not worry about pretend money; the value is reading price action around the specific levels taught in the lectures ([1:03:17](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3797)).
- Be forgiving of yourself when chopped up; experience the chop while only observing, so you learn the cost of chasing ([0:31:27](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1887)).
- Don't be upset about a stop-out after a partial banked 20 handles; being upset is "foolish especially if you're brand new" ([1:00:07](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=3607)).
- Accept being wrong sometimes ("I could be wrong still. It happens") and having a losing transaction; he wants students to see a loser in the day ([1:17:29](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4649)).
- Jokes: the "dead pull technique" (mentally "pulling" price up) is a joke about wishful thinking, not a technique ([0:20:37](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1237)).
- Slow, long videos are justified: explaining two hours of price action on a candle-by-candle basis cannot be said in fewer words.

## Glossary candidates
`BISI` — buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (caption "buy side of balance outside efficiency"); bullish gap-type array.
`ORG / RTH Opening Range Gap` — gap between the prior regular-trading-hours settlement and the 9:30 ET open.
`First presented fair value gap` — the first FVG formed in a session (day), kept as a level into following days.
`Low hanging fruit objective` — nearest high-probability target, especially on a consolidation-profile day.
`Foothold` — an array price dips into and then launches from.
`Handle` — one point (1.00) of price movement; 4 ticks on NQ.
`Cover costs` — stop moved to entry plus enough to pay commissions.
`Octant / quadrant` — 12.5% / 25% steps of a graded range.
