## Concepts

### Premium vs. Discount Sensitivity
- **Kind:** definition + filter
- **What ICT says:** When a PD array (FVG, imbalance, inefficiency) is in its first utilization (original direction), it shows **premium sensitivity** = bodies allowed only in the lower half (bearish) or upper half (bullish), with wicks allowed to exceed the boundary. When inverted, it flips—discount sensitivity = bodies in the opposite half. This is the key to reading smart money intent: watch bodies, not wicks. A wick touching a level is permissible; a body crossing into the forbidden half signals loss of conviction.
- **How to identify:** 
  - **Premium sensitivity (bearish FVG, first utilization):** Candle bodies stay in lower half of the FVG; wicks can wick into upper half. High bodies above the midpoint = loss of premium.
  - **Discount sensitivity (bullish inversion, second utilization):** Candle bodies stay in upper half; wicks can wick down into lower half. Low bodies below the midpoint = loss of discount.
- **How to use:** When you see bodies violating the expected half, reduce conviction. Use it as a warning signal. If you're short and bodies start printing in the upper half of a bearish FVG, the setup is weakening. If you're long and bodies print in the lower half of a bullish inversion, the setup is weakening.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ 1m, April 21, 2026. Bearish FVG formed; ICT expects bodies in lower half (premium sensitivity). When bodies respect the lower half, the short setup is valid. Later, an inversion FVG forms—ICT looks for bodies in upper half (discount sensitivity). If bodies can't sustain in the upper half, the bullish setup is invalid and price should revert to the bearish FVG's first utilization.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:17](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=977), [0:17:51](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1071), [0:28:07](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1687), [0:39:11](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2351)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** A wick that touches the boundary is always permissible (called a "mohawk"—a small wick outside the FVG). It doesn't invalidate the FVG. What matters is the body placement on the next candle(s). Bodies in the forbidden half = loss of structure.

### Low-Probability Conditions & Trading Discipline
- **Kind:** process + psychology
- **What ICT says:** When the economic calendar has major events (Fed speaker, news, data releases), precision drops. The market becomes 50/50 because the data can go either way. In these conditions, the "correct" bias may no longer matter—one piece of news can reverse everything in seconds. Smart trading in low-probability conditions is: (1) identify your model/bias, (2) wait for price action to confirm it, (3) if price doesn't confirm, do nothing and lose zero dollars. Forcing a trade because you're "right" in low-probability conditions is gambler's mentality.
- **How to identify:** Check the economic calendar (Forex Factory). Look for "high impact" events (Fed speakers, major data releases, FOMC decisions). If these are happening, assume low probability until proven otherwise.
- **How to use:** Don't force entries. Wait for your target level to be reached. If it's not reached before the event, accept it and walk away. You're not "wrong"; you're just waiting. "If information presents itself, I'll call candlesticks. If it doesn't, I'm waiting for more information." Doing nothing is a valid trade decision.
- **Example in this video:** April 21, 2026, 10:00 ET Fed chair speaks. ICT was looking for MNQ to drop to Monday's RTH opening range gap low (~26,695). Instead, the Fed caused unpredictable moves. He took a small short position early (when the bias was clear), but reduced conviction as news approached. By 10:00, he was watching, not trading, because "low probability for precision."

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Low probability doesn't mean zero probability. It means you adjust your risk management and accept that your model may not work today. It's not a failure if price doesn't hit your target in low-probability conditions; it's a wise pause.

### Rock Climber Analogy (Price Structure & Footholds)
- **Kind:** metaphor / analysis framework
- **What ICT says:** Think of price action like a rock face. Your goal (at the top) is a high you want to reach. The current price is where you're standing. Between where you are and the top are footholds (support levels, FVGs, imbalances) you can use to "climb" higher. By identifying these footholds (PD arrays), you can forecast where price "wants" to climb. It's not about predicting the future; it's about seeing the available structure price can use to move higher or lower.
- **How to identify:** Draw a vertical line from the current price up to the resistance/high you want to reach. Identify all the PD arrays between them: FVGs, sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiencies, order blocks, equal highs, etc. These are the footholds.
- **How to use:** When price breaks above a resistance and then consolidates, ask: "What's the next foothold?" Use that PD array as your next entry point or partial target. This helps you think of price structure as a series of connected levels, not random noise. On the way down, use the same logic: handholds are support levels you can use to slow a descent.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ is trading near a high at 26,830 (Monday's RTH gap high). To forecast if price can reach a higher high (all-time highs or the inversion FVG above), ICT identifies the footholds: the sell-side imbalance / buy-side inefficiency, the midpoint of the wick (consequent encroachment), the inversion FVG, etc. Each foothold is a test point where price must prove its intention to continue higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:33:41](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2021), [0:34:28](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2068), [0:35:16](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2116)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This analogy works for uptrends. For downtrends, reverse it: the goal is at the bottom, and you're looking for handholds (resistance levels) to slow or stop a fall.

### Order Block Identification (CISD—Change in State of Delivery)
- **Kind:** rule + advanced entry model
- **What ICT says:** An order block forms when price prints a series of consecutive candles in one direction (e.g., up close candles), then reverses sharply. The last candle in that series—specifically, the opening price of that last candle—becomes the order block (a bearish order block if it's reversing from bullish). However, the rule depends on which candle is "largest" in the series: (1) if the last candle is the **largest**, use its opening; (2) if it's the **smallest**, also use its opening; (3) if they're all **equal**, use the opening of the **first** candle in the series.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Find a series of up-close candles (or down-close, for bullish order blocks).
  2. Look for the reversal candle (opposite direction).
  3. Measure the body size of the last candle vs. the series average.
  4. Apply the size rule above.
- **How to use:** Plot the order block level on your chart. When price returns to that level later in the day, it acts as an entry point or a support/resistance. Order blocks are areas where smart money aggressively liquidated or established positions, so they're often reversal points.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ charts show multiple candle series; ICT references the rule when analyzing where to enter shorts. The order block opening becomes a reference level for both entries and stop placement.
- **Timestamps:** [0:42:26](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2546), [0:43:15](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2595)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Don't "write this in your book and sell it on Amazon" — there are many YouTubers misinterpreting this rule. The candle size (largest, smallest, or equal) determines which opening to use. This is a precise rule, not an approximation.

### Draw on Liquidity (DOL) & Entry Models
- **Kind:** entry signal + process
- **What ICT says:** A "draw on liquidity" occurs when price approaches a known liquidity level (a gap, an old high/low, a pool of buy-side or sell-side orders). Not every draw is an entry. An entry requires **order flow confirmation**—additional price action (a specific candle formation, body placement, wick behavior) that signals smart money is about to move. Just because price reached your target doesn't mean you enter; you wait for the order flow signal. "It's not 'he said that level, so let's go short.' That's not what this is at all."
- **How to identify:** Identify where price is going (RTH gaps, old highs/lows, key wicks). Watch for price to approach that level. When it does, pause and ask: "Do I see order flow confirmation here?" Look for bodies in specific halves of FVGs, bodies respecting key wicks, or small reversal candles. If you don't see that confirmation, don't enter.
- **How to use:** Use draws on liquidity as an **observation** tool. When price hits your target level, annotate it and study what happened. Did bodies respect the level (order flow confirmed)? Or did it blow through? This builds your intuition. Over time, you'll recognize order flow signals instinctively. In real-time trading, use it as a filter: draw on liquidity + order flow confirmation = entry.
- **Example in this video:** ICT drew on liquidity at Monday's RTH opening range gap low (26,695) on April 21. Price approached it. But because the Fed speaker was at 10:00, he didn't force an entry—he waited for order flow confirmation. The market didn't provide clean confirmation due to the news event, so he reduced conviction.
- **Timestamps:** [0:25:44](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1544), [0:26:32](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1592), [0:37:19](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2239)
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Many retail traders watch ICT's livestreams, see a level, and assume it's a "signal" to trade that level. It's not. It's an **observation point**. The entry signal is the order flow. If you're a new trader watching, focus on learning order flow patterns, not just level identification.

### Smaller Timeframe Trading (15-second, 30-second Charts)
- **Kind:** skill / advanced technique
- **What ICT says:** While ICT typically trades 1-minute charts, the same logic applies to smaller timeframes (15-second, 30-second). On smaller timeframes, you're looking for mini versions of the same PD arrays: small FVGs, small order blocks, small imbalances. These form and resolve quickly (seconds), so entries are precise but require fast execution. The benefit: if you can read a 30-second chart correctly, you can read any timeframe.

### Emotional Discipline & Journal Methodology
- **Kind:** psychology + long-term skill development
- **What ICT says:** Build your journal to record observations, not complaints. Don't write, "I was wrong, the market didn't do what I expected." Instead, write: "I was looking for price to do X, but price was unwilling. Here's what price did instead." This reframing prevents bitterness and self-criticism from poisoning your development. Many traders destroy their potential by beating themselves up early on; this creates a lifelong pattern of unsatisfactory results (you succeed but never feel good enough). Start fresh: observe neutrally, record facts, stay constructive.
- **How to identify:** After each trading session or study session, review: Did price hit your target? Did your model work? If not, why? Write it down.
- **How to use:** Use your journal as a learning tool, not an emotional release. Build a library of observations so you can later back-test and refine your model. Don't share your trading journal on social media early on—external criticism (from people who don't know your full model) can derail your development. Build in private.
- **Example in this video:** ICT's experience: he made a lot of money early on but beat himself up internally, so the wins never felt good. Now, as a grandfather, he's focused on being present for family rather than obsessing over trading. The lesson: emotional discipline is as important as technical skill.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG) — Revisited with Validation
- **Kind:** rule + validation framework
- **What ICT says:** Not all FVGs that "look" like inversions are valid. An inversion FVG is valid only if it meets specific criteria: (1) The original FVG was used in its first utilization (bodies respected the zone correctly), (2) price traded **through** the original FVG (fully leaving a body outside), and (3) price then retraced **back into** that gap showing the new (inverted) characteristic. If price wicks outside a gap but doesn't leave a body outside, the inversion has not been confirmed yet. This nuance prevents false inversions from being traded.
- **How to identify:** 
  1. Mark the original FVG (with appropriate body placement).
  2. Watch price trade above/below it and leave a body outside.
  3. Watch price retrace back in. 
  4. Only then does it act as inversion with certainty.
- **How to use:** Don't assume every gap that looks inverted is inverted. Wait for the confirmation (body outside, then retrace with new characteristic). This prevents premature entries.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ shows several gaps. Some look like they could invert, but ICT emphasizes: "We did not trade outside of this gap yet. We just wicked outside. A small body, but that's not validating the inversion FVG." The wick alone doesn't confirm; you need a body outside and a retrace with the new characteristic.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated

- **Body placement (premium sensitivity, bearish):** No bodies in upper half of FVG. Wicks allowed above; bodies must stay below or at midpoint.
- **Body placement (discount sensitivity, bullish):** Bodies in upper half of inversion FVG. Wicks allowed below; bodies must stay above or at midpoint.
- **Mohawk rule:** A single wick touching the boundary of an FVG is permissible and doesn't invalidate the structure. It's only when bodies cross that the setup is compromised.
- **Order block candle size rule:** 
  - If last candle in series is largest = use its opening.
  - If last candle in series is smallest = use its opening.
  - If all are equal = use opening of the first candle.
- **Fed/news time filters:** 10:00 ET Fed chair, Powell speech, 8:30 ET economic data. Treat these as low-probability conditions; reduce conviction and accept doing nothing.
- **RTH gap targets:** Monday's RTH opening range gap low as first major target for the short setup (drawn from earlier analysis).
- **Inversion confirmation:** Wick outside gap + body outside gap + retrace with new characteristic = validated inversion. Wick alone is insufficient.
- **Draw on liquidity:** Price reaching your target level is an observation, not an entry signal. Wait for order flow confirmation (appropriate body placement, candle reversal) before entering.
- **Equity/demo account:** Demo account used to avoid regulatory issues (CFTC, NFA) and to prevent viewers from claiming they were "put into a trade" by ICT.

## Process, risk & psychology

**Pre-trade setup:**
- Check economic calendar (Forex Factory) for news timing.
- Identify your directional bias (are there 10+ up closes? Has a key wick been broken?).
- Set your target levels (RTH gaps, old highs/lows, key wicks).
- Accept that news events make your targets low-probability to reach before the event.

**During the trade:**
- Watch bodies in FVGs; ignore wicks at extremes.
- As news approaches, reduce conviction and accept that you may not trade.
- If you enter pre-news, use tight stops and scalp partials. Don't hold through the event.
- Use smaller timeframes (15-second, 30-second) to enter/exit precise ticks during larger moves.

**Handling low-probability days:**
- It's not a loss to do nothing. Doing nothing = zero loss.
- Observe price action without trading; annotate levels and review after hours.
- This builds tape-reading skills even when you don't participate.
- Accept that forcing a trade in low-probability conditions is gambler's mentality.

**Emotional discipline:**
- Journal observations neutrally. Don't write complaints or self-criticism.
- Build your model in private; don't invite external criticism early.
- Accept that you won't always hit your target. That's OK. Your model is right; the conditions were just 50/50 today.
- Remember: doing nothing is a valid trade decision.

**Learning & skill development:**
- Demo account first, real money later, after backtesting.
- Each time you observe price, you're building intuition.
- Small price runs on tiny timeframes (15-30 second) are valid study material; they contain the same logic as larger moves.
- You're responsible for your own trading. ICT is teaching concepts, not giving signals.

**Life balance:**
- Trading is important, but family is more important. Manage your time accordingly.
- Don't let trading consume you. Take breaks, be present for loved ones.

## Glossary candidates

`Premium sensitivity` — Bodies allowed only on one side of a PD array (lower half for bearish, upper half for bullish) while wicks can exceed the boundary. Indicates smart money control and respect for the zone.

`Discount sensitivity` — Bodies allowed on the opposite side after inversion. Wicks can exceed but bodies define the intent.

`Mohawk` — A single wick that extends beyond the boundary of an FVG or support/resistance level. Always permissible; it's only when bodies follow that the structure is broken.

`Foothold / Handheld` — A PD array (FVG, imbalance, order block, equal high/low) that price can use to "climb" higher or lower. Part of the rock climber analogy.

`Draw on liquidity (DOL)` — When price approaches a known liquidity level (RTH gap, old high/low, key wick). Not an entry signal by itself; requires order flow confirmation.

`Order flow confirmation` — Specific candle body placement, reversal patterns, or wick behavior that signals smart money intent at a draw-on-liquidity level. Confirms an entry or rejection.

`Low-probability conditions` — When economic calendar events (Fed, CPI, NFP, major speeches) are happening or imminent. Precision drops; treat as 50/50.

`Consequent encroachment` — The midpoint or key wick level within a PD array. Bodies must respect it in premium sensitivity; bodies breaking above/below it signal loss of control.

`CISD` — Change in State of Delivery; the order block that forms after a series of directional candles reverses sharply.

`Demo account` — Practice/educational account (not real money) used to teach price action concepts without regulatory liability. Viewers trade their own real accounts at their own risk.

`Inversion FVG (validated)` — A PD array that has been traded through (body outside the original zone) and is retracing back in with a new characteristic. Requires both the exit and the re-entry to be confirmed.
