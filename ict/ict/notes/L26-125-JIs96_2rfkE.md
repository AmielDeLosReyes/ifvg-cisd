## Concepts

### PD Array Settings & Volume Imbalance Sensitivity
- **Kind:** rule / refinement / how to identify
- **What ICT says:** When toggling a FVG from "standard" display to "volume imbalance" display, the visual can shift significantly. A standard FVG (high of one candle to low of next) may look like a single gap, but when toggled to show volume imbalance, you see the UNIFORM gap (the bodies' gap, not wick-to-wick). Use the volume imbalance view because it shows where bodies actually are, not where wicks touched.
- **How to identify:** On TradingView, toggle between FVG display settings. Standard shows full gap; volume imbalance shows where the actual price bodies don't touch.
- **How to use:** Mark inefficiencies using volume imbalance view. This is more accurate for predicting where price will be drawn because bodies (not wicks) show true order flow.
- **Example in this video:** Gold daily chart: same candlestick range, but toggling settings shows different levels. Volume imbalance display = "jumps off the chart" and shows the bodies' behavior more clearly. Market has used the volume imbalance levels consistently; standard FVG view was less predictive.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is a CHARTING SETTING adjustment, not a market concept. Choose the view that shows bodies respecting levels most accurately, and use that consistently.

### Balance Price Range (BPR) & Dealing Range
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** A balance price range = an area where price traded UP and then DOWN (or vice versa), showing TWO directional deliveries within the same range. BPR is relevant because it suggests a range may be established; price may trade inside it or use specific levels within it for reversals.
- **How to identify:** Candlestick or range where price traded up (high touched), then closed down (low touched), or vice versa. Two candlesticks showing two directions = balanced.
- **How to use:** In BPR, expect price to use inefficiencies within the range rather than break out. If price breaks out of BPR, it's likely a shift from high-resistance to low-resistance conditions (or vice versa). Use BPR as context for whether to expect range-bound or trend-following behavior.
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: BPR exists from a prior candle that had both up and down delivery. When price is inside this BPR, he expects it to use the inefficiencies and stay inside. When it breaks BPR on a close, it can open the door to new draws (lower levels).
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:26](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2426) — "Balanced price range exists here... we've seen two directions"; [0:41:13](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2473) — explaining BPR implications for retracements
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** BPR is a context filter, not a standalone signal. It tells you what kind of price action to EXPECT, not what to trade. If you're bearish and price is inside a BPR, you expect lower prices to come eventually, but they may not come immediately (BPR may be respected first).

### Nesting PD Arrays & Multi-Level Inefficiencies
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** When two PD arrays (FVGs) are positioned one inside the other (nested), they create nested inefficiencies. The OUTER array is typically the premium/discount bias; the INNER is a secondary reference. When you have nested arrays, price can utilize either. High-volatility conditions may cause price to wick through outer array into inner array and back (wild swings), so stops must account for this.
- **How to identify:** Mark FVGs on your chart. If one FVG is entirely above/below another FVG of opposite nature (e.g., premium FVG nesting above a discount FVG), you have nesting.
- **How to use:** In nesting zones, expect wider stops and more volatile retracements. Don't assume price will respect just the outer array; be prepared for it to visit the inner array on wicks. Stops must be wide enough to avoid being shaken out on noise. Targets can utilize either level.
- **Example in this video:** Pound dollar (or EURUSD): two PD arrays nested. Upper array = premium (normal FVG). Lower = inversion FVG below. When price retraces, it can touch outer, inner, or bounce between them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:55:33](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=3333) — explaining nested arrays on EURUSD; [0:56:20](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=3380) — clarifying why both arrays matter
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Nesting isn't always present. Only relevant when multiple inefficiencies are stacked. When present, it's because the market is showing BOTH bullish and bearish potentials; use it as a warning to use wider stops or wait for directional clarity.

### Breakaway Gap (vs Measuring Gap vs Closing Gap)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Breakaway gap = a gap that remains OPEN (unfilled) and signals the start of a trend or impulsive move. Measuring gap = a gap that forms mid-trend and projects the extent of the remaining move. Closing gap = a gap filled on a subsequent trading period. A breakaway is strong if it STAYS open and price doesn't retrace into it.
- **How to identify:** After a breakout of a level, if a gap is left unfilled and price accelerates away, it's breakaway. If price comes back and fills it, it becomes a closing gap (weak breakout).
- **How to use:** Identify breakaway gaps to confirm strong directional move. Use a filled gap as a failed break. Unfilled gaps = trend strength. Example: short a market expecting sell-off; if price gaps down and doesn't fill the gap, the sell-off is strong. Target = measuring gap (or further inefficiencies).
- **Example in this video:** Crude oil: run from low up to near inefficiency high, then pullback. Small gap left in retrace could act as breakaway gap if price doesn't return to fill it. If it stays open, next run up is likely.
- **Timestamps:** [0:41:13](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2473) — "I prefer it doesn't do that because I think this should act as a breakaway gap"; [0:40:26](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2426) — discussing next fair value gap as measuring gap
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Breakaway gaps are more meaningful in intraday timeframes; on daily/weekly, gaps are sometimes filled weeks later (less relevant for short-term trades). Context matters: in strong trend, gaps stay open; in reversals, gaps are quickly filled.

### Discount Sensitivity vs Premium Sensitivity (Price Action Behavior)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Discount array = inefficiency below current price (from down-close candle). When price retraces into discount array, "discount sensitivity" means bodies should stay in UPPER half of the inefficiency. If bodies breach lower half, discount is lost. Premium array = inefficiency above price. Premium sensitivity = bodies must stay in LOWER half. Sensitivity = the boundary that bodies must respect to maintain bullish/bearish control.
- **How to identify:** Mark FVG. Identify if it's above (premium) or below (discount) current price. When price retraces, watch if body respects the boundary. Upper half vs lower half = midpoint (CE).
- **How to use:** In discount array, if retracement bodies stay above CE, it's bullish. If they breach below CE on close, discount is lost and bear control is in question. Same logic reversed for premium. Use this to confirm or invalidate your bias.
- **Example in this video:** ES March 09: FVG formed (premium = above price). When price retraced, bodies came DOWN into it but didn't close below CE = bullish, discount sensitivity held. Price then ripped higher.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:09](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1569) — explaining discount sensitivity on S&P; [0:27:47](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1667) — "Bullish order flow is going to respect the upper boundaries of a discount array"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This is the CORE of reading order flow without looking "inside the candle" at tick data or volume profiles. Bodies + levels = pure order flow.

### Relative Equal Highs/Lows & Old Highs/Lows (Liquidity Pools)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Old highs/lows = liquidity resting at prior significant levels. Relative equal highs = price is making a similar high to a prior high but not higher (resistance). These are pools of buy-side (above, for shorts to cover) or sell-side (below, for longs to cover) liquidity. Algorithm will run these levels to "sweep" trapped traders' stops.
- **How to identify:** Mark on daily/weekly prior significant highs and lows. When price approaches them, mark as potential liquidity pool.
- **How to use:** Anticipate runs to old levels. Use these as target references. Stop placement: beyond old levels (to avoid being swept). If you're short and old high is being run, either exit or expand stop beyond the high (if risk allows).
- **Example in this video:** Dow: old high from prior month is a draw. Nasdaq August lows are a draw for first half of year. Dollar index: old high is a draw if risk-off scenario plays out.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:05](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=665) — discussing August lows as draw; [0:21:27](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1287) — "these lows here... uh again, this is March contract, so... I like these"; [0:28:35](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1715) — marking sell-side and buy-side liquidity
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not all old levels matter. Significance = how recent, how violently rejected, and how many times price has returned to it. Recent highs/lows within 5–10 days are more relevant than years-old levels.

### Contract Rollover: Nearby vs Next Month Inefficiencies
- **Kind:** definition / process
- **What ICT says:** When March contract is about to expire, he examines both March (nearby/front month) and June (next month out) contracts. Inefficiencies that exist in June but NOT in March can be used as hidden targets for March trading. This is because June is thinner (less volume), showing unmet inefficiencies. Those inefficiencies will eventually be filled once June becomes the front month. Use June inefficiencies as target guidance for March trades.
- **How to identify:** Pull up continuous, March, and June contracts side-by-side. Compare for inefficiencies. If June shows a clear gap that March doesn't have, mark it.
- **How to use:** When trading March, use June's inefficiencies as additional target levels. This gives you "hidden" targets that the retail crowd isn't seeing (they only look at front month). Also, it's a way to identify longer-term draws.
- **Example in this video:** Nasdaq March vs June: June shows spotty inefficiencies (less helpful visually), but when comparing to March and continuous, the overall picture becomes clearer. He uses this to confirm bearish draw to August lows.

- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Rollover mechanics: March expires around mid-month (roughly 3rd Friday); traders begin shifting to June as March nears expiration. Volume migrates. Use this to time when hidden inefficiencies become relevant.

## Glossary candidates

`Balance Price Range (BPR)` — A range where price traded in BOTH directions (up and down); suggests price may stay inside and use inefficiencies before breaking out.  
`Discount Sensitivity` — In a discount array (inefficiency below price), bodies must respect UPPER HALF (stay above CE); if they breach below CE, discount is lost.  
`Premium Sensitivity` — In a premium array (inefficiency above price), bodies must respect LOWER HALF (stay below CE); if they breach above CE, premium is lost.  
`Breakaway Gap` — An unfilled gap at the start of an impulsive move; signals trend strength if it remains open (not filled).  
`Measuring Gap` — A gap in the middle of a trend; projects the remaining extent of the move (measure from first gap to measuring gap = additional distance).  
`Liquidity Pool` — Old highs/lows or resting buy/sell orders at key levels; algorithm will run these to sweep trapped stops.  
`Risk-Off` — Geopolitical or market crisis driving investors to safe havens (dollar, gold, bonds) and selling risky assets (stocks, commodities except safe-haven commodities).  
`Front Month (Nearby)` — The contract with nearest expiration; carries highest volume and open interest. Prices are tightest here.  
`Next Month Out` — Futures contract for delivery month after front month; thinner, may show inefficiencies front month doesn't have; use as target reference.
