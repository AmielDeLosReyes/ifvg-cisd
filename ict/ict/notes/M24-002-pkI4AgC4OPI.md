## Summary
This lecture turns Lecture #1's loose commentary into a daily, rule-based observation protocol for beginners (aimed at ICT's son Caleb, who spends August only journaling, not trading). He clarifies the "before 7 AM" confusion. Beginners start at 7:00 ET and look for relative equal highs and lows that form after 7:00. They repeat the hunt at 8:00 and 9:00. The first 30 minutes after each hour should show a move against the expected draw (a Judas swing). He shows how to annotate inefficiencies correctly: SIBI/BISI by the middle candle's close, keeping the low, CE and high, and drawing the gap to the candle body when a volume imbalance is present (new). He also explains why the highest-precision inversion FVG is the first FVG in the leg that runs relative equal highs, inside the bearish breaker's range. Once the state of delivery turns, focus only on same-side FVGs (all SIBIs when bearish) and extend them to the right. Smaller pieces: Fibonacci standard deviations per hour as a rough range finder, the "immediate rebalance" pattern, and using the 15-second chart (Price Delivery Continuum Theory) for missed entries and tighter stops. He explicitly calls the 15-second material "extra credit", not for beginners.

### 7:00 AM ET start ("punch-in time") & the London-session clarification
- **Kind:** rule / refinement (corrects Lecture #1 wording)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Draw a light vertical line at 7:00 ET. That is the beginner's "punch-in time" and the earliest point you refer to in price.
  - At 7:00 the hunt for relative equal highs and lows **begins**. Beginners must **not** look for relative equal highs or lows that formed before 7:00, even obvious ones right before 7:00 or in London. Ignore them.
  - The apparent contradiction in Lecture #1 ("look before 7" vs "don't look before 7") is explained like this: anything before 7:00 is really London session data and its *profile*. That means: did London make the high or low of the day? Is it consolidation-to-expansion, a trend day, or seek & destroy? (This is not volume Market Profile.) Advanced students who trade London setups, or who use London to anticipate a New York continuation or a New York reversal profile, may use pre-7:00 data. Beginners should not.
  - New York session reversal profile: if London (2:00–5:00 ET) has dropped, a NY reversal profile means price most likely trades back above and overlaps everything that started at 2:00.
- **How to identify:** 7:00 vertical line. Ignore all swing points to its left. Exception: see the London-range rule below.

### Hourly hunt for relative equal highs/lows (7:00, 8:00, 9:00)  (aliases: three windows of opportunity)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - At 7:00, scan the 15m, then the 5m, then the 1m for relative equal highs or relative equal lows that form **after** 7:00.
  - At 8:00 and again at 9:00 you start the whole process over. At 8:00 you may look back before 8:00, but never before 7:00, because you're working inside the morning-session range. Same at 9:00: anything back to 7:00 is fair.
  - Morning session = 7:00–11:00 (technically to noon). ICT likes to be done by 10:15–10:30.
  - **Never annotate or anticipate a pool that hasn't formed.** "Don't predict the setup … wait for the setup." Only take a mental note of a *potential* pair. ICT can anticipate (for example, calling iFVGs before they form), but beginners can't.
  - If relative equal highs and lows are both present (a consolidation), wait for one side to be taken. It usually then attacks the other side.
  - If no obvious pair exists on any of the three timeframes, move to annotating inefficiencies (see the FVG sections).
  - The relative equal high/low is this model's draw on liquidity. Pick the target, then re-measure each hour.
- **How to use:** Get a short-term bias by watching how price attacks or is attracted to a relative equal low or high. Once the bias is set, "stick with it even if it turns against you" during observation.
- **Example in this video:** Aug 6, 2024 (index futures; the instrument isn't named, but he measures in "handles"). On the 15m at 7:00 there's only a single high, so no relative equal highs yet. The two lows visible are before 7:00, so they're ignored. On the 5m, a potential relative equal low pair exists *if* price rallies, but only as a mental note. On the 1m, relative equal highs formed after 7:00 and were then run.

### High-probability relative equal high
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** A relative equal high is high probability when the right-side high is **slightly lower** than the left one: the market failed to get back to the first high. The algorithm will reprice to it because it "wants to disrupt" that pool. By symmetry, the right-side low would be slightly higher for lows. He only states the highs case explicitly.
- **How to identify:** Two highs at roughly the same level, where the second is a bit lower. It may only look equal when the chart is compressed.
- **Timestamps:** [1:12:51](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4371) "did I teach you yesterday…" (the right one slightly lower). [2:54:59](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10499) restated on the afternoon run to the day's high.

### Smooth vs. jagged (liquidity engagement)
- **Kind:** definition (continued from Lecture #1)
- **What ICT says:** Price delivery has two simple characteristics. When price takes an area that was smooth (resting equal highs or lows) and makes it **jagged**, that was an engagement for liquidity. Expect the opposite direction to follow, especially when other factors (breaker, iFVG) support it. After a run of buy-side liquidity, go back through that price leg for setups.
- **How to identify:** Zoom out or compress the chart. A Larry Williams tip: if you're losing touch, zoom out, go up a timeframe, or look from across the room. Jaggedness that's invisible zoomed in becomes obvious.
- **Example:** Aug 6: relative equal highs after 7:00 were run ("sharp frenzy … blood in the water up here"), so don't trade up into that area and look lower. Later, the lows were made jagged, so look for buy side, a run on the high (the 7:00 high).

### First 30 minutes after the hour = opposing run (Judas swing / manipulation)
- **Kind:** rule (clarifies Lecture #1)
- **What ICT says:**
  - The first 30 minutes after 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00 is a window where you expect a move **opposite** to your expected draw. ### FVG classification & annotation (SIBI / BISI)  (aliases: inefficiencies, imbalances)
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - The middle candle's close names the gap. A **down-close** middle candle = **SIBI** (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency): it offered delivery only on the downside and is inefficient for any move back up. An **up-close** middle candle = **BISI** (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency).
  - SIBI definition: a down-close candle where the previous candle's low and the next candle's high don't connect. The only bridge between them is the down-close candle.
  - Everything from the first candle's low to the third candle's high is inefficient and "invites" price to trade back into it. That doesn't mean now, or even this session.
  - **Keep the original FVG levels** (low, consequent encroachment/midpoint, high) even after partial fills, because they're used again. Mentally note the **remaining unfilled portion**. Tiny remaining slivers matter: ICT has placed limit orders just beyond such slivers and had them run with no significant drawdown.
  - If price later revisits, it may just tap the high of the gap. If it goes above and comes back down, he uses the candle's wick and its halfway point as the retracement limit, and the gap becomes an inversion FVG (caption somewhat unclear here).
  - "These inefficiencies are the real support and resistance": price kisses them, turns on a dime, and bodies stop at them.
  - Color scheme is up to you. He won't share his exact colors, so copycats can't pass his charts off as theirs.
- **How to identify:** After checking for relative equal highs/lows on 15m, 5m and 1m (or finding none), annotate all inefficiencies, starting on the 1m and working back up.
- **Timestamps:** [1:03:11](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3791) annotate inefficiencies once the relative equal highs/lows check is done; down-close = SIBI. [1:04:49](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3889) small remaining gaps used for limit orders. [1:05:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3939) keep original levels, note the remaining portion. [1:23:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4996) keep low/CE/high; inefficiencies are the real S/R.

### Volume imbalance inside an FVG: draw to the body, ignore the wick
- **Kind:** refinement (he calls it new and his own)
- **What ICT says:**
  - A volume imbalance is the separation between one candle's **body** and the next candle's body. If the bodies don't overlap or at least touch, there's a volume imbalance.
  - When an FVG has a volume imbalance in it, the FVG's boundary should incorporate the volume imbalance, which means taking it to the **body** rather than the wick. "The wicks are not the true narrative." A wick is like a child coloring outside the lines.
  - Wicks inside such an FVG that reach only up to the CE (halfway) or less are disregarded. Use the candle body.
- **How to identify:** Zoom in. Check whether adjacent candle bodies leave a gap. If they do, re-draw the FVG edge at the body.
- **Example:** Aug 6, 1m: he corrects one of his own SIBI drawings because its edge should include a volume imbalance between bodies.
- **Timestamps:** [1:06:28](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3988) "look closely, what's wrong". [1:07:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4036) VI definition. [1:08:53](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4133) VI means ignore the wick. [1:09:40](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4180) wicks up to CE or less are ignored; take it to the body.
- **Nuance:** He says you won't find this in books or among "five-minute trainer" copies. The exact wording on which wicks are ignored is a little loose in the captions.

### Inversion FVG — the highest-precision form  (aliases: iFVG, role reversal)
- **Kind:** entry model / refinement
- **What ICT says:**
  - **All** of his PD arrays have an inversion aspect: every one can reverse its role. He introduced iFVGs on Twitter Spaces "last year" and will detail them in his book.
  - The highest-precision, most trustworthy iFVGs depend on this context: after 7:00 there's a smooth area (relative equal highs), price runs it (a stop hunt), and an FVG **inside the range that creates the bearish breaker** fails.
  - In the bearish case, the inverting gap is the **BISI** in the up-leg that ran the relative equal highs. Without the time and narrative context, a beginner would treat that BISI as a buy and get collapsed on.
  - Required behavior: price runs above the gap, comes back below it, then redelivers up into it (it acts as resistance) before a deeper protraction lower. That's what his old tweets meant by "want to see this FVG act as an inversion FVG".
  - Once it inverts, the BISI is the only buy-side gap of interest. After a valid return it "sells off", and your focus shifts to SIBIs.
- **How to use:** Sell the return into the iFVG. Respect its CE. For stops, see the breaker section.
- **Example:** Aug 6, 1m, just after 7:00: relative equal highs form and are run. The up-leg's BISI then fails. Price breaks down, returns into it, respects CE, and breaks lower. Later another return gives an OTE as well.
- **Timestamps:** [1:13:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4419) all PD arrays invert. [1:14:27](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4467) highest-precision iFVGs need the post-7:00 relative equal highs context. [1:16:54](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4614) the BISI in the breaker range; beginners would buy it. [1:43:32](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6212) must go above, then below, then redeliver.

### Breaker vs. FVG in the stop-hunt leg (premium entry) + breaker stop rules  (aliases: bearish breaker)
- **Kind:** entry model
- **What ICT says:**
  - The breaker, as first taught on BabyPips around 2011–2012: a high, a low, a stop run above the high, then a reversal back into the range. The most sensitivity is in the **lowest down-close candle prior to the run-up**. The bearish breaker can be the consecutive down-close candles.
  - The refinement: look at the leg from the breaker up to the high where stops were taken. **If that leg has an FVG, navigate away from the breaker and use that premium gap.** Others will trade the breaker or the low point and won't be comfortable selling near the high. The gap created by the drop off the iFVG is "the sweet spot": your OTE, "your 2022 model".
  - **Breaker stop:** above the **highest high of the consecutive down-close candles**, plus maybe one or two ticks. Many times he uses the exact high, because a good breaker won't want to go back into its full range.
  - **But** if there's a gap inside the stop-hunt leg, price can return all the way up into it, maybe with a small "mohawk" wick spike outside while the body stays inside or below the gap. The breaker-based stop isn't safe in that case.
- **How to use:** Use the stop-hunt-leg FVG as the primary short entry. Use the breaker only when no such gap exists.
- **Timestamps:** [1:15:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4516) breaker history. [1:16:06](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4566) sensitivity in the lowest down-close candle. [1:20:04](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4804) the FVG in the stop-hunt leg beats the breaker. [1:28:53](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5333) breaker stop rules and the mohawk caveat.
- **Nuance:** He suggests going back over your old breaker studies or losing trades. This signature (the gap in the stop-hunt leg) is often the factor you missed.

### One-sided delivery focus: extend every same-side FVG
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Once the state of delivery turns bearish, focus **only on SIBIs** ("every single SIBI") and every short-term high, even single ones, because stops sit above them. Extend each SIBI to the right. This is what "extend it to the right" meant on Twitter.
  - **Ignore BISIs** while bearish. Don't highlight or extend them. The exception: if price does something violent and starts to reverse, attention moves to the BISI as a potential inversion FVG.
  - Even inefficiencies that have already been used get extended in time. Price will push up into them, resist, and continue toward the draw.
  - Don't expect a simple "price enters gap, sell it". Anticipate some manipulation. Example: a return into an old SIBI that first takes the stops of shorts who sat just above the energetic drop, then breaks down off CE. Bodies respect the gap. Wicks may do a little damage, but not to random locations.
  - This removes the ambiguity of "which FVG do I pick". The rest comes with experience.
- **Example:** Aug 6, 1m, roughly 8:00–9:00: price bumps back into an old SIBI, works around its CE, drops, and leaves a new inefficiency. Bodies stop exactly at the levels while wicks bump the bottom of the SIBI. Price breaks lower.

### How far can it go: ranges & draw
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - After a reversal pattern, measure the potential run using the range **prior to that reversal pattern** that formed after the relative equal highs were taken. Price "gravitates to that low".
  - For this beginner model, the target is the opposing relative equal high or low.
  - For holding longer (advanced, not for Caleb), use the **previous day's low or high** as the next logical draw.
  - Other ranges that can anchor your own model: the 7:00–8:00 hour range, the highest high and lowest low since 9:00, and the 9:30 opening. When price trades to them, you're out. You don't need 100 handles a day.

### Consolidation & over-used gaps: when NOT to engage
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - Long, protracted consolidations with both relative equal highs and lows: sit still. Otherwise you're "playing the breakout game", and he is "not a breakout artist".
  - Once one side is run, it usually attacks the other.
  - If price is back inside a SIBI that has already been overlapped and respected **twice**, that's low probability. Even if it then crashes 1,000 points, it isn't a trade he'd take. The day is complete.
  - If the day isn't obvious, sit on your hands and turn off the charts. "I don't know" is an acceptable and respectable answer. A choppy, range-bound day ("unfulfilled delivery of price") is to be avoided.
- **Example:** Aug 6: both relative equal lows and highs form. Price runs higher, then down below the lows. Then it sits back in a twice-used SIBI: no trade, day done (morning).

### Hourly Fibonacci standard-deviation range finding
- **Kind:** refinement (homework exercise)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Done **after the fact** only. For each hour, anchor the Fib from the hour's high to the lowest low: 7:00, then 8:00, then 9:00 (the highest high and lowest low up to 9:00).
  - His settings project standard deviations: one SD, two SD, and **2.5 SD**, which is usually the extreme for wicks. The caption is unclear on exact level labels ("two, which is one standard deviation").
  - Projections from different hours that cluster close together give a ballpark of how far price can reach, without needing ADR.

### Immediate rebalance (+ breakaway gap, order block)
- **Kind:** definition / example
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Immediate rebalance:** an extended-range candle goes up. Price drops back and **just touches that candle's high**, rather than filling a gap, then runs to an objective (liquidity or an inefficiency).
  - He also wanted a nearby FVG to **stay open** (not traded fully down into the candle high). An FVG that stays open acts as a **breakaway gap**.
  - These "drills and exercises" teach anticipatory price reading and lead later to entries, stops and management.
- **Example:** Aug 6, after the AM selloff: there's a disruption at the lows (sell side taken), and relative equal highs sit above. He expects a poke above the day's high, which is the initial high near 7:00 (the "blue level"). Price does an immediate rebalance (touches the candle), rallies, trades back down into a down-close candle (an **order block**), closes the inefficiency once more, then expands up through the 7:00 high. That's a 40+ handle run. After touching the high of day he'd be done: no afternoon trading.

### 15-second chart & Price Delivery Continuum Theory
- **Kind:** refinement ("extra credit", explicitly not for beginners)
- **What ICT says:**
  - **Price Delivery Continuum Theory:** with an expected draw, the fractal market offers continuous participation. Once a timeframe has used up its entry opportunities, go to a lower timeframe (down to 15s or 5s) instead of chasing.
  - The 15s chart is "very forgiving". It lets you get into a move you missed on the 1m/5m/15m, or cut stop size (handles or pips). It is **not** a shortcut. If you can't read 1m/5m/15m price, you'll get chopped up on 15s.
  - **Limit orders:** don't demand perfection. Place the limit **just above** the level (for longs), e.g. just above a wick's midpoint, not at the exact extreme. That's the "low-hanging fruit" where volume is. Expect occasional non-fills even when price touches or slightly exceeds your price: "don't be a dick for a tick". Constant extreme fills invite B-book broker slippage and requotes.
  - **Adding:** when the run is already "long in the tooth", an add-on partial can't be larger than one contract. Pyramiding is hard to teach. Don't start yet; be consistent with small size first.
  - Tighter stops via 15s are for later, once you're managing real risk.
- **Example:** Aug 6 rally leg on 15s: the 1m immediate rebalance shows up as price returning to rebalance the "quick sudden run from this close to that close". Then relative equal highs, an FVG, price trading outside it, and back in with a 1m immediate rebalance to a deeper discount. That's the first return after taking buy side and a 15s long or add-on. Later, a down-close candle inside an FVG that returned into an old inefficiency is another buy, with the limit just above the wick's midpoint.

### Algorithmic delivery vs. buying/selling pressure
- **Kind:** definition (worldview)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Price is scripted by time and price, not buying and selling pressure. The algorithm refers to salient prior price points at given times.
  - HFT-style algos don't buy while price expands up. They wait for price to reach the inefficiency threshold, then fire long entries on the **first downtick** after it's met.
  - Time & sales show few contracts move price. One contract books a price.
  - "Volume precedes price" really means volume is larger where stops are taken (below lows, sell stops). Further beyond a level, volume thins, which is why gaps appear as price presses higher.
  - Limit-up analogy: one contract can open a market limit up.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- No timeframe above 15m for intraday study. Use 15m / 5m / 1m, with 15s optional.
- All times are New York local.
- 7:00 ET vertical line = start. Beginners ignore all relative equal highs/lows formed before 7:00.
- Delineate 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00 with vertical lines. Restart the relative equal highs/lows hunt at each hour. After 8:00 you may look back to 7:00, never earlier.
- Scan order: 15m, then 5m, then 1m for relative equal highs/lows. If there are none, annotate inefficiencies.
- High-probability relative equal high: the right high is slightly lower than the left.
- First 30 minutes after 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00: expect a run opposite to the expected draw (Judas). It's not strictly confined to those 30 minutes.
- AM session first characteristic: retrade into the London range (2:00–5:00 ET high/low).
- Charts show from about 6:00 ET. Show pre-7:00 only if the morning session trades to the London high or low.
- Morning session is 7:00–11:00 (technically noon). ICT prefers to be done by 10:15–10:30.
- FVG naming: down-close middle candle = SIBI, up-close = BISI. Keep the low, CE and high. Note the remaining unfilled portion.
- Volume imbalance = adjacent candle bodies not overlapping or touching. With a VI in the FVG, draw to the body. Ignore wicks reaching only up to CE.
- Always mark the first FVG prior to a stop run (it's the CISD).
- Prefer an FVG in the stop-hunt leg over the breaker. Breaker stop goes above the highest high of the consecutive down-close candles, plus 1–2 ticks (often the exact high). That stop is unsafe if the stop-hunt leg contains a gap.
- When bearish: extend every SIBI and note every short-term high. Ignore BISIs unless there's a violent reversal.
- A gap overlapped and respected twice, with price back inside it, is low probability. Don't trade it.
- OTE = 62–72% retracement plus 70.5, after a market structure shift.
- Fib SD projections per hour (high to lowest low): 1 SD, 2 SD, and 2.5 SD (wick extreme). Only meaningful when they converge with an old high or low.
- An add-on partial late in a run is at most one contract.
- Limit order goes just above the level (for longs). Don't demand the exact extreme.
- R multiple only needs to be greater than 1. No 2:1 or 3:1 minimum.
- Laptop of about 15 inches at minimum. No smartphone-only learning.

## Glossary candidates
`Punch-in time` — 7:00 AM ET, the beginner's earliest reference point and the start of the relative equal highs/lows hunt.
`SIBI` — Sell-side Imbalance, Buy-side Inefficiency: an FVG whose middle candle closed down.
`BISI` — Buy-side Imbalance, Sell-side Inefficiency: an FVG whose middle candle closed up.
`Volume imbalance` — a gap between consecutive candle bodies (bodies don't overlap or touch). Inside an FVG it means the gap is drawn to the body and the wicks are ignored.
`High-probability relative equal high` — two near-equal highs where the right one is slightly lower.
`Smooth vs. jagged` — resting equal highs/lows (smooth) that get run (jagged) show a liquidity engagement, with the opposite direction expected next.
`First FVG prior to the stop run` — the gap in the leg that ran liquidity. Its failure (inversion) is the change in state of delivery and acts like an order block.
`Opposing 30-minute window` — the first 30 minutes after 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00, when a counter-move (Judas) is expected. Captioned as "pression range" (unclear).
`London range` — the high and low from 2:00 to 5:00 AM ET. The AM session tends to retrade into it.
`Immediate rebalance` — price returns to just touch the high of an extended-range candle (no gap fill) before running on.
`Breakaway gap` — an FVG that stays open (isn't traded back into) during a run.
`Mohawk` — a small wick spike above a gap while the candle body stays inside or below it.
`Price Delivery Continuum Theory` — the fractal market keeps offering entries on lower timeframes (down to 15s) once higher timeframes have used up theirs.
`Unfulfilled delivery of price` — choppy, range-bound action with no clear draw. Stand aside.
`Don't be a dick for a tick` — don't demand an exact fill. Allow limit orders to miss sometimes.
`Street money` — retail traders who use support and resistance, i.e. the liquidity being engineered.
