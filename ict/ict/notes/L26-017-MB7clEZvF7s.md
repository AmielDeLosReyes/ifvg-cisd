## Summary
Part 3 of "How New Students Should Start" is about managing information instead of cluttering the chart with heat maps, liquidity maps and dozens of lines. ICT builds separate, clean TradingView layouts ("matrices") that each hold one family of reference points: (1) Monthly Macro Keys: open/high/low/close of the last three months plus the previous week's high and low, used to see whether price is in premium or discount relative to the last three months, month and previous week; (2) a New Week Opening Gap matrix (rolling five weeks); (3) a first presented FVG (and NDOG) matrix; (4) an RTH opening range gap matrix. He then reads the 2 Sep 2026 NQ price action through them: a daily volume imbalance that sold off with no support, a retracement stopping at the 50% of Tuesday's RTH opening range gap, octant/quadrant grading of PD arrays, and a new coinage, the "shadow" (the overlap of two nested RTH opening range gaps) that validates an order block. He also tells brand-new students to stop trying to find setups after 11:00 a.m. ET on the Wednesday of non-farm payroll week and to concentrate on logging and analysis.

## Concepts

### Information management: separate layouts/matrices, no clutter  (aliases: "be a good manager of the data", "workspace/layout")
- **Kind:** rule | process
- **What ICT says:**
  - Heat maps, liquidity maps and busy indicators make charts unreadable; what you need is key levels, PD arrays and a higher-timeframe/macro perspective, handled as a daily/weekly/monthly regimen: "you do X, Y, Z to get A, B, C data."
  - These reference points are used to "call price moves" and measure the strength/weakness and continuity of a price run.
  - Use layouts/workspaces (in TradingView, or any platform with saved layouts) and keep each layout to one family of data; avoid dragging all annotations onto one chart. His own cluttered chart equals what's on his notepad; he does most of his data management with journals and "piece of paper 99% of the time," annotating charts for students to follow his logic.
  - Everything he teaches is stored as levels you can toggle; tap into monthly keys anytime via the "monthly keys" layout; copy layouts by quarter.
  - He insists this is not complicated and tells those who think it is to leave; the stated cost of not managing data: "if you mishandle the data, you're not going to do well."
- **Timestamps:** [0:00:54](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=54) — manage key levels, PDAs, HTF macro; [0:03:21](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=201) — notepad equals chart; layouts; [0:39:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2352) — copy per quarter; "be a very good manager of the data".

### Monthly Macro Keys layout  (aliases: "monthly keys", "HTF macro perspective")
- **Kind:** definition | rule
- **What ICT says:**
  - On the monthly chart mark the high, low, open and close of each of the last THREE months (here June, July, August 2026, labeled e.g. "August 2026 monthly high/low/opening/closing price"). Optionally extend to 12 months for a full calendar year.
  - Also keep the previous week's high and low (Monday–Friday range) as one reference; he is not tracking more than that: only monthly OHLC (three months) and previous week's high and low. (Sunday data: some platforms compress or omit Sundays; figure it out for your platform.)
  - Style he uses: highs and lows in blue; opening prices in black dashed lines.
  - Nothing else on this layout: no FVGs, order blocks, breakers, first presented FVGs, NWOGs or NDOGs.
  - Use: determine where price sits relative to the three-month range (here June's high to July's low) and the last month / year: premium (above 50%) or discount (below 50%) vs equilibrium. On 2 Sep 2026 (second trading day of September) price was sitting at about the midpoint of the three-month range, "right inside equilibrium," so the weekly and daily charts must give more information. Also measure where price is relative to the previous week's high/low (here the lower portion, a discount) and where the week opened relative to that range (it opened in the premium side).
  - "Quarterly shifts" tie to seasonal tendencies: the big swing-trader moves (he points to the 2016/2017 mentorship for quarterly shifts); understanding where the next quarterly shift is likely to go provides a daily edge.
  - Traders using a short-term mentality (1–3 days, "one shot one kill") use this logic of premium/discount over the last three months/previous week to frame their trade.
  - Write the levels, measure the previous week's range, and grade it (fib, "gradient levels and octants"); then take the fib off. Result: "key information about premium vs. discount relative to previous week's high/low, last month's high/low, last three months' range."
- **How to use:** Build the layout once per month/week, record the fib/gradient levels in your journal, remove the clutter afterwards.
- **Example in this video:** NQ monthly, 2 Sep 2026: June high and July low frame the 3-month range; price at 50%; weekly: opened in the premium of the previous week's range but now in a discount; daily: also at equilibrium of the relevant range.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:14](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=314) — last three months OHLC on the monthly chart; [0:06:52](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=412) — where are we relative to the three-month range, nothing else on the chart; [0:08:30](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=510) — halfway/equilibrium; [0:10:06](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=606) — colors/styles, previous week high/low; [0:14:08](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=848) — summary: key levels in reference to the real dealing range by time.
- **Nuance / caveats:** "Open, high, low, and close in deference to time" (his phrase) is his substitute for heat maps/liquidity maps.

### Premium / Discount / Equilibrium relative to the dealing range (macro version)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Upper portion of the range is premium, below 50% is discount; at the 50% line is equilibrium. For short-term traders (1–3 days or "one shot one kill") use this to frame the trade. He applies it simultaneously to three months, previous week and daily ranges.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:18](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=558), [0:12:33](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=753), [0:14:08](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=848).

### Seasonal tendency for index futures (September–February)
- **Kind:** rule (context)
- **What ICT says:** September is usually a weak, bearish month for index futures: they usually trade down into a low in September or October; rarely the seasonal low is made in November; then a flurry of action into year-end and up into the first week of February is the usual catalyst. In the last week of August it can already begin. He cautions it is a general rule of thumb, not a "must": "they can twist this thing around and go against the grain." He had told students the previous week that price would enter this seasonal sell-off.

### Daily PD array grading: volume imbalance, octants/quadrants, "no support in open air"
- **Kind:** refinement | example
- **What ICT says:**
  - He had told students for a week that the sell-side objective was a daily volume imbalance (shaded blue) with a SIBI/volume imbalance and the consequent encroachment of a wick as an impact level. Price reached the high of that gap on one day and closed outside of it, a confirmation of lower prices; it then failed to reach the volume imbalance that day.
  - Treat a slightly up-close candle's tiny body as part of one big volume imbalance when the open/close difference is small: his example candle opened 288.00 and closed 289.50 (1.5 handles, six ticks), so he treats the whole range as the volume imbalance: draw it to the body with no extension, and include all levels graded inside.
  - Question he poses: what should price do trading down through a daily area with only a wick and open space (no bodies to build support)? Answer: not staggered/struggling; "there isn't a whole lot of opposition or obstruction," so price travels quickly through. Monday it traded down through once and then absorbed; on Tuesday at about 3:00 a.m. it "cascaded" through the whole gap in the overnight session with no support at any of the usual support/resistance spots.
  - Grade the PD array (the blue daily gap) from low to high with a fib: split into eighths: highest octant at the high, then upper quadrant, the second octant above CE (midpoint), CE, lower octant below midpoint, lower quadrant, lowest octant, and lowest (his wording on octant/quadrant is loose in captions; the structure is eighths with quarter levels marked). On the 1-minute chart the market then used these levels precisely: it broke below the highest octant, came down to the upper quadrant, back up to the upper octant, sold off to the lower octant, back up to the upper quadrant, broke lower to and through CE "as you would expect"; with weak order flow it does not return to CE once broken; it then traded to the lower quadrant, back up to the octant, down to the lowest octant/quadrant and out of the gap, using the low of the daily FVG, the middle (dotted CE of the big gap), and the lowest octant "bang" as the lowest volume imbalance on the daily.
  - He argues the algorithm "really uses these levels because it's algorithmic, it's scripted" (floor pivots mentioned as other levels).
  - Having reached this objective during non-farm payroll week, price was "gyrating in no man's land" at CE.
- **How to use:** Grade any PD array you are interacting with (daily range of the gap) in octants/quarters; use CE and octants as intraday support/resistance in the direction of order flow; expect fast delivery through areas with no bodies.
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily and 1m, 31 Aug–2 Sep 2026: daily blue volume imbalance (price traded down through it Tuesday around 3:00 a.m.); graded levels interact precisely on 1m through Wednesday.
- **Timestamps:** [0:16:32](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=992) — SIBI/CE reaction and close outside; [0:17:19](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1039) — treating a small-body candle's range as a volume imbalance; [0:19:05](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1145) — open space = no obstruction; [0:22:21](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1341) — grading with fib low to high; [0:24:44](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1484) — octant/quadrant walk-through on 1m; [0:27:08](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1628) — lowest octant and CE of the daily gap.
- **Nuance / caveats:** The octant/quadrant terminology is inconsistent in the captions ("octant", "quadrant", "percentile"); treat as eighths/quarters of a PD array. Price values are caption-derived.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) matrix: definition and how to build it
- **Kind:** definition | how-to
- **What ICT says:**
  - NWOG = difference between the Sunday 6:00 p.m. ET open (every Sunday) and the previous Friday's last print (ETH 4:59 p.m. on the 1-minute chart). If a holiday prevents trading on Friday, use Thursday.
  - Keep a rotating display of at least the last five weeks' NWOGs on a layout with nothing else on it; label each gap by its date and keep colors consistent. (He suggests labeling with the date, e.g., "NWOG Aug 30", any format works.)
  - How to draw (TradingView): switch to electronic trading hours (ETH); use the calendar "go to date" to jump to the previous Sunday at 18:00 (military time 1800 = 6 p.m. ET); zoom in; anchor the rectangle at the Friday close at 4:59 p.m. (the last print, down-close candle: anchor to the close) and drag up to the Sunday 6:00 p.m. opening price. Repeat going back week by week (example weeks: Sunday Aug 30; Friday Aug 21 4:59 p.m. close to Sunday Aug 23 open).
  - Manage the objects via the object tree: rename each (e.g., "NWOG Aug 23"), toggle visibility, copy the layout for each quarter and delete old ones; hiding old ones keeps them in the list for later.
  - Reading the matrix: hovering over a gap highlights it; use consequent encroachment of the gap; price "meandering around an empty space between two key PD arrays" can be read relative to the nearest NWOGs.
- **Example in this video:** Layout with five NWOGs and first presented FVGs; on 2 Sep price was in no-man's land between a previous Monday first presented FVG and Tuesday's first presented FVG (and Wednesday's), far from an NDOG and with another far below.
- **Timestamps:** [0:31:57](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1917) — NWOG definition, Sunday 6 p.m. vs Friday close, holiday rule; [0:32:46](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1966) — keep five rotating weeks; [0:33:35](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2015) — drawing via ETH + calendar; [0:35:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2112) — anchor to Friday 4:59 p.m. close, Sunday 6 p.m. open; [0:37:36](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2256) — object tree, rename; [0:39:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2352) — quarterly copies.
- **Nuance / caveats:** Reconciles with Oct 2024 videos in this batch (NWOG = Friday ETH settlement to Sunday 6 p.m. open). He says the matrix is for students; he himself uses paper journals.

### RTH Opening Range Gap matrix: marking, grading, nesting, and "shadow"  (aliases: "regular trading hours opening range gap matrix", "shadow")
- **Kind:** definition | how-to | new coinage (shadow)
- **What ICT says:**
  - Make a layout containing only RTH opening range gaps. Toggle to regular trading hours (not electronic). The previous day's final print is the 4:14 p.m. ET 1-minute candle (RTH close); the 9:30 a.m. ET open is the other side of the gap. Example: Tuesday 1 Sep 2026's RTH opening range gap runs from Monday's 4:14 p.m. close (its high, since the market opened lower) down to Tuesday's 9:30 open (caption misspeaks "Wednesday's"). The "consequent encroachment" is the midpoint; "I don't care if you don't like the name."
  - How to grade an older gap: on the RTH ORG matrix chart use the calendar to jump to the date at 9:30 (e.g., Aug 26 at 9:30), drag a fib from the previous day's 4:14 p.m. close to the very first tick of the 9:30 open, and keep it consistent.
  - Nesting: in consolidation you will have many overlapping RTH ORGs (and overlapping first presented FVGs, NDOGs or NWOGs). Of the nested gaps, look at the largest/widest one that price is inside (the pink shaded gap for Tuesday Sep 1 here); "if there's ever a doubt of what I would be using" (rule statement is truncated in the caption).
  - Price behavior on 2 Sep: price opened low, meandered, went up into a proportion of last Wednesday (Aug 26)'s RTH opening range gap; the retracement went only to half of Tuesday's RTH opening range gap (CE) and the candle bodies showed bearish order flow, so the low was taken; then price came back up into the lowest octant/lower quadrant of Wednesday Aug 26's RTH ORG, tagged it, took out the low and went lower; later, "chopping wood but reacting off key levels."
  - SHADOW (new coinage): where Wednesday Sep 2's RTH opening range gap overlaps Tuesday's pink Sep 1 RTH opening range gap, the overlap region (a different hue) is a "shadow" — a shared space of nested gaps. A down-close candle (order block) anchored inside the shadow is valid; price respected that area repeatedly. "This is for order block lovers." Two RTH ORGs bleeding together make a very strong support or resistance level; far more precise than classic support/resistance or trend lines, where you would have had to guess whether to buy a high.
  - Order block stack: consecutive down-close candles count as ONE order block; the change in state of delivery is at the opening price (of the first candle in the stack); extend that level; price rallying and not trading back to it signals bullish; it then rallied to the lower quadrant of Wednesday Aug 26's RTH ORG.
  - He says he can find setups even in "slop" like this but you "have to be very nimble," and he teaches students not to require these setups.
- **How to identify:** Overlap of two (or more) RTH ORG boxes; look for an order block (down-close for support) anchored in the overlap; check bodies' behavior vs CE and octant levels.
- **How to use:** Treat the shadow/anchored order block as a precise support/resistance; use CE and lowest/lower octants of larger nested gaps as buy/sell reactions; use as context for journaling.
- **Example in this video:** NQ 1m, Wed 2 Sep 2026: Tuesday 1 Sep RTH ORG (pink) with CE hit by a retracement only; Wed Sep 2 ORG overlap = shadow; an order block (down-close candle) anchored in the shadow supports the rally back to the lower quadrant of Aug 26's RTH ORG; a later stack of consecutive down-close candles forms one order block whose opening price is the CISD level.
- **Timestamps:** [0:47:16](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2836) — RTH ORG matrix, nesting; [0:49:38](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2978) — 4:14 p.m. final print, 9:30 open; [0:51:15](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3075) — grading Aug 26 gap via calendar; [0:53:42](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3222) — retracement only to half the gap; [0:54:32](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3272) — shadow defined; [0:55:20](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3320) — order block anchored in shadow validated; [0:58:33](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3513) — shadow precision vs classic support/resistance; consecutive down-close candles = one order block, CISD at opening price.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Monthly Macro Keys layout: O/H/L/C for the last 3 months (optionally 12) + previous week's high and low; nothing else; highs/lows blue, opens black dashed.
- Premium above 50%, discount below, equilibrium at 50% of the range.
- NWOG: Sunday 6:00 p.m. ET open vs previous Friday last print (4:59 p.m. ETH); use Thursday if Friday was a holiday; keep a rolling five weeks; label by date.
- RTH opening range gap: previous day's 4:14 p.m. ET last 1-minute candle to the 9:30 a.m. ET open, on RTH view.
- First presented FVG = first FVG formed after 9:30 a.m. ET.
- Mark 10:00-type time lines properly (not covered here).
- Seasonal: Sept bearish, low in Sept or Oct, rarely Nov, rally into first week of Feb (rule of thumb).
- Brand-new traders: stop looking for setups and be done by 11:00 a.m. ET on the Wednesday of non-farm payroll week.
- Hold times mentioned: one shot one kill = 1–5 days hold; short-term = 1–3 days.
- Daily volume imbalance example candle: open 288.00, close 289.50 (1.5 handles = 6 ticks; caption values).
- Macro: 6:50–7:10 a.m. ET.

## Glossary candidates
`Monthly macro keys` — three-month OHLC plus previous week's high/low layout for premium/discount context.
`Matrix (layout)` — a saved chart containing only one family of reference levels (NWOG matrix, FPFVG matrix, RTH ORG matrix).
`Octant / quadrant` — eighths/quarters of a PD array range used for grading (CE at the midpoint).
`Gradient levels` — the fib-graded divisions of a range.
`NWOG` — New Week Opening Gap: Friday last print to Sunday 6 p.m. ET open; keep five weeks.
`First presented FVG` — first FVG after 9:30 ET.
`RTH opening range gap` — previous 4:14 p.m. ET close to 9:30 a.m. ET open on RTH chart.
`Shadow` — overlap zone of two nested RTH opening range gaps; an order block anchored there is valid and strong.
`Nested PDAs` — overlapping NWOGs/RTH ORGs/FPFVGs; use the widest one price is inside (partly inferred).
`Five pillars of algorithmic price delivery` — monthly/weekly/daily OHLC keys plus the gap/FVG matrices (list only partly stated).
`One shot one kill` — position trade held 1–5 days, framed with HTF premium/discount.
`Quarterly shift` — large swing move tied to seasonal tendency (taught in the 2016/2017 mentorship).
