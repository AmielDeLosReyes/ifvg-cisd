## Concepts

### First-presented FVG: 3-day life cycle and reuse  (aliases: first presentation FVG, first FVG 9:30-10:00)
- **Kind:** rule / refinement
- **What ICT says:** Carry each day's first-presented FVG (the first FVG that forms in the opening range 9:30–10:00 ET) forward on your chart for a 3-day life cycle. After the third day its importance wanes in his view, though it can still be used. He shows yesterday's (Thursday Aug 29) first-presented FVG in green and describes today's price reacting to it. Opening range = 9:30 to 10:00.
- **How to use:** Price tapping beneath relative equal lows, touching the 9:30 opening price, then popping off the consequent encroachment (CE) of yesterday's first-presented FVG is, to him, a possible single narrow "model": one small, repeatable thing to look for, then sit still the rest of the day.
- **Example in this video:** After the open (NQ with a big premium gap) price dropped into yesterday's first-presented FVG, took the relative equal lows beneath, tagged the 9:30 opening price, popped off the CE of yesterday's FVG, and rallied to pump above the initial/minor buy-side liquidity, forming today's first FVG in the 9:30–10:00 window. Price then came back into the opening range and into that new FVG. Later in the morning price traded down into yesterday's first-presented FVG again ([0:59:15](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3555)).
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:33](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=393) — 3-day life cycle and the pop off the CE; [0:11:06](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=666) — "that could be your model"; [0:20:58](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1258) — recap of the opening sequence; [0:55:05](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3305) — watching whether price gets back above today's first FVG.

### Immediate rebalance (immediate rebound) rule  (aliases: immediate rebounds)
- **Kind:** rule (caption partly unclear)
- **What ICT says:** After an expansion candle, a bullish PD array will usually see the very next candle trade back to the level (the high of the candle prior to the expansion, i.e. the edge of the gap) and then run immediately — sometimes within the same candle, sometimes the very next. If it returns once more and closes with its body into the area, price has to show quick "go go go" strength, otherwise it is "problematic".
- **Example in this video:** Candle runs up into the opening price, next candle trades down into the level (immediate rebalance), then another dip closes on the body; he says it now must expand to make a higher high of the day or the idea fails.
- **Timestamps:** [0:12:45](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=765) — rules for immediate rebounds; [0:14:57](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=897) — after leaving the ORG, does it keep going or come back in?

### Naked chart with raw price levels (notepad method)  (aliases: levels in raw form, naked chart)
- **Kind:** process / refinement
- **What ICT says:** He keeps his chart naked and keeps the levels he is watching in a notepad in raw price form, so he gets a clean measurement between what price is doing and what it can do by reading the price axis against his notes. He anticipates price running to those levels; they only appear on the chart when price presents and "books" there. He draws levels on charts in these lectures only for teaching. Having all levels drawn without an outlined narrative is confusing and lets trolls say "it will hit one of them".
- **How to use:** For each level ask: if it is a premium level, does it repel price lower or stop it going higher? If a discount level, does it hold price up or is it smashed through easily? Couple that with an easy narrative (time of day, draw on liquidity); let setups that go against your bias pass.
- **Timestamps:** [0:19:05](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1145) — what next level is it reaching for; [0:21:56](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1316) — too many levels without a narrative; [0:23:31](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1411) — notepad and naked chart.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** In the same stretch he mentions an "event horizon" — roughly the midpoint region between the Aug 18 NWOG above and the nearest NDOG below, used as a raw-number reference zone (caption unclear on the exact definition).

### Layered NDOG/NWOG clusters as speed bumps  (aliases: nested opening gaps, high-resistance run conditions, "congestion")
- **Kind:** rule / condition filter
- **What ICT says:** When new day and new week opening gaps are nested close together near price (here the Aug 27 NDOG, the Aug 18 NWOG and the Aug 16 NDOG), price needs a real, significant move to slice through, and the algorithm "constantly refers back" to them while they are within the time frame he specifies. A cluster acts as an initial draw. If price fails to even reach the lowest threshold of the cluster (here the Aug 18 NWOG low) and drops below its low, it is tipping its hand that it does not want to go higher. This is not a lack of buyers or a sellers' imbalance (he calls that nonsensical): you are measuring the efficiency of price reaching premium arrays and whether it has the "pursuit" to reach the next one. If instead price slices aggressively through (one or two candles), ignoring them, it signals it is in a hurry, usually because of an imbalance or liquidity pool it is targeting.
- **How to identify:** Plot the last few NDOG/NWOG ranges; check proximity to current price. With few of them near price, runs are cleaner and more elongated ("interstate highway early in the morning"); clustered close together they impede flow and produce short-lived moves ("speed bumps").
- **How to use:** If bearish, you want discount arrays "demolished" with no respect, for a fast run; if price struggles at each one, the market is not fast, do not push. Test: does a premium level repel price, does a discount level hold it?
- **Example in this video:** NQ Aug 30: opens with a huge premium opening gap above, runs up, falls about one tick short of the Aug 18 NWOG high (about 19,629 vs a 19,628.75 high), rolls back into the 9:30 opening price / Aug 27 NDOG high, and chops for the rest of the morning with repeated short-term highs taken and little follow-through.
- **Timestamps:** [0:24:20](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1460) — nested gaps, what slicing through means; [0:25:08](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1508) — failing to reach the NWOG low = tipping its hand; [0:31:15](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1875) — cleaner when gaps are far apart; [0:32:49](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1969) — disrespect of the array shows urgency; [0:38:38](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2318) — one tick short of the NWOG high.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Builds directly on lecture 19/20 (layered gaps, high-resistance runs). New here: the "tipping its hand" test and the explicit proximity/clean-run comparison. He also calls the resulting chop "time distortion" (caption unclear).

### Fridays and holidays: when not to push your edge  (aliases: day of week tendency, Friday into a long weekend, holiday volume)
- **Kind:** rule / day-of-week tendency
- **What ICT says:** This is the Friday before Labor Day weekend. He is not saying "never trade Fridays": people take his words out of context. Generally he likes to make money before Friday because Friday is the last bit of a daily/weekly candle: in a bullish week the daily candle opens near its low and closes near its high, so Friday usually will not make a much higher high or have a deep retracement and mostly works inside Thursday's range. On a holiday Friday, deep-pocketed institutions are largely not participating, so you do not get clean institutional order flow; they roll stops to a manageable level and leave. Treat the week as non-farm-payroll-like: the easier price action comes early in the week. Also be wary of the first day after a holiday (Tuesday) while volume irons out.
- **How to use:** He only trades such a day if he can identify a daily/4h/1h pool of liquidity or inefficiency target; otherwise he does nothing. His son Caleb is told not to trade Fridays going into holiday weekends. Never push (dull) a sharpened, tested edge on days like these ("don't push, push, push"); the market is fickle.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This clarifies, not contradicts, earlier "Friday" advice. He explicitly rejects the shorthand "ICT says never trade Mondays/Fridays". He also says light volume does not mean price runs because of buy/sell pressure: it means fewer participants for the algorithm to mark to market against; you need the stage set (participants, clean runs) for efficient trading.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:26](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=266) — holiday weekend, no stream Monday/Tuesday; [0:28:01](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1681) — don't trade Fridays into holidays, treat week like NFP week; [0:48:41](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2921) — only trade with a daily/4h/1h target; [0:49:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2967) — Friday is the last of the daily candle; [0:56:46](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3406) — don't push your edge.

### Economic calendar planning and news days  (aliases: news drivers, NFP, FOMC, fake first run)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** On the weekend, look at the whole next month of the calendar for heavy, medium and high-impact news drivers. The days before high-impact events (e.g. before FOMC) can be quiet; the public thinks "the casino is open" on days like NFP Friday. Students can trade those days only after the initial shock wave, and hold no opinion going into them. The first run on a high-impact news driver is the fake one (the "first mouse" gets caught; you pick up the cheese afterwards). News events like FOMC and NFP are, in his words, heavily manually intervened.

### Back-testing price at known levels and the "hunter" model  (aliases: backtesting, model building, hunter analogy)
- **Kind:** process
- **What ICT says:** Over the weekend go through the charts and study how many times and at what time of day price traded into a given area (here the Aug 18 NWOG with the Aug 27 NDOG inside): how it behaved, what formations appeared, whether there were frameable runs, what it aimed for, how long it took from a first FVG or a low being taken until the opposite-direction run, and how much range it moved. Framing that is "the first beginning point of a limitless supply of setups". Hunter analogy: tracks in the snow are the evidence, a hide (tree stand) is your trade location, and you hunt the one thing (deer, elk) you prefer; likewise each trader picks the specific repeating phenomenon that appeals to them. He says he can make a new model every day because they are built on how the algorithm behaves around certain events, and that he will teach next week how to use higher-timeframe charts in a top-down approach and anticipate setups in advance.
- **How to use:** Start with whatever jumps out at you; it need not be your model for life. Journal the examples, screenshot them.
- **Timestamps:** [0:39:41](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2381) — study old price action, start with what jumps out; [1:00:07](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3607) — checklist of what to review; [1:01:41](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3701) — hunter analogy; [1:04:54](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3894) — next week plan.

### Journaling, positive self-talk and not announcing predictions  (aliases: reticular activating system, tape reading, scar tissue)
- **Kind:** psychology / process
- **What ICT says:** Journal the examples you could not see live, with positive self-talk, to "trick" your subconscious into expecting those conditions (activating the reticular activating system) so you spot them while tape reading. Do not share predictions on social media: it raises the need to be right and to save face, and being wrong then costs you emotionally (scar tissue). Journaling the call and feeding the journal positive recognition avoids chasing significance, being liked, or respected.
- **Timestamps:** [0:40:29](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2429) — journal with positive self-talk; [0:41:18](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2478) — don't tell people what you think will happen; [0:42:06](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2526).

### Liquidity is the lifeblood; algorithm "patches" inefficiency; inducement and smart-money traps are not real  (aliases: algorithm doctrine, engineering liquidity, one contract marks price)
- **Kind:** doctrine / definition
- **What ICT says:** The market moves on liquidity. Where a price range has none (an inefficiency), price is "deficient" and goes there to provide liquidity/efficiency ("patchwork"), then decides whether to continue its current program (e.g. sell program, reprice lower) or reach to liquidity above the inefficiency. It can "engineer" liquidity: encourage traders to use obvious levels (relative equal highs as resistance, stops above/below old lows) — that engineering is real. Price moves because only one contract needs to trade between one buyer and one seller (mark to market); candles do not measure how many contracts traded; volume is a red herring. The algorithm does not see your stops or know how many contracts sit under an old low; it goes to levels defined by reference points. He states that "inducement" (as taught by some who got leaked content) and "smart money traps" are not real concepts.
- **Timestamps:** [1:15:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4527) — liquidity as lifeblood, engineering liquidity; [1:16:16](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4576) — "inducement" and smart-money traps not real; [1:17:52](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4672) — one contract is enough, volume a red herring; [1:19:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4767) — patching inefficiency, then next program.
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Directly contradicts the usual "inducement" framing in ICT-derived retail content; also repeats and sharpens lecture 19's statement that the algorithm does not know how many orders are resting.

### Dealers vs traders; floor traders and yesterday's high/low  (aliases: market makers are dealers, floor pivots)
- **Kind:** aside / definition
- **What ICT says:** Market makers/desk traders are dealers who handle price piped to them; they do not originate it and often cannot call where price is going. Ex-pit floor traders had a "pulse of the market" edge and used yesterday's high/low plus floor pivot numbers (e.g. R1 just above yesterday's high suggests a run to yesterday's high); on charts today they struggle, and he says VWAP and volume profile are excuses for entries, not edges. The bridge from pit activity to chart is the PD array/liquidity framework he teaches.
- **Timestamps:** [1:20:14](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4814) — dealers not traders; [1:22:34](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4954) — floor traders, pivots and yesterday's high/low; [1:28:11](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=5291) — VWAP/volume profile.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- First-presented FVG (first FVG in 9:30–10:00 ET) stays relevant for a 3-day life cycle.
- Opening range = 9:30–10:00 ET.
- Keep levels in a notepad as raw prices; chart stays naked while trading; draw levels only to teach.
- Layered NDOG/NWOG near price = congested, short-lived moves; fast slice through them = urgency; failing to reach the cluster's lowest edge and dropping = it won't go higher.
- On Fridays before long weekends do not trade heavily; only trade with a clear daily/4h/1h target.
- Bullish week: Friday tends to work within Thursday's range with a small higher high at most.
- High-impact news: wait for the initial shock; first run is fake; hold no opinion going in.
- Check the next month's economic calendar on weekends.
- Levels: buy-side about 19,694–19,695 (quadruple RE high, untouched); Aug 18 NWOG high about 19,629, price topped at about 19,628.75 (one tick short).
- Backtest checklist: how often, what time, what formation, run size, time from FVG/low taken to reversal.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Keep the sharpened knife sharp: do not force trades in congestion; do not overtrade or over-leverage "thinking the next trade is the one" ([0:54:14](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3254), [0:55:56](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3356)).
- Professional mindset vs "fauxfessional": beware opinions from people who rent cars/servers, trade replay, or sell courses; learn from those who tell you where you are likely to get hurt ([0:51:04](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3064)).
- Journal positive self-talk; do not seek significance on social media ([0:42:06](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2526)).
- Sitting still is a trade: not trading a muddy, holiday Friday is fine ([0:11:06](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=666)).
- A beginner is not expected to navigate congested price; setups look easy when pointed out but you must backtest ([1:29:46](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=5386)).
- Rely on your own logic rather than following live streamers or signals (the "poor person with a pan" image) ([1:06:33](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3993)).
- Know your model before the day and where it will form; retail waits for an indicator to flash ([1:07:22](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4042)).

## Glossary candidates
`First-presented FVG` — first FVG formed in the 9:30–10:00 opening range; kept for 3 days.
`Immediate rebalance (rebound)` — the next candle after an expansion candle trades back to the gap edge and then runs.
`Event horizon` — the area halfway between nearby reference gaps (NWOG above, NDOG below) as used for raw-number levels (caption unclear).
`Speed bumps` — layered NDOG/NWOG levels that slow the algorithm's flow.
`Tipping its hand` — price disrespecting (or failing to reach) a level, revealing intent.
`Fauxfessional` — his term for pretend professionals/gurus.
`Time distortion` — his name for choppy price under a cluster of PD arrays (caption unclear).
