## Concepts

### Regular Trading Hours Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG)
- **Kind:** rule / definition
- **What ICT says:** RTH ORG is defined by comparing the previous day's settlement price (4:14 p.m. ET) to today's 9:30 a.m. ET opening. If opening is higher = premium gap; if lower = discount gap. Can be divided into quadrants and octants (eighths) to create reference levels. Previous 5 days' RTH ORGs remain relevant reference points even after price has moved away.
- **How to identify:** On daily chart, mark previous day's 4:14 p.m. closing and current day's 9:30 a.m. opening. Draw line between them. When market opens above settlement = premium; below = discount.
- **How to use:** On intraday charts (1-min, 5-min), algorithmic price action will respect these levels, particularly quadrant and octant subdivisions. Bodies of candlesticks should respect these support/resistance levels. If they do, it's algorithmic. Use as bounce/fail references for order flow confirmation.
- **Example in this video:** Thursday's RTH ORG high was used as octant reference on Tuesday intraday. Candlestick bodies stopped precisely on this octant level multiple times. When price refused to go above it after driving up, it indicated algorithmic rejection of further upside.
- **Timestamps:** [0:09:25](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=565) — explaining settlement price anchors; [0:14:07](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=847) — showing octant levels in use; [0:15:43](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=943) — confirming Thursday's gap becomes Tuesday's discount array
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** RTH ORG uses regular trading hours only (9:30–4:14 ET), not electronic hours. Must distinguish between Monday's gap (using Friday settlement) and Tuesday's gap (using Monday settlement). When price trades above an RTH ORG, it becomes a discount array for future references if you're bullish.

### Sell Side Imbalance Buy Side Inefficiency (SIBI)
- **Kind:** definition / rule / entry model
- **What ICT says:** SIBI is a gap that appears when a candlestick leaves a gap below it (sell side unfilled). The rule: "If it wants sell side, this SIBI will remain unfilled." If market does not take the sell side (stays above the SIBI wick), that SIBI will completely fill in later when price retraces. If the SIBI does stay partially open, it means sell side will eventually be taken.
- **How to identify:** Find a down close candlestick with a gap below its low; the unfilled portion is the SIBI. The gap is the inefficiency; the body close shows delivery direction.
- **How to use:** Anticipate: if SIBI unfilled → market doesn't take sell side liquidity → SIBI fills in on retrace. If SIBI fills → market is going lower to take sell side. Stop placement: just above SIBI high if you're short expecting sell side. Entry: on the retrace into SIBI if using as reversal signal.
- **Example in this video:** 10:07 candlestick created a SIBI by closing down with a gap below. The gap (inefficiency) was 24,892.50 level. Market action: price later tried to fill it but stopped at consequent encroachment (higher), proving it was NOT going down to take sell side, so SIBI stayed largely unfilled.
- **Timestamps:** [0:03:00](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=180) — explaining the post about SIBI; [0:04:37](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=277) — showing the SIBI on chart; [0:06:13](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=373) — proving it will fill if NOT taking sell side
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** This logic is algorithmic/conditional, not mechanical. Beginners misread it as "the candle will stay open forever." What it means: IF market is bullish AND doesn't take sell side, THEN SIBI will fill. Read the logic, not just the gap. He contrasts this with people who say "ICT was wrong—the candle didn't stay open," but they missed the condition.

### Consequent Encroachment (CE)
- **Kind:** definition / refinement
- **What ICT says:** The midpoint (50% level) of a fair value gap or inefficiency. When a candlestick body touches or stops at CE, it shows algorithmic precision and order flow balance. CE acts as a turning point: bodies stopping at CE indicate hesitation, likely reversal.
- **How to identify:** Divide any gap (SIBI, premium FVG, opening range gap) into two halves. The line separating them is CE. Mark it on your chart.
- **How to use:** If price retraces into an inefficiency and stops at CE (not lower), it's a sign the inefficiency will NOT be fully closed; expect bounce. If price closes BELOW CE on a body, the inefficiency is likely to be fully exploited.
- **Example in this video:** Multiple times on Tuesday, price came down into 10:07 SIBI, touched CE (consequent encroachment midpoint), and bounced. This showed bullish control and failure to take sell side.
- **Timestamps:** [0:06:13](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=373) — "stopped dead in its tracks" at CE; [0:18:09](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1089) — teaching bodies vs wicks at CE level
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Wicks are allowed to go through CE; only bodies matter. Wicks are "coloring outside the lines like a child." Bodies tell the truth.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) — First Presented vs Premium/Discount Arrays
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** FVG is created when a candlestick leaves a gap. Premium FVG = gap left above price (when close is above open). Discount FVG = gap left below price (when close is below open). "First presented FVG" is the FIRST FVG that confirms the direction of the move. Must have displacement (candle not trading back into opening range). Displacement = move outside the previous day's or session's range in the direction you're trading.
- **How to identify:** Identify the gap (high of one candlestick to low of next, or vice versa). Confirm displacement has occurred. Only then label it "first presented FVG." Do not form-fit or cherry-pick; only the first one that meets criteria.
- **How to use:** First presented FVG becomes your initial resistance/support. Entries: trade into it expecting reversal OR trade above/below it expecting continuation. Targets: subsequent buy/sell side liquidity pools. Stop loss: outside the FVG opposite your direction.
- **Example in this video:** At 9:44 a.m., after displacement upward, the first presented FVG was identified (with consequent encroachment annotated). It became the initial resistance that price later tested and held, then bounced from.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:40](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1660) — defining first presented FVG vs secondary; [0:28:27](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1707) — showing the FVG on Tuesday chart; [0:29:16](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1756) — trading into premium FVG
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** He emphasizes NOT calling a FVG "first presented" until displacement is confirmed. Many traders form-fit and call every gap a FVG. His rule: no displacement = wait for more data. Displacement = now it matters.

### Inversion Fair Value Gap (iFVG)
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** iFVG is a fair value gap used in REVERSE. A candlestick that hasn't displaced below its opening (if bearish scenario) or above its opening (if bullish) can function as an iFVG. Entry: trade below it (if it's a premium array) and then back UP into it, using the iFVG to confirm bullish order flow inside it.
- **How to identify:** Look for a candlestick whose body never extended into the opposite direction's range. Example: a down-close candlestick (bullish bias) whose body stayed above the midpoint, with upper gap unfilled.
- **How to use:** If using as iFVG, trade back into the upper portion and watch for order flow confirmation. Stop loss below iFVG midpoint (consequent encroachment). Target: buy side liquidity above.
- **Example in this video:** The 8:57 SIBI was analyzed as an iFVG. Price traded down to test it, then bounced and ran higher, using it as a launching pad.
- **Timestamps:** [0:26:52](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1612) — introducing iFVG concept
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** iFVG is NOT the same as a regular FVG. It's a candlestick that never violated a certain threshold, allowing it to serve as reversal confirmation.

### Order Block (Propulsion Block variant)
- **Kind:** definition / entry model
- **What ICT says:** An order block is a candlestick (or series of candlesticks) that represents institutional accumulation or distribution. When price trades above an order block's opening after it closes below, it's bullish; when price trades below its opening after it closes above, it's bearish. A propulsion block is an order block that "propels" price—the signal to extrapolate.
- **How to identify:** Find a down-close candlestick during a downtrend (represents selling at support). When price later breaks above its open = activation. The prior candlestick(s) backing it up (if also down-close) = the propulsion setup.
- **How to use:** Once the signal candlestick (order block) has its opening breached on an opening or close basis, project upward extrapolation to next liquidity level. This is NOT support/resistance; it's a trigger.
- **Example in this video:** Not deeply explored, but referenced in passing as part of broader price action reading.
- **Timestamps:** [0:30:04](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1804) — mentioning "it can wick beyond, but bodies stop at octant"
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Propulsion blocks are a subset of order blocks and imply rapid extrapolation ("like rocket fuel").

### Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL) vs Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Buy-side liquidity = resting above current price (buy orders waiting for a rally to fill). Sell-side = resting below current price (sell orders from shorts wanting to cover). Identifying these pools and whether they'll be taken = the core of his algorithmic trading.
- **How to identify:** Look at the daily high (BSL) and daily low (SSL) of prior days' ranges. Also, intraday, look at incomplete runs or failed breakout levels that leave liquidity hanging.
- **How to use:** If market structure is bullish, you expect price to run to BSL and stop (or wick through and reverse). If bearish, expect SS. Entry: anticipate the liquidity run and size accordingly. Targets: the known liquidity pools.
- **Example in this video:** Intraday highs (25,000 146.75, 25,108 even) = minor buy side. Sell side at 24,892.50.
- **Timestamps:** [0:08:38](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=518) — listing the levels; [0:11:01](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=661) — explaining how algorithm uses sell-side info
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Not all highs/lows = major liquidity pools. Know your context and whether a level was "fought over" (many rejections) vs "breezed through."

### Displacement
- **Kind:** definition / rule
- **What ICT says:** Displacement is a candlestick or price move that goes AWAY from the previous session's/day's opening range. Bullish displacement = close above the range. Bearish = close below. Displacement confirms direction and is necessary before calling a FVG "first presented."
- **How to identify:** Check if the candlestick closes outside the prior session's open/close range in the direction of your intended trade.
- **How to use:** Only trade setups (FVGs, order blocks) after displacement in your bias direction is confirmed. No displacement = no signal = wait.
- **Example in this video:** Tuesday morning opened down but displaced UP during the day, confirming the bullish bias. This allowed the "first presented FVG" to be called.
- **Timestamps:** [0:27:40](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1660) — emphasizing displacement confirmation
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Displacement must be meaningful (a full candlestick or clear move, not a small wick).

## Glossary candidates

`SIBI` — Sell Side Imbalance By-side Inefficiency; a gap left by a down-close candlestick where the low is not filled, signaling whether sell-side liquidity will be taken.  
`Consequent Encroachment (CE)` — Midpoint of a fair value gap or range; candlestick bodies respecting this level show algorithmic order flow; breach of CE on closing basis indicates gap will be fully exploited.  
`Octant` — One-eighth subdivision of an opening range gap; algorithmic price action often stops to the tick at these gradient levels.  
`Displacement` — A candlestick or move closing outside the prior session's opening range in the direction of intended trade; required to confirm bias before calling setup "first presented."  
`iFVG` — Inversion Fair Value Gap; a candlestick that hasn't traded into the opposite direction, usable as a reversal confluence when price retraces into it.  
`RTH ORG` — Regular Trading Hours Opening Range Gap; defined by previous day's 4:14 p.m. settlement price and current day's 9:30 a.m. opening; can be premium or discount and subdivided.
