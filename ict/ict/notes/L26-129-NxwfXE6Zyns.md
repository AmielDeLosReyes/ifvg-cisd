## Concepts

### High Resistance vs Low Resistance Liquidity Run Conditions
- **Kind:** definition
- **What ICT says:** Low resistance conditions show volume imbalances and FVGs, big one-sided range candles in one direction, small-body short-lived retracements against it, and longer protractions with more candles in the run's direction. High resistance conditions are the opposite: price stays inside the previous candle's range and inside inefficiencies longer than it should, "not in a hurry to go anywhere". Typical at the start of the year; he expects the shift toward more freely running conditions toward spring (last couple of weeks of March, April, May).
- **How to identify:** Price keeps overlapping the prior candle's range, wicks poke outside and come back, inefficiencies take a long time to fill.
- **How to use:** In high resistance, be highly selective and cherry-pick days; precision is worse (a wick-only deviation outside a range is not a clean signal).
- **Example in this video:** NQ daily: a single small deviation outside the range with a close "a little concerning from a precision stance", but consistent with high resistance conditions.
- **Timestamps:** [0:04:43](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=283) — high resistance conditions stated; [0:09:30](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=570) — what low resistance looks like; [0:20:37](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1237) — conditions to change toward spring
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Same family as "high/low resistance liquidity run" conditions in the model list.

### Inside Day (two versions) and compression -> expansion  (aliases: inside day on a body basis)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** The classic inside day: today's highest high is lower than the previous day's high and today's lowest low is higher than the previous day's low. His version also counts the day as inside when the body of the candle is inside the previous day's range even if the wicks exceed it. He says this body-inside day "in and of itself is a PD array" signalling compression; compression that builds over days (smaller ranges, inside days) releases like a spring, either higher or lower, giving sustained price runs for a day trader.
- **How to identify:** Compare the daily body with the previous day's high-to-low range (his version) or the full high-low (classic).
- **How to use:** Expect protraction tomorrow/Wednesday. With his bearish bias he expects big down-close candles and an expansion to the downside that explores the relative equal lows. If price takes out the high (the swing high noted below) he revisits whether he is wrong.
- **Example in this video:** MNQ daily Monday Feb 23: Friday's low was 24,677.00; Monday traded to 24,667.75 (almost 10 handles lower), and Monday's high was lower than Friday's high, but the body stayed inside Friday's range. Low was only wick damage.
- **Timestamps:** [0:05:32](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=332) — two versions of an inside day; [0:07:08](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=428) — Friday low 24,677 vs Monday low 24,667.75, body inside; [0:07:55](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=475) — compression builds then releases; [0:10:17](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=617) — body-inside = a PD array, anticipating protraction
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Same concept as flagged in the Feb 27 video (L26-127). He warns that clipping this as a sound bite makes it sound like he is predicting direction; he opened with a bearish premise.

### Discount Wick graded with CE  (aliases: wick consequent encroachment, close below the midpoint of the wick)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** The wick below Friday's low is a discount wick (the discount side exploring lower). Grade it with a fib: if price trades below the wick's CE (midpoint) and closes below it, that generally (not always) indicates continuation in that direction. If it cannot close below the wick's midpoint, it usually means high resistance conditions continue and the low will not be explored for now.
- **How to identify:** Draw the fib across the wick; level 0.5 = CE (he shows his fib settings via screenshot, new viewers can copy them).
- **How to use:** Close below the CE of the discount wick -> expect long black down candles toward the lows and then the relative equal lows below ("club 64"). On the upside, a wick above the high is acceptable because of volatility, but a close above it would make him consider himself wrong, and he would then expect a draw toward the high.
- **Example in this video:** Notepad level: the half level at 24,608 must be breached on a closing basis. He removes the fib afterwards to keep the chart pristine.
- **Timestamps:** [0:11:52](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=712) — discount wick; [0:12:42](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=762) — close below CE continues, no close keeps high resistance; [0:15:04](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=904) — 24,608 half level on notepad
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Keep the chart clean: "the least amount of things you have on your chart, the easier it is to follow a narrative".

### RTH Opening Range Gap (RTH ORG) — premium vs discount, high/low assignment
- **Kind:** definition (precise)
- **What ICT says:** The gap is between where price opens at 9:30 ET (regular trading hours) and the previous day's RTH close, which prints at 4:14 pm ET on the chart (closing candle; the last 4:14 candle on the time axis). If the 9:30 opening price is above the previous RTH close it is a premium gap: the 9:30 open is the RTH ORG high and the previous close is the low. If the 9:30 open is below the previous RTH close it is a discount gap: the 9:30 open is the RTH ORG low and the previous close is the high. Do this every day and keep several days of levels (he also marks the CE/gradient levels when wide).
- **How to identify:** Toggle the chart to RTH to see the close; then mark the gap. In Monday's case "last trading day" is Friday, so Monday's RTH ORG is also the new week opening gap.
- **How to use:** These levels are the key levels for entries and targets; price reacts at them "to the tick", often during macro times; he keeps roughly a week of ORG levels (Thursday ORG low/high, Friday ORG high/low, Tuesday ORG high/low, etc.) on his chart.
- **Example in this video:** Friday RTH close 25,079.00; Monday 9:30 open 24,981.25 (lower), so a discount gap: RTH ORG high = 25,079.00 (same price as the NWOG top) and RTH ORG low = 24,981.25. Later: Tuesday's RTH ORG high hit at 24,799.5 exactly at a candle high during a macro time, leaving Tuesday's ORG low untouched, then Friday's RTH ORG low was touched.
- **Timestamps:** [0:21:27](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1287) — NWOG and prior close 25,079; [0:22:17](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1337) — 9:30 open 24,981.25 = ORG low; [0:25:34](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1534) — premium vs discount rule; [0:43:53](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2633) — stacking prior days' ORG highs/lows
- **Nuance / caveats / changes:** Relative to Monday: "Thursday/Friday/Tuesday" ORG levels are from the previous week's sessions. He says the opening range is the 30-minute window starting 9:30, "I don't care what anybody else says".

### Opening range at 9:30 and the Judas swing: buy-side raid then sell
- **Kind:** rule / example
- **What ICT says:** At 9:30 the market runs liquidity in the first 30 minutes: it tends to take close-proximity buy-side and close-proximity sell-side, then "the real move" ensues. A rally into buy-side at 9:30 is the Judas swing (manipulation, trap) designed to induce buyers; smart money takes the other side of that raided buy-side and sells, to drive price toward lower lows (wording about buy stops vs sell stops is caption unclear).
- **How to use:** Anticipate at minimum that price reaches the near buy side; then look for shorts if bearish. A 15-minute time frame is the "bellwether" chart for lay of the land.
- **Example in this video:** 15m, Monday: price rallied into an inefficiency toward the NWOG but could not reach it; at 9:30 it took buy-side (relative equal highs) and tagged a premium SIBI / CISD level on the 15m (pink line) before turning.

### SMT divergence (ES vs NQ) — when it is appropriate
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Use SMT (divergence between closely correlated assets, here ES vs Nasdaq) only when there is a liquidity run or a high-volatility injection such as news; the 9:30 open and the first 30 minutes always qualify. He says SMT is "wonderful, but there's a place for it and it's not in every setup"; if you look for it outside liquidity runs or the opening range you are looking at the wrong time. He never settled what the T stands for (smart money tool/technique).
- **How to identify:** ES kept pushing higher highs at 9:30 while Nasdaq made a lower high.
- **How to use:** Confirms weakness in the asset that failed (Nasdaq), "the kiss of death" for bull-flag traders.
- **Example in this video:** MESH2026 vs MNQ at 9:30: ES made higher highs, NQ a lower high at the CISD/NWOG area.

### Reading weakness with the CE and fib levels after a drop  (aliases: no candles in the upper half, institutional order flow)
- **Kind:** refinement
- **What ICT says:** After a sharp drop leaves a gap, you do not want to see the whole gap filled; you want a small portion left open. Bodies cannot get back to CE (0.5); wicks may do the damage. In the example price passed 0.5 and 0.625 on wicks but failed to reach the 0.75 upper quadrant, and bodies stayed below CE: weakness; expect it to work lower, but do not take a tiny-stop short because volatility is exorbitant. A second attempt that did not even reach CE gave a lower high: "nobody is allowed to lay down candles in the upper half" — real institutional order flow. He stresses you don't need level 2/3 data, footprint or volume profile: open, high, low, close in time is enough.
- **How to use:** Larger candles on the down side, more candles down than up, short retracements (1-3 candles) = bearish order flow. If bullish, the same applies in reverse: price not trading into the lower half of a discount PD array.
- **Timestamps:** [0:36:42](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2202) — small portion left open, bodies can't reach CE; [0:37:31](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2251) — fib levels 0.625/0.75; lower high = order flow; [0:38:18](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2298) — no need for level 2, footprint, volume profile; [0:55:46](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3346) — candle size and count as order flow

### PD array only matters at a key level, at the right time, in line with the draw  (aliases: no man's land)
- **Kind:** rule
- **What ICT says:** Any PD array that is not sitting on one of the pre-marked levels (gradient levels of ORGs/NWOG/NDOG, opening range levels) is in "no man's land" and does not interest him. The recipe: (1) the FVG/PD array is right over a gradient level, (2) at the right time (macro/session), (3) in deference to where he thinks the market will draw. With those three ingredients PDs work, "100% strike rate" (hyperbole). An FVG formed by three candles but not at any level is just a common gap. Futures are a "gentleman's market" because everyone sees the same exact tick levels; forex has broker-specific prices.
- **How to use:** Small ranges of 15-30 handles don't need a liquidity pool as a target ("cookie cutter": 20 handles, done). Closest target that makes sense.
- **Example in this video:** The market bounced off Friday's RTH ORG low the moment a candle opened and traded into it (an "easy get in and go"), and meandered between Tuesday's ORG high and Friday's ORG low.
- **Timestamps:** [0:47:49](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2869) — no man's land; [0:48:36](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2916) — key level + time; [0:49:24](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2964) — three ingredients; [0:50:12](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3012) — small range and no pool needed; [0:51:00](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3060) — futures gentleman's market

### First Presented FVG in the opening range (9:31-10:00)
- **Kind:** entry model / rule
- **What ICT says:** Use the very first FVG that shows displacement between 9:31 and 10:00 ET. Technically the window is 9:30-9:59 but he says to use minute 31 (so skip the 9:30 candle), because things can go wrong using the 9:30 candle for people who don't fully know what they are doing. Short at the CE of the FVG with the stop one tick above the candle low that forms it; if stopped out, wait for price to trade back below CE, then use any run back up (an FVG or the up-close candle = rejection block) to short again.
- **How to identify:** Displacement candle leaving a gap; the volume imbalance is drawn from the candle close to the next candle open.
- **How to use:** Sell as price trades up to CE; the next retest is the rejection block. It "should not trade above CE if it is weak".
- **Example in this video:** 9:40 ET 1m: FVG high = a candle's low; open 24,928.25, close 24,928.75; CE 24,938.5; the wick failed to reach CE twice, then a candle closed below the opening price and price protracted down to sell-side (Judas swing, high-frequency style); short above that candle's close with stop just above CE.

## Rules, checklists & numbers stated
- Trade micros: ICT himself uses micro NQ to talk concepts; minis or multiple minis are "ill-advised" unless extremely well capitalised and experienced (0:01:31).
- Opening range = 9:30 ET for 30 minutes; first-presented FVG window 9:31-10:00.
- RTH close prints at 4:14 pm ET on the chart; RTH ORG high/low assigned by whether 9:30 open is above/below that close.
- Close below the discount wick's CE -> continuation; no close -> high resistance continues.
- Daily body inside previous range = compression; expect protraction the next 1-2 days.
- SMT only at liquidity runs / news / opening range.
- Entries on the opening-price trade-through use stop/market orders (here two micros); his own would be one contract.
- Quoted levels (MNQ, Feb 23): Friday low 24,677.00; Monday low 24,667.75; half level 24,608; Friday RTH close 25,079.00; 9:30 open 24,981.25; 24,799.5 (Tuesday ORG high tag); 9:40 FVG CE 24,938.5.
- Low-hanging 15-30 handle ranges don't need a liquidity pool.

## Process, risk & psychology
- Journal honestly: after a trade, highlight what you did right first, then what could be done differently; no armchair quarterbacking, no ego inflation, no self-flagellation (0:39:56-0:41:32).
- Journaling is the only baseline: the report card is whether you stuck to the model, not trade outcomes (0:41:32-0:42:21). He criticises a self-described ICT student who called journaling a waste of time.
- "Do it when you feel it": he told his son to act on instinct in management so he learns his emotional baseline (0:39:09).
- Small size, low leverage, real account; concepts, not P&L, should inspire students (0:35:55).
- Be selective: not every day is a trading day; skipping days with no thesis is the "only cherry-picking" he does.
- Keep the chart clean; use a notepad of levels.
