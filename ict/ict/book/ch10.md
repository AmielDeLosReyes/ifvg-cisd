ICT treats a wick as an inefficiency: a gap that printed inside one candle, with a midpoint that price refers back to and that candle bodies must respect. This chapter teaches how to grade a wick, why the same wick is a premium or a discount array depending on where price is, how to read bodies against its midpoint, and how he enters, stops and takes profit around it. It also covers the mohawk (the tolerance for a poke), clusters of wicks he calls grey pools and the Obsidian, and his "shadow", which is an overlap of imbalances and not a wick.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch10_d1 | caption: A discount wick graded into quadrants and a CE, with bodies holding its upper half (illustrative) | The wick may be pierced below the CE, but the bodies stay in the upper half, which is what keeps the idea alive.]]

- **A wick is a gap.** Measure from the body end to the real wick tip, mark the 50% line, and call it the consequent encroachment (CE). Mean threshold is the order block's word ([2024 #8 · 1:16:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4565)).
- **Premium or discount depends on where price is.** A wick above price is a premium array, below it a discount array. The same wick flips when price crosses it ([2026 #72 · 34:09](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2049)).
- **Bodies decide, wicks do the damage.** For a long above a discount wick, bodies stay above the CE. For a short below a premium wick, bodies stay below it. A wick that cannot even reach the CE is the strongest read.
- **Mohawk.** A small poke past a PD array's edge, with bodies on the right side, is permissible. Measure the allowed overshoot beforehand.
- **Trade it.** Enter between the candle's open (or close) and the wick CE. Stop at the wick extreme or just beyond the CE. Take a partial at a wick CE on the way to a level. He teaches this mostly on NQ, and also on EURUSD.
- **Clusters and overlaps.** Wick CEs can combine into a grey pool or an Obsidian zone. A "shadow" is a different thing: the overlap of two larger imbalances.

## Bodies versus wicks: which one carries the evidence

Every wick rule here rests on one habit: judge a level by where candle bodies sit, and treat wicks as excursions. Learn this first, because the wick-as-inefficiency teaching only makes sense once you see that ICT trusts bodies more than wicks.

### What it is

A candle has a body (open to close) and wicks (the travel beyond it). ICT reads the body as order flow and the wick as the excursion that takes stops and pokes through levels. His March 2026 summary is that bodies tell the narrative and wicks do the damage ([2026 #113 · 1:04:13](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3853)). Smart money reads bodies, he says, and retail ("street money") gets hurt in the wicks ([2024 #40 · 1:39:27](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5967)). A wick past a level is a child colouring outside the lines, not a failure of the level ([2024 #2 · 1:06:28](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3988)).

### Why it works

These are ICT's claims, not measured facts.

- **Where the bulk sits.** In 2022 he said wicks reach for liquidity and stops while the bulk of the volume stays inside the imbalance ([2022 Ep 22 · 8:49](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=529)). In 2024 he said the bulk of volume is in the bodies ([2024 #40 · 1:39:27](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5967)).
- **How a wick forms.** A wick exists only because the candle first traded to its extreme while still open. Until it closes, price may sit outside a boundary. Give the candle room ([2024 #11 · 47:14](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2834)).
- **Liquidity.** A wick above an FVG with bodies inside is the algorithm collecting the last liquidity at an old high before moving on ([2026 #140 · 28:31](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1711)).

### How to read it

1. Mark the level (a gap, an order block, a wick CE) and judge it on closed candles. In a clean array, bodies stay at the CE or on one side of it, and wicks may pierce it ([2024 #11 · 48:48](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2928), [2024 #45 · 14:38](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=878)).
2. A wick into a level with no body closing in it means nothing has changed. In August 2024, with a wick into a 15-minute BISI and an iFVG, he said not to try to catch the turn ([2024 #17 · 34:55](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2095)).
3. Count bodies, not wicks, for swing points ([2026 #43 · 34:42](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=2082)). Bodies slightly above an earlier body-high make "relatively equal highs with the bodies" ([2026 #27 · 15:11](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=911)).
4. Prefer the close to the open. A close beyond the midpoint of a gap or candle is usually a pretty high-probability sign of continuation, while an open beyond a level is less reliable ([2026 #30 · 2:56](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=176)).

### Where ICT sets wicks aside

- **FVG tolerance.** Price can trade through a short-term low and into an FVG, and if the bodies respect it the gap stands. In volatile markets he permits more imperfection ([2022 Ep 9 · 25:04](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1504)). On NQ's 3-minute chart he shorted the 10:33 candle as it bumped the top of an FVG, filled at 13,858.25 ([2022 Ep 11 · 9:40](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=580)). In an ES hourly case about five candles wicked above an imbalance while bodies stayed inside, then price released lower ([2022 Ep 22 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=481)).
- **Volume imbalances.** When an FVG contains a volume imbalance (VI), draw the edge to the body. A wick that bridges the VI does not remove it. Chapter 6 teaches this ([2024 #2 · 1:08:53](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4133), [2026 #115 · 4:12](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=252)).
- **Runs and daily bias.** A run on an old high need not take the wicks ([2026 #13 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=196)), and a daily candle whose body stays inside the prior day's body is an inside day despite a wick above ([2026 #127 · 7:21](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=441)).
- **Dealing ranges.** For an implied dealing range he anchors on the body high of the SIBI candle, not the wick high. The wick is a gap that is allowed to do the damage. He warned that anchoring wrongly means the outline will not work ([2026 #49 · 27:18](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1638), [31:18](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1878)).
- **FOMC whipsaws.** When price whips both sides, ignore those wicks and use the body-defined swing as the real range ([2022 Ep 40 · 31:12](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1872)). Chapter 14 covers news days.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading a wick-through as invalidation.** What counts is whether bodies close beyond, and stay.
- **Treating the body rule as a law.** On a US-holiday-volume day in 2026 he said a close below a wick's CE, normally a clue, "could be not true today" ([2026 #69 · 9:17](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=557)).
- **Assuming one Fibonacci anchor.** In 2022 he anchored expansions on bodies normally and on wick extremes on FOMC days, because volatility is high ([2022 Ep 23 · 11:58](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=718)). The FOMC whipsaw rule says to ignore wicks. The notes do not reconcile them, and they seem to answer different questions: one projects a target, the other defines the range.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the idea is unnamed: wicks outside an FVG are fine, and volatility decides whether he anchors on bodies or wicks. In August 2024 he names the tolerance (the mohawk) and starts grading the wick itself as a gap, which is the next section. In 2026 the body rule is explicit and applied to every array. The one tension is the anchor: a wick is graded from its tip, while a dealing range is anchored on the body. He never says one cancels the other.

### Watch it

- [2022 Ep 22 · 8:49](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=529): bodies stay inside while the wicks reach for liquidity.
- [2024 #40 · 1:39:27](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5967): bodies tell the narrative, victims live in wicks.
- [2026 #43 · 34:42](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=2082): count the bodies, not the wicks.

## The wick as an inefficiency: measuring and grading

### What it is

ICT's claim is that a wick is a gap even though it printed inside a single candle. He presented this live on August 14, 2024, said mentorship students never got it, and said he had never seen anyone else split a wick into quadrants ([2024 #8 · 21:06](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1266), [1:46:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=6413)). The range is the full wick, from the real tip (the very highest high or lowest low) to the end of the body it grows from. How long the candle took to print it does not matter ([2024 #8 · 51:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=3068)).

The midpoint is the **consequent encroachment (CE)**. For a wick he says CE, not mean threshold, because mean threshold is an order-block term ([2024 #8 · 1:16:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4565)). Chapter 6 teaches CE, quadrants and octants for gaps, and a wick uses the same levels:

| Level | Position in the wick's range, counting from its low |
|---|---|
| Low of the wick | 0% |
| Lower quadrant | 25% |
| CE | 50% |
| Upper quadrant | 75% |
| High of the wick | 100% |

On a discount wick (below the body) the low is the tip and the high is the body end. On a premium wick (above the body) it is the other way round. In 2026 he adds eighth lines and calls all of these gradient levels, a tool for confirmation and not a primary signal ([2026 #130 · 13:42](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=822)). He insists this is not "quarters theory". It is the same grading he applies to any gap.

It works on every time frame, down to sub-1-minute charts. His reason is that price is price: a 1-minute candle's open, high, low and close behave like a daily candle's ([2024 #8 · 51:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=3068)).

### Why it works

ICT's reasons, again his claims:

- **A wick is a balanced range.** Price went from the candle's close to the low and all the way back, so it traded both ways through that stretch. That is why a wick outranks a body-based order block, "just like a gap" ([2026 #72 · 45:28](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2728)).
- **The algorithm refers back to it.** To know when big runs will come, he says to go to the daily chart and measure wicks, CE, old highs and lows and inefficiencies. Supply-and-demand traders do not grade wicks into halves ([2026 #21 · 56:13](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=3373)).
- **It is simple and visual.** He called the CE a barrier or boundary, with no volume data behind it ([2026 #77 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=390)).

### How to spot it

1. Pick a wick that matters: on a swing high or low, a big news candle, or a big wick on the hourly or daily near price ([2024 #8 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=923), [2024 #37 · 18:35](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1115)). In April 2026 he chose a high because its wick was longer than those of the candle before it and the two after ([2026 #96 · 8:02](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=482)).
2. Prefer a wick that touches a graded level of the range you are already working with. Asked why one wick and not another, he said the first wick to use the graded line counts, and later wicks that miss it are just candles with wicks ([2026 #49 · 39:19](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2359)).
3. Measure from the real tip to the body end. In 2024 he kept candle borders on so tips plot exactly ([2024 #8 · 51:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=3068)). A random line floating in space means nothing ([2024 #32 · 1:00:22](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3622)).
4. Mark the 50% line, and the quadrants if you will use them. On a long wick, draw a Fibonacci, read the 50% level and delete the Fibonacci. Eyeball a short wick ([2024 #8 · 26:02](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1562), [2026 #129 · 15:04](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=904)).
5. Decide where price is: above the wick or below it. That sets the label and the rule (next section).

### Worked example

**A news candle, NQ 1-minute, August 14, 2024.** The 8:30 candle opened, fell more than 100 handles in seconds and printed back up to close in its body. ICT marked the CE and lower quadrant of that long lower wick. Price ran up into the July 28 weekly opening gap (Chapter 11), reversed, traded down to the wick CE and held there with the bodies. Later it explored toward the lower quadrant and the low ([2024 #8 · 5:18](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=318), [22:44](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1364)).

**A daily wick, NQ, 2026.** Thursday's daily low was 18,370 and the CE of its wick was 18,456. In the session he reviewed, price bounced and accumulated at that CE, which he read as institutional buying. If bodies do not go below it, he expects consolidation before the next leg. Find these on the daily chart first, then let intraday charts reference them ([2026 #6 · 2:29](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=149), [4:09](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=249)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **CE is not support.** Support does not push price. What matters is whether price can reach the level at all ([2024 #11 · 3:02:30](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10950)).
- **CE is not mean threshold.** Mean threshold halves an order-block candle (Chapter 9). Equilibrium halves a range that traded both ways. CE halves a gap or a wick.
- **No "tail".** A wick is a wick ([2024 #28 · 55:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3335)). Auto-captions also garble "consequent encroachment" into variants such as "consequent correction". They mean the same CE.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the wick has no level of its own. On August 14, 2024 the wick CE is presented as new. In August and September 2024 he adds the premium and discount labels, the candle-1 wick layer and wick clusters. By 2026 it is routine, with names like "mile marker" and eighth-line grading. One inconsistency: in January and April 2026 he says nobody else grades wicks into halves ([2026 #140](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=719), [2026 #105 · 37:18](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=2238)), yet in February 2026 the notes record him calling wicks as gaps "not a new concept" ([2026 #133 · 1:41](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=101)). The notes do not reconcile the two.

### Watch it

- [2024 #8 · 5:18](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=318): the wick midpoint, and an FVG that cannot trade to its CE.
- [2026 #19 · 58:20](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3500): wicks as "wonderful little mile markers".
- [2026 #140 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=719): grading a wick like an FVG, with quadrants and eighths.

## Premium wicks and discount wicks

### What it is

A wick above the price now trading is a **premium array**. A wick below it is a **discount array** ([2024 #28 · 55:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3335)). The label comes from where market price sits relative to that specific wick, which ICT calls the first thing to check ([2026 #96 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=530)). The same wick is a discount wick while price is above it. After price falls below it and rallies back up into it, it is a premium wick ([2026 #72 · 34:09](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2049), [2026 #73 · 1:11:00](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4260)).

[[DIAGRAM ch10_d2 | caption: One wick, two labels: discount wick while price is above it, premium wick once price rallies up into it from below (illustrative) | The wick never moves; only price's position changes, and with it the side of the CE that bodies must respect.]]

He also uses the words loosely: "discount wick" can simply mean the wick in the lower part of a candle ([2024 #32 · 1:00:22](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3622)). The relative rule is the one that matters. On his notepad an hourly wick is "60m CE" with a small D for discount ([2024 #37 · 19:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1177)).

### Why it works

It is the PD-array logic of Chapter 4 applied to a wick, and he says he gets no benefit from renaming it ([2024 #28 · 55:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3335)). The 2026 words are premium sensitivity and discount sensitivity. A premium array should show premium sensitivity: price meets it and bodies do not hold above its midpoint. A discount array should show discount sensitivity: bodies stay in the upper half ([2026 #19 · 59:08](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3548), [2026 #34 · 25:19](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1519)). His rule of halves for a revisited wick: if bullish it holds the upper half, and if bearish price goes at least to the half and lower ([2026 #49 · 7:30](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=450)).

### How to spot it

1. Grade the wick and extend the CE line to the right.
2. Ask where price is. Above the wick: discount wick. Below it: premium wick. Ask again after every large move.
3. Find the line bodies must respect. It is always the CE, and the rule is easiest to hold in price terms.
    - **Long above a discount wick.** Bodies stay in the upper half, above the CE, and may not bury themselves below it ([2026 #21 · 26:50](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1610), [2026 #38 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=97)). On the usual lower wick, that upper half is the one nearest the body.
    - **Short below a premium wick.** Bodies stay below the CE, with no bodies buried in the upper half ([2026 #32 · 14:50](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=890), [2026 #49 · 7:30](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=450)). On the usual upper wick, that forbidden half is the one at the tip.
4. Look for confluence. Wick CE, FVG CE, the 6 p.m. opening price and a prior candle high stacked together was his August 2024 example ([2024 #8 · 22:44](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=1364)).

### How to trade it

For a short from a premium wick, his April 2026 entry zone runs from the CE down to the low of the wick. "Anything inside of that" is useful, with the stop outside the wick high or the CE and the target on the sell side ([2026 #96 · 34:31](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=2071)). Entries and stops for both directions are in the trading section below.

A premium wick can also be a speed bump in a bull market. In April 2026 he said a wick reaching into a zone above price, combined with an FVG or gap, is a premium array that price is unlikely to close above. A close above it neutralises the array and confirms the bullish bias ([2026 #100 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=48), [1:41](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=101)).

### Worked example

**The flip, London killzone, June 2026, 15-minute chart.** A previous day's wick draws the eye. On Tuesday's range, at 3:00 a.m. (the London open killzone, Chapter 12; the notes give no time zone, presumably ET), price traded up into the CE of the previous day's wick, took out the minor buy side and sold off. That wick was a discount wick while price was above it and a premium wick coming from below. The daily high formed exactly at the CE, with the bodies supporting the logic ([2026 #73 · 1:07:04](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4024), [1:07:50](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4070), [1:11:47](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4307)).

**The same flip, September 2024, NQ.** On the NFP day he marked discount wicks below the August 13 daily FVG, with a volume imbalance under them as the next draw if price kept falling. On the 1-minute chart a discount wick became a premium wick once price traded below it ([2024 #24 · 1:28:24](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5304), [3:08:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11297)). A leg that breaks a wick's midpoint and spends time below it he called accumulation or transition ([2024 #24 · 40:57](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2457)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Labelling by the candle's shape.** A lower wick is not a discount wick forever. Re-label it as price moves.
- **Mixing up the halves.** Think in price. For a long, bodies stay above the CE. For a short, below it. Which half is nearest the body changes with the wick's shape, but the CE line does not.
- **Confusing it with a dealing range's premium and discount.** Chapter 4 compares price with a range's midpoint. A wick's label compares price with that wick ([2026 #119 · 18:15](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=1095)).

### How the teaching changed

He first sorted wicks into premium and discount arrays on August 14, 2024 ([2024 #8 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=923)), and on September 6 began calling the ones below price "discount wicks". By 2026 the idea carries the sensitivity words and is stated as a rule of position.

### Watch it

- [2026 #96 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=530): premium wick versus discount wick, relative to market price.
- [2026 #72 · 34:09](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2049): the same wick is discount above and premium below.
- [2026 #73 · 1:07:50](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4070): the London killzone flip.

## Reading bodies against the wick CE

### What it is

Once a wick is graded, its CE is a checkpoint. The question is not whether price touches it but what the bodies do. The CE plays two roles. It is a **hold test** for your side: a long above a discount wick, or a short below a premium wick. It is also a **trigger** when you want price to go through the wick: a close beyond the CE opens the next target.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that a retracement tells you who is in control. If you are long and price cannot even dip into a level that could support it, that is immediate feedback that price wants higher. Support is not pushing anything. The failure to reach it is the information ([2024 #11 · 3:02:30](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10950)). The mirror is bearish: if a wick cannot even touch the CE of an inefficiency above, it cannot deliver a midpoint ([2026 #49 · 44:09](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2649)). A wick may pierce the CE. The body is the evidence.

### How to read it

For a long above a discount wick, strongest to weakest. A short below a premium wick is the mirror.

1. **Price cannot reach the CE.** Bodies stay in the upper half and the wick does not touch the midpoint. He calls this indicative of bullishness and a stronger validation than a touch ([2026 #44 · 8:56](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=536), [2026 #71 · 20:41](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1241)).
2. **A wick touches or pierces the CE, bodies hold.** This is respected. Even a wick a hair of a tick past a graded level is fine if no body closes through ([2026 #140 · 46:11](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2771)).
3. **A body dips beyond the CE, then is recovered at once.** The next candle, or the one after, must show willingness to go back and preferably close above. The wick should be overtaken by that candle or the very next, and if it is that candle, it is really strong ([2026 #18 · 19:46](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1186), [2026 #38 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=97)).
4. **Bodies stay beyond the CE.** For a long, a body below the CE invalidates the short-term bullishness ([2026 #71 · 20:41](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1241)). For a short, he phrases it more mildly: a close above the midpoint is a concern, not a reason to bail out, and means more information to manage ([2026 #19 · 48:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2895)). Tighten risk rather than hope ([2026 #18 · 18:54](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1134)).

After a higher high made with a wick, hold longs only while bodies stay at or above the CE of the wick that made the prior high. When a wick has pushed into the CE of a gap, the next candle should show willingness to reach its own midpoint and close beyond it ([2026 #101 · 1:04:47](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3887), [46:58](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2818)).

**As a trigger.** When you want price to run through a wick, the close is the signal. For a discount wick below Friday's low (February 2026), a close below its CE generally (not always) indicates continuation. If price cannot close below it, high-resistance conditions continue and the low is not explored for now ([2026 #129 · 12:42](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=762)). The same close is his first-hour confirmation for a PM-session continuation ([2026 #113 · 47:34](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2854)). On the daily chart a wick-only run through the CE is not enough: he wants a body through it ([2026 #49 · 5:53](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=353)).

**As a warning.** If a drop leaves a wick that does not close below its CE, real order flow for lower prices is in jeopardy ([2026 #70 · 14:50](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=890)).

**Inside a range.** An up-wick with a body in the lower half is a rejection of the upside. A down-wick with a body in the upper half is a rejection of the downside. In a short, an up-wick with a lower-half body means hold or add. A body in the upper half means consider a partial or a tighter stop. The body's half is the order-flow truth and the wick is the bait ([2026 #25 · 6:45](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=405), [19:52](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=1192)).

### Worked example

**NQ 1-minute, August 27, 2026, the 10:07 ET candle.** ICT was long after a raid below relative equal lows, with bodies in the upper half of a graded wick. The 10:07 candle opened in that upper half and wicked down to 29,502.00, one tick above his stop at 29,501.75, then responded immediately upward. He read the wick as now acting as a barrier and expected a large up-close candle. He copied the same half-wick measure onto another candle's open to show the logic repeating at each scale ([2026 #21 · 26:50](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1610), [34:05](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2045), [34:53](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2093)).

**Waiting for the close, NQ, 2026.** Price stutters inside an array under a wick above it (the notes give clock times only roughly, about 9:35 to 9:40). A candle wicks up to the prior wick and is rejected, which is not yet confirmation. He waits for a body to close above the wick's CE. The next candle closes above the wick and above the intraday high, which confirms it, and price runs to the buy-side level at 29,118.5. A close above a wick high is, to him, proof that the pools above are reachable ([2026 #18 · 16:42](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1002), [21:25](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1285)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading every touch as a signal.** The CE is a level for bodies, not a bounce guarantee.
- **Treating the close rule as a law.** He says "generally" and "usually". On Fed-speaker days he expects wild wick-type moves and says to give the trade room ([2026 #62 · 12:12](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=732)). Chapter 14 covers news days.
- **Trading when two CE conditions point opposite ways.** In the shadows lecture, price trapped between two opposing wick CEs was a reason not to trade ([2026 #71 · 44:22](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2662)).

## Trading the wick: entries, stops and targets

### What it is

ICT uses the wick CE for four jobs: an entry reference, a stop reference, a partial or target, and a level to trail behind. In each, the wick has to be a real one, in the direction of your narrative, with bodies behaving.

### How to trade it

**Entry, long from a discount wick.** Once the array you are trading is validated and a wick has formed, enter anywhere from the candle's open down to the wick's CE. A more conservative entry runs from the candle's close down to the CE. The stop is the low of the wick. He frames risk from that low to your entry, and targets the buy side immediately above. He stressed he was showing the logic, not telling anyone where to buy live. The notes record no fixed R multiple or size ([2026 #71 · 22:20](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1340), [26:26](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1586), [29:36](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1776)).

[[DIAGRAM ch10_d3 | caption: A wick-CE long: entry band from the candle's open to the CE, stop at the wick low, target the buy side (illustrative) | Risk is measured from the wick low, and the next candle never trades below the CE.]]


**Entry, short from a premium wick.** Sell from the CE down to the wick's low. On April 15, 2026, in MNQ, he shorted at about the half of a big premium wick near 10:05 ET, stop at the high, target the gap below and the sell side. He called it drill practice in a decoupled market ([2026 #101 · 42:08](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2528)). On April 20 he shorted the CE-to-close region of a candle after price ran to the wick CE with no body at or above it ([2026 #96 · 22:30](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=1350)).

**Entry at the CE itself, September 2024.** On a micro NQ 1-minute chart he measured a long lower wick from the body to its low. The CE was 19,974.25 and he shorted there, targeting the CE of a lower wick at 19,930.50. The exit filled at 19,933.75 ([2024 #32 · 1:00:22](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3622), [1:01:10](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3670), [1:01:59](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3719)).

**Stops, from loose to tight.**

- **Wick extreme.** The low of a discount wick for a long, the high of a premium wick for a short ([2026 #71 · 29:36](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1776), [2026 #101 · 42:08](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2528)).
- **Just beyond the CE.** This allows one more spike but exits before a full reversal ([2026 #53 · 7:09](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=429)). A long's stop sits under the signal wick's CE, allowing for a mohawk ([2026 #87 · 15:34](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=934)).
- **Exit on a body.** For a short, if price breaks above the wick CE the setup is broken ([2026 #109 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1293)).

**Trailing.** As new wicks form, raise the stop to just beyond the CE of the wick that supports the position ([2026 #22 · 6:33](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=393)). In a June 2026 trade the first trail went to the CE of the signal wick ([2026 #77 · 5:40](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=340)). In another review, once partials were done, the balance stop sat just above that CE ([2026 #87 · 1:01:11](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3671)). Chapter 19 covers stops and partials in general.

**Targets and partials.**

- **At an old high.** If a buy-side run targets a level and a wick sits just below it, the wick midpoint is a partial. He starts scaling as price enters the mid-wick, exits ahead of a premium level, and scales something out early because price can fall short ([2024 #35 · 11:16](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=676), [12:12](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=732)).
- **On relative equal highs or lows.** If the anchor swing (the leftmost one) has a wick, grade it. Price may only trade into the wick and reject. He always takes a partial between the wick's lower quadrant and its CE and does not need the extreme taken. Stopping short of the CE signals weakness ([2026 #138 · 1:19:18](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4758), [1:20:05](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4805)).
- **A nearer objective.** On an hourly chart he treated the CE of a big discount wick as the first, lower-risk objective on the way to the sell side beneath it. The worst case was a dip below the CE, a return, then the dive ([2024 #37 · 19:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1177), [54:57](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3297)).

**Filters.** Be on side with a narrative first. Treat holiday-volume and Fed-speaker days as degraded, and skip trades where two opposite wick-CE conditions hold.

**Instruments.** ICT teaches this mostly on NQ and MNQ, so the prices here are NQ points. The notes also show wick CEs on EURUSD, ES, MES, YM and crude oil, and he said the same wick-CE entry appeared on a CFD chart ([2024 #32 · 1:01:59](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3719)). The rule needs only open, high, low and close, so it carries to gold CFDs, but the notes hold no gold-specific wick number. Scale distances to gold's own range.

### Worked example

**Raid, wick, long: NQ, a Tuesday afternoon and a Wednesday morning in 2026.** On the Tuesday, sell-side liquidity was raided at 2:06 p.m. ET. The wick of that candle was graded and price never got a body below its CE. The next candle opened below the order block's opening price, and he said to go in and take a long. On the Wednesday a first presented FVG formed after a sell-off, price could not reach that FVG's CE, and a wick below it did not touch the wick CE. He bought from the candle's open down to the wick CE with the stop at the wick low. Price tagged the buy side just above, dipped back into a suspension block (Chapter 9) and traded significantly higher, out of the bearish daily FVG ([2026 #71 · 20:41](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1241), [29:36](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1776)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Entering on a touch alone.** In that lecture the wick-CE test is the qualifier. The wick must follow a validated array, with bodies behaving.
- **Treating the CE as a hard stop price.** He also calls it a checkpoint: if no body violates it, the long is still on side ([2026 #21 · 26:50](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1610)). Judge by closes, then place the actual stop with a margin.
- **Expecting the exact print.** He called an entry landing exactly at the planned level probably random, and warned that price can fall short of a target ([2026 #45 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=436), [2024 #35 · 12:12](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=732)).

## Higher-time-frame wicks as draws

### What it is

A wick on the daily, hourly or even weekly chart is an inefficiency price can be drawn to later. In August 2024 he treated a weekly wick as a gap with a CE ([2024 #9 · 33:00](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1980)). Find these on the higher time frame first, then let the 15-minute and 1-minute charts show how price behaves at them ([2026 #6 · 4:09](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=249)).

### How to trade it

- **Next-day magnet.** Thursday's daily wick CE is a magnet for Friday's bounces ([2026 #6 · 2:29](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=149)). A previous day's discount wick (Thursday's, from 9:55 a.m. ET) was the Friday target. Price ran to its upper half and midpoint, then reversed. The target is the upper half or midpoint, not the whole wick ([2026 #20 · 2:24](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=144), [3:12](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=192)).
- **Discount wick as a draw.** On a daily Nasdaq chart a discount wick had its low near 29,021 and its CE at 29,221.75. Lower time frames pulled down to it, it delivered to that level, and price reversed sharply higher. He calls such a wick low-hanging fruit and expects mean reversion once it is swept ([2026 #66 · 2:28](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=148), [20:02](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=1202)).
- **Two graded wicks.** In February 2026 the lower of two graded daily discount wicks became the intermediate-term draw. After a breakdown, the 1-minute chart traded into its gradient levels, around 18,225 ([2026 #133 · 5:39](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=339), [8:55](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=535)).
- **Reach target.** The October 30, 2025 daily wick he chose on April 20, 2026 was reached by the April 21 rally, which he took as proof he was reading intent correctly ([2026 #94 · 32:34](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1954)).
- **Quadrant of a daily wick.** On January 2, 2026 the lower quadrant of a daily swing-high wick was 25,267.50, and the day's low formed there, which he said was not random. Bodies stopped just above it ([2026 #140 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=719), [46:11](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2771)).
- **The close rule.** A body closing below the CE of a daily discount wick is the confirmation to look for the next array below, such as a volume imbalance or a suspension-block CE. A rally back to the CE from below should fail ([2026 #49 · 4:19](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=259)).

### Worked example

**Projecting the day's low, NQ, a Friday afternoon in 2026.** After price traded through his called level at 28,400, ICT asked where a reasonable daily low would be.

1. **Yesterday's range.** Anchor a Fibonacci from its low to its high, since price is above and projecting down. That gave 28,111.50. The notes do not record the ratio.
2. **A wick from three days back.** Treat it as a gap, because price is above it and trading down into it. A level from its gradient plotted at 28,097.25.
3. **Check both.** Anchor a Fibonacci at each level and read the values back. Both matched.
4. **Blend them.** The midpoint between the two projections is a CE of two projections and a high-probability draw. Measuring the old wick from its low to its high gave 28,070.25, the cluster extreme, and a high-side level at 28,124.25. The notes do not make clear how these relate to the wick's own CE.
5. **Watch the 1-minute chart.** Price traded into those levels, wicked through, rallied and returned. The day's low formed inside the cluster.

His lookback rule is that the last three days sum up your universe of liquidity and inefficiencies, and once price breaches that range you look further back. Daily highs and lows form inside a cluster of PD arrays, not at a single one ([2026 #42 · 48:39](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=2919), [50:16](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=3016), [51:52](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=3112), [54:14](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=3254), [58:14](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=3494)).

## Wicks beside other arrays: precedence and layering

### What it is

When a wick sits close to an FVG or order block, ICT says the wick is the more salient PD array and price gives it more sensitivity. A wick can also be layered with a gap to give two levels of inefficiency.

### How to trade it

- **Stops go beyond the wick.** Shorting into an FVG with a wick nearby, put the stop above the wick, not just above the FVG. Expect price to work inside the wick range before committing through the gap, and wait for a candle that closes outside both ([2026 #111 · 7:06](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=426), [2026 #112 · 14:17](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=857)). On a MES chart he said the real obstacle was the wick, not the swing low ([2026 #112 · 16:58](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=1018)).
- **A wick outranks a body-based order block.** His reason is that a wick is a balanced price range ([2026 #72 · 45:28](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2728)). On EURUSD in March 2026, two consecutive SIBIs sat next to a wick, and bodies stayed inside the wick while wicks poked out ([2026 #113 · 4:59](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=299)).
- **No FVG below? The wick is the next PD array.** In June 2026, with no inefficiency below a bullish iFVG, the next PD array was the wick and its CE the boundary ([2026 #77 · 5:40](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=340)).
- **A wick directly against the gap.** If a SIBI sits directly against a wick, with no candle between, he said to use the wick's gradient levels and not the FVG. With one candle between, the SIBI is valid as a premium array. The captions are confusing, so treat it as "adjacent wick means wick levels, otherwise the FVG" ([2026 #127 · 46:07](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2767), [46:55](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2815)).
- **Candle-1 wick, "extra credit".** If candle 1 of an FVG has a wick next to the gap, that wick is a second inefficiency, and the stop can go one tick below its CE instead of below candle 2's low. He said this takes months of observation to trust and that private students saw it first. On a live 1-minute bullish FVG (about 11:00 ET) his drawdown was about 1.75 handles ([2024 #28 · 2:00:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7214), [2:01:53](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7313)).
- **Look left.** When analysing a gap, check the prior candle's wick. If it already extends into what would be the gap, that wick is the real PD array ([2026 #105 · 37:18](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=2238), [52:10](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=3130)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using only the FVG's edge.** When a wick sits beside it, the wick is the level price tests first.
- **Mixing the wick with a volume imbalance.** When an FVG overlaps a VI, use the VI range as the true inefficiency ([2024 #18 · 2:34:49](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9289)). Chapter 6 teaches VIs. He also writes "rejection block" on long-wick candles, a label from the block family (Chapter 9) ([2024 #38 · 2:44:10](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=9850)).

## The mohawk: how far a wick may go

### What it is

A **mohawk** is ICT's name for a small wick, or a body, a little outside a PD array's exact boundary, behaviour you as the analyst allow. In August 2024 he said to annotate it as a mohawk when journaling ([2024 #18 · 2:33:59](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9239)). The same behaviour appears in 2022 as "coloring outside the lines" and permissible price action. In January 2026 he defined it by the picture: a wick above an FVG while bodies stay inside ([2026 #140 · 28:31](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1711)). Captions spell it "mohawk" and "Mohawk".

[[DIAGRAM ch10_d4 | caption: A mohawk on a bearish FVG: the wick pokes past the gap's high, the bodies stay below the CE, and price falls (illustrative) | The stop sits above candle 2's high, so the poke does not reach it.]]

### Why it works

ICT gives pictures rather than a mechanism. A candle is still forming, so price may go outside a level before it closes. He compares it to a boxer's feint or a pitcher's curveball ([2024 #29 · 31:28](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1888)). In 2026 he called it the algorithm collecting the last liquidity at an old swing high ([2026 #140 · 28:31](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1711)). He also said delivery is muddier and less crisp in the days before high-impact news ([2024 #29 · 30:37](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1837)).

### How to spot it

The skill is measuring the allowed overshoot beforehand, so a scary candle does not shake you out ([2024 #13 · 40:33](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2433)).

1. **Look left.** Take the highest wick to the left of the gap and use its CE. That is where the overshoot should stop. On August 20, 2024 the 1-minute FVG ran from one candle's high to another's low, and the wick ran to the CE of that wick: perfect delivery ([2024 #13 · 22:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1364)).
2. **Check nearby inefficiencies.** With a VI or short-term high nearby, expect a tick or so beyond it at most. A close a tick or two beyond an edge (a close just above a VI high at about 19,598.25, caption approximate) did not change his view ([2024 #4 · 1:32:03](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=5523), [2024 #18 · 2:31:24](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9084)).
3. **Nest the time frames.** On the 15-second chart, bodies stayed inside the 1-minute FVG and only the wick reached its low ([2024 #13 · 24:20](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1460)).
4. **Know the ideal.** A bullish FVG ideally trades only into its upper half. Leaving the lower half open is extremely strong, and overlapping it completely is still permissible ([2024 #13 · 21:05](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1265)).

### How to trade it

- **Do not abandon the thesis for a tick or two.** Label it and carry on ([2024 #18 · 2:33:59](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9239)).
- **Pre-news days: be selective.** Skip the lowest entry threshold, candle 3's edge. Let price run to the gap's extreme, then sell inside the wick that creates the mohawk, with the stop above candle 2 ([2024 #29 · 32:17](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1937)). Chapter 8 has the first-presented-FVG version.
- **Stops allow for it.** A long's stop sits under the signal wick's CE, allowing for the mohawk ([2026 #87 · 15:34](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=934)).
- **Let the minor high be run first.** In April 2026 he said he would rather see a spike through a minor buy-side high, which he called a mohawk, and then short after the drop ([2026 #99 · 40:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2442)).

### Worked example

**NQ AM session, September 30, 2026.** Price respected the high of Monday's RTH opening range gap (Chapter 11) and the wick "as I teach it", a premium wick. The wick poked up as a small mohawk above the gap while the bodies stayed below it, which he read as bearish order flow. An FVG was traded up into, folded over and traded back into, a reclaimed bearish FVG. Price then sold off and took the liquidity below ([2026 #3 · 9:53](https://youtu.be/kRShZKZlvJ4?t=593)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Letting a mohawk become a close.** A body that closes beyond and stays is no longer a mohawk. Of a premium wick he said a body at or above the CE means he is probably wrong ([2026 #96 · 33:44](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=2024)).
- **Calling it a signal.** In consolidation a wick that touches and closes back is permissible but the weakest form of order flow ([2026 #25 · 12:41](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=761)).
- **Inventing a method.** He said he can anticipate about 90% of mohawks and is not teaching how ([2024 #8 · 2:03:41](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7421)). The notes hold no such method.

## Grey pools, the Obsidian and opposing CEs

### What it is

When wicks sit near each other, ICT reads the group as one array. His names in the notes are not consistent ("grey pool", "gray pool", "gray pole"), and he also called the Event Horizon (a zone between two opening gaps, Chapter 11) a gray pool. Keep these apart:

- **Grey pool, a cluster of overlapping wicks (September 2024).** The grey area made by wicks of different lengths overlapping is something nobody pays attention to, but the algorithm refers back to it. Looking lower, he looks for discount arrays and a clustering of wicks, not only FVGs or relative equal lows. He deferred the details to his book or another video ([2024 #29 · 43:32](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2612), [44:21](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2661)).
- **Gray pool, the zone between wick CEs (2026).** Grade two adjacent discount wicks on a down move, mark each CE and split the area between them in half. That midpoint is a magnet and, entered from above, acts as "rocket fuel" for a drop. His April 28, 2026 MNQ example put it near 27,205 (approximate). Another 2026 lecture describes two or more graded wicks whose 0.5 levels converge and expects a decisive break through the spot ([2026 #90 · 11:58](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=718), [13:37](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=817), [2026 #36 · 5:47](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=347)).
- **Obsidian, two opposing wicks (August 2026).** The zone between the CEs of an upper wick and a lower wick, the later wick higher when you are bearish.
- **Opposing premium and discount CEs (September 2024).** An upper wick CE as the sell and a lower wick CE as the target.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that these levels sit inside candles, so most traders cannot see them, but the algorithm refers back to them. Price that keeps tapping inside an Obsidian zone is building a false consolidation that bull-flag traders will buy, and it will deny them ([2026 #29 · 33:27](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=2007)). Of the opposing-CE trade he said it does two things at once: takes the short-term high (liquidity) and goes to a price the algorithms refer back to ([2024 #26 · 44:36](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2676)).

### How to trade it

**The Obsidian, bearish.**

1. Start with a bearish narrative from the daily chart. Without the right direction, he said, "this isn't going to work in your hands".
2. Find two opposing wicks, one above a candle and one below another. The later wick must be higher.
3. Mark the CE of each wick and extend both lines right.

## Shadows: where two imbalances overlap

### What it is

This is not about wicks, despite the lecture title ("Shadows Validating PD Arrays"). In 2026 ICT used "shadow" for the region where one PD array overlaps another imbalance. The overlap is a PD array in its own right, named because it casts a shadow over the other imbalance ([2026 #71 · 36:12](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2172)). His example: on the daily NQ chart a bearish FVG (pink) and a suspension block (orange, Chapter 9) overlapped in salmon. That region is the shadow, and it can be graded with quadrants, octants or finer. Other methods, he said, neither look at the overlap nor grade it ([2026 #71 · 34:32](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2072)).

### How to use it

A newer PD array has to touch the shadow (its high, its low or anywhere between) to count as anchored. That Wednesday, a bullish FVG formed at the low of the overlap, and the next FVG sat exactly on an octant line, which made it valid ([2026 #71 · 36:59](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2219), [39:30](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2370)). The same lecture then taught the wick-CE grading and entry covered above. The notes show the shadow validating FVGs and do not say it validates a wick.

The idea appeared earlier with opening range gaps. Where Wednesday's RTH opening range gap overlapped Tuesday's, the overlap was a shadow, and a down-close candle anchored in it respected the area repeatedly. He called two gaps bleeding together a very strong support or resistance, far more precise than classic lines ([2026 #17 · 54:32](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3272), [55:20](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3320), [58:33](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=3513)). Chapter 11 teaches those gaps.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading "shadow" as the old name for a wick.** Here it means an overlap of imbalances.
- **Treating any two boxes as a shadow.** It is an overlap of higher-time-frame arrays, and the new array must touch its graded levels.

## Answers

1. A discount wick, because price is above it. The CE is 18,220. For a long, bodies stay above it, in the upper half (18,220 to 18,240). Reread "Premium wicks and discount wicks".
2. Yes. The wick pierced the CE (18,214 is below 18,220) but the body (18,245 to 18,250) stayed above it. Beyond a gap's edge, a poke like this with bodies on the right side is a mohawk. Reread "Reading bodies against the wick CE" and "The mohawk".
3. It becomes a premium wick. For a short, bodies stay below the CE (18,220) and the wick may poke through. A body at or above it means you are probably wrong. Reread "Premium wicks and discount wicks".
4. It must show immediate willingness to go back above, preferably closing above, so the wick is retaken by that candle or the very next. If not, tighten risk or exit. Reread "Reading bodies against the wick CE".
5. Above the wick, not just above the FVG. The wick takes precedence and price tends to work inside it first. Reread "Wicks beside other arrays".
6. Anchor on the body high of the SIBI candle, because the wick is a gap that is allowed to do the damage. He warned that anchoring wrongly means the outline will not work. Reread "Bodies versus wicks".
7. No. The stated rule is that the later wick must be higher than the first for a bearish Obsidian, and here the later wick is lower. You also need a bearish narrative from the daily chart. Reread "Grey pools, the Obsidian and opposing CEs".
8. A shadow is the overlap of two imbalances, not a candle's wick. A new PD array has to touch it, at its high, its low or between, and sit on a graded level such as an octant. Reread "Shadows".
9. The geometry transfers, since it needs only open, high, low and close: grading, premium or discount by position, the body rules, and the entry and stop logic. The NQ prices and handle counts do not, and the notes hold no gold-specific wick numbers. Reread "Trading the wick".
