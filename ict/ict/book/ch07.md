An inversion FVG (iFVG) is a fair value gap that has failed at its first job and is then traded for the opposite one: a bullish gap that price closes beneath becomes resistance, and a bearish gap that price closes above becomes support. This chapter sets out ICT's exact conditions for that flip (closes, bodies, wicks), which gaps invert best, how a reclaimed FVG differs and how his labels drifted, the balanced price range (BPR) in each of its uses, and the trade models built on them. After it you should be able to say whether a gap on your chart has really inverted, how clean its retest is, and when to stand aside.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch07_d1 | caption: a bullish gap in an up-leg that ran the buy side, closed through by a body, then retested from below (illustrative prices) | Notice that the flip happens on the body that closes under the gap's low, and that the retest wick enters the upper half while the bodies stay in the lower half.]]

- An iFVG is the same three-candle gap used in reverse. A wick through the gap does not invert it. A candle must close beyond it ([2026 #80 · 3:56](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=236)).
- On the retest, bodies stay in the half of the gap nearest to where price is coming from. Wicks may enter the far half. A body in the far half is a warning, and a close over the whole gap kills the idea.
- The best inversions have a reason: the gap sits in the leg that ran a stop pool, or it is the first presented FVG, or it sits on a graded level, and time and narrative agree.
- The entry is rarely the first tap. ICT sells the lower half or the CE with a stop above the gap, or waits for the next FVG on a lower timeframe. If no new FVG forms, there is no trade.
- A reclaimed FVG is a gap that price closed through and then recovered, so it returns to its first job. ICT's use of the word wobbles, and this chapter lays out each version.
- A balanced price range is a span that price has crossed in both directions. As a boundary it should hold. As a state it is neutral. If it gives way, ICT reads manual intervention and steps aside.

## Inversion FVGs

### What it is
An ordinary FVG is the gap between candle 1 and candle 3 that candle 2's body ran through (Chapter 5). A gap left by up-close candles is a BISI (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency), the bullish FVG. A gap left by down-close candles is a SIBI, the bearish one. The CE (consequent encroachment) is the gap's midpoint (Chapter 6).

ICT gives every gap a first job. In his 2026 wording, the first utilization of a BISI is buy-side delivery: price above it drops in, and it should send price higher ([2026 #41 · 17:07](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=1027)). The first utilization of a SIBI is sell-side delivery, a premium array that should send price lower. The inversion FVG is the second utilization, the same gap read with reversed logic ([2026 #23 · 49:11](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2951), [2026 #47 · 9:37](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=577)).

- **Bearish iFVG:** a BISI that price closes below. The gap now caps rallies, and shorts are taken on the return into it.
- **Bullish iFVG:** a SIBI that price closes above. The gap now supports pullbacks, and longs are taken on the return into it ([2026 #74 · 3:32](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=212)).

Four points to hold on to:

- The iFVG is not a new gap. It is the same three candles with a new role. Nothing about the candles changes.
- Every PD array can invert. In August 2024 ICT said all of them have an inversion aspect, and that this technically doubles his count of 81 arrays ([2024 #2 · 1:13:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4419), [2024 #3 · 10:59](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=659), [2024 #20 · 1:36:56](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5816)).
- Not every FVG becomes one. He says so directly ([2024 #8 · 2:04:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7467)), so do not pre-label every gap as a future iFVG.
- It is rule-based, not a zone. He says an iFVG is measured from the open and close of specific candles ([2026 #114 · 4:46](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=286)), that such gaps do not go stale ([2026 #96 · 25:45](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=1545)), and that the precision is why he rates it above supply and demand ([2026 #117 · 15:15](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=915)).

On his charts, an orange gap means "I expect this to fail" or "its role has reversed" ([2024 #7 · 59:27](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=3567), [2024 #28 · 1:07:01](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4021)). In 2026 he colours a gap green once it has turned bullish, as a mental aid he admits may be wrong ([2026 #74 · 3:32](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=212)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is about information and trapped orders, not about magic levels.

1. **A failed gap is information.** In 2024 he said he wants to see PD arrays fail, because it confirms the directional expectation is still on ([2024 #7 · 1:20:27](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=4827)). A stop-out on an FVG trade usually means an iFVG is forming. He calls the loss a premium paid for information, and says to trade the other way with half the size ([2024 #28 · 52:18](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3138)). In 2026 he adds that a failed FVG gives him an out in every scenario, which critics mock ([2026 #23 · 49:11](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2951)).
2. **Footing.** A PD array above price is first a handhold. Once price is above it, it becomes a foothold. A SIBI that price has overtaken is the foothold for the next push higher ([2024 #18 · 16:24](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=984), [2026 #137 · 30:56](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1856)).
3. **Trapped orders.** In a bearish iFVG, every upward poke into the gap is smart money selling to public buyers who want new highs. ICT calls it an accumulation of shorts ([2026 #2 · 31:31](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1891)). For a bullish iFVG, once a candle closes above, the sell stops just run sit right behind price. He says price should then reach for the liquidity above rather than go back and forth ([2026 #21 · 18:02](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1082)).
4. **Bodies are the order flow.** He reads each candle's open, high, low and close, which everyone can see, and not volume or order counts ([2026 #2 · 21:52](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1312)). He calls bodies the real volume, and a wick is just an inefficiency being delivered to ([2026 #32 · 1:10:17](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4217)).
5. **The gap does not change, the context does.** A BISI looks like a buy. Whether it is one depends on narrative and order flow. In his words, a gap that would have been bullish with bullish flow must become an inversion when the flow is bearish ([2026 #120 · 35:54](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=2154)). A beginner who buys it anyway gets run over ([2024 #2 · 1:16:54](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4614)). Critics who see an FVG fail and say the concept does not work are ignoring this ([2026 #30 · 23:45](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1425)).

### How to spot it
The checklist below is the bearish case. Mirror it for a bullish iFVG.

1. **Mark a gap that had a first job.** Take a bullish gap (BISI), from candle 1's high to candle 3's low, and mark its CE. ICT's 2026 criteria start here: the original gap was used in its first utilization, with bodies respecting it ([2026 #95 · 32:05](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1925)).
2. **Wait for a close through it.** A candle must close below the gap. A wick below it does not count ([2024 #10 · 2:24:44](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8684)).
3. **Wait for a new candle to come back.** The candle that closes through cannot also be the retest. A following candle comes back up and touches the gap: its low, its CE or its high ([2024 #10 · 1:17:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4622)).
4. **Grade the retest by the bodies.** Bodies stay in the lower half. Wicks may enter the upper half. The better the gap, the less it is touched.
5. **Check that price leaves quickly.** ICT wants price to enter in one or two candles and leave at once. Many candles inside is a weakness ([2026 #114 · 20:42](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1242)).
6. **Check the context.** Look for a stop run, a first-presented gap, a graded level, and a time that fits. The rest of this section covers each.

The diagram's four grades come from different lectures and are not one official scale. The "textbook" grade is the pass/fail test from April 2026. ### Which gap inverts best
ICT does not say every gap is equal. The notes point to seven things that raise a gap's quality.

- **The gap in the leg that ran the stop pool.** On Aug 6 2024, after 7:00 there was a smooth area of relative equal highs. Price ran it, and the BISI in the up-leg that ran them, inside the range that made the bearish breaker, then failed. He called this the highest-precision form ([2024 #2 · 1:14:27](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4467)). In 2026 he repeats the rule: the gap that matters is the one that formed before the equal highs were taken, and it inverts after the break lower ([2026 #41 · 22:46](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=1366)). For breakers, see Chapter 9.
- **The first gap in the displacement leg.** On Apr 20 2026 he marked the FVG that formed right before the displacement that took a short-term low, the first one in the leg. He said the 9:31 candle's gap was not the first-presented one and promised to explain why. The explanation is not in the notes ([2026 #96 · 31:20](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=1880)). If the displacing leg holds two FVGs, he moves the stop above the CE of the higher one ([2024 #38 · 2:41:47](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=9707)).
- **The first presented FVG after the open.** On the 1-minute chart, the first gap after 9:30, earliest 9:31. In a bearish bias he picks the up-close candle's gap. The first return reacts best, and a second return is weaker ([2024 #37 · 2:17:08](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8228), [2024 #37 · 2:18:47](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8327)). Chapter 8 covers this gap in full.
- **A gap anchored to a graded level.** ICT says the candle must lie on a gradient level between two graded pools, and that this is what validates the setup ([2026 #82 · 12:07](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=727)). Other anchors he used were the CE of the 7-9 range ([2026 #29 · 31:02](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1862)) and an eighth line of the opening range gap ([2026 #136 · 22:56](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1376)). An Asian-session iFVG was valid only because it was anchored to Tuesday's opening range cap ([2026 #4 · 0:09](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=9)).
- **A gap big enough to matter, ideally nested.** Tiny separations are not helpful ([2026 #96 · 25:45](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=1545)). Several gaps nested together make a very strong inversion area ([2026 #121 · 4:53](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=293)). A volume imbalance at the gap's edge gives a precise level ([2026 #137 · 30:56](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1856)).
- **A higher-timeframe gap with a lower-timeframe trigger.** A weekly iFVG was his draw for a Monday retracement ([2024 #25 · 4:34](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=274)). Daily iFVGs served as bias anchors on EURUSD and GBPUSD, held until price proved him wrong by trading above them ([2026 #115 · 8:54](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=534)). For an hourly first utilization, he drops to the 1-minute to watch the bodies reject the CE ([2026 #76 · 12:22](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=742)).
- **The left-side gap of a swing high.** On Jan 2 2026 he preferred the gap on the rising, buy-side-of-the-curve half of a swing high over the usual right-side gap. It is hyper-sensitive in his words. The cost is that you may never get filled ([2026 #140 · 6:23](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=383), [2026 #140 · 10:23](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=623)).

Time and narrative can veto a good-looking gap. On Jan 12 2026 the daily draw was above, at relative equal highs of 26,133.5. Asked whether he would short a pre-9:30 iFVG, he said no: the market was in a holding pattern until the 9:30 release ([2026 #137 · 23:49](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1429)). The 9:30 candle opened at the opening range low, dipped to the iFVG's low and ripped higher ([2026 #137 · 28:29](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1709)).


### How to trade it
ICT taught this on index futures (NQ, MNQ, ES) with 1-minute and 15-second charts. The concept itself he also applied to DXY, EURUSD, GBPUSD, crude oil, Bitcoin, gold and silver. The notes mention gold only twice, so the clock-based parts below are index-futures rules unless stated.

- **Retest entry.** Sell the return into the lower half or the CE of a bearish iFVG. The setup stays bearish while price does not go back above the midpoint, and the ideal path is a wick that stops just under the CE and leaves ([2024 #17 · 37:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2239)). In the Micro vs. Mini lecture he works the lower half or CE of the iFVG and aims at sell-side liquidity below ([2024 #31 · 15:30](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=930)).
- **Lower-timeframe trigger.** After price leaves the iFVG and displaces away, take the next FVG that forms on a 15-, 30- or 45-second chart, his institutional order flow entry drill (Chapter 18) ([2024 #17 · 50:14](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=3014)). One candle may touch back into the iFVG. If that touch candle overlaps so that no clean gap forms, wait for the next displacement. He will not enter without a fresh gap ([2024 #31 · 38:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2312), [2024 #31 · 43:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2612)).
- **Sitting in the gap is not the entry.** Price trading into the first gap and camping there is not the trigger. The trigger is the displacement away and the new FVG it leaves ([2024 #43 · 24:29](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1469)). No FVG, no trade ([2024 #43 · 36:57](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2217)). In 2026 he adds that many traders enter at the retracement and get whipsawed ([2026 #104 · 35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126)).
- **Early entry, when convicted.** In a strong market the retest may never come, so on Oct 16 2024 he entered inside the iFVG as price first touched it or at its CE. He took 6 contracts there and 4 more at the gap's other edge, rather than wait for a return that might never come and miss both the fill and the pyramid ([2024 #45 · 39:59](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2399), [2024 #45 · 45:43](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2743)). On Monday-morning NQ in 2026 he did the same from the low end of a bullish iFVG and added each time price kissed its high ([2026 #38 · 0:50](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=50)). Note that for longs the notes show entries across the gap, while the body rules apply to the retests that follow.
- **Close-proximity entry.** If you miss the tap, a market buy just above the wick high is equal to a buy stop at that candle's high. ICT used it on Sept 30 2026 after missing both the drop and the tap ([2026 #3 · 7:29](https://youtu.be/kRShZKZlvJ4?t=449)).
- **Adds.** Add on the bounce into the iFVG, not on the initial break ([2026 #36 · 2:32](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=152)). Add inside the wick and the lower quadrant of a bearish iFVG ([2026 #62 · 2:26](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=146)). Add on each retest that fails to reach the CE, but not when you are already close to your exits ([2026 #96 · 36:06](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=2166)). On volatile seek-and-destroy days he entered several iFVG zones in turn and pyramided as each confirmed ([2026 #22 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=99)).
- **Stop, short.** Above the gap's high with a tick of room ([2024 #19 · 1:04:08](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3848)), or above the high of the candle that traded completely through the iFVG ([2026 #69 · 4:17](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=257)). When the displacing leg holds two stacked FVGs, the stop goes above the CE of the higher gap. A wick into the upper gap is then normal, provided the body stays in the lower half ([2024 #38 · 2:43:23](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=9803)).
- **Stop, long.** Below the signal candle's low: if price trades back down there, he is wrong ([2026 #87 · 5:40](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=340)). After the first partial, roll it to just under the iFVG ([2026 #74 · 5:11](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=311)).
- **Targets.** The next low-hanging low or relative equal lows ([2024 #18 · 18:02](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=1082)). The 9:30 opening price first, then the lows ([2024 #9 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=733)). A minor sell-side at least 20 handles away on NQ ([2024 #31 · 43:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2612)). A nested NDOG/NWOG cluster (Chapter 11) ([2024 #17 · 37:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2239)). For longs in 2026, the event horizon first and then the 1-hour minor buy side ([2026 #74 · 3:32](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=212)).
- **Management.** Take partials at the CE of wicks and gaps and at each new low ([2024 #38 · 3:12:39](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=11559)). Once the low is taken he does not want price back at the iFVG, and if it returns the short is in jeopardy ([2026 #62 · 2:26](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=146)). When bodies stop re-entering the upper half, he lowers the stop above the highs ([2026 #71 · 0:01](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1)).
- **When it fails.** If price closes back through the iFVG, it has lost its character ([2026 #35 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=530)). A gap that was inverting against you can itself flip: ICT warned that the iFVG must not become an inversion snapping into his stop ([2026 #62 · 2:26](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=146)).
- **Filters.** In choppy high-resistance conditions, bearish FVGs often fail and invert, so do not force the bearish view ([2026 #52 · 9:32](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=572)). On Fridays, iFVGs are less reliable because Sunday-open gap risk overrides intraday moves ([2026 #114 · 20:42](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1242)). He framed the Oct 3 2024 scale-out as the gap between an experienced trader and a beginner, so do not copy the size ([2024 #41 · 8:24](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=504)).
- **Gold and CFDs.** The two gold mentions: in March 2026 a small volume imbalance on gold was touched back, then traded below and treated as an inversion before a sell-off ([2026 #113 · 16:01](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=961)). In a 2026 futures review he said he looks for a failed iFVG at highs on gold ([2026 #71 · 23:09](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=1389)). The one-tick and to-the-tick precision examples come from futures, where he notes everyone shares the same low ([2026 #21 · 18:49](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1129)). ICT did not say how this carries to CFD feeds. A CFD broker prints its own candles, so test any tick-level rule on your own broker before relying on it.

### Worked example
**Aug 23 2024, NQ 1-minute, the 9:30 session and afternoon.** ICT called the sequence live, and price moved over 200 handles. The notes give the levels by name, not by price, and the captions for this stretch are rough, so the order of touches is approximate ([2024 #16 · 22:08](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1328)).

1. The open raided the buy side and made relative equal highs. Price tagged the daily SIBI high from Jul 23, swept above the NDOG, and ran to the mean threshold of the daily bearish order block from Jul 22.
2. It then dropped without building candles at the CE of a small gap, so he expected another drive above the high. It ran just over the equal highs (a turtle soup, Chapter 3) and fell back through the order block ([2024 #16 · 23:47](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1427)).
3. Short zone: any price between the opening price and the order block's mean threshold. He said you can add anywhere inside it ([2024 #16 · 24:34](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1474)).

## Reclaimed FVGs

### What it is
A reclaimed FVG is a gap that lost its first job and then won it back. Price closes through it, which ends the first utilization. Later price closes back through it in the original direction, and the gap returns to its first utilization.

ICT showed it on Jul 15 2026. A bullish suspension block was invalidated by a close below. When price later closed above, it "picked up its first utilization" and he recoloured it dark green ([2026 #61 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=527)). On Aug 25 2026 he drew the same cycle for a bearish gap: a close above makes it an inversion, and a close back below returns it to bearish ([2026 #25 · 14:41](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=881)).

[[DIAGRAM ch07_d3 | caption: how a gap's role changes on a close and changes back on a close, for both origins | Notice that the same gap carries different names depending on the candle that made it: ICT calls the bullish row's last box a reclaimed bullish FVG.]]

### Reclaimed versus inversion
The cleanest statement is from Feb 28 2026 ([2026 #126 · 46:27](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2787)). An inversion FVG forms when price trades through a gap of the opposite character: a down-close candle's gap (SIBI) that price goes above, or an up-close candle's gap that price goes below. A reclaimed gap is a bullish BISI that price traded through to the downside, or to its CE, and then rides back above. It is not inverted. Its character changes but stays bullish. Had the same gap formed on a down-close candle, it would be an inversion.

So the name follows the candle that made the gap, not where price is now. ICT made the same correction about himself on Sept 30 2024. A gap that price goes back above and then drops into is a reclaimed bullish FVG, because it came from an up-close candle. The week before he had mislabelled one as an inversion, although what he expected price to do had been right ([2024 #37 · 2:19:34](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8374)).

The bearish mirror is the same. When price runs up through a SIBI and then closes back below it, bodies confirm the run was a stop hunt, and the gap becomes a reclaimed bearish FVG. Any retracement into it is a premium array to sell from. Bodies stay in the lower half, and wicks may poke through the upper half ([2026 #113 · 57:55](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3475), [2026 #113 · 59:28](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3568)).

### How to spot it
1. **A gap with a clear first job.** For the bullish case, a BISI that price respected from above.
2. **A close through it.** That ends the first job. The gap now works as a bearish iFVG.
3. **A close back over it.** Price must get above the gap's high. ICT says this usually comes with a sudden, abrupt market structure shift ([2024 #18 · 1:04:25](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3865)).
4. **A return with bodies in the right half.** Bodies respect the upper half. A wick may poke one or two ticks below, so watch whether the body gets back into the upper half while the candle forms ([2026 #126 · 47:13](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2833)).
5. **A condition check.** Reclaims are a low-resistance tool, as the next part explains.

### How to trade it
- **Trade with the reclaim.** Once reclaimed, the gap is no longer a reversal tool. It is continuation support (bullish) or resistance (bearish), and the bias flips from reversal to continuation ([2026 #116 · 18:18](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=1098)).
- **Low resistance versus high resistance.** In low resistance conditions he buys, scales and adds into a reclaimed gap, which the note puts at about 90% of the time. In high resistance he does not, and the odds are poor. The approach works in either if combined with narrative and time ([2026 #126 · 47:13](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2833), [2026 #126 · 48:00](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2880)). Chapter 16 covers the two conditions.
- **A 2024 remark pulls the other way.** In October 2024 he said a second touch of a PD array is typical of high-resistance days, and reclaimed arrays are unlikely in low resistance because price is gone in one move. A trailed stop gets hit when price goes back into the FVG ([2024 #49 · 1:56:56](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7016)). The notes do not reconcile the two. Follow the 2026 rule, which is the one he trades.
- **Stepping stone.** For his son he called a gap that turned to resistance and was then reclaimed a simple everyday setup, a stepping stone toward the minor buy side ([2024 #28 · 1:07:01](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4021)).
- **Entry and stop.** Buy the retest into the reclaimed gap, with a stop just under it ([2026 #74 · 5:11](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=311)). Do not trade the opposite way during the inversion phase before the close back through ([2026 #25 · 15:30](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=930)).
- **Reclaimed order blocks.** The same word is used for order blocks: a bullish block that is violated and then recovered, or a bearish block that price returns to and rejects at once ([2024 #28 · 1:40:43](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6043), [2024 #18 · 3:15:36](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11736)). Chapter 9 covers blocks.

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Using the word loosely.** In live commentary the label slips: the notes list "reclaimed bullish FVG" as an alias for a failed SIBI used as a bullish iFVG in two 2026 lectures ([2026 #87 · 16:22](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=982), [2026 #99 · 13:47](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=827)). ## Balanced price ranges

### What it is
ICT uses "balanced price range" for several related ideas. They share one test: has price been offered in both directions through the same span? Volume imbalances and other single-candle imbalances are in Chapter 6.

1. **Two-way delivery, the base idea.** An inefficiency has had one-sided delivery. A down move is sell-side delivery, and what the gap lacks is buy-side delivery ([2024 #3 · 22:10](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1330)). Trading back into the gap does not balance it. Price must trade there and leave, and then it is a BPR ([2024 #3 · 1:57:39](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=7059)). Filling a one-sided gap delivers it efficiently, but that is not a BPR ([2024 #42 · 56:59](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3419)). His picture is a paint roller: once an area has been rolled from both directions you need not go deep into it again, and price returns only to the edge ([2024 #4 · 1:57:50](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7070), [2024 #26 · 19:40](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=1180)).
2. **Overlapping opposing gaps.** A bearish and a bullish gap that share the same price space ([2024 #28 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1440), [2024 #28 · 1:18:14](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4694)). In September 2024 he drew it as a blue box ([2024 #26 · 15:18](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=918)). In 2026 it is a SIBI beside a BISI ([2026 #101 · 46:10](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2770)). On Aug 7 2026 he took the highest high and lowest low of both gaps as the larger BPR ([2026 #39 · 6:21](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=381)). The overlap-only reading and the combined-span reading are not reconciled in the notes. A practical way to hold both, which is my reading and not ICT's, is to treat the overlap as the core and the combined span as the outer limits.
3. **A wick-and-body overlap inside a gap.** On Aug 14 2024 he said that when one candle's wick overlaps the next candle's body inside a gap, the overlap is a BPR and is the real range of the inefficiency. You encapsulate it rather than draw the plain three-candle FVG. He called it a PD array students had never learned. Price then reacts at that boundary instead of filling the whole FVG ([2024 #8 · 2:02:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7373)). This is not a volume imbalance, which is a separation between two bodies.
4. **A range crossed both ways.** On Aug 23 2024 he mapped a range with a small SIBI inside. Price went up into it, broke lower, came back up over the whole prior run, broke lower again, returned once more, and only then left below the low. Going up, down and leaving is not enough ([2024 #16 · 14:47](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=887)). On Sept 19 he called the area between an initial high and the stop-run high a BPR because it held real orders, breakout buyers that smart money sold to ([2024 #31 · 53:21](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3201)). In April 2026 he described it as a range that bounces between two tested levels and works as a defensive barrier ([2026 #94 · 18:59](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1139)).
5. **A delivered volume imbalance.** An up candle goes through the imbalance, later a down candle lays a body over it, and both sides have delivered. ICT then calls it a small BPR ([2024 #46 · 35:15](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2115)). In June 2026 he added that such a gap can then take on inversion character if price trades above it, and warned this version is more complicated and more likely to fail ([2026 #72 · 30:58](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1858), [2026 #72 · 43:04](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2584)).

[[DIAGRAM ch07_d4 | caption: a BPR from overlapping opposing gaps (left) and a range crossed both ways (right), illustrative | Notice that the left BPR is only the shared price, while the right one is a range price has crossed twice in each direction before it leaves.]]

### Why it works
- **Both sides have already been served.** A balanced span has finished its business, so price comes back to its edge and needs something significant to get through. ICT expects price to respect the far edge like a brick wall ([2024 #11 · 3:29:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12578)).
- **High resistance liquidity.** The buy-side stops above a BPR's high are high resistance liquidity. Reaching them takes something very significant, in his example manual intervention, as around Fed, payroll, CPI and PPI ([2024 #16 · 16:28](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=988)).
- **When it gives way, step aside.** A BPR that fails to hold means manual intervention, with the market maker's hand in the mix. His advice is to stand aside, and to trade after the news driver, not before ([2024 #11 · 3:34:02](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12842)).
- **Time and speed.** The time an inefficiency took to build is the time to expect for its return, and a sudden move invites a fast return of large candles. If price sputters inside the gap, that is high resistance ([2024 #42 · 1:00:19](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3619), [2024 #42 · 1:01:57](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3717)). On a 60-minute or 4-hour BPR, speed was his tell: a fill with speed means the character has changed ([2024 #17 · 1:11:14](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=4274)).

### How to spot it
1. **Overlapping gaps.** Find a SIBI and a BISI that share a price span, then mark the overlap and the combined highs and lows.
2. **Range crossed both ways.** Map a range with a small imbalance, confirm price traded both directions inside it, confirm it left with displacement, and watch whether the range's far edge holds ([2024 #16 · 18:03](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1083)).
3. **Volume imbalance.** Find the gap between two bodies, check whether bodies have been laid over it from both sides, and treat it as a BPR only once both have delivered and price has left. A wick-only imbalance, where one candle crosses and returns, behaves like a breakaway gap and is not delivered ([2024 #46 · 41:36](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2496)).
4. **Size check.** He does not hang much on tiny, insignificant imbalances ([2024 #46 · 37:34](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2254)).

### How to trade it
ICT's two usages point in different directions, so decide which one you are applying.

- **As a boundary.** If you are long, do not expect price to trade below the BPR low. Price going into it and not past it is the tracking you want ([2024 #11 · 3:36:25](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12985)). A BPR top that price cannot get back above is weakness, and a short with the BPR as premium array fits ([2024 #26 · 15:18](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=918)). After both sides have delivered and price leaves, the BPR acts as a discount array on a return from above, and he said a long can roll its stop to just below the imbalance ([2024 #46 · 40:49](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2449)).
- **As a defense.** Keep stops just outside the BPR. If it breaks, that is information that smart money has shifted. Take the new PD array forming the other way ([2026 #94 · 18:59](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1139)).
- **As a neutral state.** On Mar 27 2026 a gap traded above, below and above again became a BPR. He said the gap was no longer a reliable bias tool. Do not trade off the gap alone, and wait for a decisive break with confluence such as a fresh lower-timeframe FVG, a macro time or a liquidity level ([2026 #114 · 54:50](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=3290)). On Mar 8 2026 he said price inside a BPR tends to use the inefficiencies inside it, and a close outside can open new draws. It is a context filter, not a signal ([2026 #125 · 40:26](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2426)).
- **With the iFVG.** In the same Mar 27 lecture, an iFVG that stops being valid may act as a BPR instead ([2026 #114 · 20:42](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1242)).

### Worked example
**Aug 23 2024, NQ 1-minute, before the open.** The BPR range sat above, and price displaced below its low. ICT was not interested in longs, because of the factors on the daily chart. He extended the BPR's high and low through 9:30 ([2024 #16 · 13:11](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=791)). Returning to the BPR low and reaching into it was fine by his logic, but reaching its high would signal manual intervention, and he would stop chasing and be done for the day ([2024 #16 · 16:28](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=988)).

**Oct 17 2024, NQ 1-minute/5-minute, the 8:30-9:00 range.** A bullish body was buried in one volume imbalance (buy-side delivery). Later a down-close candle laid over it (sell-side delivery). He called it efficiently delivered and a small BPR. Price then went back above it, and he wanted no dip back below. A second imbalance had only wicks, as a single candle went down through it and back up, so he treated it like a breakaway gap and not as delivered ([2024 #46 · 35:15](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2115), [2024 #46 · 41:36](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=2496)).

**Aug 19 2024, NQ 15-second.** One 15-second candle passed down through the previous candle's range and one passed back up, so the region was offered both ways. Price dropped from a 1-minute inefficiency into the BPR, did not take out its low, and rallied. Later retracements left it intact. He added that a 15-second BPR can look like a plain wick on the 1-minute chart ([2024 #11 · 3:29:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12578)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Up, down, leave.** One round trip is not a BPR. In the range usage he wanted back-and-forth delivery first ([2024 #16 · 14:47](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=887)).
- **Calling a filled gap a BPR.** A single-sided inefficiency that price has traded back over is efficiently delivered, but not balanced ([2024 #42 · 56:59](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3419)).
- **Calling a BPR an entry.** In 2026 he treats it as context and defense. The entry comes from an FVG or iFVG with a time and a draw.

| Concept | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FVG | One-sided delivery through three candles | A gap awaiting its other side |
| Efficiently delivered | Price has traded back over a one-sided gap | Done its job, not balanced |
| BPR | Both sides delivered and price left | Boundary or neutral state |
| Volume imbalance | A separation between two bodies | Becomes a small BPR when both bodies have been laid over it |

### How the teaching changed
- **2022 and early 2024: the overlap.** The notes say the 2022 definition was the overlap of a SIBI and a BISI, and the same notes say the Aug 14 2024 wick-and-body version differs from it ([2024 #8 · 2:30:01](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9001)). Sept 2024 returns to opposing gaps sharing a space.
- **Aug to Oct 2024: more uses.** On the 15-second chart a BPR is a brick wall ([2024 #11 · 3:29:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12578)). In the 1-minute range version it is a place price must cross both ways and a guard against manual intervention. In October the volume imbalance conversion appears.
- **2026: context and defense.** Mar 8 and Mar 27 make it a neutral state, and Apr 21 makes it a defense. Aug 7 gives the combined-span version and calls it very reliable once identified, with price using it repeatedly as a pivot ([2026 #39 · 10:21](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=621)).
- **A conflict in role.** The reliability language (2024, Aug 2026) and the neutral-state language (Mar 2026) pull apart: one says expect the edge to hold, the other says do not rely on the gap. The notes do not resolve it. A workable split is that the edge is the thing to respect, and the gap's directional message is used up.

## Trade models built on these

The models themselves belong to Chapter 17 and the entry drills to Chapter 18. This section shows where the iFVG, the reclaimed FVG and the BPR slot into them, using the sequences the notes spell out.

### Model 1: the stop run, then the iFVG
1. Time and narrative point to a smooth pool, such as relative equal highs after 7:00. Price runs it ([2024 #2 · 1:14:27](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4467)).
2. The gap in the leg that ran the pool fails. A body closes through it. The buy side is swept ([2024 #16 · 27:46](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1666)).
3. The day is now in a sell model: consolidation, a rally as manipulation, then a break lower. That break is the low-risk sell.
4. Retest with bodies in the lower half and the upper half open. Target the sell side and any nested draw.

The same logic runs on breakers (Chapter 9). When there is an FVG inside the breaker's leg, ICT highlights it as a potential iFVG and extends it to the right as a PD array price can draw up to ([2024 #4 · 32:02](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1922)). His January 2026 pre-news "A+" long used the mirror image. Lows were taken, price rallied back above the lowest part of a bullish breaker, then dropped into an iFVG inside that breaker and held, and ran to the minor buy-side and the low of the inefficiency above. He called the run from the iFVG a model in itself ([2026 #136 · 22:56](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1376)).

### Model 2: first presented FVG, inversion, next FVG
This is the model he gave his student Caleb. It uses the first FVG after the 9:30 open on the 1-minute chart and a 15-second trigger.

[[DIAGRAM ch07_d5 | caption: first presented FVG, then inversion, then a new FVG as the trigger (bearish case) | Notice that the gap that inverted is never the entry: the entry waits for a fresh FVG, and without one the answer is no trade.]]

1. Mark the first presented FVG ([2024 #43 · 18:25](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1105)). With a bearish bias, use the up-close candle's gap ([2024 #37 · 2:17:08](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8228)).
2. Price trades through it and away. A body closes beyond. If it only wicks, it has not inverted and his earlier mode stays ([2026 #18 · 45:39](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2739)).
3. Price returns. The return should reach the CE to the tick, hold the lower half, and leave. The first return reacts, and a second return inside the gap is weaker ([2024 #37 · 2:18:47](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8327)).
4. Price displaces away and leaves a fresh FVG on the 15-second chart. That gap is the entry. Without it, no trade ([2024 #43 · 36:57](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2217)).
5. If price instead rolls over the top and fills the upper half, he stands still: no bias, no touch, no trade ([2024 #9 · 43:53](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2633)).

The bullish mirror: price drops into the first presented FVG, rallies above it, and returns. The ideal return dives into the lower quadrant, leaves the lowest part untouched, and keeps bodies in the upper half. ICT called that the signature that the algorithm will send price higher ([2024 #35 · 46:46](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2806)). Target the mid gap or the equal highs. On Oct 7 2024 no 15-second FVG formed, so he did not trade ([2024 #43 · 36:57](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2217)). In low resistance conditions this delivers precisely, but in high resistance the sequence is unlikely and he does not force it ([2024 #43 · 32:45](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=1965)).

A 2026 version at the 10:00 Silver Bullet (Chapter 17): the first presented FVG at 10:00 was a SIBI. Price tried to sell off from it, took the swing low and the bodies, and then closed back above it. It became a bullish iFVG, and price rallied toward the SIBI's low ([2026 #26 · 13:00](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=780)).

### Model 3: the small-timeframe Model 2022
ICT says the iFVG is the last major upside displacement before price hits a premium array and folds over. The first FVG after the rotation is then the entry zone. In his PM example, price used the iFVG's CE, sold off, used another FVG, consolidated in a bearish pennant, and broke lower into the close. He stressed that nothing was new, only the old material presented to help build a narrative ([2026 #141 · 42:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2544)).

### Model 4: buy side, sell side, then the rally
The strongest version of the beginner's model: a short-term high is taken, then a short-term low, then price rallies using an FVG, which can be an iFVG. The entry was slightly above the CE of a wick and the iFVG. The target was the short-term buy side, with a 0.25 extension of the old high in the notes (caption unclear), and the exit allowed for the spread because price does not always reach the target ([2024 #33 · 16:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=997)). ### Model 5: the failed FVG, traded back
When an FVG trade stops out, you may be watching an iFVG form. Trade the other way with half the size you lost on, and treat the loss as paying for a better view of the next run ([2024 #28 · 52:18](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3138)). ### Model 6: nested gaps at a sell-side low
On Jun 8 2026 price took relative equal lows. A small bullish FVG and a bullish iFVG sat nested at that low, which he called two qualified areas giving discount sensitivity. He bought 20 contracts there ([2026 #77 · 2:27](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=147), [2026 #77 · 3:15](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=195)). After entry he wanted price to run through the high without touching the orange area again, and he held while bodies stayed above the previous wick's low ([2026 #77 · 4:49](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=289)). ### Model 7: a higher-timeframe iFVG as the draw
A weekly iFVG above price was his objective for a Monday retracement, and he wanted price to tag it before selling ([2024 #25 · 4:34](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=274)). A daily iFVG held price after a fast drop, and a 1-minute CE refinement did the execution ([2026 #40 · 5:01](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=301)).
