ICT does not start a day by looking for an entry. He first decides where price is probably being drawn (the bias), then how that move will be used on traders (the narrative), and he cross-checks both against the other indices and the dollar. This chapter shows how he builds that picture from the higher timeframe down, how SMT divergence and decoupling work as filters, and how DXY frames currencies and metals. After reading you can write a short if-then plan for the session and know which chart, and which comparison, answers each question.

## Narrative versus bias

### What it is

Bias is a direction. Narrative is the reason and the mechanism. ICT's shortest form is two questions asked before you sit down at the charts. Is price likely to go up, for buy stops (buy-side liquidity, BSL) or to rebalance an imbalance (a fair value gap, FVG) above? Or down, for sell stops (sell-side liquidity, SSL) or an imbalance below? ([2022 Ep 12 · 5:43](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=343)). Liquidity is Chapter 3 and FVGs are Chapter 5.

He claims the market prints candles on only two principles, liquidity and imbalance: "Imbalance, rebalance, run for liquidity. That's it." ([2022 Ep 12 · 1:11:48](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=4308)). By 2024 the narrative also has a human side. You need a view of how a sudden move or a shift in sentiment entices traders to buy or chase, unseats them, and lets deeper pockets take their seats ([2024 #37 · 1:06:58](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4018)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that an algorithm, the *price engine*, delivers price. It offers price and buyers and sellers agree to it. It only needs to know the time and where price should go, not how many contracts trade, and it cycles through timeframes so inefficiencies and liquidity get used ([2024 #12 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1293), [2024 #12 · 31:57](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1917)). In 2024 he adds that only two things happen algorithmically: price goes to liquidity or reprices an inefficiency ([2024 #37 · 1:10:59](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4259)).

Two conclusions follow, both his claims. Retail patterns (harmonics, Elliott waves, supply and demand) do not drive price. He asks how the market could pick between conflicting disciplines on a given day ([2022 Ep 12 · 6:33](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=393)). And a pattern alone is not enough: seeing a low taken and expecting higher prices needs context for why the market should do it. Students who come from "SMC" without a narrative end up in analysis paralysis or false confidence ([2022 Ep 7 · 14:38](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=878)).

### How to build one

1. Answer the two questions: is the draw above (BSL or an imbalance) or below (SSL or an imbalance)?
2. Ask who is in the trade now and who would regret being long (or short) if price moved to the old low (or high) ([2024 #37 · 2:22:46](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8566)).
3. Frame the best case for both sides. His example: after a month-plus rally, a drop from a daily FVG to the sell-side under relative equal lows is not picking a top. It lets smart money cover shorts and accumulate longs, which is constructive for the higher-timeframe bullishness ([2024 #37 · 1:08:34](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4114)).
4. On each timeframe, ask how traders there would read the chart and where their stops sit ([2024 #27 · 30:34](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1834)).
5. Write the contingency. If you are wrong, price may take the buy-side first, entice buyers, then break down. Wait for the first FVG to form and break ([2024 #37 · 1:27:06](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5226)).
6. Put the heaviest size on the trade that fits the narrative and less on the counter-trend side ([2024 #37 · 1:08:34](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4114)).

### Worked example: who gets hurt on the way to the high

The mentorship's NQ example uses 12,553.25 as the draw (the notes give no date). In Episode 29 the bias is bullish, with NQ drawing to relative equal highs at that price. ES had a daily swing low but no equal highs, and the bias was still warranted ([2022 Ep 29 · 0:05](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=5)). Episode 30 names the people on the wrong side. The run up triggers buy stops and breakout and bull-flag buyers, who are then taken into drawdown and stopped out below the relative equal lows. Morning longs trailing stops under the lunch lows are taken out too, so they miss the real move. Shorts from around 12,530 feel safe and are then squeezed. The FVG overhead looks like resistance but is not ([2022 Ep 30 · 2:31](https://youtu.be/CL94EnGqB4Q?t=151), [2022 Ep 30 · 4:08](https://youtu.be/CL94EnGqB4Q?t=248)).

### Narrative as a contrarian filter (2026)

In 2026 ICT adds that retail is fed headlines that prime it to expect a direction, and smart money often trades the other way to clean out stops. When US-Iran talks suggested calmer tensions, retail was primed to buy and he shorted because the chart (a gap, daily low targets) pointed down ([2026 #6 · 24:30](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1470)). On an employment-data Thursday, strong numbers primed bullishness, but he sold toward a new-week-gap low he had already named ([2026 #16 · 24:18](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1458)). It is a filter, not a rule. Chart structure stays primary, and he says headlines cannot be used consistently ([2026 #6 · 22:53](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=1373)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Pattern without context.** A swept low plus "it should go up" is a pattern. A narrative explains why and who is trapped.
- **Narrative as a story you defend.** He keeps a contingency and says not to impose your will on the market.
- **Cast of candles.** In 2026 he calls some candles *leading* (they carry the story at the right time, such as the SIBI-forming candle in the 9:50 to 10:10 macro) and others *extras* (a tiny FVG inside a consolidation before the low breaks). Ask whether a gap is on the path to the draw ([2026 #141 · 22:25](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1345)). He calls it an analogy, not a formal concept.

## Daily bias, in ICT's definition

### What it is

Daily bias is a judgement, from the daily chart, of whether the next daily candle is likelier bullish or bearish. The question behind it is where price will draw next: up to an old high or down to an old low. You keep the bias until the target is reached ([2022 Ep 7 · 7:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=445), [2022 Ep 7 · 3:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=205)).

A bearish bias means you expect down-close candles and hunt intraday shorts. You can still take a long with far less leverage. It does not mean every day closes down. In his NQ example the bearish leg from the high to the old low had only two up-close daily candles. When the algorithm is expanding directionally, some days still close up, and you may lose trying to short them ([2022 Ep 7 · 5:47](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=347)).

He is blunt about the limits. People asking about daily bias usually want the exact high and low of every day with no losses, and he calls that impossible. Bias is a quick frame for the day, and some days you adjust. In the same lecture he admits that even he sometimes has no clear read and demands more price action from the 9:30 open ([2022 Ep 7 · 3:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=205)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is hierarchy. The daily is the parent, with the bulk of the volume and order flow, and banks work off it. Every lower-timeframe swing is subordinate ([2022 Ep 12 · 10:36](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=636)). Institutional order flow is rooted in higher-timeframe bias, and short counter-bias moves are natural retracements ([2022 Ep 19 · 16:50](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1010), [2022 Ep 19 · 25:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1529)).

The draw is the draw whether or not price has reached it. In his bullish EURJPY example the old high above is a likely draw whether it trades there or not ([2022 Ep 8 · 2:32](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=152)). A daily order block at 4501.25/4504 did not need to be hit for him to know it was the draw ([2022 Ep 16 · 14:32](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=872)).

### How to spot it

[[DIAGRAM ch15_d2 | caption: A bearish daily bias in sequence (illustrative prices, schematic candles) | Only two of the six candles in the sell-off close up, so a bearish bias is not a call for every day.]]

1. Find the liquidity just run. In his bearish NQ example price ran BSL above relative equal highs on the daily ([2022 Ep 7 · 0:09](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=9)).
2. Look for the daily market structure shift (MSS, Chapter 2): price breaks a swing low with displacement.
3. Expect a retracement up into a daily FVG, then delivery to the old low.
4. Name that old low as the draw and keep the bias until it trades.
5. For a bullish bias, mirror it. In the EURJPY daily, relative equal lows were raided (SSL taken) and price rallied. Two structure breaks followed: a first swing high taken, a drop back, then a second swing high taken. Price then traded down into an FVG in the up-leg and was sent higher toward the old high ([2022 Ep 8 · 1:45](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=105), [2022 Ep 8 · 2:32](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=152)).
6. Note what would prove you wrong. The high that forms at the daily FVG on the 1h is a long-term high that should not break. If it breaks, the daily analysis is probably wrong ([2022 Ep 12 · 10:36](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=636)).

### How to trade it

- **Size follows the bias.** Use maximum non-overleveraged size with the bias and go smaller against it. His example account allows at most 10 contracts. He risks a fixed percentage, prefers 3.5% and says his maximum is 4.5%. For students he says under 1%, about 0.5% or 0.25%, because heavy risk behind a move you do not understand breeds fear of executing ([2022 Ep 7 · 4:11](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=251), [2022 Ep 7 · 4:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=298), [2022 Ep 7 · 9:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=590)). Risk is Chapter 20.
- **Even the right side can be too big.** In the August 19, 2024 lecture, after more than two weeks with no down daily close, he said he would trade 1, 3 or 4 contracts, not 10, 15 or 20, because most of the expected daily move was done ([2024 #12 · 46:18](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2778)).
- **Commit to one direction.** Take shorts when bearish and filter out the other side unless clearly proven wrong. Flipping long and short draws the account down. Know in advance what you are looking for so you do not chase impulsive swings ([2022 Ep 18 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=672)).
- **Stay with it until the draw is reached.** If each day is bullish with perhaps one exception, expect the bias to hold until the high is taken. Then a day or two of retracement into the FVG the rally created is logical, before price tests and rallies again ([2022 Ep 37 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=48), [2022 Ep 37 · 1:35](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=95)).
- **Do not pick tops and bottoms.** He does not mind missing the long-term high or low ([2022 Ep 19 · 18:26](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1106)).
- **A dip against the bias is a retracement.** With a bullish daily and intact structure, an intraday dip is a continuation entry, not a reversal short ([2026 #97 · 11:45](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=705)).
- **If the high-timeframe level breaks, the analysis is wrong.** Demand more information and sit on your hands, or take the stop as a cost of business, not as a flawed model or a failed trader ([2022 Ep 12 · 11:25](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=685)).

### Worked examples

**EURJPY daily, ahead of 10 February 2022 (bullish).** ICT expected the 10 February candle to be bullish. Even a failure swing short of the old high is tradeable if there is enough range from the open to the high, like aiming at the post in horseshoes even when you miss. He used the Forex.com feed on TradingView and avoided other feeds ([2022 Ep 8 · 3:20](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=200)).

**NQ, April 2026 (bullish, bias two weeks old).** In the lecture dated April 18, 2026 he traded the one-minute chart on a daily bias set about two weeks earlier. The daily draw was the all-time high at 26,859. The session opened with a dip to the consequent encroachment (CE, the 50% point) of Friday's RTH opening range gap (Chapter 11) at 26,574.75. He called it a pullback and a draw on liquidity, not a reversal, and says he was never bearish on the intraday ([2026 #97 · 1:29](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=89)).

**NQ, January 12, 2026 (bullish).** Daily relative equal highs at 26,133.50, flagged about a week earlier, were the draw. He stayed bullish on the daily, framed most setups that way, and said the level might be reached that day or that week, possibly with CPI or PPI running it ([2026 #138 · 1:11:15](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4275)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Calling it trend following.** He says he is not teaching that the trend is your friend. His frame is premium to discount and back within market structure ([2022 Ep 19 · 25:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1529)).
- **Mistaking a retail trend change for a reversal.** A failed higher high followed by a break of the prior swing low looks like a trend change to retail. With a bullish higher timeframe it is often a deep discount buy ([2022 Ep 12 · 20:08](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1208)).
- **Counter-trend as a new student.** He warns you will lose money swinging between buys and sells. Naturally contrarian students may pick spots where retail sees a top, but new students should first learn to trust the higher-timeframe bias ([2022 Ep 19 · 26:16](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1576)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the daily bias was something to find each day (Episodes 7, 8, 16, 18). By Episode 40 he called that unrealistic and moved to the weekly expansion. In 2024 he says beginners need one bias and a named model with a draw, while the advanced stage is trading price by time of day and what it is reaching for with no fixed bias, which he says takes a decade or more ([2024 #13 · 57:22](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3442)).

## Weekly bias: the weekly expansion

### What it is

ICT presents this as the simplification of all his earlier daily-bias lessons, with nothing held back. A bias every day is unrealistic, because if he fixes one before the market trades he will sometimes be wrong. There is no cookie-cutter formula, and the rest is experience ([2022 Ep 40 · 20:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1247)).

Instead, read the weekly chart and ask where the week's expansion is gravitating ([2022 Ep 40 · 21:37](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1297)):

- an old high to run above;
- an old low to run below;
- an imbalance above or below to revisit;
- nowhere much, because this market has no news this week while others do, so expect a lacklustre market.

He is not trying to catch the whole weekly range, buy the weekly low or predict the weekly close. He wants the direction of the bulk of the volatility. The test question is "Who's in the crosshairs?": if longs have been making money, is there a low to run to stop them out?

### How to trade it

1. **Check the calendar ahead,** even into next month, and focus only on days with high- or medium-impact events ([2022 Ep 40 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1487)).
2. **Go top-down:** weekly, daily, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, then 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute, to whichever chart shows a clear FVG under the model rules.
3. **On the news day, in your killzone, in the weekly direction,** look for a run up into an FVG or above a short-term high (the stops), then a breakdown with displacement, then an FVG to short. Mirror it for longs ([2022 Ep 40 · 25:34](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1534)). Killzones are Chapter 12.
4. **If the move happens before the event, it is missed.** Use another pair.

This gives a developing student permission not to know the daily bias outside these rules. The lecture was about forex, and that day he stayed on USDCAD because the calendar put forex volatility there ([2022 Ep 40 · 46:24](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2784)). He applies the same weekly-first habit to index futures in the example below.

### Worked example: the weekly NQ read for December 1, 2024

On the weekly NQ chart ICT marked November's high and low. He saw an imbalance and a volume imbalance, with buy-side and sell-side sitting at those areas before the open. He favoured a run higher while trying to avoid picking a top, and expected a break of November's high if price kept rising. One level to watch was 21580.25, from extending the retracement leg to the high. The notes call this a quarter of the range above the high and say a later video states that as his rule of thumb ([2024 #50 · 0:53](https://youtu.be/1m5yz6Vh_Dw?t=53)).

He stayed bullish on the indices until proven wrong. If the 15-minute low he had marked was lost, that changes things for the week. He added that no one knows the Sunday opening price ([2024 #50 · 4:55](https://youtu.be/1m5yz6Vh_Dw?t=295)). The notes call 21580.25 a frame of reference, not a guarantee, and say price stopped short of it in a later video.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Forecasting the whole week.** Developing students should keep forecasts to about 5 days. Forecasting the weekly range took ICT six years ([2022 Ep 12 · 29:45](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1785)).
- **Skipping the contingency.** He claims the weekly analysis was in the high 90s percent accurate in his private mentorship, and still keeps a contingency ([2024 #37 · 1:27:06](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5226)).

## Top-down analysis, the parent chart and the 15-minute bellwether

### What it is

Top-down analysis means starting on the timeframe where the draw is visible, marking a few levels, and carrying exactly those levels down to the chart where you execute. His sequence shifts a little by lesson.

| Lesson | Sequence he describes | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 Ep 2 (NQ) | Weekly (bias), daily (liquidity and draw, transposed down), hourly (week framed from Monday midnight New York to Friday), 15m (stop-run levels marked with short line segments), then 2m (1 to 3m) for the MSS and FVG entry | [2022 Ep 2 · 18:43](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1123) |
| 2022 Ep 4 (NQ, Jan 27, 2022) | Start on the 5m and work down. Earlier episodes used the 15m for liquidity pools | [2022 Ep 4 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/L-ReMHiavPM?t=346) |
| 2022 Ep 6 (NQ, Feb 3) | Start on the naked 15m. At 8:30 look left for the first swing high, then step down through 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1m for the MSS and FVG | [2022 Ep 6 · 25:31](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1531) |
| 2022 Ep 40 | Weekly, daily, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, then 5 to 1m | [2022 Ep 40 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1487) |
| 2024 #27 | A sweep of daily, 4H, 1H, 15m, 5m, plus a matrix of 5/4/3/2/1-minute charts and one of 45/30/15/5-second charts | [2024 #27 · 29:47](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1787) |

In Episode 2, "intraday" means anything below the daily. The starting points do not conflict. You begin where the draw can be seen and finish where the entry can be placed.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is parent and child. The daily is the parent and all minor swings are subordinate. You can navigate on a 1-minute chart, but only inside that context ([2022 Ep 12 · 10:36](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=636)).

The levels you carry down are not lower-timeframe support and resistance. The two lines on his hourly were the daily FVG boundaries, and the red line was the daily short-term high. Without that context, lower-timeframe price looks like noise ([2022 Ep 37 · 6:21](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=381)). In an ES example, price hit the daily FVG low, consolidated, dropped to it again, then ran the short-term high for a bullish MSS. The next draw was the buy-side above ([2022 Ep 37 · 7:07](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=427)). His 2026 summary is the same: transpose higher-timeframe reference points down, think in premium and discount, grade the levels, and treat time as the most important factor ([2026 #17 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1245)).

### How to do it

1. **Mark the draw on the daily** and only the levels that would confirm or change the bias. Keep it simple, because many new students get lost in the timeframes ([2022 Ep 39 · 4:58](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=298)).
2. **Mark the previous month's high, low and close** on every timeframe, including the seconds tab if you trade below 1 minute, so the macro levels stay visible without flipping charts. He labels the monthly low as sell-side liquidity and leaves the monthly high unlabelled. He skips the monthly open because it ends up baked into a new week opening gap (NWOG), which he tracks instead ([2026 #19 · 10:53](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=653), [2026 #19 · 15:49](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=949)).
3. **Carry the daily FVG edges and short-term high down** to the 1H and 15m ([2022 Ep 39 · 0:09](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=9)).
4. **On the 15m, shade the FVG,** then drop to the 5m and watch price trade into the shaded area. A shaded daily FVG works the same way: on the 5m price dips into its lower end and rallies ([2022 Ep 38 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=664), [2022 Ep 38 · 28:22](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1702)).
5. **Drop to 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute** for the MSS and FVG. The same FVG can show on the 4m, 2m and 1m, and lower timeframes give tighter entries and more chances ([2022 Ep 6 · 26:19](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1579), [2022 Ep 6 · 28:42](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1722)).
6. **Drawing the levels is a training aid.** While learning, draw them. ICT himself watches a naked chart ([2022 Ep 2 · 22:51](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1371)).

The notes record the month's close as 5:59 pm ET, but the futures electronic close behind the NDOG and NWOG elsewhere in this book is 4:59 pm, so check your own chart. These sequences were taught on NQ, ES and forex pairs. The notes hold no gold-specific top-down sequence, so for XAUUSD apply the same stack and verify the levels on your own feed. ICT used the Forex.com feed on TradingView and avoided others, so a level can sit at a slightly different price on another CFD feed ([2022 Ep 8 · 1:45](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=105)).

### The 15-minute bellwether

A bellwether chart is the one that shows how the whole flock is moving. ICT says "You can find everything for intraday trading" on the 15-minute chart. If forced to pick one timeframe he would pick the 15m, from which he can swing trade, short-term trade, day trade and scalp. Even when executing on the 1-minute you need the 15m storyline for the panoramic view ([2022 Ep 10 · 33:46](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2026), [2022 Ep 10 · 34:35](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2075)).

In Episode 38 he warns that staying on one timeframe without carrying the higher-timeframe analysis down means trading blind. He adds that the logic scales to any timeframe as long as you know where price is drawing ([2022 Ep 38 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=664)). In 2024 he explains why the 15m is enough: on it you can see the 60-minute and 4-hour highs and lows, so it alone gives bias, draw, reversals, continuations and stops to aim for. You may add 1H to 4H charts, just not so many that the analyses conflict ([2024 #37 · 36:31](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2191)). In 2026 he calls it a go-to that bridges the daily context and 1-minute scalps, and says the 1-minute is noisy where the 15m shows clean structure ([2026 #66 · 0:53](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=53)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Drawing daily levels as hourly support and resistance.** They are daily PD array edges carried down for context.
- **Putting big risk behind a 1-minute idea with nothing on the daily.** In 2026 he says that without daily support it is not helpful to place so much risk and trust in a one-minute view ([2026 #29 · 33:27](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=2007)). Read the daily as the foundation and the 15m as the execution frame ([2026 #66 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=243)).
- **Overloading the chart.** If a map has more than four or five zones, simplify ([2026 #68 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=198)).

## The draw on liquidity and the look-back window

### What it is

The higher-timeframe draw is the level price is being pulled toward: an old high holding buy stops, an old low holding sell stops, or an imbalance that wants rebalancing ([2022 Ep 12 · 5:43](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=343)). It answers the first narrative question, and every bias in this chapter ends in one. The draw need not be reached for the trade to work. In 2024 he says the skill is framing a move from a beginning point to an end worth 15 to 20 handles ([2024 #13 · 1:00:32](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3632)). In March 2026 he says a higher-timeframe "macro" means about six months for him, not the 20-minute window of Chapter 12, and gives first-half-2026 objectives for indices, gold, silver, DXY and Bitcoin as opinions, not advice ([2026 #113 · 8:10](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=490), [2026 #113 · 21:39](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=1299)).

### How far back to look: 20, 40 and 60 trading days

ICT's own parameter is that PD arrays (the gaps, blocks and pools in Chapters 5 to 10) older than 60 trading days are generally not used. He corrects a commenter: it is trading days, not calendar days ([2024 #18 · 1:26:19](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5179)). The notes file this under his algorithm's data ranges (IPDA).

He ranks sensitivity. An array formed in the last 20 trading days is more sensitive than one from 40 days ago, which is more sensitive than one from 60 days ago. If you only focus on the last 20 days you work with the most active arrays and never need to complicate it. Arrays never hide and are always on the chart ([2024 #18 · 1:27:06](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5226)). It is a judgment boundary, not a hard rule: his wording is that it depends on how he feels about market structure and where he thinks the market may go. A practical reading is to look first at pools and gaps from the last 20 days, widen to 40 and 60 if the near ones are unclear, and treat older ones as background. He did not give that as a procedure.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Counting calendar days, or mixing up "macro".** The window is 60 trading days. "Macro" means a clock-time window in Chapter 12, an algorithm mechanism in the 2022 SMT lectures (below), and about six months of objectives in 2026.

## Lay of the land, if-then planning and giving up a bias

### What it is

The lay of the land is a map. Before the open, scan the daily or 15m chart and mark the high and low of the range you expect, the key inefficiencies (major buy-side and sell-side liquidity), and the minor imbalances. Draw the dealing range high and low, one extreme inefficiency at each end, and the minor buy-side and sell-side inside. That is your thesis for which prices are in play. If more than four or five zones appear, simplify ([2026 #68 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=923), [2026 #68 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=198)). Dealing ranges are Chapter 4.

The conditions are if-then statements. Instead of "price will go down to X", write: if price takes out the buy-side here and closes below the CE, then I look for a draw to the sell-side. You are no longer defending a prediction. You are watching whether conditions are met, and if not, you move to the next if-then without losing conviction ([2026 #68 · 17:01](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=1021)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is psychological. If-then removes ego from the forecast and is the opposite of confirmation bias: if price does A, expect B; if it does C, look for D. Both are prepared responses ([2026 #68 · 17:48](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=1068)). In 2024 he gave the same idea as programming: define where the data comes from (the arrays), where the first setup forms each day (the first 30 minutes after 9:30), what the first data point is (settlement versus the 9:30 open), and what conditions trigger action. He calls it making it binary, to remove discretion for a beginner ([2024 #31 · 1:07:57](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4077)).

### How to do it

1. **Check where price opens in the range.** Near the high, expect a draw to the low and watch the minor zones on the way. At the midpoint (equilibrium) you have no edge, so wait for directional intent. The notes record waiting for a move of 1 to 2 percent from centre, without saying in what units ([2026 #68 · 14:36](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=876)).
2. **Write the binary morning.** If the open is above the settlement, bias lower to the gap midpoint, wait for the 9:31 candle to close, and if an FVG exists at 9:32 make it the focus. Then wait for a setup that meets the 20-handle filter. If neither scenario is present, sit still ([2024 #31 · 1:08:44](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4124), [2024 #31 · 1:09:31](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4171)). Gap and settlement arithmetic is Chapter 11.

### Worked example: giving up a bearish bias

Episode 38 is ICT's lesson in changing gears. Sometimes the right move is not to reverse but to step aside ([2022 Ep 38 · 2:22](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=142)). On the ES daily the original draw was 4070, below relative equal lows. The signs the bearish idea was wrong:

1. Multiple lows were taken, yet price reversed without offering a high-probability short ([2022 Ep 38 · 3:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=189)).
2. The 4070 target never traded, so the liquidity stayed in place.
3. Price stayed inside the daily FVG. The low was 4076.00.
4. A short-term high was taken, giving a bullish MSS.
5. Price returned to the new FVG and was supported.
6. SMT showed ES making a higher low while NQ made a lower low (next section).

The low of the day formed at 9:10 ET, with a rally at the 9:30 open. ICT gave up the morning session and waited for the algorithm to show an afternoon priced bullish, because early in the day price could still wipe out that low ([2022 Ep 38 · 44:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2661)). His advice for a failed bias: do not panic or ask others for their opinion. Rebuild the bias from what the market did at key times such as the 9:30 volatility, and wait for a setup that fits the new narrative. Not trading a wrong idea is not a loss.

In February 2026, after about six months away from forex, he described a soft gray area on DXY, EURUSD and GBPUSD: no evidence, no bias, no trade. He would check once or twice a week and move to day-by-day only when price showed something conducive ([2026 #129 · 57:22](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3442), [2026 #129 · 1:01:21](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3681)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reversing instead of waiting, or writing the branches after the fact.** Stepping aside is a valid decision, and the if-thens go on paper before the open.

## SMT divergence

### What it is

SMT divergence compares closely correlated markets at the same swing point. One takes the level and the other refuses to. In the stock indices the usual pair is NQ (Nasdaq-100 futures) and ES (S&P 500 futures). If both trade below an old low but one fails to make the lower low, that is a bullish SMT. If one makes a higher high and the other does not, that is a bearish SMT.

The name arrived gradually. In Episode 7 (2022) he calls it a *crack in correlation* and does not yet say SMT ([2022 Ep 7 · 22:34](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1354)). In Episode 22 it is confirmation without an indicator, an *x-ray view* of real accumulation and distribution ([2022 Ep 22 · 45:00](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2700)). In Episode 29 he says he dubs it SMT ([2022 Ep 29 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=247)). He says it means smart money technique or smart money tool and admits he never settled what the T stands for ([2022 Ep 38 · 15:48](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=948), [2024 #20 · 34:47](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2087)).

### Why it works

These are ICT's claims, given over several years.

- **The refuser is being accumulated or distributed.** If NQ takes a low that ES does not, ES is accumulating while NQ takes liquidity. The weaker index's lower low is only a stop hunt below a short-term low ([2022 Ep 35 · 7:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=479), [2022 Ep 38 · 15:48](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=948)). At a high it mirrors: the index with the higher high has run stops, and the laggard is the weak one, which shows distribution ([2022 Ep 22 · 43:24](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2604)).
- **It is the algorithm declining to deliver.** In Episode 7 the Dow holding up is not buyers coming in. It is an unwillingness to deliver lower, which he calls a macro: something inside the algorithm that prevents or enables delivery of price ([2022 Ep 7 · 28:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1700)). That is an earlier, different use of "macro" than the clock-time windows in Chapter 12.
- **Breadth.** The S&P holds 500 stocks, the Nasdaq 100 and the Dow 30 ([2024 #29 · 38:44](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2324)). In 2026 he reads a Nasdaq higher high that the S&P does not match as only a few mega-caps driving the move, which is heavy distribution and not sustainable ([2026 #116 · 38:16](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2296)). He also reads a Nasdaq lower high against an S&P higher high as the weakest index pulling the market lower while stronger ones hide distribution ([2026 #123 · 11:05](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=665)). The notes describe the roles inconsistently in one on-screen example, so rely on the general rule: the index that fails to confirm is the weak one.

### How to spot it

[[DIAGRAM ch15_d3 | caption: A bullish SMT at a low, NQ above and ES below (illustrative candles, schematic) | NQ trades below the 8:30 low while ES holds above it, so only one index delivered the sell-side.]]

1. **Start with an idea, not with the comparison.** You are already looking for a reason to go higher: an old low, a raid, an MSS. SMT only confirms that ([2022 Ep 7 · 23:26](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1406)). Without a bias you often see an apparent SMT that vanishes as the markets re-sync ([2022 Ep 35 · 7:13](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=433)).
2. **Pick the moment.** Divergence tends to show at 8:30, 9:30 and news releases ([2022 Ep 35 · 7:13](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=433)). In 2026 he is stricter: use it only at a liquidity run or a volatility injection such as news. The 9:30 open and first 30 minutes always qualify. It is not in every setup, and looking for it elsewhere means looking at the wrong time ([2026 #129 · 31:11](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1871), [2026 #129 · 34:19](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2059)).
3. **Set up the comparison in TradingView.** Click the "+" compare button and add the other contract. In its settings change the plotted price from close to **Low** when comparing lows, and **High** when comparing highs ([2022 Ep 35 · 6:26](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=386), [2022 Ep 22 · 44:12](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2652)). For a longer view he used daily line charts of the high or low, not the close ([2026 #123 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=198)).
4. **Compare the same swing points,** this low versus that low, the way you would talk to yourself in a journal ([2022 Ep 38 · 41:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2469)). Anchors he used: the 8:30 low, the 10:00 AM low and the 2:30 a.m. high ([2022 Ep 35 · 7:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=479), [2022 Ep 29 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=198), [2022 Ep 22 · 42:35](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2555)).
5. **Use the same contract month on both charts.** In 2024 he caught himself reaching for the September contract while comparing against December ([2024 #29 · 39:31](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2371)). NQ trades March (H), June (M), September (U) and December (Z) and expires the third Friday of the month. In August 2024 he said he expected to trade September until roughly the first two and a half weeks of September, then December ([2024 #19 · 28:37](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1717), [2024 #19 · 30:16](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1816)).
6. **Check the roll adjustment.** In June 2026 he showed that the "adjust for contract rollover" setting changes the picture. With it on or off YM makes a higher high either way, ES makes a slightly higher high only without the adjustment, and NQ does not ([2026 #72 · 41:25](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2485), [2026 #72 · 48:45](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2925)). Use the same setting on both charts before trusting a one-tick divergence.

### Reading it from candle bodies

[[DIAGRAM ch15_d4 | caption: A bearish SMT read from bodies (illustrative candles, schematic) | ES takes the prior wick high but its body closes below the prior body high, while NQ beats both.]]

In September 2024 ICT refined the comparison. NQ made a higher high in both wick and bodies. ES's wick went higher, but its bodies at the high stayed below the prior high. He counts that as SMT: wicks do the damage, but bodies hold the bulk of the volume, so compare the bodies of the swing highs and lows and not just the wicks ([2024 #29 · 41:10](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2470)). The notes do not spell out the direction, but the example reads as a bearish divergence at a high.

In 2026 the same habit appears as a rule for levels. Bodies repeatedly closing above a level are bullish, and below it bearish. Wicks under a level with bodies above suggest a retracement or reversal, while bodies buried below confirm a break ([2026 #53 · 28:30](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1710)). That is a separate lesson from SMT, but both ask which part of the candle shows commitment.

### How to trade it

- **Confirmation only.** It must confirm an idea you already hold (a draw, a raid, an MSS). Then execute with the FVG and MSS logic in your instrument ([2022 Ep 7 · 23:26](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1406)). His 2026 wording: "It is not a selection tool. It is not a timing tool." It is a qualifier and risk flag that works best when you already have a bias ([2026 #133 · 7:19](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=439)).
- **Never alone.** He says he blew accounts in the 1990s trading these divergences without a narrative ([2022 Ep 7 · 25:57](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1557)).
- **At a low, favour the index that held.** With a bullish bias, an ES higher low against an NQ lower low, especially against the 8:30 low, is the telltale sign. Buy the stronger index's FVG or order block ([2022 Ep 35 · 7:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=479)). In Episode 29 he traded ES, the more structured one, after it held while NQ went lower ([2022 Ep 29 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=247)).
- **At a high, the weak index is the short.** In April 2026 he says the weakest index moves smoothly with the least retracement, while the strongest has deeper retracements and more time distortion. Short the weak one and wait for retracements in the strong one. If long in a weakening market, ES weakness can time an NQ long once ES bottoms first ([2026 #103 · 34:39](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2079)). The notes give no single rule for which index to execute in. He prefers NQ for volatility, with 100 stocks against ES's 500 ([2024 #45 · 1:05:13](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3913)).
- **Use it to size and scale.** When long NQ and NQ makes a higher high that ES does not confirm, he will likely take a partial, because the divergence signals weakening momentum ([2024 #5 · 1:42:01](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=6121)). On September 30, 2024 he says SMT only helps him decide whether to use his largest leverage, and he notes "NQ −" and the time on a notepad instead of drawing it ([2024 #37 · 42:04](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2524)).
- **It conditions the entry.** On October 16, 2024, if ES refuses to go lower, the NQ FVG that forms below becomes an inversion FVG (Chapter 7) ([2024 #45 · 38:23](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2303)).
- **It does not call a top.** An NQ high that ES does not match lowers the odds of continuation. If price then breaks down technically, you have the best of the best ([2024 #20 · 34:47](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2087)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using SMT alone.** The 1990s lesson in his words, and the 2026 qualifier wording.
- **Dow-led divergence.** The Dow can lull and then run, cancelling the signal.
- **Confusing SMT with decoupling.** SMT is a two-index comparison at a specific level, used as confirmation. Decoupling is the three-average condition filter in the next section.
- **Reading it as a reversal call.** In 2024 he says it does not mean a top. The June 2026 notes record that he expects a reversal and call a case with NQ higher and ES lower a Judas swing trap before a sharp sell-off ([2026 #79 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=672)). Treat the 2024 wording, lower odds of continuation, as the safer reading.

1. **2022.** A three-index crack in correlation with the Dow (Ep 7), then the SMT name and the ES versus NQ pair (Ep 22, 29), then the TradingView compare setup (Ep 35, 38). That sits uneasily with the 2022 wording above. Both are his statements, and the later one comes from a trader with other filters.
3. **2026.** The comparison is used more, as a filter: breadth and distribution (March), qualifier (February), when it is appropriate (February), and the sick sister name (April). The Dow is compared again, but under the heading of decoupling.

## Decoupling: when the three averages disagree

### What it is

For stock-index trading ICT wants NQ, ES and the Dow (YM) moving the same way. That is a symmetrical market and the ideal environment. When they do different things the market is decoupled ([2024 #37 · 1:31:57](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5517)). He says it is the only element of Dow theory he uses, credits the idea to a book author whose name the captions garble, and says he taught it in his 2016 to 2017 core content.

If you trade NQ and want to be long, you want ES and the Dow to support it: all three bullish ([2024 #45 · 7:34](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=454)). When NQ rallies, ES stalls and YM drops, you have decoupling. In April 2026 he says it breaks the algorithmic precision of price and lowers the probability of clean structure ([2026 #106 · 7:17](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=437)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that price delivers cleanly only when the major averages agree. The Dow can be the *canary in the coal mine*: when it refuses to do what the other two do, that may foretell something ([2024 #37 · 1:41:59](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=6119)). A decoupled day is one to sit still on, because you are likely to get sand kicked in your face. His reason is that manual intervention is coming (Chapter 14) ([2024 #37 · 1:34:22](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5662)). He says Fed speeches and FOMC cause decoupling.

### How to spot it

[[DIAGRAM ch15_d5 | caption: Reading NQ, ES and the Dow before you trade (decision flow) | Agreement is the ideal. A mismatch is SMT only at a liquidity run, and decoupling otherwise.]]

1. **Open the three charts together** around the open and check that highs and lows are being taken together ([2024 #37 · 2:09:53](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=7793)).
2. **Expect it on no-news mornings.** Decoupling is common when no news is due ([2024 #45 · 6:46](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=406)).
3. **Look at the candles.** Price turns fuzzy: candles overlap the prior candle's range and revisit the same prices (seen in ES), against the clean ranges and imbalances in NQ ([2024 #45 · 1:05:13](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3913)).
4. **Name who is out of step.** On October 16, 2024 at 9:00, ES dropped while the Dow went higher and NQ also dropped. NQ and ES briefly shared a direction, the Dow did not ([2024 #45 · 1:02:44](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3764)).

### How to trade it

- **Sit and wait for agreement.** Sometimes the three re-sync within the session. Sometimes only after the morning, so the afternoon is better ([2024 #45 · 12:11](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=731)).
- **After a Fed speaker, wait 15 minutes** for things to re-sync. If they have not, keep waiting. Stick to a single instrument so you are not tempted to pick the right one ([2024 #37 · 1:34:22](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5662)).
- **If they stay decoupled all day,** it is tradable only by very short-term scalpers, which he does not teach. A new student should not trade it ([2024 #45 · 6:46](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=406)).
- **If you trade it anyway,** tighten stops, reduce size or stop for the session ([2026 #106 · 22:39](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=1359)).
- **Write the sequel in your notes.** As soon as you see decoupling, expect an SMT-type divergence later in the session, after which they go back in sync ([2024 #45 · 1:02:44](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3764)).
- **When all three agree** and price returns to the first presented FVG and then shows displacement, he calls it easy: drop to a 15-second chart and take the next FVG in that direction ([2024 #45 · 12:11](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=731)).

Decoupling is one of three adverse conditions he taught together on September 30, 2024: high resistance liquidity runs are tradable but need a wider stop, seek and destroy cannot be traded, and decoupled markets are tradable but targets probably will not be reached ([2024 #37 · 2:32:04](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=9124)). The first two are Chapter 16.

### Sick sisters and strong versus weak markets (2026)

In April 2026 ICT gives the same disagreement a new label. Closely correlated markets should confirm each other. When one makes a higher high and the other does not, the lagging market is the *sick sister* and the leader encourages it. The laggard does not need to match the size of the move, since a higher high on MES is all that is necessary. A decoupled tape like that he reads as heavy manipulation ([2026 #101 · 1:40:50](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6050)). In his example NQ made slightly higher highs than ES and YM at about 10:05 ET, and MES caught up with its own higher high at about 10:20 ET. NQ then broke down on a 1-minute inversion FVG and the decoupling faded for the near term ([2026 #101 · 41:21](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2481), [2026 #101 · 57:07](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=3427)). Another April lecture's notes file a similar idea as "six sisters", which the sick sister notes say is a captioning variant.

### Worked example: Wednesday, October 16, 2024

The Dow was going higher or consolidating, NQ was weak and ES shrugged off weakness. The opening range gap was anemic again. NQ took its sell-side, did not trade back up into its first presented FVG at first, and then, after the sell-side run, made a lower low while ES held and returned into the range ([2024 #45 · 38:23](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=2303)). ICT forecast that SMT before it happened. If the Dow held or rose, ES failed to take its low, and NQ made the lower low and came back into its range, the three would return to agreement and NQ would go back to the first presented FVG ([2024 #45 · 56:03](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=3363)). He admits he gets decoupled days wrong a lot, and that delivery to the sell-side still did not make it a trade worth taking ([2024 #45 · 12:11](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=731)).

In the lecture dated April 10, 2026 he describes the Good Friday session after a CPI miss: NQ tried higher, ES hesitated and YM declined, and he closed interest and stepped away. Decoupling often precedes big moves, so it is a warning flag and a reason to reduce risk ([2026 #106 · 6:26](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=386)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Forcing a trade because price still delivers.** Delivery to a PD array is not the same as a high-probability trade.
- **Counting a Dow-only divergence as SMT.** In late 2024 it is the decoupling signal.
- **Forgetting the forex version.** The forex equivalent is a symmetrical triad: GBPUSD and EURUSD positively correlated, DXY the opposite. If DXY is flat while GBPUSD rises and EURUSD falls, the trade is a EURGBP short ([2024 #37 · 1:53:24](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=6804)).

### How the teaching changed

By his account the idea is old (2016 to 2017), but it is named and taught in detail only in September and October 2024, as a condition filter beside high resistance runs and seek and destroy. In 2022 the Dow's disagreement was itself the SMT signal. By October 2024 it is the opposite: a Dow disagreement is decoupling, and only ES against NQ counts as SMT. The 2026 notes keep both. One April lecture calls a YM, NQ and ES comparison SMT while noting the Dow acts independently because of its weighting, and says to lean on ES against NQ ([2026 #103 · 30:33](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=1833)). Another compares YM with NQ and calls it decoupling, not SMT ([2026 #99 · 28:55](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1735)).

## Intermarket analysis: the dollar index and currency crosses

### What it is

Intermarket analysis means reading one market through its relatives. Among indices that is NQ, ES and the Dow, covered above. In forex and metals ICT uses the US Dollar Index (DXY) as the anchor.

- **Dollar against foreign currencies.** A bullish DXY means bearish EURUSD, GBPUSD and the other foreign currencies against the dollar ([2022 Ep 19 · 19:13](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1153)). The pair needs DXY as a barometer, and the relationship is inverted. He calls it the *teeter-totter*: a higher dollar puts weight on the euro ([2022 Ep 20 · 15:50](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=950)).
- **Dollar against risk assets.** A rising dollar is risk-off, a flight to quality where money pours into the dollar and out of foreign currencies, stocks and index futures. A falling dollar is the reverse ([2022 Ep 21 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=100), [2022 Ep 21 · 2:28](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=148)).
- **Dollar against metals and oil (2026).** If DXY rises, EURUSD and GBPUSD fall, and gold, silver and oil tend to go lower. He also says the dollar often moves inverse to commodities such as oil and bitcoin ([2026 #72 · 13:06](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=786), [2026 #72 · 37:24](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2244), [2026 #75 · 12:41](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=761)).
- **Crosses from their legs.** To frame a cross such as EURJPY, check each leg alone. On the daily, 6E (euro futures) was clearly rising and the yen was weak, so strong euro plus weak yen meant EURJPY higher. He calls this a real simple way to frame exotic crosses, then you apply time of day and wait for the pattern ([2022 Ep 8 · 11:56](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=716), [2022 Ep 8 · 12:42](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=762)).

### How to use it

1. **Set the DXY draw on its daily chart.** In 2022 his targets were the 100 level, 101, 101.947 and just short of 103. He treated the drop from 101 as a retracement, not a top ([2022 Ep 19 · 8:02](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=482)). On June 7, 2026 his plan was a pullback into the inversion FVG, then a run to 101.977 ([2026 #73 · 1:42](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=102)).
2. **Mark the matching level in the pair.** For EURUSD it was the previous week's high, which holds buy stops.
3. **Watch the two together.** If DXY is falling to a level you identified as a buying point, a EURUSD rally at that moment is likely a false breakout and a short at the exact moment DXY hits its level ([2022 Ep 20 · 8:44](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=524), [2022 Ep 20 · 10:17](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=617)).
4. **Frame the indices the same way,** inversely to DXY ([2022 Ep 21 · 2:28](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=148)).
5. **Skip USD pairs when DXY is stuck.** With DXY in a daily range, EURUSD and GBPUSD are choppy. Wait for the range to break. Crosses such as EURJPY, AUDJPY and GBPJPY still move ([2026 #84 · 5:37](https://youtu.be/dwMgP2zkH0w?t=337)).
6. **Take profit at the target band.** In his June 2026 review he recalls telling traders to take profits on short EURUSD and GBPUSD and drop the bullish DXY idea once price stretched into the target band with a full-body candle near the high ([2026 #72 · 13:06](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=786)).

### Worked example: DXY at 99.92 and a EURUSD false breakout

ICT said that if he were making the market he would take DXY just below 100 (about 99.95 to 99.92) to convince everyone it had topped, then reverse it higher ([2022 Ep 19 · 20:01](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1201)). As DXY reached the 99.92 area, EURUSD ran above the previous week's high, clearing the buy stops at the same instant, then reversed lower ([2022 Ep 20 · 8:44](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=524)). DXY then rallied through relative equal highs toward his 102 and 103 objectives while ES (June 2022 contract) turned lower from its daily bearish order block, and EURUSD made the new daily low he had been calling ([2022 Ep 21 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=243), [2022 Ep 21 · 4:51](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=291)). He did not take either trade: he was asleep and was not trading forex then.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Borrowing one sentence as your reason.** The Episode 19 notes warn against using one of his lines about the dollar to justify your own trade.
- **Bonds.** The notes behind this book contain no bond or yield lesson from ICT. The only rate remark is the interest-rate differential point above, so do not read a bond rule into his method from this chapter.
- **Gold.** The notes give one relationship: a higher dollar goes with lower gold, silver and oil. They give no gold timing rule and no gold SMT pair. Use DXY as a bias check and take the entry from the gold chart.
- **Treating the correlation as a trigger.** In 2026 he uses it to confirm directional bias and to avoid trading against correlated moves. Geopolitical events can override it for a while, but order flow eventually reasserts it ([2026 #75 · 3:13](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=193)).

### How the teaching changed

The 2022 lessons are forex-heavy: components of a cross, DXY as barometer, risk-on and risk-off. In 2024 he keeps one line: forex needs an extra step, so always refer to the dollar index ([2024 #37 · 36:31](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2191)). In 2026 he extends the dollar relationship to metals, oil and Bitcoin, and says he went about six months without looking at forex before returning in February 2026 ([2026 #129 · 57:22](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3442)).

## From bias to trade: the January 2, 2026 walk-through

ICT's January 2, 2026 NQ short shows how the pieces feed an entry. Entry mechanics are Chapter 18 and the opening range gap is Chapter 11.

1. **Bias and target.** The night before, he said price would drop and he was not interested in going long. He told his group the target: half of the RTH opening range gap, 25,567.25. 2. **Premium and trigger.** Price rallied into a premium inversion array at the top of the day, taking several stages of buy-side (an old relative equal high pool and the 9:48 short-term high). A first presented FVG formed on the sell-off, and he wanted it to offer no resistance, with no bodies up there and price ripping through its low ([2026 #140 · 32:31](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1951)).
3. **Retest and run.** A failed retest (a wick just above the FVG's midpoint, then a close below its low) means heaviness, which he called very bearish. Price ran to the half gap and through it, swept the opening range gap's CE, and dropped to the lower quadrant of a daily wick at 25,267.50, with over 200 handles of washout that day ([2026 #140 · 34:07](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2047)).

The notes do not give his stop or size, and the captions confuse the "first presented FVG" and the red box in places. Read it as an outline, not a trade plan.
