Market structure is the record of swing highs and swing lows that price leaves behind, and displacement is the energetic move that tells you which of those swings just mattered. ICT uses them together: price runs a pool of stops, breaks the nearest opposing swing with force, and leaves a gap to trade. After this chapter you should be able to mark swings the way he does, rank them short-, intermediate- or long-term, and tell a real market structure shift from a harmless poke.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch02_d1 | caption: bearish market structure shift, candle by candle (illustrative prices) | Notice the order: stops are run first, the short-term low breaks second, and the trade is only the retrace into the gap the break left behind.]]

- A swing high is three candles: a high with a lower high on each side. A swing low mirrors it. No five-bar fractal is needed.
- Swings rank. Any swing high is short-term. One with lower short-term highs on both sides is intermediate-term. One with lower intermediate-term highs on both sides is long-term. A high made by refilling a fair value gap is intermediate-term at once.
- A market structure shift (MSS) is the break of the short-term swing that formed just before price ran liquidity. Without that liquidity run first, the break means little.
- The break should come with displacement: energetic, preferably a candle body closing beyond the swing, leaving a fair value gap between the displacement high and low. No gap there, no trade.
- Intraday, ICT calls it a shift, and it may deliver only one leg. A break through an intermediate-term swing is a significant break, with larger targets.
- Structure frames the idea; it does not decide it. The narrative (the climate, the calendar, the volatility on offer, and above all time and the draw on liquidity) decides whether a shift is worth trading.

## Swing highs and swing lows

### What it is
A swing high is the top of a small turn in price; a swing low is the bottom of one. ICT's definition is short and strict. A swing high is a candle whose high has a lower high on the candle just before it and on the candle just after it. A swing low is a candle whose low has a higher low on both sides. That is three candles in total, on any timeframe ([2022 Ep 10 · 17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064), [2022 Ep 40 · 4:08](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=248)).

He keeps this apart from the Williams fractal and the MT4 fractal, which need five candles. By then the move is gone: "If you're waiting for five candles you missed the boat" ([2022 Ep 40 · 4:57](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=297), [2022 Ep 16 · 1:50](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=110)). Three candles plus the proper context is enough to start hunting ([2022 Ep 10 · 18:31](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1111)).

The swing is confirmed when the candle after it closes ([2024 #28 · 1:02:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3776), [2024 #29 · 1:24:16](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5056)). On a daily chart that means waiting for the next day's close. Once it closes, he starts looking for the trade it suggests ([2022 Ep 16 · 1:00](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=60)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is about where traders leave their stops. A trader who is short a falling market trails the protective buy stop to just above the most recent short-term high, because the books teach it. A long trails a sell stop under recent lows ([2022 Ep 19 · 24:41](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1481)). So every swing point marks a cluster of resting orders: buy stops above highs, sell stops below lows ([2024 #29 · 1:24:16](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5056)). Those clusters are liquidity, the fuel he says price is delivered to (Chapter 3).

He says he needs no heat map or order-book tool to find them. He knows where the stops sit because he knows what short sellers do ([2026 #136 · 46:33](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2793)). In 2026 he describes swing points as institutional reference points: a break through one with displacement shows participation in that direction ([2026 #79 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288)).

### How to spot it
1. Pick your framing timeframe first. He builds structure there (the 1-hour chart in one lesson) and calls marking every swing on the lower timeframes overkill.
2. Find a candle whose high is above the high of the candle before and the candle after. For a low, find one below both neighbours' lows.
3. Wait for the third candle to close. Until then the swing is not confirmed.
4. Note what kind of high it is. He prefers to see a bodied swing high get run as a stop hunt, rather than a tiny wick high ([2024 #28 · 1:24:59](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5099)).
5. Keep the labels in your head. He says his chart annotations are "lipstick" for teaching; he labels swings mentally to avoid distraction.

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Waiting for five bars.** A five-candle fractal confirms two candles later than ICT's swing. On a 1-minute chart that delay is the whole move.
- **Calling it early.** Until the third candle closes, the middle candle is not yet a swing.
- **Wicks versus bodies.** In 2026 he says swings are not limited to the wick high and wick low. When comparing swings, look at the bodies in the rotation; the swing high is the highest body element (the close, or the open if that is higher) ([2026 #112 · 46:45](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2805)). In another review he reads a swing high straight off the bodies: high, higher high, lower high ([2026 #119 · 36:40](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=2200)).
- **Loose shorthand.** One 2026 review uses "short-term high" for the top of the last few 1-minute candles ([2026 #54 · 13:57](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=837)). The three-candle definition is the one he restates in 2026 ([2026 #129 · 15:04](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=904)).
- **Relative equal highs and lows.** Two swing points at almost the same price form a liquidity pool (Chapter 3). One refinement belongs here: if the left swing high is slightly higher than the right, he says there is a very high probability that price runs above both ([2024 #1 · 1:14:55](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4495)). For lows, the deeper low comes first.
- **Minor versus primary.** The day's first low is minor sell-side. Once it is taken, the new low is primary, a "much more meaningful event" ([2024 #28 · 1:20:40](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4840)). The same applies to highs on the buy side.

### How the teaching changed
- **2022.** Three candles, read from the candle highs and lows, repeated across the series ([2022 Ep 2 · 42:59](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2579), [2022 Ep 19 · 1:17:04](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4624)).
- **2024.** Same definition. He recalls circling "ring highs" on hand-drawn daily charts ([2024 #8 · 37:25](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2245)), adds that the swing is confirmed at the close that completes it, and prefers bodied highs as stop-hunt targets.
- **2026.** The count is unchanged ("three candles entirely", [2026 #129 · 15:04](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=904)). His daily example: Thursday's high, Friday's higher high (which also took a short-term high), Monday's lower high; that swing high is the one he did not want to see broken on a close. What changes is the yardstick: in 2026 he compares swings by their bodies ([2026 #112 · 46:45](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2805)), where 2022 used the candle highs. Both versions are on record; if they disagree on a chart, note both levels.

### Watch it

- [2022 Ep 40 · 4:08](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=248) — a daily swing high right after an old high is run.
- [2024 #28 · 1:02:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3776) — a swing low confirmed at the close.
- [2026 #112 · 46:45](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=2805) — compare swings by their bodies.

## Short-, intermediate- and long-term swings

### What it is
Not every swing carries the same weight. ICT ranks them in three tiers, a scheme he says he took from Larry Williams' market-structure chapters and then extended. He says the result is far more than just lower lows and lower highs, and he is clear that it is not supply and demand or support and resistance ([2022 Ep 11 · 14:29](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=869)).

The ranks nest like boxes. Take highs first; lows are the mirror image.

- **Short-term high (STH):** any swing high, the three-candle kind from the last section.
- **Intermediate-term high (ITH):** a swing high with a lower short-term high on its left and a lower short-term high on its right.
- **Long-term high (LTH):** a high with a lower intermediate-term high on both sides.

The tiers are relative to the chart you are on. On a 3-minute chart he calls the top tier a "long-term intraday high" and admits it sounds like an oxymoron ([2022 Ep 11 · 10:28](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=628)). In his old chart-book habit he drew one circle over a short-term high, two over an intermediate one and three over a long-term one ([2022 Ep 11 · 13:42](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=822)).

[[DIAGRAM ch02_d2 | caption: swing highs ranked by rings, one for short-term, two for intermediate-term, three for long-term (illustrative) | Notice that rank comes from the neighbours: each ITH has lower STHs beside it, and the LTH has lower ITHs beside it.]]

### ICT's addition: the rebalance rule
In the next episode he added what he calls the main takeaway. Any time price returns to rebalance an imbalance, meaning it trades back to fill a fair value gap (Chapter 5), the swing high made at that refill is immediately an intermediate-term high. If price falls to fill a bullish gap, that low is an intermediate-term low ([2022 Ep 12 · 13:00](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=780)). So there are two routes to the rank: a short-term high flanked by lower short-term highs, or a high formed by rebalancing a gap ([2022 Ep 12 · 41:08](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2468)).

### The weak intermediate-term high
Ideally the ITH sits above the short-term highs beside it. When the rebalance high is not higher than the short-term high on its left or right, ICT says it is only rebalancing. He reads the market as exceedingly weak, with the algorithm tipping its hand, and says he pioneered the idea himself ([2022 Ep 12 · 22:31](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1351), [2022 Ep 12 · 37:56](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2276)).

This answers a retail puzzle: which way does a pennant or triangle break? Retail sees a low, a high, a higher low and a failed higher high. The weak ITH says it breaks down. He calls the consolidation a "false bull flag" and likes to fade it ([2022 Ep 12 · 12:14](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=734), [2022 Ep 12 · 14:35](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=875)). With a bearish higher-timeframe premise, a weak ITH justifies anticipating the failure. He treats the up-close candles that formed it as a bearish order block (Chapter 9) and looks for an aggressive short.

[[DIAGRAM ch02_d5 | caption: three ideas on intermediate-term swings: the rebalance rule, the weak ITH, and a significant break (schematic) | Notice that the weak ITH sits below the short-term high beside it, and that the significant break takes out the ITL, not only a short-term low.]]

### The intermediate-term low
The mirror idea is simplest on the buy side. A low with a higher low on each side is an intermediate-term low. Once price has filled a gap and begins to rally from it, he says it should not be taken out until the objective is reached ([2022 Ep 13 · 16:01](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=961), [2022 Ep 13 · 21:35](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1295)). In his MNQ example the retracement low into an hourly order block was the ITL.

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is that rank tells you how much the algorithm cares about a swing. Three threads run through the notes.

- **Nesting.** The same pattern repeats at every scale: a run on relative equal highs, a break with displacement, a gap, a retrace whose bodies respect the gap. He calls it a fractal. A retracement inside bearish structure makes a short-term high that should respect the order flow and head for the sell stops below ([2022 Ep 22 · 24:54](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1494), [2022 Ep 22 · 26:27](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1587)).
- **Stops resting on the rank.** In a bearish leg the nearest ITH is a lower high against the LTH, so it is unlikely to be exceeded. With sell-side liquidity below acting as a magnet, he shorts close to it ([2022 Ep 11 · 11:17](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=677)).
- **Where real runs start.** In 2026 he says significant runs form at intermediate- and long-term highs and lows, not short-term ones. Part of the skill is knowing how they should form, at what time (inside a macro) and halfway between targets. A low made by an inverted FVG that is respected after a macro can be an ITL (Chapter 7) ([2026 #19 · 1:21:02](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4862)).

### Checking the leg: up-close and down-close candles
Rank also tells you whether a leg is still healthy. In a bearish swing, every up-close candle should keep price from trading above it. Price may come back and touch one, which then acts like a bearish order block or a speed bump, but it should not trade through it. If price cannot overtake the nearest up-close candle, it does not need to reach those above, and price "remains heavy" ([2022 Ep 12 · 49:10](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2950)).

In a bullish swing, down-close candles support price and should not be overlapped from above. Overlap is acceptable only if a short-term low sits close by, so that its sell stops are taken before price re-accumulates ([2022 Ep 12 · 53:13](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3193)). With no nearby swing low there is nothing to worry about. His summary: "Bullish swings: down-close candles are support. Bearish swings: up-close candles are resistance." ([2022 Ep 12 · 48:22](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2902)). In his NQ example, rallies traded back into up-close candles after an order-block short but never overtook them, and price kept falling ([2022 Ep 12 · 51:35](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3095)).

### Short-term highs and lows as minor liquidity
The lowest rank is still useful, because trailed stops sit there. After an energetic sell-off that prints a new lower low, shorts trail their stops to just above the most recent short-term high. That cluster is the minor buy-side liquidity. Traders who kept stops above the relative equal highs leave a higher pool intact, so there are two levels to consider ([2026 #136 · 45:47](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2747)). In a down move, minor pools are pause zones rather than reversal zones, and are often swept on the way down ([2026 #79 · 8:46](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=526)). On the long side he marks a recent high one or two bars back as minor buy-side, wants price to tag it on the opening rally, and takes half the position there ([2026 #139 · 14:11](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=851)). In a long, he takes a partial above each broken short-term high rather than tightening the stop ([2024 #13 · 30:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1844); Chapter 19). Chapter 3 covers liquidity pools in full.

### How to spot it
1. Work on your framing timeframe. Mark swing highs and lows there, ideally in your head; he calls marking every swing on the lower timeframes overkill.
2. Promote a swing high to intermediate-term when the short-term highs on both sides are lower. Promote it to long-term when the intermediate-term highs on both sides are lower. Lows mirror this.
3. Separately, label any swing made where price came back to refill a gap as an ITH (or ITL), at once.
4. Check the neighbours. Is the ITH above the short-term highs beside it? If not, treat it as weak.
5. Check the leg. In a bearish idea, have rallies taken out any up-close candle? If yes, the leg is in doubt.
6. Build trust by backtesting: he suggests classifying swing highs and lows on past charts to see how the nesting behaves before you rely on it.

### How to trade it
- **Short near the ITH.** With a bearish leg and a draw below, he shorts close to the current ITH. It is a lower high against the LTH, so it is unlikely to be exceeded ([2022 Ep 11 · 11:17](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=677)).
- **Stop above the ITH.** The high that carries the imbalance should not be exceeded, so the stop goes above it. After the swing low breaks (an internal bearish shift), any rally is a suspect rally that sets up another run lower ([2022 Ep 18 · 43:49](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2629), [2022 Ep 18 · 46:56](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2816)).
- **Roll the stop late.** After the first partial, leave the stop where it is. Only when a significant intermediate-term low on the 1-minute chart is taken, which is also a full target, may you roll it down. Beginners who trail aggressively get stopped right before the big move ([2022 Ep 6 · 40:15](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2415), [2022 Ep 6 · 41:01](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2461)). Chapter 19 covers management.
- **Significant break and targets.** A break of a short-term low and an intermediate-term low (or just the ITL) is more meaningful than taking one short-term low. Measure the long-term high to long-term low range and replicate it lower for generic targets. Or anchor a Fibonacci on the ITH, where the decline actually began, down to the long-term low that breaks; in his NQ 1-hour example the −1.5 standard deviation projection marked the low ([2022 Ep 12 · 28:58](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1738), [2022 Ep 12 · 30:34](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1834), [2022 Ep 12 · 32:11](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1931)). He ties the long-term range to a higher-timeframe range, here the daily FVG ([2022 Ep 12 · 25:44](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=1544)). Chapter 4 explains the standard-deviation projections.
- **2026 projections.** After marking a swing low and high he projects 100%, 150% and 200% of the swing range below the low and checks the result against other levels, such as the daily low. He calls it plain arithmetic on structure ([2026 #9 · 9:59](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=599)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

## Market structure shift and break in market structure

### What it is
A market structure shift (MSS) is ICT's trigger for a turn in the market. In its bearish form: price runs the buy stops above an old high (or relative equal highs), then breaks below the short-term swing low that formed on the way up. The bullish form is the mirror image. He says it appears almost every day, many times a week, in one direction or the other, and that it applies on all timeframes ([2022 Ep 2 · 27:43](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1663), [2022 Ep 6 · 18:21](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1101)).

His full candle-by-candle version, for a bullish shift, from Episode 3:

1. **Liquidity first.** For a bullish shift, price trades below an old low and runs the sell stops under retail "support": a double bottom or a single low. The bearish shift mirrors this with old highs or relative equal highs.
2. **Mark the swing.** The short-term swing high that formed right before that low was taken out is the level to beat. It is a candle with a lower high on each side.
3. **Break it.** The candle that trades through that short-term high is the MSS candle. It does not have to close above it.
4. **Significance.** The break matters only if liquidity was taken first ([2022 Ep 3 · 6:35](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=395), [2022 Ep 3 · 13:45](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=825)).

After the MSS candle closes, watch the next candle. When it closes, has a fair value gap been left? That gap is the earliest place to trade. He calls this the complete, algorithmic definition: nothing else is needed.

### Shift versus break
ICT uses two words with different weights. For intraday work he says "shift", not "break". An intraday shift signals a likely intraday draw, may deliver only one leg, and leaves the opposing high still available the same day. A "market structure break" means more in context and can lead to prolonged, multi-day movement ([2022 Ep 3 · 2:39](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=159)). In Episode 2 he had simply called the same signature a break in market structure. Other courses abbreviate this as "BOS"; the notes do not use that term in ICT's voice, so this chapter keeps his two words. Three related labels sit nearby:

- **Internal shift.** Breaking the swing low is an internal bearish shift. Any rally afterwards is a suspect rally that sets up another run lower ([2022 Ep 18 · 43:49](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2629)).
- **Significant break.** Taking out a short-term low and the intermediate-term low (or just the ITL) is more meaningful than one short-term low (see the previous section).
- **Which shift counts depends on your range.** Students see several short-term breaks and ask which is the MSS. His answer: it depends on the range you are working within. When he was playing a run up to a premium, the MSS was the break of that range's relevant high, followed by a drop into the FVG he bought ([2022 Ep 36 · 4:55](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=295)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is about what the stop run leaves behind. Retail shorts hold buy stops above the old high, and breakout buyers place entries there. When price runs those stops, smart money has taken the other side of that flow. The break of the short-term low is the "signature" that tips the market's hand ([2022 Ep 2 · 27:43](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1663)). A break with no liquidity taken first has spent no fuel and proves little.

That is also why he will not anticipate the shift. After the high is taken, do not rush in; wait for the signature ([2022 Ep 2 · 28:31](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1711), [2022 Ep 3 · 1:50](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=110)). He adds that there are ways to sell right above the highs without waiting for a shift, but says students do not have that skill yet. High-frequency algorithms, he notes, use market structure on the 3-, 2- and 1-minute charts and on sub-minute charts.

### How to spot it
Bearish version; flip everything for bullish.

1. Mark the pool the stops sit in: an old high, relative equal highs, or a recent short-term high (Chapter 3).
2. Watch price trade through it. The high of that run is the displacement high.
3. Find the short-term swing low that formed on the way up, the one made right before the run. If several are available, the right one depends on your range.
4. Wait for a candle to trade through that low. Prefer a big body that closes below it. A wick through the low and back is not convincing, because price might make another high ([2022 Ep 6 · 19:08](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1148)).
5. Mark the displacement low, the low or close beyond the swing low.
6. Look for a fair value gap that formed between the displacement high and the displacement low. No gap in that range means no trade: wait, or check another market, since one of them will have a setup every trading day ([2022 Ep 6 · 19:55](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1195), [2022 Ep 6 · 21:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1289)).

### How to trade it
- **Entry.** Only after the shift do you look for the gap, and you sell the retrace into it (entry mechanics: Chapters 5 and 18). If you miss it and price later returns to touch the FVG, you can still short there ([2022 Ep 11 · 17:40](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=1060)).
- **Premium only for shorts.** The FVG must sit at or above the equilibrium of the displacement leg (next section).
- **Do not be ultra-aggressive in high volatility.** He was not interested until a 15-minute short-term low at 3915.25 broke (the notes do not name the instrument). A trader who wants to be ultra aggressive could short smaller breaks, but he did not want to catch a break of a minor low and then watch price run the relative equal highs above ([2022 Ep 33 · 1:01](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=61), [2022 Ep 33 · 10:37](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=637)).
- **Stop and targets.** The stop goes above the ITH. Targets: a nearby short-term low for a partial, then the sell-side below the previous day's low in his ES example ([2022 Ep 25 · 27:48](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1668)). Chapter 19 covers management. With one contract you cannot take a partial: wait for displacement below a key low, move the stop to just above that candle's high, and repeat at each key low ([2026 #127 · 57:11](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3431)).

**Where the clock fits.** Everything above is timeframe-free. ICT's timing rules here were taught on index futures, with one forex model (Episode 40); the notes give no gold-specific version.

- **8:30 ET.** The news embargo lifts and the algorithm starts seeking liquidity, though it may wait until 9:30. When he is bearish, the 8:30 to 9:30 hour is expected to run higher to set up shorts. The 9:30 equity open is manipulation time and looks like a breakout to retail ([2022 Ep 25 · 31:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1861), [2022 Ep 25 · 34:11](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2051), [2022 Ep 25 · 34:57](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2097)).
- **1:30 PM ET.** The earliest he wants to trade the afternoon, because an equity algorithm macro starts then. Look for the first swing high and swing low and apply the same stop-hunt logic ([2022 Ep 5 · 31:47](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1907), [2022 Ep 5 · 33:22](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2002)). Chapter 12 covers macros.
- **News.** A small break, "toe in the water", is not enough; wait for energetic displacement through the short-term low, which in his forex example was the 8:30 release itself. A pre-news short is riskier. Only high and medium impact events interest him, and the New York killzone for forex runs 7:00 to 10:00 AM ([2022 Ep 40 · 8:09](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=489), [2022 Ep 40 · 9:43](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=583)). In 2024 he adds a minimum: wait at least 15 minutes after such news ([2024 #37 · 6:54](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=414)). Chapter 14 covers news days.

### Worked example
**NQ, March 2022, 15-minute and 2-minute charts (the Episode 3 homework chart).** The 15-minute chart showed an old low near 14,600 with sell stops beneath it, and relative equal highs at about 14,820 to 14,860 with buy stops above.

1. On the 2-minute chart price dives under about 14,600 and runs the sell stops.
2. It then rallies through the short-term swing high formed just before that low. That is the bullish MSS.
3. The next candles leave an FVG. Price trades down into it and then takes off.
4. It runs the buy stops above the highs and through the relative equal highs.
5. Above the equal highs a swing low forms and is broken. Because buy stops were taken first, this is a bearish MSS.
6. A rally into the new FVG is the short, targeting the sell stops below the lows and an imbalance below the 50% of the range ([2022 Ep 3 · 1:50](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=110), [2022 Ep 3 · 6:35](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=395)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **A break with no liquidity run behind it.** It is a break, but not a shift worth trading.
- **A wick through the low.** If price snaps back it may go on to make another high. Wait for a body.
- **A break with no energy.** In Episode 5 viewers criticised him for ignoring a swing low that "looked violated". His answer: the break lacked displacement, so there was no trade ([2022 Ep 5 · 18:06](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1086)).
- **Turtle soup sells.** On a high-resistance day each push above a short-term high looks to other traders like a bull flag or a bullish micro-shift. To ICT it is a run on buy stops: shorts are stopped, breakout longs are trapped, and the sell-off follows. If you are bearish, those small bullish-looking shifts that merely take short-term buy stops are the sells ([2024 #36 · 2:14:03](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=8043)).
- **A shift that fights your bias.** Episode 29 shows a textbook-looking short that he rejected: the FVG was not above 50% of the displacement leg, and his bias was bullish ([2022 Ep 29 · 4:57](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=297)).

- **Ep 12.** The significant break (short-term plus intermediate-term low) is introduced.
- **2024.** At 8:30 on 25 October he defines the shift candle by candle: price breaks above a short-term high, displacement leaves the inefficiency, and a second swing high broken qualifies the shift further. ## Displacement

### What it is
Displacement is the energetic move that makes a break believable. ICT's picture is an elephant dropped into a children's swimming pool: after price runs above an old high and trades back down, you want an obvious, violent move, not a lethargic or anemic one. Only when that happens do you look for the fair value gap and sell the retrace into it ([2022 Ep 5 · 18:06](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1086), [2022 Ep 5 · 18:52](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1132)).

In 2022 the precise form comes in Episode 6. A bearish shift needs the break of the short-term low to be quick: energetic, a real willingness to go lower, preferably a big candle that closes below the low. Two labels then mark the range. The high that ran the liquidity is the **displacement high**. The low, or close, beyond the short-term low is the **displacement low**. The FVG you trade must form in between them ([2022 Ep 6 · 19:55](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1195)).

Later years add framings. They are listed side by side under "How the teaching changed", because they are not identical. What stays constant is that the move is committed, body-led and leaves a gap.

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is that displacement is the signature, the thing that "tips its hand". Having a line or a rectangle at a PD array is not enough; there has to be displacement off the level. The usual order is a run on stops before price trades to the array, then a trade at the array, then a hard drop or rally away. Only then do you wait for the gap or order block that move created ([2024 #33 · 7:46](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=466)). Demanding it keeps you out of choppy trades ([2024 #33 · 32:29](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1949)).

In 2026 the logic is participation. A strong, committed move through structure, with bodies showing aggression and little retracement into the broken level, signals institutional involvement, and the direction it takes is the institutional direction ([2026 #79 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288)).

A related 2024 idea grades how price trades after it breaks a low. If it is lethargic, it may reverse. If it turns quick and heavy, it is going to the next pool of liquidity. Heavy bearish price is a run of candles making lower highs that struggle to make higher candle highs ([2024 #19 · 40:44](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2444), [2024 #19 · 48:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2883)).

### How to spot it
1. **Bodies, not wicks.** Look for a candle or a few candles with committed bodies and little wick, closing beyond the swing. A wick through the level and back is not displacement ([2022 Ep 6 · 19:08](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1148), [2026 #75 · 6:24](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=384)).
2. **It pierces a swing and leaves a gap.** The break pierces the short-term swing, with liquidity taken first, and leaves an imbalance: a sell-side imbalance (SIBI) after a low is pierced, or the bullish equivalent. A candle that merely prints a new low without piercing the prior low is not displacement ([2026 #127 · 58:44](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3524), [2026 #79 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288)).
3. **The next candle keeps going.** Price does not slide straight back into the broken level ([2026 #127 · 1:00:21](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3621)).
4. **Speed builds toward the target.** Once price trades beyond the 50% point of the range from its high to the low it is aiming for, each candle should be lower than the last. There is no fixed number of points: an example was about 25 handles on NQ, and he calls it relative to the range and a knack ([2024 #19 · 46:23](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2783)).

[[DIAGRAM ch02_d3 | caption: an anemic break beside a displacement, with the gap shaded (illustrative prices) | Notice what separates them: the big body that closes beyond the low, and the gap it leaves for price to return to.]]

### How to trade it
- **Displacement is the gate.** Displacement plus FVG is a trade; an anemic break with no FVG is a wait ([2022 Ep 5 · 20:25](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1225)). In 2026 he says no displacement means no signal, and that displacement is needed before an FVG can be called "first presented" (Chapter 8; [2026 #122 · 27:40](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1660)). A tiny first FVG of the day is not a trade unless price shows displacement off it ([2024 #33 · 10:14](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=614)).
- **The gap must sit in the displacement range.** If there is no FVG between the displacement high and low, there is no trade. Wait or look at another market ([2022 Ep 6 · 21:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1289)).
- **Equilibrium filter, a "hard line rule".** Shade the displacement leg and put a Fibonacci on its high and low. 50% is equilibrium. For a short, the FVG entry must be at equilibrium or higher, in premium. He does not want to sell in discount, and says following this alone keeps you out of many ill-fated trades, though not all ([2022 Ep 25 · 36:29](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2189), [2022 Ep 25 · 38:52](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2332)). It ruled out a counter-bias short in Episode 29 ([2022 Ep 29 · 4:57](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=297)). Chapter 4 teaches premium and discount.
- **Longs use the up leg.** Draw the Fibonacci from the low to the high of the impulsive swing in the direction you want to trade; the retracement to and below 50% is the discount. A common error is measuring a high-to-low leg and using its 50% as a discount for a long ([2022 Ep 34 · 15:18](https://youtu.be/t8flQP5jy-Q?t=918), [2022 Ep 34 · 16:04](https://youtu.be/t8flQP5jy-Q?t=964)).
- **Confirmation after entry.** After entering he wants to see displacement; when it comes, he says to sit back and let it work ([2022 Ep 11 · 16:52](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=1012)). After an entry at the open he wants price to expand aggressively, with no dilly-dallying ([2022 Ep 15 · 5:00](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=300)).
- **Sideways price: wait.** When the market goes back into congestion he has no bias and waits for displacement either way to define a new range. After a break lower, look for a retrace above 50% into the 62 to 79% premium; the reverse if it breaks higher ([2022 Ep 19 · 50:52](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=3052), [2022 Ep 19 · 51:41](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=3101)).
- **Do not over-stay.** After a swing high or suspension block, price often displaces sharply. That is the money move, and he warns against staying in a trade that has already displaced a long way ([2026 #119 · 37:26](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=2246)).

### Worked example
**ES, 5-minute chart, a morning session (Episode 5).** He walks through four attempts in order.

1. The first poke above the swing high is followed by a weak, anemic move lower. No trade.
2. A wick snaps back without leaving an FVG. No trade.
3. A matching high is followed by a slight, anemic dip below. No trade.
4. Price trades up again and "smashes down". The next candle closes leaving an FVG. That is the short.

The target was the sell-side liquidity below the swing low formed before 8:30, and price reached it. He adds that patterns sometimes fail, on news or when the market rolls over, which is why the stop and money management exist ([2022 Ep 5 · 20:25](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1225), [2022 Ep 5 · 21:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1275)).

**ES, 5-minute chart, the equilibrium filter (Episode 25).** The displacement leg ran down from the 9:30 high, breaking several short-term lows, so the leg extended to the final low. Its equilibrium was 4044.50. An FVG visible on the 4-minute chart sat below 4044.50, in discount, so it was not valid for a short ([2022 Ep 25 · 36:29](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2189), [2022 Ep 25 · 38:02](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2282)). The anemic-versus-displacement diagram above contrasts the two kinds of break with illustrative prices, not the lecture's charts.

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Calling any break of the low displacement.** Energy is the test, not the break.
- **Engulfing candle.** He asks students not to call the displacing candle an engulfing candle. He describes it as leaving the area where the previous candle closed and opening a gap ([2026 #72 · 16:21](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=981)).
- **A bounce or a wick back.** Displacement shows bodies committed to one side and not retracing immediately into the broken level. A bounce is different ([2026 #79 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=288)).
- **Measuring the wrong leg.** The equilibrium filter uses the displacement leg, and for a long it uses the up leg.

## Three drives, staging and climax: stair-stepped highs

### What it is
These patterns share one picture: price makes a series of highs, each taking out the one before. Retail reads strength. ICT reads buy stops being run, with smart money selling into them.

- **Three drives.** A swing high, a higher swing high, then a higher one again, pressing into an old high on any timeframe. The third high need not take out the old high. He credits Raschke and Connors' *Street Smarts* for the pattern and prefers "three drives" to the book's name ([2022 Ep 5 · 15:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=942), [2022 Ep 5 · 17:19](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1039)).
- **Staging, or engineered liquidity.** Small short-term highs, each taken out only just enough to clear the previous one, with no large expansion candles ([2024 #25 · 32:03](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1923), [2026 #60 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=288)).
- **Capitulation or climax.** A parabolic run of highs, a final blow-off, then a fast reversal. The notes call it his flavor-of-2026 teaching ([2026 #83 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=254)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is that every failed swing high is sold by bears whose buy stops sit above the previous high. Those stops keep getting taken, so liquidity is already being run while smart money builds its shorts ([2022 Ep 5 · 15:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=942)). Each breakout buyer is the other side of that trade ([2024 #25 · 1:31:41](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=5501)). When the buy stops stop being absorbed, the sell is set up. In 2026 he puts it plainly: they run it up, make a short-term high, and that liquidity becomes the counterparty for shorts ([2026 #60 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=288)).

### How to spot it
1. Three ascending swing highs approaching or around an old high or relative equal highs (three drives).
2. Three or four small short-term highs inside a premium, each marginally above the last (staging).
3. A parabolic run with three or more progressively higher highs and sharper reversals, then a hard close below the range (capitulation) ([2026 #78 · 9:31](https://youtu.be/UTrm6uCOUZo?t=571)).
4. If there is no stair-stepping, only one steady run up, anticipate the old high being taken, not necessarily by much, then look for an energetic move lower.

### How to trade it
- **2022: wait for displacement and a gap.** Treat the pattern as liquidity being run, then wait for displacement lower and an FVG to sell ([2022 Ep 5 · 16:31](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=991)).
- **2024: do not sell into the highs.** After the most recent swing high is taken, expect speed away from it. If there is no speed, price will probably keep pressing higher, so do not short. The cue is the failure of the last high plus a break of the prior short-term low ([2024 #25 · 33:43](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2023)).
- **2026: short near the third high.** He describes shorting at or near the third high and targeting the sell-side pools below, with more confidence when it combines with the previous day's high and relative equal lows ([2026 #91 · 13:36](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=816)).
- **Climax.** After a climax spike expect a fast reversal into the gap left behind, with entries refined by FVG and CE rules (Chapter 6). The notes say this trade needs 1 to 2 years of experience and that beginners should focus on longs ([2026 #83 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=254)).
- **Shorting a bull market.** A new trader should not; study, paper trade and tape read first. If you must trade against the trend, be highly selective and take profits early ([2026 #86 · 12:09](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=729), [2026 #86 · 22:27](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1347)).

### Worked example
**NQ, 24 April 2026.** Price had been trending higher, making multiple highs. Thursday's high traded above Wednesday's daily high, which confirmed three drives to him. He shorted at Thursday's high with relative equal lows below as the target, and says it worked ([2026 #91 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=672), [2026 #91 · 12:47](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=767)). His 2022 picture is an ES 5-minute chart with an old high near 4514.50 (the caption is unclear) matched by a later high and several rising short-term highs ([2022 Ep 5 · 15:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=942)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Reading three lows as a reversal pattern.** Retail treats three consecutive lows, or momentum divergence on RSI, CCI, MACD or stochastics, as a reversal signal. ICT says the bodies said nothing had changed, and that he lost money on divergence in 1992 to 1994 ([2024 #17 · 49:27](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2967)).
- **Chasing parabolic moves.** In 2026 he says that when markets turn parabolic and everyone is excited, the top is near. Gold and silver are his examples; after capitulation, wait for structure to reset ([2026 #84 · 6:24](https://youtu.be/dwMgP2zkH0w?t=384)).

### How the teaching changed
2022 asks for displacement and an FVG after the pattern. 2024 adds the engineered-liquidity view and the warning not to sell into the highs. 2026 describes shorting at or near the third high and adds climax and capitulation. One waits for the gap, the other describes entering near the high; the notes do not say he withdrew either.

## The Judas swing as a structure trap
The Judas swing is the false first move that runs one side's stops before the real move. Chapter 13 teaches it as part of the daily template; here is only its link to structure. It is the liquidity run that comes before a shift. When he is bearish, the 8:30 to 9:30 hour is expected to run higher to set up shorts ([2022 Ep 25 · 34:11](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2051)), and in 2024 he calls the first side taken the trap and says not to trade it ([2024 #11 · 1:53:43](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=6823)).

The 2026 notes describe it in several ways:

- a one-directional run at the 9:30 open to a short-term high, then a sharp reversal ([2026 #54 · 9:51](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=591));
- a rally or spike, often news-driven, that lures buyers before a sharp reversal, to be faded ([2026 #75 · 11:53](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=713));
- a break of a level by one tick or several, then a sharp reversal ([2026 #114 · 26:58](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1618));
- a brief counter-move into a bullish order block, read as confirmation of the original direction and a possible point to add ([2026 #90 · 8:46](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=526)).

In every version a false move comes first and the real move follows. Chapter 13 sorts out when he uses each.

## Structure frames the idea, narrative decides
Market structure is not the answer. It frames an idea, and the idea must fit the day's narrative: the climate, the economic calendar, and the volatility on offer ([2022 Ep 22 · 12:04](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=724)). In 2026 he puts it more bluntly: "Narrative trumps market structure." Time distortion, where the algorithm holds price in consolidation or delays delivery until the right time, can break the current structure, so moves appear to come from nowhere for people who only use structure ([2026 #141 · 39:11](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2351)). It is all a derivative of time first, then price ([2026 #141 · 40:00](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2400)). That does not make structure useless; it makes it secondary to the draw and the clock (Chapters 1 and 15).

When swings disagree across markets, precision drops. If NQ, ES and YM make different swings, ICT reduces size and does not trade with high conviction until they agree (a failure-swing divergence; Chapter 15) ([2026 #106 · 7:17](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=437)).

## Check yourself

1. On a 1-minute chart three consecutive candles have a high, a higher high and a lower high. When can you call the middle one a swing high, and why does ICT not wait for five candles?
2. Swing highs left to right: 10, 14, 11, 20, 12, 16, 13 (illustrative). Which are intermediate-term highs, and which one is long-term?
3. Price falls to fill a bullish FVG and turns up. What rank does ICT give that low, and what should happen to it if the leg is genuine?
4. In a bearish idea, price fills a gap and makes a high that is below the short-term high on its left. What does ICT read, and what does retail see?
5. Price pokes a few ticks above relative equal highs, then slides under the last swing low on small, overlapping candles, leaving no gap. Is this a market structure shift worth trading?
6. Episode 3 says the shift candle need not close beyond the swing; Episode 6 prefers a close. How do you reconcile the two, and which carries more weight?
7. A displacement leg runs from 4,060 down to 4,030 (illustrative). An FVG sits at 4,042. Can you short it, and why?
8. You are short after a shift and take a partial at the first target. Price pulls back. Where does ICT say to put the stop, and when may you roll it?
9. Your chart shows a textbook bearish shift, but your daily bias is bullish. What do Episodes 22 and 29 and the 2026 teaching say?

## Answers

1. After the third candle closes. A five-candle fractal confirms two candles later, and by then he says you have missed the move. Reread "Swing highs and swing lows".
2. The 14 and the 16 are intermediate-term (each has lower short-term highs on both sides). The 20 is long-term, because the intermediate-term highs on both sides (14 and 16) are lower. Reread "Short-, intermediate- and long-term swings".
3. An intermediate-term low, by the rebalance rule. If the leg is genuine, it should not be taken until the objective is reached. Reread the same section.
4. A weak ITH: only rebalancing, a very weak market, expect the break lower. Retail sees a pennant or bull flag, which he calls a false bull flag and fades. Reread "The weak intermediate-term high".
5. No. The liquidity run happened, but the break lacked displacement and left no FVG, so it is a wait. Reread "Displacement".
6. Both are on record: Ep 3 says the candle need not close beyond, Ep 6 prefers a big body closing beyond, and 2026 stresses the close. The close carries more weight. Reread "How the teaching changed" under the shift section.
7. No. Equilibrium is 4,045, so 4,042 is in discount, and a short FVG must be at or above equilibrium (his hard line rule). Reread "How to trade it" under Displacement.
8. Leave it where it was, above the ITH. Roll it only after a significant intermediate-term low on the 1-minute chart is taken, which is also a full target. Reread the swings section, "How to trade it".
9. Structure only frames the idea; the narrative decides. With a bullish bias he had no interest in a counter-bias short, even with a gap and a break. Reread "Structure frames the idea, narrative decides".
