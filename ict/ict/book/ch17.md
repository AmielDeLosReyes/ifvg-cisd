An ICT model is a complete trade recipe: a time window, a reason price should move, a trigger, an entry, a stop and a target. This chapter assembles the models ICT has named between the 2022 Mentorship and the 2026 lectures, each as a step-by-step sequence with its time window and a worked example, and it says plainly where the notes give only fragments. After reading you can tell which model you are looking at, what must happen before you act, and what turns it into a "no trade". The building blocks (liquidity, FVGs, order blocks, sessions) are defined in the concept chapters and linked here, not re-taught.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch17_d1 | caption: The seven-step skeleton shared by every model in this chapter | The models differ most in the trigger (shaded) and in the clock window, not in the order of the steps.]]

- **A model is a catalyst, not a pattern.** ICT defines an entry model as the thing that puts you in the trade given your directional expectation, with a reward that is a multiple greater than one of what you risk ([2024 #2 · 2:03:46](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7426)).
- **The core 2022 sequence:** price runs an old high or low, then displaces through the nearest short-term swing the other way, leaving a fair value gap. You enter on the retrace into that gap. No displacement through the swing means no setup ([2022 Ep 24 · 33:16](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1996)).
- **Many later models are that sequence with a different clock or a different draw.** The Silver Bullet fixes the window at 10:00 to 11:00. Caleb's 2024 model fixes the draw on opening gaps. Turtle soup names the first step.
- **The market maker buy and sell models are maps of a whole move.** The advice is to act on the second stage, not the first ([2026 #15 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1392)).
- **Pick one and keep it.** A model that gives no setup today is not worse than one that did. Grabbing a "right-now model" to justify getting in is what blows accounts ([2024 #17 · 2:23:58](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=8638)).
- **The 2026 names (Obsidian, Enigma, chain of custody, time and price grids) mostly add measurement and validation.** They grade where and when the reaction should occur. They do not replace the trigger and entry.

## What counts as an ICT model

### What it is

In ICT's definition an entry model is the catalyst for the trade: "the thing you're betting on panning out". Relative to your stop you need a multiple greater than one. You do not need 2:1 or 5:1, only reasonable, anticipatable fluctuations ([2024 #2 · 2:03:46](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7426)). Journaling, he says, makes your eye gravitate to one entry model, whether that is breakers, FVGs, inversion FVGs (iFVGs) or mitigation blocks ([2024 #2 · 2:02:59](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7379)).

He also names three constants under every model: time; why price should move, or why it moved; and price going up to an inefficiency or buy-side liquidity, or down to an inefficiency or sell-side liquidity. The captions for this passage are partly unclear ([2024 #38 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4426)). Time comes first: he calls it the first aspect of algorithmic price delivery, which is why a model that must form inside a fixed window, such as 10:00 to 11:00, forces accountability. If it does not form, you do not trade ([2024 #49 · 1:43:15](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6195), [2024 #49 · 20:34](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1234)).

### The skeleton every model follows

Read each model below against these seven steps. The models differ in what fills each slot, and several notes leave a slot empty. I mark those as "not stated".

1. **Context.** The clock window and a directional idea with a draw: an old high or low, or a gap price should reach (Chapters 15 and 12).
2. **Setup.** Liquidity gets run: an old high or low, relative equal highs or lows, or the first side of the opening range (Chapter 3).
3. **Trigger.** Price displaces the other way through a swing, or flips the state of delivery. This leaves a gap or a block (Chapters 2 and 9).
4. **Entry.** A limit order at a PD array formed by that move: an FVG, an order block, an iFVG or the wick CE (Chapters 5 and 18).
5. **Stop.** Beyond the swing or beyond the gap-forming candle (Chapter 19).
6. **Target.** The next pool or gap, often a "low-hanging fruit" first and a larger extreme second.
7. **Management.** Partials at the first target, no tight trailing, and stopping for the day once the objective is met ([2024 #47 · 18:14](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=1094)).

### How many models, and how to choose

ICT started the 2022 YouTube Mentorship with a deliberate cut: one simple model with few moving parts, built so that "a model my daughter could look at" would not drown in options. His advanced private-mentorship material, he said, produces strong traders but leaves most students in analysis paralysis ([2022 Ep 1 · 2:38](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=158), [2022 Ep 1 · 3:26](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=206)). He adds that if he started over he would use only that model ([2022 Ep 18 · 5:37](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=337)).

By 2024 he describes a larger toolbox. He keeps separate models for choppy markets, for trends (three or four of them), for reversals, for the market maker buy and sell models, and for intraday volatility, which is Enigma ([2024 #17 · 1:16:53](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=4613)). He says the paid mentorship holds about 12 models, two of them easy bread-and-butter ones and the rest rule-heavy and demanding a lot of waiting ([2024 #49 · 1:44:04](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6244)). He counts 81 named items in total and says the names are for his own tracking, not a reason to adopt one as a pet technique ([2024 #1 · 1:08:27](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4107)).

His selection rules are blunt. Pick one model and do not swap it because another would have worked today. A model that does not fire earns you no commission, no loss and practised patience ([2024 #17 · 1:26:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=5179)). The same lecture traces a chain of teaching models: OTE (his flagship for years), then the 2022 model, then the Silver Bullet, then new-day and new-week opening gaps plus an FVG as Caleb's model ([2024 #17 · 1:17:40](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=4660)).

You can also stack them. In one morning drop he pointed to an OTE, a 2022-model entry, a Silver Bullet and an institutional order flow entry drill. Entering on one and adding on a lower one is allowed, but pyramiding shifts your average price toward the target and can leave heavy early size underwater ([2024 #19 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2247), [2024 #19 · 55:15](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3315)).

Two of the names you may meet are not explained in the notes this chapter draws on. "Reaper" FVG is mentioned without a definition, and "Hangman PD array" appears only in a video title. "Zircon" does not appear at all. They are left out rather than guessed at.

## The 2022 Mentorship model

### What it is

The 2022 model is the first model ICT taught on YouTube. It has one idea at its centre: a run on liquidity, a displacement that breaks the nearest short-term swing the other way, and a fair value gap left behind that you trade back into. He taught it on index futures (ES and NQ). He says the concepts scale to any timeframe and that forex traders can use them ([2022 Ep 1 · 4:12](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=252)). Later he calls it the simplest way to work intraday and daily order flow, usable in forex and bonds and, with caveats, gold ([2022 Ep 18 · 6:25](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=385)).

For gold, that is the whole instruction in the notes. The clock times below (8:30 data, 9:30 equity open) are index-futures times. The notes do not say how he would shift them for a gold CFD.

The model goes by several names: the 2022 model, "Model 2022", the "YouTube model" and, in 2024 and 2026, simply the thing you do after a high-then-low sequence. A repeating "signature" is what he asks you to learn: price creates an FVG, breaks a low (or high), comes back and fills the gap, partly, halfway or completely ([2022 Ep 19 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=389)).

### The sequence (bearish; reverse everything for longs)

[[DIAGRAM ch17_d2 | caption: The 2022 model, bearish version (illustrative candles, no prices) | The short-term low is the trigger: only a displacement leg through it makes the gap worth selling.]]

1. **Context.** Know the time and have a directional idea. Set TradingView to New York time so daylight saving never confuses the clock ([2022 Ep 21 · 11:14](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=674)). Your bias should be "corrected and calibrated" by what price did at key times such as 9:30 ([2022 Ep 38 · 42:46](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2566)).
2. **Setup.** Price runs above an old high or relative equal highs (REH). The model forms after this run ([2022 Ep 24 · 31:41](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1901)).
3. **Trigger.** As soon as price trades above the highs, scan back through the run-up and find the nearest short-term low. Price must break below it with displacement. That energetic leg is "the foundation of the setup". If price merely drifts back under the highs, "there's no setup there" ([2022 Ep 24 · 33:16](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1996), [2022 Ep 24 · 34:03](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2043)).
4. **Entry.** Find the FVG inside the displacement leg and sell when price retraces into it. In his Feb 3, 2022 example the limit sat one tick above candle 3's high, the near edge of a bearish gap ([2022 Ep 6 · 35:06](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2106)). A partial fill is acceptable: an "average" entry with sound money management is enough ([2022 Ep 19 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=389)).
5. **Stop.** Above candle 2 or above candle 1 (the swing high) of the gap pattern ([2022 Ep 6 · 35:06](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2106)). On the long side he put it about one tick below the low candle of the move ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925)).
6. **Target.** In the Feb 3 example: first partial at the 50% of the move where a bullish FVG sat in discount, then the relative equal lows with sell stops below ([2022 Ep 6 · 38:14](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2294)). For longs: the short-term high or buy-side liquidity (BSL) ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925)).
7. **Management.** Build size with the largest piece first (see "Position building" below). Do not trail stops tightly: the retracement that shook out tight trailers in the Feb 3 example came before the lows were taken ([2022 Ep 6 · 38:14](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2294)).

If you read the model as "high, higher high, failure, break of the swing low, first FVG", you have it. He adds that the retrace into the gap typically lands near 50% of the parent swing, the whole swing from the low to the high that produced the gap ([2022 Ep 19 · 13:38](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=818)). He stresses that the volume of the move is in the candle bodies, so you let wicks poke through the gap ([2022 Ep 19 · 15:14](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=914)).

### The second entry strategy: raiding an old FVG

ICT calls the REH version and this one "the two entry strategies for this model". The level that gets raided can be an old opposing FVG instead of equal highs. Price trades up into an old FVG overhead, and it does not need to close the gap: anywhere between its low and high is good. Simplify by treating the low of that FVG exactly like the line of relative equal highs. Once price trades above it, go back through the up-leg, find the short-term low trigger, and wait for it to break with displacement. The new FVG in that leg is what you sell ([2022 Ep 24 · 35:37](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2137), [2022 Ep 24 · 36:24](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2184)). For longs, the high of an old FVG below price plays the part of the relative equal lows.

### Time window

ICT ties the model to clock times, all in New York time.

- **8:30.** Look to the left, before 8:30, for a short-term high. After 8:30, price rallies above the 8:30 opening price and runs those stops, breaks down with displacement and an imbalance, then rallies back into it. That retrace is the entry. On the day he restated this the model did not form on the 5-minute chart, because price never rallied above the 8:30 open ([2022 Ep 21 · 21:35](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1295)).
- **9:30.** At the equity open he runs a four-question check on the 15-minute chart. Did it take a high out? Did it break a low? Was that displacement? Did it trade back up into the imbalance? If all four are yes, you can sell, and a swing trader can aim for the old daily relative equal lows ([2022 Ep 22 · 9:38](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=578), [2022 Ep 22 · 10:25](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=625)).
- **Other windows.** A 1:30 PM and a 3:00 to 4:00 PM setup appear in the 5-point model below.
- **Chart choice.** He asked students to check the 5-, 4-, 3-, 2- and 1-minute charts for homework and used the 2-minute chart that day ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925)). He also says staying on the 15-minute chart and trading the daily range is fine, because everything is fractal ([2022 Ep 16 · 47:05](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2825)). Your style (scalper to position trader) decides how far you stretch the timeframe ([2022 Ep 11 · 24:49](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=1489)).

### Worked example 1: NQ short, February 3, 2022

Instrument: NQ March 2022, with a bearish daily idea because price had already risen a lot. Chart: 15-minute for the level, 1-minute for the trade. He drew a line at the first swing high to the left of 8:30 ([2022 Ep 6 · 32:36](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1956)).

1. After 8:30 an early run above a short-term high left an FVG and sold off. A scalper could take that. For the daily range he wanted the 15-minute high instead.
2. At 9:30 price turned volatile, making a low and another low. These build sell stops while the highs stay intact.
3. A shallow poke above the 15-minute high produced overlapping candles, no FVG and no swing low taken. His verdict: nothing there ([2022 Ep 6 · 34:18](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2058)).
4. Then price made a higher high above the line and broke the nearest swing low. The candles between the highest high and that break (candles 1, 2 and 3) left a bearish FVG ([2022 Ep 6 · 35:06](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2106)).
5. Entry was a limit one tick above candle 3's high. Price returned into the gap three times, the third nearly filling it, and each return felt as if it would keep rising.
6. Targets: the 50% of the entry-to-swing range, with a bullish FVG below in discount (first partial), then the relative equal lows. About 60 handles came in roughly 11 one-minute candles. His paper trade was short 2 NQ minis at 14,792.50, a partial at 14,675 and a limit exit at 14,647 ([2022 Ep 6 · 43:24](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2604)).

### Worked example 2: NQ long, timed with ES

The sequence on the long side: price trades below an old low (sell-side liquidity, SSL, taken), displaces up and breaks a swing high (a bullish market structure shift), and you look back through the up-leg for an FVG. If price keeps going lower after your entry, "that's what your stop is for" ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925)).

On the NQ 2-minute chart the old low was the one formed before 8:30, and the 10:04 candle was the low, so the stop went about one tick under it. NQ showed no FVG at entry time. ICT went long at 14,505.5, near a bullish order block, and exited at 14,622.75 above the 14,620 high: about 117 handles on one mini ([2022 Ep 7 · 20:07](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1207), [2022 Ep 7 · 37:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2278)).

The trick: ES had the clean signal. It traded below the old low, displaced up, broke a swing high and left an FVG, and the 10:36 candle traded down into it. He used that timing to buy NQ. His summary: "trading what's not in the chart" ([2022 Ep 7 · 18:34](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1114)). The notes do not give the date of this session.

### Position building: largest piece first

On April 29, 2022 ICT sold NQ in three steps and covered all six contracts at 13,285.00. He sold 3 at the midpoint of a 1-minute order block, 2 in the FVG just after it filled (a "close proximity entry"), and 1 when price retraced back into the bearish order block. The last add had only 4.5 handles of heat (13,335.00 entry, 13,339.50 worst). He calls the whole sequence a market maker sell model ([2022 Ep 22 · 32:58](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1978), [2022 Ep 22 · 34:33](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2073), [2022 Ep 22 · 36:59](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2219)). The logic is that the equity built on the first contracts makes the small add's heat insignificant. He never keeps adding one more contract. In 2024 the same habit appears as six contracts at the best price and four later ([2024 #3 · 1:50:23](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6623)). He adds that many trades in his statements use close-proximity entries and other methods he has not taught publicly, so do not judge his precision from them.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Selling every pullback under the highs.** The nearest short-term low must break with displacement. A dip back under the highs is not the trigger ([2022 Ep 24 · 34:03](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2043)).
- **Taking setups in the middle of the range.** ICT puts the probability at 50/50 when price trades back into the middle of the range. In one example an FVG "should have offered more movement lower" but did not properly take out the short-term low before rallying ([2022 Ep 32 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=254), [2022 Ep 32 · 5:02](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=302)).
- **Forcing a setup when the market is not delivering that way.** A sell-off can happen with no model setup. He reads that as a market that is not likely to deliver in that direction, and the real move may come the other way, where setups do form ([2022 Ep 30 · 7:19](https://youtu.be/CL94EnGqB4Q?t=439)).
- **Demanding every setup.** He counted eight tradeable setups inside one move and said nobody needs them all. Each could be the one that fits your own model ([2022 Ep 23 · 9:37](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=577), [2022 Ep 23 · 10:23](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=623)).
- **Looking for it in your own chart only.** A correlated index can print the FVG while yours does not (the ES/NQ example above).

### Stacked FVGs: allow for the second one

In 2024 he added a refinement and labelled it "model 2022" logic. When a small FVG sits just above a lower one, expect price to reach into the upper one before you decide the entry failed ([2024 #31 · 20:59](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1259)). A stop just above the first gap's candle high can be hit when price wicks up to the second gap. He gives two approaches: enter the first FVG with a wider stop that clears the second, or wait for price to trade into the upper FVG and use that as the entry with a somewhat larger stop. The ideal is that candle bodies stay in the lower half of the upper gap while only a wick pokes into it ([2024 #40 · 57:26](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3446), [2024 #40 · 58:14](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3494), [2024 #40 · 1:38:40](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5920)). A third FVG further up is a "breakaway gap": it shows heaviness, and price does not need to go to it ([2024 #40 · 1:37:53](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5873)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** the model is an FVG trade. You use the gap and move on.
- **2024:** the model is a reference point inside bigger trades. A 2022-model entry is one of several valid entries in a single move ([2024 #19 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2247)).
- **2026:** he now says to keep and project the first presented FVG after the failure. In his words this is new: in Model 2022 as originally taught you did not keep that gap, and the "revelation" is to extend it into the future. The shape he describes is a short-term high, a higher short-term high above it that rejects, then a break of the swing low ([2026 #140 · 7:58](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=478), [2026 #140 · 26:54](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1614)). On January 2, 2026 the short-term high was the 9:48 candle, whose neighbours both had lower highs. He says he uses it every day, several times, on lower timeframes. In a PM drop he also calls an inversion FVG "model 2022 on a very small time frame" ([2026 #141 · 42:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2544)).
- **Loose naming in 2026.** One February 2026 lecture describes Model 2022 as a candle pattern: a reversal candle, then a confirming up-close, with the stop below the reversal candle ([2026 #130 · 16:53](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=1013)). A later 2026 review (undated in the notes) calls a break-and-retest of a swing low "Model 2022 / OTE" ([2026 #66 · 10:26](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=626)). Both look like loose shorthand. The precise 2022 definition is the one in the sequence above.

## The 5-point intraday model (2022, built for his son)

### What it is

Late in the 2022 series ICT presented "the model I teach my son". It is the same raid, displacement and gap logic as the main 2022 model, with a clock attached and a deliberately tiny profit target. He built it around his son's short attention span and chose index futures because there are no spread problems as in forex, the market is professional and liquid, and delivery is systematic ([2022 Ep 39 · 39:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2360)). Do not confuse it with the 2024 "Caleb's model" later in this chapter, which has a different draw and entry.

### The sequence (bearish at 8:30; mirror for buys)

1. **Context.** Time first. Set the chart clock to New York time and know the four windows: 8:30 (the news embargo lifts, the first time element), 9:30 (equities open), 1:30 PM (the PM session after lunch) and 3:00 to 4:00 PM (the "market on close macro", which he says forms a small setup most days). The draw on liquidity must be clear, buy-side above or sell-side below. This is not trading a level to watch it repel ([2022 Ep 39 · 39:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2360)).
2. **Setup.** At 8:30 on a bearish day, price sits above the midnight opening price. It runs a short-term high (the buy stops of 1-minute traders), so it is now at a premium ([2022 Ep 39 · 46:36](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2796)).
3. **Trigger.** Price trades lower through a short-term low and leaves an FVG.
4. **Entry.** Price rallies back up into the FVG and you sell short there ([2022 Ep 39 · 47:23](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2843)).
5. **Stop.** Not stated in the notes for this model.
6. **Target.** Exactly 5 ES points, then out, even when the framework would allow 30. Afterwards watch whether price reaches the liquidity pool (old lows or relative equal lows; in the example the 4100 big figure). He says that later he will take a partial at 5 and hold the rest for the pool.
7. **Management.** Count your trades (below).

His reasoning for 5 points: it is easy, and it is the lowest threshold a setup should yield. Less than that is ultra-short-term scalping where commissions kill you. Even on a choppy day an experienced trader can reasonably take 5 points once in the morning and once in the afternoon.

### Trade-count rules

- **Morning:** one trade, at 8:30 or at 9:30. If he gets his 5 points at 8:30 he does not trade 9:30. If he misses 8:30 he tries 9:30. A loss at 8:30 may be made back at 9:30.
- **Afternoon (1:30):** traded live only if he missed the 9:30 setup or took a morning loss. If the morning was profitable, the afternoon is demo or paper only. Not pushing the edge teaches discipline, not greed.
- **In total:** up to three opportunities a day, but he looks for just one to yield 5 points ([2022 Ep 39 · 1:06:24](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=3984)).

Earlier in the series he made his son take a trade at every window (8:30, 9:30, PM and sometimes 3:00 to 4:00) as practice.

### Worked example (ES; the date is not given in the notes)

At 8:30 ES was above the midnight open and ran a short-term high. It then traded lower through a short-term low, leaving an FVG, rallied into it, and sold toward the sell-side pool at 4100/4101. At 1:30 PM buy stops were taken. Price broke down, returned to a bearish breaker, displaced lower and broke relative equal lows ("this low, this low and this low"), leaving an imbalance. Price traded back up into it, and he sold for 5 points, then watched for 4070 and the daily FVG ([2022 Ep 39 · 46:36](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2796), [2022 Ep 39 · 1:10:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4220)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading the 5 points as a promise.** ICT says his son's account grew from about $2,810 to about $18,300 in roughly four weeks of live trading. He stresses that he co-piloted minute by minute and that you should not quit your job on it ([2022 Ep 39 · 39:20](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2360)). Treat it as his claim, not evidence for your account.

## OTE and the other ways to enter the same idea

### What it is

OTE is the optimal trade entry: the 62 to 79% retracement zone of a swing (Chapter 4 teaches it). In the 2022 series ICT used it as one of several ways to enter one bullish idea. In 2024 he calls it the flagship pattern of his teaching for about ten years ([2024 #19 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2247)).

### The sequence (bullish)

1. **Context.** A bias that has been "corrected and calibrated" by what price did at key times such as 9:30 ([2022 Ep 38 · 42:46](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2566)).
2. **Setup.** A low is taken, in the turtle-soup variant by a lower low that violates it.
3. **Trigger.** A swing from the low up to a high, which gives you the range to measure.
4. **Entry.** He offers four ways, in answer to "which FVG do I use?":
    1. **OTE:** fib from the low to the high and buy the 62 to 79% retracement.
    2. **Turtle soup:** a low, then a lower low violating it, with the OTE measured on top of that.
    3. **FVG in discount:** from the low to the high, find the FVG below the 50% level and buy when price trades to the top of it.
    4. **Buy stop above the swing high:** after price trades into the FVG, place a buy stop above the swing high formed just before price entered the gap. This is "buying strength". It is not his preferred strategy.
5. **Stop.** For variant 3, below the lower low. For variant 4, just below the lowest low after the order triggers ([2022 Ep 38 · 42:46](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2566), [2022 Ep 38 · 43:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2613)). Not stated for variants 1 and 2.
6. **Target.** Not stated in this lecture. A 2026 review describes targets as higher-level inefficiencies or old highs ([2026 #66 · 11:13](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=673)).
7. **Management.** Not stated.

### Worked example (described, no prices)

In a 2026 review ICT describes an OTE like this. Find a swing low, wait for price to trade through it, then watch price come back and test that same level. Enter long at or just above the swing low on the retest, with the stop below the swing low. The review's example: a swing low was broken on a Monday, price returned to test it, and the low-hanging objectives were the first gap above and then higher inefficiencies. The notes call it his oldest, simplest model, usable on any timeframe ([2026 #66 · 10:26](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=626)). This break-and-retest wording differs from the fib-based OTE of 2022. Use the fib definition from Chapter 4 as the baseline, and expect a retest of a broken low to be the same idea described loosely.

### Stacking with other entries

Because OTE, the 2022 model, the Silver Bullet and the institutional order flow entry drill can all appear in one drop, he treats them as stackable. Enter on a higher one and add on a lower one. The average price moves toward the target as you add, so size the adds carefully ([2024 #19 · 55:15](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3315)).

- [2022 Ep 38 · 43:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2613): the buy stop above the swing high.

## Turtle soup

### What it is

Turtle soup is ICT's name for a run on liquidity that fails: a fake break below an old low that then rallies, or a fake break above an old high that then drops ([2022 Ep 7 · 25:09](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1509)). The name comes from Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' book *Street Smarts*, which he says introduced him to stop runs (he bought it in the mid-1990s and has used the concept since 1995). That book's version fades a break of the 20-day high or low, against the trend-following Turtles of Richard Dennis. ICT says he took the name and the stop-hunt idea, not the rules, and that he counts candles and structure, not days ([2024 #5 · 5:20](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=320), [2024 #5 · 7:49](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=469), [2024 #37 · 34:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2051)).

He presents it as a way to find where retail is pegged. Resistance gets marked, breakout traders buy above it, and price then reverses toward the other side. What separates it from a strong push is what happens next: a "nitrous oxide" push through the level keeps going, while the fake-out goes right back in ([2024 #5 · 51:33](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3093)).

### The two forms (2024)

By August 2024 he narrows the definition. Turtle soup is a run on liquidity that is not always above an old high or below an old low, and there are only two forms ([2024 #8 · 3:33:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=12807)).

[[DIAGRAM ch17_d3 | caption: The two forms of turtle soup on one sell program (illustrative candles, no prices) | Form 1 is the reversal: the highs are run, an objective is reached, and the state of delivery flips. Form 2 is the entry inside the sell program that follows.]]

1. **Form 1: a reversal that changes the direction of delivery.** Take out the buy stops above old highs (bodies as well as wicks), hit an objective such as a new week or new day opening gap that acts as a magnet, then show a change in the state of delivery (CISD, sustained for a session, a day or longer). The order block or breaker is part of the reversal. In his words, breakers tend to be turtle soups in changing, sustained price runs ([2024 #8 · 2:22:04](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8524)).
2. **Form 2: an entry mechanism inside a program already unfolding.** Every short in a bearish FVG is a sale above an old high, namely the candle high that marks the gap's edge. Every long in a bullish FVG is a purchase below an old low. This is not support and resistance but an absence of buyers who were ever afforded the chance above that candle's high ([2024 #8 · 2:46:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9965)).

He also names a harder variant: selling into a rally, into a premium array, on a bold bullish candle above short-term highs. It needs much more experience and complementary PD arrays, and he says not to start there.

### The sequence (reversal form, bearish)

1. **Context.** A higher-timeframe narrative with a draw below. In his September 5 example price sat inside a daily bearish FVG, with an older daily FVG and sell-side liquidity below ([2024 #24 · 2:35:27](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9327)).
2. **Setup.** A short-term high is run at the right time of day. Choose the zone before price gets there: in his September 5 example it was above the Wednesday daily high (the lower edge of the daily bearish FVG) and below that FVG's upper edge.
3. **Trigger.** The high is pierced, an objective is reached, and delivery flips (CISD). A wick above the short-term high into the premium of the higher-timeframe FVG is the piercing in his execution.
4. **Entry.** Sell as price retraces into the FVG left by the drop, or on the piercing wick itself when the plan allows it. Place the limit at the near edge of the gap ([2024 #8 · 2:11:46](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7906) for the long mirror).
5. **Stop.** Above the three-candle FVG pattern. It is too far if price trades above candle 1's high. The tight, advanced version is the wick CE plus 2 ticks ([2024 #8 · 3:04:43](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11083)). In the live trade below the stop distance is not given.
6. **Target.** The opposite extreme: the sell side below, the daily FVG, its low or its CE.
7. **Management.** Adds on the wick are allowed (pyramiding). Do not trail the stop on pyramided adds ([2024 #24 · 1:01:43](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3703)). Sit still after entry ([2024 #24 · 1:00:07](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3607)).

### Worked example 1: NQ, August 14, 2024 (both forms)

Price ran above short-term highs and relative equal highs into the July 28 new week opening gap (NWOG), then showed a CISD at the open of the order block. That was form 1. After it, he shorted into the FVG that formed in the sell leg. That was form 2. He adds that if price had reached the NWOG on the 8:30 first leg, then poked above again at 9:30 and failed, that second poke would have been the turtle soup ([2024 #8 · 1:10:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4228)).

The same lecture contains a lesson on entries. On the first retracement price stopped within a tick of the FVG and fell away, so even a limit one tick above the old candle high was not filled. He called it punishment for trying to be too perfect and recommended fallback entries (the wick CE, the next array) ([2024 #8 · 11:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=676)).

### Worked example 2: NQ, September 5 to 6, 2024

Instrument and context: NQ on Thursday, September 5. Price sat above the Wednesday daily high, inside a daily bearish FVG. After the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range, it traded up into a 1-hour and 15-minute FVG and wicked above the high. ### The 2026 variants

- **A two-stage buy-side pool (April 14, 2026, Asian session).** Price runs above two buy-side pools, then reverses. The notes' wording is loose. Entry is above the second pool or in the "soup" zone between the pools, with the sell side below as the target. ICT says it works best inside macro windows, and may fail in a very strong bull market ([2026 #100 · 3:26](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=206)).
- **The first-hour range (March 28, 2026).** After a one-sided first-hour range, breakout sellers are enticed to chase the break of support. A shallow run below the low, then back inside, is the turtle soup. He called it live. His line: "we're flipping retail logic upside down and attacking them with it" ([2026 #113 · 53:06](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3186)).
- **A false breakout at a high (April 6, 2026).** A break above a relative equal high or resistance that fails back into the range. The notes' version: once a candle has closed above resistance and the next few close back inside, short the breakout, with the stop above the breakout high and the cover at the range midpoint or prior lows. It works best in consolidating, non-trending markets ([2026 #112 · 26:26](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=1586)).
- **A low that fails (March 9, 2026, ES).** Price traded below an earlier low and bounced straight back up. His reading: do not short below the low while it is being tested. If it fails, trade long above it, with the stop below the low just tested and the target at buy-side liquidity or the next key level ([2026 #123 · 27:47](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1667)).
- **"To it and through it" (April 15, 2026).** When price sits just under relative equal highs after sell-side was already run, a move through the highs should accelerate. A shallow peek above and a fall back inside is a false breakout and points to an intraday reversal. If it then rolls over and takes the low, he stands aside for the rest of the day ([2026 #101 · 1:25:37](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5137), [2026 #101 · 1:26:24](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5184)).
- **Taking profit on one.** On February 28, 2026 he closed a long when price bumped the low of a daily volume imbalance. He expected the run into the buy side there to be a turtle soup (a fake-out), so he did not wait for full gap closure ([2026 #126 · 1:13:24](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=4404)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using the book's rules.** ICT calls the Street Smarts version (fade a 20-day break) infantile and says he does not trade it ([2024 #8 · 2:24:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=8667)). In 2026 he repeats that his turtle soup is not the book's, and the notes tie it to two-stage liquidity runs and macro windows ([2026 #102 · 16:15](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=975)).
- **Candle range theory.** He dismisses this popular online version of turtle soup as worthless and claims his application is the highest tier of the idea ([2024 #24 · 2:33:05](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9185)).
- **Selling the poke itself.** The simplified 2026 summary says to short the breakout candle. In his fuller lectures the entry waits for displacement and an objective. If the high is the high of the day or a turtle soup, price must displace lower and also reach the higher-timeframe PD array, here the daily volume imbalance ([2026 #127 · 45:20](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2720)).
- **Turtle soup in a strong trend.** The poke can simply keep going. He says to identify the market's bias first ([2026 #100 · 3:26](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=206)).
- **Treating a stop-out as a model failure.** A first 2022-model or turtle-soup entry stopping out is a normal expectation. He would size it as one micro on demo ([2026 #127 · 44:33](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2673)).

### Upside frame: the quarter of the range

To frame a possible turtle soup above a broken high, he uses a rule of thumb: project one quarter of the previous range beyond the broken high. He says it is not from any technical book, only something he has used for years. On the December 2024 NQ example the Sunday video stated 21,580.25. Price made a higher high on Thursday but stopped just short (about 21,567.25, caption unclear). He said it does not need to be hit, and it could be run later ([2024 #51 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=100), [2024 #51 · 2:27](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=147)). The same −0.25 extension appears in the strongest beginner sequence below.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** a one-line definition. Run beyond an old high or low, then reverse. It is a way to find the setup, with the FVG still doing the entering.
- **2024:** a history lesson in August, then two forms (reversal and entry), then in September a statement that it is both a reversal and a continuation pattern, entered with an FVG and aimed at the opposite extreme.
- **2026:** the term stretches. It covers two-stage pools, first-hour ranges and "the first FVG after a turtle soup" (see "The first presented FVG"). ICT repeats that it is his own evolved version.

## The Silver Bullet (the 10:00 hour)

### What it is

The Silver Bullet is a time-boxed entry model: a trade taken from the first fair value gap that forms in the 10:00 to 11:00 ET hour. In 2024 ICT calls the 10:00 Silver Bullet the main focus of the day and says it is "always there" in the charts ([2024 #9 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=781), [2024 #9 · 28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683)). The notes give the window, its role and its logic from 2024. The entry, stop and target detail comes from 2026 lectures.

His reason for waiting until 10:00 is safety. The opening range (9:30 to 10:00) is a time you can get caught offside, so you stand aside and reassess at 10:00 with levels you drew in advance ([2024 #9 · 28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683)). He defines the opening range as 30 minutes and says anyone who calls it a 5- or 15-minute range is wrong ([2026 #10 · 39:53](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2393)).

### The sequence (bearish; reverse for buys)

1. **Context.** Pre-drawn levels and a directional idea. Mark 10:00 on the chart, but not as a vertical line across everything, so you can see whether the 10:00 candle forms an imbalance ([2026 #10 · 31:07](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1867)). If price expands strongly up into the NWOG before the open, he stands aside and waits for the 10:00 Silver Bullet ([2024 #4 · 23:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1384)).
2. **Setup.** The first FVG in the 10:00 hour on the 1-minute chart. The 10:00 candle itself can be part of it, or the next candles. Include a volume imbalance: when candle bodies do not meet, count that gap too ([2026 #14 · 19:06](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1146)). The gap alone is not a reason to trade. He wants "something in here to make us want to be short", a blend of ideas that builds the narrative ([2026 #10 · 32:41](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1961)).
3. **Trigger.** Price returns into the gap and shows its hand. His best case: price cleared minor buy-side, tagged the gap, did not close outside it, and closed below its midpoint. A later up-close candle that displaces above highs (a first presented FVG "with displacement") takes more command over price delivery and trumps the plain first gap ([2026 #10 · 36:42](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2202)).
4. **Entry.** Anywhere in the lower half of that gap, which he calls the most realistic for you ([2026 #10 · 39:53](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2393)).
5. **Stop.** The high of the last candle whose open is outside the buy-side imbalance or sell-side inefficiency, plus one tick, including the volume imbalance (two buy-side bodies) in that frame. This stop is wide and forces micros.
6. **Target.** First partial at the 10:00-hour low or the relative equal lows beside it: low-hanging fruit, the pullback target of the lunch macro. A longer hold goes toward the pre-market 7:00 to 9:00 relative equal lows.
7. **Management.** Take the first partial and hold the rest. A hypothetical in the lecture: two micros, one off at the first target.

A second trigger shape appears in 2024. For a buy-side run he wants one 1-minute candle to hold its low and the next candle to trade below it. He called that a possible Silver Bullet and did not execute it ([2024 #33 · 33:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2017)).

### Time window and fallbacks

- **Main window:** 10:00 to 11:00 ET. "No touch, no trade": if the idea does not materialise, wait until about 11:00 to 11:30 and fade what the market did, using it as a "lunch macro". If the retracement does not occur before 1:00 PM he expects it in the afternoon ([2024 #9 · 47:09](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2829)).
- **Afternoon:** he says there is a Silver Bullet at 2:00 to 3:00 and another at 3:00 to 4:00 PM, plus four macros in the last hour, so you can ignore the whole morning. The notes flag these windows as loosely quoted and caption-dependent ([2024 #20 · 48:16](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2896)).
- **Muddy mornings:** "sit on my hands" until 10:00 and use the first presented FVG. On one such day the first 1-minute FVG formed at 9:32 and he shaded it gray rather than coloring a direction ([2024 #33 · 22:17](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1337), [2024 #33 · 46:56](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2816)).
- **Inside the move:** setups between the high and the low of a run are generally Silver Bullet-type setups. He does not need the extreme to profit ([2024 #35 · 15:15](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=915)).
- **Fallback in 2026:** "give it till 10:00 and if nothing's obvious, then simply submit yourself to the 10:00 hour Silver Bullet" ([2026 #55 · 1:44](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=104)).

### Worked example 1: the 10:01 gap (NQ 1-minute; day not given)

The first FVG came at 10:01, a sell-side imbalance (SIBI) created by a down-close candle. The narrative around it was the lunch macro: price would seek a low formed in the 10:00 hour, with a relative equal low beside it. Shorting on the candle that made that low would be unwise. Price retraced up, cleared minor buy-side, tagged the gap, did not close outside it and closed below its midpoint. The notes give no exact prices.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating it as a bare time marker.** In the news-day lecture of August 2024 the Silver Bullet is only a clock reference, with no teaching attached ([2024 #6 · 47:24](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2844)). The model needs the gap, the narrative and the draw.
- **Entering on the first gap with nothing behind it.** "By itself it is not enough" is the 2026 position ([2026 #10 · 32:41](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1961)).
- **Confusing two "first" gaps.** The Silver Bullet uses the first FVG of the 10:00 hour. Caleb's practice model uses the first FVG after 9:30. The next section covers it.
- **Abandoning it because a creator says it stopped working.** He says nothing has changed and that it is there every day ([2024 #17 · 38:08](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=2288)).
- **Study versus trade.** For the first four to six weeks he wants the 10:00 gap logged, not predicted (see the log below).

### How the log works (2026)

In the pillars lecture he gives a study version: only the first FVG that forms in the 10:00 hour. Draw a rectangle, extend it right at first, then switch the extension off and drag the box to the first time price returns into it. Record the time it formed (his example: 10:01), how many minutes passed before price returned, and the range in handles from the highest high on the return to the lowest low it took. He expects the pattern to repeat: the gap forms, price drops to a short-term low, rallies into the gap, then sells through that low ([2026 #14 · 19:54](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1194), [2026 #14 · 23:09](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1389)). Chapter 21 covers journaling.

## The first presented FVG as a model

### What it is

The first presented FVG is the first fair value gap that appears after the 9:30 open. Chapter 8 defines it. As a model, it was ICT's practice trade for his son in 2024: take the first FVG presented between 9:30 and 10:00 together with a bias, one trade per morning session. He says it can be done every day and overlaps with nothing else, and that the first FVG in the 9:30 to 10:00 window "should give you an enormous edge in terms of timing" ([2024 #38 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4426), [2024 #38 · 2:13:11](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7991)).

### The sequence (bearish, from the October 28, 2024 example)

1. **Context.** A bias, here short from a cluster at the NWOG high. One contract only on a day like this ([2024 #49 · 1:17:35](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4655)).
2. **Setup.** A 1-minute sell-side imbalance (SIBI), the single large down candle of the 9:30 open, becomes the higher-timeframe array. Price rallies into it and overshoots slightly, trading into its upper part.
3. **Trigger.** Candle bodies respect the SIBI's low: they close and stay below it. That is displacement. A first FVG that forms inside the 9:30 candle cannot be traded: you cannot enter on a first presented FVG until 9:31 or later ([2024 #49 · 1:35:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5712), [2024 #49 · 1:38:20](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5900)).
4. **Entry.** The first clean FVG on the lower timeframe (15, 30, 45 seconds or 1 minute). Price retraces up into it, and the short is taken as price trades one tick below the FVG's low: not mid-gap, not the gap high, and no limit orders on wicks above the gap ([2024 #49 · 1:47:16](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6436)).
5. **Stop.** Normally above candle 2 (the middle, gap-creating candle) plus one tick, which is the low-risk default. If price is close to the SIBI low, or in high-resistance conditions, use the maximum: candle 1's high plus one tick, so the stop sits inside the SIBI's box and a retest does not take you out.
6. **Target.** A new low below the entry leg's low (sell-side). Take it and leave.
7. **Management.** Expect heat: price may run in profit, pull back above entry (but not to the stop), then break lower again.

### Worked example: NQ, Monday October 28, 2024 (15-second and 1-minute)

Pre-market ran up into old relative equal highs and a 9:00 high. At 9:30 price broke down on one large black 1-minute candle, whose range is the SIBI (two candles on the 15-second chart). The open sat inside an inversion FVG at a premium. Price rallied back into the SIBI, slightly above it, then broke below its low, and the displacement left the first clean FVG at about 9:37. The short went in as price retraced into that gap. Price ran in profit, came back above the entry, with the stop above candle 1's high inside the SIBI box not hit, then broke lower and filled the opening range gap CE at about 9:49, 19 minutes in ([2024 #49 · 1:35:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5712), [2024 #49 · 1:47:16](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6436)).

A second plan from the September 30, 2024 premarket session shows the other end of a trade. The entry was at the CE of the first presented FVG (an inversion) with the NWOG lying over it, and the large target was the relative equal lows. The remainder was stopped when price went back into the first FVG. He sticks to the rule that a return into the FVG or opening range gap low consumes too much of the 9:30 to 10:30 window, so there is no re-entry ([2024 #37 · 2:27:14](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8834), [2024 #37 · 2:28:01](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8881)).

### The first presented FVG after a turtle soup (2026)

By 2026 the idea is a long-lived reference. After a turtle soup (price runs a swing high, fails, and snaps back through the prior run), the first FVG that forms in the displacement is a level to keep on the chart and project forward.

1. A recognisable raid of a short-term high or low. Zoom out, not in, so your eye jumps to the standout swings.
2. Displacement through the swing the other way. Price does not need to reverse fully or take the opposing swing low. The gap has to stand out when you zoom out.
3. The first FVG left by that move is the one you log and keep. His rule is to keep it on the chart for 20 days (the caption wording is unclear) ([2026 #141 · 12:00](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=720), [2026 #141 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=815)).
4. Watch for price to return to it: a premium array if it is a sell-side imbalance, a discount array for the mirror.

His example: Tuesday December 23, 2025, NQ 1-minute, around 9:54 ET. Price ran straight up from the open, made a short-term high, rallied above it, turtle-souped back into the initial run, then displaced below the swing low. A few trading days later, pre-market price ran straight up to that gap's high and rejected ([2026 #141 · 10:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=624), [2026 #141 · 52:55](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=3175)). The notes do not say whether the reaction point is the gap high, its CE or the whole gap.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Trading a gap formed inside the 9:30 candle.** The earliest usable first gap is at 9:31 or later.
- **Using the first gap without displacement.** The 2026 ranking puts a first gap with displacement above a simple first gap ([2026 #10 · 36:42](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2202)).
- **Calling every first FVG a future inversion.** A passing line in a 2024 lecture says not every FVG should be viewed as a future iFVG, and first presentation is its own idea ([2024 #16 · 27:46](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1666)).

### How the teaching changed

- **August 2024:** stand aside from 9:30 to 10:00 (Caleb's model, next section).
- **September 23, 2024:** on muddy mornings wait for 10:00 and use the first presented FVG for the Silver Bullet ([2024 #33 · 22:17](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1337)).
- **October 2024:** the first FVG between 9:30 and 10:00 is itself the practice model, one trade a morning ([2024 #38 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4426)).
- **2026:** the gap is graded (displacement beats plain) and carried forward for days.

The August stand-aside and the October trade-inside versions are both in the notes. They are different instructions for the same window, so pick one and apply it consistently.

## Caleb's model (2024): the session draw, then a fair value gap

### What it is

In August 2024 ICT builds a beginner framework around his son. It forces one entry mechanism, the fair value gap, inside order-flow context read from price alone (not level 2, depth of market or volume profile). The aim is a baseline understanding of price action. He says the FVG is chosen because it is the easiest to see, not because it is the best, and that it "should not necessarily be yours" ([2024 #11 · 1:32:04](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=5524), [2024 #10 · 3:17:18](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=11838)). The draw on liquidity is new day and new week opening gaps. Layered or nested gaps act "like a big black hole" that pulls price in ([2024 #17 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=3064)).

The entry rule as he stated it: use the FVG only when there is an obvious draw on liquidity. Buy at the high of a discount (bullish) FVG, or sell at the low of a bearish one, using the near edge and not the midpoint ([2024 #10 · 3:17:18](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=11838)).

### The sequence (as taught August 19, 2024)

[[DIAGRAM ch17_d5 | caption: Caleb's sequence on a bullish day (illustrative candles, modelled on the August 19, 2024 NQ session; no prices) | The side taken first is the trap; the gap after the reclaim is the entry, and the limit sits at the gap's high so a partly open gap still fills you.]]

1. **Context and liquidity.** Outline relative equal highs and lows. Caleb looks only to the right of 7:00 ET, the last two hours before 9:00 to 9:30. Advanced students may add the London range and Power of 3 context ([2024 #11 · 1:46:27](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=6387)).
2. **Gap references.** Mark the RTH opening range gap, the new week opening gap and the nearest new day opening gaps.
3. **Wait.** Do not trade the first 30 minutes. Watch which side of the liquidity gets taken first. The "disturbed", jagged side is the one taken.
4. **Read the trap.** The side taken first is the trap (the Judas swing). The second side is the draw ([2024 #11 · 2:08:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7693)).
5. **Trigger and entry.** After the first side is taken, wait for price to reclaim a reference (for example back above an old NDOG), then enter on an FVG using the institutional order flow entry drill (IOFED, below).
6. **Target.** Take partials at an easy low-threshold target, such as the lower quadrant of the NWOG.
7. **Graduation.** Later steps: hold through lunch and close around 1:30 if in profit, then frame the full day, and only then daily bias from the weekly and monthly. You cannot derive a daily bias until you can read the session draw ([2024 #11 · 2:29:47](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=8987)).

### The entry: IOFED

While the displacement candle is still forming, you anticipate the gap. When the next candle opens, drop a limit at the gap's near edge. You do not try for the low end of the gap or its midpoint. The expectation is that price retraces into the gap and goes a tick or so beyond the reference candle's edge, leaving part of the gap open, then displaces. The gap that stays partly open is the sign of strength: he wants to see "little cracks in orderflow" ([2024 #11 · 1:04:46](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3886), [2024 #11 · 1:22:31](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4951)). The gap can fill completely and still be valid. You just take more drawdown ([2024 #11 · 1:32:52](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=5572)). Chapter 18 gives the full mechanics.

**The tick offset is recorded inconsistently in the notes.** The IOFED lecture puts the limit at the gap high plus one tick and says that assures a fill ([2024 #11 · 1:22:31](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4951)). The lecture of August 16 says to buy one tick below the gap's high and sell one tick above a bearish gap's low ([2024 #10 · 3:17:18](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=11838)). The October 28 entry sits one tick below a bearish FVG's low. The caption-based notes do not reconcile these. The consistent part is that you work from the near edge, never the CE, and accept that a perfect fill costs you trades. His sample scale (not for beginners): 6 contracts at the edge and 4 more at the midpoint, 10 in total ([2024 #8 · 2:50:57](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10257)).

### Stop, target and risk rules

- **Stop.** Below the swing or wick lows where price has been "traveling back and forth". He gives no fixed tick count ([2024 #11 · 1:22:31](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4951)). For shorts see the stop rules in the first-presented-FVG section.
- **Targets.** The NWOG low is the first threshold, then its CE, then its upper quadrant. He expects a pierce through the NWOG high because stops rest at the smooth highs.
- **Low-hanging fruit.** For the beginner, take the first NWOG level reached plus a spread of a tick or two and leave. Do not hold for the NWOG CE, low or last week's gap ([2024 #49 · 24:37](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1477)). Daily thresholds: 10, 15 or 20 handles on NQ or 5 to 10 on ES, about $100 to $250 a day. He says 15 minutes is the high timeframe for day trading ([2024 #49 · 59:01](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=3541), [2024 #49 · 2:13:52](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8032)).
- **Terminus.** The best-case target, not needed for the model to pay. He says that about 90% of the time his own early exits had hit one or two PD arrays in his favour before the Terminus. That figure describes his exits, not a win rate ([2024 #49 · 1:07:46](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4066)).
- **Risk.** Never trade without a structural stop. Caleb trades one micro contract, one trade a day. One win or one loss and he is done ([2024 #17 · 1:36:39](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=5799), [2024 #17 · 1:57:34](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=7054)). Chapter 20 covers sizing.

### The strongest version (September 2024)

A short-term high is taken (buy-side first), price trades down and takes a short-term low (sell-side), then rallies using an FVG, which can be an inversion FVG. In the earlier trade he described, he entered slightly above the CE of a wick and the iFVG, and aimed at the short-term buy-side with a 0.25 extension of the old high, shaving the exit for the spread because price does not always reach the target. He missed the best entry below the CE with a market order. A limit would have caught it, but it will not always do that for you. His "chicken leg" point: the meat is not at the tips of the bone, so you do not need the highest high or lowest low ([2024 #33 · 15:48](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=948), [2024 #33 · 16:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=997), [2024 #33 · 17:27](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=1047)).

### Worked example: NQ, Monday August 19, 2024

Relative equal highs sat above and relative equal lows below. After 9:30 price took the sell side first, sweeping old lows and an old NDOG, then reclaimed and ran to the buy side and the NWOG ([2024 #11 · 2:08:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7693)). The entry came around 10:20 to 10:25 ET (derived from the video clock), in what he called a Silver Bullet-type window. Price swept equal lows, ran into the August 16 NDOG low area, reclaimed it and closed above, dropped to the NDOG high, and a displacement candle began to form. While it was forming ICT said an FVG would form and that price would dip about one tick below the candle's low.

Result, from garbled captions: a first dip of two ticks to about 19,580.00, then a second dip to about 19,578.50, eight ticks (2 handles, $40 per NQ contract) under the 19,580.50 reference. He admitted he expected one tick and got more. The gap's lower portion stayed open. Price then rallied to the NWOG low, the CE and the upper area, and pushed through the NWOG high ([2024 #11 · 1:09:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4178), [2024 #11 · 2:00:54](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7254)). ### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Swapping the model when it has no trade.** Do not reach for an order block or OTE because Caleb's model is silent. The whole lesson is to stay with the assigned model ([2024 #17 · 1:26:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=5179)).
- **Calling a bias in advance.** He repeatedly says he is not calling it, only looking for reasons to hunt a setup, and lays out what would invalidate it ([2024 #11 · 2:08:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7693)).
- **Buying the low end of the gap for a tiny stop.** He says people "prematurely teaching" his concepts do this. The edge is where the fill is ([2024 #11 · 1:22:31](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4951)).
- **Gaps as a strength gauge.** Whenever a PD array such as a gap is not traded to, or is not completely overlapped, that tells you about the strength of the underlying move ([2024 #11 · 1:28:07](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=5287)).

## The opening range gap mid-gap trades (2024)

### What it is

Two 2024 trades use the midpoint (CE) of the RTH opening range gap (ORG) as the draw for the first part of the session. Both are small, time-boxed objectives, in the "get in, get to the midpoint, get out" spirit.

### The mechanical version (a turtle soup entry on a buy stop)

1. **Context.** A gap-down open of at least 40 handles. The ORG midpoint is the draw for the first 30 minutes.
2. **Setup.** A minor sell-side low already identified.
3. **Trigger.** Price pierces below that low with a down-closed candle.
4. **Entry.** Treat the piercing candle as an order block and place a buy stop at its opening price. If price never trades back up to that open, you are never filled. If it does, you are kicked in long. He calls it "very, very mechanical": you start flat and the buy stop is the only order that executes.
5. **Stop.** Not stated in the notes.
6. **Target.** The ORG midpoint (CE), then out. It is a small, high-frequency entry. The next sell-side (the equal lows) can follow.
7. **Management.** Backtest it. He says there are several equally effective ways to trade this tape.

The example: NQ 1-minute after the 9:30 open, gap down. Price traded below a minor low with a down candle, rallied to the midpoint, then fell on toward the equal lows ([2024 #40 · 1:25:23](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5123), [2024 #40 · 1:27:02](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5222), [2024 #40 · 1:28:37](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5317)). The notes describe only the gap-down, long version.

### The one-FVG-to-midpoint version

On September 4, 2024 the trade he called his model was a long from the first FVG to the ORG midpoint, with the NDOG low as the extension. His point is that you need one repeatable piece and may not care what happens after the midpoint. Pick one clear objective, take partials there, trail any remainder with a stop, and do not second-guess a trade you framed one way. The notes give no stop rule, size or R multiple for it ([2024 #22 · 49:30](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2970), [2024 #22 · 56:43](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3403)). The October 28 short above ended at the same kind of target: the ORG CE, about 19 minutes after the open.

## The market maker buy and sell models (MMBM and MMSM)

### What it is

These are ICT's maps of a whole move rather than a single entry. A sell model runs from a consolidation, through a rally that builds the high, to a distribution lower. The buy model is the mirror image. In 2026 he calls them his primary, "universal" model, says he likes best the second stage of each, and points to his 2016 and 2017 mentorship playlists for the full teaching. He says it is very close to the most openly he will ever teach the model he trades most ([2026 #15 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1392), [2026 #15 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1440)). The notes list the stages from several lectures but never a full class, so treat the picture below as a map, not a rulebook.

ICT's explanation for why such a path exists is his predator image: the algorithm moves price so that deeper pockets can build positions against retail's resting stops, which are the liquidity ([2024 #27 · 45:08](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=2708)). On the 1-minute read in September 2024 he calls the sell model a market truism (markup, then sell-off), not a Wyckoff idea; the caption is unclear on the rest of that remark ([2024 #24 · 3:07:28](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11248)).

### The sequence (sell model; buy model mirrors)

[[DIAGRAM ch17_d4 | caption: The market maker sell model as ICT lists it in 2026 (illustrative path, no prices) | The second stage of distribution is the largest and easiest move, and it can end below the original consolidation.]]

1. **Original consolidation.** A clean box of price, not the impulsive stretches ([2024 #24 · 2:49:51](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10191)).
2. **Leaves it and tests it.** Price leaves the consolidation and comes back to test it. In the September 2024 read this followed a stop run under the consolidation's lows and a rally back to its high. The rally then builds in a first stage and a second stage (accumulation, then reaccumulation) ([2026 #65 · 3:17](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=197)).
3. **Smart money reversal (SMR).** A high is taken inside the higher-timeframe premium. You do not need the SMR itself. Wait for the break of market structure at the low, then trade the retrace up into the order block. That is the low-risk sell ([2024 #24 · 2:49:51](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10191)).
4. **Entry.** Sell the retrace. In September 2024 he pyramided adds in the lower half of the order block candle (the 1-minute bearish OB at the opening price) ([2024 #24 · 2:49:51](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10191)). In July 2026 the stop sits above the breaker ([2026 #65 · 3:17](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=197)).
5. **First stage of distribution.** Steady decline, with retracements that touch prior highs.
6. **Second stage of distribution (redistribution).** The fastest, most animated stage. He calls it the "unicorn": the most powerful and easiest to hold. Sell the second-stage redistribution, or for the buy model the second-stage reaccumulation ([2024 #24 · 3:08:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11297), [2026 #16 · 20:07](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1207)). Let it run and do not exit early ([2026 #80 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=332)).
7. **Target.** The pools: sell-side below, then the lows under the original consolidation. When the market allows (rare) there is a further leg to take out that consolidation ([2026 #121 · 18:28](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=1108)).

The buy model reads: smart money reversal low, low-risk buy, first-stage accumulation, second-stage reaccumulation, running to the target ([2026 #45 · 4:02](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=242)).

Management advice from the same lectures: "second mouse gets the cheese". Do not sell the first touch of premium just because price is high. Wait for the run and the retest after the opening range ([2024 #24 · 2:55:29](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10529)). In one live trade he did not tighten his stop during the second stage, so price could breathe ([2026 #16 · 16:57](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1017)).

### Time window

There is no fixed clock for the model. It is fractal. ICT says that within every 15 one-minute candles (viewed on the 15-second chart) there is a market maker buy or sell model "every single time", about four an hour, and you only need a run on liquidity to see one. He adds that you must know the draw, because if nothing is the target you get a 100% strike rate hitting nothing ([2026 #101 · 1:53:31](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6811), [2026 #101 · 1:55:10](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6910)). The 10:50 to 11:10 macro is a timing confluence at a turn in the second stage, never a reason on its own ([2026 #65 · 13:36](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=816)).

NQ 1-minute, Thursday September 5, 2024, after 10:00.** A consolidation, a run of the lows under relative equal lows, a rally back to the consolidation high, accumulation, then a rally that took a high (outside the FVG box he had colored). **3. ### Refinements

- **It may not return to the consolidation.** In a sell model price can rally into the upper quadrant of the range, fail, and never revisit the original consolidation. If you do not know when the model completes, you can sit in a good trade, think it is easy, and be reversed ([2026 #136 · 59:19](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=3559)).
- **Bullish context.** In a July 2026 review he says that when the picture is bullish the model will not take out the original consolidation but stop in deep discount, outside the sell-side liquidity ([2026 #65 · 20:51](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1251)). The note flags that this needs other confluences.
- **Work from the daily down.** He labelled an intraday short as a sell model built top-down: with nothing on the daily chart supporting the idea, a 1-minute picture alone is not enough to carry that much risk ([2026 #29 · 33:27](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=2007)).
- **A warning wick.** If a drop's wick fails to close below its CE, real order flow is in jeopardy and price may run for the buy-side. He labelled that day a sell model with the 9:30 run as the Judas ([2026 #70 · 14:03](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=843), [2026 #70 · 14:50](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=890)).
- **Use it as a defensive check.** If price should go lower but instead breaks up, a buy model may be in effect. That is a reason to sit out or reverse bias, not a primary signal ([2026 #115 · 13:39](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=819)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Selling the first stage.** The advice is consistent from 2024 to 2026: the second stage is the trade ([2024 #24 · 3:08:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11297), [2026 #15 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1440)).
- **Treating it as Wyckoff.** The notes of one 2026 lecture warn that it is not Wyckoff distribution but ICT's own categorisation ([2026 #16 · 16:57](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1017)).
- **Trusting the stage names.** The notes mix "accumulation" and "reaccumulation" in the sell model, and one lecture lists a third accumulation stage as implied. Count the legs, not the labels.

## One Shot, One Kill (OSOK)

### What it is

ICT uses "one shot, one kill" at three scales, so check which one he means. All three share one principle: very few trades, each framed in advance with a clear target.

1. **The sniper mindset (September 2024).** Take "one or two, maybe three" surgical trades a week, not an everyday setup, and do not spray a magazine hoping to hit something ([2024 #24 · 59:19](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3559), [2024 #24 · 1:00:07](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3607)).
2. **The weekly Terminus (September 30, 2024).** The sell-side target for the week (relative equal lows plus the weekly BISI high area) is his Terminus. If a short trades into it, his "weekly one shot one kill has been satisfied" and any other trading that week is just scalps. His heaviest leverage is reserved for the short aimed at that sell-side ([2024 #37 · 1:01:22](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3682), [2024 #37 · 1:02:55](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3775)).
3. **A 2 to 5 day swing (2026).** A single high-conviction entry with a defined target, executed expecting to be closed within two to five days. Don't hold over a weekend because of gap risk. When the target hits, you are done with that swing and do not look for the next trade immediately ([2026 #66 · 24:46](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=1486)).

### The sequence (the sniper version)

1. **Context.** Define the target first, such as a higher-timeframe FVG low, then find the highest-timeframe premium array to enter from ([2024 #24 · 2:36:15](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9375)).
2. **Setup.** Sell the highest premium array available and aim at the extreme.
3. **Trigger.** The FVG is the scope. The moment price steps into the gap, or approaches its CE, it is in the crosshairs.
4. **Entry.** "Exhale" and enter as price enters the FVG ([2024 #24 · 1:01:43](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3703)).
5. **Stop.** Beyond the imbalance candle: below it for a bullish FVG, above its high for a bearish one. Which candle he means is unclear in the captions. In an order block entry he uses the block's high plus one tick. Place it once, with logical price points, and never chase it.
6. **Target.** The largest magnitude on offer. His example was about 360 NQ handles from the Thursday short to the Friday FVG low. Be ready to absorb fluctuations in between.
7. **Management.** Do nothing extra after entering. Sit still, and do not announce trades on social media. The stop for pyramided adds is not trailed up. Start with humble goals: 5 handles in ES and 10 to 15 handles in NQ.

### Worked example: NQ, Thursday September 5 to Friday September 6, 2024

This is the turtle soup short described above, seen from the OSOK side. ICT sold near the high of the day after price swept the highs, working from a bearish order block with sell-side annotated below. The whole position, an initial fill plus adds of 4 and 4 contracts, counted as one trade. He held through the day and covered with a limit order after price reached the CE of the August 13 daily FVG during Friday's London session ([2024 #23 · 0:01](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=1), [2024 #23 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=438), [2024 #23 · 8:05](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=485)). He labels the delivery a market maker sell model that went to the daily gap's midpoint, a level he had flagged in earlier streams ([2024 #23 · 1:38](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=98)). The notes do not give his stop distance or his initial size.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading OSOK as "never trade more than once".** In the sniper form it is a rate: one to three a week.
- **Copying the size.** He deliberately mentions a prop-firm style profit target. Beginners are told to start with 5 ES or 10 to 15 NQ handles a day.
- **Mixing the scales.** A weekly Terminus, a sniper entry and a 2 to 5 day swing are different time horizons. The notes flag that the weekly use is not the same as the intraday one.

## The Indigo entry (2024)

### What it is

Indigo is a pattern ICT named in October 2024: a purge of liquidity followed by an order block that trades into that liquidity, with a volume imbalance on top of it for a bullish setup (below it for a bearish one). In his shortest form: an order block inside liquidity with a volume imbalance on top ([2024 #38 · 51:15](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3075)). He says it is a combination of several factors, that more will come later, and that he doesn't need the name.

### The sequence (bullish)

1. **Context.** A reason to be long: price goes to liquidity (sell stops under relative equal lows) or to a discount inefficiency. The main reason is liquidity.
2. **Setup.** The sweep must go beyond the lows into a higher-timeframe reference, here the upper quadrant of a daily dual imbalance. Smooth lows or an NDOG low alone are too insignificant to buy at, and you do not buy early into "too smooth" levels ([2024 #38 · 53:36](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3216)).
3. **Trigger.** A down-close candle forms (the order block) with a volume imbalance above it, and price crosses that candle's open as validation.
4. **Entry.** Anywhere in the order block, from its open to its high or the wick (the "watering hole"). Add as price touches the top of the volume imbalance ([2024 #38 · 1:08:55](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4135)).
5. **Stop.** Below the sweep.
6. **Target.** The minor buy-side pools, then the buy-side level (20,287 in the example) and the top of the daily box.

### Worked example: NQ, Asian session, September 30 into October 1, 2024

At about 8:00 to 8:10 PM ET price made relative equal highs and then dropped aggressively. Breakout buyers above had stops under the equal lows and a wick. Below sat a real gap and an NDOG. Price drove below the equal lows into the real gap and into the upper quadrant of the daily dual imbalance. A down-close candle formed with a volume imbalance on top, ICT went long inside it and added at the top of the imbalance. He did not buy earlier. Price rallied through the minor buy-side pools to 20,287 ([2024 #38 · 53:36](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3216), [2024 #38 · 1:29:48](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5388)). He refuses to call it supply and demand. The notes mark this model as partly taught.

## The 2026 coinages

By 2026 ICT adds names that grade where and when a reaction should happen. For these the notes give descriptions and examples more than step lists, and he frames several as optional. I follow the notes and say where a step is missing.

### Obsidian: two opposing wicks

**What it is.** In the first teaching, "Obsidian is two opposing wicks within a narrative". For a bearish case the second wick must be higher than the first. You split each wick in half (its CE), extend those midpoints to the right, and price tends to work between the two levels ([2026 #29 · 26:58](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1618), [2026 #29 · 24:28](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1468)). An earlier lecture (August 25, 2026) used the same wick midpoints inside a pre-market consolidation as range levels with support and resistance, and noted that closing below an Obsidian shows willingness to drop further ([2026 #25 · 26:35](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=1595), [2026 #25 · 34:35](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=2075)).

**The sequence (bearish).**

1. **Context.** A bearish narrative from the daily chart. You have to be right on direction, "otherwise this isn't going to work in your hands".
2. **Setup.** A candle with a wick above and a candle with a wick below, the later wick higher. A SIBI or gap between the wick low and wick high can serve as the entry reference. When the second wick forms higher, that confirms the Obsidian.
3. **Trigger.** Price closes below the structure, then retraces into the zone without a body in the upper half of it. Bodies later respect the wick's CE.
4. **Entry.** Short inside the zone between the two CEs and add each time price re-enters. In the example the entries were market orders.
5. **Stop.** Not stated. He lowers the stop as price drops.
6. **Target.** Low-hanging fruit first (30,137.50 in the example), partials at the lows and first relative equal lows, and more if price reaches the upper quadrant of the 7:00 to 9:00 dealing range.
7. **Management.** Repeated taps inside the zone are "building a false consolidation" that bull-flag buyers will buy, and it will deny them.

**Example.** NQ 1-minute on a Monday, after a Judas-style run that swept the Friday morning buy-side pool. Two opposing wicks formed (the second higher) with a FVG between them anchored to the CE of the 7:00 to 9:00 range. He shorted inside. Price dropped below a BISI, whose close validated an iFVG a tick under the gap high, and bodies could not touch it on retests. He took partials at the lows, the relative equal lows and the NWOG upper quadrant, with the final order resting just above 30,137.50 ([2026 #29 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1419)). Price digits in the captions are unreliable.

**Two readings.** The August 25 notes say Obsidian is for consolidations and not trends. The other lecture uses it inside a bearish narrative after a run-up. Both are in the notes, and he promises the details in a later video. The bullish mirror is implied, not taught.

### Enigma: a model, then a way of measuring

In 2024 Enigma is one of ICT's own models: the one for intraday volatility, needing no bias, no long-term draws and no PD arrays ([2024 #17 · 1:16:53](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=4613)). In June 2026 it is his name for the way he looks at price: in measurements, and at where the algorithm will interface with the marketplace. He says it is not an indicator, cannot be bought and is not coded by anyone else, and that you do not need it to trade his core concepts profitably ([2026 #72 · 0:00](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=0), [2026 #72 · 9:47](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=587)).

Its working output in that video is an FVG projection. He teaches two targets, a low-hanging-fruit objective and an extreme, and says the story is clearest between them. His FVG measurement grew from wicks only, to adding the volume imbalance, which gives two measurements of one inefficiency ([2026 #72 · 12:15](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=735)).

1. **Find the displacement.** On the DXY daily, candle 1 closed at about 100.997 and candle 2 opened higher (over 101.005), leaving a volume imbalance and an opening range gap.
2. **Low-hanging fruit.** Draw a fib from the open of candle 2 up to the low of the later candle that showed its willingness to go higher. Project the 1.25 level forward. That gave 101.795 ([2026 #72 · 21:17](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1277)).
3. **Extreme.** Re-anchor the same fib to candle 1's close ("the body tells the story, the wicks do the damage"). The 1.25 level then gave 101.805 ([2026 #72 · 22:04](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1324)).
4. **The band.** Fib the band between the two levels. Its CE is 101.80. The Wednesday high printed at 101.80 and price then retraced. He says he called it live ([2026 #72 · 24:34](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1474)).

The anchor wording is ambiguous in the captions, the 1.25 level is stated with no justification, and he calls the whole layer optional. For gold, the notes contain no Enigma example.

### Chain of custody: key PD array to key PD array

**What it is.** The question: how does price travel from one key PD array to the next, and what makes an array "key"? His answer rejects buying and selling pressure, order counts and volume ("none of that means anything") and says the delivery is algorithmic and autonomous. It is disrupted briefly when it suits them, then returns ([2026 #47 · 0:47](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=47), [2026 #49 · 55:11](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=3311)). The notes call this a compilation of what he demonstrates, not a formal checklist.

**The steps as demonstrated.**

1. Establish the bias and the draw on liquidity (for example full closure of an RTH opening range gap, Chapter 11).
2. Pre-draw the levels you will watch, as he does on a notepad before the move.
3. Grade the range: octants, quadrants and the CE (Chapter 6). Place the PD arrays that sit on those levels.
4. Validate each array by contact with a graded level and by body behavior. "Bodies not allowed in the upper half of a bearish PD array" is a rule he claims is his alone ([2026 #49 · 57:32](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=3452)).
5. Trade from the validated array toward the next one. While each array fails to reverse price, with no close outside in the opposite direction, the chain continues. A close outside breaks the chain and means recalibrate ([2026 #61 · 5:33](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=333)).

**Examples.** For Monday's RTH ORG: first presented FVG, then an iFVG at the upper octant, a gap at the CE, a gap on the lower quadrant, and an imbalance on the gap low (Friday's last print) ([2026 #47 · 0:00](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=0)). On the daily: a May 5, 2026 bullish BISI with small volume imbalances at each end. Price drove up into the upper one at the open, which also coincided with Friday's NDOG. ICT shorted there and price fell through the graded levels (upper octant, upper quadrant, lower octant above the CE, the CE, the octant below it, the lower quadrant) to the block's low, with bounces on the way ([2026 #48 · 0:50](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=50), [2026 #48 · 7:29](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=449)). He had told a student the day before that price would draw up into that volume imbalance and then sell off. Price digits in those captions are unreliable.

### Time and price grids

**What it is.** The pre-market dealing range (7:00 to 9:00 ET) gives the high and low from which the day's levels come. Divide it into 8 octants for the price lines. Add time lines at 7:00, 7:30, 8:00, 8:30, 9:00 and the minute the low formed. ICT calls the result "the keys to the kingdom" ([2026 #43 · 6:22](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=382), [2026 #43 · 12:01](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=721)).

The hourly version is the algorithmic time and price grid. Grade a range (a gap, a suspension block) into octants and quadrants for the horizontal lines. Add vertical lines at a macro's start (xx:50) and close (xx:10). He likens the grid to a multiplication table carried forward to the next macro ([2026 #44 · 27:25](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1645), [2026 #44 · 28:13](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1693)).

**The steps.**

1. Set the bias from the daily range: a draw above the high, or a retracement to a low.
2. Pick the octant or quadrant where you expect a reaction.
3. Wait for a PD array (FVG, iFVG or order block) to form there.
4. Trade inside the macro or just ahead of it, with the stop from the array.

Where a graded level meets a macro time, he expects a turning point ([2026 #44 · 29:04](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1744), [2026 #44 · 31:31](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1891)). His example: in the 9:50 to 10:10 macro the lowest candle came at 10:10, with an order block and a BISI anchored to the lowest octant. In the 10:50 to 11:10 grid, the last candle at 11:10, the macro close, is where the iFVG-led sell-off ran out ([2026 #44 · 29:57](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1797), [2026 #44 · 42:38](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2558)). You still need a bias first, and you do not need to trade only inside macro windows.

### Two validation filters

- **Price and time together.** Price at a target with a macro, a swing-high or low timing or Fibonacci time lining up means "chances are you probably got the tiger by the tail". Price without time is luck, time without price is guessing ([2026 #82 · 19:19](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=1159)).
- **Gradient anchoring.** An iFVG is more reliable if its candlestick lies on a graded level between the two pools. In his words, the candle has to be on a gradient level ([2026 #82 · 11:20](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=680), [2026 #82 · 12:07](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=727)).

### Other 2026 setups

- **Parameter-based entries before news.** Do not guess. Mark two zones, one above and one below (for example "drops to a lower FVG zone then rises to the new week opening gap, or runs to the NWOG first then drops"). Wait for the 8:30 release and let price tell you which one is active. The other zone becomes your invalidation. On the PPI Tuesday, price ran up to the NWOG and he shorted on the way down ([2026 #60 · 1:49](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=109), [2026 #60 · 11:19](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=679)).
- **Bread and butter.** An inversion FVG with bodies above its midpoint, then the next new FVG forms, bodies reject its lower half, and entry comes at the break of the low (or a body close above the midpoint). Target the next minor buy side or prior short-term high. The stop is tight, below the entry candle's low or at the CE; the notes give 2 to 5 handles on NQ. He says it is simple, obvious and high probability, and that some will still stop out ([2026 #104 · 35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126)).
- **Simplicity as a rule.** For the first 6 to 8 weeks of tape reading, focus only on where the daily suspension block is and whether price will fill it ([2026 #108 · 38:19](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2299)).
