Before any entry model, ICT wants you to decide what kind of day you are in. His vocabulary is a spectrum. At one end are low resistance liquidity runs, where price moves fast and cleanly to an obvious target. At the other are high resistance runs, where candles pile onto the same range and nothing is precise. Seek and destroy, time distortion, large-range days, clean days and fickle days are all names for parts of that spectrum. After reading you can classify a session within its first hour, decide whether to trade it at normal size, at minimum size or not at all, and flag a probable large-range day the evening before.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch16_d1 | caption: Low versus high resistance delivery on the same vertical scale (illustrative) | Low resistance leaves open gaps behind big, separated candles; high resistance stacks overlapping candles inside one range.]]

- **Resistance is a delivery type, not a direction.** You can know where price is going and still get a slow, overlapping trip there. It usually arrives eventually, after retracements and re-tests ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)).
- **The simplest visual test.** If each candle shares at least half of the previous candle's range, you are in high resistance. Big, well-separated candles that leave open gaps are low resistance ([2024 #49 · 1:54:28](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6868), [2024 #49 · 1:36:46](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5806)).
- **The two-sides test.** If you can build a convincing case for both directions, the day is 50/50. Sitting still costs nothing, and ICT calls it the one position that wins every time ([2024 #20 · 1:13:12](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4392)).
- **Seek and destroy is the aggressive form of high resistance.** Every high and every low gets taken, and price lives inside every gap. Do not file a merely hard day under that name ([2024 #35 · 37:03](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2223), [2024 #36 · 2:14:50](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=8090)).
- **Time distortion** (many candles, little distance) is either a market being held until a release, or you being on the wrong timeframe. Wait for the release, or go up a timeframe to find the target ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898), [2026 #116 · 55:00](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3300)).
- **Compression precedes expansion.** A daily range below the average of the last three days flags a large-range day soon. ICT says about 90% of large-range days deliver as low resistance runs ([2024 #48 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443), [2024 #48 · 1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906)).

## High and low resistance liquidity runs

### What it is

A liquidity run is price travelling to a pool of resting orders or to an imbalance (liquidity is Chapter 3, fair value gaps are Chapter 5). *Resistance* describes how hard that trip is. ICT is explicit that the word has nothing to do with chart support and resistance. High resistance means the market is resisting your ability to find efficient, low-risk trades ([2026 #126 · 11:28](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=688), [2026 #126 · 12:16](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=736)).

A **low resistance liquidity run (LRLR)** is a very fast run to an obvious target, with an obvious stop level and an obvious draw. The market behaves as if it is in a hurry. ICT says the signature is "speed and distance": big displacement candles, little shared range between consecutive candles, plenty of open inefficiencies and no meaningful retracement ([2024 #49 · 4:33](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=273)). The analysis is not conflicted, and the trade gives immediate positive feedback ([2024 #20 · 1:17:38](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4658)).

A **high resistance liquidity run (HRLR)** is the opposite. Price goes back and forth, ranges overlap, every fair value gap gets closed in, and price spends a long time inside inefficiencies. Open FVGs are "far and few between" ([2024 #48 · 1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793), [2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)). It is not impossible to trade, but ICT steers beginners away from it and tells them to cut leverage and frequency.

Two points of precision. First, the vocabulary is about **delivery**, so the same setup definition is valid in both conditions. What changes is its probability and its precision ([2024 #43 · 52:42](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3162)). Second, LRLR is not an everyday condition. ICT says that is why you will not trade every day, and that this is fine ([2024 #49 · 4:33](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=273)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation rests on how he believes price is delivered. An algorithm offers price along a path toward liquidity and inefficiencies. When it is simply delivering, it leaves inefficiencies and liquidity pools behind it, a "porous wake". When candles instead spend their time back and forth over previous ranges, he reads that as intervention, meaning someone working the previous ranges and going after orders, rather than clean algorithmic delivery ([2026 #136 · 1:17:24](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=4644), [2026 #136 · 1:18:12](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=4692)). In that 2026 usage "intervention" means working old ranges. It is not the news-driven manual intervention of Chapter 14.

In 2026 he goes further: high resistance conditions are intentionally engineered to punish traders already in positions and wipe out those trying to enter, before price delivers to an obvious level ([2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326)). Treat that as his claim about intent, not something the chart proves.

What do the candles do in a low resistance run? In a bullish run, down-close candles support price when it returns to them, and FVGs are left partly open instead of being fully closed. In a bearish run, up-close candles are repelled lower and down-close candles get eaten into. He says you can read all of this from price alone, with no footprint or Level 2 ([2024 #48 · 1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744), [2026 #126 · 13:53](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=833)).

In 2022 the term appears as a statement about one trade idea. He explained why not to short into a market with many supporting ranges below. A reversal would have to pierce and break all of them. That makes it a high resistance liquidity run, unlikely to form or pan out though not impossible, so price is more likely to go up to keep going higher than to go up and then reverse ([2022 Ep 35 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=719)).

### How to spot it

Ask the question early, before you look for an entry: which condition is delivery in? ICT puts it to viewers directly, and answers with a diagnostic: are fair value gaps spending a lot of time inside them? ([2024 #43 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3064)).

1. **Overlap test.** Take the last 10 to 20 candles on your trading timeframe. Do bodies overlap the previous candle? Are there few gaps? Does price keep returning inside a small range? If yes, it is high resistance ([2026 #101 · 1:10:29](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4229)). His 2024 version of the same test: each candle shares at least half of the previous candle's range ([2024 #49 · 2:02:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7356)).
2. **Inefficiency test.** Are FVGs being traded inside for long stretches, or displaced away from? On Monday Oct 7, 2024 price chopped around the opening range gap and the first presented FVG without displacing, and the gap midpoint was not reached in the first 30 minutes ([2024 #43 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3064)). On his April 15, 2026 drill day no FVGs formed for roughly the first 10 minutes after the open ([2026 #101 · 18:18](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1098)).
3. **Retracement test.** High resistance pulls back deep each time, and PD arrays are not delivered to as precisely ([2024 #43 · 56:55](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3415), [2024 #43 · 1:00:09](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3609)). Low resistance shows an inefficiency, then a clean retracement of one or two candles, then a run ([2026 #82 · 18:32](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=1112)).
4. **Timeframe continuity.** Use the 15-minute chart as the bellwether. Clean price action should show on the 1-minute and on your executable timeframe. In his Feb 27, 2026 NQ example he shaded a 15-minute block where each candle overlapped the previous one ("a big block of nonsense") and said a 1-minute trader inside it gets cooked ([2026 #126 · 10:40](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=640), [2026 #101 · 1:10:29](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4229)).
5. **Early warnings.** ICT lists "canaries in the coal mine": a higher high followed at once by a wilt, a shallow peek above a high and straight back inside, stutter instead of acceleration, and sharing the prior range. These force you to babysit positions ([2026 #101 · 1:10:29](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4229)).
6. **Body placement.** In a high resistance grind, watch where bodies close. Bodies kept at or above a low you hoped to break mean sellers are not through. Bodies buried below it are the confirmation ([2026 #53 · 28:30](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1710)).

The comparison below pulls the signatures together.

| Feature | Low resistance | High resistance |
|---|---|---|
| Candles | Big, well separated, little shared range | Each shares at least half the previous range; ICT likens the look to a UPC barcode |
| Inefficiencies | Many, left partly open (a breakaway gap) | Few; FVGs get closed in and price lives inside them |
| Retracements | Shallow, small-bodied, one or two candles | Deep, repeated, imprecise |
| Delivery to target | Straight to the PD array, quickly | Eventually, after hours of overlap |
| Feel | Immediate positive feedback, no babysitting | Heat after entry, many stop-outs |

Sources for the table: [2024 #48 · 1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744), [2024 #48 · 1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793), [2026 #101 · 1:12:04](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4324), [2026 #101 · 1:32:04](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5524).

ICT also tells you to write both characteristic lists in your daily journal and have them in front of you at the start of each day ([2024 #48 · 1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128)).

**On gold and CFDs.** ICT demonstrated these tests on NQ, MNQ, ES and MES. The overlap test needs only candle ranges, so you can run it on any chart, but the notes contain no gold example. The pre-session recipe below, by contrast, depends on index-futures features (the 9:30 ET cash open and its opening range gap).

### The pre-session recipe for an easy day

In October 2024 ICT gave a checklist for the kind of day where he is fully engaged from the open. It is not a guarantee, but he says the probabilities are off the charts ([2024 #49 · 2:29:00](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8940)):

1. A medium- or high-impact news driver at 8:30 or inside the first hour of the 9:30 session.
2. Not a Monday.
3. Very clear draws on liquidity, such as clustered new week and new day opening gaps (Chapter 11).
4. An opening range gap of at least 45 handles on NQ, preferably 75 or more. The notes do not make clear which instrument the 45 applies to beyond NQ context, and ICT gave no gold equivalent.

His high resistance counterpart is a Monday with no medium or high impact news at 8:30 or between 9:30 and 10:30 on the economic calendar ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)). Two caveats from the same video. A news driver does not guarantee low resistance, and no news does not guarantee high resistance. These are probabilities, the climate a day sits in, and he explains why news tends to produce low resistance at [2024 #49 · 2:09:49](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7789).

His January 2026 commentary supplies a reason for the no-news half. Market makers need counterparties, so deep pockets wait for participation. With no news ahead (CPI on Tuesday, PPI on Wednesday), they do not run aggressively either way, which produces a holding pattern in the 8:00 to 9:00 hour ([2026 #138 · 1:29:01](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=5341)).

### How to trade a low resistance run

- **Take the clean model.** Pay at or below the PD arrays that will hold. Price goes where you want it to go, then you step to the sidelines and wait for the next setup ([2024 #43 · 52:42](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3162), [2024 #48 · 1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128)).
- **Size and stops.** This is the only condition where he says to trade larger and with confidence. Stops can be very tight and can be moved aggressively because retracement is not a concern ([2024 #49 · 2:29:00](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8940), [2026 #87 · 1:21:20](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4880), [2026 #56 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=923)).
- **Scaling.** In low resistance he says he scales in and adds about 90% of the time, including adding if price pokes below the bottom of the gap. In high resistance he does not ([2026 #126 · 13:53](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=833)). That is his habit, not a rule for you.
- **Do not fade it.** In a 2026 example, price opened in open space between two daily inefficiencies and ran ten 1-minute candles in a row from 9:41. He says do not fade that run, or the slow overnight grind before it. Use pullbacks into PD arrays inside the run ([2026 #44 · 14:32](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=872)).
- **Partials.** The notes hold two versions. On October 25, 2024 he says do not rush partials and manage with the stop ([2024 #48 · 1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128)). On October 28 he says take a partial or cover costs fast, then trail the stop, which he cannot do to the same degree in high resistance ([2024 #49 · 4:33](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=273)). Both agree the stop does the managing. Chapter 19 covers partials in full.
- **Know the other side.** If you are on the right side the market does not hesitate. If you are on the wrong side it is devastating ([2024 #49 · 4:33](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=273)).

### How to trade (or not trade) a high resistance run

The default answer is: do not, at least not with real money.

- **Do not use live funds.** Tape read it, paper trade it or demo trade it, and engage when price is obvious and fast ([2026 #53 · 50:36](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=3036)). On his April 15, 2026 drill day (difficulty 9 out of 10 by his rating) he told viewers up front it was a practice day ([2026 #101 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781)). For a prop-firm evaluation this matters, because he says these days can blow combine accounts or tilt you ([2024 #48 · 51:44](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3104)).
- **Danger is a single spike.** High resistance can spike through the stacked consolidation in one wick and travel 50 to 60 handles to take liquidity on the other side. It also produces repeated small false runs ([2026 #101 · 1:12:04](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=4324)).
- **If you must trade, shrink everything.** In October 2024 he said not to use maximum leverage and to start with one contract (a multi-contract trader still uses one). In July 2026 he cut his usual 6-4-10 contract plan to 3 contracts ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759), [2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326)).
- **Aim only at the first, easiest objective.** A draw such as the NWOG high does not need to be reached. If the setup does not form in the first 30 minutes, toss the idea ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)).
- **Expect heat and do not bail on it** unless the model is invalidated ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)). Take partials at obvious levels in tiers and do not try to capture the full move ([2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326)).
- **Stops: the notes disagree.** October 2024: use the maximum stop on an FVG entry (the notes give candle 1's high plus one tick) and never trail the stop, which he calls a death knell ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)). October 25, 2024: do not run the stop to breakeven quickly, or do not trade ([2024 #48 · 1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793)). In a 2026 lecture (#87): be more forgiving with the stop until the first partial is reached ([2026 #87 · 1:21:20](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4880)). July 2026: use tight stops and be ready to exit quickly ([2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326)). The common thread is a stop you do not tinker with; the width is not settled in the notes.
- **Why traders fail here.** They cannot manage the wild retracements: they close the trade early, or move the stop in too fast ([2026 #53 · 26:05](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1565)).
- **Do something else with the day.** Options he gives: trade the pre-market session instead (session times are Chapter 12), accept a no-trade day, or wait for a clear run at 10:00 and use the first presented FVG then (Chapter 8) ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759), [2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898), [2024 #51 · 5:41](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=341)).
- **Be selective across the year.** In high resistance conditions he says to cherry-pick days, because a wick-only deviation outside a range is not a clean signal ([2026 #129 · 4:43](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=283)).
- **A hard morning often precedes a cleaner afternoon.** If the morning is high resistance and unclear, the lunch macro and the afternoon session should be easier to read ([2024 #43 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3064); macros are in Chapter 12).

High resistance does not mean your thesis is wrong. ICT's point on July 21, 2026 was that the move did come, but traders were punished first ([2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326)).

### A second kind of high resistance: trapped under buy-side pools

In July 2026 ICT used the same phrase for a more specific picture. The market opens with a large premium gap and is then boxed in beneath several buy-side pools, such as relative equal highs or previous session highs (relative equal highs are Chapter 3). Price oscillates between the opening price and those pools without breaking cleanly either way. Bodies struggle, candles whip, and he says to step aside and wait for clarity ([2026 #52 · 7:11](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=431), [2026 #52 · 11:57](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=717)).

The notes record two readings of what this means for shorts, and they do not agree. The July 21 lecture says shorts are less attractive because there is little room to run downward ([2026 #52 · 7:11](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=431)). The July 22 review says the same zone makes it harder for price to push higher and makes shorts more attractive, while calling it a contextual observation and not a primary reversal signal ([2026 #51 · 15:17](https://youtu.be/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=917), [2026 #51 · 16:05](https://youtu.be/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=965)). What both agree on is that it is not a signal to act on by itself.

### Worked example

**Thursday and Friday, October 24 and 25, 2024 (NQ).** This pair is ICT's own side-by-side lesson. The steps:

1. Wednesday October 23 was a large down day.
2. Thursday October 24 stayed inside Wednesday's range, with a higher low and a lower high. ICT calls it high resistance, and says it was visible on the 15-minute, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1-minute charts and on sub-minute charts ([2024 #48 · 51:44](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3104)).
3. Friday October 25 was the expected large bullish day. The order block at the lower quadrant held, the FVG that formed left its lower portion open (a breakaway gap, Chapter 8), and price ran in a straight line to the NWOG (Chapter 11) and the 15-minute smooth highs ([2024 #48 · 1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744), [2024 #48 · 1:25:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5128)).

What to notice: the same instrument on consecutive days, with the small inside day coming first. The "Large-range, small-range and inside days" section below uses this pair for the range arithmetic.

**Monday October 28, 2024 (NQ, December contract).** No news. The whole delivery from 9:30 to about 11:43 ET overlapped candle on candle, and price first touched the NWOG high only at 11:43, after hours of sideways trade ([2024 #49 · 2:00:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7212), [2024 #49 · 2:02:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7356)). He uses it as the clearest visual example. The draw was right and the trip was miserable, which is the whole point of the distinction.

**July 21, 2026 (NQ).** The open was about 450 handles into premium with several relative equal highs above, and price bounced between the opening price and those zones all day ([2026 #52 · 7:11](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=431)). In the trading video of the same date, ICT shorts small, is stopped out, re-enters, is stopped out again, and enters once more. He takes partials at intermediate relative equal lows. For a long stretch, bodies kept rejecting below the relative equal lows with wicks poking lower. Only later did bodies bury below them and price accelerate down ([2026 #53 · 22:06](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1326), [2026 #53 · 28:30](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1710)). He calls these teaching days, not money days.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading the calendar as destiny.** News does not guarantee low resistance, and an empty calendar does not guarantee high resistance ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)).
- **Mistaking a slow grind for an easy trend.** A one-sided "buy program" of tiny candles can look calm. It is not an LRLR and not a place to fade; see the section on clean days and one-sided runs ([2026 #44 · 15:19](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=919)).
- **Hindsight.** In high resistance, ICT says nothing is high probability just because a setup appears. The danger is looking back and wishing you had traded it ([2024 #43 · 52:42](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3162)).
- **Treating the whole day as one state.** The skill he wants is recognising the transition from low resistance (and high probability) to something lesser, and noticing when a rough morning hands over to a cleaner afternoon ([2026 #101 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781), [2024 #43 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3064)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022.** The term describes one trade idea: a reversal that would have to break many supporting ranges is a high resistance run and unlikely to pan out ([2022 Ep 35 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=719)).
- **2024.** It becomes a market-state filter. In August he applies it as a participation filter and says it restates earlier lectures ([2024 #20 · 1:17:38](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4658)). In October it gets its fullest treatment across three lectures: the diagnostic question (Oct 7), the two characteristic lists (Oct 25), and the overlap test with the easy-day recipe (Oct 28). He says no one else teaches it this way and that every new mentorship opened with high resistance profiles because you first have to know when probability is low ([2024 #48 · 1:19:53](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4793), [2024 #38 · 3:24:44](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=12284)).
- **2026.** He answers repeated comment requests with a visual lecture (Feb 28), gives a seasonal rhythm (below), adds the buy-side-pool version (July) and a blunt frequency claim: conditions like a heavily overlapping, wicky day are about 98% of the time, so low resistance is the exception ([2026 #126 · 10:40](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=640), [2026 #87 · 51:17](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3077)).
- **Where the notes pull apart.**
    - *News.* October 2024: a news driver at 8:30 or in the first hour is part of the easy-day recipe ([2024 #49 · 2:29:00](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8940)). 2026: the days when CPI or PPI print (Monday to Wednesday that week) are the hard ones, and Thursday and Friday afterward are the easy ones ([2026 #56 · 10:37](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=637), [2026 #56 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=923)). On April 21, 2026, with a Fed chair speaking at 10:00 ET, precision dropped and he was watching rather than trading ([2026 #95 · 3:27](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=207)). A workable reading is that the release window is unpredictable and the days after it are cleaner, but the notes do not state that reconciliation. Chapter 14 covers waiting rules around releases.
    - *Frequency.* 90% of large-range days are low resistance, and non-large days are probably high resistance about 90% of the time (2024); heavily overlapping days are about 98% of days (2026). Both are his rough figures, not statistics ([2024 #48 · 1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906), [2026 #87 · 51:17](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3077)).
- **Seasonal rhythm (his rule of thumb from experience, not a statistic).** The early-year months are the accumulation phase and mostly high resistance, so he does not participate for the first month or two. He dabbles around the first or second Tuesday of February to gauge the tone: do orders come easily, do you feel in sync, are targets met easily? Participation normally picks up in the second week of February. Freer running is expected toward the last couple of weeks of March, April and May. Summer is mostly high resistance with perhaps one or two low resistance days. Fall, from the last two weeks of August or early September through the first or second week of December, is low resistance. The holiday period returns to about 50/50 ([2026 #126 · 25:48](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=1548), [2026 #129 · 20:37](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1237)).

## Seek and destroy days

### What it is

"Seek and destroy" (S&D) appears in two senses in the notes, a day type from 2024 and a pattern of alternating runs from 2026.

**The 2024 day type.** An S&D day is one where every high gets taken out and every low gets taken out. The tell is that price spends time inside every inefficiency that forms. It consolidates inside gaps instead of leaving them, and liquidity on both sides is swept again and again ([2024 #35 · 37:03](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2223)). ICT's example is Monday September 23, 2024, a day he found unfavorable and sat out.

**The 2026 pattern.** The market maker takes out buy-side and sell-side liquidity in alternating runs. Price runs up through prior highs (buy-side stops), reverses and runs down through prior lows (sell-side stops), then reverses up again. Each swing extends beyond the last: a low, a higher high, a lower low, a higher high. ICT says that is "the seek and destroy model", and that institutions use it to collect both long and short stop-losses. It is frequent in event-driven weeks (Jackson Hole, FOMC, NFP) and less common in quiet ones ([2026 #20 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664), [2026 #20 · 11:56](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=716)).

[[DIAGRAM ch16_d2 | caption: The 2026 seek and destroy sequence: low, higher high, lower low, higher high (illustrative) | Every swing runs past the previous one, so the stops on each side are collected in turn.]]

S&D relates to high resistance as the extreme to the general. In the October 28, 2024 lecture ICT says that Monday was high resistance but not S&D, although it could have become one, and the notes describe S&D as the aggressive form of this condition ([2024 #49 · 1:35:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5759)).

### How to spot it

1. Mark the highs and lows on a 1-minute chart. Look for the alternating pattern in which each swing extends beyond the one before ([2026 #20 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664)).
2. Check whether price is consolidating inside each gap that forms, instead of displacing away from it. That is the 2024 tell ([2024 #35 · 37:03](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2223)).
3. Check that both sides' liquidity is being swept repeatedly, not once.
4. Look at the calendar. The 2026 pattern is more pronounced in event-driven weeks ([2026 #20 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664)). A medium-impact release can still produce nothing: on September 23, 2024 the 9:45 Flash Manufacturing PMI caused a flurry, no meaningful run to highs or lows, and a rejection ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)).
5. Watch for a cork near the target (next subsection).

### The cork pattern

Before breaking through to a new high, price often consolidates in a tight, odd-shaped block just below the target (for longs) or just above it (for shorts). ICT calls it a cork. It is not a classic bull flag or bear flag. It is a sloppy block that resists moving for a few candles, and once it "pops the cork" price runs quickly to the next target. He says the pattern is characteristic of seek-and-destroy days. His advice is patience: do not exit the trade out of impatience, because the consolidation is the set-up for the next move, like a champagne bottle being opened ([2026 #22 · 19:49](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1189), [2026 #22 · 20:36](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1236), [2026 #22 · 23:01](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=1381)). A related picture, accumulation with bodies held inside a gap, appears in the time distortion section.

### How to trade it

The notes hold two stances, from different years and for different situations.

- **2024: avoid it.** Avoid the day or wait. He contrasts it with a day where price does not spend time inside the inefficiencies, which is tradeable ([2024 #35 · 37:03](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2223)). On the S&D morning of Sept 23 he waited until 10:00 and the first presented FVG ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)). On December 5, 2024, in a video he titles as a seek-and-destroy review, the morning's 15-minute action was fuzzy and the 9:00 hour stayed inside a single candle's range. He wanted an obvious run at 10:00 before acting ([2024 #51 · 3:15](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=195), [2024 #51 · 5:41](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=341)).
- **2026: expect the second run and hold if the thesis is intact.** Anticipate multiple "second runs" in which price reverses to take the other side's stops. Do not panic on the reversals, since they are part of the pattern. Hold through them if your original direction is intact on the larger timeframe ([2026 #20 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664)).

The notes do not reconcile these. A reasonable reading is that the first is about a day where you have no clean entry, and the second is about a position you already hold on a higher-timeframe idea, but ICT does not say that. Given his steer to newer traders to stay away from hard days, the 2024 stance is the safer reading.

**Monday September 23, 2024 (NQ, 1-minute and 15-second, 9:30 to 10:35 ET).** Price chops inside the previous week's gaps and the unfilled lower part of a premium opening range gap. It clears the early highs, then consolidates under and around them. At 10:31 it is still ranging between the same high and low. ## Time distortion and consolidation

### What it is

Time distortion means time passes but price does not travel. ICT uses the phrase in three ways in the notes, and they overlap.

- **A held market (2024).** Price spends too much time inside inefficiencies: a gap, an FVG, a wick, the unfilled part of an opening range gap. That is the signature of a market being held and not yet permitted to run. Buying and selling pressure exist but price is kept artificially in a tight range. When the range is released, the move is cleaner and more sustainable. He wants to see price spend time above old highs (accumulating new longs) or below old lows (accumulating new shorts), not inside inefficiencies ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898), [2024 #33 · 42:00](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2520), [2024 #33 · 1:02:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3757)).
- **A candle characteristic (July 2026).** Several candles form in a tight range without covering much distance. It typically happens near support or resistance or before a directional break, and he expects a breakout soon ([2026 #58 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1245)).
- **A timeframe mismatch (March 2026).** Price spends an extended period in a range with no clear intent. On the 1-minute chart it is chaos, but on the 15-minute chart price is plainly working toward a specific inefficiency. He says it is not that price is hard to trade, it is that you are on the wrong timeframe ([2026 #116 · 52:40](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3160)).

Earlier in 2024 he had used the term for price waiting for a macro or a time. The September 23 lecture extends it into a practical do-not-trade filter ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)). The notes use time distortion and high resistance almost interchangeably in 2026. For example, on April 15, 2026 he describes the high resistance look (back and forth inside consolidations, often inside a wick), calls it time distortion, and says to wait for it to resolve by leaving the range ([2026 #101 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781)).

[[DIAGRAM ch16_d3 | caption: ICT's March 2026 fix for time distortion: go up a timeframe until the target shows (illustrative) | The same chop on the 1-minute chart makes sense once a higher chart shows the gap price is working toward.]]

### How to spot it

1. **Too many wicks that are not adjacent**, so you cannot draw a clean precision level from them ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)).
2. **A PD array that is only touched**, with no displacement away from it.
3. **A "technical" day turning into a holding pattern**, or a medium-impact release (on Sept 23, the 9:45 Flash Manufacturing PMI) that causes a flurry but no run to highs or lows followed by rejection.
4. **Re-entry into the same gap.** Price keeps returning inside it, with overlapping candles, small ranges and many equal-ish wicks. It can look like a range between two obvious highs or lows ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)).
5. **The 2026 test.** You are on a 1-minute or 5-minute chart, price oscillates inside a defined range testing both ends, you have taken three or more trades and been stopped or chopped, and there is no breakout ([2026 #116 · 52:40](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3160)).

ICT admits he cannot teach exactly when not to trade from a static chart: you have to see it. His instruction is to screenshot it and label it so you remember what it looked like ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898)).

### How to trade it

**If the market is being held (2024): stand aside.** Trading ahead of the release gets you chopped up. With muddy morning probabilities he waited until 10:00, when the Silver Bullet window starts (Chapter 17), and used the first presented FVG then. Revisit the afternoon, which should be good once the range is released ([2024 #33 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=898), [2024 #33 · 41:14](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2474), [2024 #33 · 1:02:37](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3757)). The same logic appears in 2026: if there is no first FVG after the opening bell, wait until 10:00 and do not force it ([2026 #99 · 32:58](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1978)).

**If it is a timeframe mismatch (March 2026): go up.**

1. Do not trade a distorted timeframe. You will be stopped out repeatedly.
2. Step up one timeframe at a time (1-minute, 5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour) until the picture clarifies.
3. Ask what inefficiency or level price is working toward on that higher chart.
4. Return to your trading chart and take entries that advance toward that target, not entries inside the range.

The inverse also holds. If a higher timeframe is spending a lot of time in high resistance, drop to a lower one to find trades within the range ([2026 #116 · 55:00](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3300), [2026 #116 · 55:51](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3351)). His wider rule on April 6, 2026: if you see shared ranges or chop with no direction on your working timeframe, go up one or more timeframes and find the inefficiency you are inside ([2026 #111 · 20:18](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=1218)).

**If you are already in a trade, the notes split.** In July 2026 he says use the distortion to tighten stops or take partials ([2026 #58 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1245)). In April 2026, when bodies stay inside a pre-established FVG in your direction and only the wicks poke out, he reads it as accumulation: hold through the wick noise and do not reduce ([2026 #98 · 19:50](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1190)). The difference is where the bodies sit. Bodies respecting the zone in your direction support holding. Bodies drifting out of it do not.

### Worked example

**March 21, 2026 commentary (NQ).** On the 1-minute chart price oscillates in a tight range. ICT boxes it as time distortion and says it feels like a root canal. On the 5-minute chart it is still distorted but less so. On the 15-minute chart it is suddenly clear: price is working toward a specific gap, and the 1-minute chop now makes sense ([2026 #116 · 54:13](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3253), [2026 #116 · 55:00](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3300)).

**April 6, 2026 (NQ).** The 1-minute chart was choppy all morning. On the 5-minute chart there was an inversion FVG (Chapter 7) whose high end the 1-minute bodies refused to touch. That explained the mess and pointed to the next move down ([2026 #111 · 20:18](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=1218), [2026 #111 · 1:20:16](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4816)).

**April 17, 2026 (MNQ).** Candles in a distortion have wicks that hurt, but bodies sit in the lower or middle of the pre-session FVG over three to five candles. He reads that as accumulation: smart money building, not shaking out. Once accumulation ends, price leaves the zone and runs hard ([2026 #98 · 19:50](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1190), [2026 #98 · 20:38](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1238)). The notes add that time distortion is often seen around lunch (11:00 to 13:00 ET) and in low-volume hours.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Blaming the market for a timeframe problem.** Per the 2026 teaching the market is always working toward something. You are on the wrong chart to see it ([2026 #116 · 52:40](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3160)).
- **Reading accumulation into every chop.** Ask whether bodies respect the zone or break out of it. Wicks are noise and bodies carry the information ([2026 #98 · 19:50](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1190)).

## Large-range, small-range and inside days

### What it is

ICT measures a "large range" day against the average daily range of the last three days. If today's range is bigger than that average, the day is outside the normal three-day range. A day that closes with a range smaller than the three-day average is a small range day, which he calls mathematically defined and very sensitive. A stricter version uses a five-day average: when daily ranges start printing smaller than the last five days, a large-range expansion day is close ([2024 #48 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443), [2024 #48 · 1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906)).

The principle is that compression precedes expansion. He pictures a metal spring twisted tighter and tighter until it releases (an image he borrows from Larry Williams), so the smaller the ranges get, the bigger the likely expansion ([2024 #48 · 17:05](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1025)). The large day usually comes the very next day, or at worst the second day after ([2024 #48 · 1:37:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5857)).

[[DIAGRAM ch16_d4 | caption: The three-day average range test, using the Oct 23 to 25, 2024 sequence with illustrative numbers | An inside day that prints below the three-day average flags the next day as a large-range candidate.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is the cycle. Markets alternate between large and small ranges, and small ranges are the precursor to large ones. He credits Larry Williams and repeats it in 2026: a small retracement inside a SIBI (Chapter 5) followed by a small candle in a bearish run means large ranges are coming, with short-term lows as the targets ([2026 #126 · 15:29](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=929)). He also names Toby Crabel's smallest-range-in-seven-days idea (from the book "Street Smarts" by Raschke and Connors, 1995) and Williams' inside-day precursor as influences. He says the three-day average is his own repeating theme ([2024 #48 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443)).

### How to spot it

1. Compute the average daily range of the last three days (optionally five).
2. If yesterday's range is below that average, or yesterday was an inside day, flag the next day as a large-range candidate ([2024 #48 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443)).
3. Take the direction from somewhere else. The compression gives no direction, so you still need higher-timeframe order flow or the draw on liquidity (Chapter 15) ([2024 #48 · 1:37:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5857)).
4. Check the sample. He admits an expansion day inside the last three days can skew the average.

The arithmetic needs only daily highs and lows, so it can be run on any instrument. ICT taught it on NQ and gave no gold figures.

### How to trade it

It is a volatility filter that says **when**, not **which way**. On a large-range day he is willing to trade pre-market and hold, expects little retracement, and in the bullish case expects the day's open to be near the low ([2024 #48 · 1:38:26](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5906)). He says about 90% of large-range days deliver as low resistance runs (he first misspoke "high"). If the day is not a large-range day you will probably meet high resistance, "not all the time but 90%" ([2024 #48 · 42:58](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=2578)). Use the low resistance rules from the first section once the day starts delivering.

### Worked example

**October 23 to 25, 2024 (NQ).** Wednesday October 23 was a large down day. Thursday October 24 sat inside Wednesday's range, with a higher low and a lower high, which flagged Friday as the expected large bullish range day. Thursday then delivered as high resistance and Friday as low resistance, as described in the first section ([2024 #48 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=443), [2024 #48 · 51:44](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3104), [2024 #48 · 1:19:04](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4744)). The diagram uses invented range figures to show the arithmetic.

### When the range itself is the risk

In April 2026, an event-driven market printed 1-minute candles spanning 400 or more handles and 5-minute candles spanning 1,200 or more. ICT calls this not normal. One MNQ 1-minute candle opened at 24,600, reached 24,748 and fell to 24,360, a 388-handle range in 60 seconds. If your size assumes normal 20 to 40 handle stops, such a candle wipes you out. Reduce size or use wider stops and accept fewer trades. Several 1-minute candles of 100 or more handles also count as expansion ([2026 #109 · 39:10](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=2350), [2026 #109 · 42:21](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=2541)). These are NQ and MNQ handles, and the notes give no gold equivalent.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating compression as direction.** The spring only tells you it will release.
- **Assuming every large day is easy.** The 90% is his rough figure, and the other 10% still exist.
- **Expecting the move on the exact next day.** The notes say next day, or at worst the second ([2024 #48 · 1:37:37](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5857)).
- **Confusing a large range with large risk per candle.** A large-range day can still be low resistance and tradable, whereas the 2026 volatility-expansion warning is about candle size relative to your stop.

## Clean days, sloppy days and one-sided runs

### What it is

In August 2024 ICT described the same split in different words. A day or session is **clean** if you see all of the following ([2024 #10 · 1:38:31](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5911)):

1. Impulsive legs that leave a single FVG.
2. A single short-term low.
3. A run to an obvious level.
4. A retracement that makes one FVG.
5. A quick move to another obvious level.

If those are missing, do not push hard and demand more conviction before acting. His apple analogy: one green apple among red ones is found at once, but among many reds your eye wanders. Clean means one obvious draw ([2024 #10 · 46:11](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2771)).

An **unorganized, sloppy or "fickle"** day is the opposite: overlapping candles (a "picket fence"), a balanced price range with no speed, and many little gaps, highs and lows with no obvious side. It is usually the day people get chopped up. The cure is to wait for displacement and then watch how price reacts when it returns into the new inefficiency ([2024 #10 · 1:44:12](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6252), [2024 #10 · 1:54:02](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6842)). The notes also record that he places fickle conditions after the weekly 20% retracement, without explaining the figure.

The **one-sided "train" day** is the other bad environment. ICT cites that week's Thursday and Tuesday. Do not chase: get in before it starts or stay out, and hold if you are on board ([2024 #10 · 29:51](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1791)).

How to use it: screenshot examples of both day types and label them. Beginners should avoid trading the 8:30 news (he told a student to wait until after). On a choppy Friday, do your one setup and be done ([2024 #10 · 1:38:31](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5911)).

### One-sided is not the same as low resistance: the buy program

Two lectures separate a smooth-looking grind from a real low resistance run.

On September 17, 2024 NQ kept ticking higher with no FVG, only tiny volume imbalances that rebalanced at once. Bodies stayed inside the prior PD array and no displacement formed. ICT calls this a **buy program**. It will keep offering higher prices regardless of how many people are short, and it squeezes until it reaches an inefficiency or liquidity (here the 1-minute short-term high and the top of the breaker). Being offside is extremely painful. He declined the short as an illustration of exactly where not to trade, and wanted to see a small portion stay open before shorting. If no FVG is offered, do not force an entry. After a one-sided run, wait for an energetic displacement and an inefficiency ([2024 #29 · 1:43:56](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=6236), [2024 #29 · 1:45:32](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=6332), [2024 #29 · 2:09:04](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=7744)).

In a 2026 lecture he draws the contrast within one day. The overnight move was a slow, systematic grind of very short candle ranges, "death by a thousand cuts" for shorts. He labels it efficient price delivery, not an LRLR, and not something to short. After the 9:30 open, price began inside open space between two daily inefficiencies and ran ten 1-minute candles in a row from 9:41, about 200 handles in ten minutes. That is the low resistance kind. His reading is that the daily suspension block was controlling the overnight grind, and once the open escaped it, the run was free. ## The 50/50 test and the sit-out filters

### What it is

Every day-type label above feeds one decision: trade, trade small, or sit still. ICT's cleanest test does not need any of the vocabulary. Can you build a convincing case for both directions?

- **2022, the one-sided framing test.** If a bullish idea is easy to frame and a bearish one is a hard stretch, the bullish side is high probability, and the reverse. If it "could go either way", do not take it. These are the regret trades, the kind that blew his accounts in the 1990s ([2022 Ep 12 · 1:01:17](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3677)).
- **2024, the 50/50 market.** If the market can be framed both ways, it is not worth putting money behind. Study it ("tape it") with no weight on the outcome. When references conflict, accept that it is not time to trade. If you would need to be perfectly nimble with zero forgiveness for being wrong on either side, that is a high-probability losing day ([2024 #20 · 52:22](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3142), [2024 #20 · 1:02:52](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3772)).
- **2026, one-sidedness.** A high-probability setup is obvious and undeniable. Bodies line up, wicks reject the same level, levels cascade in your favor with minimal retracement noise. One-sided does not mean a straight line. It means the trend is so obvious that wicks and consolidations are minor noise ([2026 #104 · 32:18](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=1938), [2026 #118 · 14:31](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=871)).

[[DIAGRAM ch16_d5 | caption: The day-type decision: can you frame both sides, and are candles sharing ranges? | Two questions sort the day into sit still, minimum size, or trade the clean model.]]

His one-paragraph summary of high-probability trading puts this first. You must know where the market goes next at the right time of day, where a retracement is likely but not beyond, and where partials come so you need not be right until the end. And you first determine when it is LOW probability, which is why each new mentorship opened with high resistance profiles ([2024 #38 · 3:24:44](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=12284), [2024 #38 · 3:25:32](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=12332)).

### How to apply it

1. Before the session, write the best case for each side (bias and narrative are Chapter 15).
2. If one side needs a stretch to defend, it is one-sided. If you can defend both, it is 50/50 and you sit still. Keep your interest in the levels without acting.
3. When the session opens, run the overlap test. Overlapping candles with few gaps take you to minimum size or out. Separated candles leaving open FVGs take you to the clean model.
4. If a needed ingredient never forms, take no trade. It is a missed move, not a losing trade.
5. If the first FVG has not even been traded to and nothing qualifies, close the charts and review that night ([2024 #20 · 1:02:52](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3772)).
6. Check the calendar. When major events are scheduled (a Fed speaker, data, FOMC), precision drops and the market becomes 50/50 because the data can go either way. Identify the model and bias, wait for price to confirm, and if it does not, do nothing and lose zero. You are not wrong; you are waiting ([2026 #95 · 3:27](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=207), [2026 #95 · 13:06](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=786)). Waiting rules around releases are Chapter 14.

## Reading the run after you are in

The same condition vocabulary applies once a trade is open. Chapter 19 covers partials and stops in full, and these are only the condition-specific checks.

- **Speed, distance and velocity.** After price breaks a key level such as equilibrium or a CE, look for acceleration without hesitation. If the break is slow or keeps retracing, conviction is in question ([2026 #82 · 13:42](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=822)).
- **Fewer than seven candles.** After a close above the CE of a wick (candle one), ICT wanted the run to the buy-side to take fewer than seven candles. When it did not, he brought his stop up and scaled out. He insists this is not a rule: "ICT counts candles and he only buys at a specific number" is not what he is saying. It is a quality check, and if the move takes longer than expected you reduce risk or exit instead of hoping. He also wanted price moving within the next three candles after a tiny short-term high, because three candles are enough for a swing low to form ([2026 #87 · 38:02](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2282), [2026 #87 · 43:56](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=2636), [2026 #87 · 56:54](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=3414)).
- **Bodies sharing the same space.** On a short, when bodies stop making new lows and start repeating the same highs near your target, order flow is waning. He took 20% to 50% off, depending on distance to target ([2026 #91 · 22:22](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1342), [2026 #91 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1388)).
- **Consolidation.** Do not hold everything for the ultimate target. Plan exits at minor levels (CE of key wicks, quadrants, octants; Chapter 6) and take 25% to 75% of the position at each. On August 25, 2026 he shorted 6 contracts into an NWOG, planned a partial at the first target, did not take it, and said 2-2-2 in three exits would have been better than his 6-then-4 ([2026 #25 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=977), [2026 #25 · 24:12](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=1452), [2026 #25 · 30:29](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=1829)).

## Answers

1. It is high resistance (overlap test, no FVGs). Expect deep retracements, imprecise delivery and heat. The default is not to trade live. If you do, use minimum size (one contract), the maximum stop, aim only at the first objective, and toss the idea if it has not formed within 30 minutes. Reread "How to trade (or not trade) a high resistance run".
2. News driver at 8:30 or in the first hour of the 9:30 session; not a Monday; clear draws such as clustered new week or new day opening gaps; an opening range gap of at least 45 NQ handles, preferably 75 or more. The gap condition depends on an index-futures cash open, and ICT gave no gold equivalent. Reread "The pre-session recipe for an easy day".
3. The average is 240, and 120 is below it, so the next day is a large-range candidate. The large day usually comes the next day, at worst the second. It gives no direction. You still need higher-timeframe order flow or the draw on liquidity. Reread "Large-range, small-range and inside days".
4. He says mislabelling hard days as S&D is an excuse, and that two S&D days in one week never happen. A hard day with many FVG revisits and deep retracements is high resistance. Reread "Seek and destroy days", Mistakes.
5. Do not trade the distorted timeframe. Go up (5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour) until you find the inefficiency or level price is working toward, then trade 1-minute entries toward that target. It has worked when the chop makes sense against a higher-timeframe gap or target. Reread "Time distortion and consolidation".
6. The buy program lesson: with no FVG and no displacement, a slow grind will keep offering higher prices and squeeze shorts until it reaches an inefficiency or liquidity. Do not force the short. The 9:41 run is the low resistance kind: big-bodied candles (ten 1-minute candles, about 200 handles) through open space. Reread "One-sided is not the same as low resistance".
7. No. ICT says that when you can frame both directions the market is 50/50 and not worth money, and sitting still is a position that cannot lose. If price later does the bullish thing, sitting still was still the right call, not a regret. Reread "The 50/50 test and the sit-out filters".
8. Never trail it or run it quickly to breakeven (Oct 2024); be more forgiving until the first partial (2026 #87); use tight stops and exit quickly (July 2026); maximum stop on an FVG entry (Oct 28, 2024). The common thread is that you do not tinker with the stop; the notes do not settle the width. Reread "How to trade (or not trade) a high resistance run".
9. Long case: bodies inside the FVG with wicks as noise is accumulation, so hold through the wicks and do not reduce, as long as bodies keep respecting the zone. Short case: bodies sharing the same space near the target signals waning order flow, so take roughly 20% to 50% off. Reread "Time distortion and consolidation" and "Reading the run after you are in".
