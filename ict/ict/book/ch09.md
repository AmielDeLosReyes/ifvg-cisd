An order block is the candle, or run of same-coloured candles, that sits just before a displacement, and ICT's most developed claim is that one price on it, the opening price of the run, marks a change in the state of delivery (CISD). This chapter teaches the order block, the CISD that validates it, the breaker and the rest of the block family (mitigation, rejection, propulsion and suspension blocks), and it flags every place where his definitions shifted or clash. After it you should be able to mark a block from its first candle's open, say what validates or breaks it, and decide where to enter and where the stop goes. Fair value gaps are Chapters 5 to 8 and wicks are Chapter 10; here they appear only where a block leans on them.

## The order block

### What it is

An order block is the footprint of a turn. Price has been delivered one way, a few candles of the other colour print, and then price runs hard the opposite way. The bullish block is the down-close candle or candles (each closes below its open) before a rally. The bearish block is the up-close candle or candles before a drop.

ICT's definition from 2024 on can be read candle by candle.

1. Start at the displacement, the fast move that leaves a fair value gap (displacement is Chapter 2, the gap is Chapter 5).
2. Look at the candle immediately to its left. If it is the right colour, look at the one before it, and keep going until one is not. The whole run is one block, not several ([2024 #7 · 2:31:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9092)). Three up-close candles are one consecutive bearish block ([2022 Ep 18 · 38:17](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2297)).
3. The key price is the open of the first candle in the run, the one furthest from the displacement. In a down-close run that is the highest open. ICT calls it the change in the state of delivery ([2024 #7 · 2:32:21](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9141)).
4. The block's range runs from that open to the close of the last candle in the run, and the narrative sits in the bodies. The midpoint of that body range is the mean threshold ([2024 #7 · 2:34:51](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9291)).
5. The block is validated when price trades through the open in the direction of the displacement (above it for a bullish block) ([2024 #7 · 2:52:31](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10351)).
6. It needs a supporting array nearby, usually the fair value gap the displacement left. In 2022 he put it flatly: without an imbalance there is no order block ([2022 Ep 18 · 40:39](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2439)). In 2024 he said a block is "never an island unto itself" ([2024 #7 · 2:31:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9092)).

In 2022 he gave a simpler public form: a down-close candle, an imbalance left when price moves away, and a narrative that price is going to buy-side liquidity. Nothing else, and no engulfing candle ([2022 Ep 13 · 17:38](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1058)). His fuller 2022 test for a high-probability block had three parts: it sits inside a liquidity pool, it has a gap, and a market structure shift (MSS, Chapter 2) follows ([2022 Ep 3 · 14:32](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=872)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that price is delivered by an algorithm that moves between two states, offering buy side and offering sell side. A run of up-close candles that lifts price began at the first candle's open. When price later trades back below that open, the state of delivery changes from offering buy side to offering sell side, now reaching for sell stops. The algorithm remembers the level, and that is what makes it sensitive ([2022 Ep 3 · 24:06](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1446)).

He gives a bookmark analogy. Price was falling, paused with up-close candles, then resumed. The algorithm marks that spot and comes back to it, the way you return to a bookmarked page to pick up the story. The displacement that follows is what qualifies the block, because it creates the gap ([2022 Ep 19 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1388), [25:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1529)).

Three points follow from this, all in his words.

- **It is not supply and demand.** There is no "fresh zone" requirement, and he will trade through candles and fractals ([2022 Ep 18 · 40:39](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2439), [2024 #7 · 2:31:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9092)). A demand zone needs an anchor of buying, but in one October 2024 trade price was below every low and no zone existed ([2024 #38 · 1:18:32](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4712)).
- **The algorithm does not know your stop.** It works from the common logic of stops placed or trailed just beyond recent short-term highs and lows ([2022 Ep 19 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=1388)).
- **Candles alone fail.** There is no "institutional candle". A block matters only when narrative and time agree ([2024 #6 · 1:07:05](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4025)).

He also claims the reaction is exact. In an October 2024 NQ example the low of the very next candle matched the order block's mean threshold to the tick, which he offers as evidence of a price engine ([2024 #48 · 1:15:03](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4503)). Treat that as his claim.

### How to spot it

For a bullish block; mirror it for a bearish one.

1. Start from the narrative. You need a bias and a draw on liquidity (Chapters 3 and 15). In a bullish bias only bullish blocks, bullish breakers and bullish gaps count ([2024 #48 · 1:10:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4214)).
2. Look for the liquidity event: a run on sell-side stops, ideally inside or just after a discount array. In 2022 he wanted the series to sit inside the liquidity pool ([2022 Ep 3 · 14:32](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=872)).
3. Find the displacement that breaks a short-term high and leaves a gap.
4. Count down-close candles leftward from the displacement. Mark the open of the first one.
5. Draw the body range from that open to the lowest close and mark its midpoint. Ignore the wicks ([2024 #8 · 1:29:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5393)).
6. Check for support: the gap overlapping the lower part of the block, or a graded level such as a quadrant or octant (Chapter 6). He wants the array at or below the lower quadrant of the dealing range in a bullish case, and skipped a nearby gap because most of its body sat above that quadrant ([2024 #48 · 1:18:15](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4695)).
7. Check the clock. His August 2024 example formed after 8:30 ET, when he says the algorithm starts looking for liquidity or inefficiency ([2024 #1 · 53:10](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3190)).

A rule of thumb from August 2024: inside a macro (Chapter 12), price needs a reason to go to liquidity. Look for a short-term low to run, an order block, or a gap. You will usually have one, at best two, and never all three at the same price and time ([2024 #7 · 2:49:18](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10158), [2:50:06](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10206)).

### Reading the block: four prices and the mean threshold

ICT names four prices on a block: its low, its open, its mean threshold and its high ([2022 Ep 35 · 0:05](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=5), [2022 Ep 19 · 1:20:58](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4858)). For a bearish block above price, these are his 2022 rankings of how likely each is to be reached.

|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Bottom of the lowest candle in the run | Easiest to reach from below, so the first target | [2022 Ep 19 · 1:20:58](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4858) |
| Open | Open of the first candle in the run | The CISD line; the sweet spot for entries | [2024 #6 · 1:11:48](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4308) |

| High | Top of the block | The least likely level to trade; price should not pierce it | [2022 Ep 21 · 7:17](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=437), [2022 Ep 25 · 8:02](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=482) |

**The mean threshold (MT).** MT is the midpoint of an order block. The word for the midpoint of a gap or a wick is consequent encroachment (CE, Chapter 6). Some students call both CE, and he corrected that ([2024 #6 · 1:21:45](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4905), [2024 #13 · 14:45](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=885)). In 2026 the same word covers a breaker's midpoint ([2026 #34 · 14:15](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=855)).

Four rules for reading price against the block.

- **Bodies decide, wicks colour.** He wants bodies to stay in the half nearest the open. Wicks can spike deeper, even through the MT, without hurting the block ([2024 #7 · 2:34:51](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9291)). A wick may poke a little past the whole range and it still counts ([2024 #8 · 1:27:26](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5246)).
- **A body close through the MT is a breach.** For a bullish block, a 5-minute body closing below the MT means the block is breached and no longer a good place to expect price to stop. That does not guarantee lower prices, but lower becomes more likely. The ideal is a drop into the upper half with the MT never touched ([2024 #40 · 26:52](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1612), [27:42](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1662)).
- **Perfect delivery touches the open, not the low.** The cleanest bullish block is price touching the open, perhaps reaching toward the MT, with no later body below the MT, then going higher ([2024 #35 · 23:26](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1406)). He would never enter expecting price to reach the candle's low, even though it sometimes does ([2024 #48 · 1:27:50](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5270)).
- **Tolerance is a tick or two.** A body should not exceed a PD array's boundary; if it does, by one tick, "no more than two". Wicks can go a few ticks outside. On NQ he allows about two ticks, on ES generally one ([2024 #18 · 1:21:28](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4888)).

Reaching the MT of a bearish block has two outcomes, and he teaches both. If price reaches the MT and is rejected, the block is working. In 2022 he said that trading up into a daily bearish block's MT without rejection bodes well for a continuation through to the short-term high beyond it ([2022 Ep 35 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=527)). In August 2024 price worked into the MT of a daily bearish block (19,881.75 in the notes) and he looked for rejection there ([2024 #16 · 12:24](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=744)).

One more calibration. In September 2024 he ranked arrays by rigidity: a volume imbalance is the weakest, because price moves back and forth through it; wick midpoints are footholds; and the order block is the firmest. Adjust the stop to the array, tight for a block and flexible for a volume imbalance ([2024 #35 · 21:52](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1312), [24:13](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1453)).

## The change in the state of delivery (CISD)

### What it is

The change in the state of delivery is the moment delivery flips from one side to the other. In ICT's order-block teaching it happens at one price: the open of the run of candles that delivered price. For a bearish block, buy-side delivery has been lifting price through a run of up-close candles. When price trades below the open of that run, delivery changes from buy side to sell side and the block is validated ([2024 #8 · 1:24:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5056)). Once it flips, every premium array that forms in the new sell program matters: new short-term highs, new bearish up-close candles and new gaps ([2024 #8 · 1:18:32](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4712)).

He treats the two names as one thing. On a daily chart in September 2024 he called the open of the up-close candle both the CISD level and "my order block" ([2024 #37 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1388)). Later that day he used the open of three up-close candles as the last line in the sand: while short, he did not want price trading above it ([2024 #37 · 2:25:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=8737)).

### Which open to use

The open of the first candle in the run is the 2024 rule. The notes show several other choices, and they do not obviously agree.

| Situation | Open to use | Source |
|---|---|---|

| A wrong choice he corrected | Marking it on the lowest down-close candle. He asked a creator to fix a video that did this | [2024 #17 · 1:25:32](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=5132) |
| Two down-close candles, drawn live in 2022 | The higher of the two, using its uppermost open | [2022 Ep 26 · 8:45](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=525) |
| Open that lines up with a level (2026) | The candle whose open matches a significant level such as relative equal lows; he calls it a technical choice, not an arbitrary one | [2026 #36 · 2:32](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=152) |
| Largest candle (2026) | In a falling run, the largest down-close candle, judged on its body | [2026 #72 · 44:41](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2681) |
| Last candle of larger candles (2026) | Scan left for the largest consecutive up-close candles and mark the open of the last one | [2026 #65 · 0:50](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=50) |
| Size rule (April 2026, garbled) | A rule keyed to candle sizes: the last candle's open if it is the largest or the smallest, the first candle's open if all are equal | [2026 #95 · 42:26](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2546) |

A run of equal candles gives the first open under every version. Where sizes differ, the 2026 notes lean toward the largest or last candle, and the April 2026 note is hard to follow, so check it against the video before relying on it. He warns in that lecture that many people misstate the rule.

### Trade through, or close?

His repeated, explicit position is that trading through the open is enough. The block does not need a close beyond it ([2024 #8 · 1:18:32](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4712), [2024 #24 · 2:25:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=8749), [2026 #49 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2214), [2026 #71 · 16:35](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=995)). In 2026 he adds that other teachers say a close is required, and answers that it is not ([2026 #57 · 4:55](https://youtu.be/KHkUPAsdyfk?t=295)). One reason he gives: when smooth lows are taken, sell-side liquidity has been purged, so a close is not needed ([2026 #71 · 16:35](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=995)). The condition is the same every time: your narrative has to be right.

The notes also contain the stricter wording. On August 12, 2024 the validation was a later candle closing below the open ([2024 #6 · 1:10:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4253)). A July 2026 note says changes of delivery do not apply until price closes below the open ([2026 #65 · 0:50](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=50)), and a March 2026 note (2026 #123) says only bodies count, not wicks. The notes do not reconcile these, and those summaries may be loose. The block's difference from an inversion FVG, which does need a close (Chapter 7), is worth keeping in mind.

### CISD versus a market structure shift

A market structure shift needs price to trade below a short-term low and close there. The block is validated earlier, when price trades below its open ([2024 #8 · 1:24:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5056)). In 2022 he described the order: as soon as price trades below the block's open the algorithm starts delivering sell side, and then it breaks the swing low, having "changed gears internally" ([2022 Ep 18 · 46:07](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2767)). So the CISD is the earlier, cheaper clue and the MSS is the later, more visible one (Chapter 2).

### Other uses of the phrase

- **A complete model.** In February 2026 he sequenced it as daily draw, higher-timeframe premise, 15-minute CISD, lower-timeframe entry. The 15-minute CISD was drawn from a candle's open, shown against a premium SIBI that buy-side runs up into ([2026 #129 · 17:24](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1044)).
- **Delivery sought.** In March 2026 he described it as the change from seeking buy-side liquidity to seeking sell-side liquidity, often marked by an opening price that becomes the block ([2026 #120 · 32:45](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1965)).
- **Loose readings.** Two 2026 notes describe a CISD as a change in candle size or character, such as a large candle followed by a smaller one ([2026 #89 · 42:17](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2537)). These do not match the opening-price definition. Treat them as unconfirmed.

### Worked example: August 14, 2024, NQ 1-minute

The setup. The day before, ICT pointed to the July 28, 2024 new week opening gap (NWOG, Chapter 11), plus a daily short-term high and a daily volume imbalance, as an upside objective. Price built relative equal highs and failed to reach the NWOG on the 8:30 ET news run ([2024 #8 · 1:18:32](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=4712)).

1. After the open, price ran into the NWOG and swept the highs.
2. Two up-close candles formed at the top. Together they are the bearish block, and its range is the bodies of both ([2024 #8 · 1:29:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5393)).
3. Price then traded below the open of that two-candle run. That is the CISD ([2024 #8 · 1:27:26](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=5246)).
4. It fell to the CE of the 8:30 wick, retraced into a gap and kept going down. The block sat just below the NWOG high.
5. The stop for a short taken on the retrace goes one tick above the highest point of the premium wick above the block. If a spike wicks up and you are stopped, he re-enters short there ([2024 #8 · 3:11:08](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11468)).

## Trading the order block

### Entries

Pick the entry that fits the timeframe and the narrative. All of these are ICT's, from different years.

- **Return to the open, or into the upper half.** For a bullish block, any return to the open or into the upper half of the run is the entry. If you know the range, you do not have to sweat the best price. Extend the open forward and watch whether bodies respect it ([2024 #7 · 2:34:51](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9291), [2:38:03](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9483)).
- **Sell the first return.** For a bearish block, sell as price trades back up to the open, on an uptick, after the next candle has opened below it. The sweet spot is the open, or between the open and the candle's low. Trade it the first time it returns; in his example price went only about one tick above the entry area ([2024 #6 · 1:11:48](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4308)).
- **Open to mean threshold.** On a daily bearish block in September 2024 he shorted retests from the open up to the halfway point and wanted the upper half never traded through ([2024 #24 · 3:00:17](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10817)).
- **Limit order plus spread.** In 2022 the buy limit sat at the series open inside the gap, plus about 3 pips "or whatever for spread" ([2022 Ep 3 · 15:20](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=920)). In 2024 he cites spread as the reason price trades one tick past an exact level ([2024 #45 · 23:38](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1418)).
- **The gap that fails.** In a bearish narrative, a retrace that cannot even touch the CE of a SIBI is a weakness signature. He then treats that up candle as a bearish block, waits for price to move away and come back up into the bottom of the candle, and sells. NQ 1-minute, August 7, 2024, shortly before 1:00 PM ET: candle low 18,320.25, fill 18,321.25, six contracts. Had the retrace touched the CE, he would have waited for another attempt a little higher ([2024 #3 · 1:44:47](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6287), [1:47:12](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6432)).
- **Inside the candle before it is obvious.** If price runs up and stops short of a wick's CE, the next candle that opens and trades into that candle's body can be entered before anyone calls it a block ([2024 #8 · 2:34:43](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9283)).
- **Add on returns.** In 2026 he adds on the retrace to a validated bearish block ([2026 #70 · 6:40](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=400), [2026 #49 · 37:44](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2264)).

### Targets and management

- **Targets.** Buy stops above relative equal highs ([2022 Ep 13 · 12:51](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=771)). - **Partials.** You can take partials at any PD array on the way and need not hold to the lowest low. He says to identify them before entry.

### Which block to use

A block is a candidate until it passes his filters. The October 25, 2024 lecture lists them.

1. A bias and a draw on liquidity are set. Only bullish arrays in a bullish bias ([2024 #48 · 1:10:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4214)).
2. The time fits: a macro such as 8:30 with the algorithm spooling, mindful of the calendar and the day of the week.
3. Price first trades into a discount array, then displaces, breaks a short-term or swing high and leaves a gap or block. He waits for the algorithm to tip its hand and does not buy just because price is in a zone.
4. The array sits at or below the lower quadrant of the dealing range, in a bullish case ([2024 #48 · 1:18:15](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4695)).
5. For blocks, use the body of the lowest down-close candle, two consecutive closes as one range, and the mean threshold ([2024 #48 · 1:14:15](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4455)).
6. The gap should leave part of itself open, in low-resistance conditions (Chapter 16).

From 2026 add a seventh: the block should lie on a graded level. A bearish block has to touch or lie on a graded level, the upper quadrant in his example, to be "perfect" ([2026 #49 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2214)). A bullish block inside a bullish gap, anchored to the lower octant, with bodies not violating its MT, is valid ([2026 #44 · 19:22](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=1162)). Octants and quadrants are Chapter 6.

## Breaker blocks

### What it is

A breaker is a failed swing that becomes a level. For the bearish version, price makes a high, then a low, then a higher high that runs the buy-side liquidity above the first high. It then drops back through the low. The bearish breaker is the down-close candle right before the big move up that took out the short-term high, the lowest down-close candle before the run-up ([2022 Ep 10 · 40:07](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2407), [2022 Ep 39 · 21:00](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1260)). It can be a run of consecutive down-close candles ([2024 #2 · 1:16:06](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4566)).

The bullish breaker is the mirror: low, high, lower low. Find the high in between, extend it forward, and when price trades back down into that area it is the breaker ([2022 Ep 37 · 0:02](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=2)). In 2024 he put it this way: when price drops through relative equal lows, the whole run of up-close candles before the drop is the breaker, and the last up-close candle is the most sensitive ([2024 #1 · 1:23:49](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5029)). His 2026 shorthand is "high, low, higher high" ([2026 #54 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=1222)).

[[DIAGRAM ch09_d3 | caption: A bearish breaker, step by step (illustrative) | Notice that the breaker candle sits at the low, before the run-up, and the trade comes only after price has dropped back below that low.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the run above the high is a run on buy-side stops, and after it the market should not hold the high. Price falls back through the low, and the zone left behind is where it will be repelled. He says the market will mostly stay in the lower half of the breaker candle ([2022 Ep 10 · 40:56](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2456)). In 2026 he describes the block as an inversion: it was first used as a bullish block to run the liquidity above, and it flips once price trades below its low ([2026 #34 · 12:41](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=761)). After a breaker repels price, expect acceleration through the sell side below, because price should not hold the low to go higher ([2022 Ep 39 · 21:48](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1308)). Bodies respect the level even when wicks run through it ([2024 #1 · 1:23:49](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5029)).

### How to spot it

1. Have a higher-timeframe premise and a directional draw. Without them you cannot tell a breaker from a market structure shift ([2024 #1 · 1:22:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4979)).
2. Know what the market is likely reaching for. In 2022 he said that without that, "it's not a breaker, it's a gamble" ([2022 Ep 39 · 20:13](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1213)).
3. Find the stop run: high, low, then a higher high that takes the buy side.
4. Mark the down-close candle or candles at the low, just before the run-up. Use bodies, not wicks ([2026 #65 · 12:46](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=766)).
5. Wait for price to drop back through the block's low. That displacement validates it, and the SIBI it leaves is the sell-side imbalance ([2026 #34 · 14:15](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=855)).
6. Look at the leg from the breaker up to the high. If it holds a gap, that gap may be the better entry (below).

### How to trade it

- **Entry in 2022.** Short the gap inside or below the breaker. Stop just above the breaker candle, not above the higher imbalance further up ([2022 Ep 10 · 40:07](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2407)).
- **2024 refinement.** If the stop-hunt leg has a gap, navigate away from the breaker and use that premium gap. Others trade the breaker or the low and will not sell near the high. The gap left by the drop is his "sweet spot", the OTE of the 2022 model. Use the breaker only when no such gap exists ([2024 #2 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4804)).
- **Stop in 2024.** Above the highest high of the consecutive down-close candles, plus a tick or two, and often the exact high, because a good breaker will not want to go back into its full range. If there is a gap inside the stop-hunt leg, price can return all the way up into it with a small spike outside while the body stays inside, so the breaker-based stop is not safe then ([2024 #2 · 1:28:53](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5333)).
- **Entry and stop in 2026.** Sell at the mean threshold or deeper in the lower half, the "VIP seating" midpoint. The stop goes at the high of the candle that created the SIBI through the low. If that is too big, trade with lower leverage. He dislikes a retail break-and-retest at the candle's low because it adds heat ([2026 #34 · 14:15](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=855), [17:26](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1046), [18:13](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1093)). In July 2026 he put sell stops just above the breaker's body, not its wick, and read a close above the breaker as failure ([2026 #65 · 24:01](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1441)).
- **Displacement, not a poke.** Do not sell the first wicky poke below a breaker. Wait for a body to close below with displacement. Two wicks and no displacement mean price will probably make one more run up first ([2024 #25 · 21:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1282), [24:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1473)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Breaker versus market structure shift.** The same swings can read as either. Only the higher-timeframe premise and draw decide ([2024 #1 · 1:22:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4979)).
- **Breaker versus order block.** A block marks where delivery changed. A breaker is a failed swing where stops were run. In 2026 he describes a bearish breaker as an old bullish block that has inverted ([2026 #34 · 12:41](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=761)). An FVG that overlaps a breaker at the same price is what he called a "unicorn" in July 2026 (see the end of this chapter).
- **Looser labels.** In August 2024 he called an up-close candle with a long lower tail, where structure broke and longs were cleared, the bullish breaker. The notes say this is not the classic definition ([2024 #18 · 1:19:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4744)).
- **Conflicting 2026 notes.** The July and June 2026 notes describe the third swing as a higher high that stays below the first high ([2026 #65 · 12:46](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=766), [2026 #80 · 3:10](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=190)). One April 2026 note marks the original broken swing itself as the breaker level ([2026 #105 · 4:41](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=281)). These are summaries; check the videos. This chapter follows the version in which the stop run is the point.

- **2024.** The BabyPips history (about 2011 to 2012) and the gap-in-the-leg refinement ([2024 #2 · 1:15:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4516)); body-versus-wick rules; looser uses; and the remark that everything he keys off has an inverted aspect: PD arrays can be treated the opposite way once violated ([2024 #29 · 1:35:09](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5709)).
- **2026.** The inversion framing, mean-threshold entry, and the compact "high, low, higher high" wording.

## Mitigation blocks

The notes use this name twice and define it only loosely.

In 2022, after a market structure break to the upside, price came back down into a mitigation block that also held a gap. His call was to buy and aim for the buy stops in the PM pool. The notes list 4532 and 4540 as ranges to think about but do not say how they relate to the block ([2022 Ep 16 · 34:23](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2063), [35:09](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2109)).

In March 2026 he drew the contrast with an order block. A down-close candle would have been a bullish block, and its gap a bullish FVG, if price had been going up for real. But in a bearish narrative where the rally was only a run for liquidity, the same candle is a mitigation block: a rejection point, not an entry. His NQ example on March 13 was the 9:30 open, which lay on the upper quadrant of the RTH opening range gap (Chapter 11). It served as a parent block, then later as a mitigation block when price ran the buy side and reversed sharply ([2026 #120 · 30:25](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1825), [38:18](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=2298)). The rule he states is that a block's function is set by the order flow that follows and by the narrative, not by how the candle formed.

The notes give no candle count, stop or target for a mitigation block. Treat it as a role a block can take on, not a separate pattern.

## Rejection blocks

### What it is

The notes hold two readings. The better supported one is body-based: a rejection block is a candle's closing price, a level that bodies cannot get beyond. He traces the idea to the PD array lectures in month four of his 2016 mentorship ([2026 #32 · 22:08](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1328), [2026 #113 · 1:05:49](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3949)).

- **At a block.** The close of an order-block candle is the rejection block, used as a stop ([2024 #4 · 1:08:37](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4117), [2024 #6 · 1:11:48](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4308)).
- **In a swing.** The lowest down-close candle in a swing is a rejection block ([2024 #11 · 42:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2533)). For a swing high, it is the highest up-close candle in the swing, at its closing price ([2024 #18 · 2:43:34](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9814), [2026 #40 · 45:45](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2745)).
- **After a short run.** An up-close candle that runs into a PD array without reaching its CE and then drops is a run on rejection. Its closing price is the rejection block ([2026 #113 · 1:05:49](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3949)).

The second reading is wick-based. Several 2026 note sets describe a candle with a long wick and a close back away from it, rejecting the level the wick reached ([2026 #106 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=774), [2026 #117 · 1:01:28](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=3688), [2026 #119 · 15:03](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=903)). Those notes are looser. A line in the August 26, 2024 lecture was also unclear, and the notes say not to read it as a definition ([2024 #17 · 0:08:05](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=485)). Both readings agree on the practical meaning: bodies should not get past this price. The wick side of it belongs to Chapter 10.

### How to trade it

- **As a stop reference.** In an August 2024 NQ short his stop sat above the rejection block, the closing price of the highest up-close candle in the swing. A good rejection block is traded to one time, and price does not go back above it. He was comfortable only because other conditions lined up: a propulsion block, an inversion and an untouched upper half ([2024 #18 · 2:43:34](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9814), [2:51:38](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10298)). The later run reached exactly 19592.75, the close of that up-close candle, and reversed ([2024 #18 · 2:50:01](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10201)). The notes mention a caption reading 19552.75 once and call it an error.
- **As a partial.** A candle's close below the bodies, the wick's CE, the lower quadrant and the old low are all places he takes partials (2024 #8). At the low of a drive down he looks at the area where bodies stop, not simply at the low, and wants some position off before it turns ([2026 #2 · 32:19](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1939)).
- **As a trigger.** In February 2026 his plan was to mark the close of the up-close candle after buy side ran, sell once price trades below it, and put the stop at the middle of the new week opening gap with one contract ([2026 #129 · 29:30](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1770)).
- **As a read.** Price taking out a rejection block shows strength, and not every rejection leads to a reversal ([2026 #104 · 25:44](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=1544)).

## Propulsion blocks

### What it is

The notes give several wordings that share a core: a propulsion block is an order block used as a launch point, and its body holds the correct side of a mean threshold.

- **August 2024.** After a bearish block's open is crossed, an up-close candle that sits there is another block, which he calls a propulsion block, an accelerator that pushes price lower ([2024 #16 · 13:59](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=839)). A later lecture is more exact: a candle that trades back into a prior block, one that reached an inefficiency, and whose body does not breach, close above or cross that block's mean threshold. He treats that as a tipping of the hand, and the next candle that digs into the prior block adds conviction ([2024 #18 · 2:36:26](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9386), [2:39:37](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=9577)).
- **March 2026, parent and child.** The propulsion block is a child block that trades into a parent block lying on a gradient level. It need not lie on a level itself, because it borrows the parent's discount sensitivity. In a bullish case it cannot go down to half of the parent's range ([2026 #120 · 32:45](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1965)).
- **March 2026, last candle.** The propulsion block is the last down-close candle of a series. When price trades above its open, it propels price up, "like rocket fuel". The minimum is that price never breaches its mean threshold on a retrace ([2026 #123 · 27:00](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1620)).
- **April 2026, two candles.** Two consecutive down-close candles qualify if the lower body stays above the mean threshold of the higher one. A wick may rarely touch just below the mean; the rule is about bodies ([2026 #97 · 15:44](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=944)).

[[DIAGRAM ch09_d4 | caption: A propulsion block against its parent order block (illustrative, after 2024 #18 and 2026 #120 and #123) | Notice that the child's body stays above the parent's mean threshold, and the trigger is price trading above the child's own open.]]

### How to trade it

Enter near the propulsion block's open and add at the CE of the next wick ([2024 #18 · 2:54:49](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10489)). Or enter when price trades above the open of a bullish one, with the stop just below the block or its mean threshold and the target at the next unmet buy-side liquidity ([2026 #123 · 27:47](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=1667)). Two in a row are "double rocket fuel" in his words. After it forms, it acts as a defence: price should not trade back into its mean ([2026 #97 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=1041)).

The notes disagree on which candle is the propulsion block: the child that retests a parent, the last candle of a series, or a two-candle pair. The notes also say the full rules are saved for his books, and that the 2024 captions are rough on exactly which candle is which. Use the shared test, the body against the mean threshold, and check the video for the rest.

## Suspension blocks

This is a 2026 name, and the notes are the least settled here. One definition is stated far more often than the rest, so build on it and treat the others as open questions.

### What it is

The best-supported definition: a suspension block is a daily BISI or SIBI (a buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency, or the reverse; both are gaps from Chapters 5 to 8) with a volume imbalance at its high and another at its low. A volume imbalance is a place where two neighbouring candles' bodies do not overlap. The single candle between them is "suspended" between the two ([2026 #72 · 47:07](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2827), [2026 #40 · 26:26](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1586), [2026 #19 · 30:20](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1820)).

The test is mechanical. Compare the open and close of the candles on each side of the gap; if the bodies do not overlap, there is a volume imbalance there, even if it is "very, very small" ([2026 #47 · 1:34](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=94)). A BISI with a volume imbalance only at its low is just a gap, not a suspension block ([2026 #21 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=97), [52:58](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=3178)). On a daily chart he toggles the ETH/RTH setting to reveal an imbalance that one setting hides ([2026 #138 · 1:13:40](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4420)).

[[DIAGRAM ch09_d5 | caption: A suspension block drawn as three candles (illustrative; geometry follows 2026 #72's one-sentence definition) | Notice that the middle candle is the block, and the two small gaps between bodies at its top and bottom are what make it one.]]

He says it is not a liquidity void. A void is a stretch with no trading at all; this is a gap between candles. He dates the idea to a 2025 release ([2026 #141 · 44:48](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2688), [45:38](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2738)).

### How to use it

- **Bearish block above price.** Its high acts as a premium array: price rallies into it and is sent lower. If price trades below the volume imbalance, the CE of the block is the next target, "premium array down to discount array" ([2026 #49 · 9:06](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=546), [9:53](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=593)).
- **Bullish block above price.** A real bullish day should cut through it "like a hot knife through butter". Hesitation signals weakness ([2026 #139 · 10:01](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=601)).
- **Grading.** Divide it into quadrants and octants and treat each level as a reaction point for intraday entries ([2026 #71 · 8:30](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=510), [2026 #140 · 4:47](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=287)). Chapter 6 covers the grid.
- **Inversion.** Price that closes through a bearish one treats it as an inversion FVG, and a failure to hold above its midpoint turns it bearish ([2026 #72 · 47:07](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2827), [2026 #133 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=390)).
- **Weekly and intraday.** The weekly version is usually the gap from the new week's open ([2026 #56 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=243)). A small FVG treated as one served as a silver bullet entry: he shorted its CE with two contracts ([2026 #32 · 1:14:15](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4455)).

### Looser descriptions, unresolved

Other lectures use the word for something else, and the notes do not reconcile them.

- A small-body candle with wicks at both ends that marks a pause before the next candle picks a direction ([2026 #66 · 15:16](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=916), [2026 #67 · 5:19](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=319)).
- A consolidation range, in one lecture also called a breaker block ([2026 #114 · 6:25](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=385)).
- An order block with a volume imbalance at its end ([2026 #130 · 8:08](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ?t=488)).
- A zone just below a relative equal low that he avoids targeting ([2026 #35 · 0:47](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=47)).

If you see the word in a video, check which of these he means. The gap between two bodies, at both ends, is the version he repeats most.

*Vacuum block.* The notes treat it as a wick-and-gap idea, not a suspension variant. It is covered in Chapter 8.

## Other names in the family

These appear in a few 2026 lectures and are not defined fully in the notes. Treat them as labels to recognise, not tested setups.

- **Unicorn.** A breaker that sits at the same price as an FVG. On NQ 1m in London on July 14, 2026 he bought a retracement into such a pair after a sweep of the overnight low and called it "super strong". The notes give no stop or prices ([2026 #63 · 8:52](https://youtu.be/bz4QNPcpmWE?t=532)).
- **Bolo.** A defensive PD array, not an entry. One reading builds it from a rejection block (a candle that wicks down and closes back up) plus the CE of that wick; the zone between them is the bolo. He keeps the stop outside it and treats a touch as a sign the idea is wrong, so you do not re-enter on the same idea ([2026 #97 · 18:57](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=1137), [2026 #96 · 43:58](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=2638), [44:47](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=2687)). The spelling is uncertain in the captions. A propulsion block can play the same defensive role ([2026 #97 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=1041)).
- **Hangman PD array.** A candle whose body closes near a key level such as a CE while its wick runs well beyond it and rejects back; buyers who went long into it are "hanging". The note's description is hard to follow, so watch the video ([2026 #58 · 27:09](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1629)).

## The fulcrum projection

A fulcrum point is a measured-move target he links to blocks and swing structure. The core idea has not moved much between 2022 and 2026.

In the 2022 swing projection, a swing low is decisively broken and price retraces into an FVG. The broken low is the fulcrum point. Measure from the swing high down to it and project the same distance lower ([2022 Ep 16 · 8:06](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=486)). Anchor the measure on candle bodies, not wicks: the highest body of the high and the lowest body of the low ([2022 Ep 16 · 8:53](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=533)). On ES 15m that gave 4514.50 and then 4501.25, and the day's low was exactly 4501.25 ([2022 Ep 16 · 12:05](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=725)).

The projection is valid only if the swing low breaks. Choose the level that lines up with liquidity or a big figure; on EURUSD that was 1.0901 beside relative equal lows at 1.09 ([2022 Ep 17 · 5:51](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=351)).

In 2026 he pictures a door and hinge: the low where a rally started is the hinge, and the rally's size swings equally far below it ([2026 #41 · 26:47](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=1607)). One lecture names the swing high the fulcrum instead and calls the projection a reasonable estimate, never the only target ([2026 #35 · 4:46](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=286)). That naming clash is unresolved. Targets are covered in Chapter 19.

## Check yourself

1. Four up-close candles of different sizes sit at the top of a rally, and then price drops hard and leaves an FVG. Which candle's open is the 2024 key price, and what does the block's range cover?
2. Price trades three ticks beyond the key price of your bearish block but never closes beyond it. Is the block validated under ICT's repeated 2024 and 2026 position? Name one place the notes word it more strictly.
3. A bullish block is three down-close candles. A 5-minute body closes below its mean threshold. What does that tell you, and what does it not?
4. A student calls the midpoint of a block "the CE". What is the correction, and where does CE apply?
5. You sold the open of a bearish block that has a long premium wick above it. Where does the August 14, 2024 stop go, and what does he do if it is hit?
6. Price prints a high, then a low, then a lower high, then breaks the low. Is the candle at the low a breaker in the 2022 and 2024 teaching?
7. The leg that ran the stops in a breaker setup contains a clean FVG. Which array does the 2024 refinement favour, and when does the breaker come back?
8. A candle trades into a prior bullish block but its body closes below that block's mean threshold. Is it a propulsion block?
9. A student says any daily BISI with a volume imbalance at its low is a suspension block. Is that right?
10. A fulcrum projection is drawn from a swing high, but the swing low never breaks. Can you use it?

## Answers

1. The open of the first candle in the run, the one furthest from the displacement. The range is the bodies of all four candles, with the midpoint as the mean threshold. Reread *The order block*.
2. Yes: trading through the open is enough, and he says a close is not required. An August 12, 2024 note has a later candle closing beyond it. Reread *Trade through, or close?*
3. The block is breached and is no longer a good place to expect support, so lower prices become more likely. It does not guarantee lower prices. Reread *Reading the block*.
4. It is the mean threshold (MT). CE is the word for the midpoint of a gap or a wick. Reread *Reading the block*.
5. One tick above the highest point of the premium wick, not just the block. If a spike stops you out, he re-enters short there. Reread *Trading the order block*, under Stops.
6. No. The version he teaches has a third swing that runs the first high and takes the stops, then a drop through the low. Some 2026 notes differ, so check the video. Reread *Breaker blocks*.
7. The FVG in the stop-hunt leg is favoured as the sweet spot. Use the breaker only when no such gap exists. Reread *Breaker blocks*, under How to trade it.
8. No. A propulsion block's body holds the correct side of the parent's mean threshold. Reread *Propulsion blocks*.
9. No. A volume imbalance only at the low makes a simple BISI. The best-supported test needs one at both ends, though looser uses of the word exist. Reread *Suspension blocks*.
10. No. The projection is valid only if the swing low breaks. Reread *The fulcrum projection*.
