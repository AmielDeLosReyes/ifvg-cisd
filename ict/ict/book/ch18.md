An entry is the part of a trade ICT calls easy: a specific price, sent as an order, at a level the model has already named. This chapter covers how he places that order (limit or market, one tick through, never the extreme), the entry drill he calls IOFED, and what he does when the order misses or he wants to add. After reading you should be able to write down the draw, the stop and the order price before you click, and know what you will do if price never fills you. Stops and partials are Chapter 19; sizing and prop-firm rules are Chapter 20; the FVG (fair value gap) is Chapter 5.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch18_d1 | caption: IOFED on a bullish FVG: limit at the top, stop at the bottom, target beyond (illustrative) | The order sits at candle 3's low (or one tick above), the CE is the gap's midpoint, and the stop sits under candle 1's high, the bottom of the gap.]]

- Frame the trade in this order: the draw (where price should go, Chapter 3), the stop (where you are wrong), and only then the entry. ICT calls the entry the "secondary concern" ([2024 #38 · 2:05:26](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7526)).
- Enter with a limit order at a specific price, not a zone. Price must trade at least one tick through it for a sure fill, so give the order room and never aim for the exact extreme.
- IOFED (Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill) is a partial entry into an FVG you expect to stay partly open. Limit at candle 3's low or one tick above it, stop under candle 1's high, best fills from the CE (consequent encroachment, the gap's midpoint, Chapter 6) to the top of the gap.
- Take only setups with room. ICT wants about 20 NQ handles (1 handle = 1 full point = 4 ticks) from entry to the first target, and he dislikes 10-handle trades.
- A missed limit is not the end of the idea. If price is now at a better price, market in with the same stop. If price ran away, follow the delivery down to a lower timeframe or the next array, and do not chase or fade it in anger.
- Adds go biggest first and shrink. The whole position is one trade with one objective.

## Limit orders, market orders and the one-tick rule

### What it is

A limit order is a resting order at a price. To buy, you place a buy limit below the market and wait for price to trade down to it. To sell short, you place a sell limit above the market and wait for price to rise to it ([2024 #38 · 2:05:26](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7526); [2024 #38 · 2:07:06](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7626)). A market order takes whatever price is on offer right now.

ICT's fill rule is that price must trade at least one tick through your limit to be sure of a fill. Touching the price may not fill you, and bigger size may need more ([2024 #38 · 2:07:55](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7675)). He has had real limit orders where price traded two ticks past his price, did not fill him (spread, queue position), and then ran without him ([2024 #7 · 2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457)).

A stop is mandatory on every trade. He calls it your insurance policy and the market telling you the idea was wrong, "a good time to be wrong" ([2024 #38 · 2:02:14](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7334)). The one market order he permits in his drills is an exit from a trade you know you are offside on. He reads a market-order entry as a sign of rushing and emotion.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about resistance. He wants entries and targets with the lowest barrier: the easiest fill, the "low-hanging fruit" where volume actually trades, and where the spread cannot decide the outcome ([2024 #7 · 2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457)). Demanding the exact extreme invites non-fills. He also says constant extreme fills invite slippage and requotes from a B-book broker, meaning a dealing desk that takes the other side of your trade ([2024 #2 · 32:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1959)).

Size makes it worse. On a 14-contract exit he set his limit a little off the exact level so the order would fill. With 100 contracts, he says, touching the level is not enough ([2024 #24 · 1:34:44](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5684)). Hold the demand for perfection for your journal, where he calls a perfect-but-unfilled idea a moral victory. Do not carry it onto the order ticket ([2024 #24 · 1:37:53](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5873)).

### How to trade it

1. Write the draw first: the old high or low, the inefficiency, the NDOG (new day opening gap, Chapter 11) the trade is aimed at.
2. Write the stop second: the level that proves the idea wrong.
3. Then choose the entry, a PD array (a level price is drawn to or reacts from, Chapter 4) that fits those two.
4. Offset the order from the exact level, on the side price reaches first. The notes give these examples:
    - A long limit goes a tick above the top of an FVG, or just above the midpoint of a wick ([2024 #7 · 2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457); [2024 #2 · 2:48:36](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10116)).
    - A short taken at a small gap: sell limit just below the candle's high, stop at the high, target one tick above the draw level ([2024 #38 · 2:05:26](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7526)).
    - Covering a short: ICT's FX example fluffs a 1.0900 target to a 1.0905 limit ([2022 Ep 17 · 21:00](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1260)). His NQ cover of 14 shorts used about 18,770 instead of the 18,765.25 level, roughly 5 handles of profit given up to get everything off.
5. Size the offset to the instrument. In FX he says fluff exits by 3 to 5 pips, and use 10 pips while you are new, because any broker can widen the spread when it suits them ([2022 Ep 17 · 20:11](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1211); [2022 Ep 17 · 21:46](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1306)). On NQ his entry offset is one tick.

Gold traders: ICT gave no XAUUSD offset. FX pips are the closest instrument in the notes, and his B-book and spread warnings are the part most relevant to a CFD broker. His test is that the spread must not decide the fill, so size your offset against your own broker's spread.

### When a market or stop order is the right tool

ICT's default is a limit order, with a few named exceptions.

- **Learning.** The student he coaches on air, Caleb, uses market orders at first, to get used to entering and feel what a trade is like. Limits come later for tight entries and meaningful stops ([2024 #33 · 14:00](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=840)). ICT skipped a setup that day because the limit he would have placed meant a stop price would have hit, and he says that stubborn precision keeps him out of marginal trades ([2024 #33 · 53:48](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3228)).
- **Proving it is live.** On live streams he sometimes clicks market buy and sell buttons only to show the session is not Market Replay ([2024 #38 · 2:40](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=160)). In the September 2024 payroll lecture he adds that limit entries would have been better.
- **A missed limit at a better price.** Market in at once, covered below.
- **Sharp reversals.** In February 2026 he taught entering at market the moment price, while rallying, trades through a specific candle's opening price (he sold two micros). The stop goes around that opening price once price has had room to drop; the caption is unclear on the side, but for a short it must sit above. He says plainly this uses stop or market orders, not limits, and differs from the FVG entries ([2026 #129 · 32:45](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1965); [2026 #129 · 33:32](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2012)).
- **A sell stop when the draw fails.** If you expected price to reach a level (an all-time high, a weekly high) and it fails to get there, a sell stop just under the small low beneath puts you short when that low breaks. If you were already long, trail your stop to just below that low instead. After the break he wants price to turn very aggressive ([2026 #73 · 40:58](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2458); [2026 #73 · 41:45](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2505)). In his NQ example price failed to reach 30,975.50, broke the small low and ran lower; he gave no fixed risk or size ([2026 #73 · 42:34](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2554)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 he already fluffed exits and said ultra-tight stops are a bad idea with size. He also allowed himself one exception to a hard stop, at the open (see the stops section below). The strict limit-only rules arrive in the October 2024 drill lectures.

In 2026 the point shifts to what happens when a limit at the target misses. His limit at 28,700 did not fill. Price came within 17 handles, and he closed the trade at about 28,717 (caption unclear on the digits), so the prediction was right and the order still missed. His advice there: if you get nervous, take one partial off to satisfy the urge, and holding the rest becomes easier ([2026 #49 · 47:18](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2838)).

One 2026 note says he places limits 2 to 5 ticks below key arrays and targets ([2026 #60 · 19:18](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=1158)). The captions are rough and the notes do not say whether that is below a long target or a short one. Every explicit example from 2022 and 2024 above offsets on the near side, so do not read the 2026 line as "always below."

## IOFED: the Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill

### What it is

IOFED is a partial entry into a fair value gap that you believe will not fill completely. ICT re-taught it in the 2024 mentorship in exactly those terms, and he is strict about when it applies. Unless he (or you) says a particular gap should stay open, you are not looking at IOFED. It is not the default entry for every FVG, though you can adopt it as your own model ([2024 #7 · 2:18:35](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8315)).

Take the bullish case. The gap runs from candle 1's high (the bottom) to candle 3's low (the top). The best fills sit from the CE (consequent encroachment, the gap's midpoint, Chapter 6) up to the top, and the lower half is the least probable fill. If you are wrong and price does trade down to the low, it usually does not matter, because the CE-to-top fill has already happened ([2024 #7 · 2:18:35](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8315)).

He told viewers to screenshot one point: any trade one tick below that low, the gap's top as the context reads, already counts as an IOFED ([2024 #7 · 2:02:32](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7352)). Price does not have to dig into the gap. He says the same logic applies to a premium array, where price trades up into it, with the sensitive area flipped. He did not walk through the bearish case.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that some runs have low resistance, and in those runs the lower part of a gap stays open. A breakaway gap, left behind by displacement after a consolidation or stop hunt, is his example. Price returns to the top of the gap, trades only part of the way in, and goes on ([2024 #48 · 1:24:41](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5081)). With an order block beneath the gap as extra support, price should not trade down to the bottom, so the stop can be very close.

On the Aug 19, 2024 session he called the same kind of entry the low-risk buy: price should not come back below it and should keep pressing higher ([2024 #12 · 25:33](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1533)). In 2026 he reads the partial fill itself as information. A gap entered only a little, without even reaching the CE, shows smart money buying early and expecting continuation ([2026 #61 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=527)). A volume imbalance or SIBI (Chapters 6 and 5) that is only partly filled is bullish; a fully filled one is weaker ([2026 #22 · 15:29](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=929)).

His wording for the whole idea is "low resistance barriers to entry."

### How to spot it

1. A fresh FVG left by displacement, which you expect to stay partly open: a breakaway gap, or one left after a consolidation or stop hunt.
2. A clear target above (a higher-timeframe level, an old high, a volume imbalance), so the run has low resistance (Chapter 16).
3. Price returning toward the gap. Down-close candles (an order block) under the gap are a plus.
4. At least about 20 NQ handles between the entry and the first target (see the minimum range section below).
5. A stop that fits under the gap's bottom without ruining that range.

### How to trade it

- **Entry:** a limit buy at candle 3's low, the top of the gap, or one tick above it. ICT asks you to choose between the two and to accept the extra drawdown that comes with entering higher. It is a specific price, not a zone ([2024 #7 · 2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457); [2024 #48 · 1:22:16](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4936)).
- **Never** aim for the low end of the gap.
- **Stop:** just below candle 1's high, the bottom of the gap, because in a low-resistance run price should not trade down there ([2024 #48 · 1:26:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5174)). In his August example the gap-to-stop span was about nine ticks, 2.25 NQ handles.
- **Target:** the next array or liquidity. In the August example the first objective was a candle high about 20 handles away, then a daily volume imbalance ([2024 #7 · 2:43:41](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9821)).
- **Timing in his example:** the entry fell inside the 10:50 to 11:10 ET macro (Chapter 12).
- **Scaling, his own variant:** first entry of 6 contracts at the top of a wide gap, 4 more when price reaches the CE or upper quadrant, 5 more one tick below the CE if it trades there. This assumes the lower portion stays open. In his timed illustration the entries were 10:47, 10:49 and 10:51 ([2024 #7 · 2:29:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8949)).

That scaling is averaging into a losing position. He notes at least one funded-account firm (he names Topstep, Caleb's choice) does not allow it. His workaround is to use IOFED as the only entry and pyramid with later, higher PD arrays above it, which that firm does allow ([2024 #7 · 2:29:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8949)). Check your own firm's rules (Chapter 20).

One note on the October 2024 lecture says to add as price nears the stop if the run still has low resistance ([2024 #48 · 1:26:14](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5174)). That is averaging in again, so the firm-rule warning applies.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using it on every FVG.** ICT says it is for gaps expected to stay open. A gap you expect to fill deeply is a different trade.
- **Aiming for the CE or the low end.** Price can trade two ticks past such a limit and not fill you, as it did to ICT.
- **Treating it as OTE.** The notes call this a restatement as a gap-stays-open tool, not a general OTE-style entry into any FVG (OTE is Chapter 4).
- **Calling every bounce an IOFED.** In 2026 he warns that not all IOFEDs work and sometimes price breaks through the drill, so keep the stop ([2026 #39 · 11:56](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=716)).

### How the teaching changed

The notes do not contain the original step list. They say the 2024 name-drop leaves the steps to his earlier (2016 and 2017) mentorship material, which is outside what this book is built on.

- **Aug 14, 2024:** he quizzes viewers on the name of a different entry. In a sell program, he asks what to call the retracement of an up-closed candle back into the CE of a wick, the entry if you missed the first FVG. The answer is the institutional orderflow entry drill. The next target is the wick's lower quadrant, with sell-side liquidity below, and the up-closed candle's high should not be traded above ([2024 #8 · 40:40](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2440); [2024 #8 · 41:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2488)).
- **2026:** the name stretches. It describes price trading into an array that is "qualified" by sitting at an octant (Chapter 6) or standard deviation ([2026 #39 · 11:10](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=670)), or trading down into an FVG anchored to the low of a daily suspension block (one of his block types, Chapter 9) ([2026 #44 · 45:47](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2747)). In one lecture the caption reads "institutional referral entry drill" for bodies accumulating at a discount zone, which is probably the same phrase ([2026 #6 · 44:38](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=2678)).

So 2024 gives a recipe with a limit price and a stop, and 2026 mostly uses the name to label a place where price traded in and held. The 2026 notes give no new order rules for it.

## Lowest-threshold entries and the minimum range

### What it is

A second principle behind IOFED applies to every entry: take the lowest-resistance version of it. Choose the entry and the target that are easiest, that the spread will not defeat, and do not demand precision ([2024 #7 · 2:20:57](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8457)).

A 2022 version of the same idea is the close-proximity entry. If you miss the ideal fill inside an FVG (a fumbled order, software delay), getting in very close to the gap, before price runs far, is acceptable. The point is to enter where the model suggests. In his example his fill was near the gap rather than in it, with less than about 2 handles of heat. The gap offered roughly eight candles of opportunity to get in ([2022 Ep 29 · 6:32](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=392); [2022 Ep 29 · 8:08](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=488)).

### Why it works

ICT's reason for the second half, the minimum range, is the spread and the risk model. He calls a 10-handle trade static price movement. You can be right on direction and still be stopped, and he says the risk model in NQ does not work at that size ([2024 #28 · 1:39:07](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5947)).

### How to trade it

- **The filter.** He needs about 20 NQ handles (80 ticks) of visible, timeable run. For his student's drills he says at least about 15, ideally 20. If he cannot see a simple 20-handle run he does not trade ([2024 #7 · 2:42:53](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9773); [2024 #28 · 1:38:16](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=5896)).
- **Start the clock at the entry.** The range runs from your entry to the first objective, and he says to start timing the trade at the entry.
- **What counts as a target.** The first partial is the easiest liquidity or inefficiency. The Terminus is where you are done for the day ([2024 #28 · 1:46:29](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6389)).
- **Stage 1 and stage 2.** In training, use one exit only (the first partial). After two to three weeks of daily drills with no anxiety, add a Terminus: the next liquidity, such as the CE of the opening range gap (Chapter 11), old highs or relative equal highs.
- **Example.** An entry-candle low near 19,340 with the first partial at minor buy-side liquidity (equal highs) near 19,360: 20 handles, or 80 ticks ([2024 #28 · 2:09:07](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=7747)).
- **Bread and butter.** From a 20-handle run he can take 10 to 15 handles, and he calls a setup that gives 20 to 25 a nice little bread-and-butter trade ([2024 #29 · 2:08:16](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=7696)). After a clean win on a hard day, take it and leave ([2024 #7 · 3:09:24](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=11364)).

The trade need not reach the final target. The range only has to be there to afford it.

If you cannot frame at least about 20 handles from entry to target, sit still: the move may be visible but it is not framable for this model ([2024 #29 · 1:32:44](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5564)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Counting range from the wrong place.** The range is entry to first objective, not swing to swing.
- **Forcing 10-handle trades because the direction is right.** He says that is where right-but-stopped happens.
- **Porting the number to another instrument.** He gave it for his student's NQ drills and did not claim it is universal. There is no gold equivalent in the notes.

## The price delivery continuum

### What it is

Price delivery continuum theory (PDCT) is a name ICT coined in August 2024. He tells viewers it is brand new and that mentorship students did not learn it. The notes show four threads under the one name.

1. **Follow the delivery after a miss.** If a precise limit at an FVG edge does not fill and price runs, do not abandon the idea. The delivery is underway and there is a finite target, so look for the next specific price: a new short-term high, a new up-closed candle or order block, a new FVG made by the move itself ([2024 #8 · 47:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2873)).
2. **Drop a timeframe when one is used up.** With an expected draw, the market is fractal and keeps offering participation. Once one timeframe has used up its entry opportunities, go lower, down to 15 seconds or 5 seconds, instead of chasing ([2024 #2 · 2:38:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9493)).
3. **Read order flow from price.** Read it from the open, high, low and close of each interval, not from depth of market, ladders, level-2 or Bookmap-type tools ([2024 #13 · 25:09](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1509)).
4. **PD array to PD array, timeframe to timeframe.** Technical traders should see price as delivering from one PD array to the next and from one timeframe to the next. He does not mean a generic top-down: he means specific criteria that give an obvious place to get short and an obvious place lower to take profit, or the reverse ([2024 #47 · 3:31](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=211)).

[[DIAGRAM ch18_d3 | caption: A missed limit at the first gap, and the second entry in a new FVG formed by the run (illustrative) | The target never changes; the move itself builds the next array to enter.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation of the first thread is personal. As a young trader he would miss his one setup, get angry and stop looking, even though a big move was still on offer ([2024 #8 · 45:28](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2728)). His theory is the answer: a move to a finite target keeps creating new places to join it.

On the third thread, his argument is that resting orders can be pulled (spoofed), but you cannot spoof the open, high, low and close of a candle. Booked volume shows only after price engages a level.

The test he frames is whether price can reach the next level, how fast, and whether it stops short or goes through on arrival ([2024 #13 · 25:58](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1558)). Contract counts have no bearing, and the market is drawn to inefficiency like a magnet. It needs only to touch the low end of an inefficiency first. Then you watch whether it runs aggressively to the midpoint ([2024 #13 · 29:10](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1750)).

### How to trade it

1. **Keep the target.** It is a finite price. If you do not know the specific price for an entry, stop, partial and terminus, he does not trade.
2. **Let the move build the next array.** Wait for a short-term high, an up-closed candle or order block, or a new FVG to form in the direction of the delivery.
3. **Enter on the retracement** at the lowest-barrier edge of that new array, with the same stop logic as the first plan.
4. **Check the range.** The 20-handle filter still applies from the new entry.

In his Aug 14, 2024 example the first FVG fill was missed. Price then came back into the body of an up-closed candle and traded to the CE of the 8:30 wick without taking out that candle's high. He says that could have been the entry ([2024 #8 · 40:40](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2440)). On Aug 6, a 15-second down-close candle inside an FVG that had returned into an old inefficiency was another buy, with the limit just above the wick's midpoint ([2024 #2 · 2:51:47](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10307)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Following a move with no stated draw.** The theory needs a finite price. Without one there is nothing to follow.
- **Swapping it for order-book tools.** ICT says he does not use Bookmap-type tools, and that students who understand his method do not need them. He admits they might have helped him early on ([2024 #13 · 22:44](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=1364)).

### How the teaching changed

The name appears on Aug 6, 2024 as extra credit, explicitly not for beginners ([2024 #2 · 2:38:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9493)). On Aug 14 he calls it brand new ([2024 #8 · 47:53](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2873)). On Aug 20 he applies it to reading real order flow. On Oct 21 he restates it as PD-to-PD, timeframe-to-timeframe delivery, with his chart stripped down to opening range gaps and Asian session liquidity pools ([2024 #47 · 4:20](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=260)). In 2026 the name is not used in the notes, but the practice continues in the sub-minute chart lectures below.

## Sub-minute charts: 15 seconds, 30 seconds and 1 second

### What it is

A 15-second chart shows the same price action as a 1-minute chart in smaller intervals. ICT says it only looks fast, and the levels that work on 1 minute (midpoints, highs, lows, FVGs, relative equal highs) work on 15 seconds as well ([2026 #35 · 16:05](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=965)). A morning on a 15-second chart looks like a week of 1-hour price action, so opportunities are constant. The same phenomena appear on a 1-second chart ([2024 #4 · 1:54:22](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=6862)).

### Why it works

Because the market is fractal, a small chart offers mini versions of the same PD arrays: small FVGs, small order blocks, small imbalances. They form and resolve in seconds, so entries are precise but need fast execution ([2026 #95 · 36:03](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2163)).

### How to trade it

- **Use the smaller chart for refinement.** Take direction and targets from the 1-minute chart and fine-tune the entry or exit inside that move on 15 seconds ([2026 #35 · 16:05](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=965)).
- **Join a move you missed.** The 15-second chart is "very forgiving": it lets you get into a move you missed on the 1, 5 or 15-minute chart, or cut your stop size in handles or pips ([2024 #2 · 32:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1959)).
- **Add on retracements.** If you are short a 1-minute setup and price retraces slightly, drop to 30 seconds and look for sell setups to add. He says entire 50 to 100 handle moves can form and resolve on sub-minute charts ([2026 #95 · 36:03](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2163)).
- **On the 15-second chart, an add late in a run is at most one contract.** Tighter stops from the small chart are for later, once you are managing real risk ([2024 #2 · 2:48:36](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10116)).
- **The 1-second chart is for review.** You cannot react manually to all of it. At 8:30 ET a 1-second candle shows you either were positioned or missed it ([2024 #4 · 2:00:13](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7213)). In 2026 he shows 9:34 to 9:45 AM on 1 second only to prove his grid levels hold at that resolution and for post-trade analysis, not as a method for new traders ([2026 #43 · 31:28](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=1888); [2026 #43 · 37:06](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=2226)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using 15 seconds as a shortcut.** ICT says it is not one. If you cannot read 1, 5 and 15-minute price, you will get chopped up on 15 seconds ([2024 #2 · 32:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1959)). Re-entering with a reduced stop there is not a golden ticket either ([2024 #2 · 37:28](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2248)).
- **Scalping the 1-second chart live.** He does not recommend it.

### How the teaching changed

In August 2024 the 15-second chart is extra credit. In the October 2024 drill it is the standard practice chart ([2024 #38 · 17:20](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=1040)). In 2026 the 15 and 30-second charts are an ordinary refinement tool, and the 1-second chart is kept for review.

### Watch it

- [2024 #2 · 32:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1959): why 15 seconds, and why it is not a shortcut.
- [2026 #95 · 36:03](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2163): mini PD arrays on 15 and 30 seconds.

## Missed entries, re-entries and the no-chase rule

### What it is

Entries get missed because you freeze, second-guess, turn the charts on late, or never see the setup ([2024 #29 · 4:36](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=276)). ICT calls getting into a move that has already started a very valuable skill ([2024 #29 · 9:29](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=569)). Which tool you use depends on where price is relative to the entry you wanted.

[[DIAGRAM ch18_d4 | caption: Decision flow for a missed entry, from ICT's September 2024 lectures | First ask where price is relative to your entry; the 20-handle range then decides whether a new entry is worth taking.]]

### How to trade it

**Price is past your entry, at a better price.** If you missed the limit and price is now below your bullish entry (above your bearish one), market in at once. Price is offering something better than you were willing to pay, so this is not chasing ([2024 #28 · 48:20](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2900); [2024 #28 · 49:06](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2946)). Keep the same stop (below candle 2's low in his FVG example) and the same sizing discipline.

If price touches the gap's upper quadrant or CE, buy more. Deeper than that, keep to one or two contracts, because you do not want to see price spend time in the far half of the gap.

**Price ran away without giving you the entry.** You have two choices. One is regret: get angry, chase, or worse, fade the move because you are mad at the market. The other is to accept that setups repeat, find another one and wait for the FVG to be offered ([2024 #29 · 41:58](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2518)). He pushes the second, and says wrestling the marketplace is never good.

1. Frame the move on a timeframe above your execution chart, then drop to the lower one (his examples: 15, 5, 1 minute, 15 seconds) ([2024 #29 · 7:53](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=473)).
2. Wait for a lower-timeframe FVG, or an inversion, in the direction of the move (inversions are Chapter 7).
3. Enter by the entry model, and require at least about 20 handles of room to the first target.
4. If the market never offers a gap, log it and do not be mad ([2024 #29 · 1:16:37](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4597)).

### Re-entering after you are stopped out

If you trail your stop and get stopped with a profit, the idea may still be valid, and you can go back in at the next valid PD array. In his Aug 29, 2024 example ICT was short from a bearish order block and breaker (Chapter 9), added on a wick back up, and took a small partial. He then brought the stop down aggressively and was stopped. He re-sold a return into the inversion FVG and added once he knew he was on side ([2024 #20 · 5:12](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=312); [2024 #20 · 8:27](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=507)).

The lesson he draws is about the trail, not the re-entry. If you understand the market maker sell model (Chapter 17), you do not tighten that fast: you let the second stage of redistribution deliver first. Later a trailing stop took him out while he was away, and price went on to Tuesday's low, the draw of the model ([2024 #20 · 10:01](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=601); [2024 #20 · 11:39](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=699)). In 2022 he made a related point: getting stopped early leaves beginners afraid to re-enter, which he calls infancy ([2022 Ep 13 · 25:37](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1537)).

The 2026 notes add a counterweight. After exiting a short at his planned high, ICT refuses to re-enter even if price drops into the 29,230 area: "there has to be rules." You looked for the key high to get short. If you are not in at that location you do not chase lower, and he leaves something for everybody else ([2026 #70 · 17:13](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=1033); [2026 #70 · 18:00](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=1080)). In 2026 he also describes testing a long off a daily wick CE, getting stopped, then selling short and mitigating the loss ([2026 #49 · 22:34](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1354)).

These do not contradict if you read them as one rule: re-enter only at a new valid array, and never because price is moving without you. Decide before the trade which one you are allowed to do.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Chasing or fading out of anger.** This is his "regret" branch, and it is the opposite of waiting for an offered FVG.
- **Re-entering at the same price with no new array.** The continuum gives you a new level; it does not give you the old one back.
- **Treating a better price as a reason to size up.** The extra size only goes in at the upper quadrant or CE.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the missed-entry tool is the close-proximity fill. In September 2024 it becomes a procedure: market in at a better price, or drop a timeframe and wait for the next FVG. In 2026 the new point is a rule of restraint, no re-entry after a planned exit.

- [2026 #70 · 17:13](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=1033): why he will not re-enter after the planned exit.

## Learning to enter: the Volatility Pinball drill

### What it is

The Volatility Pinball drill is a demo-account exercise for learning limit-order entries and for getting used to entering and to selling short. ICT says he built it as a game in the 1990s, on paper, using the candle's open as the hypothetical fill ([2024 #38 · 1:34:39](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5679)). It is not a model, not a signal service, and he says you will lose if you copy it with a real or funded account.

### How to trade it

The rules ([2024 #38 · 17:20](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=1040)):

- **Chart:** 15 seconds, with 1 minute as the highest timeframe. No higher-timeframe bias is framed.
- **Size:** exactly one contract. No two contracts, no pyramiding.
- **Orders:** every entry is a limit order, with stop and take-profit placed at entry. You may not move or cancel the stop, and market orders are not allowed. The trade ends at the stop or the target, whichever trips first. Wait before the next one.
- **Reason to enter:** each time a fair value gap forms, engage it, aiming at the nearest pool of liquidity or inefficiency. You can swap in order blocks or other arrays. It does not need to be "the right one."
- **Mix:** alternate buys and sells, include counter-trend drills so you feel what that is like, and prefer range-bound days.
- **Volume:** at least 10 drills a day, ideally 15 to 20, about an hour to an hour and a half. Do it for weeks, up to a few months. Progress shows in a couple of weeks and is notable by two to three months.
- **Journal:** entry time, time to stop or target, unrealized profit, emotional state. Review at the weekend and note which drills lined up with what delivered.

Frame the stop and the target first. The entry is secondary ([2024 #38 · 2:05:26](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=7526)).

His demonstration was 10 drills on NQ's 15-second chart after the 9:30 ET open, net about +$3,575 with 6 of the 10 losing, by his own count ([2024 #38 · 3:03:42](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=11022)). He is forcing himself to take whatever forms, including low-probability setups. Do not read the drill's profit as evidence of skill either way.

### Why it works

The drill is exposure training. His claim is that after weeks of drills your eye starts jumping to setups you have seen repeat, and you see setups you never noticed.

In 2026 he gives the same remedy for fear of entry: practise entries on paper, place limit orders, watch them hit or miss, and remove the emotion. After six to eight weeks the fear diminishes ([2026 #108 · 31:06](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=1866); [2026 #108 · 34:23](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2063)). Journaling and the wider practice routine are Chapter 21; the psychology is Chapter 22.

- [2024 #38 · 17:20](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=1040): the drill rules.
- [2024 #38 · 3:03:42](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=11022): the result of 10 drills and a recap.

## Adding to a position: pyramiding

### What it is

Pyramiding means adding to a position that is working, or at planned levels, with the whole thing counted as one trade. ICT's 2022 rule is that the biggest position goes on first and each add is smaller. In his MNQ demo he bought 3 micros, then 2, then 1. Buying 1, then 2, then 3 is an inverted pyramid and unstable ([2022 Ep 13 · 31:59](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1919); [2022 Ep 13 · 32:47](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1967)).

[[DIAGRAM ch18_d5 | caption: A stable pyramid (3, 2, 1) against an inverted one (1, 2, 3), from ICT's 2022 MNQ demo | The first, largest position carries the adds; the same six contracts bought the other way round are unstable.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the equity from the earlier, larger position supports the adds, so you can weather a deeper retracement. Adds are made only while price is still below the objective, and the equity builds with speed on the way ([2022 Ep 13 · 33:34](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2014); [2022 Ep 16 · 48:41](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2921)). In the 2022 demo, 6 micros needed about $1,200 of margin (a hypothetical discount broker) and took a hypothetical $10,000 account to $12,111, about 21% on one trade. He called that gearing optimal for $10,000 and said you do not need a lot to make a lot ([2022 Ep 13 · 34:21](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2061)).

### How to trade it

1. **Treat it as one trade.** One idea, one objective, one risk statement for the sum of the positions.
2. **Biggest first, then smaller.** His examples are 3, 2, 1 and 5, 3, 2. Buying 1, 2, 4, 8 is reckless, and he says he has roasted accounts doing it ([2022 Ep 16 · 49:28](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2968)).
3. **Add at planned arrays, not on impulse.** In a Sept 6, 2024 NQ live execution he named sweeps of relative equal highs (Chapter 3), retraces up into the NDOG ("anytime it touches that you add more"), first-FVG levels from earlier days, and an orange level he calls a pyramiding opportunity ([2024 #23 · 3:12](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=192); [2024 #23 · 5:39](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=339)).
4. **Add only while the reference holds.** Adds into the NDOG are valid only if price has not taken out the high between 2:30 and 3:00 PM ET. If that high is exceeded, the add is invalid. He declined to add in the final hour of that day's range ([2024 #23 · 2:26](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=146)).
5. **Shrink late adds.** On the 15-second chart, once a run is long in the tooth, an add cannot be larger than one contract. He also says pyramiding is hard to teach: do not start with it, and be consistent with small size first ([2024 #2 · 2:48:36](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10116)).
6. **Add on dips into support, not through broken structure.** In 2026 he adds on dips into support (CE, order blocks, volume imbalances) and takes partials at targets. The partials fund the position, so you do not need the final target to be profitable. The notes add the caveat: respect support when adding, and do not pyramid into weakness if price is breaking structure ([2026 #4 · 4:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=252); [2026 #4 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1392)). Partials are Chapter 19.

### Worked example

2022 Ep 26, a tape-reading example with 6 contracts. He bought the FVG after sell stops were taken, added at the order block, and sold 4 of the 6 at market once price ran above the buy-side liquidity (Chapter 3). He then moved the stop up on the final 2 and was stopped out on them. The total was roughly $5,000 of hypothetical profit ([2022 Ep 26 · 6:24](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=384); [2022 Ep 26 · 12:48](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=768); [2022 Ep 26 · 13:41](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=821)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **An inverted pyramid.** The biggest size comes last, with the least equity behind it.
- **Averaging into a loser where your firm bans it.** IOFED's 6 + 4 + 5 scaling is the example (see above).
- **Adding after the reference level has failed.** The 2:30 to 3:00 PM high example is the template: when the level that justified the add is gone, the add is gone.
- **Chasing price higher to add by hand.** In 2026 he pre-places limit orders at anticipated levels (a next inversion FVG, a relative equal high) and lets price fill them, instead of clicking in manually. The risk is that price gaps over the order and never fills it ([2026 #22 · 8:14](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=494); [2026 #22 · 13:04](https://youtu.be/SUcRywSry5U?t=784)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** strictly decreasing sizes (3, 2, 1 and 5, 3, 2).
- **2024:** adds hang on named levels (NDOG, first FVGs, relative equal highs), and ICT cautions against teaching or learning pyramiding early. His own IOFED variant, 6 + 4 + 5, breaks the strictly shrinking pattern, since the third add is bigger than the second.
- **2026:** a core position at his sweet spot, then small adds at the bottom end of a graded zone and market-order singles at graded levels ([2026 #34 · 20:35](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1235); grading is Chapter 6). In one short he scaled in on wick confirmation, then body confirmation, and says he overthinks exits. He moved a limit he had meant to place just above the low and got stopped out instead ([2026 #23 · 57:10](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3430); [2026 #23 · 58:00](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3480)). The prices and handle totals in that note do not reconcile, so this book leaves them out.

## Stops, buffers and other entry mechanics

### What it is

This section collects the stop rules the notes tie directly to entries, and one staging idea from 2026. Full stop and target management is Chapter 19.

### How to trade it

- **Stop under an order block.** The initial stop on a long goes below the mean threshold (midpoint) of the bullish order block candle. When price trades above a new down-close candle's high, the stop must stay below that candle's low, not inside it, because price can dig into that candle and use it as an order block. Once price takes out a cluster of down-close candles, you can raise the stop below that cluster. If price then breaks their lows, being stopped out was correct ([2022 Ep 13 · 24:49](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1489); [2022 Ep 13 · 25:37](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1537)).
- **FX shorts.** Put the stop above the entry candle's high, or above the swing high if you want more room. In his EURUSD example he rounded the risk to about 10 pips and aimed just above 1.0900 for better than 8:1. The captioned entry and stop do not quite match that rounding ([2022 Ep 17 · 22:33](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1353); [2022 Ep 17 · 23:20](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1400)).
- **No ultra-tight stops with size.** A 1-pip or half-pip stop might work now and then, but not once you put on size, because you will sweat every tick. A wider structure-based stop lets you walk away from the chart ([2022 Ep 18 · 44:35](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2675)).
- **A mental stop at the open, for himself only.** He normally uses a stop, but at the open stops can be taken easily and then reverse. At the screen he allows price to go slightly past the low and would close at market. He explicitly does not encourage trading without a stop ([2022 Ep 15 · 4:13](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=253); [2022 Ep 15 · 6:45](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=405)).

### Buffer entries

In high-volatility minutes after the open (his example: 9:30 to 9:40 ET), ICT suggests building a buffer. Take a small long in the pre-market, 5 to 10 minutes before the open. Then use its profit as a cushion so you can hold an aggressive short entry at the open through some adverse price action. In his example the long gained about 50 to 100 points. The short went in within an iFVG (Chapter 7), with its initial risk offset by that gain ([2026 #83 · 42:03](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2523)).

He also recommends micro contracts for small accounts or prop-firm attempts ([2026 #83 · 1:51](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=111)). Whether your evaluation allows opposing trades is a rule to check with your firm. ICT gave the idea without those details (Chapter 20).

## Check yourself

## Answers

1. Limit at 19,004.00 or one tick above (19,004.25), stop just below 19,000.00. You want the fill in the upper half, from the CE (19,002.00) to the top, and the lower half is the least probable fill. See IOFED: how to trade it.
2. Touching is not enough; price must trade at least one tick through, and larger size may need more. ICT has had price go 2 ticks past with no fill. He would sit the limit at or a tick above the top and accept the extra drawdown. See Limit orders: what it is.
3. IOFED is for a gap expected to stay partly open. Without that expectation, you are not looking at IOFED. See IOFED: what it is.
4. No. He needs about 20 handles (80 ticks), 15 at least for his student, and calls 10 static price movement where you can be right and still stopped. See Lowest-threshold entries.
5. Market in at once, because price is offering a better price than you were willing to pay, so it is not chasing. Keep the same stop. Size up only if price touches the upper quadrant or CE; deeper, keep to one or two contracts. See Missed entries.
6. Regret (chase or fade in anger) or accept that setups repeat and wait for an offered FVG, which he pushes. Before re-entering you need a specific finite target and price for the entry, stop and partial, and about 20 handles to the first target. See Missed entries and The price delivery continuum.
7. 3, 2, 1: the larger, earlier position's equity supports the adds, and the inverted pyramid is unstable. The 6 + 4 + 5 scaling breaks the shrinking pattern (the third add is larger), and it averages into a loser, which at least one funded firm does not allow. See Adding to a position.
8. It is not a shortcut: if you cannot read 1, 5 and 15-minute price you will get chopped up on 15 seconds. It helps you get into a missed move or cut stop size. The 1-second chart is for review and level confirmation, not live scalping. See Sub-minute charts.
9. You may re-enter at the next valid PD array, because the idea can still be valid. ICT said tightening the trail too fast would not be done if you understand the market maker sell model: let the second stage deliver first. The 2026 remark is the counterweight: re-enter at a new valid array, never because price is moving without you. See Missed entries.
