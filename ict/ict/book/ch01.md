ICT's method starts from one claim: price is not pushed around by buyers and sellers but delivered by an algorithm, on a schedule, to two kinds of destination. This chapter lays out that model (time first, then price; liquidity or inefficiency; smart money against retail; why he ignores indicators and order-flow tools; how he reads candle bodies instead) and the vocabulary every later chapter uses. When you finish, you should be able to face any chart with his four questions: what time is it, where is the liquidity, where is the inefficiency, and what are the candle bodies saying?

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch01_d1 | caption: price leaves balance, raids sell stops, leaves a gap, returns to it, then draws to buy stops (illustrative) | Every leg has a destination: first the stops under the equal lows, then the gap, then the stops above the old high.]]

- **An algorithm delivers price.** ICT calls it "the algorithm" or "the price engine". It offers prices to a script; buying and selling pressure does not set direction.
- **Time comes first.** The engine works to a schedule (6 PM, 7 PM, 8:30, 9:30, the macros), so frame the time window before you look for a level.
- **Price goes to only two places.** At those times it reaches for liquidity (stops above old highs, below old lows, at relative equal highs and lows) or for an inefficiency (a gap a fast run left behind).
- **Smart money feeds on retail.** The informed few buy where the crowd's sell stops sit and sell where its buy stops sit. Retail patterns, trend lines and loud sentiment show where that liquidity is.
- **Real order flow is in the candles.** He ignores indicators, depth of market and volume tools. He reads where bodies open and close against his levels: wicks may stray, bodies tell the truth.
- **Know the draw, keep it simple.** Decide where price is likely going before anything else, trade one market with one to three moving parts, and step aside when price stops doing what the model says it should.

## Price is delivered by an algorithm

### What it is

In plain terms: ICT believes price moves because a computer program offers it higher or lower, not because buyers overwhelm sellers. Retail traders, dealers and high-frequency firms all trade the prices that program shows them. Nobody "pushes" the market; everyone reacts to what is offered.

His own framing is more specific. The price engine delivers price inside a predetermined "map", calculated from prior range data: the previous day, the last three days, the previous week and the previous month ([2024 #26 · 41:25](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2485)). He compares it to a video-game maze. Each session window (Asia, London, morning, lunch, afternoon) is a map, and neither retail nor large institutions control price inside it unless there is manual intervention ([2024 #26 · 40:37](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2437)).

The engine works like a recipe that refers back to old information: the date, day of week, week of month, month, quarter, the price, and the time price traded there ([2024 #3 · 1:06:57](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=4017)). It needs to know only what time and where, not how many contracts are trading ([2024 #12 · 31:57](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1917)). The look-back it draws on is what he calls the IPDA data range; for the opening gaps in his Event Horizon zone he caps it at 60 days ([2024 #16 · 31:50](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1910)). Chapter 15 covers the 20/40/60-day ranking.

He says the same logic runs on every timeframe: a daily chart behaves like the 1-minute chart, and he includes bonds and forex ([2026 #73 · 1:12:35](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4355)). When he talks about a "fractal", he means only a small segment of price you are studying that is doing something specific, such as an old daily low viewed on the 1-hour chart ([2022 Ep 25 · 24:36](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1476)).

Who is who in this model:

- **The price engine** originates price. In his description price is first piped to top-tier institutions and retail gets the scraps ([2024 #12 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1293)).
- **Dealers** pair orders and clear trades. They trade the same feed as everyone else and do not control where price goes ([2026 #73 · 36:58](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2218)).
- **HFT algorithms** (high-frequency trading programs) work on one premise: there is liquidity above an old high and below an old low, whether it is one contract or 10,000. They harvest the swings both ways all day, adding and taking partials ([2024 #26 · 35:03](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2103)).
- **Smart money** is the informed side that engages the engine; who exactly it is changed across the years (see "How the teaching changed" below).
- **Retail** is the large, uninformed crowd whose orders supply the liquidity (see the smart money section).

Even consolidation fits the model. Chop, in his view, is the algorithm cycling through its directives, not indecision among traders ([2024 #32 · 31:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1886)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is mechanical, and he builds it from a few observations.

- **Volume is not the cause.** When price rallies, the HFT orders hitting the market are volume, not the reason it rallies; the engine keeps offering price higher. He calls "bull squeeze" and "bear squeeze" cop-outs ([2022 Ep 3 · 20:55](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1255)).
- **A handful of contracts moves price.** One contract books a price. A candle can be built from two transactions: price reprices to a level and one market order trades there ([2022 Ep 16 · 43:54](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2634)). By the same logic one contract can open a market limit up ([2024 #2 · 2:47:00](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=10020)).
- **Slippage is the engine showing itself.** A market buy expected at 14,662 that fills at 14,664 is negative slippage; a better fill is positive slippage ([2022 Ep 3 · 21:43](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1303)). Price was offered where it was offered.
- **The market is zero-sum.** Every buyer has a seller, so "buying pressure" explains nothing. What matters is where the algorithm routes price: into liquidity or into an inefficiency ([2026 #75 · 2:31](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=151)).
- **HFT entries wait for the level.** These algorithms do not buy while price expands up. They wait for price to reach the inefficiency threshold, then fire entries on the first downtick after it is met ([2024 #2 · 2:43:49](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9829)).
- **"Volume precedes price" is misread.** It only means volume is larger where stops are taken. Beyond the level, volume thins, which is why gaps appear as price presses on ([2024 #2 · 2:44:37](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9877)).

Because the market is scripted, he says, it is not free: it repeats at the same times each day ([2024 #32 · 31:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1886)). He goes further and calls it "rigged". Retail does not control the Sunday open or the 9:30 first print; both are set in advance from the prior close. He says that is a good thing, because it is where the edge comes from ([2024 #49 · 2:04:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7452)).

### How to spot it

The evidence he points to is precision. If price were random, it would not keep stopping on pre-mapped levels.

1. Mark your levels before price gets there: old highs and lows, open gaps, and their midpoints.
2. Watch whether price reaches a level to the tick. When the HFT algorithms and the engine's delivery agree at a level, he calls it a "handshake", and price hits it exactly, for example the CE (midpoint) of a wick ([2024 #26 · 44:36](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2676)).
3. Watch whether candle bodies stop at the same price again and again ([2026 #35 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=530)).
4. Watch whether bodies refuse a level while wicks test it. He reads repeated refusal as the algorithm's signature, not coincidence ([2026 #107 · 15:49](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=949)). The order flow section below teaches this test.
5. Expect the precision to be easiest to see in ES. He calls ES the "gentleman's market", NQ "wild" and YM wilder still ([2026 #35 · 5:36](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=336)).

### Worked example

In a 2026 ES day-trade review on the 1-minute and 15-second charts, ICT points to 7,754.50. The same price shows up as the high of one candle and the open or low of others, several times, with bodies closing and the next candle opening at it to the tick ([2026 #35 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=530)). He says you don't see that in NQ. To him this is the strongest evidence that price is mechanical rather than chaotic, and that it follows the PD array structure (PD arrays are his umbrella term for the reference levels price is drawn to: gaps, blocks, old highs and lows; Chapter 4). He sums it up as "to the tick" ([2026 #35 · 9:41](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=581)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating support and resistance as walls.** If the algorithm is repricing lower, old highs, "supports" and indicators do not matter. It keeps offering lower prices until it reaches the resting sell stops ([2024 #32 · 42:40](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2560)).
- **Reading a big resting pool on the order book as a target.** Ladders can be spoofed, and a large pool on one side does not mean price goes there ([2024 #32 · 35:25](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2125)).
- **Blaming the HFT firms.** He defends the algorithms as adding liquidity; retail blames them for losses that come from misreading liquidity ([2024 #26 · 35:03](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2103)).
- **Expecting tidy delivery on news days.** On NFP-type days the "handlers" stir price so it looks disorganized. The setups are still there, but framing them takes more understanding. Chapter 14 covers news days and manual intervention.
- **Thinking wide teaching will break it.** His view is that most people will not be disciplined and rule-based, and new speculators keep arriving ([2022 Ep 22 · 29:48](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1788), [2022 Ep 22 · 31:24](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1884)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022: a belief to verify.** He states it simply (HFT orders are volume, the algorithm offers price) and challenges students to check it in their own charts ([2022 Ep 3 · 20:55](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1255)).
- **2024: the machinery.** The price engine, session "maps", prior-range data, the handshake, the scripted market and the "rigged" first print all arrive in the 2024 lectures.
- **Market maker versus dealer: he contradicts himself.** On August 30, 2024 he says market makers and desk traders are dealers who handle price piped to them and do not originate it ([2024 #21 · 1:20:14](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4814)). On October 28, 2024 he says a market maker is the entity that originates price, as opposed to a dealer trying to stay delta neutral ([2024 #49 · 2:04:12](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7452)). In 2026 he drops "market maker" and contrasts dealers (pair and clear, no control) with "smart money" ([2026 #73 · 36:58](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2218)).
- **Who stands behind delivery also shifts.** In 2024 the algorithm delivers price and smart money engages it with its own algorithms and discretion ([2024 #24 · 43:21](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2601)). In 2026 he describes a "smart money" entity behind delivery itself: manual intervention once, autonomous and algorithmic now. Wyckoff's "composite man" was an analogy; ICT says his version is happening "for certain" ([2026 #73 · 38:33](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=2313)).
- **NQ versus ES.** In 2024 he says NQ is wilder and so leaves cleaner inefficiencies, while ES is "fuzzy"; read NQ to infer ES, and do not expect candle-for-candle agreement ([2024 #24 · 3:29:55](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=12595)). In 2026 he calls ES the precise market where bodies stop to the tick and NQ the noisier one, preferring NQ for its bigger moves while calling ES easier for new traders ([2026 #35 · 6:25](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=385)). The two views stress different things (gaps versus body precision); Chapter 20 covers instrument choice.

## Time first, then price

### What it is

In plain terms: ICT asks "when?" before he asks "where?". A level only matters if price is due to reach for it in the window you are trading.

His definition has two halves ([2024 #5 · 56:22](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3382)). "Time" means the specific times he teaches: 6 PM, 7 PM, 8:30, 9:30, the macros and so on (Chapter 12). "Price" at that time is always either an inefficiency or liquidity ([2024 #5 · 1:30:05](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5405)). The market operates on time first, then price, and his tools applied at the wrong time will not work.

The idea is old in his teaching. In 2022 he says smart money uses no secret indicators: it looks at time and price, and time is the most crucial element, because time of day builds the likelihood of volatility and of short-term reversals ([2022 Ep 6 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=196)). In 2026 he calls it "scheduled delivery": everything he teaches is tied to when it will happen, like a TV schedule for a game, and that cancels randomness ([2026 #73 · 1:18:54](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4734)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation follows from the scripted engine: if delivery is scheduled, it repeats at the same times each day. Patterns without time, he argues, fool you. Rainfall statistics plotted over a year can look like a head and shoulders or a bull flag; you are "fooled by randomness" unless you know when things are due ([2026 #73 · 1:19:41](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4781)).

Time also limits what price can still do. He gives three cases.

- **Into the close.** The afternoon has built-in mechanisms that make the market accelerate into the close. Around 3:40, 3:50 and 4:00 PM, market-on-close orders produce very aggressive pricing that forces traders to cover or exit ([2022 Ep 5 · 26:09](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1569)).
- **The day's real range.** The bell rings at 4:00 PM New York time, but trading continues a little after. He uses 4:30 PM as "the close" to judge the day's full range, then decides which imbalances and liquidity pools are left ([2022 Ep 9 · 8:57](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=537)).
- **Friday.** On the last day of the week price must reach its targets before the 5:00 PM Eastern close, after which comes the weekend. It cannot afford to waste time consolidating, which is why he calls time "the most important factor" ([2026 #56 · 35:27](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=2127), [2026 #56 · 35:45](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=2145)).

### How to spot it

The 2026 lectures turn "first time, then price" into a procedure ([2026 #138 · 41:39](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2499)). Each trading window looks back at a reference window that came before it.

1. Decide which window you will trade.
2. Draw vertical lines around its reference window (table below).
3. Find the highest high and lowest low inside that window. That completes the time aspect.
4. Inside that range, scan for smooth highs and lows (relative equal highs and lows, Chapter 3), compressing or zooming out the chart to see them.
5. Decide which of them is likely to be run in your trading window.

| You trade | Reference window to scan | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-market, 7:00–9:00 AM | The overnight Asian session, 7 PM–5 AM, plus the London range, 2:00–7:00 AM | [2026 #138 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2214) |
| The PM session (afternoon) | 2:00 AM–11:00 AM, from the close of Asia to London "lunch" | [2026 #138 · 40:04](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2404) |
| The next Asian or London session | The New York session, 9:30 AM–4:00 PM | [2026 #138 · 1:33:46](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=5626) |

Rolled forward, each session uses the previous session's range as its reference. A beginner, he says, needs nothing more than the price action inside the 7–9 AM window plus the overnight highs and lows and any overnight relative equal highs and lows.

He gives other time anchors elsewhere. In 2024 he lists ranges you can build your own model on: the 7:00–8:00 AM hour range, the highest high and lowest low since 9:00, and the 9:30 opening. When price trades to them, you are out; you do not need 100 handles (index points) a day ([2024 #2 · 2:22:49](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=8569)). In 2026 his starting set for new students has three time-based pillars: the 7:00–9:00 AM pre-market with its high, low and phase; the RTH opening range from 9:30 to 10:00 and its consequent encroachment (midpoint); and the Silver Bullet at 10:00 ([2026 #10 · 7:24](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=444)). Chapters 12 and 17 teach those.

### How to trade it

- **Pre-market.** Frame the Asian range (plus London), find its smooth highs or lows, and expect the window to run one of them. On Monday January 12, 2026, walking the procedure at 8:25 AM, he identified the Asian highs as the draw before 7:00 AM ([2026 #138 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2214)).
- **Afternoon.** His hypothetical: at 1:30 PM, take the 2:00–11:00 AM range and find its high and low. If the high end has already been made (a premium market), look for smooth lows inside the range as the next liquidity run ([2026 #138 · 40:04](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2404)). For the PM session he says to use NQ and ES and avoid the Dow as "unruly".
- **Late afternoon.** Hold afternoon positions in the direction of the daily bias into the close, because of the 3:40 / 3:50 / 4:00 PM acceleration ([2022 Ep 5 · 23:42](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1422)).
- **After the close.** At 4:30 PM, review the day's full range and list what imbalances or liquidity pools remain ([2022 Ep 9 · 8:57](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=537)).
- **Friday.** Ask what price can still accomplish between now and 5:00 PM ET. Filter out any idea that would need more time than is left; on a Friday afternoon do not expect multiple round trips ([2026 #56 · 35:27](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=2127)).

A note on instruments. ICT teaches all of these clock times in New York time (ET) on NQ and ES index futures. The 9:30 first print, the 4:00 bell, market-on-close orders and the 5:00 PM Friday close are features of US index futures. The notes for this chapter do not show him mapping these windows to gold, so if you trade XAUUSD you are using his index-futures clock.

### Worked example

In a 2026 NQ weekly-range review ([2026 #56 · 35:27](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=2127)), the week's delivery was not finished on Friday morning.

1. The week's delivery still needed price to hit 28,512 and then retrace into the TGIF level (Chapter 13).
2. Because the week closes at 5:00 PM Eastern, all of that had to fit inside one session.
3. He treated the 7:00 AM pre-market start as "rocket fuel": the session opens give price the time it needs to reach its targets.
4. The practical rule he draws: on Friday, rule out any move that would need too much time, and expect straight runs to targets rather than drawn-out consolidation.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Right price, wrong time.** His tools applied outside their time windows will not work ([2024 #5 · 56:22](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3382)). Knowing when a setup is "not the right time" despite the right price is, he says, part of experience ([2026 #43 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=1293)).
- **Framing only the killzone.** If you only use killzone ranges (his named session windows, Chapter 12), you may miss that price already took the level outside the killzone ([2026 #138 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2214)).
- **Mixing up the closes.** 4:00 PM is the bell; 4:30 PM is the practical close he uses to judge the day's range; 5:00 PM ET is the close that ends the futures week on Friday.
- **Pattern-spotting without a clock.** A head and shoulders or bull flag with no time context is the rainfall chart again.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** time is "the most crucial element" ([2022 Ep 6 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=196)), with specific clock effects such as the market-on-close run and the 4:30 PM close.
- **2024:** a precise definition (time = the taught times; price = liquidity or inefficiency), session windows as the engine's "maps", and hourly and opening ranges as model anchors.
- **2026:** "scheduled delivery", a look-back window for each trading window, and Friday's time limit as a filter. There is no contradiction across the years; the idea only gets sharper.

## Liquidity and inefficiency: the only two destinations

### What it is

In plain terms: when the scheduled time arrives, price goes somewhere for a reason. In ICT's model there are only two reasons, and every later chapter is a refinement of one of them.

- **Liquidity** is resting orders, above all stop orders. Buy stops rest above an old high (buy-side liquidity); sell stops rest below an old low (sell-side liquidity). Relative equal highs or lows, two or more highs or lows at about the same price, are the clearest pools ([2024 #5 · 1:30:05](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5405)). Chapter 3 teaches liquidity in full.
- **Inefficiency** is a separation that a fast run leaves behind: a stretch of price that was offered in only one direction. The fair value gap (FVG) is the main form (Chapter 5); the volume imbalance is a smaller one (Chapter 6).

The opposite of both is balanced delivery. When price goes back and forth and each candle overlaps the prior candle's high and low, delivery is very balanced: there is no liquidity and no inefficiency inside it ([2024 #1 · 1:25:28](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=5128)). Runs, by contrast, leave separations. In the same lecture he points out that a spike stopping right at the top of a breaker after a balanced creep is meaningful ([2024 #1 · 1:55:05](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6905)).

He also names institutional price levels: the big figure (the 00 level) and the 20, 50 and 80 levels, where a lot of commerce and liquidity transacts ([2022 Ep 17 · 4:16](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=256)). His example is EURUSD, with the 1.0900 big figure as the downside objective because it sat at the daily relative equal lows ([2022 Ep 17 · 5:03](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=303)). He taught this on forex; the notes do not say how he applies it to gold or index futures.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation ties the two destinations together.

- **Stops are where the volume is.** Volume swells where stops are taken, below lows and above highs. Further beyond the level, volume thins, and that is why gaps appear as price presses on ([2024 #2 · 2:44:37](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9877)). A run on liquidity therefore creates the inefficiency that price later comes back for.
- **The algorithm patches what it skipped.** He calls the algorithm filling inefficiencies "patchwork" ([2024 #18 · 3:14:47](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11687)).
- **Price seeks the opposing pool.** Once one side is taken, the other becomes the draw. If buy-side was just taken, find the nearest obvious sell-side pool that has not been traded to ([2024 #32 · 48:21](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2901)). In 2022 he states it as a rule of anticipation: price seeks opposing liquidity ([2022 Ep 6 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=100)). The smart money section below explains why, through order pairing.

### How to spot it

1. In your reference window, mark the old highs and lows and flag any relative equal highs or lows (Chapter 3).
2. Mark the gaps that runs have left (Chapter 5) and note which are still open.
3. Note which pools have already been taken. A pool that was just run points you to the opposite one.
4. If price sits between a minor high and a minor low, list the only three outcomes: it stays in the range, runs the buy side, or runs the sell side ([2024 #47 · 35:11](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2111)).
5. Look for a tie-breaker. In his example it was the opening gap: price was below the prior settlement, so he wanted to see it draw higher into 9:30 ([2024 #47 · 35:58](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2158)). Settlement and opening gaps are futures features (Chapter 11).
6. Check the candles in front of you. Overlapping candles mean balanced delivery, with nothing to do inside it; separating candles mean a run is underway.

### How to trade it

In 2022 he boils entries down to "the only two patterns" you need ([2022 Ep 5 · 36:34](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2194)):

1. **Liquidity first.** A swing low is violated in a stop hunt, then price rallies: you buy the sell stops ([2022 Ep 5 · 35:47](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2147)). Mirror it for shorts above a swing high.
2. **Inefficiency first.** If no swing low is raided, look for a sudden displacement higher (a fast, one-sided move, Chapter 2), then an FVG. Buy when price trades back down into the FVG.

No breakers, no order blocks and none of the "15 gimmicky names" are needed, he says, but the logic has to be there for either one. Both work in the morning and afternoon sessions. Later chapters add precision, not new reasons.

Pulling the trigger still needs confluence, several things lining up at once: the liquidity level, an FVG, a further level break, another FVG, then delivery ([2022 Ep 33 · 11:25](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=685)). In his example 3855 was the target only if 3915.25 broke first ([2022 Ep 33 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=733)).

Targets come from the same map ([2024 #2 · 1:21:40](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4900)):

- For the beginner model, the target is the opposing relative equal high or low.
- To size the potential run after a reversal pattern, measure the range that formed before the reversal (after the relative equal highs were taken); price "gravitates" to its low.
- For longer holds he uses the previous day's high or low as the next logical draw ([2024 #2 · 1:41:55](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6115)).

He likes the image of a rock climber ([2026 #89 · 30:10](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=1810)). You don't climb the sheer face; you plan footholds and handholds: order blocks, FVGs, wicks, relative equal highs and lows. Once price grabs a foothold, with a body touching and closing beyond it, the next handhold comes into view ([2026 #89 · 37:31](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2251)). His shorter version: "one PDA at a time" ([2026 #32 · 18:04](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1084)).

### Worked example

NQ futures, pre-market, October 21, 2024 ([2024 #47 · 35:11](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2111)). This is a framing example; the notes do not record the outcome.

1. Price sat between a minor high and a minor low before the open.
2. Without a protocol, he says, you would be guessing off one candlestick pattern, one order block or one FVG.
3. With one, the question shrinks to three options: stay in the range, run the minor buy side, or run the minor sell side.
4. The tie-breaker was the gap. Price was below the prior settlement, so he wanted to see it draw higher into 9:30 ([2024 #47 · 35:58](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2158)).
5. In the same session he describes the retail trap on the other side: traders who see "we're way down" chase shorts and get wiped out once the gap starts to fill ([2024 #47 · 32:46](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=1966)).

A second, shorter case: on September 20, 2024 price dropped straight through "supports" and old highs to the relative equal lows. To him the algorithm was simply repricing lower until it reached the resting sell stops ([2024 #32 · 42:40](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2560)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Hunting levels inside balance.** Overlapping, back-and-forth candles contain neither liquidity nor inefficiency. They are not a setup; they are where you wait.
- **Trading one pattern without a protocol.** One candle pattern, one order block or one FVG is a guess until you know which pool or gap is due ([2024 #47 · 35:11](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2111)).
- **Reading an old high as resistance.** To ICT it is a pool of buy stops. Once it is taken, look for the opposing pool rather than a breakout continuation.
- **Forgetting the draw.** Graded levels and PD arrays help, but they do not replace knowing where price is likely going (see the last section).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** the "two patterns", opposing liquidity, and confluence before the click.
- **2024:** the crisp definition (price at the scheduled time is either liquidity or inefficiency), the contrast between balanced and separated delivery, and the HFT premise of liquidity above old highs and below old lows.
- **2026:** the same idea in "routing" language: the algorithm is not creating supply and demand, it routes price into liquidity or inefficiency ([2026 #75 · 2:31](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=151)). Nothing contradicts the earlier version.

- [2022 Ep 5 · 36:34](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=2194): the only two patterns you need.

## Smart money and retail: who supplies the liquidity

### What it is

In plain terms: ICT says the market has two camps, and the small informed camp trades against the large uninformed one. Retail's stops and entries are the raw material that smart money fills against.

His 2022 name for this is the "market efficiency paradigm" ([2022 Ep 6 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=100)). A small circle of informed money faces a large collective of speculative, uninformed money. New money keeps arriving while blown-up accounts leave. Smart money, he says, uses no secret indicators such as stochastics or RSI. It looks at time and price, and it feeds on retail, which is typically wrong about both bias and stop placement.

The working rules are short ([2022 Ep 6 · 0:02](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2)). Look to enter longs where retail sells and shorts where retail buys. Anticipate price seeking opposing liquidity. Do not trade a pattern for its own sake, an indicator reading or momentum.

His example of a smart-money "narrative" ([2022 Ep 6 · 6:24](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=384)): is the daily range going higher, and how? One answer is to go lower first, tempt shorts, run the sell stops so longs can be bought there, then rally into the afternoon and the close.

[[DIAGRAM ch01_d3 | caption: how smart money's orders pair with retail stops, sell stops first and buy stops second (illustrative) | After the sell stops are purged, smart money is long, and its natural counterparty is the buy stops resting above the old highs.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is order pairing ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:28:07](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5287)). Every order needs a counterparty, and stops resting beyond old highs and lows are orders waiting to be matched.

1. Price purges the sell stops below an old low. Smart money's buy orders pair with those sell orders, so it is now net long.
2. To get out, it must sell to willing buyers at higher prices. Those buyers are the buy stops resting above old highs.
3. So once the sell side is taken, the draw is the buy side. After a buy-side raid, the logic mirrors.

He sums it up as a continuum: smart-money buys pair with retail sells, and smart-money sells pair with retail buys ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:31:17](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5477)). His warning is blunt: if you do not think about how your order is paired before you click "enter", you are gambling ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:28:54](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5334)).

Two things keep the arrangement alive. Retail's tools mark where its stops go: it looks for connected dots, not liquidity or inefficiency, and he says banks do not trade connected dots ([2024 #20 · 59:27](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3567)). And new speculators keep arriving, so teaching the idea does not exhaust it ([2022 Ep 22 · 31:24](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1884)).

### How to spot it

The retail tells are places where a crowd's orders pile up.

1. **A flag.** Little bull flags drifting down into a lower target are, in his words, fake retail traps ([2024 #23 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=390)). A bear flag printing where he wants to buy "goes further in the odds" of a reversal, because it draws in sellers ([2026 #45 · 3:15](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=195)).
2. **A phantom trend line.** He does not use diagonal trend lines, but he looks for where retail would draw a perfect diagonal under a series of lows, because such a line is fakeable ([2024 #20 · 58:36](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=3516)). He expects the liquidity around it to be disrupted before or at the 9:30 open. A clean, obvious line opposite to his expectation becomes a target; otherwise he uses clean highs and lows ([2026 #138 · 1:08:53](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4133)).
3. **A level that is never retested.** The best moves leave an imbalance open on the way out, denying retail the "free ride" of a retest ([2024 #38 · 1:24:58](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5098)).
4. **The retail reading of the open.** Retail compares yesterday's close with today's open ("we're way down"), chases shorts, and is wiped out when the gap starts to fill. The professional view is today's open to today's close, the real accumulation or distribution ([2024 #47 · 32:46](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=1966)). Chapter 11 covers opening gaps.
5. **Trapped breakout traders.** He does not trade breakouts, but after the first 30 minutes he asks who is caught: shorts if a low was taken, longs if a high broke ([2024 #43 · 37:51](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=2271)).

He also reads crowd sentiment directly.

- **Squawk box (2022).** He listened to index-futures streamers as a contrarian gauge. When they were eager to buy a sloppy morning, he wanted a low to form first, to collect their stops ([2022 Ep 9 · 13:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=826)).
- **Chat as an indicator (2024).** The best scenario is people proclaiming longs (his example: 15 contracts on an unpassed funded account) as price trades into an FVG or just above a short-term high, with his own technical criteria present ([2024 #6 · 1:29:46](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5386)).
- **Watering hole.** If most chatters aim at a level his technicals say will not be taken, he expects them to "fall on the sword". He calls this anecdotal, and a soft filter with no numeric criteria ([2024 #24 · 37:47](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2267), [2024 #17 · 14:33](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=873)).

### How to trade it

- **Use retail positioning as location, not signal.** The pattern tells you where stops sit; your own setup still has to be there (Chapters 2 to 9). Do not take long signals from small flags when the draw is down into a daily PD array ([2024 #23 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=390)).
- **Be contrarian only with technical criteria present.** Contrarian by temperament is a trap ([2024 #6 · 1:59:48](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=7188)). Crowd conviction at an obvious level is resting liquidity, not confirmation ([2024 #24 · 43:21](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2601)).
- **Do not wait for the retest retail wants.** If an imbalance stays open as price leaves, keep the position.

### Worked example

NQ, 1-minute chart, London session on Friday, September 6, 2024 (NFP day) ([2024 #23 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=390)).

1. Price was grinding lower in repeated tiny bull flags. Retail reads each as continuation.
2. ICT had described the algorithm's target in advance: it would "spool down" into the 9:00 low and then dig into the August 13 daily FVG low. The notes mark the "9:00" as caption-unclear (9:00 AM that day or a prior level).
3. His reading: each flag adds long entries and stops that the algorithm uses on the way down. The draw was lower, so the flags were bait, not buy signals.
4. No depth of market or level 2 is needed; the higher timeframe and his levels are enough ([2024 #23 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=438)).

The notes record the setup, not the final fill, so this is a reading example, not a trade log.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Fading every flag.** In 2026 a flag aligned with higher-timeframe context can be valid (below).
- **Contrarian by temperament.** Disagreeing with the crowd is not an edge. The technical criteria are.
- **Expecting banks to trade your patterns.** He says banks do not trade this framework ([2024 #24 · 43:21](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2601)).
- **Using the retail view of the open.** Close-to-open is the crowd's frame; open-to-close is his professional one.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** the market efficiency paradigm, order pairing, sentiment as a squawk box.
- **2024:** sentiment becomes a named tool (chat as indicator, watering hole), always as a soft filter. "Fake retail traps" and "trend line phantoms" appear, and in an ES review he says he does not use bull flags or trend-line breakouts and fades them ([2024 #18 · 3:13:10](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11590)).
- **2026: a partial reversal on flags.** Small bull flags that fight the draw are still traps, but he now says a bull flag can be "trustworthy" when higher-timeframe context supports it, and he wants it to prove itself with a break above its highs and follow-through ([2026 #32 · 18:04](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1084), [2026 #139 · 7:02](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=422)). Both versions stand: fade flags that fight the draw, respect flags that agree with it.

### Watch it

## What he ignores: indicators, volume and the order book

### What it is

In plain terms: ICT builds on time, price, liquidity and gaps, and he leaves out the tools most retail traders start with. This is a stated stance, not a tested result, and the notes flag it as his opinion.

His skip list, in the order he taught it:

- **Indicators and oscillators.** Smart money uses no stochastics or RSI ([2022 Ep 6 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=196)). In 2024 he adds moving averages: none of them tells price what it will do ([2024 #26 · 44:36](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2676)).
- **Classic technical analysis.** He read John Murphy's textbook in 1992 and 1993 and calls it useful mainly for learning what not to do, since the crowd follows it. Trend lines are subjective, because you must choose which swing low to anchor. His line: "we don't do technical analysis, we do technical science", meaning price reduced to measurable relationships ([2022 Ep 12 · 15:21](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=921), [2022 Ep 12 · 16:09](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=969)).
- **Depth of market (DOM), level 2 and the order book.** In 2022 he simply did not trade with the DOM: spoofing means resting orders may not be there, and he saw no advantage, though he would not argue with someone it works for ([2022 Ep 16 · 31:14](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1874)).
- **Volume tools.** VWAP and volume profile are excuses for entries, not edges ([2024 #21 · 1:28:11](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=5291)). In 2026 he adds footprint charts, order-book heat maps and contract counts ([2026 #141 · 24:46](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1486)).

### Why it works

ICT gives four reasons.

- **The data is old.** Contract counts, footprints and profiles describe what has already traded. He calls order-flow data "yesterday's weather" and casts himself as the forecaster, working from where liquidity and imbalance sit ([2026 #140 · 34:07](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2047)). On footprints and level 2 he says none of it is valuable "because it's already happened" ([2026 #117 · 15:15](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=915)).
- **The ladder can be spoofed.** Time and sales, volume profile and the "book map" are used after the fact to justify moves. Ladders are not all real orders, and a big pool on one side does not mean price goes there ([2024 #32 · 35:25](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2125)). In another 2024 lecture he adds that big banks have been fined for spoofing ([2024 #12 · 31:57](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1917)).
- **Price does not need volume.** A handful of contracts reprice a market (first section of this chapter).
- **Time and where are enough.** The algorithm needs to know what time it is and where price is, not how many contracts traded.

He adds that he wished he had such tools only in the beginning, because he did not yet know liquidity ([2024 #32 · 35:25](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2125)). That lecture is titled "CFDs vs. Futures", but the notes record only the algorithm stance, not a CFD-specific comparison.

### How to trade it

You are not asked to buy a replacement tool. Use time, liquidity and PD arrays for direction ([2024 #32 · 35:25](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2125)), and read candle bodies for confirmation (next section). Chapters 3 to 9 teach the levels.

His 2026 position is softer than it sounds. If heat maps or an order-flow tool help you see salient pools of liquidity, use them, but he calls them a "visual crutch". He says he would have used BookMap in 1992 had it existed, and that if what you do makes consistent money, no one's opinion should bother you ([2026 #140 · 34:56](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2096)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Mixing two meanings of "order flow".** In the footprint world it means contracts traded at each price. In his 2026 definition it means when orders arrive, read through bodies (next section).
- **Treating sales claims as rules.** The notes themselves call lines such as "nobody else teaches this" marketing, not testable rules.
- **Dropping what already works for you.** The January 2 lecture says so explicitly ([2026 #140 · 50:13](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=3013)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** a personal choice. No stochastics or RSI, no DOM, and no argument with anyone it works for.
- **2024:** a firmer case. Ladders are spoofed bait, VWAP and volume profile are excuses, and real order flow is "not depth of market, not level two" ([2024 #23 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=438)).
- **2026:** the stance gets a positive replacement (bodies) and a tone that varies. The notes say the January 12 lecture attacks volume more strongly than the January 2 lecture, which tells you to keep what works. Both are his words, so read them as a range.

## Real order flow: candle bodies tell the truth

### What it is

In plain terms: ICT swaps volume-based "order flow" for a visual test. Where a candle's body opens and closes, measured against a level, tells you whether the algorithm is still pushing or has stopped.

His definition, from 2026: real order flow is not the number of buys and sells at a price, which is past execution. It is the timing of orders, when they are scheduled to arrive (macros, algorithmic clustering). Price moves because of order timing and rate of change, and one contract is enough if the timing is right ([2026 #6 · 12:18](https://youtu.be/UVVmS0de0g0?t=738)). You see that timing in the candle. The open and close are, he says, the two most important prices, because they define the body, and he treats wicks as noise ([2026 #58 · 31:52](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1912)).

He applies the idea in three forms.

- **Midpoint test ("do not trespass").** In an inefficiency (a gap) that agrees with your bias, price may wick into the far half, but it may not lay down a body there. Bearish: bodies stay in the lower half of the gap. Bullish: bodies stay in the upper half ([2026 #116 · 57:25](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3445)). Chapter 6 teaches the midpoint (CE, consequent encroachment).
- **Target test.** For a short, bodies must close below the target level (a first presented FVG low, an octant level and so on). Wicks below it with bodies above it signal weakness ([2026 #16 · 13:22](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=802)).
- **Key-level test ("bodies cannot touch").** In a bearish phase, wicks may tag a key level, but bodies stay out of it. When bodies repeatedly refuse the level, the algorithm is active there ([2026 #107 · 13:26](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=806)).

[[DIAGRAM ch01_d4 | caption: a bearish gap with bodies in the correct half, beside the same gap with one body in the wrong half (illustrative) | Wicks may cross the midpoint; only a body that closes in the upper half breaks the bearish read.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that smart money executes so that bodies honor a level while wicks test it. If a body breaches the level, or even rests on it, he reads the downside as spent and bullishness as possible again ([2026 #105 · 42:58](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=2578)). Wicks are where price is allowed to "color outside the lines"; the body is where it commits ([2026 #111 · 7:54](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=474)).

He treats repeated refusal as proof, not coincidence. When bodies keep failing to touch a line, he says an algorithm is directing them, and the notes advise accepting that rather than asking why ([2026 #107 · 15:49](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=949)). It is the same "to the tick" evidence as in the first section.

### How to spot it

His own checklist, from the March 21, 2026 lecture ([2026 #116 · 57:25](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3445)):

1. Mark an inefficiency (a sell-side or buy-side imbalance) and its midpoint, or a key level.
2. State your bias. Bearish expects bodies in the lower half; bullish expects them in the upper half.
3. After each candle closes, read where the body sits, not where the wick went.
4. If no body lands in the wrong half, the inefficiency is still valid. If one does, that is a violation and suggests a change of direction.
5. Count the refusals. The longer bodies keep refusing a level, the stronger the signal ([2026 #107 · 13:26](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=806)).

In a 2026 review he states it as one line: a body closing in the upper half of a PD array is bullish, and one in the lower half or the middle is bearish ([2026 #23 · 32:01](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1921)).

### How to trade it

- **While holding a short:** bodies in the lower half mean keep holding. Bodies in the upper half mean start thinking about exits. Longs are the mirror. No bodies in the zone at all, as the notes read it, is the best case, a clean continuation ([2026 #16 · 13:22](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=802)).
- **On a violation:** consider exiting or reversing the bias ([2026 #116 · 1:00:42](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3642)).
- **Do not flip early.** Keep your bias until a body finally breaches and closes beyond the level ([2026 #107 · 13:26](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=806)).
- **Do not over-read one warning.** In one lesson, a retracement with bodies in the upper half was a warning, but he judged it a stage-1 event, not a reversal ([2026 #16 · 18:31](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=1111)).

He calls the rule visual and simple, and criticizes traders who reach for footprints when the close is enough ([2026 #116 · 1:00:42](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3642)).

### Worked example

MNQ, daily chart, review dated April 8, 2026 ([2026 #107 · 13:26](https://youtu.be/3Uu2IB6kI28?t=806)).

1. The level: the lower quadrant of a daily suspension block (a gap-type block, Chapter 9), near 25,000.
2. Price was in a downtrend and retraced up into that area several times.
3. Each time, the question was whether a body closed above 25,000 or stayed below it. Bodies stayed below, so each retrace confirmed the bearish read.
4. When price finally ran lower, he took it as proof the signature was right.

On April 11 he gave the other side: when bodies finally touched that level on a retracement, it signaled a potential bullish shift ([2026 #105 · 43:47](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=2627)).

A shorter case is crude oil on June 18, 2026. Price rallied but never got a body above the CE it needed. He called that "algorithmically weak", and price then delivered lower ([2026 #75 · 5:36](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=336)).

Instrument note: he demonstrates this on index futures, crude oil and EURUSD ([2026 #117 · 15:15](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=915)) and calls the key-level version universal. The notes show no gold example.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Applying it everywhere.** The rule is for inefficiencies and key levels, in the direction of your bias. It is not a read of all price action ([2026 #116 · 57:25](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=3445)).
- **Counting wicks as violations.** Wicks beyond a boundary are normal. Sitting through them while waiting for the bodies is a critical skill, and the notes call it one of the hardest parts of the learning curve ([2026 #111 · 1:38:58](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5938)).
- **Reading a body before marking the level.** The test needs a defined midpoint or key level first.
- **Toothpick support.** When a large block of price action rests on one thin wick or small body, he calls the support weak, like a building held up by a toothpick. He does not buy into it ([2026 #61 · 13:36](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=816)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022 and 2024:** the idea appears without body rules. In 2022 he says to read liquidity and delivery state, not pressure ([2022 Ep 3 · 20:55](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1255)). In 2024 a market he calls "heavy" keeps refusing to trade the upper half of arrays on the way down ([2024 #18 · 3:13:10](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11590)).

## Know the draw, keep it simple, and know when the model is wrong

### What it is

In plain terms: before any level, ICT asks where price is probably going. The tools in later chapters pay off only if that answer is right, and only if you notice quickly when it stops being right.

- **Know the draw first.** It is the first thing he teaches. If you do not understand it, he says, everything you do afterwards will be wrong. The dealing range and its octants and quadrants tell you where PD arrays should form. Arrays at graded levels are seen as higher probability, but that does not replace knowing the draw ([2026 #34 · 1:35](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=95)). Chapters 4 and 6 teach the grading, and Chapter 15 teaches building the draw.
- **A key swing invalidates the idea.** If you are bearish and an imbalance is rebalanced, the next short-term high should be lower than the intermediate-term high (ITH). If price trades above it, the idea is probably flawed ([2022 Ep 12 · 41:55](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2515)). Chapter 2 defines the swing ranks.
- **The canary.** Miners carried canaries; a dead canary meant bad air. In trading, when price does something it "shouldn't" (shows reluctance, fails to take a level it should, wicks that do not reach a CE they should), the environment has changed ([2026 #104 · 45:46](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2746)).
- **Deferred delivery.** Sometimes price goes the expected way by an unexpected path. That is still a valid move, only delayed or restructured ([2026 #82 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=332)).
- **Simplicity.** Look for something with one, two or at most three moving parts, in one market ([2026 #141 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=99)).

### Why it works

ICT's reasoning is that a model which predicts specific things can be proven wrong quickly. The canary only works if you know what should happen; without a model you cannot see it violated ([2026 #104 · 45:46](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2746)).

Stacking tools blurs that. He calls using everything at once (COT, seasonals, Judas swing, Model 2022, Silver Bullet) his own mistake at 20, and says veterans do not stack ([2026 #141 · 4:51](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=291)). Specializing in one market lets you accumulate its recurring levels and behaviors ([2026 #141 · 14:24](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=864)). Experience, he adds, cannot be bought or transferred; he teaches the concepts and the grid ([2026 #43 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=1293)).

### How to spot it

A pre-trade routine that follows from these ideas:

1. State the draw: which pool or gap, in which time window.
2. Write what should happen on the way: which level must be taken, and where bodies must stay.
3. Write the invalidation: the swing whose violation voids the idea.
4. Limit the idea to one market and one to three moving parts.

### How to trade it

- **Invalidation breaks:** go to the sidelines and do not re-enter. The next setup may be the same session, the same day or next week. While the key high holds, trust the move and hold ([2022 Ep 12 · 52:26](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3146), [2022 Ep 12 · 50:47](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=3047)).
- **The canary dies:** do not defend the old thesis. Pivot your expectation to the opposite side and wait for confirmation of the new one ([2026 #104 · 20:06](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=1206)).
- **Direction right, structure different:** trust the direction and targets, and do not disqualify the move because the path differs ([2026 #82 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=332)).
- **Do not pick the major turn.** He says he lost more money calling long-term lows than from anything else. There is "a lot of meat" between turning points, and the real turn will be obvious ([2022 Ep 25 · 22:13](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1333)).
- **Beginner scope:** the 15-minute, 5-minute and 1-minute charts are enough, with the 15-minute checked once in the morning ([2024 #1 · 56:26](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3386), [2024 #1 · 1:43:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6239)). He ignores the 15-second and 5-second charts while the 1-minute consolidates ([2026 #131 · 22:24](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1344)).
- **Shorting a primary bull market** takes 1 to 2 years of experience; beginners should stay with longs ([2026 #83 · 11:18](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=678)).

### Worked example

Early Monday, April 13, 2026, futures opening-range tape reading ([2026 #104 · 20:06](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=1206)).

1. His model said price should take the sell side.
2. It showed reluctance and did not take it when it "should" have. That was the canary.
3. He did not defend the idea. He pivoted to expect a run higher, which happened.
4. The notes say he did not get frustrated and simply flipped the switch. They do not give levels or the instrument.

A second case is May 28, 2026 on NQ ([2026 #82 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=332)). He expected a two-stage drop (down, consolidate, down). He called what came deferred delivery and still counted it as delivered to the right level. The notes' two summaries differ on the path, one saying one sweeping move, the other saying price came back up first, so use it for the principle only.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Defending the thesis after the canary dies.** The point of the canary is to leave that line of thinking.
- **Re-entering after invalidation.** His 2022 rule is to stay out; forcing the trade is how accounts are blown.
- **Using deferred delivery as an excuse.** It applies when direction and targets still deliver, not whenever a trade goes wrong.
- **Graded level equals draw.** A PD array on an octant is higher probability, not a forecast ([2026 #34 · 2:23](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=143)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** invalidate on a key swing. Once the ITH or ITL breaks, the idea is flawed, so stand aside and do not re-enter.
- **2026:** invalidation becomes the canary, with a different next step: pivot to the opposite expectation and wait for confirmation. Deferred delivery adds flexibility about structure while holding targets firm. The two versions differ on what happens after the break, and the notes do not reconcile them.
- **2026:** the simplification message recurs. In the new-students series he says every individual concept can be a model, so you need not go beyond the three pillars to start ([2026 #10 · 7:24](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=444)).
