ICT grades every price against a range. In the upper half of a range price is expensive (premium), in the lower half it is cheap (discount), and the midpoint is equilibrium. This chapter shows how he chooses that range, how he measures it with the Fibonacci tool (equilibrium, Optimal Trade Entry, and the negative extensions he calls standard deviations), and how the same half-and-half logic runs inside every PD array. After it you should be able to say, for any setup, which range you are measuring, whether you are selling a premium or buying a discount, and how far the move can reasonably run.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch04_d1 | caption: A bullish dealing range graded into premium, equilibrium, discount and the OTE band (illustrative) | The OTE band of a bullish leg sits wholly inside discount, so an OTE long is always a discount purchase.]]

- Measure the dealing range you are actually trading, swing high to swing low. Its 50% level is equilibrium.
- Sell only in premium (above equilibrium) and buy only in discount (below it). ICT claims every profitable trade, by anyone's method, obeys this.
- Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) is the 62% to 79% retracement of a swing, with the Fibonacci anchored on candle bodies, not wicks. It becomes "optimal" when it overlaps an FVG or other PD array, ideally inside a macro time window. One 2024 lecture is noted as 62% to 72% plus a 70.5 level, which is probably the same band (see the OTE section).
- Project targets with negative Fibonacci extensions (−0.25, −0.5, −1, −1.5, −2), which he calls standard deviations. Alone they are useless to him; where they meet an old high, old low or PD array they become targets.
- Inside every PD array the halves matter too. A bearish array should not take bodies in its upper half; a bullish one should not take bodies in its lower half. Wicks may poke through; bodies decide.
- A PD array interests him only when it sits on a pre-marked level, at the right time, in line with the draw on liquidity. Everything else is "no man's land".

## Premium, discount and equilibrium

### What it is

Take any price range from a high to a low and cut it in half. The upper half is premium: price there is expensive relative to the range. The lower half is discount: price there is cheap. The dividing line is equilibrium, the range's 50% level.

ICT's precise version: drop a Fibonacci on the range from its low to its high and read the 0.5 level ([2022 Ep 4 · 0:56](https://youtu.be/L-ReMHiavPM?t=56)). At or above 50% is premium; at or below 50% is discount ([2024 #4 · 1:17:30](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4650)). In 2026 he also states it as the midpoint between two pools of liquidity: upper pool plus lower pool, divided by two ([2026 #82 · 7:10](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=430)).

He treats this as his overbought and oversold reading. Price dropping into a fair value gap below equilibrium on the hourly is, to him, an oversold condition that needs no indicator to plot ([2022 Ep 36 · 0:59](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=59)). You do not even need the Fib tool: in 2024 he finds the midpoint by eye ([2024 #40 · 1:34:41](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5681)).

The same words label PD arrays. A *PD array* (premium/discount array) is any reference in price that price is drawn to or reacts from: an FVG, order block, breaker, volume imbalance, wick, opening gap, or old high or low. One above current price is a premium array, used to sell; one below is a discount array, used to buy ([2026 #125 · 26:09](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1569)). The individual arrays are taught in Chapters 5 to 11.

The arithmetic does not depend on the instrument. In the notes he applies it to NQ, ES and MNQ futures, to EURUSD and EURJPY, and to Bitcoin. What does depend on the market is which range you measure: his session ranges use New York clock times on index futures (next section). The notes contain no gold-specific version of any of this, so on XAUUSD you carry the same logic over and choose your own ranges.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about who buys cheap and who buys expensive. Retail traders ("the street") buy momentum and sell weakness, always chasing. A professionally minded speculator buys discount prices and sells premium prices. Larger pools of capital will not engage while price rallies off the open; they wait for a return into discounted liquidity, below old lows ([2024 #9 · 30:30](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1830)).

He also frames it as pain. In a long run lower the shorts hold the profit, so the next move goes against them ([2024 #4 · 1:16:43](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4603)). And entry quality falls as your entry gets closer to the target: a short taken below equilibrium is already near where price is going ([2024 #40 · 1:22:07](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4927)).

His strongest claim: every profitable short, by anyone's method, was entered in a premium and exited in a discount, and every profitable long the reverse. He calls this a "100% strike rate" and says anyone not viewing price this way is doing it wrong ([2024 #37 · 20:26](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1226)). Treat that as emphatic teaching, not a measured statistic.

### How to spot it

1. Choose the dealing range you are trading (next section): the swing high and swing low of the leg in play.
2. Mark its 50% level, with a Fib set to 0.5 or by eye.
3. Ask where price is now: premium, equilibrium or discount, and how deep.
4. Classify every candidate PD array the same way. Above the line is a premium array for shorts; below it, a discount array for longs ([2024 #37 · 1:02:55](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3775)).
5. Check the bigger ranges too. In 2026 he grades the last three months, the previous week and the daily range at the same time ([2026 #17 · 9:18](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=558)).

### How to trade it

- **Sell premium, buy discount.** For shorts, take FVG or wick entries above the midpoint of the range being traded; for longs, below it ([2024 #37 · 45:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2711)). In a 2022 long he ticks two boxes out loud: below 50% of the range, and inside the order block ([2022 Ep 13 · 19:11](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1151)).
- **Do not chase a rich open.** After a gap-higher open and an impulsive 8:30 run, he would not buy a breakout or even a bullish FVG: the run is too long in the tooth and likelier to revert to fair value below ([2024 #9 · 24:26](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1466)). Nothing forces you to pay too much, and he suggests writing that in your journal ([2024 #9 · 15:26](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=926)). That day he waited for the 10:00 Silver Bullet (Chapter 17).
- **Do not short a deep discount.** Selling at the low of the day's range goes against what he taught in the first month of his 2016 mentorship ([2026 #64 · 44:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2654)). If you are short and price is already in deep discount, it must accelerate lower; lingering there is weakness ([2026 #114 · 1:28:25](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=5305)).
- **Use equilibrium as the first objective.** From a premium short, equilibrium is the first take-profit and the lower pool comes next ([2026 #82 · 7:10](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=430)). In 2022 he takes a partial at the first discount array below equilibrium, because price may come all the way back ([2022 Ep 40 · 12:07](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=727)). In a 2026 NFP trade he took one of two contracts off at the 0.5 between his entry and the target ([2026 #39 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=475)). Partials in general are Chapter 19.
- **Read daily bias from it.** When the daily chart pulls back to about 50% of its dealing range (a short-term discount), he expects the next day, or the day after, to be bullish ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:18:37](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4717)). He then picks the easiest premium array above as the target and trades only the morning move toward it, not the whole leg ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:21:47](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4907)).
- **Expect chop at equilibrium.** When daily price oscillates around its 50%, bias is hard to read, even for him. He calls these consolidation hurdles: trade intraday liquidity only, take smaller objectives, get in and out ([2022 Ep 7 · 10:37](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=637)). Back at equilibrium there is no directional advantage ([2026 #103 · 48:38](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=2918)).

### Worked example

NQ futures, his review of 28 May 2026 ([2026 #82 · 13:42](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=822)).

1. He marks the two liquidity pools framing the move: 30,055.75 above and 29,886 below.
2. Equilibrium is their midpoint. The arithmetic gives 29,970.875; he calls it roughly 29,970 and draws it as a red dashed line.
3. His plan: sell short only on the premium side of that line, above about 29,970 ([2026 #82 · 7:10](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=430)).
4. The expected path for a premium short that shows weakness: down to equilibrium, where the first profit comes off, then to the lower pool at 29,886. The signal he watches is price getting through the halfway point.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Measuring the wrong range.** What looks like deep discount on an old range may be equilibrium or premium of a new one. When price takes out an old low, the range resets and the new low becomes the reference ([2026 #117 · 35:09](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=2109)).
- **Selling any bearish FVG.** A bearish FVG that forms deep in discount is not a premium entry. In his 2026 NQ example it failed after five candles and price returned to the old low ([2026 #64 · 45:01](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2701)).
- **Reaching for an indicator.** In 2024 he calls divergence on any indicator useless for this ([2024 #4 · 1:15:55](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4555)), then softens it: it works only when it coincides with an order block or FVG inside a macro ([2024 #31 · 1:20:47](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4847)).
- **Two meanings of "premium".** "Price is in premium" describes where price sits in the range. "A premium array" is a PD array above price. In his 2024 example the FVG he sold was both: above price and in the upper half of the range he was trading ([2024 #40 · 1:35:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5729)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022.** Equilibrium is the 0.5 of a Fibonacci on the swing, and the tool is for the midpoint only ([2022 Ep 7 · 1:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=110)). Premium and discount replace overbought and oversold indicators.
- **2024.** He repeats the midpoint-only claim ([2024 #4 · 1:15:55](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4555)), though the same year he teaches OTE and projections with the tool. He adds the 100% strike-rate claim and Inception to Terminus.
- **2026.** He grades several ranges at once, defines equilibrium between two liquidity pools, warns about range resets, and moves premium and discount inside each PD array. He calls buying in a discount a month-one lesson of his 2016 mentorship.

### Watch it

- [2024 #37 · 19:37](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1177) — why not framing price as premium or discount is "doing it wrong".
- [2024 #9 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=733) — he refuses to buy a too-rich open.
- [2022 Ep 19 · 1:18:37](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=4717) — daily equilibrium as a next-day bias tool.
- [2026 #64 · 44:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2654) — do not sell short in an extreme discount.

## Dealing ranges: which range you measure

Premium and discount mean nothing until you say which range they belong to. The same price can be deep discount on one range and equilibrium on another. Choosing the range is therefore the first decision, and most of ICT's later refinements are about making that choice less arbitrary.

### What it is

A dealing range is the high-to-low span price is currently working inside. In 2026 he describes it as a consolidated area, or a high-to-low range, with smaller ranges nested inside larger ones: a daily range contains several 15-minute ranges, each containing 1-minute ranges ([2026 #67 · 2:24](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=144)). You mark its high and low prominently. Inefficiencies inside the range are your targets; those at the edges are the extremes. If price breaks outside the range, a new range is forming ([2026 #68 · 16:13](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=973)).

The 2022 version is the same idea as a discipline: even with a longer-term bias, every trade idea is framed by its *current* dealing range, measured from this high to that low ([2022 Ep 32 · 5:02](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=302)). Swings and liquidity pools are taught in Chapters 2 and 3; here they are only the anchors.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that delivery refers back to ranges. In 2026 he says the levels of a predetermined range are carried forward, as if an algorithm were pulling its data from that range ([2026 #42 · 15:56](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=956)), and that the first hour of the cash session is the range the algorithm refers to ([2026 #113 · 35:17](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2117)). These are his claims about how price is delivered (Chapter 1), offered as observation.

He also gives a practical reason for beginners. Trading inside a defined range removes the need to know where the next big run is. Big runs overshoot any range you draw, and a new trader is not equipped to find them yet; aim for base hits inside the range ([2026 #136 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=733)).

### How to spot it

Which range to use depends on what you are doing. These are the choices in the notes, from slow to fast:

1. **The latest swing on the chart you trade.** For a daily read, take the most recent swing low (the one that took out a short-term low, digging into sell-side liquidity) and the swing high, and use the Fibonacci only for the 50% line ([2022 Ep 9 · 0:53](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=53)).
2. **The most recent stage of a staged leg.** If a leg delivered low to high, retraced a little, then ran again, measure the latest stage: short-term low to short-term high. Wait for a drop into discount, ideally into an FVG ([2022 Ep 35 · 3:14](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=194)).
3. **Two liquidity pools.** Equilibrium is the upper pool plus the lower pool, divided by two ([2026 #82 · 7:10](https://youtu.be/NS5EcfChL3o?t=430)).
4. **Several scales at once.** In 2026 he grades the last three months, the previous week and the daily range together, and anchors the range lines at the highest high so they stay visible on the daily chart ([2026 #17 · 9:18](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=558), [2026 #19 · 21:28](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1288)).
5. **A time-boxed range for the session.** Before 9:30 ET, the pre-session low and high; the 7:00 to 9:00 ET range; the 9:30 to 10:30 ET first hour's dealing range; Friday's final cash hour as Monday's reference ([2026 #136 · 19:49](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1189), [2026 #42 · 11:52](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=712), [2026 #113 · 43:30](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2610), [2026 #37 · 0:46](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=46)). The windows themselves are Chapter 12.
6. **A range whose far end has not traded yet.** See Inception to Terminus below.

The first hour's range is the box from 9:30 to 10:30 ET snapped to the highest high and lowest low printed in between; the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range sits inside it, and equilibrium is its 50% ([2026 #113 · 43:30](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2610)). Do not confuse the two ranges.

### How to trade it

- **Use the range as the canvas.** Pick the draw, the stop and the target from its edges and its halves. In his January 2026 pre-session example, an inefficiency sitting near three quarters of the low-to-high range was a valid target. A run to it was enough; price did not need to reach the equal highs ([2026 #136 · 19:49](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1189)).
- **When two highs compete, use the lower one.** He took the lower, smaller reference high to keep things simple and to reduce the need to be right. It was also the more reactive high. Using the larger one would still have been acceptable, he said ([2026 #136 · 36:19](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2179), [56:08](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=3368)).
- **When urgency beats the big picture, drop a timeframe.** In 2022 a 15-minute displacement leg did not retrace into premium, as he expected, because price was rushing toward a weekly level. On the 1-minute chart the short-term dealing range showed the same FVG retrace *was* at or above equilibrium ([2022 Ep 33 · 13:00](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=780), [14:36](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=876)).

### Worked example

NQ, pre-session plan for the 9:30 ET open, his lecture of 29 January 2026 ([2026 #136 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=733)). The notes give the structure but no prices.

1. Before 9:30 he defines the range: the pre-8:30 low, where sell-side was taken but price failed to reach a lower eighth, up to a pre-session high.
2. Two highs compete: a lower high and a higher relative equal high. He picks the lower one. The sell-off from it was more energetic, and when two highs are close and obvious, price may bump the lower one, stay inside the range and never clear the higher one. That is what he watches for between 9:30 and 10:00.
3. Inside that range, an inefficiency sat roughly three quarters of the way from low to high. He called three quarters of the range the optimal-trade-entry area in loose speech, and treated a run into that inefficiency as the objective.
4. Price ran into the inefficiency in the upper quadrant and failed there without returning to the original consolidation, then sold off ([2026 #136 · 59:19](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=3559)). He ties this to the market maker sell model, where price can retrace to three quarters of the range without revisiting the consolidation (Chapter 17).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Confusing it with an opening range breakout.** The 5-, 15- and 30-minute ORB ranges influencers trade are not his reference, and he does not buy broken resistance or sell broken support. He offers the first-hour range as a side study, not a replacement for a working model ([2026 #113 · 35:17](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2117)).
- **Ignoring a reset.** Once price takes out the low that anchored the range, the old labels are void (see *Mistakes* in the first section).
- **Copying the clock to another market.** ICT built the 9:30 to 10:30 and 7:00 to 9:00 boxes on index futures. He did not say how they map to gold. If you test them on XAUUSD, treat that as your own experiment.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022.** One current swing, measured for its midpoint ([2022 Ep 32 · 5:02](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=302)), plus the most-recent-stage rule (Episode 35) and the lower-timeframe fallback (Episode 33), which refines an earlier, stricter premium rule: premium is relative to the range you measure.
- **2024 to 2026.** The range becomes the unrealized one (next section). Named, time-boxed ranges appear: pre-session, 7:00 to 9:00, the first hour (the notes flag it as new in the 2026 series) and Friday's final hour. Ranges nest, and the lower-high rule arrives.

### Watch it

- [2026 #136 · 19:49](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1189) — the dealing range is whatever sits between the pre-session low and high.
- [2026 #67 · 2:24](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=144) — a smaller portion of a larger dealing range.
- [2022 Ep 35 · 3:14](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=194) — use the most recent stage of the leg.

## Inception to Terminus: grading a range that has not delivered yet

A swing you can already see is easy to measure. In 2024 ICT moved the same idea forward in time: measure the range from where you expect to enter to where you expect price to go, before price gets there. That turns premium and discount into an entry filter.

### What it is

Before every trade, he says, you must know two things. The *Terminus* is where price is going: the liquidity target. The *Inception* is the PD array, the point of interest, where the move begins ([2024 #40 · 1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833)). The range between them is the one you grade. Its 50% line is equilibrium, and for a short you only enter above it.

He calls this grading the unrealized dealing range: the range has not delivered yet, but you can anticipate where setups will form inside it ([2024 #40 · 1:34:41](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5681)). In 2026 it is the unrealized or implied dealing range, one whose far end has not traded yet. His example runs from where the day's displacement candle opened to the high so far; because price had taken the previous day's high, he calls the extension to the next draw all but certain, while adding that it is an opinion you must submit to time ([2026 #72 · 16:21](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=981), [18:02](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1082)).

[[DIAGRAM ch04_d2 | caption: A short framed from Inception to Terminus, with two candidate FVGs (illustrative prices) | Only the FVG above equilibrium qualifies; the one below it sits too close to where price is going.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about distance. Entry quality falls as the entry gets closer to the target, so a short taken below equilibrium is already near where price is going and has little room left ([2024 #40 · 1:22:07](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4927)). The Terminus sits beyond the liquidity because the liquidity has to be engaged: the objective is not just to reach a price but to trade through it ([2024 #40 · 1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833)).

In his 2026 model of how smart money offsets, the range has two stages: partials at discount inefficiencies and the half gap, then the rest at the next sell-side pool. The span from the entry high to the final low is the implied dealing range ([2026 #131 · 43:26](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2606), [49:13](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2953)).

### How to spot it

1. Name the Terminus first. In his 2024 example it was just below a set of relative equal lows, beyond the liquidity.
2. Name the Inception: the volume imbalance or FVG area where the move should start. Chapters 5 and 6 teach those arrays.
3. Find the midpoint. You do not need the Fibonacci tool: by eye, the midpoint between an inversion FVG and the relative equal lows is where equilibrium sits.
4. Classify every candidate FVG against that midpoint. For a short, those above it are premium arrays; those in the lower half are not.
5. When several FVGs remain, take the one in the upper half. In his example the next gap above the chosen one was a breakaway gap ([2024 #40 · 1:35:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5729)).
6. Re-measure when stops are taken. Each time price takes stops, the range widens to the new extreme or the new intended target ([2026 #21 · 22:48](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1368)).

### How to trade it

- **Short only from premium arrays; long only from discount arrays.** Target the Terminus, beyond the liquidity ([2024 #40 · 1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833)).
- **Pyramid only on the cheap side of equilibrium.** His rule: if long, add at the range's equilibrium or lower; if short, at equilibrium or higher. Once price has crossed equilibrium, the halfway point of the run you expect, you are not permitted to add. He calls it a rule of thumb that serves him well ([2026 #21 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1321)). The 2024 notes carry an earlier form: pyramid only while price has not passed the 50% mark of the way to the target ([2024 #26 · 47:52](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2872)).

### Worked example

NQ 1-minute, his tape-reading lecture of 2 October 2024 ([2024 #40 · 1:20:33](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4833)). Prices are not in the notes.

1. Pre-market had rallied. The Inception was a volume imbalance and FVG area after that rally.
2. The Terminus was just below a set of relative equal lows, so the sell-side liquidity would be engaged.
3. He drew equilibrium between the two. The volume imbalance and the first FVG sat in the premium half; the next gap above was a breakaway gap.
4. The market respected the premium arrays and delivered to the sell-side below.

A second, with numbers: in a 2026 NQ review he went six contracts long, added four in discount (ten in all), took four off just above the first high and left six for the Terminus. Equilibrium of the original low-to-old-high range was 29,514.50, and his add sat above it. He had already planned a target above that high, so he re-measured the range to that target; the line moved up and the add fit ([2026 #21 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1419), [30:58](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1858), [41:40](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=2500)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Taking the nearest FVG whatever its half.** A gap in the lower half of a short's range may print beautifully and still be a low-probability entry, because it is close to where price is going.
- **Treating the Terminus as an exact price.** It is the liquidity and the space beyond it.
- **Moving the target to make the add fit.** Some accused him of form-fitting the 29,514.50 re-measure. ICT answered that he had taught the rule behind a paywall and thought it on the fly. The practical protection is his own chain-of-custody advice to write down the levels you will watch before the move, not after the fact ([2026 #49 · 55:11](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=3311)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2024.** Inception and Terminus appear as new terms, and the pyramiding limit appears as not going past the 50% threshold of the target.
- **2026.** The range gets the name unrealized or implied, and the pyramiding rule is stated in full, with the re-measure caveat.

### Watch it

- [2024 #40 · 1:22:07](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4927) — equilibrium, and why a short below it is low probability.
- [2024 #40 · 1:35:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5729) — choosing between FVGs by their half.
- [2026 #21 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1321) — the pyramiding rule, stated.

## Optimal Trade Entry and his Fibonacci settings

Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) is premium and discount with a depth attached. Instead of "buy somewhere below 50%", it says buy the retracement that has reached 62% to 79% of the swing. This section covers the pattern, the exact Fibonacci levels he types in, and the point that trips most people: which prices, bodies or wicks, you anchor the tool on.

### What it is

OTE is a retracement into the 62% to 79% zone of a swing, measured after a market structure shift (Chapter 2). Because the zone is deeper than 50%, a long OTE always sits in discount and a short OTE always in premium. In the lecture of 6 August 2024 he calls it his flagship pattern on the channel since 2012, and says Aug 6's stop hunt and breakdown was a classic one ([2024 #2 · 1:36:14](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5774)). Later that month he puts the flagship period at about ten years ([2024 #19 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2247)).

What makes it "optimal" is overlap. A 62% to 79% retracement with an FVG inside it is the entry. The retracement alone is only the zone ([2022 Ep 22 · 48:17](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2897)). Chapter 5 teaches the FVG.

He first presents it in 2022 as a very simple strategy and stresses that you need not be precise about the entry price. Be precise about directional bias and about where price is reaching for. He calls these *forgiving entries* ([2022 Ep 1 · 24:11](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1451)).

### Bodies or wicks

For premium and discount he measures the whole range, high to low ([2022 Ep 22 · 46:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2799)). For OTE he anchors on candle bodies: the lowest open or close and the highest open or close in the swing. His reason is that wicks are stop runs, and the bodies get to the heart of the matter. He adds that people who say he misuses the Fibonacci have missed this ([2022 Ep 22 · 49:05](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2945)).

The same body habit shows up in 2026. To find a day's high he anchors on a swing low using bodies and watches the −0.5 level. In his NQ example the swing low was about 29,316 and the −0.5 level about 29,413. A wick ran to 29,414.75, and the bodies then pulled back to align with the level; he shorted from there ([2026 #23 · 53:59](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3239), [54:46](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3286)).

Ranges whose edges are the liquidity use the extremes: the TGIF weekly range runs from the week's highest high to its lowest low ([2024 #36 · 1:19:25](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=4765)), and so does each hour of the standard-deviation drill ([2024 #2 · 1:54:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6856)). The notes give no single sentence covering every case. The pattern they show is extremes where the high or low is a pool, bodies where you measure a swing's real travel for OTE or a projection.

### Why it works

ICT's case is layered: depth gives the price, a PD array gives the place, and time gives the moment. In September 2024 he said a 62% to 79% retracement is only about a three-in-four thing alone. Add a PD array that agrees and a macro window and it becomes a loaded deal, which may not run 100 handles but will run back to the top of the framed range. Those are his figures, not measured statistics ([2024 #31 · 1:20:00](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4800)).

His macro windows are the last 10 minutes before each hour and the first 10 after it, xx:50 to xx:10, which he also calls time and price theory ([2024 #31 · 1:13:32](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4412)). Macros are Chapter 12.

### How to spot it

1. Find a swing that broke structure (Chapter 2). Without a shift you only have a pullback.
2. Anchor the Fibonacci on bodies: the highest open or close to the lowest open or close of the swing.
3. Mark 0.5, 0.62 and 0.79. The band between 0.62 and 0.79 is the OTE.
4. Look for an FVG, or another PD array, that overlaps the band.
5. Check the clock. A macro window, xx:50 to xx:10, adds to the case.
6. Know your target before you enter: the next liquidity pool, or a standard-deviation projection (next section).

### How to trade it

- **Enter on the overlap.** Price trades up (for a short) or down (for a long) into the band and the FVG inside it. That is the entry ([2022 Ep 22 · 48:17](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2897)).
- **Take profit at a projection.** In his 2022 NQ example a one-standard-deviation projection was hit precisely, then price retraced: a good profit-taking area ([2022 Ep 22 · 50:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=3039)).
- **Counter-trend version.** Against a bearish bias he bought a sell-side raid and targeted only the 62% retracement of the prior drop, or the area above the bodies of the down-move. He calls that bread-and-butter, roughly 30 to 40 pips. It is against the order flow, something to backtest, and he says it is not for developing students to trade live ([2022 Ep 19 · 49:15](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2955), [54:49](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=3289)).
- **It stacks with other entries.** In one morning drop he points out OTE, his 2022 model, a Silver Bullet and an institutional order-flow entry drill. All are valid and can be combined; a lower one can be a pyramid add, which moves your average price toward the target, so size it with care ([2024 #19 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2247), [55:15](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3315)). Chapters 17 and 18 cover those entries.

On XAUUSD the levels are unit-free, so the same 62% and 79% apply. His examples, though, are NQ, ES and forex (EURUSD, EURJPY), measured in handles and pips.

### Worked example

NQ, a high-to-low swing, his 2022 Mentorship Episode 22 ([2022 Ep 22 · 46:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2799)). The notes show the structure but give no prices.

1. He drops the Fibonacci on the swing from high to low, anchored on bodies.
2. Price trades up into the OTE band, and an FVG sits inside it. That overlap is the entry.
3. The body-anchored band puts the retracement right up to almost the 79% level.
4. A one-standard-deviation projection below the low is reached precisely. Price then retraces, which he marks as a good place to take profit, also below the low ([2022 Ep 22 · 50:39](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=3039)).

A counter-trend case with prices: EURUSD, hourly and 15-minute, in the 2022 Mentorship Episode 19. A slam lower runs the previous daily low, which he gives as 1.08061. Price rallies, leaves an FVG, and runs back toward old highs into the 62% retracement of the high-to-low, a short-term premium. On the 15-minute chart a displacement off the old low leaves a clean FVG. He buys the retrace into it and sells at the hourly 62% retracement for about 30 pips ([2022 Ep 19 · 47:41](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2861), [49:15](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2955)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Trading the band without a reason.** Alone it is the three-in-four zone. He wants the PD array and the time window too.
- **Anchoring on wicks.** You get a wider, shifted band (diagram above). He says wicks are stop runs.
- **Taking the counter-trend version live too early.** He flagged it as not for developing students.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022.** In Episode 7 he says "no OTE, no indicators" and uses the Fibonacci only for the midpoint ([2022 Ep 7 · 1:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=110)). By Episodes 19 and 22 he teaches the 62 and 79 levels and his Fibonacci settings.
- **2024.** He again says the Fibonacci is only for the midpoint ([2024 #4 · 1:15:55](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=4555)). Yet on 6 August the OTE is recorded as 62% to 72% plus a 70.5 level, and the notes say he first misspeaks the lower number as 60 ([2024 #2 · 1:37:01](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5821)). In September it is 62% to 79% again ([2024 #31 · 1:20:00](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4800)). 70.5 is the exact midpoint of 62 and 79, so the August "72" is most likely a caption slip for 79. Treat 62 to 79 as the working band and note that the sources conflict.
- **2026.** He describes OTE as the flagship he taught for years before he taught PD arrays, and "three quarters of the range" becomes loose shorthand for it ([2026 #136 · 19:49](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1189)).

## Standard deviations: how far a move can run

Premium and discount tell you where to enter. Projections tell you where a move can reasonably end. ICT uses the Fibonacci's negative levels for this and calls them standard deviations. The name is his; there is no statistics behind it. A "standard deviation" here is a copy of a swing's height laid off beyond one of its ends.

### What it is

Take a swing. Project its height again beyond the extreme that lies in the direction you expect price to go. One full swing height is −1, which he also calls one standard deviation or a measured move. Half of it is −0.5, and a quarter is −0.25 ([2024 #31 · 54:55](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3295)). Each example in the notes follows this direction rule, but the notes never state it as a rule, so check it against your own drawing.

The question it answers, in his words, is how far you can reasonably expect price to go below a low ([2024 #31 · 54:08](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3248)). Which swing you measure changes with the day:

| Swing measured | Levels used | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The leg right after price leaves a consolidation, before the discount retracement | −1 | [2022 Ep 8 · 8:46](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=526) |
| The daily range, to learn where a low can form | 0.5, 1 and 2 | [2022 Ep 22 · 49:05](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2945) |
| Each hour's high to lowest low (7:00, 8:00, 9:00), after the fact | 1, 2 and 2.5 | [2024 #2 · 1:54:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6856) |
| The leg before a run, the "wind-back" | −1 and −1.5 | [2024 #5 · 59:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3572) |
| A dealing range, for the run beyond its low | −0.25, −0.5, −1 | [2024 #31 · 54:55](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3295) |
| The 7:00 to 9:00 ET range low up to the first high after 9:30 | a negative extension, caption unclear (shown as −0.5) | [2026 #29 · 12:29](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=749) |
| The 7:00 to 9:00 ET range, low to high | −0.5 as the projected daily high | [2026 #42 · 13:28](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=808) |

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is the wind-back. In 2024 he describes the leg before a run as a slingshot pulled back, with the run as its release, so that leg measures the run ([2024 #5 · 57:57](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3477)). In 2026 he adds that the first leg off the low of the 7:00 to 9:00 range is a draw because it is not random ([2026 #29 · 13:15](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=795)). A projection becomes a target when it lands where something real rests, such as an old high or low, relative equal highs or lows, or an opening gap quadrant, because that is where stops sit ([2024 #5 · 59:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3572), [2024 #31 · 54:55](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=3295)).

### How to spot it

1. Pick the swing: the leg that prepared the move, or the time-defined range (Chapter 12).
2. Lay the Fibonacci on it and project beyond the extreme in the direction of the target.
3. Mark −1 first. It is the plain measured move and the easiest level to defend.
4. Look for confluence at or near that price: an old high or low, relative equal highs or lows, a PD array, an opening-gap quadrant.
5. If nothing rests near −1, look at −1.5 as a place where price may overshoot, and at −0.25 or −0.5 as stepping stones.

### How to trade it

- **Treat −1 as low-hanging fruit.** It is an obvious exit, especially if it reaches an old high or relative equal highs where real stops rest. With more than one contract, partial there with a limit order ([2024 #5 · 59:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3572)).
- **Aim for the zone between −1 and −1.5, not the exact tick.** He calls it the gray area and exits near its middle, since being too precise often leaves the limit unfilled. The caption for the exact level is unclear, most likely about −1.25 ([2024 #5 · 1:03:34](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3814)).
- **Leave room when others know the level.** He published a downside objective of 30,137.50 and set his limit two ticks above the level at 30,137.50 because he had called it publicly. He wanted a little fluff ([2026 #29 · 12:29](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=749)).
- **Treat the projection as live.** If a higher high forms after 9:30, it replaces the anchor and you re-measure from the new high down to the low; a lower low gives the next target. A projection from the new high to a lower low gave 30,090.00 ([2026 #29 · 14:51](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=891), [18:52](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1132)).
- **Re-deliver the retracement range.** Take a small rally-then-pullback range and project the same range above the new high. If price trades there, bring the stop to just below the pullback low; if not, leave it where it is. The line also works as a partial level, and he eyeballs it. He calls this an advanced topic ([2026 #18 · 1:09:30](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4170), [1:12:51](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4371)).
- **Half-leg target after a stop hunt.** Measure the high-to-low of the leg that raided equal lows, mark its equilibrium, then project that half-range above the leg's high. If price takes the relative equal highs it should at least reach this level; failing to reach it is weakness ([2026 #101 · 1:29:36](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5376)).

### Worked example

NQ, a Friday after a big post-FOMC rally. The notes do not give the date. His 2026 lecture on high-precision intraday price action ([2026 #42 · 11:52](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=712)).

1. He anchors the Fibonacci at the low of the 7:00 to 9:00 ET range and drags it to the high, with −0.5 added to the settings.
2. The −0.5 plots at 28,723.00. He calls it a projected daily high.
3. His reasons for expecting the high first: price had drifted lower in 7:00 to 9:00, it was a Friday after a big rally so a deep retracement was due, and buy-side liquidity (relative equal highs) sat above.
4. The plan: take out the buy side, then retrace deeply to 28,400.
5. Price ran up and printed the high about 2.75 handles above 28,723 ([2026 #42 · 25:31](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1531)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using it alone.** The hourly drill is "useless" by itself in his words. It matters only when a projection converges with a real old low or high ([2024 #2 · 1:55:03](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6903)).
- **Treating the hourly drill as a live signal.** He frames it as a homework exercise done after the fact. Projections from the 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00 hours clustered on 6 August, and price went below an old low and came close to the deepest one but fell short, which he accepted ([2024 #2 · 1:53:30](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6810), [1:56:38](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6998)).
- **Mixing up the same number with different jobs.** −0.5 is a stepping stone beyond a dealing range in 2024 and the projected daily high of the 7:00 to 9:00 range in 2026. The anchor decides what the level means.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022.** −1 is the expansion target when there is no old high or low nearby; the settings are 0.5, 1 and 2 ([2022 Ep 8 · 8:00](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=480), [2022 Ep 22 · 49:05](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=2945)). In the EURJPY example the −1 landed at about 133.15 and matched the high of the move at 133.153.
- **2024.** Levels multiply: 1, 2 and 2.5 for the hourly drill; −1 and −1.5 with a gray area; −0.25, −0.5 and −1 beyond a dealing range.
- **2026.** The anchors become time-defined ranges, the daily range gets a −2 line, and he adds the re-delivery and half-leg projections. He says the precision teaching should not be broadcast, because it can stop working once everyone knows ([2026 #83 · 30:11](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1811)).

## Premium and discount inside a PD array: the half rule and sensitivity

The half-and-half test also works at the scale of a single array. In 2024 ICT began applying it to FVGs, order blocks and inversion FVGs, and in 2026 he gave the two outcomes names: premium sensitivity and discount sensitivity.

### What it is

Every PD array has a midpoint (its CE, Chapter 6) and so an upper and a lower half. His rule: if you are bullish, you do not want the lower half of the array traded to; if you are bearish, you do not want the upper half traded to. The array should show it cannot deliver price in that half ([2024 #18 · 1:23:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4984)). The same applies to inversion FVGs and order blocks, and in 2026 to wicks: a bullish daily wick must be able to post bodies above its midpoint, and a bearish one keeps bodies in its lower half ([2026 #29 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=97)).

The 2026 names follow the side the array lives on. A premium array sits above price; it shows *premium sensitivity* when bodies stay in its lower half. A discount array sits below price; it shows *discount sensitivity* when bodies stay in its upper half ([2026 #125 · 26:09](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1569), [2026 #95 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=977)). Only bodies count. A wick that pokes into the forbidden half is allowed, and he nicknames a small wick outside the gap a "mohawk".

[[DIAGRAM ch04_d5 | caption: One bearish FVG, two outcomes: bodies stay in the lower half, or a body closes above the CE (illustrative) | A wick above the midpoint is fine; a body closing there is the warning.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that wicks are probes and bodies are intent. He says bodies plus levels give you order flow without tick data or a volume profile ([2026 #125 · 27:47](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=1667)). If bodies cannot even reach the midpoint of an array while wicks can, that is a sign of no conviction ([2026 #111 · 7:06](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=426)). A body that closes fully beyond an array's far boundary is a confirmation; a wick alone is not ([2026 #61 · 2:23](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=143)). He insists this is not supply and demand ([2024 #18 · 1:35:08](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5708), [2026 #29 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=196)).

### How to spot it

1. Mark the array's high, low and midpoint, on the time frame where the array exists ([2024 #11 · 2:53:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10418)).
2. Name the protected half: the upper half of a bearish array, the lower half of a bullish one.
3. Watch bodies, meaning opens and closes, not wicks.
4. A wick into the protected half is fine. A body there means sensitivity is lost.

### How to trade it

- **Enter in the allowed half.** For a short, that is the lower part of an order block or candle. The older shortcut was only the low of the up-close candle or the opening price; the half rule refines it ([2024 #18 · 1:23:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4984)).
- **If the protected half is traded into, demand an instant response.** The lower half of a bullish array can be traded into, but price must respond at once from the deepest part. If it does not, you probably have a dud: cancel, take a scratch or lose only commission ([2024 #18 · 1:35:08](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5708)).
- **Hold while bodies respect the half; cut back when they do not.** In the 2026 rule, bodies in the upper half of a bearish gap mean the setup is weakening; staying in the lower half is confirmation to hold or add ([2026 #102 · 22:33](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1353)).
- **Add with the untouched half.** An untouched protected half also lets him pyramid with more confidence ([2024 #18 · 1:23:04](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=4984)). The add still has to meet the equilibrium rule from the previous section.
- **Read failure as information.** A bearish gap that spent too many candles with no premium sensitivity, in a deep discount, failed after five candles and price went back to the old low ([2026 #64 · 45:01](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2701)). A gap above price that price cannot even reach the midpoint of is, to him, a strong reason to flip bearish ([2026 #51 · 10:30](https://youtu.be/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=630)).

### Worked example

MNQ 1-minute, 21 April 2026 ([2026 #95 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=977)). The notes give no prices.

1. A bearish FVG forms. He expects bodies in its lower half: premium sensitivity.
2. While bodies respect the lower half the short stays valid. Wicks into the upper half do not change that.
3. Later an inversion FVG forms. He now expects bodies in its upper half: discount sensitivity.
4. If bodies cannot hold the upper half, the bullish idea is invalid and price should revert to the first gap's original direction ([2026 #95 · 28:07](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1687), [39:11](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2351)).

In an August 2024 NQ short, price left the upper half of a volume imbalance and inversion FVG untraded while wicking into the lower half and the CE, which gave him the confidence to add ([2024 #18 · 2:54:01](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=10441)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Counting a wick as a violation.** Only bodies break the rule.
- **Trusting the labels over the test.** In the notes his 2026 wording is not always consistent: the same array is sometimes called by the side it sits on and sometimes by who is trading it. Use the bodies-in-which-half test, not the label.

### How the teaching changed

## The PD array grid: pre-marked levels, time frames and the chain

By 2026 ICT describes all of this as looking at price through a grid. The notes contain no ranked list of array types, so this section does not invent one. What they do contain is a set of filters that decide whether an array deserves your attention at all.

### What it is

- **A notepad of static levels.** He keeps price points written down and asks where price sits in the last five days' range and how long it has spent in discount versus premium. If he is bearish, price has stayed in the lower half and a red-folder event is due, he does not load up on new shorts, because the event can be used to rip against the real move ([2026 #127 · 38:02](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2282), [38:50](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2330)).
- **Level, time, draw.** A PD array interests him only if it sits on a pre-marked level (the gradient levels of opening gaps, the opening range), at the right time (a macro or session), and in line with where he thinks the market will draw. Anything else is in no man's land and is just a common gap ([2026 #129 · 48:36](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2916), [49:24](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2964)).
- **Tethering.** After 9:00 ET, any PD array that forms must touch or lay across one of the 7:00 to 9:00 range's levels: its quadrants, octants, midpoint, high or low. Those are specific prices, not zones. He presents this as observation, not proven fact ([2026 #42 · 15:56](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=956), [19:09](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1149)).
- **A ladder of time frames.** Lower time frames should respect higher-time-frame arrays: draw the FVG as a rectangle and carry it down, and judge whether bodies go beyond it, since wicks may overshoot. His September 2024 ladder is monthly, weekly, daily, 4-hour, 1-hour, 15-minute, 5-minute, 1-minute, nothing between and sub-minute excessive ([2024 #24 · 2:43:33](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9813)). Chapter 18 develops this as the price delivery continuum.
- **One array at a time.** Price has to prove itself at the next array before you assume the far target ([2026 #29 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=247)). He also says to learn with a single array as your model before learning the rest ([2026 #44 · 46:34](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2794)).
- **The chain.** Price is handed from one PD array to the next. Each array is a waypoint; while none of them produces a body closing outside it in the opposite direction, price continues to the next. A close outside breaks the chain and you recalibrate ([2026 #61 · 5:33](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=333)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that delivery is algorithmic and autonomous, so the explanation should be visual and mathematical rather than retail support and resistance or supply and demand ([2026 #47 · 0:47](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=47)). Grading one specific inefficiency removes the ambiguity of which high or low to use ([2026 #49 · 55:11](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=3311)). Those are his claims.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Trading any three-candle gap.** Without a level and a time, it is not what he trades.
- **Assuming a single time-frame ladder.** In August 2024 he describes a continuum from weekly through 4H, 3H, 2H, 1H, 30m, 15m, down through 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 minute to sub-minute charts ([2024 #11 · 2:53:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10418)). In September he restricts it to the eight standard frames above. State both and decide for yourself. Chapter 18 returns to this.
- **Expecting everyone to see your tick.** He calls futures a gentleman's market because everyone sees the same tick levels, while forex prices are broker-specific ([2026 #129 · 51:00](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3060)). He did not discuss CFDs, but a CFD feed is also broker-specific, so expect your levels to differ slightly from a futures chart.

### How the teaching changed

- **2024.** The emphasis is the price delivery continuum: higher-time-frame arrays respected by lower ones, and time as the most important factor.
- **2026.** The emphasis moves to pre-marked levels, tethering after 9:00, no man's land and the chain of custody, all from the 2026 lectures.

2. You are bearish. After a big drop, a clean bearish FVG forms right at the low of the day's cash-session range. Do you sell it? Give ICT's reasoning.
3. A bearish swing has a high wick at 120 and its highest body at 116. Its low wick is at 96 and its lowest body at 100. Where is the OTE band if you anchor on bodies, and why not on wicks?

7. A leg runs 140 down to 100. Where are −1 and −1.5 below it, and where does he aim?
8. Months ago price took out the low of a range. It now retraces to the midpoint of the new range, and a trader buys because it is still deep discount of the old range. What is wrong?

## Answers

1. Equilibrium is (30,055.75 + 29,886) / 2 = 29,970.875, about 29,970. The first objective is equilibrium and the second the lower pool at 29,886. Reread *Premium, discount and equilibrium*, worked example.
2. No. Selling the low of the range goes against his first-month lesson, a short from deep discount must accelerate, and a gap that lingers there is a failing array; price tends to return to the old low. Reread *Premium, discount and equilibrium*, how to trade it.
3. 100 + 0.62 × 16 = 109.92 up to 100 + 0.79 × 16 = 112.64. Wicks would give 110.88 to 114.96. He anchors on bodies because wicks are stop runs. Reread *Optimal Trade Entry*, bodies or wicks.
4. The 117 wick pokes into the upper half, which is allowed, and the 112 close keeps the body in the lower half. The 118 close is above the 115 midpoint, so premium sensitivity is lost and the short is weakening. Reread *Premium and discount inside a PD array*.
5. With a target of 140, equilibrium is 120, so 125 is on the expensive side and not allowed. With 160, equilibrium is 130 and 125 is allowed. Moving a target to fit an add looks like form-fitting, so write the Terminus down before you enter. Reread *Inception to Terminus*.
6. The lower one: it is the smaller reference, the more reactive high, and it reduces how much you need to be right. The larger would have been acceptable. Reread *Dealing ranges*, how to trade it.
7. The leg is 40 points, so −1 is 60 and −1.5 is 40. He aims in the gray area between them rather than an exact tick, and values it most where an old low sits. Reread *Standard deviations*.
8. The range reset when the old low was taken. The retrace is at equilibrium or premium of the new range, so it is not a discount buy. Reread *Premium, discount and equilibrium*, mistakes.
9. No. Without a pre-marked level, the right time and agreement with the draw, it is in no man's land and is just a common gap. Reread *The PD array grid*.
