ICT does not treat a gap or a range as one block. He cuts it into levels (the midpoint, the quarter lines and, from 2026, the eighth lines) and then watches whether candle bodies obey them. This chapter teaches those levels, what bodies and wicks should do at them, why a gap that keeps its far half open is a strong signature, and how volume imbalances change where a gap is drawn. After it you should be able to grade any gap or range on your chart and say what price must not do for your idea to stay valid.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch06_d1 | caption: A bullish gap graded into nine levels, with the pullback staying in the entry half (illustrative) | The CE splits the gap into an entry half and a far half, and the far half is the one that must stay open.]]

- **Grading** means putting the Fibonacci tool, or a rectangle with a midline, on a gap or range and marking its high, low, midpoint, quarter lines (quadrants) and, in 2026, eighth lines (octants). He insists it is "grading", never "quarters theory" ([2024 #20 · 6:49](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=409)).
- **Three midpoints, three names.** Consequent encroachment (CE) halves a gap or wick, the mean threshold halves an order-block candle, and equilibrium halves a range that has traded both ways.
- **The half rule.** In a bullish gap you want to buy in the upper half and see the lower half left open. In a bearish gap you sell in the lower half and want the upper half open. Wicks may poke through; bodies decide. Fair value gap (FVG) anatomy is Chapter 5.
- **Time inside a gap.** One quick touch is healthy. In July 2026 he gave counts: one or two candles inside, three weakens the idea, five means failure.
- **Volume imbalance (VI).** The space between two candle bodies where the wicks still overlap. Include it when you draw a gap, and always include it on CFDs.
- **Validity in 2026.** A gap matters most when it touches a graded level of a pre-marked range. A gap floating between the levels is lower probability.

## What you are grading: inefficiencies

### What it is

An inefficiency is a stretch of price that was offered in only one direction, so the algorithm is expected to come back and offer it the other way. The fair value gap is the main form (Chapter 5). The volume imbalance, taught below, is a smaller one. ICT says price moves between two kinds of reference: old highs and lows on one side, inefficiencies on the other ([2024 #1 · 52:21](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3141)). Chapters 1 and 3 cover the liquidity side.

The name follows the middle candle. A down-close middle candle makes a sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency (SIBI): it delivered only down, so the gap invites price back up. An up-close middle candle makes a buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency (BISI) ([2024 #2 · 1:03:11](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3791)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that where a price range has no liquidity, price is "deficient". It goes back to patch the hole, then decides whether to continue its current program or reach for liquidity beyond ([2024 #21 · 1:19:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4767)). He says candles do not measure how many contracts traded, and volume is a red herring ([2024 #21 · 1:17:52](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4672)). The evidence is open, high, low and close, with no need for level 2 data or a footprint chart ([2026 #129 · 38:18](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2298)). He calls these gaps "the real support and resistance": price kisses them, turns, and bodies stop at them ([2024 #2 · 1:23:16](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4996)).

### How to spot it

1. Mark old highs, old lows and open gaps on the 15-, 5- and 1-minute charts before the 8:30 data. He says the algorithm "spools" for both between 8:00 and 8:30 ET ([2024 #1 · 57:16](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3436)).
2. Keep the original levels (low, CE, high) after a partial fill, and note what is still unfilled. Tiny slivers matter. He has placed limit orders just beyond a sliver and had them run with no significant drawdown ([2024 #2 · 1:04:49](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3889), [1:05:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3939)).
3. Judge context. Not every three-candle gap is an FVG that matters. The form he values most is the one that does not fill in ([2024 #17 · 21:06](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1266)).
4. Separate a touch from a rebalance. Price rebalances an old gap only when it trades in and then leaves the range. A mere touch is redelivery ([2024 #44 · 32:23](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1943)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Marking everything.** He admits he does not keep all the higher-time-frame arrays on his own chart, because it disorients him. He draws them for teaching, and prefers numbers and a few lines ([2024 #37 · 14:23](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=863), [2024 #26 · 3:02](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=182)).
- **Treating "invites price back" as "price must return now".** The gap invites a return, but not necessarily now or this session ([2024 #2 · 1:03:11](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3791)).

## Consequent encroachment, mean threshold and equilibrium

### What it is

Consequent encroachment (CE) is the midpoint of a gap or inefficiency. ICT uses it two ways: as an entry level and as a gauge of strength or weakness ([2024 #3 · 31:04](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1864)). He applies the same word to the midpoint of a wick ([2026 #114 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815)) and of an opening range gap ([2026 #5 · 7:30](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=450)).

He keeps three words for three kinds of midpoint.

| Name | What it halves | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Consequent encroachment (CE) | A gap, inefficiency or wick | [2024 #3 · 31:04](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1864), [2024 #44 · 3:01](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=181) |
| Mean threshold | An order-block candle; in 2026 also a breaker (Chapter 9) | [2024 #44 · 3:01](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=181), [2024 #7 · 3:42:24](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=13344), [2026 #34 · 15:03](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=903) |
| Equilibrium | A range that has been delivered both up and down (Chapter 4) | [2024 #3 · 31:04](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1864) |

A gap is not a range, he says. Nothing traded inside it (his example was the gap between the 5 PM stop and the 6 PM restart), so it has no equilibrium. The arithmetic is the same for all three. The names tell you what kind of structure you are halving. CE is always one precise price, not a zone ([2026 #114 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation has three parts.

- **The overshoot is the spread.** When price trades into a gap up to the CE "plus a little bit more", the small bump past the level is the algorithm giving the spread to anyone who used that level, so they get filled. He says it is not buying pressure ([2024 #2 · 1:10:29](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4229)). Perfect delivery to a CE therefore trades one tick beyond it: to buy at 20,568 on NQ, price must trade at least to 20,567.75 ([2024 #44 · 19:32](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1172)).
- **The midpoint is the threshold between bullish and bearish order flow.** In 2026 he reads bodies in the upper half of an array as bullish and bodies in the lower half as bearish. Wicks may exceed the CE; bodies stopping short of it show pressure the other way ([2026 #23 · 16:03](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=963)).
- **A gap should not be fully used.** If price wants to go on in your direction, it will not need the far boundary. This idea is developed in the section on reading price at the levels.

### How to spot it

1. Draw the gap from its high to its low, including any volume imbalance (see the drawing section below).
2. Put a line at 50%. A Fibonacci at 0.5 and a rectangle with a midline give the same level ([2024 #28 · 26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585)).
3. Keep the original CE after a partial fill ([2024 #2 · 1:05:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3939)).
4. On a wick, halve the wick's own extremes and treat that price the same way ([2026 #114 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815)).

### How to trade it

The CE does four jobs in his teaching.

- **Gauge.** Can price reach it, and what do bodies do there? The section on reading price at the levels covers this.
- **Entry.** A realistic hypothetical short in a bearish FVG is at the CE or one tick below it, never "the exact high". The body should stay below the gap's high, with wicks allowed above ([2026 #10 · 17:38](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1058)). He has entered "exactly at the CE" of a 1-minute gap after a daily inversion FVG was tagged ([2026 #40 · 5:01](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=301)). For a short he rests a limit just above the CE. He says to let it come to you, because chasing price lower is needless drawdown ([2026 #70 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=48)).
- **Stop reference.** The CE of a prior array or wick is the line that must hold. Longs go just below it and shorts just above. If a closing body violates it, the idea is wrong ([2026 #85 · 0:53](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=53), [2026 #90 · 5:38](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=338)). In one April 2026 MNQ short he trailed the stop down to just above each new CE ([2026 #90 · 20:51](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=1251)). On a bullish FVG entry he used the CE as the stop ([2026 #101 · 36:33](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=2193)). Stops in general are Chapter 19.
- **Target.** For a daily array he says the CE is always a draw ([2026 #127 · 56:22](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3382)). On 6 September 2024 a market maker sell model delivered to the CE of the 13 August daily FVG, and he had flagged that level in advance ([2024 #23 · 1:38](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=98)).

### Worked example

NQ, 1-minute chart, Tuesday 15 October 2024 ([2024 #44](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=181)). Levels are as quoted in the notes.

[[DIAGRAM ch06_d4 | caption: The daily SIBI of 17 July 2024 graded in quarters, with the 15 October sweep to one tick under the CE (levels as quoted, candles schematic) | A buyer at the CE needs price to trade through it, so the low is a tick beyond the line, not on it.]]

1. His objective since the mentorship began was to draw up into the daily SIBI from 17 July 2024, a single down-close daily candle. He graded it with dotted lines: low 20,410, lower quadrant 20,489, CE 20,568 and upper quadrant about 20,646.75 ([2024 #44 · 3:01](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=181)). The high is not quoted.
2. At about 5:08, inside the 4:50 to 5:10 window, price swept down to 20,567.75, one tick under the CE, and reversed ([2024 #44 · 17:53](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1073)).
3. His explanation: a smart-money buyer at the CE has to be filled, so price must trade at least one tick through it ([2024 #44 · 19:32](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1172)).
4. Later price swept relative equal highs and reached the upper quadrant, with Monday's high above as buy-side liquidity ([2024 #44 · 3:01](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=181)).

He taught the one-tick overshoot on NQ, where a tick is 0.25. The notes do not translate it to gold. Broker feeds differ ([2024 #32 · 1:06:05](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3965)), so on a CFD expect the extreme to land near the level rather than on the exact tick.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Calling a gap's midpoint "equilibrium".** He corrects this. Equilibrium belongs to a delivered range ([2024 #3 · 31:04](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1864)).
- **Treating the CE as a wall.** Wicks pass through it. What counts is whether bodies can reach it and stay beyond it.
- **Expecting the exact tick.** Delivery is one tick through, as above.
- **A conflicting definition in three notes.** The notes for [2026 #83 · 23:54](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1434), [2026 #85 · 0:53](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=53) and [2026 #89 · 8:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=518) record CE as the extreme of the first body that enters the gap. Every other lecture, including later 2026 ones ([2026 #114](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815), [2026 #122 · 6:13](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=373)), halves the range. This looks like a note-taking slip; use the 50% line.

### How the teaching changed

- **2024.** CE is introduced on 7 August as the gap's midpoint, an entry level and a gauge ([2024 #3 · 31:04](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1864)). On 16 September he calls it his most important level once the gap is drawn with its volume imbalance ([2024 #28 · 26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585)).
- **2026.** It spreads to wicks, daily wicks and opening gaps, and failure to touch it is called immediate feedback ([2026 #114 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815)). The wording gets looser. The notes for [2026 #115](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=1100) title it "Midpoint / Equilibrium", and [2026 #117 · 8:49](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=529) defines it as the midpoint between any two price levels, such as a low and a lower low.

### Watch it

- [2024 #44 · 17:53](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1073) — the one-tick sweep of a daily CE.

## Quadrants: grading a gap in quarters

### What it is

To grade a gap, put the Fibonacci tool across it so the lines sit at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. That cuts it into four equal parts ([2024 #3 · 33:25](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2005)). In his speech a "quadrant" is the 25% or 75% line: the upper quadrant, the CE and the lower quadrant ([2024 #27 · 4:17](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=257)). He also uses the word loosely for the quarter of the gap beside the line, as when he calls the upper quadrant the top 25% ([2026 #80 · 3:56](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=236)).

He rejects the label "quarters theory" or "quarterly theory". The practice is grading an inefficiency or a price swing, and he says it was already in the 2017 mentorship core content ([2024 #20 · 6:49](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=409), [2024 #48 · 1:07:01](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4021), [2026 #138 · 22:33](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1353)). Anything with a high and a low can be graded: a gap, a wick, an opening gap, a time-defined range. This section covers gaps. Ranges follow later in the chapter.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that delivery is scripted, so its reference points are exact. Price respects the upper and lower quadrants to the tick ([2024 #3 · 33:25](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2005)). He says grading removes the doubt about which levels to use as support and resistance ([2026 #49 · 16:14](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=974)).

The second part of his logic is that a fully used gap has done its job and says nothing about what comes next. If price wants to go higher, it will not need the low boundary of a bullish gap ([2024 #27 · 54:45](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=3285)). When an inefficiency is bullish, he does not expect the candle that made it to be overlapped completely. He expects sensitivity in the upper half ([2024 #27 · 6:07](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=367)).

### How to spot it

1. Draw the gap's high and low, including any volume imbalance.
2. Set the Fibonacci levels to 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. From 2026 he also adds the eighth lines (next section).
3. Name the entry half. In a bullish gap it runs from candle 3's low down to the CE. In a bearish gap it runs from candle 3's high up to the CE ([2024 #28 · 26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585)).
4. Name the far half, the one that should stay open.
5. After a partial fill, keep the original lines and note what remains.

### How to trade it

- **Buy the upper half, sell the lower half.** The ideal bullish gap sees price drop only into the upper half and leave the lower half alone. He calls that gap extremely bullish and says you should not worry about the stop. The bearish mirror sells in the lower half with the upper half left open, and he says that gives a very strong probability you are right ([2024 #28 · 26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585), [43:27](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2607)).
- **Spend little time in the entry half.** Ideally one candle enters it and at most two, and then the run starts. If candle 4 drops into the gap right after it forms and runs at once, the other half will probably stay open and the move is on a fast track ([2024 #28 · 53:58](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3238)).
- **Wicks may poke through the far side; bodies must not close beyond it** ([2024 #28 · 26:25](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1585)).
- **Size down beyond the CE.** Anything past the midpoint is a weaker location, so he uses one or two contracts there ([2024 #28 · 53:58](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3238)).
- **Scaling, as a demonstration only.** For a bearish gap he sells 6 contracts at candle 3's high minus a tick, adds 4 at the lower quadrant, and adds about 2 more at the CE or in the upper half while bodies stay inside candle 1's low. The bullish mirror adds at the midpoint, the lower quadrant and the gap low, because the stop sits below candle 2's low. The counts illustrate what he does. He repeats that learners should not pyramid and should trade one contract ([2024 #28 · 29:35](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1775), [35:16](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2116)).
- **Hourly gaps tolerate more.** On 12 September 2024 he graded an hourly BISI at 25, 50 and 75. His levels of interest were the CE and the lower quadrant, one contract at each, with the stop at least 2 to 3 ticks below the gap's low because price can still touch it. He did not want to buy merely because price was cheap. He wanted a little more information first, though not full confirmation. Where a prop firm forbids adding to a loser, he says to wait for the signature in the section on reading price at the levels instead of scaling ([2024 #27 · 54:45](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=3285), [1:02:46](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=3766)).
- **Exit at the easiest quadrant.** For targets he finds the relative equal highs first, asks how far beyond them price can reach, then looks for an opening gap there. If it is in reach he grades it and uses its lowest quadrant, the easiest barrier to profit from ([2024 #11 · 3:08:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11330)). A limit exit needs price to trade one tick through, so he sets it one tick beyond where he wants price to reach ([2024 #11 · 3:12:01](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11521)). Chapter 19 covers targets.

### Worked example

NQ, Thursday 12 September 2024 ([2024 #27](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=257)). The notes give no clean prices.

1. An hourly BISI and a 5-minute BISI sit below the opening range gap. He drops a 25/50/75 Fibonacci on the hourly one ([2024 #27 · 4:17](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=257)).
2. Price opens and drops into the upper half of the 5-minute BISI, which shows sensitivity. It rallies to the opening range gap high, then drops again into the lower quadrant of the hourly BISI, sweeping the initial low of the day, and rallies. Bodies respect the lower quadrant ([2024 #27 · 54:45](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=3285)).
3. He explains why that swing low mattered. It formed at the upper quadrant of the hourly BISI, so the liquidity beneath it was the interest ([2024 #27 · 39:29](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=2369)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Buying the deepest line by default.** A resting limit at the CE or lower often misses the trade. See the table below.
- **Reading "quadrant" as a zone.** It is a line, though he sometimes means the quarter beside it. Check which he means from the chart.
- **Applying the 1-minute rule to an hourly gap.** The hourly gap above allowed a dip to the lower quadrant, but not through the gap's low.

## Octants and gradient levels

### What it is

An octant is half a quadrant: 12.5% of the range. The Fibonacci tool is set to eight equal parts, so a graded array has nine lines ([2026 #19 · 27:57](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1677)). In 2026 he calls the whole set of graded lines gradient levels. He says he first used the word to avoid confusion when talking about quadrants and eighths, and in one February lecture he adds sixteenths ([2026 #133 · 1:41](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=101)).

Read from the top of a daily gap, the lines are: the high, the upper octant, the upper quadrant, the octant above the CE, the CE, the octant below the CE, the lower quadrant, the lowest octant and the low ([2026 #21 · 7:32](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=452), [2026 #49 · 15:28](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=928)). In numbers they are 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.625, 0.75, 0.875 and 1.0 ([2026 #120 · 24:03](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1443)). He singles out the 0.375 and 0.625 lines, and he also names the 0.875 octant ([2026 #112 · 21:44](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=1304), [2026 #111 · 8:40](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=520), [2026 #30 · 29:28](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1768)).

The numbers depend on where you anchor the Fibonacci. In his daily-gap examples the 0.125 line is the first below the top. In his February SIBI example the numbers count up from the gap low (0.5, 0.625, 0.75). The notes for two July 2026 lectures also record him using "octant" for the quarter lines ([2026 #54 · 14:45](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=885), [2026 #58 · 14:28](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=868)). Name each level by its position and you will not get lost. Opening gaps and ranges are graded the same way, and wicks too ([2026 #133 · 5:39](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=339)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the market uses these levels because delivery is algorithmic and scripted ([2026 #17 · 24:44](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1484)). The levels exist before price reaches them, so you can write them down ahead of time, for example at 10:30 for the first-hour range ([2026 #113 · 1:22:24](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4944)). A run that does not trade to an octant or quadrant is, to him, manipulation ([2026 #113 · 1:20:49](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4849)). His summary of the whole idea: "Everything has to be anchored to these gradient levels. Otherwise, you're guessing." ([2026 #120 · 24:03](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1443))

### How to spot it

1. Settle your bias and the draw on liquidity first. Grading never replaces them ([2026 #47 · 3:13](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=193)).
2. Grade the array or range you are interacting with: the Fibonacci across the whole inefficiency, with octants, quadrants and the 50% line. Include its volume imbalance. His daily BISI in the late-August examples runs from one candle's close up to the low of the candle that created it, volume imbalance at the low included ([2026 #21 · 0:49](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=49)).
3. To read an anchored Fibonacci, find its leftmost anchor point and look straight down. All the levels line up there ([2026 #21 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1419)).
4. Look for PD arrays whose key candle touches a graded level. The lectures name different candles. Candle 2 touches an octant ([2026 #44 · 48:08](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=2888)); candle 3 is tethered to one ([2026 #26 · 13:49](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=829)); or the gap simply lies across a level ([2026 #131 · 30:33](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1833)).
5. Keep the ones that agree with the time of day ([2026 #47 · 3:13](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=193)).

### How to trade it

- **Use the level as a validity filter.** A PD array is valid when it is anchored to an octant or quadrant of the range you are grading, and just looking like a gap or order block is not enough ([2026 #44 · 16:07](https://youtu.be/4SJlJdIANCo?t=967)). A gap sitting randomly between octants has less significance ([2026 #43 · 13:37](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=817)). Not all gaps are created equal ([2026 #131 · 32:08](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1928)). The wording runs from "valid" to "high probability", and the notes do not say whether an untethered gap is invalid or merely weaker ([2026 #26 · 13:49](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=829)). Chapter 4 gives the same tethering rule for the 7:00 to 9:00 range.
- **Run the body test.** For a short, bodies must close below the target octant to confirm continuation, and failure there means reversal ([2026 #16 · 10:06](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=606)). If price reaches a lower quadrant but cannot reach the octant above it, he reads weakness ([2026 #68 · 0:49](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=49)). Rallies whose bodies cannot even touch the upper octant are the same idea ([2026 #54 · 14:45](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=885)).
- **Take partials at the levels.** He teaches students to "run down equity": as price approaches the lower graded levels, take a partial at each one (lowest octant, lowest quadrant, octant below the CE, the CE). Treat each level like sell-side liquidity. Let the last piece ride to the sweep of the low ([2026 #48 · 11:34](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=694), [12:22](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=742)). He adds that you never get all of a move ([2026 #48 · 13:13](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=793)). Chapter 19 covers partials.
- **Add inside the zone.** On 15 July 2026 he built a position between the lower quadrant and the upper octant of the new week opening gap, adding at several levels and treating the zone as a premium array ([2026 #60 · 13:46](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=826)).
- **Stand aside in a narrow range between levels.** When price spends a long time between octants and quadrants, he sits out. A retail-looking bull flag in New York lunch failed that way ([2026 #48 · 14:02](https://youtu.be/XKjdVwBUs2E?t=842)).

## Reading price at the levels: bodies, halves and time

Grading draws the ruler. This section is about what price should do against it. Chapter 4 teaches the half rule as premium and discount sensitivity, and Chapter 5 gives the basic clock for time inside a gap. Here the tests are collected, with the extra ones the CE, quadrant and octant lines add.

### What it is

Every test judges bodies, not wicks. A wick is a probe that may pass a level. ICT's phrase for a wick one tick above a daily gap was "colouring outside the lines" ([2026 #127 · 47:42](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2862)). The evidence is open, high, low and close in time, with no need for level 2 data, footprint or volume profile ([2026 #129 · 38:18](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2298)).

He asks four things of a graded array. Where are the bodies? How far did the wicks go? How long is price staying? How fast is it moving?

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that a market going somewhere does not need the whole array. A bullish market should leave inefficiencies open, so no unfinished business sits below ([2026 #32 · 20:32](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1232)). If price cannot even trade down to a bullish gap's CE, that is very bullish, especially when no bodies were buried below it. His word for the pairing of orders at a price is "glad-handing" ([2026 #32 · 19:43](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1183)).

He also says the algorithm cannot hide. Bodies that cannot touch a premium level show it in the delivery ([2026 #28 · 7:11](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=431)). These are his claims, not tested statistics.

### How to spot it

Run the tests in this order.

1. **Where are the bodies?** Mark the CE. A bearish array keeps bodies below it and a bullish array keeps them above it ([2026 #23 · 16:03](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=963)).
2. **How far did the wicks go?** Wicks may pass the CE and more. Weakness shows in bodies stopping short of the next line, as in the example below.
3. **Can price reach the CE at all?** In 2024 a bearish rally that fails even to touch a gap's CE was "decidedly weak". The next bearish order block or 15-second FVG entry would probably be a winner, heading for the inefficiency below, a single low or relative equal lows ([2024 #5 · 16:37](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=997), [17:23](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1043)). In March 2026 a rally that could not reach the midpoint of a daily wick was distribution, not a bull flag. One failure is interesting and repeated failure confirms it ([2026 #114 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=815), [22:01](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1321)).
4. **How many candles are inside?** He counts candles only inside inefficiencies. A market in a hurry enters with one candle, leaves no body there and does not reach the CE. One or two is ideal, three shifts the odds lower, and the fifth candle is the last ditch ([2026 #64 · 41:53](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2513), [43:27](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2607), [44:14](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2654)). He denies using the retail 13th, 17th or 21st candle counts.
5. **How many visits?** After price first reaches a gap's CE it should leave. A second trip before the target is a failure sign ([2024 #33 · 48:34](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=2914)). In a clean market price dips a toe in and repels. Repeated touches weaken a gap, as they weaken a trend line ([2024 #36 · 1:34:43](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=5683)).
6. **What happens at the far boundary?** In a sell program on a bearish gap, the upper half stays open. Wicks may poke above and bodies stay under ([2024 #6 · 1:14:27](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4467), [1:17:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4629)). If price reaches the top, he needs an immediate rejection and a displacement ([2024 #6 · 1:18:58](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4738)).
7. **How fast is the move?** A slow, lethargic move toward a target means you may be wrong. A fast, direct one raises conviction. The notes call time the central tenet of that May 2026 review ([2026 #83 · 23:54](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=1434)).

### How to trade it

- **Hold or add while bodies respect the level.** In May 2026 he treated repeated failures to bring bodies back to the CE as permission to stay short and add. In his example the move was already done, so he took what was there and left ([2026 #83 · 37:20](https://youtu.be/qf5qokimwO4?t=2240)). Beginners stay at one contract (see the scaling note under quadrants).
- **Cut when a body crosses the line.** A short is wrong if a body closes above the CE of the wick or gap it relies on. He exits half or all, depending on the stop ([2026 #93 · 12:08](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=728)). If bodies enter the upper half of a SIBI you are short, the down move is losing conviction, so reduce or exit ([2026 #94 · 7:02](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=422)).
- **Skip the second visit.** On 23 September 2024 his short from the first FVG failed on a second tag of the CE. He had said it would probably fail because price spent too long there, and he told viewers to journal the pattern ([2024 #33 · 59:29](https://youtu.be/Qg3O1GkJDeQ?t=3569)).
- **Use the CE as a pivot.** On 3 February 2026 he stayed bearish as long as NQ could not hold above the midpoint of a daily suspension block. Price rallied to it on the 1-minute chart, hesitated and reversed. It is a decision point, not an entry ([2026 #133 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=290)).
- **Read higher-timeframe arrays by the bodies.** Price that stalls at an hourly BISI's CE for hours is meeting resistance, and he says to wait for a clear break ([2026 #52 · 11:10](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=670), [11:57](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=717)). Wicks into a daily or weekly array with no bodies in its upper half are a rejection, stronger than on lower time frames ([2026 #111 · 1:46:18](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=6378)).
- **On the long side, watch the upper half.** When price returns into a BISI acting as support, the upper half gives the strongest buying. A small portion left open means the market is unwilling to go lower. A close below the gap's low is the last line of defence ([2026 #40 · 34:31](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2071), [35:17](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2117), [32:55](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1975)).
- **If price cannot fill the half and runs,** ICT calls it a breakaway gap and expects fast, large range expansion ([2024 #3 · 33:25](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2005)). Chapter 8 covers it.

### Worked example

A bearish gap, lecture of 23 February 2026 ([2026 #129 · 36:42](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2202)). The notes give neither the instrument nor prices, so the picture is illustrative. Levels count up from the gap low, the edge price enters, as in his example.

[[DIAGRAM ch06_d2 | caption: A bearish gap graded in eighths (illustrative): wicks cross the CE, bodies stay below it, and the second rally prints a lower high | Wicks may pass the CE; what counts is that bodies never get back to it and the second try cannot even touch it.]]

1. A sharp drop leaves a gap. He does not want it filled completely. He wants a small portion left open, and bodies that cannot get back to the CE.
2. Price rallies. Wicks pass 0.5 and 0.625 but fail to reach the 0.75 upper quadrant, and bodies stay below the CE. He reads weakness and expects it to work lower. He still passes on a tiny-stop short, because volatility is exorbitant.
3. A second attempt does not even reach the CE, so it prints a lower high. His reading: "nobody is allowed to lay down candles in the upper half" ([2026 #129 · 37:31](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2251)).
4. Candle size and count agree. Larger candles down, more candles down than up and retracements of one to three candles are bearish order flow. If bullish, the same applies in reverse ([2026 #129 · 55:46](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3346)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating a wick through the CE as a break.** Only a body closing beyond it counts.
- **Reading the half rule as a promise.** In 2024 he admitted the inefficiency sometimes closes entirely and price still goes lower, though he dislikes that in a trade ([2024 #6 · 1:17:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4629)). In March 2026 he added that a SIBI left open is conditional on the market's intent. People who said he was wrong because the candle did not stay open missed the condition ([2026 #122 · 6:13](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=373)).
- **Using the count on every time frame.** The notes give the one-to-five counts for an intraday gap. They do not scale them to hourly or daily arrays.

### How the teaching changed

The tests arrived in layers. In August to October 2024 came the upper half open, the CE visited once, the toe in the water, and lower-half-only respect on a SIBI on 3 October ([2024 #41 · 6:49](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=409)). In 2026 they became body-versus-CE tests, glad-handing, and in July the explicit candle counts.

## Volume imbalances: the body gap inside a gap

### What it is

A volume imbalance (VI) is a pair of adjacent candles whose bodies do not touch or overlap, while their wicks still do. You measure from one candle's open or close to the next candle's body. Up-close or down-close makes no difference ([2024 #16 · 9:11](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=551)). One tick is enough. In March 2026 a close of 24,828.50 followed by an open of 24,828.75 counted ([2026 #120 · 26:28](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1588)).

A candle with no body, where open equals close, is ignored. Measure between the bodies on either side of it ([2024 #38 · 59:13](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3553)). With two broad bodies and a small candle between them, the imbalance is the range between the broad bodies, not the small candle's body ([2024 #26 · 52:39](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=3159)). He calls such price porous and "in need of patchwork": price comes back to redeliver, and algorithms sell into it, aiming for the lows below ([2024 #26 · 47:02](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2822)).

Three things get confused. This table separates them.

| Name | What separates the candles | Wicks | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume imbalance | The bodies | Still overlap | [2024 #16 · 9:11](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=551) |
| Fair value gap | Candle 1's extreme and candle 3's extreme, with candle 2 between | Do not overlap across the three candles | [2024 #28 · 17:01](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1021) |
| True liquidity void ("real common gap") | Every price between one candle's high and another's low | Nothing traded there | [2024 #38 · 1:00:48](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3648) |

Only the last is a liquidity void by definition. A gap can carry a VI at one end or both, and when it does he says to note it ([2026 #120 · 26:28](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=1588)). Chapter 5 teaches the FVG. Chapter 8 covers the vacuum block and the regular-hours void.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the algorithm references candle bodies. On 1 October 2024, about 8:10 PM ET in the Asian session, a body on the NQ 1-minute chart stopped at 20,229.5 and the very next candle's low was 20,229.5 "to the tick". The wick did the damage while the bodies rested on the box ([2024 #38 · 1:05:41](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3941)). An area delivered down and back up has offered both sides, so it is balanced. This is the paint roller again ([2024 #38 · 1:01:37](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=3697)).

He describes it two ways that fit together. It is the "least rigid" array: price is expected to trade through it and come back, not respect it hard ([2024 #43 · 55:54](https://youtu.be/DzJpkxI057g?t=3354)). It is also his most flexible: it shows whether price will sustain a run, and it serves as an entry, a partial or a stop reference ([2024 #44 · 1:13:55](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4435)). Candles never show how many contracts traded ([2024 #21 · 1:17:52](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4672)), so "volume" here is a name, not a measurement.

### How to draw it

[[DIAGRAM ch06_d3 | caption: Including the volume imbalance in a bullish gap (illustrative) | The VI is the body gap between candle 1's close and candle 2's open, and including it drops the gap's CE from 106 to 104.]]

1. **Draw the gap the classic way.** For a bullish gap, candle 1's high to candle 3's low (Chapter 5).
2. **Check the bodies at the gap's far end.** Is candle 2's open above candle 1's close? Then there is a VI. Anchor the box's bottom at candle 1's close, not its body high or wick. His example: a close of 25,165.75, a next open of 25,166.00 and a candle low of 25,177.50 gave a box from 25,165.75 to 25,177.50 ([2026 #121 · 3:17](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=197), [4:05](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=245)). That box is 11.75 points tall, with a midpoint of 25,171.625 by my arithmetic.
3. **Extend the box through the VI.** His 2024 rule: "You can't just use the wick... that's not valid" ([2024 #28 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1440)). The CE then comes from the full box, and in a bullish gap it sits lower than the wick-only CE.
4. **Know the two measurements.** The shallow edge is candle 2's open and the deep edge is candle 1's close. He taught wicks first and added the VI later, so as not to overload students ([2026 #72 · 10:37](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=637), [11:25](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=685)).
5. **Indecisive neighbours.** When small-body candles sit beside the gap, take the extreme open or close among them and extend the box over the whole group before taking the CE. The naive drawing is not wrong, he said, only less accurate ([2024 #46 · 21:13](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1273)).
6. **Box stacks as one.** Two VIs one candle apart are boxed as a single array ([2024 #37 · 16:09](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=969)). So are two SIBIs joined by a VI ([2026 #136 · 10:33](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=633)).
7. **On CFDs, always include the VI.** Broker pricing varies, and without it you lack a true depiction of the zone. On futures he sometimes strips it out to focus on wicks ([2024 #32 · 1:06:05](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3965)). The notes show him marking VIs on a gold daily chart in 2026 ([2026 #125 · 42:00](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2520)).

Chart settings change what you see. He said platforms offer one setting that shows VIs and another that shows an inefficiency, and he uses the VI one ([2026 #118 · 8:53](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=533)). On a gold daily chart, switching an FVG from the standard display to the VI display moved the levels, and he preferred the VI view because it shows where bodies are ([2026 #125 · 42:48](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2568)). The notes call it a TradingView display setting but name no indicator, so check what your own tool draws against rule 2. Contract-roll and settlement toggles also move VIs on futures charts ([2026 #126 · 1:02:22](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3742)). The notes do not say whether they apply to a gold CFD.

### How to trade it

- **As an entry.** Enter as price trades down into the VI while the candle is still going against you, not after it turns. Add if price is inside the VI and runs. Keep a stop in every execution ([2024 #44 · 1:13:55](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4435), [1:16:19](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4579)). He read VIs on a 30-second chart: the 15-second chart is spotty and the 5-second offers too many ([2024 #44 · 1:21:03](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4863)).
- **Bullish order flow keeps it open.** Price should only dip a toe into the VI and move on ([2026 #18 · 48:57](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=2937)). After the 8:30 run, a VI inside a BISI should hold, and each small down-close candle should support price until the next level is taken. If that fails below the candle's low, he stands aside until 9:30 ([2024 #4 · 39:28](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2368), [40:19](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2419)).
- **Short into a VI above.** On 9 August 2024 at 7:58 AM, price hit the initial sell-side reference and dropped. ## Grading ranges, wicks and blocks

Anything with a high and a low can be graded. Chapter 4 chooses the dealing range. This section applies the grid to objects the gap sections left out: time-defined ranges, wicks and blocks.

### The overnight range

- **Definition.** The highest high and lowest low between midnight and 5:00 a.m. New York time. Count the candles from 12:01 to 4:59 and leave out the midnight and 5:00 candles. The chart must be set to New York time ([2024 #44 · 8:58](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=538)).
- **Grading.** Fibonacci levels 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. The 0.5 is equilibrium, and the 0.25 and 0.75 are the lower and upper quadrants. He draws them dashed, to tell them from the dotted quadrants of the daily SIBI. The aim is to predict, in close proximity, where PD arrays will form ([2024 #44 · 10:37](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=637)).
- **When.** Only when the overnight is part of a larger consolidation inside the prior day's range. If Asia ran one way and kept going, skip it ([2024 #44 · 12:12](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=732)). He said it works on forex as well as indices.
- **Use.** Get a higher-timeframe bias first. If bullish, look for arrays forming below the graded levels. Below equilibrium is discount and below the lower quadrant is better. He called every bullish FVG below these levels high probability. If bearish, look just above them ([2024 #44 · 1:00:12](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=3612)). Do the work before the 6:00 a.m. pre-market and act inside the macros ([2024 #44 · 36:07](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=2167)).
- **The upper half.** On a bullish day he did not expect pullbacks to the low. The upper quadrant had less precision, because price tends to rip through the range high.
- **If bias flips,** everything inverts: bullish FVGs below become inversion FVGs and bullish order blocks become bearish ones ([2024 #44 · 1:04:11](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=3851)). Arrays that stop respecting price are manual intervention, and he sits on his hands (Chapter 14). Partials use the −0.5 and then −1 extensions ([2024 #44 · 1:05:00](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=3900), Chapter 4).

[[DIAGRAM ch06_d5 | caption: The overnight range, 12:01 to 4:59 ET, graded into quadrants, with a bullish FVG forming below the lower quadrant (illustrative) | On a bullish day the arrays worth trading form below the graded levels, and the upper half is less precise.]]

It is not a signal on its own. Bias comes first, time (the macro) second and the array's location third.

### Worked example

NQ 1-minute, Tuesday 15 October 2024 ([2024 #44](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=538)). The notes give no overnight prices.

1. Bias is bullish: an election year and a daily SIBI above. He grades the 12:01 to 4:59 range.
2. Price makes the overnight low into about 4:30 and turns up. At about 5:08 it sweeps down to the daily SIBI's CE and reverses (see the CE worked example).
3. The rally takes out relative equal highs, then drops and leaves an FVG below the overnight equilibrium, inside the 5:50 to 6:10 macro.
4. Price redelivers into it, runs to the overnight equilibrium, drops to the lower quadrant, taps the FVG, then runs to the overnight upper quadrant, where buy stops sit above the range high ([2024 #44 · 22:49](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1369)).
5. At about 8:30 price spools up with less precise upper-half action. It then consolidates below Monday's high, a buy-side level.

### Dealing ranges: where the array must sit

On 25 October 2024 he graded a swing from the high of a retracement leg to a pre-market low, drawn from candle bodies. He ignored a stop-run wick as "a little bump" ([2024 #48 · 1:05:25](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3925)). Equilibrium is the cut. The best long entries are at or below it, and adds stop there ([2024 #48 · 1:07:49](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4069)). A bullish order block or FVG had to sit at or below the lower quadrant, or he would not use it ([2024 #48 · 1:11:50](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4310)).

He softened that once. A later FVG whose CE overlapped the range's equilibrium, a convergence of two midpoints, was tradable with a limit at the CE. The notes treat it as a secondary or add entry ([2024 #48 · 1:20:40](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4840), [1:21:29](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=4889)). The 2022 version is about targets, not entries. On an ES hourly chart he skipped an FVG overlapping the 50% level and wanted one fully in the premium as the draw, with bodies staying inside it ([2022 Ep 40 · 31:59](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1919), [32:46](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1966)).

### Wicks, candles and blocks

- **A wick.** Halve it between its own extremes. Failing to reach a daily wick's midpoint was distribution, and a re-entry into an iFVG that trades to the wick's CE is a premium entry ([2026 #114 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1771)). In February he graded two daily discount wicks with gradient levels, and price on the 1-minute traded into them ([2026 #133 · 5:39](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=339)).
- **A candle.** On 13 August 2024 he used the mean threshold of an up-closed daily candle, about 19,255 as captioned, as the next reference beyond a daily VI ([2024 #7 · 3:42:24](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=13344)).
- **A bearish breaker.** The mean threshold is the candle's 50% (blocks are Chapter 9). In 2026 he named five fill levels inside a bearish breaker, from its mean threshold down to its low: the mean threshold, the octant just below it, the lower quadrant, the octant above the low, and the low. In numbers from the low these are about 0.5, 0.375, 0.25, 0.125 and 0, which is the note-taker's reading. Any can carry a limit order. The midpoint is the best seat and lower levels are for scaling, with the stop at the displacement candle's high. He rejects "zones" and the shotgun approach ([2026 #34 · 15:03](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=903), [15:51](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=951), [16:38](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=998)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Grading a one-way overnight, counting the boundary candles, or charting in the wrong time zone.**
- **Anchoring on a stop-run wick.** Use bodies, as he did on 25 October 2024.
- **Treating the grid as a signal.** Bias, time and location all have to agree.
- **Copying the clock to gold.** The notes give the overnight grid for forex as well as indices. The 9:30 to 10:30 and 7:00 to 9:00 windows come from index futures, and the notes do not map them to gold.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the notes show only a range's equilibrium: a premium FVG was the draw, and an FVG overlapping the 50% was skipped. In October 2024 came the overnight range (15 October) and the lower-quadrant dealing range rule (25 October), both in quarters. In 2026 the windows are graded with octants and carried forward, with first-hour projections and gradient levels between pools.

## Answers

1. The CE is 28. The quadrant lines are 24 and 32. The octant lines are 22, 26, 30 and 34, so the nine lines run 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32, 34, 36. A drop to 31 stays in the upper half, so the lower half is left open. That is the signature he calls extremely bullish. Reread: Quadrants.
2. No. The CE is 208. A wick through it is allowed, and bodies stayed below it. The wick also failed to reach the 212 upper quadrant. A second rally that cannot even touch the CE prints a lower high and reads as weakness. Reread: Reading price at the levels.
3. The first. A market in a hurry enters with one candle and does not reach the CE. Five candles is the last ditch. The notes give the counts only for intraday gaps and do not scale them to hourly or daily arrays. Reread: Reading price at the levels.
4. Yes. The bodies do not touch while the wicks overlap, and one tick counts. With bullish order flow it should stay open and price should at most dip a toe into it. Reread: Volume imbalances.
5. The candles from 12:01 to 4:59 count, and the midnight and 5:00 candles are left out. The chart must be set to New York time. Skip the grid when Asia ran one way and kept going instead of consolidating inside the prior day's range. Reread: Grading ranges, wicks and blocks.
6. It has less significance, because a valid array is anchored to a graded level. The notes do not say whether such a gap is invalid or only weaker. Reread: Octants and gradient levels.
7. Price must trade to 14,999.75, one tick through, because a buyer at the CE has to be filled. Broker feeds differ, so on a CFD the extreme may land near the level and not on the exact tick. The notes do not translate the one-tick rule to gold. Reread: Consequent encroachment.
8. No. He stops keying off a daily VI once a later daily body covers its range. He will not use it for new entries or targets, though he may still use it for stop management. Reread: Volume imbalances.
9. The lower quadrant is 125 and equilibrium is 150. The FVG at 110–122 sits below the lower quadrant, so it qualifies. The FVG at 140–160 has its CE at 150, on the equilibrium, so it is a secondary entry with a limit at the CE. The FVG at 130–140 is above the lower quadrant and its CE is not at equilibrium, so he would not use it. Reread: Grading ranges, wicks and blocks.
10. Treat the counts as a demonstration. He tells beginners to trade one contract and not to pyramid. For the no-adding case he says to wait for the extra information, not to scale. The tests in the reading-price section (bodies respecting the line, a single visit to the CE, a fast move) are the kind of evidence to wait for before one entry. Reread: Quadrants and Reading price at the levels.
