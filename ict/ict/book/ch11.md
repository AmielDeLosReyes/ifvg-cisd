Index futures leave three kinds of price hole: the New Day Opening Gap (NDOG), the New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) and the RTH opening range gap (ORG). ICT treats all three as places the algorithm returns to, uses stacks of them to set a bias, and teaches the ORG midpoint as his most repeated first target of the morning. After this chapter you can mark each gap on a 1-minute chart, grade it, apply the cluster filter and the mid-gap model, and recognise Event Horizon, the 2026 midpoint tool built on the same arithmetic.

## At a glance

- **NDOG.** On the 1-minute chart, the close of the 4:59 PM ET candle against the open of the first 6:00 PM candle. Draw the high, the low and the midpoint (CE) as rays, date it for the trading day it serves, and keep about five ([2024 #3 · 18:58](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1138)).
- **NWOG.** Friday's 4:59 PM close against Sunday's 6:00 PM open. There is one per week, keep about five, and ICT says it carries more weight for swing trades ([2024 #3 · 45:19](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2719)).
- **A filled gap is not finished.** Do not discard a gap because price traded through it. The algorithm keeps referring back to its high, low and quadrants ([2024 #13 · 10:43](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=643)).
- **Clusters set bias.** At 8:30, count the recent NDOGs and the NWOG above and below price. Price tends to press toward the side that holds more of them; ICT's estimate is "80% or higher" at first, never 100% ([2024 #4 · 20:33](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1233)).
- **RTH ORG.** On the RTH chart, the 4:14 PM settlement against the 9:30 AM first print. ICT claims price trades to the gap's midpoint about 70% of the time by 10:00, wants at least 40 NQ handles, and tells you to test the figure yourself ([2024 #29 · 1:09:21](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4161)).
- **Event Horizon (2026).** The 50% line between two known levels, such as two liquidity pools. It is mostly a partial-profit checkpoint and only occasionally a fade level ([2026 #73 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=436)).
- **Instruments.** The three gaps were taught on NQ and ES. The only CFD bridge in the notes is for the ORG on an index CFD, and no gold rule exists ([2024 #32 · 1:04:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3866)).

## The futures clock behind the gaps

### What it is

Index futures (NQ, ES and their micros) do not trade around the clock. The regular trading hours (RTH) session has its bell at 4:00 PM ET, then spends about 15 minutes settling, so the day session is done at 4:15 ([2024 #5 · 1:21:06](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4866)). The last RTH print is the 4:14 PM candle on a 1-minute chart; there is no 4:15 candle ([2024 #47 · 5:55](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=355), [2024 #18 · 47:32](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2852)).

Electronic trading (ETH) carries on to 5:00 PM, stops for one hour and restarts at 6:00 PM. On Friday it does not restart until Sunday 6:00 PM ([2024 #11 · 18:02](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1082)).

So two different "closes" matter. The RTH settlement (the 4:14 PM print) anchors the ORG. The electronic close (the 4:59 PM candle, the last print before the stop) anchors the NDOG and the NWOG. ICT stresses that these are different prices and that many traders miss the distinction ([2026 #139 · 3:50](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=230)).

### How to spot it: reading the prints

1. Work on the 1-minute chart. On a 5-minute chart the last RTH candle shows as 4:10, so you cannot read the exact close or open ([2024 #47 · 5:55](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=355)).
2. Set the chart to New York time so what you see matches what he teaches ([2024 #9 · 4:36](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=276)).
3. Use the RTH/ETH toggle at the lower right of TradingView. ETH runs 6:00 PM to 5:00 PM; RTH runs 9:30 AM to 4:14 PM ([2024 #42 · 1:06:01](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3961), [2026 #138 · 1:21:42](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4902)).
4. Know what each view hides. On the ETH chart, overnight trading fills the space between the 4:14 close and the 9:30 open, so the ORG disappears; the only gap left is the one-hour break ([2026 #132 · 15:31](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=931)).
5. Confirm the mode every time you toggle. ICT admits he once mis-weighted a level because the wrong mode was on; he says aloud "I'm back in electronic trading hours" and checks the chart label ([2024 #11 · 55:11](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3311)).
6. For Monday, the "previous day" is Friday, or the last settlement before a holiday ([2024 #47 · 5:55](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=355)).

### Does it apply to gold or CFDs?

ICT taught all three gaps on index futures. In the notes, the nearest he comes to other markets is this. He draws an NWOG on crude oil on a 5-minute chart ([2026 #76 · 3:19](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=199)). He uses Event Horizon on Bitcoin, GBPUSD and DXY ([2026 #73 · 5:42](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=342), [2026 #72 · 5:44](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=344)). He gives a bridge for index CFDs: a CFD trades around the clock, with no settlement break and no 4:15-to-6:00 gap, so it has no gap function. To approximate the ORG, mark the close of the 4:14 PM ET candle (the "settlement") and the 9:30 open on the CFD chart; the same two prices carry the same information ([2024 #32 · 1:04:26](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3866)).

Nothing in the notes applies the NDOG, NWOG or ORG to XAUUSD, and none defines a gold settlement or a gold 9:30 gap. Treat any gold version, including the 70% figure, as your own research. Two reassurances from ICT: if the exchange went 24-hour and the NDOG vanished, the NWOG and the relative equal highs and lows would remain ([2024 #3 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1041)), and extended Nasdaq futures hours do not worry him ([2026 #27 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=435)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Rollover.** A continuous contract hides the gap at the roll, and the "settlement price" toggle can change what you see. ICT does not use the toggle to find fair value gaps; he uses it to reveal volume imbalances, and he asks you to check both settings ([2026 #105 · 13:20](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=800)). During a roll, gaps from the prior contract bleed over, so watch for artificial runs that fill them ([2026 #118 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=389)).
- **"Settlement" means two things.** 4:14 PM for the ORG and 4:59 PM for the NDOG and NWOG. Mixing them shifts the gap edges.

## The New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)

### What it is

On every weekday evening except Friday, index futures stop at 5:00 PM ET, pause for an hour and reopen at 6:00 PM. The NDOG is the space between the last price before the stop and the first price after the restart [2024 #3 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1041).

ICT's recipe is exact. On the 1-minute chart, take the close of the 4:59 PM candle and the open of the first 6:00 PM candle [2024 #3 · 18:58](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1138). The higher of the two prices is the gap high and the lower is the gap low. If the 6 PM open is above the 5 PM close it is an up gap; if below, a down gap [2024 #4 · 13:17](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=797).

There are three levels: high, low and consequent encroachment (CE, the 50% midpoint; see Chapter 6). Put a Fibonacci tool across the gap, read 0.5 as the CE, and add 0.25 and 0.75 for the quadrants [2024 #4 · 15:40](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=940). Quadrants are worth drawing only on a big gap. Under about 20 NQ handles, eyeball the middle; he calls a gap of roughly 66 handles "significant" and plots quadrants on it [2024 #4 · 14:06](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=846). Over 20 handles he always annotates the CE [2024 #7 · 11:38](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=698).

Draw each level as a horizontal ray that runs to the right. Label it with the date, because the date tells you how old, and so how important, the level is [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673). The date is the trading day the gap serves: the gap that forms at 6 PM on Tuesday is Wednesday's NDOG [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673). 

If the 5 PM close and the first 6 PM print are the same price, there is no gap. Draw one line at the 6 PM opening price and use the 6 PM price, not the 5 PM one. It is a single level rather than a range, and it can be swept and revisited like an NDOG [2024 #8 · 9:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=567). Even with no gap, price gravitates back to the 6 PM price as the first real level, ahead of an FVG that may stay open [2024 #8 · 1:53:20](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=6800).

[[DIAGRAM ch11_d2 | caption: A down-gap NDOG graded into quadrants (illustrative prices) | The 4:59 PM close is the gap high, the first 6:00 PM print is the gap low, and the CE and 0.25 and 0.75 lines are drawn as rays dated for the next trading day.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about inefficiency and time. The gap is a place where the market did not trade, and he says the algorithm refers back to such points of reference based on time and price [2024 #4 · 27:10](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1630). That is why a gap is not used up when price fills it. He contrasts this with the retail view of "gap filled, done" and with supply-and-demand "fresh zone" logic [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673).

He adds a sensitivity rule. Price is especially drawn to an NDOG it has touched only once; that gap is still hot, and he even uses the word guarantee for the return. Treat that as his claim, not a law. He also says a trade need not reach the NDOG the same day; if it did not trade there during the day, he would look for it in London that night [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673).

### How to spot it

1. On the 1-minute chart in electronic hours, find the close of the 4:59 PM candle and the open of the first 6:00 PM candle.
2. If they are equal, draw one line at the 6 PM open and stop.
3. If not, mark the gap high and gap low. Add the CE (0.5), and the 0.25 and 0.75 lines only when the gap is over about 20 NQ handles.
4. Extend each as a ray and label it "ND" plus the date of the trading day it serves.
5. Keep the last five on the chart. A full history can live on a separate TradingView layout [2024 #3 · 28:41](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1721).
6. Note how many times price has touched each one. A gap touched once is the sensitive one.

### How long to keep it

His starting rule is five trading days, not calendar days, and holidays are skipped. A gap made Tuesday for Wednesday is valid Wednesday (day 1), Thursday, Friday, Monday and Tuesday (day 5), so it is good until Wednesday of the next week. He calls this a starting rule, not a hard cutoff, and says he still uses gaps nine months old when they fit the narrative [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673). The table under "How the teaching changed" shows how this loosened over time.

### How to trade it

The NDOG is a draw and a reaction level, not an entry by itself. The entry is still an FVG, an iFVG or another array.

- **As the narrative behind a target.** An intraday objective such as a relative equal high or low is tied to an NDOG or NWOG, which supplies the reason price wants to go there [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673).
- **Liquidity on the way.** For a bullish run to a gap above, look for relative equal highs just below it or at it, so there is liquidity to take on the way to the magnet. For a bearish run to a gap below, look for relative equal lows just above it [2024 #13 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=394).
- **The easy setup.** On August 20, 2024 price opened below the NDOG and he watched its high as the draw. A 15-second BISI that overlapped the NDOG was what he called a bread-and-butter setup [2024 #13 · 0:49](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=49).
- **Entry mechanics.** In his order-entry drill, price delivers to the NDOG, rallies and leaves an FVG. The entry is the FVG candle's high plus one tick, filled on the retrace. The first objective is the high of the daily SIBI, then the volume imbalance [2024 #13 · 51:03](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3063), [2024 #13 · 51:49](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=3109).
- **Time first.** Do not trade because a gap is marked. Time must be the trigger first [2024 #5 · 19:52](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=1192).

### The 6 PM protocol: initial liquidity

Right after the 6 PM open, ICT wants price to do two things. It moves away from the NDOG and takes a short-term high (a minor buy-side pool), then crosses back over the NDOG and takes a short-term low (a minor sell-side pool), or the reverse order. Trading below the gap is not enough; a short-term low must be taken [2024 #5 · 36:16](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2176).

That new high and low are the "initial" buy-side and sell-side liquidity for the evening: the first levels where the traders of that moment keep their stops. He says it shows the algorithm calibrating and engineering liquidity [2024 #5 · 41:56](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2516). Crossing through the NDOG is expected and is not a failure of the level [2024 #5 · 36:16](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2176).

If price dawdles after 6 PM and does not push above or below by 7 PM, he expects a dull Asia and says not to trade [2024 #5 · 43:32](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2612). If it does, look at 7 PM for a retracement into the NDOG that does not travel back to the initial level on the other side, then trade toward the initial liquidity on the far side and the larger pools beyond it. He says the same initial-liquidity logic applies to the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range [2024 #5 · 36:16](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2176).

### Worked example: August 7, 2024

On Tuesday, August 6 the 5 PM close was above the 6 PM open, a down gap, which made it Wednesday's NDOG. After the open, NQ traded up into the gap and stopped to the tick at a quadrant level, 18,049.50, which he called perfect. It hovered around the upper quadrant, dropped to the CE, then to the lower quadrant, and took a low there. It never touched the actual gap low before rallying away. At 7:00, 8:00 and 9:00 price was far above the gap; later it fell back and closed into it [2024 #3 · 31:52](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1912).

Read it as a map of levels, not as a trade. The sequence is quadrant, CE, quadrant, each acting as a stopping place, while the outer edge was never needed. The diagram above uses illustrative prices because the notes do not give the gap edges.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Retiring it after one fill.** The common error is disregarding a gap as soon as it fills, "throwing the baby out with the bathwater" [2024 #13 · 10:43](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=643).
- **Mixing it up with the RTH opening range gap.** The NDOG is the 5 PM-to-6 PM globex gap. The ORG is the RTH settlement to the 9:30 open. The opening range is the 30 minutes after 9:30 [2024 #10 · 23:24](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1404).
- **Trusting every 2026 definition.** Some 2026 notes describe the NDOG with other anchors, such as the previous 4:14 PM close or the RTH close [2026 #23 · 58:00](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=3480), [2026 #76 · 3:19](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=199). The clearest 2026 statement repeats the 2024 build, 5 PM settlement to 6 PM restart [2026 #27 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=435). This chapter uses that build.

The idea starts in 2022 as a weekly Sunday gap (see the NWOG section). The name and the full rule set arrive in August 2024. ICT says he first showed the NDOG on Twitter Spaces in spring or summer 2023 and let public interest fade because he felt it was too good to hand to everyone [2024 #6 · 27:14](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1634). The shelf life then loosened:

## The New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)

### What it is

The NWOG is the weekly version of the NDOG: the range between Friday's 5:00 PM ET close and Sunday's 6:00 PM open [2024 #3 · 28:41](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1721). Mark it the same way, with a high, a low and a CE, and label it "NW" plus the date. He writes it out as "New Week Opening Gap August 18 2024" [2024 #11 · 3:08:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11330).

Whichever of Friday's last tick and Sunday's first print is higher is the NWOG high. When a viewer asked why the gap is not measured lower, he answered that the last tick is the close, and that is what you anchor to [2024 #11 · 3:08:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=11330). If a holiday closes Friday, use Thursday [2026 #17 · 31:57](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1917).

### Why it works

The logic is the NDOG's, scaled up: the gap is a real place where the market did not trade efficiently, so it still works six to nine months later [2024 #3 · 28:41](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1721). ICT calls it the higher-tier "black hole" draw. There is one NWOG per week against five NDOGs, and for a swing trade the NWOG carries more weight, because the market makers have had the whole weekend to digest information before setting the opening price [2024 #3 · 45:19](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2719).

### How to spot it

1. Use the 1-minute chart. Find Friday's last electronic print (the 4:59 PM candle) and Sunday's first 6:00 PM print [2026 #17 · 31:57](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=1917).
2. In TradingView, switch to electronic hours, use "go to date" and jump to the previous Sunday at 18:00, then zoom in [2026 #17 · 33:35](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2015).
3. Anchor a rectangle at Friday's close (on a down-close candle, anchor to the close, not the low) and drag to Sunday's opening price [2026 #17 · 35:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2112).
4. Repeat week by week for at least five weeks. Name each object in the object tree, for example "NWOG Aug 23", keep colours consistent, and hide old gaps rather than deleting them [2026 #17 · 37:36](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2256).
5. Keep the matrix on a layout with nothing else on it, and copy the layout each quarter [2026 #17 · 39:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2352).
6. Add the CE and, for a wide gap, the quarters and eighths. He shows a NWOG graded this way on the 1-minute chart [2026 #134 · 4:55](https://youtu.be/zyKAPNk_prA?t=295).

### How to trade it

- **As a magnet on Monday.** If price opens and rallies away from the NWOG, never trades back to it during Sunday evening or London, and shows no runaway move, then on Monday a run to the NWOG is an easy one in his view. Know where you can exit before the terminus, because price need not reach it [2026 #30 · 18:50](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1130).
- **As support on a bullish day.** On August 19, 2024 (ES) price stayed above the NWOG all day and used it as support for the buy. The lunch-macro drop came back to the NWOG and the 11:17 low, then rallied [2024 #12 · 31:06](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1866).
- **As unfinished business.** In July 2026 price had come up into a NWOG without fully closing it, which he read as decidedly weak, and he said there was unfinished business above [2026 #63 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/bz4QNPcpmWE?t=774). Relative equal highs near price are only "minor" buy-side when a NWOG sits higher as the more prominent target [2026 #64 · 7:19](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=439).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **It is not Monday's RTH opening range gap.** Monday's ORG runs from Friday's 4:14 PM RTH settlement to the 9:30 open. ICT calls it a separate and generally more relevant reference for the opening session. On October 21, 2024 the NWOG even encapsulated Friday's RTH settlement price [2024 #47 · 9:20](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=560).
- **Loose 2026 wording.** Several 2026 notes say the NWOG starts at "Friday's 4:14 PM close" or "RTH close" [2026 #23 · 42:42](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=2562), [2026 #16 · 0:02](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=2), [2026 #56 · 5:43](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=343). His build lesson uses the electronic 4:59 PM print [2026 #17 · 35:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2112). Check your gap against that.

### How the teaching changed

The 2022 precursor is the Sunday opening gap. In Episode 34 he plots the gap between Friday's close and the Sunday evening reopen (about 6 PM ET) and extends it across the week. He says there is a lot of valuation around it, so it acts as a dynamic level: support if price is above it, resistance if below. He knowingly uses the retail words "support" and "resistance" only for this gap, to make it understandable [2022 Ep 34 · 8:48](https://youtu.be/t8flQP5jy-Q?t=528).

In August 2024 the NWOG is named and given a five-week life, and he previews it as the draw in "Caleb's model" [2024 #2 · 1:16:54](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4614). On October 25, 2024 he says keep only the current NWOG [2024 #48 · 1:29:29](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5369). In 2026 the emphasis is that it never expires: extend it through the whole week, because price dives back inside it later in the session [2026 #34 · 19:01](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1141).

## Clusters: opening gaps as bias, magnet and speed bump

### What it is

A cluster is two or more of the recent opening gaps sitting on top of or close to each other. ICT's test: compare the current NWOG with the last five NWOGs or NDOGs. If any two overlap, even by one level of one gap encroaching on the range of another, it is a cluster [2024 #49 · 1:29:34](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=5374), [2024 #49 · 39:03](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=2343).

Open overlapping gaps mark places where no buying or selling took place, "natural value areas", and more overlap means a higher probability that price is repriced there. Whether price traded through them in the past does not matter. He calls the cluster a magnet draw on price, not a requirement to reach it, and compares it to getting near a stadium before you look for the signs [2024 #49 · 39:50](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=2390).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the algorithm has to refer back to old inefficiencies at the opening bell, so you can gravitate toward them instead of watching order-book heat maps [2024 #6 · 28:19](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1699). He calls these gaps the first central tenets of bias, and notes that support-and-resistance lines or pitchforks will not show a nested cluster [2024 #17 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=3064).

### How to spot it: the 8:30 test

1. At 8:00, mark the relative equal highs and lows that sit into 8:30 [2024 #4 · 26:23](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1583).
2. Just before 8:30, find price against the last five NDOGs and the NWOG. Count how many sit above and how many below [2024 #4 · 20:33](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1233).
3. Add context. Has price already dropped into a deep discount of the recent range? Which side has been profitable lately? That side is the one that gets squeezed [2024 #4 · 20:33](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1233).
4. If most gaps sit above, the bias for the 8:30 move is up; if most sit below, down. If price sits between two gaps, neither direction is more likely until the 8:30 move, and you wait rather than copy the breakout traders who buy above and sell below the nearby highs and lows [2024 #4 · 26:23](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1583).

He puts the initial hit rate at "80% or higher", explicitly not 100% [2024 #4 · 33:38](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2018). It holds with or without a news release, and a gap already traded to still counts [2024 #4 · 27:10](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1630).

### The 7:00 session-bias version

On August 12, 2024 he gave the same idea at 7:00. If price is above the NWOG and the NDOGs sit clustered below it (last week's NDOGs, Friday's, last week's NWOG), the market is likely to enter a sell program. He then does not look to go long even if price springs higher, and he expects that rally to fizzle above old highs formed after 7:00 [2024 #6 · 1:35:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5709).

This is a session bias, not a daily bias. He wants one run that is measurable, one-sided and headed to a level you can predetermine, "not random" [2024 #6 · 1:09:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4195). The routine is rule-based: wait for relative equal highs to form after 7:00, wait for the pierce, enter a bearish PD array, and aim for sell-side liquidity, then the NWOG and the NDOG [2024 #6 · 1:38:13](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=5893). 

### How to trade it

- **Pick the first level.** A beginner targets the nearest cluster level. A trader shorting from above takes the highest level of the cluster first, the NWOG high; a trader buying takes the lowest [2024 #49 · 39:03](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=2343).
- **It is a filter.** A cluster below price does not mean short only. If you want a long, wait to see whether price drops toward the gaps first [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673).
- **Against the cluster.** If you expect a short but the gaps cluster above, give the first 30 minutes after 7:00, 8:00 or 9:00 a chance to spike up into them. Then wait for a market structure shift, a breaker or an inversion FVG before shorting [2024 #3 · 27:53](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=1673).
- **With the opening range gap.** If the mid-gap fill (the next major section) points the same way as the cluster, expect a 3/4 closure, a full closure or more. He says there is still a draw beyond the CE [2024 #49 · 1:14:10](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4450).
- **Caleb's model.** The draw is the NDOG or NWOG, and the entry is the first presented FVG or an iFVG. The progression he lists is OTE, then the 2022 model, then the Silver Bullet, then this one (Chapter 17). He insists you do not swap the model because it has no trade today [2024 #17 · 1:17:40](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=4660).

### Nested gaps: black hole or speed bump

Stacked gaps behave differently from lone ones. Nested together, they act like a gravitational pool that price visits back and forth for days; ICT says it is not one and done [2024 #18 · 1:25:31](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5131). He also treats them as a resistance test. A run through many gaps is held back, as if something were tangled around its legs [2024 #20 · 1:52:16](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6736).

- **Slices through in one or two candles.** The algorithm is in a hurry, usually because of an imbalance or liquidity pool it is targeting [2024 #21 · 32:49](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1969).
- **Petering out or chopping inside.** That is a high-resistance run; if it chops, stay out [2024 #20 · 1:49:05](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6545).
- **Fails to reach even the lowest edge of the cluster and drops below its low.** Price is tipping its hand that it does not want to go higher [2024 #21 · 25:08](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1508).
- **A few gaps far apart.** Runs are cleaner, like an interstate highway early in the morning; clustered gaps are speed bumps [2024 #21 · 31:15](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1875).

You are measuring how efficiently price reaches premium arrays and whether it has the pursuit to reach the next one [2024 #21 · 24:20](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1460). Chapter 16 covers high and low resistance in full.

### Worked example: August 8, 2024

Price had already dropped into a deep discount of the range from the low up to Wednesday's high, taken relative equal lows inside a BISI, and bounced from that BISI's upper quadrant.

At 8:30 it rallied into Wednesday's NDOG low, CE and high, and then to Tuesday's NDOG, as he had forecast the day before [2024 #4 · 20:33](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1233).

## The RTH opening range gap (ORG)

### What it is

The ORG is the space between the previous session's regular-trading-hours settlement and the first print of the 9:30 AM ET open [2024 #10 · 25:38](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1538). ICT says these two prices are the only reference points you need on the regular session [2026 #47 · 4:52](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=292). The settlement is the last RTH print, the close of the 4:14 PM candle on the 1-minute chart [2024 #47 · 5:55](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=355).

If the open is above the settlement, it is a premium gap: the open is the ORG high and the settlement is the low. If the open is below, it is a discount gap: the settlement is the high and the open is the low [2026 #129 · 25:34](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1534). Until 9:30 you do not know which, so label the settlement line "RTH ORG settlement" and rename it high or low when the open prints [2026 #19 · 41:46](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2506). Name the gap for the day you are trading: Monday's ORG, not "Friday's" [2024 #47 · 10:57](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=657).

Three look-alikes need separating.

- **The opening range** is the 30 minutes from 9:30 to 10:00, a travelled distance from the open to its extreme. The gap is an inefficiency, not a distance [2024 #10 · 23:24](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1404). He says there is no 15-minute opening range [2024 #5 · 1:22:41](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4961).
- **The NDOG** is the 5 PM-to-6 PM globex gap from the night before.
- **An FVG** is a three-candle pattern. The ORG is not one [2024 #19 · 1:00:07](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3607).

Which "last price" he anchors to has varied. In February 2026 he said you can also use the 5:00 PM settlement, as taught the year before [2026 #126 · 1:01:33](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3693). Later that year he insisted the 4:14 final print is correct [2026 #47 · 6:28](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=388).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation has three parts. First, the regular session did not trade between the settlement and the open. Price therefore rushes back into that unpopulated area, even though overnight electronic trading already visited some of it; he compares it to a soda cap being released [2026 #138 · 1:48:33](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=6513). Second, a gap lower is a lure to get traders short, and the fill follows [2024 #13 · 38:09](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2289). Third, he calls the half-gap behaviour algorithmic [2024 #18 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=475).

He also treats the gap as a higher-timeframe level: you cannot get more higher timeframe than session closings and opens [2026 #132 · 13:07](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=787). 

### How to spot it

1. After the close or first thing in the morning, switch to RTH on the 1-minute chart and read the last 4:14 candle. Use its close [2026 #138 · 1:21:42](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4902).
2. Draw a line there and label it "RTH ORG settlement". He pre-plots it around 9:00 to 9:25, and if price is far below, labels it ORG high in advance [2024 #18 · 2:16](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=136), [2026 #126 · 1:01:33](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3693).
3. Park a second line away from price. At 9:30, drag it to the first print. Do not treat a pending price as the open [2026 #99 · 7:23](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=443).
4. Drop a Fibonacci across the two prices. The 50% line is the CE; add the quadrants. A rectangle with its mid-level option on does the same [2026 #14 · 14:13](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=853).
5. Measure the gap in handles and compare it with the size table below.
6. Set an alert on the CE so a one-tick cross rings [2024 #18 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=475).
7. Check the mode label (RTH or ETH) before you trust any line [2024 #11 · 55:11](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3311).

### Premium and discount openings

**Premium (open above settlement).** The first-30-minute bias is down. In the 2026 drill he looks only for shorts, and only for things that will take part in a move lower, using the gap's lower sell-side pools as the draw [2026 #99 · 22:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1362). A large premium opening is expensive and he will not buy it [2024 #25 · 26:56](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1616). The 9:30 opening price is the first target, the midpoint the second, and a full closure is the best case but often does not happen [2026 #111 · 1:21:46](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=4906). If price will not hold the 9:30 level, expect it to head for the CE; a close above it shows bulls defending [2026 #112 · 15:22](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU?t=922).

**Discount (open below).** The first-30-minute bias is up. He calls shorting simply because "we open lower" the retail-minded thing and says he never does it [2024 #47 · 26:21](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=1581), [2024 #47 · 36:46](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2206). At an all-time high in April 2026 he added that a discount gap can be bought only after price proves it by trading above the gap high and holding the gap as support [2026 #96 · 19:19](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=1159).

**Do not chase.** On August 29, 2024 NQ opened with a large premium gap and ran straight up with no pullback. He would not buy it; he looked for shorts. If you miss the bus, he says, the next one comes in about 20 minutes, like another macro. The gap can stay open all day, but price tends to come back [2024 #20 · 18:34](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1114), [2024 #20 · 19:22](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=1162).

### The 70% claim

ICT says that when a gap forms, price trades to its midpoint (CE) about 70% of the time between 9:30 and 10:00 [2024 #22 · 14:48](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=888). He also phrases it as seven times out of ten [2024 #49 · 1:14:10](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=4450), and as a 70% strike rate [2024 #40 · 1:32:16](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5536). He always adds that it is not 100%.

What the claim does not say matters as much. It is about trading to the midpoint, not holding it, and not closing the gap; wicks can exceed levels [2024 #22 · 14:48](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=888). It does not require a bias. He says a student studying it needs no daily or session bias [2024 #18 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=475). And it is a tendency inside a narrative, comparable to his 70% rule for the Tuesday London session setting the weekly extreme [2026 #126 · 1:03:09](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3789).

**His evidence is his word.** He says he derived it back to 1987 using pre-Globex numbers [2024 #22 · 33:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2015), and defends it against a Twitter study with data going back about 40 years [2024 #41 · 5:11](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=311), [2024 #42 · 1:24:50](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=5090). He also tells students not to take it on faith: journal every gap, and compute your own statistic at year end [2026 #14 · 15:50](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=950), [2026 #99 · 5:29](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=329).

**The window varies.** Most lectures say 9:30 to 10:00. One 2026 lecture says 9:31 to 10:00 [2026 #113 · 38:37](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2317), and another frames it as the first hour, with 30 minutes for newer traders [2026 #105 · 33:17](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=1997). A 2026 refresher says the rule no longer applies after 10:00 [2026 #43 · 25:49](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=1549). Yet he also says that if the midpoint is missed early, you still look for it later; it simply matters less on a high-resistance day [2024 #47 · 36:46](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2206), [2024 #49 · 1:46:29](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6389).

## The mid-gap model and grading the gap

### What it is

The mid-gap model is ICT's simplest morning rule. When the open leaves a gap of useful size, expect price to try for the gap's midpoint in the first 30 minutes, and trade toward it. He calls the CE his "always first draw" [2024 #28 · 1:15:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4547). Even when price does not reach it, the CE anchors the bias, because you expect price to try. The levels he lists as targets before the CE are the high of day, the opening price (a static price, not a zone), buy-side liquidity such as smooth or equal highs, and the gap's lower quadrant [2024 #28 · 1:15:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4547).

He teaches it as a minimalist framework for beginners, including his sons [2026 #140 · 22:00](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1320), and says the idea alone could serve as a complete model [2024 #47 · 36:46](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=2206). He also stresses that it is a low-hanging-fruit model, not the best-of-the-best setup [2024 #22 · 14:48](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=888).

### How to trade it

1. **Check the size.** Under about 40 handles, use the table above: wait for 10:00 or use other tools [2024 #32 · 20:11](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1211).
2. **Take the direction from the open.** Premium gap, look for shorts to the half gap. Discount gap, look for longs. No daily or session bias is required [2024 #18 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=475), and he says you do not even need to be right on bias for the retracement trade [2024 #13 · 38:09](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2289).
3. **Choose the entry array.** The usual choice is the first presented FVG after 9:31 (Chapter 8) [2024 #22 · 59:51](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3591). A beginner version waits for a PD array to be traded to, such as an hourly BISI or relative equal lows, and enters into it [2026 #52 · 0:47](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=47). In 2026 he prefers an FVG that formed before the session, in the 7:00 to 9:30 window, over one that forms inside the opening range [2026 #99 · 23:28](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=1408). With a 75-handle or larger gap he would go in at the bell without waiting for the first FVG [2024 #44 · 40:15](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=2415). Entry mechanics are in Chapters 5 and 18.
4. **Target the midpoint, and take partials before it.** On a huge premium gap he removes risk by taking one contract off beneath the relative equal lows and then lowering the stop [2026 #53 · 13:23](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=803). After breaking a short-term low, move the stop to cost; with more than one contract take a partial below that low [2024 #29 · 57:15](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=3435).
5. **At the midpoint, bank most of it.** He suggests taking 75 to 80% off. If you want the whole gap, put the stop above the short-term low and leave the balance as a free look [2024 #29 · 58:04](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=3484). If you cannot choose partials, take two, keep the stop at cost, and let the market take you out of the rest; the target is only a reference [2024 #29 · 2:07:28](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=7648).
6. **Decide what comes next.** After the half gap, price either continues to 3/4 and full closure or rejects the half and runs in the gap's direction, a "gap and go" [2026 #131 · 21:34](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=1294). Use the higher-timeframe bias and the liquidity in the path. If a premium gap closes fully, use the bottom of the gap as resistance and expect a move down in multiples of the gap, two to three standard deviations [2024 #42 · 1:13:32](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=4412). 7. **Journal it.** Log the gap size, whether and when the CE traded, and what it offered [2026 #10 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1487), [2026 #14 · 16:40](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1000).

After a loss he says not to make it up: take the setup by the rules, on the smallest size, the next day [2024 #29 · 2:07:28](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=7648).

### Beyond the midpoint: an NDOG or NWOG inside the gap

On September 4, 2024 NQ opened over 100 handles below Tuesday's settlement. If an NDOG or NWOG sits inside the ORG and above its midpoint on a discount day, ICT says the odds improve that price keeps going from the midpoint up to that gap, which he rates a very strong 70% setup [2024 #22 · 33:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2015).

You do not need both gaps, nor the whole gap. The low of the gap is the first level reached and is the target [2024 #22 · 37:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2255). His stated model is: buy the first FVG, take a partial at the midgap, and make the NDOG low your terminus [2024 #22 · 59:51](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3591). A longer swing around both sides of the NDOG toward full closure is a bonus. Longs that did not take profit at the NDOG got run over quickly, so take something off there [2024 #22 · 36:47](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=2207). On the day price reached the NDOG, then sold off sharply around the 10:00 news print.

## Event Horizon: the midpoint between two levels

### What it is

Event Horizon (EH) is ICT's 2026 name for the 50% line of a range between two known price levels. The ends can be two liquidity pools, an old high and a target, two old lows, or a gap edge and a high. Anchor a Fibonacci on one level, drag it to the other, delete every level except 0.5, and that line is the EH [2026 #73 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=436). Or compute it: (level A + level B) / 2 [2026 #97 · 4:40](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=280).

The arithmetic is the CE of Chapter 6, but the ends are not the edges of a gap. In June 2026 he treated the two as one: the midpoint of the range between two liquidity pools is the consequent encroachment, which he also calls the event horizon [2026 #72 · 5:44](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=344). In February he said he first taught it with one NWOG above price and one below, then marked the halfway point between them [2026 #127 · 21:26](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1286). In March he called it a new coinage that nobody has in their books [2026 #121 · 15:13](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=913).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is partly geometric and partly about precision. Old highs and lows are finite, specific locations, so the middle between them is a very strong candidate for price to reach, and price may only touch it and turn around [2026 #127 · 18:10](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1090), [2026 #73 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=436). A wide range often looks empty in the middle, yet price gravitates to the midpoint with uncanny precision; the notes describe it as pure geometry rather than a level price has touched before [2026 #105 · 6:02](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=362), [2026 #97 · 4:40](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=280). 

For the news-day fade, the logic is efficiency. A new leg higher leaves buyers' protective sell stops below the post-news low. Shorting from the EH gives willing sellers a lower pool to pay out into [2026 #2 · 7:30](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=450).

### How to spot it

1. Pick two finite, specific levels. His examples: two old lows; a 1-hour minor buy-side pool and an old high; the CE of a 15-minute FVG and an old high; the NWOG high and an inversion FVG high [2026 #74 · 2:44](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=164), [2026 #69 · 12:28](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=748), [2026 #104 · 35:26](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=2126).
2. Draw the Fibonacci with only the 0.5 level showing, anchored on exact highs and lows ("on air"), not on candle bodies [2026 #2 · 10:42](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=642).
3. Ballpark is fine. He says not to be so precise, and an eyeballed line can be off by a couple of points; afterward he checks it against a graded range to show how close it was [2026 #62 · 5:39](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=339), [2026 #127 · 19:51](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1191), [2026 #69 · 25:51](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1551).
4. You can nest them: draw one from the first low and one from the second, and treat the zone between as a small inefficiency [2026 #73 · 8:05](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=485).
5. Redraw if the range resets. If price breaks the lower low and you treat that as a new range, the old EH no longer applies [2026 #117 · 27:14](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=1634).

### How to trade it

He uses EH in four different ways. They share the same level and differ in the job it does.

- **A partial and a stop checkpoint.** At the EH, "an event on the horizon", you must decide where to move the stop and whether to take something off. His message is to pay the trader and not demand target-or-nothing [2026 #73 · 28:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=1696). Examples: one contract off at or just below the EH, then raise the stop [2026 #74 · 4:23](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=263); 25% off at the EH in a down-trending Bitcoin short, or add to the short there [2026 #117 · 27:14](https://youtu.be/w2zTfhjScDg?t=1634); 20 to 50% off, and in one 10-lot trade 2 to 3 lots [2026 #90 · 27:20](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=1640); one of three micros for a beginner [2026 #19 · 1:10:00](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4200). 
- **A second target against the flow.** When trading against higher-timeframe order flow, he took the first partial at a minor low and the second at the EH between two lows [2026 #86 · 42:52](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=2572).
- **A confirmation.** A close beyond the EH raises confidence that the pool behind it will be taken; a failure to close beyond suggests a bounce or reversal [2026 #73 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=436), [2026 #72 · 5:44](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=344), [2026 #64 · 38:36](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2316). Speed matters too: price that goes through with stretched, heavy bodies is strong, and he does not need a measured move [2026 #90 · 27:20](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=1640). On June 22, 2026 wicks had gone above the halfway point but bodies were not yet "laying on" it [2026 #74 · 8:19](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=499).
- **A fade level.** In one news-day scenario he sells at the EH of an unrealized range (the worked example below).

The same level can be a reference rather than a trade. One April 2026 review calls it a way station, not a reversal point: pause there and ask whether price can continue, rather than trading at it [2026 #97 · 4:40](https://youtu.be/9nmjg331xGQ?t=280).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **It is not the ORG midpoint.** The ORG's CE is the middle of a real gap between two prints. The EH is the middle of any range between two levels, drawn from geometry. - **An isolated level.** On its own the EH means nothing. It needs a convergence of several smart-money concepts [2026 #2 · 36:23](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2183).
- **Applying it to gold by assumption.** The notes show it on NQ, ES, Bitcoin, GBPUSD and DXY, not on XAUUSD.

## Check yourself

1. At 6:00 PM on Tuesday NQ reopens 12 handles above the 4:59 PM close. What do you mark, what do you call it, and do you add quadrants?
2. A friend says yesterday's NDOG "is done" because price traded through it at 10:15. How does ICT answer, and what does he say about gaps touched only once?
3. At 8:30, Tuesday's NDOG, Wednesday's NDOG and the NWOG sit above price, and Thursday's NDOG sits just below. What bias does the cluster test give, how confident is ICT, and how do you handle a plan to short?
4. At 9:20 on an ETH chart you cannot find any gap between yesterday's close and the current price. What is going on, and what do you do?
5. NQ opens 28 handles above yesterday's settlement. What do his filters suggest for 9:30 to 10:00?
6. NQ opens 110 handles above settlement and price is running up with no pullback. A friend wants to buy the run. What does ICT do, where is his first target, and how does he describe the 70% figure?
7. You are short from the first FVG on a large premium gap, and price taps the CE at 9:47. List two things ICT suggests at this point.
8. A video claims "ICT proved that 70% of gaps fill to the midpoint". Using only the notes, how do you answer?
9. It is a discount-gap day and an NDOG sits inside the ORG, above its midpoint. How does that change the plan?
10. Define Event Horizon, say how to draw it, and name two different jobs ICT gives it. Then say why you cannot assume the same rules for XAUUSD.

## Answers

1. Mark an NDOG for Wednesday: the 4:59 PM close and the 6:00 PM open. At 12 handles it is under about 20, so eyeball the midpoint and skip the quadrants. Reread "The New Day Opening Gap".
2. A fill does not retire a gap; he treats discarding it as throwing away something still useful, and says price keeps referring back. A gap touched only once is the most sensitive, though his guarantee wording is a claim, not a law. Reread "Why it works" under the NDOG.
3. The bias is up, toward the nearest gap above, with his estimate "80% or higher" at first and never 100%. It is a filter, not a rule. To short, give the first 30 minutes after 7:00, 8:00 or 9:00 a chance to spike into the gaps, then wait for a market structure shift, a breaker or an inversion FVG. Reread "Clusters".
4. The ETH chart is filled by overnight trading, so the ORG does not show. Switch to RTH on the 1-minute chart, read the 4:14 PM close and the 9:30 open, and confirm the mode label. Reread "The futures clock".
5. 28 handles is below the 30 to 40 "little unchanged" band and under the 40 minimum. He would sit still until 10:00 and consider the Silver Bullet. Reread "Size filters".
6. He would not buy a very expensive premium. He looks for shorts, waits rather than chasing, and treats the 9:30 open and the midpoint as the first targets. The 70% is his claim that the midpoint trades by 10:00, not a promise. Reread "Premium and discount openings" and "The 70% claim".
7. Take most of the position off, 75 to 80%, at the mid gap, and leave a runner with the stop above the short-term low if you want a free look at the full gap. If you cannot choose, take two partials with the stop at cost. Reread "The mid-gap model".
8. The notes record 70% as his statement from his own data, going back to 1987 or about 40 years. They contain misses and late hits, and he tells students to journal their own statistic. Reread "The 70% claim".
9. The odds improve that price continues from the midpoint to that gap. The low of the NDOG is the terminus, with a partial at the midgap and something taken off at the NDOG. Reread "Beyond the midpoint".
10. Event Horizon is the 0.5 line of a range between two finite levels, drawn with a fib showing only 0.5 and anchored on exact highs and lows. Jobs: a partial and stop checkpoint, a confirmation by a close beyond it, a second target, or a news-day fade. For gold, the notes give no settlement, gap or 70% rule, and the CFD bridge was for an index. Reread "Event Horizon" and "Does it apply to gold or CFDs?".
