ICT's trade management rests on one promise: you should not need to be right about the final target to get paid. He anchors the stop to the structure that built the entry, splits the target into a near one and a far one, takes partials on the way, and tightens the stop only after price has proved the idea. This chapter covers each piece in that order, then the rules for holding, for cutting a stalled trade, and for his "double tap" exit. After reading you can write a management plan before you click buy or sell: where the stop goes, where you get paid, when the stop moves, and what makes you leave early.

ICT taught nearly all of this on index futures (ES, NQ and their micros), quoting distances in handles, meaning index points. ICT gave no gold-specific management numbers in these lectures. Carry across the structure and the ratios, not the point counts.

## Where the stop goes

### What it is

A stop is the price at which the trade idea is wrong. In ICT's models it is not an amount you can afford, picked from your mood. It is a structural point: a candle of the entry pattern, a wick, a swing.

Because the entry (an FVG, an inversion FVG, an order block: Chapters 5, 7 and 9) already tells you where the idea fails, the stop location is "defined". ICT's rule is then flat: the hard stop must be placed, period. A hard stop is a resting stop order, not a promise to get out by hand ([2022 Ep 41 · 40:34](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2434)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation starts with the retail problem. A retail short either has no stop or does not know where to put one above an old high. Too shallow and it is hit early. Too high and price spikes through to reach it, then reverses in the trader's direction after a big loss. He calls this the trap smart money exploits ([2022 Ep 6 · 4:52](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=292), [2022 Ep 6 · 5:38](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=338)).

The second half is how the algorithm "finds" stops. ICT says it cannot see any individual's stop. It knows where people will have them, from short-, intermediate- and long-term highs and lows and from the imbalances. He calls his vocabulary a visual language for this: "not exactly like the algorithm does, but very close" ([2022 Ep 12 · 34:40](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=2080)). Liquidity itself is Chapter 3.

The lesson for management is that a stop is only useful if it has a reason. A stop with a structural reason tells you when to leave. A stop that is a guess is either hit early or sits so far away that the loss grows.

### Where to put it

He has named several anchors over the years. They share one idea: put the stop just beyond the part of the entry pattern that price should never trade through if the idea is right.

### How to size and tighten it

A wider stop does not have to mean more risk. ICT says the money at risk is set by leverage, meaning your position size, not by the pip distance ([2022 Ep 18 · 35:54](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2154)). The same idea shows up in 2026: with 10 contracts he could have scaled out along the move, but he used 2 because the stop he needed was larger ([2026 #32 · 1:16:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4597)).

Four rules on size follow from this.

1. **Pick the anchor first, then the size.** If the structural stop is too much risk, his answer is a smaller contract, meaning micros ([2022 Ep 25 · 43:39](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2619)).
2. **Underfunded means skip.** If you cannot stand the stop on a micro, he says you are underfunded and a $100 account is set up to fail ([2022 Ep 25 · 43:39](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2619)).
3. **Keep it from becoming too wide.** In 2026 he adds that you should never place the stop so wide that it covers excessive risk, and that you must follow your rules even when the stop is tight ([2026 #4 · 11:11](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=671)).
4. **Do not overleverage a funded account.** He calls 15 contracts on a Topstep-style account the wrong answer; reduce size so the risk is measured ([2024 #20 · 1:41:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6064)). Sizing proper is Chapter 20.

In high volatility he goes further with "pack small, play big". As the notes record it, he uses very small risk per trade and lets winners run. His June 2026 example was a short that drew down only a couple of dollars and then ran over 1,600 handles ([2026 #76 · 14:06](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=846), [2026 #76 · 21:20](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=1280)). The notes also list 3-5 ticks for the stop on such days against about 10 normally, and 0.5-1% of the account as risk. Those figures come from the summary of that lecture, so test them before you rely on them.

### Futures versus forex and CFDs

ICT draws a line that matters if you trade gold as a CFD. In futures every participant sees the same highs and lows. His example is 29,502.00 on the September NQ at 10:07 ET. The broker is only a facilitator and cannot widen the spread, so tight stops are workable ([2026 #21 · 19:39](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1179)).

In forex each broker has its own highs, lows and pool of liquidity, and can widen the spread to take your stop. His line: the futures market is a gentleman's market and in forex "you're a cowboy" ([2026 #21 · 20:26](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=1226)). He says manipulation exists in futures too.

He made that comparison for forex. A gold CFD is also priced by your broker, so the same caution plausibly applies, but ICT did not say that about gold. Treat a very tight CFD stop as a risk you are choosing.

### Do not publish your stop

In 2026 he reports that stops he shares in public get run. His reasoning: with millions of followers, even a small share copying him puts a large pool of orders at one price, and the market goes to that price first ([2026 #54 · 25:07](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=1507), [2026 #73 · 30:37](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=1837)). On one live stream he showed his stop and price went straight to it ([2026 #54 · 30:41](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=1841)).

The reader's takeaway is the mechanism: a stop level that many people share is itself liquidity. It is a claim of his, not a measured result, and he said he plans to dial back public participation.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **A stop with no reason.** Retail's two failure modes, too shallow or too high, are both guesses ([2022 Ep 6 · 4:52](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=292)).
- **No hard stop.** A trader who says they know when to get out without placing one tells him they do not have it together. Skipping the stop means you will decide where you are wrong only when you get there ([2022 Ep 41 · 15:08](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=908), [2022 Ep 41 · 40:34](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2434)).
- **Fearing the stop.** If it is hit, he says, it has done you a favor. Your job is then to manage the next trade with less risk and climb out of the drawdown step by step ([2022 Ep 41 · 40:34](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2434)). Watch whether price keeps showing the order flow you expected, not whether the stop might trip ([2022 Ep 41 · 14:21](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=861)).

### How the teaching changed

The anchor drifted but the idea did not. In 2022 the stop is a tick or two beyond the FVG candle, with a wider swing-high option. In 2024 it is a tick beyond the middle candle's low, with a conservative first-candle version. In 2026 it is the order block candle, or the wick of an iFVG, with the same insistence that it be tight and obeyed.

One layer was added over time. The hard stop stays the backstop, but in 2024 and 2026 he also exits early when a candle closes beyond the invalidation area. That is covered in "Cutting a trade that is not working".

## Targets: low-hanging fruit, best case and the terminus

### What it is

A target is where you expect to be paid: a draw, usually an old high, an old low or an imbalance (Chapters 3 and 15). ICT's management lectures split it into two tiers. The **low-hanging fruit (LHF)** is the near, probable target. The **best case** is the far one that needs momentum to keep going.

He also uses **Terminus** for the final target, the place where nothing is left on the position. You can have several partials on the way, but only one terminus.

### Low-hanging fruit

The oldest form is in 2022. Aim for the closest target, an old low or an imbalance when short. Do not get fancy aiming for the lowest low, because the market can deny it. Take the easiest target and let the market go further without you ([2022 Ep 2 · 47:49](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2869)).

His reason is mechanical. To cover a short you need willing sellers at a low price, and they sit with sell stops below lows. In that lecture he exited as price hit the top of the target imbalance and accepted leaving money on the table. In hindsight he wished he had kept a small runner for the old daily low ([2022 Ep 2 · 38:54](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2334)).

Three refinements came later.

1. **Exit just before the objective.** For an LHF target he exits "right before" it, since price does not always touch the exact level. He also says not to try for precision early: get the big piece in the middle ([2026 #32 · 1:18:11](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4691)).
2. **Define both tiers up front.** LHF is the first major inefficiency or liquidity pool in your direction. Best case is the deepest inefficiency or furthest liquidity. Take the bulk off at LHF (he says 80%) and keep one or two contracts as runners. If LHF prints and best case does not, you are still profitable. If best case prints, the runners are a bonus ([2026 #67 · 0:49](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=49), [2026 #67 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=97)).
3. **Build on small, repeatable moves.** He tells newer traders to reject the urge for 500- or 200-handle moves and aim for drawdowns of 20-40 handles that happen dozens of times a day. He says this is not weakness, it is how you build an edge, and you scale up once you have skill ([2026 #105 · 24:24](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=1464), [2026 #105 · 25:15](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=1515)).

He also states a personal floor on index futures. When the run from entry to the nearest low was about 10 handles, he said people do take 10-handle payouts, but he wants 15 at minimum. A further sell-side pool gave about 15, and he would not take the trade if it were under that ([2026 #127 · 55:36](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3336)). That is his number for his stops; yours depends on your stop and your instrument.

### The best case and the terminus

**Terminus** is the word he uses from 2024 on for where you think the trade ends. Frame the start and the terminus before you take anything. Inside the range, look for short-term highs or lows (single or relative equal) and inefficiencies, and choose the easiest one to reach: the top of a gap, its CE or its low. Do not demand the halfway point or the extreme ([2024 #10 · 1:24:29](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5069)).

The terminus is the point where nothing is left on, even if partials came earlier. Have an exit strategy before price arrives, because without one emotions take over and a profitable trade gets lost ([2024 #35 · 33:49](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2029)). The balance of the position needs a stop below the structure, where price should not go unless the idea is wrong.

His example is a long. He planned to start distributing as price engaged the August 22 high ([2024 #35 · 29:50](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1790)). The terminus he named for the trade was the Tuesday noon-hour relative equal highs, about 20,214.75, not the August 22 high, because he had an appointment and could not guarantee price would travel that far. Reaching the August 22 high or beyond would be a "moral victory", not something to hold for ([2024 #35 · 34:36](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2076)). The notes do not give the relative heights of the two levels.

In 2026 he treats the far target as the best case. If you have no way of getting paid before price reaches the terminus, he says, it is very hard to be profitable early in a career ([2026 #30](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM)). Another lecture calls it the ultimate target you add as a final runner exit, one most traders will not reach ([2026 #80 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/bqmqylAuLcI?t=332)). He also posts the terminus level on X before the session, so that nobody can say he cherry-picked it ([2026 #91 · 18:21](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1101)).

### Footholds between you and the goal

In April 2026 he gave a rock-climber picture. The target is the top of the face and price stands somewhere below it. Between them are footholds: FVGs, imbalances, order blocks, equal highs. Draw a line from price to the goal, list the PD arrays in the way, and ask "what is the next foothold?" after each break ([2026 #95 · 33:41](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2021), [2026 #95 · 34:28](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2068)).

Each foothold is a test of intent, and it is also a candidate for a partial. His MNQ example near 26,830 (Monday's RTH gap high) listed the sell-side imbalance, the midpoint of a wick, and the inversion FVG above ([2026 #95 · 35:16](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2116)). For downtrends, reverse it. Chapter 6 covers CE and Chapter 7 covers inversions.

### One measured-move aside

In 2024 he mentioned a classic pattern called three drives, from the book *Street Smarts*, when price pierced the weekly objective three times. His own twist is to measure from the low before the first high to the third swing high. The first target is half of that range, then the low itself ([2024 #7 · 45:01](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2701)).

He does not rely on it. Three higher highs do not guarantee a reversal, and he has seen the pattern mark only an intermediate retracement. He prefers to wait for the next easy draw on liquidity ([2024 #7 · 46:36](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2796)). The notes behind this chapter contain no standard-deviation or fixed-extension projection rules, so targets here are levels that exist, not formulas.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Targeting the lowest low.** The market can deny it, and the easy target is enough ([2022 Ep 2 · 47:49](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2869)).
- **Holding for a publicly shared level.** He did not want to hold for the previous month's high even though he had shared it, because headline-driven moves can reverse it ([2026 #32 · 1:21:25](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=4885)).
- **Skipping the plan for the far target.** Unless the bulk is off at LHF, he says, the best case will torture you ([2026 #67 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=97)).
- **Keeping going after the draw is made.** Once your target is hit or the setup breaks, exit; do not keep running because you got one right ([2026 #108 · 38:19](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2299)).
- **Confusing a terminus with a partial level.** A partial is a place to take some off. The terminus is where the last of it goes.

### How the teaching changed

The near-versus-far idea is constant: closest target in 2022, "Terminus" in 2024, LHF versus best case in 2026. The word Terminus appears in 2024 for a realistic final target. In one 2026 lecture it is the far runner target that most traders will not reach.

The two versions pull in different directions. In 2024 he picks the easiest draw as the terminus and drops a farther high he cannot guarantee ([2024 #35 · 34:36](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=2076)). In 2026 the terminus is the last bit of what you expect, a best case you do not rely on to get paid ([2026 #30](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM)). Both agree on the rule: decide the final exit in advance, and do not make the whole trade depend on reaching it.

There is also a tension on size of target. In 2024 he warns that taking only 5 handles in ES or 10-15 in NQ will stall your growth, and that holding for the daily range is the progression ([2024 #24 · 3:53:14](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=13994)). In 2026 he praises 20-40-handle low-hanging fruit as the base of a sustainable edge ([2026 #105 · 24:24](https://youtu.be/RY67bW2UhxY?t=1464)). The notes read the pair this way: small goals are for beginners, and holding for more is the growth path.

## Partials: getting paid before the terminus

### What it is

A partial is closing part of the position at a planned level. The contracts you keep are the **runners**. ICT has several names for it: funding the position, paying yourself, taking the risk out and, when you feel uneasy, giving yourself the cookie. They all mean the same act: bank some realised profit so the rest of the trade is no longer your only chance to be paid.

### Why it works

ICT's reasons are partly arithmetic and partly psychological.

- **A partial cannot lose.** He says no partial has ever lost money, and there is never an unprofitable partial profit ([2024 #20 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4804), [2026 #91 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1388)).
- **It removes the need to be right.** Take the first partial, move the stop to cover costs, and the trade cannot lose, barring a gap ([2024 #6 · 18:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1089)). In 2026 he adds that even if the last contracts are stopped out and price then runs to target, the trade was still profitable ([2026 #30](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM)).
- **You do not know when it will turn.** That is his basic reason for taking partials at all ([2024 #11 · 2:24:12](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=8652)).
- **Not taking one hurts.** Giving back 60% of open profit with nothing banked is, in his words, uncomfortable ([2024 #11 · 23:36](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1416)).
- **It pays for being early in a career.** If you have no way of getting paid before the terminus, it is very hard to be profitable early on ([2026 #30](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM)).
- **It protects what you have banked.** Once part of the trade is locked in as profit, do not let the unrealised remainder erode. His analogy: you would not work Monday to Wednesday and then do Thursday and Friday for free ([2026 #30 · 28:40](https://youtu.be/F8s_vfK9HaM?t=1720)).
- **It frees the stop to do its job.** A partial is realised profit, so it scratches the itch to move the stop prematurely ([2024 #20 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4804)). We are motivated by reward and fear consequence, so a banked reward makes the next decision calmer ([2026 #19 · 1:12:25](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4345)).

There is also a payoff argument. If you aim for about 5:1 gearing (risk 1 to make 5), you can be wrong a lot. He thinks, but is not sure, that a win rate near 38% still makes money at that ratio ([2024 #20 · 1:41:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6064)). Treat that as his arithmetic, not a promise. The notes flag that this runs against the all-or-nothing stop-or-target mindset.

Finally, a personality point. He says he wrestles with himself in a winning trade, wants additional entries and cannot just hold, so he scales out early. He says moving the stop and checking back later might make more money, but he cannot live with himself in the trade that way. The lesson is about fit, not a recommendation ([2024 #7 · 52:14](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=3134), [2024 #7 · 1:09:12](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=4152)).

### Where to take them

Decide the first partial before you enter. His triggers, roughly from the most mechanical to the most discretionary:

1. **The first short-term high or low past the halfway point.** In a long, if a short-term high sits at least 50% of the way to the target and price takes it out even once, take a partial there. The balance keeps its stop beyond. He presents this as a rule his private students know ([2024 #10 · 2:34:00](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9240)).
2. **Each new low taken (for a short), especially after sell-side liquidity is cleared.** See "Running down equity" below.
3. **A planned level on the way.** Examples are the event horizon, relative equal lows, a minor buy-side pool, the upper quadrant of a gap, the midpoint of a gap price trades through, and the CE of a large wick ([2024 #38 · 2:30:11](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=9011), [2026 #90 · 24:04](https://youtu.be/jz0RbL1oVK8?t=1444)).
4. **Half the gap is filled.** He removes risk there and keeps any open position's stop just above the high ([2026 #12 · 2:42](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=162)).
5. **A level that is only a best case.** After a partial he said it was not high probability that the lows would be taken; it was the best-case scenario. Price took the low out, refused to go lower, and that is why he banked it ([2026 #62 · 3:14](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=194)).
6. **You are close to target and want out.** After a straight run to within 10-15 handles of target, if you catch yourself wishing price would just get to the target, take a partial at once ([2026 #140 · 38:57](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2337)).
7. **A parabolic run.** After about three quarters of the opening range gap in a few minutes, bank something, because a retracement can be deeper than you can hold ([2026 #138 · 1:58:59](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=7139)).
8. **Doubt creeping in.** Take off one or two contracts. His line is that the remedy when you feel uneasy is either to bail completely or take something off ([2026 #60 · 18:31](https://youtu.be/LBrsLPKXZ2E?t=1111)).

Triggers 6 to 8 are partly emotional, and his advice on them differs by year. In 2024 he warns against a partial taken only because you are up $500 and afraid: define the first threshold that must be breached first ([2024 #10 · 2:36:25](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9385)). In 2026 he tells an anxious or inexperienced trader to take a small one as a reward, because it removes the need to be right ([2026 #40 · 36:56](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2216)). A fair reading is to keep your planned partials as the plan and use the small emotional one as a pressure valve.

### Running down equity

On a short, take a small partial each time a new lower low prints. His mechanical version: short 10 lots toward a target 100 handles down; close 1 lot at the first new low, another at the next, and so on. By the time price reaches the target you have taken off 5-6 lots and 4-5 are still running ([2026 #91 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=1388)). The name "running down equity" is his, and in 2024 the caption is unclear on the exact phrase.

He explains it two ways. In 2024 he teaches students to partial on every new low taken because they cannot yet tell high- from low-resistance days (Chapter 16). Even one contract rewards patience and reduces risk. It also fits the liquidity logic: longs have sell stops below that low, and price then tends to wick on through the relative equal lows ([2024 #36 · 1:49:22](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=6562)). In 2026 he says that any time price makes a new low and you have not taken a partial yet, it is a good idea to pay yourself, especially if sell-side liquidity below the low has just been cleared. It matters most while you are developing your model ([2026 #12 · 10:29](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=629)).

Where exactly? In the one-contract drills he lists candidate levels. These are below each prior low as price makes new lows, each time price makes a low, retraces and then goes below it again (even by a tick), the CE of a large wick as price trades down into it, the midpoint of gaps it trades through, and below relative equal lows. In your journal, write "partial here" at the closing price of the level that broke ([2024 #38 · 2:20:36](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=8436), [2024 #38 · 2:30:11](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=9011)).

He says annotating partials trains you to hunt for places to fund the trade, and that partials are how beginners get past fear and greed, since even one contract can feel like risking the mortgage ([2024 #38 · 2:28:37](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=8917)). The ideal reading is speed straight into the next target. A variant for longs, with eight contracts: take one off at each higher high, trail the stop under swing lows "like a noose". He said the rally did not produce enough swing highs to scale out all eight one by one ([2024 #38 · 1:27:23](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5243)).

### Two orders for one idea

A cleaner version of the same principle appears in 2022. With 2% risk you can place two orders on one setup. Order 1 has the same limit and stop and targets 3780.25, about +2.25%. Order 2 targets the short-term high or 3805.50, about 4.5% more. He totals that at about 6.75% from one idea in one day ([2022 Ep 41 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1723), [2022 Ep 41 · 29:33](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1773)). The point is the structure: two exits chosen before the entry, each with its own job.

### Worked example: a five-contract ES long

This is the trade in the ES diagram in the Targets section (2022 Ep 36). The chain of events he narrates on the 1-minute chart: a market structure shift, a rally, a 5-minute FVG, a draw on the morning high, an overshoot into a deep premium FVG, a pullback, a drop into a very good order block, a rally into imbalance, and a final drop that cleared sell-side liquidity. He describes the long as buying those sell stops ([2022 Ep 36 · 2:34](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=154)).

1. **Entry.** Long 5 contracts at 4120 in discount, with about 5 handles of heat (adverse movement) along the way ([2022 Ep 36 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=247)).
2. **Stop.** Below the swing low. It was not hit.
3. **Target.** He tweeted that sell-side liquidity was taken and price was free to run to 4143.50 ([2022 Ep 36 · 6:30](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=390)).
4. **Partial.** He sold 3 contracts at 4139.25, which is 19.25 points above the entry.
5. **Runners.** The last 2 filled by limit at 4143.75, 23.75 points above the entry. Price went a bit higher afterwards ([2022 Ep 36 · 7:17](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=437)).

The shape to copy is that most of the position (3 of 5) came off before the target, and the runners waited in a resting limit just past the tweeted level. The earlier small morning trade in the same lecture used the same partials-plus-limit pattern.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **A partial because you are afraid and up a little.** Without a defined threshold it is just a feeling ([2024 #10 · 2:36:25](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9385)).
- **A partial too close to entry.** In his 2024 long, lower levels were too close to partial at and the move could fail, so he waited for the high end of the upper inefficiency ([2024 #20 · 1:26:34](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=5194)).
- **Partials as a replacement for a stop.** A partial does not remove the hard stop on the rest. Slippage over a news gap can still hurt ([2024 #20 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4804)).
- **Dragging orders live.** While dragging a take-profit up on TradingView he ended up closed out and lost the upside on his other two contracts. He chose not to re-enter. Set partial and stop-move levels before the move and avoid editing orders during volatile candles ([2026 #74 · 5:57](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=357)).
- **Partials versus a trailing exit.** A partial is a planned level. A trailed partial lets the market pick the exit. See the next section.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the partial is a funding step: half off, stop to break-even, a limit for the rest ([2022 Ep 14 · 2:22](https://youtu.be/NUdu1n-ML98?t=142)). In 2024 the partial becomes the centre of the system. It is always profit, it has a first threshold, and it is taken at every new low by students who cannot yet read the day. In 2026 the language is psychological (pay yourself, the cookie) and sizes become explicit: 80% at LHF, 20-30% at the event horizon, one lot per low.

One tension is worth knowing. Taking a partial at each new low is the default he gives students. In a clear low-resistance run (Chapter 16) he says not to rush partials: set a sensible stop, rest limits at the targets and walk away. He still expects a position trader to have about 80% of the position off by the end of the run ([2024 #48 · 1:39:13](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5953), [2024 #48 · 1:36:46](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5806)). A fair reading is that both aim at the same thing: get paid, and do not let a screen scare you out.

## The double tap: head shot and center mass

### What it is

In a September 2026 lecture on an NQ Asian-session long, ICT gave the two-tier exit a shooting-range name. Aim for the best target but take what you can get. The **head shot** is the best-case target, a full hit. The **center mass** is the second tier, the chest hit you settle for ([2026 #4 · 5:10](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=310), [2026 #4 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1392)).

### How to spot it and trade it

1. **List the liquidity pools ahead.** The furthest sits at the top of the list; the middle tier is the center mass.
2. **Plan a partial at each tier.** Exit part of the position at each level to lock in profit and fund the runners. This reduces the need to hit the ultimate target.
3. **Treat it as a split of one trade.** It is a partition of a larger position, not a requirement to reach a single terminus ([2026 #4 · 5:10](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=310)).

In the lecture's example, the first partial came above a level near 709 (center mass). The head shot was the cap of Monday's opening range, and he took further partials at the midpoint of the event horizon, near 725 ([2026 #4 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/7M56e9O06Ko?t=1392)). The notes give only the last digits of those NQ levels. Event horizon is Chapter 11.

The double tap lines up with LHF versus best case in the previous section, and with the 80%-at-LHF rule. ICT does not say they are the same idea; that comparison is mine. What is clear in both is that the near tier takes the larger part of the position and the far tier is optional.

## Moving the stop: trail, cover costs, break-even

### What it is

The initial stop is set by structure. After that, ICT moves it in steps, each tied to something price has proven. The moves have different names. **Rolling** or **trailing** the stop means moving it with the trade (down for a short, up for a long). **Cover costs** means moving it just past entry so commissions are paid. **Break-even** means at entry.

One rule governs all of them: the stop only moves in your favor. Moving it back against the position is bad risk management ([2026 #124 · 9:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=546)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about information and fear. Price must earn the right for the stop to move. In 2022 he calls this having "time and price behind you": just because price moves in your favor does not mean you jam the stop along ([2022 Ep 21 · 37:30](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=2250)). Positions need room to breathe, so he does not trail tight right away.

The later reason is psychological. A stop moved after a partial removes the need to be right about the final target. If it then stops you, the trade has been funded and you were paid for your time ([2026 #19 · 1:11:36](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4296)). Leaving the stop at the original level and ignoring what price does leaves you with a small open profit and a lot of fear. His advice is to take a shot at the target, then protect the exit ([2026 #19 · 1:31:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5475)).

### The 2022 ladder: 50% and 75% of the way

In the final 2022 episode he gave a systematic rule and called it a foundation, not an ironclad law. Adapt it to your personality and model ([2022 Ep 41 · 47:46](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2866)).

[[DIAGRAM ch19_d4 | caption: The 2022 Ep 41 stop ladder on an entry-to-target range of 3754.75 to 3780.25; candles illustrative | The stop does nothing until 50% of the range is covered, trims a little there, and goes to break-even at 75%.]]

1. **Define the range.** Entry to target. His example runs from 3754.75 to 3780.25, which is 25.5 points ([2022 Ep 41 · 45:21](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2721)).
2. **At 50% of the range** (about 12.75 points, rounded to 12.5), the stop may be moved up by 25% of the stop-to-entry distance. It is a trim, not a jam to break-even. The caption is ambiguous between trimming by 25% and moving to 25%; he calls it moving "a little bit" ([2022 Ep 41 · 46:10](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2770)).
3. **At 75% of the range,** move the stop to break-even, and he is emphatic about it ([2022 Ep 41 · 46:57](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2817)).
4. **Throughout, check you are still on the right side.** Is price still reaching for the objective? Do down-closed candles keep supporting it? Does it run short-term lows (sell stops) and rally with energy? Does it drop into FVGs, re-accumulate and send another leg higher? If those hold, together or individually, keep holding for the target ([2022 Ep 41 · 46:57](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2817)).

He also says that in many shown trades he takes partials without rushing his stop up. The ladder is there to build trust in partials and reduce fear of being stopped out.

### Structure-based steps (2026)

By 2026 the steps are tied to price structure, not to a fraction of the range. A typical chain:

1. **Initial stop** at the candle low minus a tick or two.
2. **After adding to the position,** just below the midpoint of the iFVG.
3. **Once the average entry sits at the iFVG high,** at the iFVG low minus a tick. He says this reduces some of the risk.
4. **After a candle closes above a key level** (a wick high, the iFVG), at cover costs. He repeats it after a close above the earlier area.
5. **After a partial,** just below a wick high, which raises the paycheck.
6. **Final stop** just below a wick, since a close below it leaves the swing low as the last defense ([2026 #38 · 0:50](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=50), [2026 #38 · 19:18](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=1158)).

A second rule gates the first roll. In one lecture a candle fell vertically. He took the high of that inefficiency to the volume-imbalance low and called the midpoint a threshold, something to think of as a ladder rather than depth of market. Price must show willingness to lay bodies above it. Only after a candle closes above halfway does he roll the stop up to just below the lows of the decline ([2026 #40 · 7:34](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=454), [2026 #40 · 9:14](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=554)). A later body below the wick's CE is why price turns heavy ([2026 #40 · 21:36](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=1296)).

Other anchors he has used:

- **Just below the half-way point or the entry level** after taking 50% at a minor BSL ([2026 #139 · 14:11](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=851)).
- **A level the partials make safe.** With a large portion of a 20-lot already taken above the daily volume imbalance low, he moves the stop there. The last two contracts can then only stop out at a level that still leaves the trade in profit. He is content to be forced out there, since there is no sense in giving back good money ([2026 #77 · 11:51](https://youtu.be/kzeFqGK9gCQ?t=711)).
- **Above a broken low.** On a short he planned to bring the stop to 29,543 once the low at 29,502 was taken, and waited so a weird pop would not sting him ([2026 #15 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=774)).

### Cover costs, not just break-even

Commission means true break-even sits a little past entry. When price has moved halfway to the first partial, ICT moves the stop to cover commissions and then sits still ([2024 #20 · 1:42:42](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=6162)). He calls a break-even stop the closest thing to Nirvana ([2024 #6 · 45:30](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2730)). In 2024 he sometimes sets it at cost plus about $100, so that the trade pays him for his time ([2024 #49 · 1:54:28](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6868)).

### Never widen it

Once the stop has moved into profit, commit to it. The 2024 version: if you moved it too early, you live with the result and do not widen it back ([2024 #6 · 18:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=1089)). In 2026 he adds that victim thinking comes from moving the stop back and failing to take profit at a higher price. A stop-out after moving into profit means you got paid ([2026 #40 · 42:31](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=2551)).

### Trailing methods

He uses more than one trail. They answer different questions.

| Method | Rule | When he uses it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure ratchet | After a larger shift (a lower low taken out), move the stop to above the matching lower swing high | The 2022 default; if it stops you, you were probably wrong or price is consolidating | [2022 Ep 25 · 44:25](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2665) |
| Swing plus room | On each new swing low (or elongated candle breaking down) move the stop down, leaving about 3 handles of room | Fast, volatile markets; never let the stop go stale | [2026 #140 · 40:32](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2432) |

The dynamic trailed partial deserves a closer look. Short 2 contracts. After price breaks below the target low and builds a short-term low, every down-closed candle that creates an imbalance gets a buy stop for one contract just above its high. As lower candles print, you lower the stop to the previous candle's high. When it triggers, that contract is your partial and the other runs ([2024 #19 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1222)).

It adds no risk, no leverage and no new position. He says never to do it on charts below one minute, because they are spiky and overlap. You will be stopped out very close to the low, so accept exits that feel not good enough ([2024 #19 · 52:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3123), [2024 #19 · 53:39](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3219)). Trailing from larger candles gives back too much range intraday. The notes suggest he finds those better for swing trades held a week, but the reading is slightly uncertain.

The second (October 28) says real management and tight trailing belong to low resistance. Both agree on the high-resistance half: do not strangle price with a tight trail when candles overlap.

### Worked example: rolling a micro stop up

On March 9, 2026, on a micro E-mini S&P long, he placed the stop just below the wick of the iFVG. The lecture mentions a micro assignment with a $50 daily goal ([2026 #124 · 5:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=306)).

1. **Entry,** with the stop at the iFVG low.
2. **Price takes out a higher high.** He rolls the stop to just below that new high. He says the position now risks less than a pizza delivery would cost.
3. **Price takes out another high.** He rolls again, now locking in $50 in his example.
4. **Final exit** at the intraday-high target, about $100 before commissions ([2026 #124 · 2:34](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=154), [2026 #124 · 9:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=546)).

The point is the sequence. His stated goal is to turn a possible loss into a break-even or small profit within seconds of confirmation, and each roll follows a new high that price has already printed.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Jamming the stop to break-even too early.** Price needs room; the ladder gives you 75% of the range before break-even ([2022 Ep 41 · 46:57](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2817)).
- **Rolling before the open.** One minute before the 2026 opening bell he said he did not want to roll; he wanted to see price rip aggressively higher ([2026 #139 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=781)).
- **A stale stop.** Each new unrealised equity high is a prompt to find a way to bring the stop closer to the exit ([2026 #140 · 41:20](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2480)).
- **Trailing on a sub-minute chart.** "Never never" for the dynamic trail ([2024 #19 · 52:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3123)).
- **Confusing cover costs with a partial.** Moving the stop banks nothing. A partial is realised profit and the stop move is a separate step.

### How the teaching changed

2022 uses fractions of the range and break-even, plus the principle of waiting for structure. From 2024 he often says cover costs rather than break-even, and adds the dynamic trail and the regime split. 2026 attaches each step to a candle, wick or gap, and adds the never-stale and never-widen language.

The ladder and 2024 cover-costs rule use different anchors. The ladder works from the entry-to-target range, with break-even at 75%. The 2024 rule works from the first partial, with cover costs at the halfway point to it. He does not say which wins when both apply. In the 2022 episode he calls his rules foundations to adapt.

## Holding trades

### What it is

Holding means staying in after the first partial because you have a reason to expect more. ICT treats it as a skill that develops, not a default. In 2024 he warns that you will stagnate if you only ever take 5 handles in ES or 10-15 in NQ. The progression is holding for the daily range, or into the next day toward algorithmic targets ([2024 #24 · 3:46:06](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=13566), [2024 #24 · 3:53:14](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=13994)).

His own comfort zone is working inside the weekly range. He does not hold over the weekend, because of gap and geopolitical risk. The futures market is only closed from 5:00 to 6:00 PM daily ([2024 #24 · 3:54:03](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=14043)). Judge yourself on proficiency and precision, not windfalls: remove your top two or three windfall wins and see how you do ([2024 #24 · 3:59:47](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=14387)).

### How to spot a trade worth holding

You grow confident in a hold when three things are true. Gaps stay partly open, their CE is not touched, and the PD arrays you expected are not traded to. He still takes stops: "it's not a panacea" ([2024 #11 · 1:05:34](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3934)). The four questions from the 2022 ladder (still reaching, supported by down-closed candles, running stops, re-accumulating) are the same test in a different form ([2022 Ep 41 · 46:57](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2817)).

While in the trade, watch the candles, not the profit figure. In 2022 he was explicit that he was not watching the number under the position, only whether price kept expanding ([2022 Ep 14 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/NUdu1n-ML98?t=48)).

A 2026 example of holding on logic. After price took the ORG low (the RTH opening range gap, Chapter 11) and then the low of the NWOG (the new week opening gap), the relative equal highs above were left intact and longs were holding with stops just below. Think as the market maker: why leave those stops? The expectation is a run for them, "a hot knife through butter", not a bounce. He held the short and kept the stop on the gap side, with a retest of the ORG low expected as a last kiss before lower prices ([2026 #15 · 8:57](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=537), [2026 #15 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=774), [2026 #15 · 17:57](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1077)).

### How to hold

- **Set it and step away in a clean run.** Place the stop at a reasonable level, rest limits at the targets, and go for a 20-30 minute walk or workout. Watching a 15-second chart will scare you out. You get rewarded for staying in longer ([2024 #48 · 1:40:02](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=6002)).
- **Accept being stopped with profit on the table.** He says that to make a large amount you have to risk being stopped out with a large open profit ([2026 #15 · 20:09](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1209)). With a partial banked, he left a remainder working toward a quadruple top and said that if it came back and stopped him out during his cookout, so be it ([2024 #39 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/hfwUXg2nYhs?t=290)).
- **Hold time-limited.** If in profit, he plans to close at about 1:30 PM. Holding through lunch is a later skill ([2024 #11 · 1:05:34](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3934)). A morning-session trader is done after the run, and a position trader should have about 80% off by then ([2024 #48 · 1:36:46](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5806)).
- **Know your gap exposure.** Banked partials protect you only until a gap runs over the stop ([2024 #20 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=4804)). The three-candle trail can also keep overnight or weekend gap risk down, since a Sunday gap can open against you ([2026 #113 · 1:16:55](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=4615)).
- **Let experience teach it.** He says experience will teach you to hold ([2024 #49 · 2:44:59](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=9899)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Holding with nothing banked.** Giving back 60% of open profit with no partial off is the pain he warns about ([2024 #11 · 23:36](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1416)).
- **Holding a thesis that failed.** If a failing narrative could end the trade early, move the stop to a place where a reversal still leaves you something. Know why the trade is still viable, why you hold, and what lets you re-enter after a stop ([2024 #6 · 1:58:54](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=7134)).

## Cutting a trade that is not working

### What it is

ICT has three ways out besides the target: the stop, a close that invalidates the idea, and a discretionary exit when the structure behind the trade stops working. The second and third are what traders skip.

### The close beyond the area

If he were short with the stop at a given area and a candle closes above it, he would kill the trade rather than wait for the stop. He calls this saving the stop. His reasoning is that a close up there means price will likely come back and treat the area as support ([2024 #7 · 1:52:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=6778)).

Wicks are allowed; closes count. He said this on a hypothetical idea at a gap or high, using the same logic as wicks doing the damage while bodies tell the narrative. In 2026 he sets out the same test for a short: if a retracement closes above a wick or array that price should never close above, partially exit or cancel the transaction. The decision comes from information, not panic ([2026 #19 · 1:31:15](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5475)).

[[DIAGRAM ch19_d5 | caption: The questions an open trade answers on each bar | Two things end a trade early: a close that breaks the idea, or the draw being made; everything else is partials and stop moves.]]

### Know when to pull it

A 2026 lecture says it flatly. Once you have your target or the setup breaks (bodies close beyond a key level, a rejection block is breached), you exit. Ask two questions: have you made your draw, and is the setup still valid? If either answer is no, you pull it ([2026 #108 · 38:19](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2299), [2026 #108 · 39:07](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=2347)). That lecture frames it simply: set target and stop before you enter, exit at either, and adjust only by scaling out. The other lectures in this chapter refine that with stop rolls.

Other early-exit signs he describes:

- **Early wicks.** An aggressive trader who sees wicks early (a warning of adverse delivery) can close before the stop. It depends on experience ([2026 #62 · 21:06](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=1266)).
- **Time spent in the gap.** If price lingers in or keeps revisiting the gap before moving your way, drop to the lowest leverage at once: 1 mini or 1 micro. This is risk management, not fear ([2024 #36 · 2:09:16](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=7756)).
- **The supporting structure fails.** On the NFP short he closed manually for about $6,470 (as stated) rather than let the stop be hit. If price then went on to the target, he called that coincidence ([2026 #15 · 21:34](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1294)).
- **A mistaken entry.** If you realise you made a mistake entry, close it and see how you would manage it. That removes the weight of needing it to be right. Do not partially pyramid into a mistake ([2024 #6 · 1:17:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4629)).
- **A wrong-kind-of-day trade.** On a day he already recognises as the wrong kind, a first stop-out is a multiplier of pain, so he takes money off early on such days ([2024 #7 · 1:09:12](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=4152)).

### After the exit

A stopped trade is information. If stopped out, re-enter later rather than strangle price with a tight stop ([2024 #49 · 1:54:28](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6868)). If a stop is hit, the next step is to manage the next trade with less risk to climb out of drawdown step by step ([2022 Ep 41 · 40:34](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2434)). If you were flat and not in the trade, it is fine to sit still and call the week done ([2024 #48 · 1:36:46](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5806)).

### How the teaching changed

2022 says: place the hard stop and let it work. 2024 adds the close-based invalidation and the lowest-leverage rule. 2026 adds the explicit pull-the-plug test and manual exits on structure failure. The 2026 framing of no adjustments mid-trade sits next to his many stop rolls. My reading is that it means no discretionary tinkering beyond planned partials and structural rolls.

## Other management rules

- **One managed position, not many entries.** In a 2026 recording he built a position on an iFVG, lowered the stop above highs to the left, scaled partials at each new sell-side pool or small FVG, and rested a limit just below an SSL. He logged screenshots at every step to show it was one managed position, "not one of 17" ([2026 #71 · 1:42](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=102), [2026 #71 · 4:11](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=251)).
- **Grade the execution.** He checks whether the candle after the entry candle goes meaningfully above it (for a short). Ideally the entry candle is the high and bodies do not go higher. A wick through and then displacement lower is acceptable. It is a grading method, not self-punishment ([2026 #71 · 9:26](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=566)).
- **Log time and distance.** For a hypothetical Friday NQ setup he logged candle 1 at entry, a partial at minute 5, the balance stopped at minute 9, and about 10 handles of distance including spread in and out ([2024 #10 · 2:35:38](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9338)). Chapter 21 covers journaling.
- **Size down on passive days.** With only subtle ideas, use one or two contracts: partial one, let the other run. No maximum leverage and no crazy pyramiding ([2024 #7 · 40:09](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=2409)).
- **Adding.** Averaging matters when you add: your average entry moves, and adding too much early can leave you below the waterline for a while ([2024 #19 · 53:39](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3219)). He would add on an immediate rebalance only in a short, and only if liquidity is untouched ([2024 #11](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE)).
- **Order mechanics.** Decide levels before the move. Do not drag orders in volatile candles. He says TradingView paper-trading fills are poor at the open, and for compliance he cannot give real trade advice, so he demonstrates on paper ([2026 #74 · 5:57](https://youtu.be/k2QLuxMIZio?t=357), [2026 #139 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=898)).
- **Realistic returns.** 1% a day, Monday to Friday, compounds to about 22% in four weeks, not 20%. Fund managers call 12-15% a year stellar. 1% a day is unrealistic for a new trader; for a seasoned one it is an average, so one 5% trade in a week counts as 1% a day for that week ([2022 Ep 41 · 26:21](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1581), [2022 Ep 41 · 27:55](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1675)). Chapter 20 covers risk per trade.

## Answers

1. The middle (imbalance) candle's low minus one tick, or the first candle's low minus one tick as the conservative version. He prefers the middle candle because it keeps the risk manageable. See "Where the stop goes".
2. Take the bulk (about 80%) at the near target and hold 1-2 contracts for the far one. If only the near target prints you are still profitable, and the runners are a bonus if the far target prints. See "Targets: low-hanging fruit".
3. At 50% of the range (about 12.5 points) you may trim the stop a little, about 25% of the stop-to-entry distance. You may not jam it to break-even yet. Break-even comes at 75% of the range. The caption on the 25% is ambiguous, and he calls these foundations, not laws. See "The 2022 ladder".
4. He called 6 or 6-7 good and 8 high, with no right or wrong answer. The other 4 sit through the drawdown, and the process repeats at the next low. See "How much to take".
5. The wick alone does nothing, since wicks are allowed. The close above the area ends the trade: kill it rather than wait for the stop, because price will likely treat the area as support. See "The close beyond the area".
6. Trailing in high resistance is a death knell: overlapping candles run the stop and then the target trades. Set a sensible stop (cost plus a little) and, if stopped out, re-enter later. See "Regime changes how tight you trail".
7. Start only after price has broken the target low and is running. Use the 1-minute chart and place a buy stop for one contract just above the previous down-closed imbalance candle's high, lowering it as lower candles print. Sub-minute charts are spiky and overlapping. See "Trailing methods".
8. Partials happen at planned levels the trade must reach, and the stop moves only after proof. "Cannot lose" holds only after a partial is banked and the stop covers costs, and even then not across a gap. See "Partials: getting paid before the terminus" and "Moving the stop".
9. He says tight stops work in futures because everyone sees one price, and that forex brokers have their own prices.
