ICT treats his method as a skill you build with repetition, not a set of rules you memorise. His practice routine is simple: watch price with no money at risk, write down what you saw as if you had seen it coming, and press a button only after weeks of that. This chapter covers the whole routine: the learning path, tape reading, journaling, backtesting, the drills, chart setup and how to use his videos. By the end you can set up a daily practice with a written record, and you know what he says must be true before you risk money.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch21_d1 | caption: ICT's three learning stages with the durations he gives in 2026 and in the older mentorships | Observation comes first and lasts weeks, paper trading is a second block, and live money comes last at the smallest size.]]

- **Observe before you trade.** For 6 to 8 weeks, about an hour a day, watch live price, say what it should do, and record what it did. No orders, no entries, no stops.
- **Paper trade next, live last.** In 2026 he gives another 6 to 8 weeks of paper trading; the older lectures say at least six months of demo. Live money starts at the smallest size.
- **Journal as if you saw it coming.** Annotate old and live moves in the first person, log the numbers (time, handles, heat), review at the weekend, keep the tone constructive and the journal private.
- **Backtest on your own charts.** Hunt one pattern and mark every instance. Avoid market replay; use recorded live price or delayed live data.
- **Run drills as experiments.** One micro contract in demo, one or two drills a day, and a log of entry time, risk, time to target, drawdown and how you felt.
- **Keep the chart clean.** Levels live on a notepad in raw prices and on separate reference layouts, and one workspace stays blank.

## The learning path: observe, paper trade, then live

### What it is

ICT's path has stages, and he is strict about their order. Backtesting means studying old moves. Walking forward, forward testing and tape reading all mean watching live price without placing orders. Demo or paper trading means orders in a simulated account. Live means real money.

He says there is no deadline on this. Anyone promising to teach the method in a fixed number of days is lying, in his words ([2024 #1 · 39:25](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=2365)). He also says you cannot speed it up: demanding success by a date only lengthens the process ([2026 #13 · 58:08](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=3488)).

His first goal for any student is to learn where price is going and why, which he calls the first "mutant power". Entries, tight stops and risk management come after that, and the draw is the hard part ([2024 #6 · 2:21:36](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=8496), [2024 #3 · 46:06](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2766)). The draw on liquidity is explained in Chapter 3 and the HTF draw in Chapter 15.

### Why ICT says it works

ICT's explanation is about what money does to your attention. With nothing at risk you can focus on price itself. He calls demo "your best friend" while learning: no taxes, no realised losses, and nobody grading you ([2024 #3 · 2:07:59](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=7679)). Real money or a funded account early adds what he calls toxic thinking right before you push the button.

Observation also builds confidence, which he says beginners need most. Confidence comes from seeing your own prediction play out: "I saw something, I observed something and measured its delivery" ([2026 #19 · 1:38:40](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5920)). Watching someone else's trades live is, in his words, entertainment, not training.

He knows this is a hard sell, because new students want to trade at once. His answer is that tape reading is the primary skill and comes first: tape read first, trade later ([2026 #108 · 26:21](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=1581)).

### Readiness for live money

Nobody can tell you that you are ready; he says you will know when paper trading is boring and consistent ([2024 #32 · 56:24](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3384)). His test is behavioural. You are ready when you can sit at a chart without outside influence, see what price will do, know your entry and stop, and feel no emotional charge about the result ([2024 #45 · 1:14:45](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4485)).

You also need a written plan: what you will and will not do, entry trigger, stop, risk, target and partials, all decided before you enter ([2022 Ep 13 · 6:28](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=388)). Before pressing a demo button he wants you able to draw the whole framework on a napkin: buy or sell, initial stop, initial partial and where it ends ([2024 #10 · 3:06:10](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=11170)).

Two warning signs mean you are not ready. If you say "I know if I do X it will do Y", he calls that a tell that you lost the plot, and says to go back to demo ([2024 #49 · 2:18:41](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=8321)). If intraday price action still surprises you, apart from geopolitical shocks, you should not be trading real money.

### Prop-firm evaluations and funded accounts

You are working through an evaluation, so his position matters. He does not ban evaluations, but he puts them after the first two stages. In 2026 he calls funded-account challenges taken early "gambling", because no one gives a final exam on day one and you need experience before you can measure performance ([2026 #19 · 1:14:00](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=4440)). He advises against scraping together challenge fees as a last chance, and against judging progress by funded-account or payout results ([2024 #25 · 42:35](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2555), [2024 #22 · 51:56](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3116)).

There is a nuance. In 2024 he described a path of demo or combine, then a funded account, then withdrawals, then a live brokerage account ([2024 #10 · 42:08](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2528)). He also says he has seen students pass combines and collect payouts with a calm, process-driven approach, which he calls his own unverified observation ([2024 #42 · 1:35:11](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=5711)). Read together: evaluations come later, for people who have done the observation and demo work.

He teaches on a paper account on purpose. He says he is not a licensed advisor, does not tell anyone to trade real money, and that viewers who do so carry the responsibility themselves ([2026 #95 · 8:20](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=500)).

### How the teaching changed

The order never changed: study, observe, demo, live. The minimums did. In 2022 he asked for weeks to months of backtesting ([2022 Ep 2 · 30:51](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1851)), about a year of study ([2022 Ep 7 · 13:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=830)) and six months of demo. In 2024 he added a month-long observation phase and split backtesting (lectures 1 to 3) from walking forward (from lecture 4). In 2026 he fixes 6 to 8 weeks of tape reading and 6 to 8 of paper, repeated in several April videos. He does not say the newer figure replaces the older six months of demo, so the cautious reading is to use the longer one.

## Tape reading

### What it is

ICT defines tape reading as watching each candle form in real time, noting how it respects or breaks key levels, and building a running narrative of where price is likely to go next. It is observation, not trading ([2026 #108 · 26:21](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=1581)). He means visual reading of open, high, low and close. No footprint, order book or number crunching is needed ([2026 #114](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U)).

What you read is order of events. Which liquidity pools were taken, in what order, and which remain? He calls this reading what the market maker is trying to do by watching what it takes out first ([2026 #110 · 2:43](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=163)). Liquidity pools are covered in Chapter 3.

Bodies and wicks carry the message. Small, compressed bodies near a gap's consequent encroachment (CE, its midpoint; Chapter 6) show reluctance. A large wick above a small body shows rejection. Price that cannot reach a level it should reach is telling you it is weak in that direction, and price that arrives quickly and decisively shows strength ([2026 #118 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=438), [2026 #114 · 33:54](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=2034)).

The habit comes from his early years. In his twenties he traded from raw numbers (highest high, lowest low, contracts traded), which taught him that markets keep pressing into liquidity ([2024 #6 · 1:52:37](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6757)). On graph paper he dotted the open and logged the highest high and lowest low every 15 minutes, which made relative equal highs and lows visible ([2024 #13 · 34:49](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=2089)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about how a calm mind stores patterns. Watching candles form without trading conditions you to observe without stress and to recognise patterns by repetition, which he links to the brain's reticular activating system (the effect of noticing your new car everywhere). His condition: do not make it traumatic or forced. Make it relaxed, even with soothing music, because he says patterns are retained in calm, focused states ([2026 #89 · 31:11](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=1871)). He also calls it a form of meditation ([2026 #89 · 39:52](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=2392)).

A second reason is the goal itself. When you tape read, you are not trying to be right or wrong; you are collecting information ([2026 #114 · 47:48](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=2868)). That removes the pressure that distorts what beginners see. A third reason is that live candles flip direction in ways a finished chart never shows, and you need that exposure ([2024 #2 · 1:27:20](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5240)).

It also works as a defence. If a level should be hit and is not, that is feedback: do not trade, or scale out of what you hold. Record the feedback to refine your model ([2026 #114](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U)).

### How to do it

[[DIAGRAM ch21_d4 | caption: the daily tape-reading loop, with the route for people who cannot watch live | Every pass ends in a written record, and the same loop works on a screen recording.]]

1. **Write the levels down first.** Put the pools and PD arrays (the gaps, blocks and other reference levels of Chapters 5 to 10) that price may reach on a notepad in raw prices. Dim levels already used, such as a sell-side level once it has been taken, so you watch only the active ones ([2022 Ep 26 · 7:58](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=478)).
2. **State what you expect.** Say exactly what price should do and nothing ambiguous. You need no demo position for this. Ignore profit and loss, because that is the distraction; look at price ([2022 Ep 26 · 3:19](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=199)).
3. **Watch each candle close and judge it.** Did the body respect the boundary or breach it? Does price reach the level, leave a body or only a wick, move fast or slowly, and how long does it sit in inefficiencies? ([2026 #106](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y), [2026 #114](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U))
4. **Jot timestamped shorthand on paper while it happens.** For example, a time, an abbreviation such as FVG or CE, and what should happen next. Annotate the chart only after the run completes ([2026 #104 · 1:09:42](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=4182)).
5. **Ask the closing question.** Is price doing what I expected, and if not, why not? Then write the answer down ([2026 #89](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak)).

### What to record

His 2024 refinement turns tape reading into measurement. While you watch, state what you expect, then log four things ([2024 #10 · 57:01](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3421)):

- the time you observed the setup
- how long it took to reach the level or close over it, counted in 1-minute candles
- how much heat (adverse movement against you) price produced first
- whether price came back to take the low or high before delivering

Keep a daily tally of opportunities you saw in real time with no help. He says early on it will be near zero, weeks later three or four a session, and by the end of a year many. Correct and incorrect calls drift toward half and half, and after that the failures diminish. Record failures honestly. Do not pretend they never happened because no button was pushed ([2024 #10 · 58:37](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3517)).

An observation, in his sense, is an FVG or other PD array that, if it holds, suggests a draw to a specific level, with an explicit target. For index futures he gives minimum thresholds for study: **5 handles on ES**, **at least 10 handles on NQ (he prefers 15)**, and a range of at least **20 handles** from where the move could begin to where it could draw. If 20 handles are available, capturing 10 to 15 is reasonable, and a trade need not reach the full target ([2024 #10 · 1:14:36](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4476), [2024 #10 · 2:26:41](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=8801)). These are study thresholds, not signals. He gave them for index futures; the notes give no equivalent for gold, so log your own to find one.

The accuracy benchmark differs by year. In 2024 the ratio settles near half and half. In 2026 he asks for consistent accuracy of 60% or better before you move on to paper trading ([2026 #108](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM)).

### Worked example

**2024 #10, NQ 1-minute chart.** An FVG formed at 10:22. Price moved in favour for 9 minutes, until 10:31, but could not get above the high. A stop would have been hit at 10:37. ICT logs this as a failed first data point: the log holds the formation time, the nine favourable minutes and the stop-out time, and the tally counts a miss ([2024 #10 · 1:09:04](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=4144)).

His second example from that lecture is a gap that became an inversion FVG (Chapter 7) and rallied toward the new day opening gap (Chapter 11). Count the candles on the 1-minute chart, note whether there was heat, and note whether price returned to take the low before the run.

**2026 #110, opening-range tape reading (April 7, 2026).** After the opening range gap and the first sweep of liquidity, ICT said he would have preferred a more animated move, meaning deeper penetration of the sell side. He read the shallow dip that followed as incomplete and still expected lower prices ([2026 #110 · 25:54](https://youtu.be/gLt8kAG7noQ?t=1554)). The skill on display is grading how convincing a sweep was.

### If you cannot watch live

Live watching is best. If a job or a time zone prevents it, ICT offers a recorded alternative. Record the screen for about 8 hours, using screen-capture software such as TechSmith Camtasia, and replay it at real speed ([2022 Ep 10 · 31:25](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1885)). By 2026 the routine is more specific: record a 1-minute chart all day, and in the evening replay it, pause, say what should happen, unpause, and do this for the first hour of the day, 9:30 to 10:30 ET ([2026 #19 · 1:47:59](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=6479)).

Do it as if live. Do not look up the daily outcome or other markets first ([2026 #73 · 1:39:35](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=5975)). A clean chart with a 15-second and a 1-minute window works, and when you can, record yourself narrating what price should and should not do, which he calls an audible journal ([2026 #101 · 1:45:36](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6336)).

Delayed data is a second option. In 2024 he had his son watch delayed data while he called trades live, which gives all the learning with no hindsight advantage and no risk ([2024 #2 · 44:44](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2684)). His own 2024 capture was paused over the 5:00 to 6:00 PM ET daily break, and about 18 hours of live data played back in under nine minutes, with a running clock in the corner to show it was live data and not replay ([2024 #23 · 4:00](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=240)).

### Live data versus market replay

His objection to market replay is practical. Replay shows one settled tick at a time, which gives a false sense of how candles form, and you already know the outcome ([2024 #25 · 40:58](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2458), [2024 #7 · 1:14:52](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=4492)). In TradingView, he says, replay also hides execution boxes and time stamps ([2026 #89 · 7:50](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=470)). A real execution marker (the green dots he shows) stays when you switch from replay to live, while a replay-only trade vanishes ([2026 #100 · 7:34](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=454)), so a replay screenshot cannot prove a fill. The full change in his position is in the next section.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Tape reading is not copy-trading.** Watching his trades live is entertainment. Measure your own reading against his calls, but do the marking on your own chart ([2026 #131 · 36:11](https://youtu.be/V4KPpX-CWXk?t=2171)).
- **Do not tie observation to profit.** When you see price deliver, the right reaction is "that's neat", not "I wish I had shorted". Do not link it to evaluations or payouts.
- **Do not stare aimlessly.** Study with context and boundaries ([2026 #99 · 56:53](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3413)).
- **Do not trade every session you watch.** He says not to trade all four sessions "hog wild", while tape reading all of them builds skill ([2024 #47 · 15:00](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=900)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 tape reading was months of studying price without demo trades or target picking ([2022 Ep 10 · 31:25](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1885)); profits without it, he said, are circumstance or luck ([2022 Ep 19 · 42:57](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2577)). In 2024 it became walking forward, with measurement: time observed, time to deliver, heat, the tally. In 2026 it is a time-boxed first phase, called meditation, read through bodies and willingness.

## Journaling

### What it is

ICT uses three records, and the notes show them being used together. The first is a physical notepad for the levels you will watch and for real-time notes. The second is the study journal: annotated screenshots of old moves, observations and trades. The third is a record of your own emotional state, which can sit in the study journal or in a separate document ([2026 #104 · 1:09:42](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=4182)).

The format is up to you. He keeps handwritten leather journals and dislikes electronic storage ([2024 #18 · 3:06:52](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11212)), but a TradingView workspace works too, and he writes his first-FVG levels by hand ([2026 #141 · 15:14](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=914)). He suggests a binder or a digital section for "price action drills" ([2024 #38 · 23:41](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=1421)).

He also says the journal becomes the best technical analysis book you will own, so stop buying books, apart from those in his library video ([2022 Ep 18 · 58:47](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=3527)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is pseudo-experience. If you write each annotation as though you saw the move coming, the subconscious stores it like a memory. Reading those notes again primes you to notice the same pattern live, in the way you see your new car everywhere once you own it. He calls this the reticular activating system at work ([2022 Ep 27 · 17:39](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=1059), [2026 #87 · 1:33:23](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=5603)).

The comfort matters to him as much as the memory. In his view the subconscious wants comfort, so confident, kind notes build confidence and shorten the time to usable experience ([2026 #10 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=682)). He says he borrowed the idea from coping techniques for anxiety ([2022 Ep 27 · 18:26](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=1106)).

It is also a safety net. A journal of past wins is your counselor when a setup fails in your hands, and in a drawdown you can reread it for encouragement ([2024 #3 · 2:10:22](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=7822), [2022 Ep 16 · 40:46](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2446)). He says it takes weeks to months, certainly by the first year, and that you will do this or you will fail ([2022 Ep 37 · 20:41](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=1241)).

### How to word it

The wording is the unusual part. ICT wants first-person notes in the past tense, written as if you expected the move. His examples are warm: "I love how..." and "I was pleased to see...", or excitement that these opportunities keep repeating ([2026 #10 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=682), [2024 #18 · 1:48:46](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=6526)). Use comparative language too, such as this low versus that low ([2022 Ep 38 · 41:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2469)).

Keep the notes factual and kind. Never write that you wish you had seen it, that you will never get this, or that you were stupid ([2022 Ep 37 · 20:41](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=1241), [2022 Ep 16 · 41:32](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2492)). He asks you to treat your future self as a child you are raising into an adult trader ([2026 #10 · 23:13](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1393)). Do not over-praise either, and keep ego statements out ([2024 #22 · 53:32](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3212)).

| Instead of | Write something like |
|---|---|
| I was wrong, the market didn't do what I expected | I was looking for price to do X, but price was unwilling; here is what it did instead ([2026 #95 · 50:53](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=3053), [36:51](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2211)) |
| I wish I had shorted here and held | I saw this setting up and wanted to see displacement; it felt good to see it deliver from this FVG to that pool during these hours, as my model says ([2022 Ep 27 · 15:16](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=916), [2022 Ep 19 · 1:55:11](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6911)) |
| I lost a huge amount, I blew it | Record what you should have seen, as if you did it that way ([2026 #10 · 12:08](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=728)) |

### Honest, not toxic

His advice has two halves that look like they pull against each other, so state both. The positive-only half says even when you lose real money, record what you should have seen, and never log a dollar loss like "I lost $25,000" ([2026 #10 · 12:08](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=728)). The notes flag this as a controversial recommendation that runs against the usual advice to journal your mistakes.

The honest half says log failures as data. Record failures and study them ([2024 #10 · 58:37](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=3517)). After a trade, note what you did right first, then what could be done differently, with no armchair quarterbacking, no ego inflation and no self-flagellation ([2026 #129 · 39:56](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2396)). Keep a list of where accounts were blown or tilt began, and write protocols for them ([2024 #6 · 1:00:49](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3649)). He marked his bad days with an X in the corner of the page so he could find them ([2024 #40 · 1:17:14](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=4634)).

The way to hold both is to separate the data from the voice. Numbers, failures and emotional states go in honestly. The voice stays constructive, because he says harsh journaling left him with scar tissue that resurfaced in later trades, and his own self-punishment in his twenties cost him about six years ([2024 #2 · 21:26](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=1286), [2024 #26 · 1:16:46](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=4606)). His 2026 reflective debrief is the practical form: after execution, ask what could be improved and how well price followed your logic ([2026 #87](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU)).

### Recording your emotions

In the study journal he wants your emotional state: confident or uncertain, and did you expect it to work? In a drawdown you reread how you felt in the winning periods ([2026 #104 · 1:12:51](https://youtu.be/Il_DM4T7nzg?t=4371)). Do it for a full month of daily practice ([2024 #29 · 1:07:43](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4063)).

His anxiety journal is the concrete version. When you feel impulsive or anxious, screenshot the chart and mark it "anxious". At session end, check what price did. Often what you feared missing was never there ([2024 #25 · 19:46](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1186)). The wider psychology is in Chapter 22.

### The weekly review

The review is where the effect is built. On Saturday and Sunday you are not hunting for setups; you reread your own annotated screenshots ([2024 #18](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI)). In 2022 he suggested reviewing Monday to Friday plus the previous week ([2022 Ep 10 · 1:01:53](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=3713)), and checking intraday days against the daily and weekly charts: when the weekly high or low formed, the biggest day, pips moved, risk taken ([2022 Ep 18 · 53:14](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=3194)).

In 2024 his son's daily deliverable was a 1-minute chart (15-minute where relevant) with relative equal highs and lows drawn as lines, inefficiencies highlighted and short positive notes, with 10 charts (two weeks) brought to the weekend review ([2024 #2 · 1:45:08](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6308), [1:58:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7093)). In 2026 he adds one weekday a week of about 30 minutes on the daily chart ([2026 #41 · 6:31](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=391)). Do not replay the trade in your head afterward ([2026 #140 · 43:45](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=2625)).

### Worked example: a trade write-up

**NQ 1-minute, August 7, 2024 (demo, shown in the 2024 #3 lecture).** This is the shape of a good write-up: narrative first, then levels, entries and management. There was no news that morning, and the 1:00 PM bond auction was the driver. The new day opening gap sat below. Sell-side liquidity sat at the day-session low and at the London relative equal lows. The morning relative equal highs had already been run.

At 12:42 price rolled over, left an FVG and displaced lower. It retraced into a SIBI (a bearish fair value gap; Chapter 5) and fell short of its CE, so that candle became a bearish order block (Chapter 9). He shorted 6 contracts at 18,321.25. A big down-close candle was followed by an immediate rebalance, and he added 4 contracts at 18,291.25. He moved the stop to his first entry minus 2 ticks and relaxed ([2024 #3 · 1:50:23](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6623), [1:51:12](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6672)).

At 1:00 PM the auction candle pumped and then dumped. The next retrace stopped at the edge of the FVG: its high was 18,283.75 against an FVG candle low of 18,283.50. A limit order just below the London low then filled, and he took off all 10 contracts, which he counts as over 100 handles on the 6-contract portion. The exit price in the caption is unclear ([2024 #3 · 1:59:17](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=7157)).

The lesson he draws is about narrative. Traders on the 1-minute chart read the 1:00 pump as a failure swing and lost the plot, because the draw was the London lows and the new day opening gap. A write-up that names the draw first teaches that.

### Keep it private

He says not to share predictions on social media. Doing so raises your need to be right and to save face, and being wrong then costs you emotionally ([2024 #21 · 41:18](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2478)). Do not let others into your journal or discuss your learning with doubters, who will not offer positive self-talk ([2024 #18](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI)). Celebrate milestones in the journal, and share with a fellow student rather than a partner ([2024 #22 · 20:35](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1235)).

That is proof for an established teacher. His advice to students is to build the model in private first, because criticism from people who do not know your model can derail you ([2026 #95 · 50:53](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=3053), [36:51](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=2211)).

## Backtesting properly

### What it is

For ICT, backtesting means going back through old price and marking up the moves, so you learn to see them before they finish. In 2022 he described it as using a bar replay or plain hindsight to fill the empty space on a chart with commentary ([2022 Ep 27 · 14:30](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=870)). Doing it properly means three things: hunt one named pattern, mark it on your own charts, and measure what happened.

His image is the hunter reading tracks. You are shown once what a deer print looks like, then you go find it yourself. You decide what you are hunting, such as order blocks, FVGs that invert, breakers, the institutional order flow entry drill (IOFED, Chapter 18) or OTE (Chapter 4), and mark every instance ([2022 Ep 3 · 36:52](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2212), [2024 #18 · 1:48:46](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=6526)).

### Why it works

ICT gives four reasons.

- **Volume is the secret.** He says the amount of backtesting and logging is what separates people, and that most claim to do it but do not ([2022 Ep 3 · 40:01](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2401)). Price teaches in a way books cannot, since books only show handpicked examples ([2022 Ep 13 · 44:37](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2677)).
- **Trust has to be earned.** It comes from classifying swing highs and lows yourself, not from hindsight explanations, and it cannot be transferred by watching someone else ([2022 Ep 11 · 16:52](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=1012), [2022 Ep 12 · 4:06](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=246)).
- **Hindsight is a legitimate way to learn.** He compares it to surgeons studying medical journals, and says you cannot get hurt studying moves that already happened ([2022 Ep 18 · 54:01](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=3241)). In 2026 he adds that hindsight is only criticism if you stop there ([2026 #140 · 3:10](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=190)).
- **Holding the real thing teaches you to spot fakes.** His jade parable says that after handling real old data you recognise false moves live without help ([2026 #137 · 53:34](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=3214)).

It also builds independence. He wants you able to work without his videos, which is why he says to study old data as a routine ([2026 #140 · 17:40](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1060)).

### How to do it

1. **Pick one thing to hunt.** You need only one setup: choose the one that makes most sense to you and log it ([2024 #4 · 2:12:59](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=7979)). In 2024 he chose FVGs for his son because they are visually obvious, and told students to choose what jumps off the chart for them ([2024 #7 · 2:13:45](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8025)).
2. **Use your own chart.** Highlight and draw the gaps and levels yourself. If you only take his and agree, you are not building the skill ([2026 #26 · 15:21](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=921)).
3. **Go through old days in order and mark every instance,** including the ones that failed. Leave empty chart space and type the logic into it ([2022 Ep 16 · 38:21](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2301)).
4. **Write the notes in the first person,** as if you expected the move (see Journaling above).
5. **Measure each one:** points or handles moved, candles to the target or CE, time, and the drawdown before it worked ([2024 #18 · 1:49:38](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=6578)).
6. **Screenshot, file, review at the weekend.** Reread weekly ([2022 Ep 27 · 15:16](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=916)).
7. **Then walk forward.** Journal old moves to build pseudo-experience, then watch live with the hard right edge, where the next candle has not printed yet ([2024 #6 · 58:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3489)).

Once a week, put aside about 30 minutes, play music, and study old moves on the daily chart. He says the daily chart is where the information comes from, because it caps the daily high and low; he wants session highs and lows (such as the 7:00 to 9:00 AM window) and daily and weekly extremes logged as mechanisms of how the market books price ([2026 #41 · 6:31](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=391), [7:18](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=438)).

You need real-time data for intraday work. ### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Counting only the winners.** Mark every instance and let failures into the tally. Classifying is what builds trust ([2022 Ep 11 · 16:52](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=1012)).
- **Stopping at hindsight.** The point is to move to the right edge and test live.
- **Using his charts.** Model each example on your own charts and study why each reference point mattered ([2024 #18 · 1:37:35](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5855)).
- **Crunching numbers with no context,** or blending a new idea in too soon. Treat it as a side study and collect data first ([2026 #126 · 1:04:42](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3882), [2026 #113 · 1:24:00](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=5040)).

## The drills

### What they are for

A drill is practice, not a test. If you get an adverse result, he says not to beat yourself up ([2024 #28 · 12:08](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=728)). The goal is baseline data on how you behave when you are wrong or when a trade runs away, and he says not to hide from what it shows ([2024 #29 · 1:19:02](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4742)). Demo without a process anchors you to the outcome, so follow a model with measurable past statistics ([2024 #38 · 23:41](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=1421)).

Run drills in demo with one micro contract. The point is process and desensitisation, not money ([2024 #28 · 37:41](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2261)). The entry mechanics, the IOFED and the Volatility Pinball rules are in Chapter 18; this section covers how to use them as practice.

### Entry drills

In the 2024 FVG drills you engage a gap in demo, set the risk with the candle-2 stop and the target at the nearest liquidity, then log time of entry, whether you were stopped and when, drawdown and time to target ([2024 #28 · 1:42:26](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=6146)). Practise with market orders in paper at first, to feel what engaging price is like; limit orders for tight entries come later ([2024 #32 · 54:49](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3289)). One or two a day is fine, like an exercise routine ([2024 #29 · 1:15:01](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4501)).

The Volatility Pinball drill is the high-volume version. Take every FVG and run for the nearby high or relative equal low, all session, until you have 10 to 15 trades. He cites roughly 38% on a $10,000 account with one contract; treat that as his claim ([2024 #42 · 45:32](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=2732)). He is clear that the drill is exposure training. There are always about 1,000 handles of NQ movement available, so activity is never scarce, but high-probability, low-risk setups are, so be selective when you trade for real. Accept that you will sometimes miss a move ([2024 #42 · 47:56](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=2876), [48:44](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=2924)).

Do some drills on hard days. A very hard day (a 9 out of 10 for new students) gives ideal baseline experience, and he likens it to leg day ([2026 #101 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=781)). He also says to learn the high-resistance conditions first, so that easy ones become recognisable (Chapter 16) ([2024 #27 · 1:12:30](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=4350)). If you have already made a profit that day, do not trade live; go to demo and drill instead ([2026 #101 · 5:07](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=307)).

### Small experiments

His micro-contract experiment sets a low bar on purpose: one micro, a target of $75 to $100 a day, and stop when you hit it. Do this for a couple of weeks while you only backtest and tape read, then press the button on a single micro two to three weeks later. Log how it feels, including regret, fear and excitement, and later test holding past the target as an experiment ([2024 #31 · 1:23:11](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4991), [23:23](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1403)).

Two daily drills need no orders. Each hour, scribble the level you think is the draw for this hour and the next, and score yourself; it shows whether you are in sync with the market ([2026 #129 · 45:28](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=2728)). And each day, review the chart after the fact with a 20-handle box to see what was available ([2024 #31](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk)).

Narrate, too. Say your internal dialogue out loud, including if-then thinking ([2026 #99 · 40:42](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=2442), [51:20](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3080)). Keep it enjoyable, like a word search; if it becomes a chore, the money will not keep you at it ([2026 #99 · 52:08](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3128)).

### Drilling without a bias

The notes are thin here, so this is what they support. His beginner exercise removes the entry: show up every day, do the same thing, pick the direction and not the entry, then study the PD arrays in hindsight ([2026 #136 · 1:26:00](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=5160)). If you cannot frame both sides, which means price could go higher or lower, turn the chart off; he calls one-sided conditions the high-probability ones ([2026 #71 · 47:36](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2856)).

On uncertain days such as FOMC, he does not come in with a hard bias. Observe, tape read, and let price guide you; take only the cleanest micro-trades if you participate at all ([2026 #89](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak)). After time away, start from the daily chart, identify draws and inefficiencies, then form a bias ([2026 #130](https://youtu.be/z5ytUvVeUoQ)). Or tape read only for a few days to get back in sync ([2026 #112](https://youtu.be/m_ND0oO67cU)). In the lecture titled for this problem (2026 #134), the notes record only that he used a demo account, not replay or a recording, and would use real money only if the range broke or the market improved ([2026 #134](https://youtu.be/zyKAPNk_prA)).

### The 15-second chart

ICT treats small charts as a shooting range. Each shot is a repetition, and the logic is the same as on the daily chart, but feedback arrives in minutes instead of weeks ([2026 #72 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=1713)). Small runs on 15 and 30-second charts are valid study material ([2026 #95](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk)). Record a clean chart with a 15-second and a 1-minute window if you cannot sit at it ([2026 #101 · 1:45:36](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=6336)).

Keep three cautions in mind. Do not try to trade everything on a 15-second chart; know your catalyst, what makes price turn ([2024 #26 · 33:22](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=2002)). Smaller timeframes are not faster ([2026 #73 · 1:10:12](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=4212)). He calls 15 seconds the harder timeframe, and says that live you must analyse and act at once, with no replaying ([2026 #23](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8)). Chapter 18 has the mechanics.

### Demo hygiene

- **Use realistic size.** Over-levered paper trades condition you to ignore drawdown, and 50-lot screenshot trades teach nothing ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:54:24](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6864), [2024 #10 · 1:28:42](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=5322)).
- **Do not reset an account to erase a bad run.** Flaws are easiest to fix early ([2026 #101 · 2:07:00](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=7620)).
- **Practise drawdown on demo.** Go into it deliberately and build it back so a negative balance stops frightening you. Never on a live account ([2026 #127 · 23:46](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1426)).
- **Expect platform faults.** If a paper stop or limit does not fill, close honestly or screenshot what would have happened; on a live account, verify every fill ([2026 #101 · 30:32](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=1832), [2026 #139 · 14:58](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=898)).

## Chart setup and the naked chart

### What it is

ICT separates the chart you operate on from the charts you refer to. The operating chart is a "naked" working layout, the one he teaches and executes from. Beside it are saved layouts for specific lists of levels, and a notepad holds the numbers you only need once ([2026 #19 · 18:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1091)). The idea is to avoid clutter and overload: each extra line is something to babysit or explain.

### Layouts and workspaces

Keep reference levels in their own layouts and toggle between them instead of loading everything on one chart. His examples are new-week opening gaps, new-day opening gaps, first presented FVGs, and a layout holding only RTH opening range gaps with a running tally ([2026 #19 · 18:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1091)). In 2026 he adds separate workspaces per timeframe with graded levels, including turtle soup highs and lows and suspension block quadrants. Without them, he says, moves will surprise you ([2026 #140 · 17:40](https://youtu.be/1X0A-Lgf9RY?t=1060)). Opening gaps are Chapter 11.

Bring onto the trading chart only the levels in close proximity to price ([2024 #4 · 41:58](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=2518)). A second monitor helps; with one screen you juggle more. Turn off daily levels while you scalp the 1-minute, or clutter hides structure ([2026 #65](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g)).

Review on several chart scales (daily, 4-hour, 1-hour, 30-minute) to find session boundaries and key zones ([2026 #5](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w)). Transpose the logic from the daily chart down to the 1-minute ([2026 #29 · 32:39](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=1959)).

### Labels, colours and line weight

These conventions all come from one 2026 lecture ([2026 #19](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y)).

- **Right-justify higher-timeframe labels.** A left-justified label on a daily chart sits far off-screen on the 15-minute, so you see a price on the axis and cannot tell what it is ([26:14](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1574)).
- **Anchor lines to the right and extend them right.** Otherwise they vanish as new data prints. Save the layout after editing.
- **Put minor intraday labels just above their anchor,** not right-justified, so you find them by compressing the time axis.
- **Use fixed colours.** Buy-side liquidity in blue and sell-side liquidity in deep red, every time, so viewers know what is highlighted. Tiny liquidity levels can be short trend lines.
- **Weight lines by timeframe.** Higher-timeframe anchors are thicker, but not the thickest: a daily anchor sits one notch below the maximum, and weekly follows the same logic ([33:09](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=1989)).
- **Name the context in the label,** for example "daily SIBI," so you stay framed.
- **Remove tools you do not use,** such as high-low or midpoint lines.

Other rules apply while you learn. Use rectangles only to highlight imbalances, and no indicators ([2022 Ep 18 · 25:34](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1534)). He says indicators only compress old moves mathematically, and that volume profiles and footprint heatmaps are gimmicks, so use open, high, low and close ([2024 #6 · 1:48:10](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6490), [2026 #66](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE)). After a level is swept, fade it and focus on the next one ([2024 #1](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI)). If a chart has more than a few zones you are overcomplicating it ([2026 #68](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE)). Leave empty room on the right so you can form an idea without insisting on it ([2026 #38 · 4:05](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=245)).

### The notepad and the naked chart

[[DIAGRAM ch21_d3 | caption: a notepad of raw levels beside a naked chart, read by the price axis (illustrative numbers) | You look at the number first, find where price sits against your notes, and the chart carries no drawings.]]

His method from 2024 is to keep the chart naked and write the levels you are watching on a notepad in raw price form. You then measure between what price is doing and what it can do by reading the price axis against your notes. Levels appear on the chart only when price presents and books there ([2024 #21 · 23:31](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1411)). He draws levels on charts in lectures only for teaching. A chart with every level and no narrative is confusing, and lets critics say it will hit one of them ([2024 #21 · 21:56](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1316)).

For each level ask what a good reaction would be. If it is a premium level, does it repel price lower or stop it going higher? If a discount level, does it hold price up or get smashed through easily? Pair this with an easy narrative, meaning the time of day and the draw on liquidity, and let setups against your bias pass ([2024 #21 · 19:05](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1145)).

What goes on the pad in 2026: today's RTH gap high and low, yesterday's, Friday's, and key daily wicks ([2026 #94 · 8:36](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=516)). Glance at the pad, find the current price on the axis, and ask where you are relative to the levels. Do not look at the chart first and ask what to do; look at the number ([2026 #94 · 10:11](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=611)). In 2024 his own pad was a 9 by 5 yellow pad: the date, a sell or buy program, and each PD array with its price and time ([2024 #8 · 3:37:24](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=13044)). He claims that starting the day with no idea what to expect leaves you about 80% likely to finish unprofitable ([2024 #8 · 3:35:00](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=12900)). He adds that he does not have to trade today.

### Why not pre-commit levels

In 2026 he asks for at least one workspace that is completely blank. Marking a level before the session commits you to waiting for it and blinds you to other opportunities, especially while you lack the experience to see when you are wrong. Mark up afterwards, in the journal. At the Sunday open (6:00 PM) he starts naked ([2026 #138 · 36:54](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2214)). In 2022 he made the same case: heavy annotation locks you into "your will be done" ([2022 Ep 3 · 31:15](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1875)), and labels while trading are mental, not drawn ([2022 Ep 12 · 13:46](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=826)).

The pad and the blank chart are not in conflict. The pad holds levels as numbers, and the chart stays free of drawings that would steer your attention. The one place he keeps higher-timeframe levels on the chart as well is when you check whether price is marching toward the daily and weekly objectives ([2024 #12 · 26:21](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1581)).

### From annotated to naked

Learn with annotations, then reduce them. While learning, keep levels annotated to train your eye; once experienced, keep the chart clean and the levels on a pad, so you do not force a bias and can see when the premise is negated ([2022 Ep 17 · 2:39](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=159)). In 2026 he shows a naked ES 1-minute chart, asks the audience to call the turning points, then restores the annotations. The path is annotated charts, then notepad plus clean chart, then naked, and he says not to skip steps ([2026 #94 · 13:24](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=804), [14:58](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=898)).
