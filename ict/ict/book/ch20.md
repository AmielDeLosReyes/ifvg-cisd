ICT treats risk as arithmetic you finish before you click: a fixed percentage of equity, a stop that comes from the setup, and a contract small enough that the first two fit. This chapter covers that arithmetic, his rule for cutting risk after a loss, the one-micro, one-trade-a-day ladder he gives beginners, and the instrument side: contract values, futures against CFDs, contract months and the continuous chart. After reading you can size a trade on MNQ, know which of his numbers belong to which instrument, and judge his compounding and prop-firm remarks for what they are.

One warning before any number. ICT taught sizing on CME index futures: ES (E-mini S&P), NQ (E-mini Nasdaq) and their micro versions MES and MNQ. Every dollar figure here is for those contracts. In these notes he gave no tick value, lot size or risk figure for gold (XAUUSD), and the one forex-style number is a 30-pip stop in a coin-flip demonstration. If you size a gold CFD, keep his method and swap in your broker's dollar value per point.

## Contracts: points, ticks and dollars

### What it is

ICT counts distance in handles, which means index points. One handle is one point, one full-figure move, and four ticks. His first example is the S&P: a move from 4567 to 4568 is one handle ([2022 Ep 1 · 24:11](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1451), [2026 #18 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4426)).

The dollar value of that handle depends on the contract. The mini is the full-size contract. The micro is a smaller version of the same index, so the chart looks the same and only the dollars change.

In 2022 he put the margin for one ES contract at roughly $12,000 to $13,000, a loose estimate said on the fly ([2022 Ep 1 · 24:58](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1498)). Micros need far less, so you can start small and move up over time.

### Why micros

ICT's reasons, in the order he gave them:

1. **Margin.** If the margin on ES keeps you out, trade the micro and move up later ([2022 Ep 1 · 24:58](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1498)).
2. **Forgiveness.** On a side-by-side of the December micro and mini NQ charts he calls the micro "very, very forgiving". You can use wider stops and need less precision. With the mini you must be more precise ([2024 #31 · 19:15](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1155)). The two charts barely differ: the gap midpoint read 19844.5 on the micro and 19843.5 on the mini.
3. **Safety.** He teaches micros so that if you do something wrong in your own hands you do not hurt yourself catastrophically ([2026 #114 · 51:23](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=3083)).
4. **Volatility.** In 2026 he repeats that the market is too volatile for minis. He shows one to three micros "at most" on his broadcasts and calls 30 to 100 lots on minis ridiculous at the moment ([2026 #133 · 12:55](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=775), [2026 #132 · 17:55](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1075)).

The ladder upward is slow. Keep records in micro terms and move to minis only when the volatility no longer bothers you ([2026 #18 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4426)). Do not rush from one micro to the mini ([2026 #120 · 12:47](https://youtu.be/sPKglrJAVFY?t=767)). Add a second micro only when you can afford it ([2026 #121 · 21:43](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=1303)). If your size makes the stop too big, drop to micros. If you cannot accept micro-sized profits, he says you are gambling ([2026 #19 · 1:57:44](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=7064)).

### NQ or ES

Both are index futures, and he has said different things about which to trade.

- **2024: NQ for movement.** He prefers NQ for its volatility and more exaggerated delivery. NQ holds 100 stocks and ES holds 500, and he needs movement. ES is slower and more rounded ([2024 #45 · 26:03](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1563)).
- **2026: choose by personality.** ES is the easier market for learning precision, and NQ suits those who hunt volatility ([2026 #35 · 6:25](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=385)). Chapter 1 sets this beside his remarks on ES body precision.
- **Start with one.** Begin with NQ and do not switch between NQ and ES when the two disagree ([2024 #37 · 28:39](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1719)).
- **Use the third average as a check.** He looks at the Dow (YM), ES and NQ together. If all three move higher, he treats a bullish gap sending price up as a no-brainer. If they disagree, expect trouble ([2026 #69 · 1:35](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=95), [2026 #106 · 12:06](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=726)). Chapter 15 covers SMT and intermarket checks.

### Mapping to gold

None of the contract values above belong to gold. ICT said what he shows works in forex, gold, commodities and bonds, and that he sticks to one market so he can teach economically ([2026 #32 · 4:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=267)). That is a statement about the method, not about contract sizes.

For an XAUUSD CFD, find the dollar value of one point (and one tick) in your broker's contract specification, then use the sizing steps in the next section. That instruction is mine, not ICT's. The stop and the percentage carry across. The point counts do not: an ES stop of 9 points says nothing about a sensible gold stop.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Handle is not dollars.** A handle is a point of the index. Its dollar value is set by the contract.
- **Micro Nasdaq is $2, not $5.** The 2022 introduction note groups "micro Nasdaq" with MES at $5 a handle. His explicit later figures give MNQ $2 a point ([2022 Ep 13 · 11:16](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=676), [2026 #18 · 1:14:36](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4476)). Use $2.
- **Same setup, different dollars.** A 20-point move pays $40 on MNQ and $400 on NQ (arithmetic from the table). Judge a trade by points first, then by dollars.
- **The micro is not a different market.** Price on the micro and the mini was nearly identical in his side-by-side. The difference is the cost of being wrong.

## Percent risk and position size

### What it is

Percent risk means you decide how much of your account you may lose on one trade, as a share of current equity. ICT says to think in fixed-percentage risk of equity, not in a dollar amount per trade ([2022 Ep 20 · 15:02](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=902)). As equity grows, the contract count grows while the percentage stays the same ([2024 #45 · 1:22:00](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=4920)).

He gave several percentage figures over the years. They are not one rule. They apply to different people and situations.

| Situation | Risk he gave | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A student who does not yet know what they are doing | Under 1%, about 0.5% or 0.25% | [2022 Ep 7 · 4:11](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=251) |
| The 2022 finale's working rule | 1% per trade, with the halving rule after a loss | [2022 Ep 41 · 31:07](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1867) |
| Later, with experience | 2% is possible, and he treated it as a goal, not a start | [2022 Ep 41 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1723) |
| 2024 lecture rule | Never more than 1% risk, never more than one trade a day, stop after a loss | [2024 #6 · 2:34:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9249) |
| Coin-flip demo on a forex pair (30-pip stop) | At most 0.5%, aiming for 1% | [2022 Ep 19 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2057) |
| Funded-account micro drill | $50 or less to make $100 | [2024 #31 · 31:23](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1883) |
| ICT's own, in a trade with the bias | 3.5% preferred, 4.5% his maximum. He says not to copy it | [2022 Ep 7 · 4:11](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=251), [2022 Ep 7 · 9:50](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=590) |

The last row appears again in Chapter 15, where the size follows the bias. Treat it as his personal ceiling, not as a beginner's rule.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that every trade starts as a loser. Commission and spread are paid the moment you enter. A trader, in his words, manages losing trades and turns some of them into winners ([2022 Ep 10 · 1:03:29](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=3809)). A stop-out is therefore a cost of business, not proof that the model is broken ([2022 Ep 12 · 11:25](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=685)).

Losses are certain, so size must make a full stop-out unremarkable. His test: count the opposite candles in a swing. A bullish run has only a couple of down candles. If a loss on each of those days would blow your account, you are over-leveraged or over-trading ([2022 Ep 10 · 1:04:17](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=3857)).

He ties size to survival, not to profit. Gamblers focus on maximum profit and dismiss risk ([2022 Ep 22 · 17:46](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1066)). In 2026 he says money is made not by those who win a lot but by those who lose less ([2026 #72 · 43:53](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=2633)).

### How to size it

1. **Turn the percentage into dollars.** Use current equity: $10,000 at 1% is $100.
2. **Take the stop in points.** It comes from the setup, not from your budget (Chapter 19).
3. **Divide.** Dollars at risk divided by stop points gives the dollars per point you can carry.
4. **Pick the contract.** Divide by the contract's dollars per point and round down to a whole contract.
5. **Check the real risk.** Multiply stop points by the actual dollars per point you will hold. It will usually be a little under your limit.
6. **If nothing fits, skip the trade.** If one micro already exceeds the budget, there is no trade at this size.

He tells students to find a free lot-size calculator online and enter account size, risk percentage and stop size. Answering "basic questions like lot size" yourself is part of the job ([2022 Ep 41 · 20:43](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1243)).

### Worked example

This is his example from the 2022 finale, on the S&P. The account is $10,000 and the risk is 1%.

1. 1% of $10,000 is $100.
2. The stop on this setup is 9 points. $100 divided by 9 is about $11 per point.
3. One ES mini is $50 a point, far too big. A mini cannot be traded at 1% on this account.
4. One MES micro is $5 a point. $11 divided by $5 rounds down to 2 micros, or $10 a point.
5. Real risk is 9 points times $10, or $90. That is under 1%.
6. The target is 25.5 points. At $10 a point that is $255.

([2022 Ep 41 · 22:18](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1338), [2022 Ep 41 · 23:08](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1388), [2022 Ep 41 · 24:46](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1486).)

He rounds the $255 to about 2¼%. The arithmetic is 2.55% of $10,000, so treat his figure as rounded. The reward is about 2.8 times the risk (25.5 points to 9).

He leaves commissions, fees and spread out of this sizing: "Costs are like taxes, you just eat it." A beginner who wants to be strict can include them ([2022 Ep 41 · 24:46](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1486)). Later he does count costs, but for measuring results. On hard days, trade one micro and count how many attempts it takes to get one winner, including commissions and NFA fees, because many "I was right" wins end net negative ([2024 #36 · 58:29](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=3509)).

The 2% version of the same example: 2% of $10,000 is $200, and on the same 9-point stop that is 4 micros, or $20 a point. After a full 2% loss, the next trade risks 1%, which is 2 micros ([2022 Ep 41 · 41:25](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2485)).

Stops are not fixed. This trade needed 9 points. Others will need 5, 10 or 12, so you recalculate size every time. Deep in a drawdown the budget can shrink until you cannot afford even one micro ([2022 Ep 41 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2057)).

### When the stop is too wide for your size

Several remarks point the same way: do not stretch the stop to fit the size, and do not stretch the size to fit the stop.

- **Skip.** If the stop is too much risk for you, do not take the trade ([2022 Ep 6 · 35:52](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2152)).
- **Futures are lumpy.** In forex, position size scales to the stop distance. In futures, if the stop is too wide for one contract, you either skip or you are over-leveraged ([2022 Ep 40 · 14:30](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=870)). He said this about forex lots, not about gold.
- **Drop to micros.** A stop too big for your size means micros ([2026 #19 · 1:57:44](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=7064)).
- **Use less leverage.** If the technical stop is large, use less leverage ([2026 #34 · 18:13](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=1093)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Sizing from the profit you want.** That is the gambler's order of thinking. Budget first, then stop, then size.
- **Reusing yesterday's size.** A 9-point stop and a 12-point stop need different contract counts.
- **Rounding up.** The example rounds $11 down to $10 a point. Rounding up breaks the budget.
- **Treating 2¼% as exact.** It is 2.55% by arithmetic. The point of the example is the structure, not the decimal.
- **Copying his live size.** The 2022 percentages above are what he teaches students. His demonstrations are on paper accounts and often use more contracts (see "One contract, one trade a day").

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the rules are stated as percentages: under 1% for students, 1% as the finale's rule, and his own 3.5% to 4.5%. In 2024 the 1% cap returns in one lecture, but the beginner drills are written in dollars and contracts: one micro, $75 to $100, or $50 risked to make $100. In 2026 the lectures say "one micro" and give dollar goals ($50 a day). The notes for the 2024 NQ live execution (2024 #23) record no risk per trade, stop distance or exact size at all.

I read this as a shift from teaching a percentage to teaching a size that makes a percentage unnecessary. That reading is mine. He never withdrew the 1% rule.

## Cutting risk after a loss

### What it is

This is the drawdown rule from the last episode of the 2022 Mentorship. After a loss, the next trade risks half as much. It is a ladder with a floor.

1. You lose a trade at 1% risk. The next trade risks 0.5%.
2. Before you go back to full 1%, you must make back 50% of what you lost. His example: you lose about $100 including commission, so you earn $50 back.
3. If the 0.5% trade also loses, the next one risks 0.25%. That is the lowest you go.
4. Three losses in a row therefore cost 1% + 0.5% + 0.25% = 1.75% in total.

([2022 Ep 41 · 31:07](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1867), [2022 Ep 41 · 31:54](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1914), [2022 Ep 41 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2057).)

The same logic scales to a 2% account. After a full 2% loss the next trade risks 1%. On the 9-point stop from the sizing example, that means going from 4 micros ($20 a point) to 2 micros ([2022 Ep 41 · 41:25](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2485)).

[[DIAGRAM ch20_d2 | caption: Three losses in a row under ICT's halving ladder against the doubling-up spiral he warns about | Halving caps three straight losses at 1.75% of the account; doubling up is at 3% after two.]]

The notes record only the first step back up: earn half the loss and you may return to full risk. They do not say how to climb from 0.25%. Do not invent a rule for it.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about what a loss does to you. After a loss you feel the urge for revenge, to over-leverage, and to get the money back faster than the market allows. He calls that the loser's cycle and the gambler's mentality. Cutting the next trade's risk removes the fuel ([2022 Ep 41 · 31:07](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1867)).

The arithmetic backs him. Doubling up after a 1% loss risks 2%, and the two trades together are already 3%. Spiral to 3% or 4% a trade, take the stop off or widen it, and a 10% drawdown arrives quickly, followed by shame ([2022 Ep 41 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2057)).

He adds a frame for drawdown itself. Treat it like a bank loan you pay interest on, and keep a long view: your career is not your last five trades, weeks or months ([2022 Ep 41 · 36:38](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2198), [2022 Ep 41 · 37:26](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2246)). Revenge trading and doubling up he calls infantile ([2022 Ep 41 · 35:51](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2151)). Chapter 22 covers the psychology.

### How the teaching changed

The halving rule is 2022. Later lectures say different things about the trade after a loss, and the notes do not reconcile them.

| When | What he said after a loss | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Next trade at half risk. Earn back half the loss to return to full risk | [2022 Ep 41 · 31:54](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=1914) |
| Aug 2024 | One trade a day, and stop after a loss | [2024 #6 · 2:34:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9249) |
| Aug 2024 | Stop for the day. Return tomorrow with the same model, same risk, same contracts | [2024 #17 · 2:32:04](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=9124) |
| Aug 2024 | Expect losing streaks. Do not change the model, only reduce risk | [2024 #11 · 1:23:19](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=4999) |
| Oct 2024 | A stop-out is a cost of business. Do not re-enter bigger or straight away. Take the next setup | [2024 #49 · 47:47](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=2867) |
| 2026 | If the chart still supports the narrative, re-enter, but do not take 15 trades or over-leverage | [2026 #87 · 21:21](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=1281) |
| 2026 | In your first year, no re-entry after a stop-out. Break that rule and you tilt | [2026 #87 · 1:16:24](https://youtu.be/d8du4fMvOQU?t=4584) |

The common thread is that size never goes up after a loss. Where they differ is whether risk goes down. If you follow the 2024 beginner rule of one trade a day, you are done after the loss, and the halving rule would only matter the next day. Whether to start that next day at half size is a question the notes leave open: he says "same risk" in one lecture and "reduce risk" in another. That reading is mine.

### The over-leverage test

In January 2026 he gives a test that needs no calculator. Before the trade, ask: if I lose on this one, how eager will I be to take the next? Tension, nervousness or unease means you are over-leveraged. Picture a board meeting where a CEO and CFO review your trade size before you enter ([2026 #137 · 1:31:52](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5512), [2026 #137 · 1:33:30](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5610)).

If the size is right, a full stop-out does not put you off the next trade. It is a cost of business, a premium for being wrong, and information ([2026 #137 · 1:35:07](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5707)). Other signs of too much size:

- You feel worn out even after a winning day.
- You want to post only your winners, or show profit but never drawdown.
- You chase an equity number, which is trading the equity curve and, in his word, gambling ([2026 #137 · 1:30:16](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5416), [2026 #137 · 1:36:44](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5804)).
- A $20 win on a micro feels huge and a $10 loss feels like a catastrophe. Keep size low until it stops ([2024 #26 · 1:11:08](https://youtu.be/A1mLNIoEsq0?t=4268)).

On the study side, he suggests practising drawdown on a demo account on purpose: go into it, build the balance back, and let a negative balance stop frightening you. This is for demo only, not for a live account ([2026 #127 · 23:46](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=1426)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Doubling up to get even.** He calls "getting even" revenge, not profit, and says averaging down with no stop is the trap he fell into ([2024 #11 · 2:43:27](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=9807)).
- **Stepping back to full size too fast.** The notes tie the return to full risk to earning half the loss back.
- **Escalating after an easy win.** He also warns against raising risk after a good run, for example 3% risk after a 5R win, or trading every session plus London ([2022 Ep 41 · 43:46](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2626)). Use low targets and work up.
- **Treating the ladder as permission to trade while tilted.** Professionals, he says, are not allowed to touch their account when highly charged or emotional ([2022 Ep 41 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=2057)).

## One contract, one trade a day

### What it is

This is the beginner ladder. It limits three things at once: size (one micro), frequency (one trade a day) and ambition (a small fixed goal, then stop). Each version below comes from a different year.

- **2024 #1.** One micro, one trade a day. It makes blowing the account on day one physically impossible, and that answers the subconscious fear of blowing up ([2024 #1 · 1:03:37](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3817)).
- **2024 #6.** Never more than 1% risk, never more than one trade a day, and stop after a loss. Let the risk rules compound the account ([2024 #6 · 2:34:09](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=9249)).
- **2024 #31, the "laboratory experiment".** One micro NQ contract. Take $75 to $100 a day and stop. Do this for a couple of weeks while only backtesting and tape reading. Two to three weeks later, press the button on one micro ([2024 #31 · 22:36](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1356), [2024 #31 · 1:23:11](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4991)). Enter at market at first, not with limit orders, so you feel the entry ([2024 #31 · 33:43](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2023)).
- **2026 #124, one micro S&P.** A micro ES pays $5 a handle. Aim for a $50 win a day, which over 20 trading days is $1,000 in four weeks ([2026 #124 · 9:06](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=546), [2026 #124 · 13:15](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=795)). Do it on a demo account first, and consider real money only after weeks of consistent demo wins.

The point of the 2026 assignment is precision, not size. Heavy leverage is a double-edged sword: it magnifies profits and losses, and a lucky run can build a profit that a single bad trade erases ([2026 #124 · 11:34](https://youtu.be/clG2IDpEcjE?t=694)).

### The 20-handle filter

In the same 2024 lecture he adds a gate for the NQ drill. A setup is valid only if it offers at least 20 handles to a logical target. First get a directional bias, then find a PD array (an FVG, an order block or any other) from which price can run at least 20 handles. If only 18 handles are available, pass and feel good about the discipline ([2024 #31 · 28:58](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1738), [2024 #31 · 39:21](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2361)).

Measure from entry to the target. His examples are the low of the minor sell-side pool, the upper quadrant of the opening range gap, and the next FVG. The 20 handles is a filter, not a target. For practice, frame a 20-handle box after the session and look back at where FVG entries would have given 20 handles to a sensible target ([2024 #31 · 46:50](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=2810)).

Two cautions on the numbers. First, this threshold belongs to that beginner exercise on NQ. Chapter 19 lists a 15-handle minimum he gave in 2026. Second, the dollar goals and the filter do not line up on MNQ. Twenty handles is $40 on one MNQ, while $75 to $100 on one MNQ would be 37.5 to 50 points (both arithmetic, not his). On a micro ES, 20 handles is exactly $100, the profit of his 2026 demo trade. The notes do not show him reconciling the two.

### Frequency limits

- **Do not trade every session.** Expecting a daily profit sets up the loser's cycle after the first loss ([2022 Ep 2 · 15:32](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=932)).
- **One trade a week, then demo.** In 2022 he suggests this as a cure for over-trading. Setups form every day, which is not an invitation to take every one ([2022 Ep 13 · 35:09](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=2109)).

- **Take the one good setup and stop.** He is more impressed by restraint than by many trades ([2024 #5 · 1:20:18](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=4818)).

No trade is not a loss: no commission, no loss, and patience practised ([2024 #17 · 57:30](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=3450)). Chapter 22 returns to this.

## Hard stops

### What it is

ICT's position is flat. Use a real stop order, never a mental one ([2024 #4 · 1:02:15](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=3735)). Chapter 19 covers where to place it and how to move it. This section covers the risk rule behind it.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that being wrong is normal, and the stop is how you survive it. Fear of being wrong shows up as a wish to trade without stops, and you have to accept stops and losses ([2022 Ep 7 · 29:59](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1799)). In 2026 he says that refusing a stop because you might be wrong is what blows accounts ([2026 #73 · 1:34:49](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=5689)). Money in an account goes in and out like breath, and holding a loser until a margin call because you skipped the stop is the failure ([2026 #127 · 37:15](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=2235)).

He also says you cannot stop other participants from running stops, so the only defence is not to be over-leveraged when they do ([2024 #44 · 28:24](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=1704)). In his motorcycle story he says the big risks are the unseen ones, such as owing the broker more than your account after a gap on a CPI or PPI release ([2024 #24 · 1:49:55](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=6595)).

### Rules

1. **Place it as an order, every time.** No stop in the market means no stop.
2. **Set it once and sit still.** Do not chase your stop ([2024 #24 · 2:57:07](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10627)).
3. **Do not put it where it becomes a source of worry.** A stop that worries you is in the wrong place ([2026 #62 · 13:47](https://youtu.be/fFi2QEApVGM?t=827)).
4. **If the stop is more risk than your size allows, skip or shrink.** Never widen the stop to fit (see "Percent risk and position size").
5. **Never average down without a stop.** He fell into that trap ([2024 #11 · 2:43:27](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=9807)).
6. **Know your firm's rules on adding.** See the prop-firm section.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **A flexible mental stop.** In 2022 he sometimes held a mental stop at the open, but he says that worked only because he was watching live, and it is not advice to trade without stops ([2022 Ep 15 · 5:00](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=300)).
- **Super-tight stops.** He says you cannot use them and expect not to be stopped out a lot, and the commissions and small losses add up. Use small size and a stop that matches the setup's timeframe ([2026 #127 · 52:29](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3149)).
- **Holding through the weekend with a stop.** A stop does not protect you across a gap. See the next section, "When to cut size or stand aside".

## Futures, CFDs and forex

### What it is

Futures are contracts on an exchange: everyone trades one price feed. A CFD (contract for difference) is a product your broker prices itself, tracking an index or currency. Forex is currency trading, normally quoted by your broker as well. ICT's argument runs through the difference between an exchange price and a broker's price.

### Why it works

ICT's reasoning for futures is about the price you are given.

- **One shared price.** In futures, every trader has the same high tick, low tick, open and close, and a reputable broker only routes orders. Many of his trades came within a quarter point of his stop without touching it ([2022 Ep 16 · 24:55](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1495), [2024 #24 · 27:26](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=1646)).
- **Broker discretion in forex.** In forex, you trade your broker's in-house liquidity pool. Each broker has its own prices and can widen spreads, requote or not fill, so stops get hit and price then runs ([2022 Ep 16 · 25:42](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1542), [2024 #24 · 27:26](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=1646)).
- **Tail risk, flagged as opinion.** In 2024 he predicted a currency de-pegging, like the Swiss franc, and warned of unlimited losses in forex. This is his opinion and prediction, not a rule ([2024 #24 · 29:03](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=1743)).

### What he said, year by year

- **2022.** In the first episode, a CFD (US500 on MT4) is the route for traders outside the US. For US traders it is illegal because it does not go through a regulated exchange, so futures are the only option ([2022 Ep 1 · 25:46](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1546)). He also said forex was quiet and that indices were where the movement was, and expected currencies to turn wild within about 12 months ([2022 Ep 31 · 0:56](https://youtu.be/OYNpIeu9czw?t=56), [2022 Ep 19 · 39:47](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=2387)). "Price is price" and works the same in forex.
- **2024.** He trades only futures, NQ mainly and ES second ([2024 #24 · 27:26](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=1646)). Americans are not legally allowed to trade CFDs ([2024 #31 · 1:25](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=85)). A forex pair needs a correlated or inverse partner, such as the dollar index ([2024 #37 · 28:39](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=1719)).
- **2026.** The tone changes. He shows MNQ and US 100 (a Nasdaq CFD) side by side in April 2026 and says the price action was nearly identical: the inversion FVG, the SIBI and the targets all lined up. Time and price are slightly different, and CFD fills may be off by a tick or two, but the logic of FVGs, order blocks and relative equal highs and lows is identical ([2026 #89 · 3:05](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=185), [2026 #89 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=664)).

So the move is from "futures only" in 2022 and 2024 to "CFDs as stand-ins" in 2026. He did not retract the claim that futures give a cleaner price. He added a way for traders who cannot use futures to follow the same levels. The legal point stands through 2026: US traders cannot legally trade CFDs, and you should check your own jurisdiction.

### How to trade a CFD from futures levels

His 2026 recipe, in order ([2026 #89 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=664), [2026 #32 · 4:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=267)):

1. Put the futures chart and the CFD chart side by side.
2. Note when the same candle forms on both.
3. Measure the levels on the futures chart, using the continuous contract for the high and low context (next section).
4. Transfer those levels to the CFD chart by matching time and price. For NQ the match is US 100, and for the S&P it is US 500.
5. Trade the CFD at those levels. He says the logic and execution mechanics are the same, though your fill may differ by a tick or two.

### What this means for gold

Everything in his CFD teaching pairs an index CFD with its index future. These notes contain no pairing for XAUUSD. The statement that his concepts work in gold is about the method ([2026 #32 · 4:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=267)). His case against forex brokers was made for forex. A gold CFD is also priced by your broker, so the same caution is plausible, but he did not say it about gold. Treat a very tight CFD stop as a risk you are choosing.

### Which market to trade

Under 2022's "every week, every day" motto he did not mean every market every day. Move to the asset class where the excitement is: futures one day, forex or bonds another. A sloppy, manipulated market is a rattlesnake, and you do not touch it ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:37:40](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5860)). Within one market, start with a single instrument, as the contracts section says.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Assuming tick-for-tick equality.** The CFD can differ by a tick or two. A tight stop copied from the futures chart may not survive that.
- **Assuming CFDs are legal for you.** He is explicit that they are not for US traders.
- **Reading the forex warning as gold advice.** He gave it about forex brokers.

## Contract months, rollover and the continuous chart

### What it is

Index futures (ES, NQ, YM) trade in four delivery months: March (H), June (M), September (U) and December (Z). Each contract expires on the third Friday of its month, and you should not trade after expiration ([2022 Ep 5 · 1:49](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=109), [2022 Ep 5 · 2:38](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=158)). The charting symbol carries the month: ESH2022 in 2022, NQM26 for June 2026, NQZ25 for December 2025, and NQU2026, ESU2026 and YMU2026 for September 2026 ([2026 #116 · 46:58](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2818), [2026 #69 · 0:00](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=0)).

The front month, or nearby contract, is the one currently trading with the most activity. The next month out is the one that takes over. Rollover is the switch from one to the other. A continuous contract is a chart that stitches the months together so you see one running history.

[[DIAGRAM ch20_d4 | caption: Index futures delivery months, and the 2026 workflow: levels from the continuous chart, orders on the front month | The title of the chart tells you which one you are on: a month code means a delivery contract, no month means continuous.]]

### Rolling by open interest (2022)

In 2022 his rule for choosing the month is open interest, meaning the number of contracts still open, a proxy for where liquidity sits. About the first or second day of the week before expiration, he checks open interest on barchart.com and trades whichever month has more. When the next month's open interest passes the front month's, roll ([2022 Ep 5 · 4:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=255), [2022 Ep 38 · 40:22](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2422)). He notes this differs from how others roll.

His 2022 examples show the timing. In the first episode where it appears, March ES was the front month and June's open interest was still small (the caption reads 57,124, and says "million", which is unclear), so March stayed the contract to trade ([2022 Ep 5 · 4:15](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=255)). Later in the year, trading the June contract in May was normal, and within a couple of weeks it rolled to September ([2022 Ep 36 · 0:08](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=8)). In another episode he moved from NQM2022 to NQU2022 and ESU2022 ([2022 Ep 38 · 39:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2373)).

In September 2024 he rolled from the September to the December contract on a Tuesday. He did not say what triggered that roll ([2024 #31 · 1:25](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=85)).

### Continuous for levels, front month for orders (2026)

In 2026 he adds a second layer. His analysis always starts on a continuous-contract chart, often beginning on the monthly. It gives a field of reference for key highs, lows and inefficiencies that may not exist on the front month because of the rollover ([2026 #32 · 1:55](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=115)). Then he checks whether the daily front-month contract agrees with the levels the continuous chart gave. Far-dated contracts, such as March 2027, have no influence on the continuous chart.

In March 2026 he makes this explicit. Use the continuous contract for top-down analysis, higher-timeframe targets and daily and weekly structure. Trades are executed on the delivery month that is actively trading, but levels and targets come from the continuous chart ([2026 #116 · 43:03](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2583)). A delivery-month chart looks "spotty" at rollovers and misses information. Many traders use delivery-month charts and wonder why their levels do not match his ([2026 #116 · 41:29](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2489)).

### How to spot which chart you are on

1. **Read the title** in the upper left. A symbol such as NQ with no month is the continuous contract. A symbol with a month code (NQM26) is a delivery month ([2026 #116 · 46:58](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2818)).
2. **Look at the price action.** Continuous is smooth, with no gaps at the rollovers. A delivery-month chart looks spotty, with visible gaps where it rolls ([2026 #116 · 40:40](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=2440)).
3. **Check before you trust a level.** If your level does not match his, the first thing to check is the contract in the title.

### How to use it

1. Open the weekly continuous chart. Mark the previous month's high and low and the previous week's high and low.
2. Check those levels on the front-month contract.
3. Move to the daily and then the 15-minute chart.
4. Verify any intraday target on the continuous chart, then execute on the front month.
5. Near expiration, use the individual months to avoid roll gaps, but check both and do not rely on one without the other ([2026 #75 · 16:38](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=998)).

### Rollover week

During a roll from one month to another there is an artificial flow of buying and selling as traders leave the old front month and enter the new one. ICT calls this participation "net sum zero": no real buying or selling pressure, though algorithms and manual intervention can paint a false direction. Volume imbalances and PD arrays bleed from the old contract to the new ([2026 #118 · 1:37](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=97), [2026 #118 · 12:05](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=725)).

His advice is to keep size light, expect to be wrong, and not try to build equity that week. After rollover week the contract becomes "pristine" and normal trading resumes. On March 17, 2026, the NQ March-to-June roll, price ran straight up from the open and left relative equal lows. He collected levels and tape-read but did not actively trade. He says similar artifacts follow FOMC and nonfarm payroll days ([2026 #118 · 22:22](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1342), [2026 #118 · 23:09](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1389)).

### Back-adjusting and old highs

In April 2026, on the MNQ daily chart, he points to a B-ADJ button. With it off, the relative equal highs and old highs sit far from current price. Switching the adjustment on brings them back into a range where the PD array matrix has levels to work with. His example was the October 30, 2025 candle high at 26,864.50, with its wick CE near 26,745 ([2026 #96 · 3:17](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=197), [2026 #96 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=435)).

He calls toggling the continuous contract on and off, and judging where price sits against previous all-time highs, "the secret" ([2026 #96 · 1:00:35](https://youtu.be/r-ztz7kReYc?t=3635)). The notes do not explain the mechanics beyond that it brings price back into range. Treat it as a pointer to test on your own chart, not as a rule.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using the delivery month for levels.** That is his main warning for 2026.
- **Using continuous for orders.** You execute on the front month.
- **Rolling on the calendar.** In 2022 he rolled on open interest, not on a date.
- **Reading a roll gap as a market gap.** A delivery-month chart shows jumps where the contract changes.
- **Assuming the two views always agree.** In March 2026 he says his targets always come from continuous. In April he says to use whichever contract's levels are respected. Test both on your own charts.

### How the teaching changed

2022 was about which month to trade, decided by open interest. In 2024 he mentions a September-to-December roll but without a reason. In 2026 the emphasis moves to which chart you read: continuous for levels, front month for fills, light size in rollover week, and a back-adjustment toggle for old highs. The 2022 rule is not contradicted. The 2026 teaching sits on top of it.
