ICT teaches that price is delivered on a schedule, and that the schedule is the New York clock. This chapter lays that schedule out: sessions and kill zones, the opening prices that anchor each part of the day, the pre-market and opening ranges, the macros, the lunch and afternoon windows, and the last hour. After reading it you can mark a chart with the vertical lines ICT uses and say, for each window, what you wait for and what you leave alone.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch12_d1 | caption: ICT's windows on one New York day, 6 PM to 4 PM (overnight hours compressed) | Most of the named windows crowd into the nine hours after 7:00 AM, and each one is a block of clock time, not a price level.]]

- **Clock.** Chart in New York time and read every window in ET. For the algorithm the day starts at midnight ET ([2022 Ep 25 · 25:25](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1525)).
- **Session or kill zone.** A session is a region's whole trading stretch; a kill zone is the narrow window inside it where you expect the setup. London kill zone: 2:00 to 5:00 AM. Asian kill zone: a 2-hour window, read here as 7:00 to 9:00 PM ([2026 #138 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1882)).
- **Four opening prices.** Midnight ET for the day's range, 8:30 for the morning session, 9:30 for the equities session, and the 6:00 PM reopen that makes the opening gaps of Chapter 11.
- **Ranges.** The pre-market range is 7:00 to 9:00. If it trends, the AM session tends to chop; if it consolidates, the AM session tends to trend. The opening range is 9:30 to 10:00: 30 minutes, never 15.
- **Macros.** Twenty-minute windows from :50 to :10 around each hour, plus 8:30. They say when price should start to run, not which way.
- **Midday, close and Silver Bullet.** The lunch macro (11:30 to 1:30) retraces the morning toward the first low after 10:00. The PM session opens at 1:30; the last-hour macros run 3:15 to 3:45, then market on close to 4:00. The main Silver Bullet window is 10:00 to 11:00; the entry model is Chapter 17.

## New York time is the clock

### What it is

ICT's claim is that price does not move because of buying and selling pressure. It moves on a schedule coded into the market, so time comes first and price second. He says a price level is of little use until its time is known, and a time is of little use unless price is at a key PD array ([2022 Ep 39 · 32:55](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1975), [2022 Ep 39 · 36:05](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2165)). In 2024 he put it more bluntly: a setup that is not grounded in time first will not work, whoever trades it ([2024 #1 · 1:40:47](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6047)). Treat this as his claim, not a tested fact.

Every time he gives is New York local time. The day begins at midnight ET, and he ridicules the idea that the daily cycle starts in Wellington ([2022 Ep 25 · 46:47](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2807)). The futures day itself restarts at 6:00 PM; that print matters for gaps, covered in Chapter 11.

### How to set up the chart

1. Set the TradingView time zone to New York, wherever you live. ICT: on local time it will not work ([2022 Ep 8 · 11:10](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=670), [2024 #24 · 1:44:15](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=6255), [2026 #86 · 9:49](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=589)).
2. Draw light vertical lines at the times you will use. The ones ICT names are 2:00 and 5:00 AM, 7:00, 9:00, 9:30, 10:00, 11:30, 1:30, 2:00, 3:00 and 4:00 PM ([2022 Ep 8 · 9:34](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=574), [2024 #2 · 48:50](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2930), [2026 #86 · 27:09](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1629)).
3. Know which hours you are looking at. Regular trading hours (RTH) run 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM for NQ; electronic hours run from 6:00 PM to 9:30 AM and fill in the gaps RTH leaves, like pieces of a jigsaw ([2026 #56 · 24:13](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=1453)). A level that also trades in RTH is more significant than one that only traded overnight ([2026 #18 · 1:18:16](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4696)).

## Sessions and kill zones

### What it is

A session is the whole stretch of a region's trading. A kill zone is a narrower window inside it where ICT expects the setup to form. He restated the difference in January 2026 because brokers and others label whole sessions as "ICT kill zones", which he calls wrong ([2026 #138 · 29:00](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1740)). The windows the notes give:

- **Asian session:** 7:00 PM to about 5:00 AM. **Asian kill zone:** a 2-hour scalping window, usually with a first presented FVG and an old high or low to run ([2026 #137 · 15:42](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=942)). The 2022 and 2024 notes give it as 7:00 to 9:00 PM ([2022 Ep 3 · 33:40](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2020), [2024 #5 · 49:09](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2949)). The 2026 captions are garbled ("7:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m."), so this chapter reads it as 7 to 9 PM.
- **London kill zone:** 2:00 to 5:00 AM, same for forex and index futures. The sweet spot is 2:00 to 4:00; the window runs to 5:00 because it overlaps Asia's close ([2026 #138 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1882)).
- **London session:** the whole stretch, to about 10:00 to 11:00 AM, the European close.
- **Dead time:** 5:00 to 7:00 AM. He does nothing there and tells learners to do the same ([2026 #138 · 47:13](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2833)).
- **Pre-market session:** 7:00 to 9:00 AM, covered in its own section below.
- **New York session:** 9:30 AM open to 4:00 PM close. It subdivides into the pre-market, the AM session, the first hour's dealing range, a two-hour lunch, the PM session, and market on close ([2026 #27 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=815)).
- **2022 New York windows:** 7:00 to 10:00 AM for FX pairs and 8:30 to 11:00 AM for index futures ([2022 Ep 17 · 9:52](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=592)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is timing, not volume. Of Asia he says the algorithm "comes online" at 7 PM and markets do not move until it is time ([2024 #5 · 33:50](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=2030)). Of the New York morning, he says 8:30 "starts the hunt": from then he watches for a nearby old high or low to be violated, then for a market structure shift (MSS) and an FVG ([2022 Ep 4 · 2:35](https://youtu.be/L-ReMHiavPM?t=155)). Of 10:00 to 11:00 AM, he says the London extreme is usually set by then, so it is when European traders who held overnight take profit, and a tradeable high or low often forms (not guaranteed) ([2026 #138 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1882)).

### What each session tends to do

In 2026 he described a rhythm rather than a rule. London (2 to 5 AM) often trends. If it trended, the 7 to 9 AM range will likely consolidate, and trending can resume after 9:30 ([2026 #25 · 2:47](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=167)).

The morning may consolidate, reverse, consolidate and then break; the 11:30 to 1:30 lunch may drift one way, often lower, and then rally back ([2026 #5 · 1:01](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=61)). He rarely sees three sessions in a row trend the same way ([2026 #23 · 28:15](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1695)). Event-driven or gap-driven days break the pattern.

### How to spot it

1. Draw the whole-session box (Asia 7 PM to 5 AM, London 2 AM to 11 AM) and, separately, the kill-zone box (2 to 5 AM).
2. Mark each session's high and low, plus any intraday high or low forming just before the 9:30 equities open. ICT expects price to sweep above those highs or below those lows and create an MSS setup ([2022 Ep 3 · 32:52](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=1972)).
3. Search the whole session for the smooth equal highs or lows. Do not limit the search to the kill zone, because price may already have traded beyond it and used the level ([2026 #138 · 29:00](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1740)).
4. Take the Asian smooth highs (or lows) as the overnight draw for 7:00 AM; see the pre-market section ([2026 #138 · 53:40](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3220)).

### How to trade it

- **Kill zones are for deriving the entry and placing the order.** The fill does not have to happen inside the window. If you cannot derive an order by then, you have nothing to do that day ([2022 Ep 18 · 48:29](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2909)).
- **Pull stale orders.** In the FX New York window, an unfilled limit order goes at 11:30 AM. No good-till-cancel, or it may fill at a bad time and be stopped by a run through the equal highs ([2022 Ep 18 · 47:42](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2862)).
- **FX New York window.** Generally no new trades after 10:00 AM unless a news driver exists. A 10:00 high-impact release extends the window to 11:00 to 11:30 ([2022 Ep 17 · 24:54](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1494)).
- **Index futures morning.** 8:30 to 11:00, extendable to noon. He is still comfortable taking trades until 10:40 to 10:45 ([2022 Ep 17 · 24:07](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1447)). He expects 2 to 3 setups before noon and caps a day at four trades: two in the morning, two in the afternoon ([2022 Ep 16 · 16:54](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1014)).
- **London close.** If you are in a directional trade, have about 80% of it off between 10:00 and 11:00 AM. That hour usually, not always, makes the opposite end of the daily range. For CAD pairs on crude-inventory days he widens it to noon ([2022 Ep 40 · 16:50](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1010), [2022 Ep 40 · 17:38](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1058)).

### Worked example: Monday 12 January 2026

NQ, Monday 12 January 2026. ICT boxed the Asian session from Sunday 7 PM to 5 AM, the London kill zone from 2 to 5 AM, and the London session out to 11 AM. London's low formed at 3:43 AM, "right before 4:00", reacting off a stale daily volume imbalance ([2026 #138 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1882)).

The Asian box held smooth highs from about Sunday 10 PM. Overnight price had only gone down, so those highs sat untouched above. At 7:00 AM displacement up through a short-term high left a first presented FVG. Price dropped into it, rallied to the equal highs, then consolidated from 8:00 to 9:00 ([2026 #138 · 49:35](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=2975), [2026 #138 · 53:40](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3220)). The London kill zone told him where the low was; the Asian session told him where the draw was.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Calling the whole session a kill zone.** Session is where you look for liquidity; kill zone is where you expect the entry.
- **Chasing a big overnight run.** For equity index futures only (NQ, YM, ES), he says that after a large overnight move you should skip the New York AM session. Wait until after 1:00 PM and anticipate a raid on the lunch or morning lows ([2022 Ep 9 · 15:22](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=922)). In 2024 he restated it: an overnight trend (protraction) puts you on the sidelines in the morning, because the NY session is then likely to reverse or consolidate ([2024 #44 · 1:28:27](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=5307)).
- **Expecting Asia to behave like London.** London works "absolutely"; Asia not that often. If you must, trade yen pairs, NZD and AUD on 1 to 5-minute charts ([2022 Ep 25 · 45:59](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2759)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** kill zones appear as hunting windows. In Episode 2 he had not introduced them formally and simply liked watching 8:30 to 11:00 AM ([2022 Ep 2 · 42:09](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2529)). By Episode 3 he lists London 2 to 5 AM, New York 7 to 10 AM and Asia 7 to 9 PM.
- **2024:** the AM session starts at 7:00 for both forex and futures ([2024 #1 · 1:42:24](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6144)). Beginners ignore equal highs and lows before 7:00, even obvious ones, and treat the 7:00 line as "punch-in time" ([2024 #2 · 48:50](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2930), [2024 #2 · 49:37](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2977)). The AM session's first characteristic is to retrade into the London range, the high and low between 2 and 5 AM ([2024 #2 · 1:18:29](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4709)).
- **2026:** he restates the kill zones, adds "dead time" and treats the 7:00 to 9:00 range as the beginner's core window.

The notes never show him withdrawing the 2022 windows. His attention simply moved earlier, to 7:00, and the morning split into pre-market, 9:30 opening range and the 10:00 hour.

## The key opening prices

### What it is

ICT anchors the day to a handful of opening prices. Each answers a different question, so the first job is to know which one you are using.

| Open | Candle | Job | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midnight open | 12:00 AM ET | The day's range, daily Power of 3, the London session | [2022 Ep 11 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=435) |
| 8:30 open | 8:30 AM ET | The morning (New York) session | [2022 Ep 21 · 16:00](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=960) |
| 9:30 open | 9:30 AM ET | The equities session; a running reference and a next-day target | [2026 #28 · 9:36](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=576) |
| 6:00 PM open | first 6:00 PM ET print | The new-day and new-week opening gaps (Chapter 11) | [2024 #37 · 2:52](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=172) |

Power of 3 and the Judas swing are taught in Chapter 13. Here we only need the idea behind them: on a bullish day price ideally dips below the open before it rallies, and on a bearish day it ideally rallies above the open before it falls.

### The midnight open

Mark the open of the 00:00 New York candle on the 15-minute chart (the 5-minute if you scalp) and extend it forward to 11:00 AM ([2022 Ep 18 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1723), [2022 Ep 8 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=247)). Use the actual midnight candle's open, not the TradingView daily candle's open. He says there is nothing wrong with the daily open, but he does not use it ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:44:52](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6292)).

ICT uses it three ways.

- **Frame the day.** Bullish, he prefers to buy at or below it. Bearish, he prefers to sell above it, even though shorting a rising market "feels unnatural" ([2022 Ep 38 · 16:34](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=994), [2022 Ep 10 · 30:34](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1834)). The 2022 "classic buy day" opens at midnight, trades down to make the low of the day, then rallies. In his ES example the low printed about 9:10 ET and price ran into the 9:30 open ([2022 Ep 38 · 18:08](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1088)).
- **Read London.** The midnight open frames the London session as well as the daily range ([2022 Ep 21 · 12:50](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=770)). He says he sleeps through London about 90% of the time. At about 7:00 AM he checks: if he is bearish, price rallied above the midnight open and is now back below it, he has an edge on the daily bias and expects another rally, ideally above the 8:30 open ([2022 Ep 21 · 29:34](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1774)).
- **Judge the Judas swing.** On one ES day only two tiny moves traded above the open, which he called anemic. He wanted about 15 to 20 handles ([2022 Ep 21 · 10:27](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=627)).

In 2026 he used the same price as a magnet. On a Monday the midnight open (29,878.25 on NQ) was "a very strong draw on liquidity". It need not be the daily bias; it can be a session bias. He took five contracts off just below it, and price then showed "a whole lot of initial resistance" there. His lesson was to treat it as a first take-profit area, not an extension target ([2026 #38 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=389), [2026 #38 · 10:31](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=631)).

### The 8:30 open

The 8:30 open is the open of whichever candle begins at 8:30. It does not matter which timeframe ([2022 Ep 16 · 4:58](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=298)). It is the New York session's opening price, and in the 2022 model that session runs 8:30 to 11:00 AM ([2022 Ep 21 · 16:00](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=960), [2022 Ep 21 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1440)).

ICT gives two reasons for 8:30. In 2022 it was the time the news embargo lifts, so many releases hit then ([2022 Ep 10 · 1:42](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=102)). Later he said the algorithm comes online at 8:30 with or without news; news only exaggerates the run ([2024 #44 · 1:30:48](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=5448), [2026 #136 · 37:54](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2274)).

The 2022 model at 8:30, as he restated it: look left of 8:30 for a short-term high. After 8:30 price rallies above the 8:30 open and runs those stops. It then breaks down with displacement and leaves an FVG, rallies back into the gap and sells off, and that retrace is the entry. On the day he restated it, the model did not form, because price never traded above the 8:30 open ([2022 Ep 21 · 21:35](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1295)). Entries are Chapter 18; the point here is the open's role.

### Choosing between midnight and 8:30

Viewers were confused because he used both. His answer: midnight for the entire daily range, 8:30 for the morning session ([2022 Ep 11 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=435)). Three refinements:

1. **Bearish, and the 8:30 open is below the midnight open.** Use the lower one. It sets the minimum threshold for the Judas rally. Mirror it for bullish ([2022 Ep 17 · 18:35](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=1115)).
2. **Bullish, and price never trades back below the midnight open.** Midnight is not a factor. Use the 8:30 open ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:36:52](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5812)).
3. **Price is below the midnight open and, after 8:30, below the 8:30 open too.** The market is "really in a discount". ICT calls this the silver-platter buy. No indicator, divergence or Fibonacci is needed to see it ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:36:52](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5812)).

[[DIAGRAM ch12_d2 | caption: Bullish day: price below the midnight open, then below the 8:30 open (illustrative, no prices) | The best buy zone sits below both opens, where a sell-side run into an FVG and a down-closed candle can start the rally.]]

Worked example: ES, 15-minute and 5-minute charts, 19 April 2022. Price sat below the midnight open. At 8:30 it also dropped below the 8:30 open, ran short-term swing lows into a bullish FVG and two down-closed candles, then sprang above the short-term highs. The daily low was about 4,377 (caption unclear) ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:25:43](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5143), [2022 Ep 19 · 1:34:28](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5668)).

### The 9:30 open

At 9:30 stocks begin their New York session and volatility arrives. It can whipsaw inside a small range or sweep both buy side and sell side in a large one, and then the real move for the day unfolds ([2022 Ep 21 · 26:24](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1584)). He says the first run is usually, not always, opposite to the real move. It often builds equal lows or highs that induce traders in the wrong direction ([2022 Ep 6 · 32:36](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1956)).

Two later uses:

- **Keep the 9:30 open on the chart.** On a consolidation day price keeps returning to it, so it is a constant reference ([2026 #28 · 9:36](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=576), [2026 #28 · 12:47](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=767)).
- **Use the previous day's 9:30 open as a target.** On one day his RTH objective, which he called the terminus, was yesterday's 9:30 price. It was hit and became the RTH high ([2026 #45 · 7:16](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=436), [2026 #45 · 9:41](https://youtu.be/J9G1j3-e1GU?t=581)).

The gap between the 4:14 PM settlement and the 9:30 print is the RTH opening range gap (Chapter 11).

### The 6:00 PM open

Electronic trading reopens at 6:00 PM ET, and on Fridays not until Sunday 6:00 PM. The first print against the earlier close makes the new-day gap, or on Sunday the new-week gap ([2024 #37 · 2:52](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=172), Chapter 11). In 2024 he describes NQ rallying "off the 6 pm new-day opening gap" ([2024 #7 · 6:47](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=407)), and in 2026 he says the 1:30 to 2:00 PM range stays salient through the final hour and into the 6:00 PM restart ([2026 #86 · 27:09](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1629)).

Keep midnight and 6 PM apart. The 6 PM print is the gap's open; the midnight open is the Power of 3 anchor.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using the wrong open for the job.** Midnight is for the day, 8:30 for the morning.
- **Reading an open as support or resistance.** In 2026 the midnight open was a draw and a take-profit area, not a wall.
- **Forcing the 8:30 model.** If price never trades above the 8:30 open on a bearish day, the model is absent. Wait.

### How the teaching changed

In Episode 9 (2022) he said he likes 8:30 as the opening price for the intraday Power of 3. In Episode 10 the dashed midnight level was the one he projected from ([2022 Ep 10 · 30:34](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1834)). Episode 11 reconciles them: two reference points, two jobs ([2022 Ep 11 · 7:15](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=435)). Episodes 16 to 21 formalise both. The 2026 version adds the midnight open as a magnet and a session bias.

- [2022 Ep 19 · 1:36:52](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5812): the midnight-versus-8:30 decision rule.

## The pre-market range, 7:00 to 9:00

### What it is

The pre-market range is the high and low of the two hours before the stock-market open. Every trading day except Sunday has one. ICT calls it "pillar 1" of the daily log he gives new students ([2026 #14 · 5:12](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=312)). The notes state its edges several ways, so here they are side by side.

| Version | Window | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Half-hour framing, Aug 2024 | 7:00 to 7:30 pre-market range, 7:30 to 8:00 opening range; same again at 9:00 to 9:30 and 9:30 to 10:00 | [2024 #1 · 1:51:53](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6713) |
| Before 9:00 | Pre-market is anything before 9:00; the session starts at 7:00 | [2024 #37 · 5:18](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=318) |
| Before the bell | Anything before 9:30; he likes 7:00 to 9:30 | [2024 #49 · 7:01](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=421) |
| The graded range | 7:00 to 9:00 | [2026 #14 · 6:03](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=363) |
| Data due at 8:30 | 7:00 to 8:30 | [2026 #27 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=99) |

The reading that fits all of them: the session starts at 7:00, the range you grade is 7:00 to 9:00 (7:00 to 8:30 when a release is due at 8:30), and its high and low are final once 9:00 passes ([2026 #42 · 10:18](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=618)). Do not call this range the opening range. In 2024 he warned against it ([2024 #1 · 1:51:53](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6713)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation: if an algorithm exists, it must collect data from a range with a beginning and an end. The 9:30 open starts a new session, so the two hours before it are the data window, and an old high or low outside a time-based range matters little ([2026 #42 · 3:10](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=190)). If you reject the algorithm idea, he says to read the range as where buying and selling pressure begins and ends. A second argument is the market's rhythm: after a session of consolidation the next one moves, and after that one he expects consolidation again, so traders are lulled and then caught ([2026 #27 · 12:48](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=768)).

### How to spot it

1. Draw vertical lines at 7:00 and 9:00. Box the highest high and lowest low between them, and mark any relative equal highs or lows inside ([2026 #14 · 6:03](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=363)).
2. Classify the range as trending or consolidating (see the rule below).
3. Tell a pop from a trend. On Monday 24 August 2026 price ran up about 40 minutes at the start of the window, took the buy side, fell back inside and rotated around the midpoint. He called the run liquidity, not a trend; the range was consolidating ([2026 #26 · 7:22](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=442)).
4. Read the bodies, not the wicks. Smooth price is where the bodies are, jagged price is where the wicks are, and the range's liquidity sits at the extreme of the bodies ([2026 #26 · 8:10](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=490)).
5. Grade it. Draw the high-to-low fib and colour the 50% line differently: above is premium, at or below is discount ([2026 #27 · 14:23](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=863)). Stop the lines at 9:00 and project them into the day, so the 9:30 opening range can be checked against them. Where levels from both agree, they become stronger key levels ([2026 #10 · 9:45](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=585)).
6. Note whether price tagged a key higher-timeframe level inside the window ([2026 #28 · 5:36](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=336)).

He also divides the range into eight octants and adds time as the horizontal axis: 7:00, 7:30, 8:00, 8:30, 9:00 and the minute the low formed. He says price keeps returning to those intersections and calls the result a time-price grid. It is his own method and he admits it needs practice ([2026 #43 · 12:01](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=721)).

### The rule it feeds

| 7:00 to 9:00 does this | The AM session then tends to | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidates (a block, rotating) | Trend; direction comes from the higher-timeframe narrative | [2026 #27 · 14:23](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=863), [2026 #36 · 3:21](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=201) |
| Trends | Chop and consolidate (he puts the AM session at 9:30 to 11:30) | [2026 #28 · 4:46](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=286) |
| Trends into a key higher-timeframe level | Chop more likely still | [2026 #28 · 5:36](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=336) |
| Follows a London trend | Likely to consolidate; three trending sessions in a row are rare | [2026 #25 · 2:47](https://youtu.be/ieJSy4YsVgk?t=167) |

His claims: when 7 to 9 trends, the algorithm will go into consolidation during the AM session ([2026 #28 · 3:58](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=238)), and understanding this removes about 75% of the morning's adversity, which is his own estimate ([2026 #28 · 5:36](https://youtu.be/0olLwAiLa3o?t=336)). He also says it is "not a panacea" ([2026 #14 · 10:08](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=608)) and that the rule is probabilistic ([2026 #36 · 4:10](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=250)).

### How to trade it

- **Wait for the liquidity to form after 7:00.** At 7:00 you wait for relative equal highs (or lows) to form after the time marker, then for price to pierce them. The pierce is the raid; the reaction is the trade. A lower high beside a higher high counts, and highs formed in London are the least important ([2024 #6 · 10:46](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=646)). Prefer smooth levels to jagged ones ([2024 #7 · 23:44](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=1424)).
- **Two-sided plan for a beginner.** Mark one clear equal-high level and one clear equal-low level. It does not matter which is hit first. If one side is raided at 7:00, treat it as a false breakout and the opposite side as the next draw ([2026 #132 · 20:20](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1220), [2026 #132 · 21:08](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1268)).
- **Overnight liquidity as the draw.** The pre-market tends to run overnight and previous-day equal highs and lows, and to do so sharply, on the way to 9:30 ([2026 #56 · 26:36](https://youtu.be/NPU4gsM9jqA?t=1596)). Price often accelerates at 7:00 toward the higher-timeframe draw ([2026 #57 · 3:15](https://youtu.be/KHkUPAsdyfk?t=195)).

### Worked example: Thursday 27 and Friday 28 August 2026

On Friday the range was a block. Its two highs were at the same price, 29,539, and the bodies could not touch the midpoint of Thursday's range wick. The AM session then trended down. His summary: consolidation between 7:00 and 9:00 tells you the AM session is likely to trend ([2026 #27 · 3:15](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=195), [2026 #27 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=243), [2026 #27 · 11:56](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=716), [2026 #27 · 14:23](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=863)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Calling it the opening range.** The opening range is 9:30 to 10:00.
- **Mistaking a pop for a trend.** A 40-minute run that only takes the buy side is liquidity.
- **Expecting a guarantee.** ICT calls it a tendency, not a panacea.
- **Looking before 7:00 as a beginner.** He tells beginners to ignore equal highs and lows that formed before 7:00 ([2024 #2 · 49:37](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=2977)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** the reference was the most obvious swing high and low before 8:30 on the 15-minute chart ([2022 Ep 5 · 13:16](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=796)).
- **August 2024:** a 7:00 start line, equal highs and lows that form after it, and the half-hour pre-market and opening ranges.
- **2026:** the 7 to 9 range is graded with a fib, octants and a time grid, and the trend-versus-consolidation rule becomes the headline lesson.

The 2024 contradiction ("look before 7:00" in Lecture 1, "do not" in Lecture 2) is explained in Lecture 2: anything before 7:00 is London data, which beginners should skip and advanced students may use.

## The opening range, 9:30 to 10:00

### What it is

The opening range is the high and low of the first 30 minutes of regular trading hours: 9:30 to 10:00 ET. It is not a 5, 10 or 15-minute range. ICT rejects the 15-minute version in several lectures and calls it a gimmick ([2024 #10 · 20:07](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=1207), [2024 #11 · 19:39](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1179)). In 2026 he said it flatly: "it's not 5 minutes, it's not 15 minutes, it's 30 minutes" ([2026 #37 · 7:09](https://youtu.be/C7CwjBOtHwM?t=429)). He adds that the algorithm respects the first 30 minutes and the first 60 minutes, and calls the first 30 minutes a "script" rather than a macro ([2024 #49 · 1:44:55](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6295)).

The first hour, 9:30 to 10:30, is the first hour's dealing range. The opening range is its first half ([2026 #113 · 44:22](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=2662)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation: in these 30 minutes the algorithm sets up the initial buy side and sell side and then operates off them. Fifteen minutes is not enough information to say anything about the daily range ([2024 #27 · 18:19](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=1099)). After the first 60 minutes you have what you need to define the day's range, and he can sometimes pick the high or low of the day within three ticks from it, though he does not show how in these notes ([2024 #49 · 1:45:41](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6341)). In 2026 he adds that the first 30 minutes give the rhyme and reason for the whole morning ([2026 #99 · 57:39](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=3459)).

### How to spot it

1. Draw vertical lines at 9:30 and 10:00. Take the highest high and lowest low between them. He draws lines, not rectangles, on live streams ([2024 #10 · 10:27](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=627)).
2. Treat both extremes as liquidity: buy side above, sell side below ([2024 #11 · 19:39](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1179)).
3. Grade the range: high, upper quadrant (75%), midpoint or CE (50%), lower quadrant (25%), low. The 9:30 opening price is its own level. He stresses this is not Quarters Theory, pivots, Wolfe waves or Elliott wave ([2024 #10 · 10:27](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=627)).
4. Mark the first presented FVG on the 1-minute chart. In his words, the opening range is essentially that first presented gap ([2024 #27 · 12:03](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=723)).
5. Carry yesterday's levels forward. Past opening ranges are reference points for later days ([2024 #10 · 2:06](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=126)).

### How to trade it

- **Beginners take no entries.** Mark the range, wait for it to finish and journal it. Sitting still teaches discipline and filters out the fake setups you would chase ([2024 #11 · 19:39](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1179), [2024 #17 · 13:44](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=824)). Early in the session you can be caught offside, so in 2024 he treated the 10:00 hour as the main focus instead ([2024 #9 · 28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683)).
- **When both sides look clean, wait for the first to be taken.** If equal highs (including the 9:30 high) and equal lows are both obvious, the side taken first is the fake run. On 23 August 2024 the buy side went first, which made that whole run fake. Had price gone down first and reversed, that gap would have been an inversion FVG ([2024 #16 · 21:19](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1279)).
- **Expect the near sides to go.** At 9:30 the market tends to take close-proximity buy side and sell side, then the real move follows. A rally into buy side at 9:30 is the Judas swing, built to induce buyers ([2026 #129 · 19:04](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1144), [2026 #129 · 23:05](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=1385)). Chapter 13 teaches it.
- **With no bias, submit to the first hour.** Let the first half hour trade, take the first FVG, and let 9:45 or 10:00 news pass. Then mark the 9:30 to 10:30 high and low and grade it. The 50% line is the pivot: if you are bullish, work the upper half and ignore the lower. If you are stopped out below it, look for the lower quadrant or a full roll to the low ([2026 #134 · 6:33](https://youtu.be/zyKAPNk_prA?t=393), [2026 #134 · 13:50](https://youtu.be/zyKAPNk_prA?t=830)).
- **No gap at the bell?** Wait for the first presented FVG after the bell. If price keeps overlapping bodies and gives you nothing, wait until 10:00 ([2026 #99 · 10:38](https://youtu.be/eft9_3ekDCY?t=638)).

### Worked example: NQ, Friday 16 August 2024

Early in the session, at about 9:30 to 9:35, price dropped to a level that was both the 75% line of Thursday's 9:30 to 10:00 range and the 20% level of the week's range. It rallied from there more than once. Thursday's CE and lower quadrant had not been traded to yet, so the CE was the least he wanted to see price trade to. He expects a strong reaction where a two-day-old opening-range level, another element and a PD array agree inside a macro window ([2024 #10 · 2:06](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=126), [2024 #10 · 49:23](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2963)).

A second example: on 5 August 2024, NQ at 9:30 sold off into a bullish breaker, then reversed and ran to the 17,820.75 highs. He called the opening-range drop a Judas swing, because the real move had already been given by relative equal highs ([2024 #1 · 2:03:06](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7386)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using a 15-minute or 5-minute opening range.** He means 30.
- **Confusing the opening range with the opening range gap.** The range is a 9:30 to 10:00 time window. The gap is the space between the 4:14 PM close and the 9:30 print (Chapter 11). He mislabelled the two once and re-taught the difference ([2024 #10 · 10:27](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=627)).
- **Treating the 7:30 to 8:00 "opening range" of 2024 Lecture 1 as the main one.** It was the second half of a half-hour framing ([2024 #1 · 1:51:53](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6713)).

### How the teaching changed

The words "opening range" have meant two things. In 2022 Episode 10 it was the distance from the day's open to the manipulation extreme, projected through the open. On a bearish day you measure from the open up to the Judas high and subtract that distance from the open. He says every premium array of the morning forms between the open and its high or inside that projection below the open, and that if price leaves the zone without you, you do not chase ([2022 Ep 10 · 12:56](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=776), [2022 Ep 10 · 13:45](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=825), [2022 Ep 10 · 14:34](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=874)). He flagged this as a specific 2022 meaning, distinct from the RTH 9:30 range.

From 2024 the term means the 30-minute window. Lecture 1 also applied a half-hour pre-market range and opening range to every hour ([2024 #1 · 1:51:53](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6713)).

Two lessons point in opposite directions for learners. In 2024 beginners take no entries from 9:30 to 10:00. In April 2026 he says nearly all learning and most profit comes from the first hour, and tells students to trade only 9:30 to 10:00 or 9:30 to 10:30 for the first few months and to study only the first 35 to 40 minutes ([2026 #108 · 52:33](https://youtu.be/yqBvOeOKcBM?t=3153)). He does not reconcile the two. A fair reading is that the 2024 advice protects beginners from the offside risk, while the 2026 advice tells them where to study.

## Macros

### What it is

A macro is a 20-minute window around the top of the hour: the last 10 minutes of the hour that is ending plus the first 10 minutes of the next, written xx:50 to xx:10. In it price "spools": it runs for liquidity or rushes into an inefficiency ([2024 #4 · 23:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1384), [2026 #19 · 1:02:46](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3766)). A macro carries no direction. It only says when price should start to move, like the start of a small new race, and you want to see it move your way ([2026 #19 · 1:05:12](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3912)).

The macro is a time window, so it is not a price level and not a setup. ICT's first version was looser: a move at a specific time, or within a 20-minute period ([2024 #1 · 14:25](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=865)).

Three limits from the notes:

- There are no 20 to 40 or 30 to 40-minute macros. He calls those gimmicks ([2024 #11 · 1:01:35](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3695)).
- Arbitrary clock times are not macros. 10:40 is not one, and he rejects numerology such as 8:11 ([2026 #85 · 7:03](https://youtu.be/7zlblhLGraA?t=423), [2024 #44 · 52:06](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=3126)).
- Three windows break the :50 to :10 pattern: 8:30, the lunch macro and the last hour. They are below.

[[DIAGRAM ch12_d4 | caption: The macro clock from 5:50 AM to 4:00 PM: hourly windows plus the three exceptions (windows from the notes) | Every hourly macro is the same 20 minutes wide; 8:30, the two-hour lunch macro and the last-hour pair are the exceptions.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is order flow routed through dealers. Large institutions, such as funds and insurers, must push big blocks through the market and cannot dump an order at once. Their dealer feeds it in staggered pieces, aiming at an average price, in high-traffic windows. He adds that this does not decide direction: price goes where it goes, and the orders just print as time and sales.

The windows are simply more prone to delivery because order flow is heavier ([2024 #44 · 52:06](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=3126), [2024 #44 · 1:30:48](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=5448)). Elsewhere he says you need not know why; note that it happens and use it as confirmation ([2026 #35 · 7:12](https://youtu.be/KEqzv2yOAf0?t=432)).

### How to use macros

1. **Use time as a filter, not an entry.** In the review where he cites 10:50 to 11:10, the macro validated a turn that already had a bearish breaker, an NDOG rejection and a daily order block behind it ([2026 #65 · 13:36](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=816), [2026 #65 · 21:37](https://youtu.be/JmAJfuSkY1g?t=1297)). A level that agrees with a PD array and a macro is expected to react well ([2024 #10 · 49:23](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=2963)).
2. **Want your idea to move.** If the move you expected in the macro does not come, that is cause for concern; lower your stop. If price consolidates through the whole window, that is a problem for the idea ([2026 #19 · 1:05:12](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=3912)).
3. **Prefer the macro for the entry.** He likes a bullish MSS setup inside a macro ([2024 #4 · 23:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1384)). A setup that forms after 9:50 should have no trouble running into its target ([2024 #13 · 4:08](https://youtu.be/LMi_mO3AP08?t=248)).
4. **You do not have to trade inside it.** An entry one minute after, or an order block or CISD entry outside the window, is fine. If the buy side is being guarded in the macro, you will not get the best fill, so wait ([2026 #32 · 1:04:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3877), [2026 #32 · 1:23:50](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=5030)).

### Worked examples

**8:50 to 9:10, NQ 1-minute chart.** Price fell for about half an hour, ranged inside the macro, and made a new low near 9:00, a drop of roughly 55 to 60 handles from a volume imbalance. It consolidated, took the highs and ran up after 9:10. A hypothetical short was stopped out ([2026 #19 · 1:29:37](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=5377)).

**9:50 to 10:10, Friday 27 February 2026.** He called live that a gap would invert if price traded through it and that price would then run for liquidity in the 9:50 to 10:10 window. He also noted it looked "a little too smooth" ([2026 #126 · 29:44](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=1784), [2026 #126 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=1882)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using a macro for direction.** It says when, not where.
- **Counting any hour-and-minute as a macro.** The pattern is :50 to :10.
- **Assuming it is universal.** The notes say macros vary by index and by day, and 9:50 to 10:10 is typical for US index futures ([2026 #100 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=48)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 "macro" did not mean a clock window. In Episode 39 it meant the short sequence of instructions the algorithm runs: seek a level, move to a premium or discount, move suddenly or consolidate, then wait for another time element ([2022 Ep 39 · 34:29](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2069)). In August 2024 it became a 20-minute window, defined as :50 to :10 by Lecture 4 and refined in Lecture 11.

In October 2024 he gave the rationale, the pre-market set and the 8:30 exception. In 2026 macros appear as confirmations inside trade reviews, and he teases viewers who say they do not exist ([2026 #141 · 33:37](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2017)). Counts for the last hour differ: 2024 Lecture 12 says there are four macros (caption unclear) and names two; 2026 names three (2:50, 3:15, 3:45). No source names a fourth.

## The lunch macro and the PM session

### What it is

From 2024 on, ICT's lunch macro is the two hours from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. He tells viewers not to call it a lunch hour ([2026 #86 · 5:06](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=306)). At 11:30, whatever the morning did rolls against itself: price retraces toward a low (or high) that formed after 10:00, taking liquidity from whoever has been making money ([2024 #12 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1771), [2026 #86 · 16:07](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=967)). He says some retracement against the morning always happens in those two hours, often enough that it could be a model on its own ([2024 #24 · 1:44:15](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=6255)). After 1:30 there is no time element left for taking those stops, so price is free to continue in the morning's direction ([2024 #12 · 34:23](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2063)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation: at about 10:30 the algorithm computes the range high and low and sets the midpoint as equilibrium. Below equilibrium it looks for sell-side liquidity. Two outcomes follow. Price takes the first sell-side pool and then consolidates for the rest of the day, or it trades into trailing stops, triggers the sell side, accumulates and runs higher. He presented this as a hypothetical, arguing the other side's case ([2024 #48 · 32:28](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1948)).

### How to spot it

1. Draw vertical lines at 10:00, 11:30, 12:30 and 1:30. Block out 11:30 to 1:30 on old days and record what forms ([2026 #126 · 1:09:27](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=4167)).
2. At 11:30, ask who made money from 9:30 until now. If the market rallied, longs have stops trailed under a low ([2024 #12 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1771)).
3. Scan left from 11:30 to the first swing low you reach. It must have formed after 10:00. Lows before 10:00 do not count, partly because the 10:00 Silver Bullet run sits in between ([2024 #12 · 30:17](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1817), [2024 #12 · 33:34](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2014)). After a falling morning, use the first high after 10:00 ([2026 #126 · 1:07:53](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=4073)).
4. Choose the level by day type. This is where the 2026 notes disagree; see the changes below.
5. Check timing: the setup optimally forms in the first hour, 11:30 to 12:30. The delivery can take the second hour ([2026 #86 · 32:38](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1958)).

### How to trade it

- **New students stay out.** Do not trade 11:30 to 1:30 while you are new. You will be chopped up. Come back at 1:30 and watch how the PM session sets up ([2026 #141 · 40:00](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2400)).
- **Anchor the entry and stop to the 10:00 hour.** Draw a box across 10:00 to 11:00. From 11:00 scan left to the first high in that hour; that is what the retracement reaches. In his 28 August 2026 NQ example, price pulled up at 11:30 into that high, where his short was taken. The stop belongs just above it, and price then fell to the 30% level of the weekly range ([2026 #13 · 12:06](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=726), [2026 #13 · 11:18](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=678)).
- **Give it time.** The setup usually forms in the first hour and the target is often hit in the second. He prefers to hold through 12:30 to 1:30. If the target is not reached, it may re-run in the PM session ([2026 #79 · 7:11](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=431)).
- **Partials and adds.** Take partials into the 10:00 low at the lunch macro ([2026 #58 · 17:39](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1059)). If you are long from the pre-market, you can pyramid on the lunch retracement ([2024 #48 · 59:48](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3588)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using a low from before 10:00.**
- **Mistaking it for a day reversal.** In a bullish market the retracement is shallow, to intermediate lows only. In a bearish market it can go much deeper ([2026 #102 · 4:07](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=247)).
- **Expecting it every time.** He notes it could also keep going higher with no retracement ([2024 #12 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1771)).

### How the teaching changed

2022 had a one-hour lunch, noon to 1:00 PM, that was a no-trade hour, not even in a demo account. He called it usually not clean price action, and ignored swing lows made in it ([2022 Ep 5 · 9:11](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=551), [2022 Ep 5 · 25:21](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1521)). Entries were barred, but you could take profits, and a limit order at an order block plus a couple of points could fill in it ([2022 Ep 16 · 22:33](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1353)).

By 2024 the window was 11:30 to 1:30 and the retracement itself was the setup. The versions in the notes:

### The PM session

The PM session begins at 1:30 PM ([2024 #22 · 27:02](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1622)). The 1:30 to 2:00 interval is its opening range. ICT's advice: mark the PM highs and lows and the 1:30 to 2:00 interval for the last three days. Liquidity above and below that interval repeats because it is time-based and static, and price tends to draw back into it.

Yesterday's 1:30 to 2:00 range made relative equal lows; price rallied that afternoon, then pierced under them the next morning and reacted ([2024 #44 · 45:59](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=2759), [2024 #44 · 46:46](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=2806)). In 2026 he adds that the PD arrays formed in that interval stay salient for the PM session, the final hour and the 6:00 PM restart ([2026 #86 · 27:09](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1629)).

Fair value gaps and other PD arrays in the first PM half hour are tradeable. The FVG is not a 9:30 to 10:00 "one-trick pony" ([2024 #24 · 1:45:51](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=6351)).

Five afternoon rules from the notes:

1. **No news in the morning means trade the afternoon.** A morning without medium or high-impact data tends to be rangy, with false breakouts, and the cleaner price action comes after lunch ([2024 #3 · 4:29](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=269), [2024 #3 · 1:19:49](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=4789)).
2. **Bond auction, with conditions.** On such a day, a 1:00 PM bond auction (medium impact) can be the catalyst. It only counts when there was no morning news ([2024 #3 · 1:09:22](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=4162)).
3. **A sequence of windows.** Be at the charts by 1:30. The 1:30 to 2:30 window usually holds a sweet spot. If nothing forms from 1:30 to 2:00, use the 2:00 to 3:00 Silver Bullet; if that is missed or sideways, use the 3:00 hour to the close ([2024 #3 · 1:26:15](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=5175)).

In 2022 he preferred 1:30 to 4:00 for the afternoon trend, with a setup usually forming between 2:00 and 3:00 ([2022 Ep 3 · 34:28](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2068)). The afternoon can reverse the morning, continue it, repeat its size (a measured move) or trend after a consolidating morning ([2022 Ep 5 · 29:20](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=1760)).

## The last hour and market on close

### What it is

The last hour is 3:00 to 4:00 PM. ICT's picture is a race: everyone lines up at 8:30 and 9:30, by 3:00 the race has been run and you see who finishes strongest, then comes a flurry in the final hour ([2026 #136 · 48:09](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=2889)). Inside it he names these windows:

- A macro from 3:15 to 3:45.
- Market on close (MOC) from 3:45 to 4:00. He stresses it is 15 minutes, not 10.
- In earnings season, an algorithm that fires at 4:01 and runs into 4:15 ([2024 #12 · 42:19](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2539)).

A 2026 lecture lists 2:50 to 3:10, 3:15 to 3:45 and the MOC as the last-hour macros, and the 2024 notes say there are four (caption unclear) ([2026 #123 · 13:08](https://youtu.be/CodF_0llnuk?t=788)). The last hour is the only hour with several macros ([2024 #11 · 1:00:46](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=3646)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation: from 3:45 the algorithm refers back to daily and weekly levels. It cycles from the higher timeframes down to seconds and back up, with every inefficiency and liquidity pool incorporated. He calls this casting a lure toward the higher-timeframe level ([2024 #12 · 39:09](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2349)). In 2022 he traced the 3:00 to 4:00 behaviour to the old bond close; now the 3 PM to 4 PM closing bell sets the tone for on-close orders ([2022 Ep 32 · 11:32](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=692)).

### How to trade it

- **Pay yourself after 3:00.** If you have not taken anything by then, take something off. Hold a minimum until 3:15 ([2024 #12 · 15:08](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=908), [2024 #12 · 35:10](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2110)).
- **On a daily trend day.** Hold into 3:15 at least. The 3:15 to 3:45 retracement can be treated as noise, and the 3:45 run is aimed at the higher-timeframe target ([2024 #12 · 39:09](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2349)).

### Worked example: a consolidation-day short, 2022

On a consolidation day the market builds a range and stays inside it until 3:00 PM. Between 3:00 and 4:00 it often trades outside the range and then rams back to the middle. Before 3:00 the rally aims at buy-side liquidity. Traders see a breakout and buy, and smart money pairs with them to go short. ICT marks verticals at 3 PM and 4 PM and studies the midpoint of the day's range ([2022 Ep 32 · 5:49](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=349), [2022 Ep 32 · 6:39](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=399)).

His example: shorts from the prior day had trailed their stops above old highs. Price bumped them shallowly, then rammed through the equal highs to above 3,933.25. On the 1-minute chart a swing low broke, an FVG formed, and price retraced into it.

He sold at 3,940.75, took four contracts off at 3,908.75 (mid-range) and left a runner with a limit at 3,855 ([2022 Ep 32 · 8:14](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=494), [2022 Ep 32 · 9:07](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=547)). Entries are Chapter 18; the clock point is that the trap sits in the last hour. He adds that most traders are too afraid to short after a breakout run in the last hour, which is the edge.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating the MOC as 10 minutes.** It is 15, from 3:45 to 4:00.
- **Treating the 3:15 to 3:45 pullback as a reversal.** On a trend day it is noise.
- **Closing at 3:00 by reflex.** The rule is to take something off, not necessarily everything.

## Silver Bullet windows

### What it is

ICT uses "Silver Bullet" for an hour-long time window in which he looks for a time-based entry. The model belongs to Chapter 17; this is the clock part.

- **10:00 to 11:00 AM** is the main one. He says it is always there, and it is the main focus of his day on some lectures ([2024 #9 · 13:01](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=781), [2024 #9 · 28:03](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1683)). In 2026 he calls it the first of everybody's ([2026 #55 · 1:44](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=104)).
- **2:00 to 3:00 PM** is the afternoon fallback, and a 3:00 to 4:00 PM window is also named, as captioned ([2024 #3 · 1:26:15](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=5175), [2024 #20 · 48:16](https://youtu.be/NMCy1H2_Wr8?t=2896)).

### How to use the windows

- **As a fallback.** If price expands strongly into the NWOG before the open, he stands aside and waits for the 10:00 window ([2024 #4 · 23:04](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1384)). With no obvious idea by 10:00, submit to it ([2026 #55 · 1:44](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=104)).
- **No touch, no trade.** If the idea does not materialise, wait until about 11:00 to 11:30 and fade whatever the market did, treating it as a lunch macro. If the retracement has not come before 1:00 PM, expect it in the afternoon ([2024 #9 · 47:09](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2829)).
- **After a 10:00 release.** Do not take it until the first 5 to 10 minutes have passed, 15 in the worst case ([2024 #48 · 55:00](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=3300)).
- **Use the opening range for context.** The initial buy side, sell side and any initial PD array give context for the 10:00 trade ([2024 #27 · 14:48](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=888)).

## For gold and CFDs

ICT taught this chapter on index futures (NQ, ES, YM) and on FX pairs. The notes contain no rule specific to gold.

- **The clock carries over for FX-style trading.** He says everything he taught works in forex, not just index futures, and that the London kill zone is the same for forex and index futures ([2022 Ep 25 · 45:12](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2712), [2026 #138 · 31:22](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1882)). The 2022 FX New York window was 7:00 to 10:00 AM ([2022 Ep 17 · 9:52](https://youtu.be/5WIqHJDQ_p4?t=592)), and 10:00 to 11:00 was the London-close profit-taking hour ([2022 Ep 40 · 16:50](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=1010)).
- **The RTH pieces are equity-index pieces.** The 9:30 opening range, the 4:14 settlement gap, the 3:45 market on close and the 4:00 PM bell belong to the cash-equity session. The notes do not say whether gold respects them. Test it on your own data, as ICT tells you to ([2022 Ep 39 · 1:13:33](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4413)).
- **Macros.** The notes teach them on NQ and ES and say they vary by index and by day. Nothing in them says gold respects :50 to :10 windows.
- **CFDs.** The only CFD remark is that NQ and ES CFD traders typically close at the 11:30 London close ([2024 #18 · 1:31:08](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=5468)).

On NQ and MNQ, everything in this chapter applies as taught.

## Studying the clock

Each of these is something ICT asks students to do, and each makes the windows concrete.

- **Count setups at the key times.** Go back a month, three weeks, two weeks and one week. Count how many setups at these times offered 5 points with a liquidity run plus an FVG, and do the maths for one mini contract. He says backtesting is the only cure for the fear of missing setups that form after you leave the screen ([2022 Ep 39 · 1:13:33](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=4413), [2022 Ep 39 · 29:45](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=1785)).
- **Block out 11:30 to 1:30 on every old day** and record what forms and where ([2026 #126 · 1:09:27](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=4167)).
- **Log the 7:00 to 9:00 range** with its high, low, equal levels and phase, and the time from 9:30 until price reacted off a key liquidity level ([2026 #14 · 11:44](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=704)).
- **Be rigid about when you do not trade.** Time-based windows create accountability: if the setup does not form between 10:00 and 11:00, turn the charts off ([2024 #37 · 1:27:53](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=5273), [2024 #49 · 20:34](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=1234)).

## Check yourself

1. Your TradingView chart is on UTC and you mark "9:30" and "11:30" by the chart clock. What is wrong, and what does ICT say happens when you do this?
2. A broker's indicator shades 7:00 PM to 5:00 AM as the "Asian kill zone". What is the mistake, and where should you look for liquidity versus where should you expect the entry?
3. It is a bearish day. The midnight open is 20,100 and the 8:30 candle opens at 20,080 (illustrative prices). Which open sets the minimum threshold for the Judas rally, and what is the bullish mirror of that rule?
4. The 7:00 to 9:00 AM range trends from a 7:00 high down into a key higher-timeframe level. What does ICT expect from the 9:30 to 11:30 session, and what changes if a release is due at 8:30?
5. A tutorial tells you to mark a 15-minute opening range and take the first break at 9:36. What does ICT say about the range, and what does he tell beginners about entries in that window?
6. A friend says a run at 10:40 "was a macro". Is it? Define the macro windows, say what a macro tells you about direction, and say what to do if the move you expected does not come.
7. The morning rallied from 9:30. Lows formed at 9:52, 10:29 and 11:17. At 11:30 which low is the lunch macro's target under the basic rule, which are excluded, and when does the lunch macro end?
8. NQ has made a large overnight run and the New York open is near. What does ICT say to do in the NY AM session, and where does he tell you to look later?
9. You are long on a daily trend day and it is 3:05 PM. What are the rules for the next hour?
10. A friend says gold must reverse at 9:50 to 10:10 because "macros are in the notes". What do the notes support, and what should you do?

## Answers

1. UTC is not New York time, so every window is mis-timed ("off time, late or early"). ICT says the setup fails on local time and the algorithm runs on NY time. Set the chart to New York. Reread: New York time is the clock.
2. That box is the whole session. A kill zone is a narrower window inside it (for Asia, a 2-hour window read as 7 to 9 PM). Search the whole session for smooth highs and lows; expect the entry in the kill zone. Reread: Sessions and kill zones.
3. The lower one, the 8:30 open at 20,080. Bearish with an 8:30 open below the midnight open, ICT uses the lower open; he says to mirror that for bullish. Reread: Choosing between midnight and 8:30.
4. A trending 7 to 9 range means the AM session likely chops, and trending into a key higher-timeframe level makes chop more likely still. On a data day, frame 7:00 to 8:30 and start looking about 15 minutes after the release (Chapter 14). Reread: The pre-market range.
5. The opening range is 30 minutes, 9:30 to 10:00, never 15 or 5. Beginners take no entries in it; they mark, wait and journal. Reread: The opening range.
6. No. A macro is the 20 minutes from :50 to :10 around the hour; 10:40 is not one, and there are no 20 to 40 or 30 to 40-minute macros. A macro gives no direction, only a time when price should start to run. If the expected move does not arrive, that is a warning and you lower your stop. Reread: Macros.
7. The 11:17 low is the first low you hit scanning left from 11:30 that formed after 10:00. The 9:52 low is excluded because it is before 10:00. The macro window closes at 1:30. (On trend days his February 2026 version says to look back for the most prominent low instead of the first; the notes do not reconcile the two.) Reread: The lunch macro.
8. For index futures ICT says skip the NY AM, since chasing the run is how you lose, and wait for after 1:00 PM. Anticipate a raid on the lunch lows or the morning lows. Reread: Sessions and kill zones, Mistakes.
9. Take something off after 3:00, hold a minimum until 3:15, treat the 3:15 to 3:45 retracement as noise, and expect the 3:45 to 4:00 market-on-close run to aim at a higher-timeframe level. Reread: The last hour.
10. Nothing gold-specific. The macros were taught on NQ and ES, and the notes do not say gold respects them. Test it on your own data and journal it. Reread: For gold and CFDs.
