ICT splits price delivery into two kinds. The algorithm runs to liquidity or reprices inefficiencies with precision. Manual intervention is abrupt, one-directional and indifferent to your levels, and he says scheduled news (NFP, CPI, PPI, FOMC, Fed speakers) is where it shows up. After this chapter you can read the economic calendar the way he does, apply the waiting rule that fits each event, tell when precision has returned after a release, and scale down or sit out event-driven weeks.

## Algorithm versus manual intervention

### What it is

ICT describes every move as controlled and premeditated. It comes either from the algorithm following its code or from manual intervention ([2022 Ep 39 · 41:44](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2504)). The algorithm does only two things: it runs to liquidity, or it reprices to an inefficiency ([2024 #37 · 1:11:47](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4307)). Liquidity is covered in Chapter 3 and inefficiencies in Chapter 5.

Normal algorithmic delivery goes up a little and comes back a little. Manual intervention is the opposite: FOMC, CPI or NFP-type delivery in one direction with no pause. Once it starts it is often untradeable; he compares chasing it to chasing the wind ([2024 #1 · 55:38](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=3338)).

In 2024 he sharpened the label. Intervention is abrupt, sudden and forceful, with no chance to get in; he likens it to a "controlled demolition" rather than organic buying or selling pressure ([2024 #7 · 2:47:42](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10062)). In 2022 he had already said that an announcement such as FOMC sends price to a level well outside what is reasonable for the short term ([2022 Ep 39 · 41:44](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2504)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the same liquidity logic still applies on news days. NFP is "not algorithmic": in his telling, the people behind the move pick where it goes and send it there abruptly. Price still heads for liquidity or an inefficiency. It just gets there abruptly and farther outside the normal range ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)).

That is why he calls intervention the one thing that always makes his model wrong. Nobody can predict it, geopolitical shocks belong in the same bucket, and it is his main reason never to over-leverage ([2024 #1 · 2:07:06](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7626)). Gaps can also jump far beyond your stop, which is why brokers have you sign risk disclosures ([2024 #1 · 28:07](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=1687)). These are his claims; the chapter teaches the behaviour rules that follow.

### How to spot it

1. Moves are abrupt and one-way, with no pauses, around a scheduled release or a headline.
2. Price overshoots highs and lows by a wide margin and does not lay the high or low onto anything precise ([2024 #40 · 48:05](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2885)).
3. PD arrays (FVGs, order blocks, old levels) are not respected: price stops and turns for no reason ([2024 #40 · 42:30](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2550)).
4. Price holds a range with the low untaken, the high unbroken, many wicks and volume imbalances. ICT reads that as a holding pattern, not as the algorithm at work ([2024 #46 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1771)).
5. A major headline arrives and price moves 500 or more handles in minutes, or an off-hours move is out of proportion to the news ([2026 #109 · 8:43](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=523)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Precision is the dividing line.** Algorithmic delivery respects PD arrays and lands on levels. Intervention overshoots, or stalls for no reason.
- **"Phil".** In 2026 ICT used the name "Phil" for a sudden, local spike or drop of one to three candles with no follow-through. The telltale is a sharp move into a level that reverses on the next candle, or a long wick that closes back inside. He says Phil is not a real person ([2026 #111 · 1:29:11](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5351)). His advice is to note it, not trade the spike, and treat it as evidence that the opposite move is likely ([2026 #111 · 1:30:47](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=5447)).
- **Not every news day is intervention all day.** The label fits the minutes around the release. Afterwards price can return to script, and the test for that is in The precision test below.

- [2024 #7 · 2:47:42](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10062): "controlled demolition" and FOMC.

## The economic calendar filter

### What it is

The filter is a habit: before you plan any session, look at which scheduled releases fall in it. ICT checks the calendar on the weekend, for the whole next month, looking for heavy, medium and high-impact news drivers ([2024 #21 · 1:08:12](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4092)). For the week itself he checks forexfactory.com or any calendar before trading starts ([2024 #25 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=682)).

Which events count? Medium and high impact only; low impact is ignored. He has used Econoday since the 1990s, and also Forex Factory. He cares about US data, Fed-related events (the caption is unclear) and bond auctions, and says speeches matter less ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)). On the calendar, red-folder events are high impact and orange are medium. The no-touch rule applies to red; orange events can be traded ([2024 #40 · 1:49:55](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6595)).

The release times he works with are 8:30 ET (NFP, CPI, PPI, retail sales), 10:00 ET (ISM, JOLTS), the afternoon FOMC statement at 2:00 PM with the press conference at 2:30 PM, and Fed chair speeches at whatever time they are scheduled (10:00 AM and 2:00 PM in his 2022 examples). He also scans the 9:30 to 10:30 first hour of the session for medium or high releases ([2024 #49 · 7:48](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=468)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the report is a "smoke screen": an injection of volatility that invites people to the watering hole. The data is not the cause of the move ([2024 #25 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713)). In 2026 he put it as: events are catalysts, not causes. The levels and order flow were already set, and the event just triggers the execution ([2026 #33 · 0:49](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=49)).

Red-folder events also leave marks. He says they polarize the highs and lows formed around the event time, which then act as magnets and starting points. In one 2026 example a Monday 8:30 PPI print made the low, and price then ran to the predicted high at 25,715. The notes add that the 25,715 target is a separate idea, anchored to a suspension block, not to the news itself ([2026 #103 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/G9mVorSyvR0?t=332)).

On the raw numbers he is blunt. They are "already baked in", markets often do the opposite of what the data suggests, and when price reverses the excuse is always "it priced it in", which nobody said beforehand ([2024 #46 · 10:39](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=639), [2024 #49 · 2:10:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7836)). In 2026 he adds that he finds the NFP data untrustworthy and never cared about the figures ([2026 #40 · 14:16](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=856)).

### How to trade it

Use the calendar to set the standard for price, not to pick a direction.

| Calendar state | What ICT does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No news driver | Demand clean price. Absence of news means you must absolutely see clean price action, so skip marginal setups. He tells you to journal this. | [2024 #25 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713) |
| No news, especially a Monday | Higher chance of a high-resistance day: either a consolidation or a one-sided day with a big Judas swing. Do the work pre-market, 7:00 to 9:30 ET. | [2024 #49 · 7:48](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=468) |
| No medium or high news in the morning | Price can be aimless and choppy from 8:30. He does not hold overnight and does not take more trades than he should. | [2024 #5 · 1:34:50](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5690) |
| News at 8:30 or in the 9:30 to 10:30 hour | A "low resistance" climate, because the release is a smoke screen the algorithm uses. He says news is synonymous with that climate but does not guarantee it. | [2024 #49 · 2:08:14](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7694) |
| News, but price is wild back and forth | Do not trade that either. Much of the skill is knowing when not to trade. | [2024 #25 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713) |

Low and high resistance are the subject of Chapter 16.

The clean-price standard works the other way as well. With a release at 8:30 or 10:00 he can tolerate sloppier price, because the report supplies the volatility. If you want to avoid news volatility altogether, ICT's example was a week with PPI and CPI on Wednesday and Thursday and nothing on Monday or Tuesday: trade Monday and Tuesday, and he will also trade after PPI and CPI ([2024 #25 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=682)).

### When the news is on a currency pair

In 2022 ICT framed the morning from the pair carrying the news. USDCAD had the high-impact release, so he skipped the DXY. If USDCAD is expected to fall into a discount or run sell-side liquidity, the dollar is weaker, which is risk-on, and ES, NQ and foreign currencies rally more easily. The markets are not "correlated"; there is only risk-on and risk-off ([2022 Ep 40 · 40:47](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2447), [2022 Ep 40 · 44:46](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2686)). That morning ES rallied at the 9:30 period while USDCAD traded down into its discount ([2022 Ep 40 · 41:35](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=2495)). Intermarket bias is Chapter 15.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **"News day" does not mean "trade the news".** In the model ICT built for his son in 2022, you engage on medium or high-impact news days, and on no-news days you may practise but not at your normal risk percentage ([2022 Ep 39 · 42:32](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2552)). The later rules in this chapter govern what to do at the release itself: stay out, then trade what is left.
- **Reading the number.** Whether jobs created or lost beat the forecast is irrelevant to his method, because the chart will tell you everything ([2024 #24 · 9:38](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=578)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** "engage on news days" in the son's model (see Mistakes above), plus the habit of knowing when volatility will come and when it shrinks ([2022 Ep 29 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=198)).
- **2024:** the filter becomes two-sided. News gives permission for sloppier price; no news demands cleaner price ([2024 #25 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713)).
- **2026:** the same filter, plus the red-folder magnet idea, and a worked calendar: Monday empty, Tuesday CPI, Wednesday PPI and retail sales, Thursday Philly Fed and jobless claims. He calls the CPI and PPI days "face rippers", effectively gambling to trade through, and Monday a holding pattern forced onto pure PD arrays ([2026 #138 · 16:55](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1015)).

### Watch it

- [2024 #25 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=682): checking the week's calendar.
- [2024 #25 · 28:33](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=1713): smoke screen and demanding clean price.

## The release rule: NFP, CPI and PPI at 8:30 ET

### What it is

NFP (non-farm payroll) comes out at 8:30 ET on the first Friday of the month ([2026 #40 · 2:29](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=149)). CPI and PPI are 8:30 ET releases as well ([2024 #40 · 2:36](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=156)). In 2026 ICT calls NFP, CPI and PPI "the three-letter trades" to avoid, and says he is generally wrong about them in advance ([2026 #2 · 34:46](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2086)).

The rule has three parts:

1. Do not position ahead of the number.
2. Do not trade the release itself.
3. Wait, then work with whatever liquidity or inefficiency the move left behind.

### Why it works

ICT's reasons are mostly about execution and risk, not prediction.

- **Fills fall apart.** His example: aiming to buy 18,845 on NQ and being filled at 18,862 or worse, which wrecks your risk. Add requotes, or no fill at all ([2024 #24 · 55:22](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=3322)).
- **Brokers step back.** He says brokers "pull their engagement" because retail over-leverages funded accounts into these events. Liquidity exists; what disappears is execution, which he calls the illusion of illiquidity ([2024 #24 · 13:47](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=827)). He adds that a limit order at the opening price of the run (plus one tick) may fill about 20 minutes later, not in the first three minutes or so.
- **Size of the move.** A single 1-minute NQ candle can travel 250 or more handles on NFP ([2024 #40 · 1:49:55](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6595)).
- **No time to act.** You cannot read the data, decide and execute in time, and limit orders will not work ([2026 #2 · 34:46](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2086)).
- **Direction is not the problem.** Being right on direction does not save you. ICT says he lost heavily trading NFP in his 20s with indicators and signal services ([2026 #2 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254)). On CPI and PPI he says his anticipated direction for the first delivery was right about 2% of the time, 98% wrong (as he states it) ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)).

The same logic covers holding a trade through the print. Consolidation before CPI is warranted as people square positions. He will not participate: "your stop loss isn't going to save you", open profit can evaporate into a net loss, and this is not the last trading day of your career. If you are long into the report and it works, he will not congratulate you, because that is not what a good risk manager does ([2026 #63 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/bz4QNPcpmWE?t=774), [2026 #63 · 14:28](https://youtu.be/bz4QNPcpmWE?t=868)).

For a gold CFD trader the broker point is the one to take seriously. ICT is talking about retail brokers in general, and the notes do not say how any particular CFD broker behaves.

### How to trade it

The waiting rules differ a little by lecture, so here they are side by side.

| Situation | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| NFP, 8:30 ET | Do not position ahead or trade the release. Earliest entry about 15 minutes after (8:45 ET); 20 to 30 minutes is better. If already in, a small runner may stay. | [2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481) |
| NFP, CPI, PPI (2026) | Wait about 15 to 20 minutes into the session and see what liquidity is left in the wake. | [2026 #2 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254) |
| CPI and PPI (2024) | Do not trade in advance. After the report give it 15 to 20 minutes, then look at what liquidity or inefficiency is left. | [2024 #25 · 34:29](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=2069) |
| Any high-impact report | Minimum 15 minutes, then study the 1-minute and 15-second charts and run the midpoint test (next section). | [2024 #40 · 42:30](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2550) |
| Several reports at 8:30 | The first impulse may not be the last. Give it a couple of seconds; it may take both sides before it is done. | [2024 #46 · 18:52](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1132) |

A step order that fits all of them:

1. **Before the release.** On the 15-minute chart, frame the buy-side and sell-side pools and note which is more likely to be run. This is a clue, not an entry. He is "not always right" ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)).
2. **Be flat at 8:30.** ICT says never be in the market going into the release. If you are already in a trade when it hits, he allows leaving a small runner.
3. **Let the shock finish.** Do not judge the first burst.
4. **Mark what is left behind.** After the shock, look for smooth edges, inefficiencies and untouched pools. That is what price goes for next ([2024 #24 · 9:38](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=578)).
5. **Test for precision** before any entry (next section).

ICT's 2026 version of step 4 is to fade the reaction. After the first burst, wait for a meaningful retracement, measure from the resulting high, and trade back into the range the news delivered. His rule of thumb: whatever the market does first, he fades back into that range. In the example, shorts came from premium after a rally, and he committed only part of the position to the deeper levels ([2026 #2 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254)). The trigger is a one-directional rip from 8:30 with no retracement, then a first pullback that leaves a short-term high or low.

He frames this for advanced, contrarian students and does not want newer students engaging with NFP at all. He also expects a deeper retracement going into the 9:30 open after a straight-up 8:30 move.

CPI follows the same idea with one condition. Let it run, then usually fade the move. News can deliver in two stages like FOMC. But inside a trending run the two-stage pattern will not appear, the day is one-sided, and unless you were in ahead of time (a gamble) you will not participate. On a range-bound day CPI was an easy trade after 10:00, after waiting a little ([2026 #121 · 0:02](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=2), [2026 #121 · 0:51](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=51)).

### Worked example

NQ, Friday 6 September 2024, NFP. Before 8:30 price had already traded down to the 13 August daily FVG low, about 18,765. That was his target, and he had covered his short there. After the number price did three things ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)).

1. It ran up to a minor 5-minute buy-side level.
2. It jammed down, took a short-term low and did not break the main 15-minute low.
3. It ripped through the buy side, in a single 5-minute candle while wicking around on the 1-minute, and went into the New Day Opening Gap before 9:30 (NDOG, Chapter 11).

[[DIAGRAM ch14_d3 | caption: NFP, NQ, 6 September 2024: up, jam down, rip into the NDOG (schematic of the sequence, not to scale) | Notice that the first leg up was not the move, and that price had two legs to run before the 9:30 open.]]

Note what the notes show him doing before the release: he had already covered his short at the target. Nothing in them has him entering on the number itself, and the shock was left to show where price went.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Taking the first run.** With a single report at 8:30 the first run is usually the Judas swing. With several reports it is staggered ([2024 #46 · 18:52](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1132)).
- **Assuming FOMC-style two-stage always happens.** Sometimes the 2:00 move runs straight to the close; if you missed it, you are probably not getting in anyway ([2026 #2 · 37:57](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2277)).
- **Using the pre-release framing as an entry.** 2024 #3 shows how he reads a lean going into a 1:00 release: price had rolled over, left an FVG whose bodies respected it, and displaced lower into the 1:00 auction. He lists three draws below, the day-session low since 7:00, the London session low (a relative equal low) and the NDOG as the "black hole". The note does not say he traded it. His point is that buying and selling after the release does not cause the move, because the market is already running before the number ([2024 #3 · 1:36:44](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=5804), [2024 #3 · 1:39:12](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=5952)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** simple avoidance. The day before a Fed chair speech is quiet and tradable with experience; the day itself brings heavy volatility, and you will probably hurt yourself ([2022 Ep 29 · 0:52](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=52), [2022 Ep 29 · 16:50](https://youtu.be/QkokVv0owSE?t=1010)).
- **2024:** adds the explicit 15-minute rule, the slippage and broker mechanics, and the pre-release framing as a clue only ([2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481)).
- **2026:** two applications that point in different directions. In the October lecture he fades the reaction and stays flat through the print. In the 13 August PPI review (covered in Managing a news-day trade) he rides the first move into targets he marked earlier and says the direction is often predictable if daily levels are marked. The notes do not reconcile the two, and neither matches his remark that he is generally wrong ahead of CPI. Treat the PPI review as a demonstration, and the stay-flat rule as the rule.

- [2024 #24 · 8:01](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=481): NFP is not algorithmic; wait 15 to 30 minutes.
- [2026 #2 · 4:14](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=254): never position ahead; wait 15 to 20 minutes.

## Why the first run is the fake one

### What it is

On a high-impact news driver, ICT says the first run is the fake one. He pictures the "first mouse" getting caught, and you collect the cheese afterwards. His advice to students is to trade only after the initial shock wave and to hold no opinion going in ([2024 #21 · 1:09:47](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4187), [2024 #21 · 1:08:59](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=4139)).

Two refinements. With a single report at 8:30 the first run is usually the Judas swing, the false early move of the day (Chapter 13). With several reports at 8:30 the initial rally or decline is staggered: it feels done and is not. Give it a couple of seconds, because another impulse may come and take both sides of the market ([2024 #46 · 18:52](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1132)).

### Why it works

ICT gives three explanations that fit together.

- **Smoke screen.** The 8:30 event sometimes helps run price into a layer of liquidity he had already outlined. Other times it drops price so everyone thinks sentiment is bearish, and the 9:30 open then makes the run up. Either way, watch what price does at 8:30 and judge the open from that ([2024 #46 · 9:00](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=540)).
- **Trap, then speed.** Algorithms use the release for a Judas-style manipulation that puts retail on the wrong side in the first 30 minutes, sets the session high or low, then moves fast so trapped traders cannot exit. Price "falls out of bed" ([2024 #49 · 2:10:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7836)).
- **Unseating the profitable.** NFP is used many times to destabilise sentiment and unseat people who are currently in profit, then to go the other way. ICT calls this the pain mechanism ([2024 #42 · 24:04](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=1444)). He also labels the idea "Boogeyman trading" ([2024 #42 · 1:31:10](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=5470)).

### How to spot it

1. Map the crowd's stops before the release. In the pain-mechanism example the profitable traders were holding Tuesday's shorts after the drop into the weekly BISI (Chapter 5). Their stops sit at least above the consolidation high.
2. Look for a high slightly lower than the previous high. That makes a high-probability relative equal high, a prime candidate to be taken.
3. Remember where institutional stops live. ICT says large institutions do not trail stops to breakeven after a 10-handle move. They leave core protective stops outside the normal range, so they do not need a trade manager watching every fluctuation. The large pool is therefore at the highs, not at the bottom of the nearby inefficiency ([2024 #42 · 51:58](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3118), [2024 #42 · 1:03:37](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3817)).
4. After the release, ask whether the move took out a stop pool. A move that rises straight from 8:30 with no stop run and no candle taking out trailed sell stops is a premium being built ahead of the 9:30 open ([2024 #42 · 51:58](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=3118)).

### How to trade it

Before the release, ICT outlines the liquidity pools. At 8:30 he observes, marks the range and the volume imbalances the release created, and builds his 9:30 plan from there ([2024 #46 · 9:00](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=540)). He does not trade the 8:30 move itself.

Normally he likes to hold a position into the 9:30 open on a high or medium impact news day, but on 17 October 2024 there was nothing he would have entered on, so he stayed flat ([2024 #46 · 8:08](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=488)).

### Worked example

NQ, Friday 4 October 2024, NFP. Price had dropped to a weekly BISI (a weekly bullish FVG, the box drawn from Monday) and then did little. That left Tuesday's shorts in profit. NFP then lifted price straight up from 8:30 toward a primary buy side above Tuesday's relative equal highs ([2024 #42 · 24:04](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=1444)).

ICT laid out three scenarios and said he could not pick one:

- (a) Run up to knock out the shorts, then go lower next week without taking out the lows.
- (b) Go down and trip the shorts, then pump all day into the weekend, then lower next week.
- (c) Move around in the middle and leave both pools standing.

He says explicitly that he can be wrong, and that he was wrong on direction early on. The takeaway is not the forecast. It is the identification of who is vulnerable (profitable shorts with stops above a relative equal high) and the discipline of not committing to a scenario before price shows it.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Fading the first run blindly.** The first run being "fake" is not a signal to take the opposite side at 8:31. The waiting rules still apply.
- **Believing the headline explains the move.** Reports never explain direction; when price does the opposite, the excuse arrives afterwards ([2024 #49 · 2:10:36](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=7836)).

## The precision test: when the algorithm is back

### What it is

After a high-impact release, price is not tradable just because the clock says 8:45. ICT's test is whether PD arrays (FVGs, order blocks, old levels) are being respected again. While manual intervention is under way they are not. Price stops and turns for no reason, overshoots highs and lows, and does not lay the extreme onto anything precise ([2024 #40 · 48:05](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2885)).

### How to spot it

1. **Wait at least 15 minutes.** Then study the 1-minute and sub-1-minute (15-second) price delivery ([2024 #40 · 42:30](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2550)).
2. **Run the midpoint test.** Find a fair value gap and watch its consequent encroachment (CE), the 50% midpoint (Chapter 6). If price eats straight through the whole FVG with no respect for the midpoint, intervention is still on, so sit still ([2024 #40 · 43:18](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2598)).
3. **Look for the green light.** When PD arrays start stopping price "on the tick" and the CE supports price, the algorithm is back in control and precision tools work again.
4. **Distrust a tempting entry.** While intervention is underway the entry looks ready to pluck, but it bites. In ICT's phrase, opportunities are "cleverly disguised impossibilities" ([2024 #40 · 46:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2789)).

The same test applies to a Fed chair speech. After the speaker stops and 15 minutes pass, check whether PD arrays are respected to the tick. If they are not, don't trade ([2024 #37 · 1:15:49](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4549)).

### How to trade it

No respect means no entry. Wait until PD arrays are respected consistently, then trade normally ([2024 #40 · 42:30](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2550)). ICT's homework for viewers was to open the 1-minute and 15-second charts around the Thursday 8:30 employment data and the Friday 8:30 NFP in that week and see the absence of precision for themselves.

When price just sits in a range after the number, the rule is to sit still too. ICT calls the range a holding pattern: the algorithm is not doing anything, and someone's hands are stirring the pot. You are competing against an entity that can take both sides of the market and still go nowhere. By entering you give up your power of stillness and are at the mercy of the outcome. If price takes off without you, let it go and catch the next bus stop ([2024 #46 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1771), [2024 #46 · 30:21](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1821)).

### Worked example

NQ, Thursday 17 October 2024. Several reports landed at 8:30, and they "didn't do very much". Price stayed inside the range created at 8:30: that low was not taken, that high was not broken, and there were wicks and volume imbalances inside it. ICT's verdict was that there was nothing in there he would want to be buying, so he sat still and read the 8:30 delivery. He used it to make a point about tape reading: observation comes before even a demo account ([2024 #46 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1771), [2024 #46 · 31:11](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1871)).

The second example is NQ on Wednesday 2 October 2024, in NFP week. A number came out at 8:15 ET and price ranged around the top of a weekly BISI with minor liquidity above and below. ICT only annotated, and said any trade idea could be upset by manual moves ([2024 #40 · 2:36](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=156)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **One test is not a system.** The CE check is his "quick test". It tells you whether intervention is still on, not whether a setup exists. After the green light you still need a setup (Chapter 17) and an entry (Chapter 18).
- **Confusing consolidation with respect.** A range full of wicks and volume imbalances is not precision. It is a holding pattern.

- [2024 #46 · 29:31](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1771): the holding pattern and the power of stillness.

## FOMC and the two-stage delivery

### What it is

FOMC (the Federal Open Market Committee, the Fed's rate-setting meeting) releases its statement at 2:00 PM ET and holds a press conference at 2:30 PM ([2024 #7 · 2:47:42](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10062)). ICT says FOMC behaves like NFP, only more exaggerated, and is heavily manipulated.

The two-stage delivery, as he described it in 2024:

1. At 2:00 PM price goes one direction. He does not claim to know which.
2. At the 2:30 PM press conference there is a counter-run, usually but not always, over the top of the earlier move ([2024 #29 · 12:52](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=772)).

His 2022 version has more legs: a knee-jerk initial leg, then another leg, then a fake-out leg, and only then the real setup ([2022 Ep 23 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=100)).

[[DIAGRAM ch14_d2 | caption: The FOMC pattern from the 2022 MNQ example: the first leg after 2:00 PM ends in a discount zone near 2:30 PM, then price runs the opposite liquidity (schematic) | Notice that the target zone for the first leg is marked before it arrives, and the trade comes after it.]]

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is the same as for NFP: it is intervention, delivered abruptly. Large players do not position ahead of FOMC, so the day before is ranging and choppy with consolidations and less crisp price. He says it makes no sense to position ahead of the announcement ([2024 #29 · 15:16](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=916)). A trade taken ahead of it is a "gambler's setup", and he does not count it ([2022 Ep 23 · 14:23](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=863)).

### How to trade it

ICT's 2022 walk-through, offered for study and not as an invitation to trade, runs like this:

1. Let the 2:00 PM leg develop. After 2:00, wait a few minutes for the initial shakeout before outlining anything ([2022 Ep 23 · 0:06](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=6)).
2. Mark where the first leg should end. In the example that was a discount FVG below 50% of the range, near sell-side liquidity, which he called the end of the bears' race.
3. Wait for price to deliver into the zone. Usually by around 2:30 the initial leg that "prices in" the event, high or low, is finished and the real move starts ([2022 Ep 23 · 11:10](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=670)).
4. Look for the turn and the run on the opposite liquidity. In the example that was Tuesday's high.

The 2026 version is to expect two attempts at a resistance level and use the second break to enter the main move. ICT says that entry is lower-risk, and that 2:40 to 2:50 PM is usually when the sweet-spot entry forms ([2026 #9 · 5:12](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=312), [2026 #9 · 14:00](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=840)).

The pattern is not guaranteed. Sometimes the 2:00 move runs to the close ([2026 #2 · 37:57](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=2277)). And ICT warns that FOMC and NFP are extremely risky; they are great to study in hindsight or watch live as a case study of liquidity runs.

### What to do on and before FOMC day

| Day | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FOMC day, beginner | Avoid it entirely, so there is no regret or temptation. | [2024 #29 · 12:52](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=772) |
| Day before | Only the morning session. Do not position large or swing-trade into it. The afternoon is dead and difficult. | [2024 #29 · 1:14:14](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4454) |
| FOMC day, 2022 | You can trade the morning but must be done early. If stopped at breakeven, move to the sidelines for the day (the caption naming the event is unclear, likely FOMC). | [2022 Ep 15 · 6:45](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=405) |
| FOMC day, 2026 | Trade only the first hour (to about 10:30 AM ET), or wait until after 4:00 PM ET when he says the true move begins. Avoid mid-day. Stay neutral and do not predict the decision; anything else is observation, not real money. | [2026 #89 · 0:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=38) |

### Worked example

MNQ, an FOMC afternoon in the 2022 mentorship (the notes give no date). ICT waits for the initial shakeout. After 2:00 the market drops. He marks a blue zone as the end of the road for the initial leg, a discount FVG below 50% of the range. Shortly after 2:30 price drops into it, turns, and runs Tuesday's high ([2022 Ep 23 · 0:06](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=6), [2022 Ep 23 · 11:10](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=670)).

In the 2026 NQ September FOMC review, price pierced Tuesday's high near 2:00 PM, came back down, and at about 2:40 PM broke through again and continued lower. The note puts the announcement at about 2:15 PM and does not say which level broke, so treat the clock times as approximate. It was the first FOMC with a new Fed chair, which may be more volatile, and ICT was cautious about early partials for that reason ([2026 #9 · 5:12](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=312)). On the April 2026 FOMC morning price ground inside the opening range gap (Chapter 11) with failed attempts either way and no conviction until after the announcement. He explains that market makers do not push hard beforehand because they have afternoon plans ([2026 #89 · 0:38](https://youtu.be/tbEzAhdv_Ak?t=38)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** leg, leg, fake-out leg, then the real setup; the first leg is done by roughly 2:30.
- **2024:** a two-step: 2:00 move, then a usual 2:30 counter-run.
- **2026 #9:** the two stages become a pre-decision expectation move and a post-decision response move, with the second break as the entry. That is a different cut of the day from the 2:00 and 2:30 split.
- **2026 #89:** adds an after-4:00 PM window. The note ties it to the 6:00 PM session start but does not explain further.
- **Day-of rule:** be done early (2022), then the morning the day before with beginners skipping the day (2024), then the first hour or after 4:00 PM (2026).

## Fed speakers, 10:00 releases and Jackson Hole

### What it is

Fed speakers and testimony are treated as intervention windows. ICT says the moves people attribute to the Fed chair's words are really intervention, and he prefers to be out of the way before the speech ([2024 #11 · 30:45](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1845)). Jackson Hole week, the Fed's late-August symposium, is the one event where he reports a distinct weekly behaviour.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is the algorithm/intervention split again. When price is not running to liquidity or repricing an inefficiency, manual intervention is in play. His examples were the previous week's FOMC and the Powell speech on the day he said it ([2024 #37 · 1:11:47](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4307)). Before a speech the market is fickle and better left to novices, and he calls trading near speeches the worst time to be in the marketplace ([2024 #37 · 3:43](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=223)).

### How to trade it

**Fed chair speeches (2024 #37):**

1. Do nothing while the chair talks.
2. Do nothing for 15 minutes after he stops.
3. Then check whether PD arrays are respected to the tick: price reacts off them, or trades into them and leaves at once. That means the algorithm is back. If not yet, don't trade.
4. Timing: if testimony falls in the 9:00 to 10:00 hour, trade before 8:00 AM. If the speech is in the afternoon, trade only the morning and the first hour.
5. Do not carve exceptions. Rules say when you can, not when you might ([2024 #37 · 1:15:49](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4549)).

On the day of that lecture ICT planned no afternoon trading because of Powell and expected intervention to be guaranteed ([2024 #37 · 1:18:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4691)).

**10:00 releases (2026):** on a day with a 10:00 driver such as ISM or JOLTS, wait through the first 30 minutes (9:30 to 10:00) and let liquidity or an inefficiency set up before the news. Let the news hit, then work with whatever is left in its wake ([2026 #19 · 8:48](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=528)). In another 2026 session he would not short before the 10:00 news but would at 10:01, wanting the news to send price down to the sell side below the day's low. Price took the low but did not touch the first significant FVG ([2026 #69 · 2:23](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=143), [2026 #69 · 5:04](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=304)).

### Worked example

ES, 1-hour chart, 23 June 2022, Fed chair Powell speaking at 10:00. SSL had been taken and price rallied to buy-side liquidity. ICT's target was 3805, above an old high on the left; if it hit, he would watch for a short there. Instead price went just above a short-term high, "gave up the ghost" and went lower. His long was stopped out at +2 points, which he described as just covering expenses ([2022 Ep 41 · 1:41](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=101)).

His response is the lesson. When a high-impact event does not pan out as expected, he says it is better to step away, close the computer and come back later. He then looked at the lunch hour and the 1:30 window. The NY index AM session is 8:30 to 11:00; he could trade after 11, but he sticks to the rules to show that following them means missing some opportunities, and that is fine ([2022 Ep 41 · 3:16](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=196), [2022 Ep 41 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=290)).

### Jackson Hole week

In August 2024 (the 19th to the 23rd) ICT said Powell was expected to speak several times, with Wednesday to Friday volatile. Price delivered with a lot of overlapping back-and-forth, re-delivering over ranges it normally would not retrace so often. He called it a seek-and-destroy climate: if you roll your stop to cost or breakeven, it generally comes back and hits it. He had noticed this only for Jackson Hole, not for any other economic event, and not every single week ([2024 #11 · 30:45](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1845), [2024 #11 · 39:50](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2390)).

His handling rules:

- Decide where your first partials sit before you enter, and keep that threshold low and easy. Do not try to outsmart the market with a greedy hold ([2024 #11 · 34:43](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2083)).
- If long and price trades into a premium inefficiency or clears a short-term high, take something off.
- Do not over-trade, and expect one-directional runs to frustrate ([2024 #11 · 41:26](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2486)).
- Check it yourself on the weekend on the 1-minute charts.

In 2026 the guidance hardens: extreme volatility, frequent and violent second runs, and no real-money trading for new traders (demo or tape reading only). Experienced traders reduce size and expect whipsaws. Several livestreamers were hurt that week, and ICT said his own Friday execution could have been better because he could have closed earlier ([2026 #20 · 11:04](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=664), [2026 #20 · 19:50](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=1190)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Waiting 15 minutes and then entering without the PD-array check.** The check, not the clock, is the green light.
- **Assuming Jackson Hole is just another Fed week.** ICT singles it out as the only event with that overlapping delivery.

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** the day before a Fed chair speech is quiet; on the day, don't try to trade it. A failed 10:00 expectation means walk away.
- **2024:** the explicit speech rule (stop, 15 minutes, PD-array check) and the Jackson Hole overlapping-delivery observation, framed as something to verify yourself.
- **2026:** Jackson Hole becomes a week where new traders stay out with real money, and 10:00 releases get a 30-minute setup-first protocol.

- [2024 #37 · 1:15:49](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=4549): wait 15 minutes and check PD arrays.
- [2024 #11 · 30:45](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=1845): overlapping delivery in Jackson Hole week.

## NFP week and the weekly rhythm

### What it is

ICT treats the whole week of a major report as a different environment, and says the difficulty starts days before the release ([2024 #40 · 2:36](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=156)). His rules are written for new students. He is not saying the days cannot be traded; they drag on a newer student's accuracy and psychology, and he admits he has blown accounts on NFP and had his head ripped off by PPI and CPI ([2024 #40 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=977)).

### How to trade it

For a week with NFP on Friday at 8:30, the 2024 protocol is:

1. Monday is easy. Trade it, and possibly Tuesday.
2. Tuesday is decent until the London session of Wednesday, the last bit of good price action.
3. From the Wednesday New York session onward, Wednesday to Friday are messy and range-bound.
4. New students should not trade Wednesday, Thursday or Friday. The pre-market before 9:00 ET on Wednesday is acceptable. After 9:00, if you have not already had a trade, do nothing ([2024 #40 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=977)).

[[DIAGRAM ch14_d4 | caption: NFP week for a newer trader, Monday to Friday, with the Wednesday cut-off as each lecture gives it | Notice that Monday and Tuesday are the clean days and that the sources disagree on exactly when Wednesday ends.]]

The logic carries to other days. If the report is on Thursday or Friday, trade Monday and Tuesday. If it is on Monday or Tuesday, do nothing at the start of the week and trade the easier "salad days" later ([2024 #40 · 33:37](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=2017)). With no clear weekly or daily draw, do very little. Once the weekly draw is satisfied (that week the weekly BISI target delivered on Monday), it has done enough and he does not need another trade ([2024 #40 · 1:55:39](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6939)). He values knowing when not to trade above a tight stop or a good entry.

The CPI and PPI weeks look similar. In September 2024 Monday and Tuesday were empty and building a big premium to run against ahead of PPI and CPI ([2024 #25 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/Onca4KgfQB4?t=682)).

### If you do trade it

Experienced traders can trade NFP days but should expect whipsaws and stop-runs. ICT's 2026 guidance is to cut size by 50 to 80% and take early partials rather than hold for the full move ([2026 #16 · 3:40](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=220)); another lecture says micro contracts and tight stops, or skip the days ([2026 #39 · 0:00](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=0)). Sizing is Chapter 20. In the NFP Thursday session he said he would not normally execute on a day he had said he would not, but a student's question prompted a demonstration with 3 contracts and early partials. In the August 2026 case study the demonstration trade was on a demo account ([2026 #39 · 7:09](https://youtu.be/hIusATccCcM?t=429)).

### How the teaching changed

The Wednesday cut-off moves from source to source.

| Source | Wednesday limit | Link |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 #40 (Oct 2024) | Pre-market before 9:00 ET is acceptable; after 9:00 do nothing if you have no trade yet. An early caption has him saying the difficulty starts "after 11 o'clock"; it is unclear and he later says 9:00. | [2024 #40 · 16:17](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=977) |
| 2024 #50 (Dec 2024) | Be done by the New York session open on Wednesday. Aimed at new traders. | [2024 #50 · 8:10](https://youtu.be/1m5yz6Vh_Dw?t=490) |
| 2026 #40 | For brand-new traders the cleanest days are Monday to Wednesday up to about 11:00 ET. | [2026 #40 · 2:29](https://youtu.be/tByaPnk3fc4?t=149) |
| 2026 #16 | Heavy manipulation on Wednesday after 11:00 AM ET and on Thursday and Friday after the release. | [2026 #16 · 3:40](https://youtu.be/xpH0wDANtmM?t=220) |

So 2024 draws the line around the Wednesday open, and 2026 pushes it to about 11:00. The safe reading for a newer trader is the stricter one.

2026 adds a calendar-year idea: ICT generally waits until the second week of February before putting positions on. The notes read that as a case for reducing or pausing in early January, when NFP and political news make things "more complex than it needs to be" ([2026 #139 · 6:14](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=374)). A related note: a brand-new trader not experienced in range trading should take two or three months off and return in spring ([2026 #133 · 4:04](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=244)).

## Managing a news-day trade

### What it is

Most of ICT's news-day rules are about when to stay out. When he is in, he knows profit can vanish quickly and a stop can be jumped, and he manages with that in mind.

### How to trade it

1. **Bank partials, and park an extreme target.** On news-driven trades he places a final take-profit far beyond what he expects, as a resting limit order, in case the move runs. NFP can take unrealized profit away quickly, so he banks partials on the way: "don't fall in love with it" if price reaches a level and you have taken nothing off. His sequence: enter on the failure or CE rejection, take partials at FVGs and rejection blocks, trail the remainder, and leave a limit order at the extreme. Only some of the position is wagered on the deep level ([2026 #2 · 17:52](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1072), [2026 #2 · 33:07](https://youtu.be/59Elm8myGEc?t=1987)). Partials in general are Chapter 19.
2. **Keep the first partial easy.** In Jackson Hole week he fixed the first line of partials before entering and kept it low-threshold ([2024 #11 · 34:43](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=2083)).
3. **Be done early if the day turns.** Stopped at breakeven on an event day (likely FOMC; the caption is unclear), he moved to the sidelines for the day ([2022 Ep 15 · 6:45](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=405)). After a failed news-driven expectation he walks away and returns later ([2022 Ep 41 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=290)).
4. **Flip when price shows unwillingness.** If a short stops working (price refuses to go lower and shows bullish candles in the lower half of a bearish FVG), close it early, reassess, and flip long if bullish signals emerge, using the same daily levels as targets ([2026 #33 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=290)).
5. **Stay with one model.** A model that does not fire is not a worse model; wanting it in every day is the problem. The "right-now model", whatever you grab to justify getting in, is the one that blows accounts, so do not reach for order blocks or OTE because your model has no setup ([2024 #17 · 2:23:58](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=8638), [2024 #17 · 1:26:19](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=5179)). Models are Chapter 17.

### Worked example

NQ, PPI, 13 August 2026. CPI had printed the day before. Before the 8:30 PPI, NQ was consolidating and failing to go lower, and ICT's initial bias was short. PPI came in bullish and price ran higher into targets he had marked earlier: Wednesday's high, a level the notes label REH, and old highs ([2026 #33 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=243)).

He closed the short, went long into the same FVG from the bottom side, took a partial at Wednesday's high, took another on the relative equal high breakout, and trailed the runner to a final stop at 30,120 ([2026 #33 · 5:37](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=337), [2026 #33 · 8:03](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=483)).

His commentary matters more than the trade. The flip came from live order-flow reading, not emotion, and once you see the unwillingness to continue your original direction you act quickly. The levels and order flow were already set; the event was the catalyst. The notes do not say whether he was still short when the number hit, so read this as a demonstration of the flip, not a template for holding through a release.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Falling in love with a level.** Reaching a target with no partial taken is the failure mode he names.
- **Trailing to breakeven in a seek-and-destroy week.** It tends to get hit (Jackson Hole).

## Event-driven and geopolitical markets

### What it is

An event-driven market is one where major news (war, leadership changes, policy shifts) temporarily overrides technical analysis. ICT says algorithms are effectively paused, manual intervention increases, and price becomes unpredictable. Normal tape-reading rules do not apply, and the best traders do not fight such a market; they sit out ([2026 #109 · 2:19](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=139)).

His April 2026 example: threats of an attack on Iran, then a sudden ceasefire announcement, and crude oil gapped down more than $18 within minutes. He called it manual intervention, not supply and demand. Smart money and governments place large bets ahead of announcements and use the announcements to trigger their moves, so a move right after a headline is someone being ready to execute, a pre-positioned short in this case ([2026 #109 · 8:43](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=523)). The notes read this as an allegation of insider trading or at least pre-positioning, which an individual trader cannot act on.

### Why it matters to a gold trader

ICT told 2022 viewers that gold usually needs a geopolitical or "something crazy" event to move. Otherwise he found it highly manipulated, with stop hunts and consolidations, and not ideal for aggressive short-term trading by newer traders, though some people profit in it ([2022 Ep 18 · 51:39](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=3099)). In 2026 he said big money was migrating out of stocks into event-driven markets such as gold, silver and crude, which is why indices were lethargic ([2026 #116 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=527)). Read together, the remarks suggest a gold trader meets event risk more often than an index trader, though ICT does not say that outright.

### How to spot it

1. Geopolitical headlines, conflicts, major policy announcements, leadership changes, or a pending Fed chair appointment.
2. Chop and reversals with 400-handle candles and wider spreads ([2026 #109 · 2:19](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=139)).
3. A headline followed by a move of 500 or more handles in minutes ([2026 #109 · 8:43](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=523)).
4. Off-hours moves (Asian session, pre-market) out of proportion to the news ([2026 #109 · 20:47](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1247)).
5. Price moving on headlines, not structure, with no clear bias.

### How to trade it

- **Cut size by 50% or more, or step away.** Accept being wrong more often, do not demand normal profitability, be grateful to break even, and consider tape reading instead ([2026 #109 · 10:20](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=620)).
- **Use wide stops and never hold without one.** If a manual move goes against an oversized position, you are wiped out before you can react.
- **Do not hold overnight into potential event risk.** The no-news-morning rule was the same ([2024 #5 · 1:34:50](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5690)), and in March 2026 he applied it to geopolitical events ([2026 #115 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=394)).
- **Use fundamentals as a risk filter, not a signal.** They can override technicals and you cannot anticipate that; you can only avoid it. If a major event such as a new Fed chair announcement is imminent, sit out ([2026 #115 · 24:43](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=1483)).
- **Expect long consolidations in a crisis.** Market makers are not incentivised to take large positions, so expect fewer explosive moves and wider spreads; cut leverage and skip lower-conviction setups ([2026 #116 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=527)).
- **Scale to the environment.** In February 2026, with markets stuck in large ranges, ICT told brand-new traders to take two or three months off, and experienced traders to use micros, one to three contracts instead of 30, and only high-confidence setups. High volatility questions everyone's methodology; it is the environment failing, not the trader ([2026 #133 · 12:55](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=775), [2026 #133 · 4:04](https://youtu.be/486N8ow3U2A?t=244)).
- **Surgical or nothing.** In April 2026 he recommended dialing back leverage and trading only surgical, one-trade-per-week-quality setups, and said he was taking demo trades rather than using live capital ([2026 #111 · 4:43](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=283), [2026 #111 · 12:44](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=764)).

### Between sessions: "tomfoolery"

In March 2026 ICT described central banks, agencies and market makers making false moves in the pre-market (around 7:00 AM ET), after hours and between sessions, when you cannot participate. In his telling the aim is to trap retail, prepare positioning for events, or move price on non-news days. His rule: avoid trading the pre-market and after-hours in that environment, do not hold into potential geopolitical events, and assume moves between sessions are designed to trap you ([2026 #115 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=394)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Using fundamentals as a trading signal.** The geopolitical story explains why consolidation persists; it does not predict direction ([2026 #116 · 9:35](https://youtu.be/YrBrgUON9fE?t=575)).
- **Mixing up "Phil", "tomfoolery" and the release rules.** Phil is a one-to-three-candle spike, tomfoolery is a between-session trap, and the release rules cover scheduled times. All three sit under manipulation or intervention in his vocabulary.
- **Hearing fear instead of risk management.** ICT is explicit that he is not spreading fear; he wants to be wrong about the worst predictions ([2026 #111 · 53:14](https://youtu.be/5ycp9oGKPms?t=3194)).

### How the teaching changed

- **2022:** gold needs a geopolitical event to move, and is frustrating for aggressive short-term trading by newer traders.
- **2024:** intervention and geopolitical shocks are the one thing that always makes his model wrong, and Middle East tension and the US election can cause big runs even on a no-news morning ([2024 #1 · 2:07:06](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7626), [2024 #5 · 1:35:38](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=5738)).
- **2026:** "event-driven market" becomes a named regime with its own rules, and the vocabulary grows with "Phil" and "tomfoolery".
- **Pre-market:** in 2024 he does his preparation in the 7:00 to 9:30 ET window and trades before 8:00 AM on testimony days ([2024 #49 · 7:48](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=468), [2024 #37 · 3:43](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=223)). In the March 2026 geopolitical context he says to avoid the pre-market and after-hours. The two fit only if you read the 2026 line as specific to an active geopolitical environment.

## Answers

1. Do not position ahead of the release or be in the market going into 8:30. A tight stop does not help because the move can jump past it with slippage, and he says a stop loss will not save you. Reread: The release rule.
2. First that at least 15 minutes have passed (8:45 is the earliest, 20 to 30 minutes is better), and second that PD arrays are being respected on the 1-minute and 15-second charts (the midpoint test). Reread: The release rule; The precision test.
3. Intervention is still on, because price ignored the CE. Sit still until PD arrays stop price on the tick again. Reread: The precision test.
4. 2024: the pre-market before 9:00 ET is acceptable, but after 9:00 with no trade yet, do nothing, so at 10:15 you stand aside. The 2026 version says Monday to Wednesday up to about 11:00 ET is cleanest for brand-new traders, so 10:15 would still be inside the window. Reread: NFP week and the weekly rhythm.
5. A counter-run at 2:30, usually, over the top of the earlier move. He marks a discount FVG below 50% of the range near sell-side liquidity as the end of the first leg, and waits for price to deliver into it. Trading ahead of the announcement is a gambler's setup. Reread: FOMC and the two-stage delivery.
6. Do nothing during the speech and for 15 minutes after he stops, so not before 10:35. Then price must be respecting PD arrays to the tick; if not, don't trade. Reread: Fed speakers, 10:00 releases and Jackson Hole.

9. With several reports the move is staggered: it feels done and is not. Give it a couple of seconds for another impulse that may take both sides before reading it as finished. Reread: Why the first run is the fake one.
