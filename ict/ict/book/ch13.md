ICT treats every candle as a small story in three acts: price sits near its open, makes a false move that traps traders, then delivers the real move. He calls this Power of 3 (accumulation, manipulation, distribution, or AMD), names the false move the Judas swing, and applies the idea to the day, the week and smaller slices of time. After this chapter you can frame a day by its open, plan around a weekly profile, and apply ICT's calendar filters (NFP week, Fridays, holidays, seasonal tendencies) and his inside-day signal for compression.

## Power of 3 (AMD) on the daily candle

### What it is

Power of 3 (also written Power 3, or AMD) is ICT's template for how a candle forms. The three parts are accumulation, manipulation and distribution.

ICT's definition of a bullish day: the opening price is near the low of the day or session. Price first trades lower to make an important low, which is the manipulation. It then rallies to create the high and closes near the high ([2022 Ep 9 · 2:34](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=154)). A bearish day is the reverse: open, a rally (small, or even a large wick), a decline, and a close near the low ([2022 Ep 10 · 17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064)).

For the bearish day he names each part precisely ([2022 Ep 10 · 12:56](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=776), [16:09](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=969)):

- **Accumulation.** The open and anything above it. This is where shorts are built, and where smart money (and ICT and his students) aim to enter.
- **Manipulation.** The first rally above the open, a "sucker play" for breakout traders. It only goes up in order to go down. This is the Judas swing.
- **Distribution.** From the low of the day to the close, where the short positions are closed out.

The low often forms late. If price has been beaten down all day, ICT says it is probably near its low around 3:30 to 3:45 PM ET, though sometimes there is a sudden continuation instead ([2022 Ep 10 · 16:56](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1016)). You do not need to predict the close. The aim is to anticipate the fake move at the start ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)).

The pattern is fractal, meaning it repeats at smaller scale ([2022 Ep 21 · 24:48](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1488)). In 2024 he asked students to imagine the AM session as if it were the whole day closing at 4:00 PM. You would see the same open, fake rally, drop and close near the low, and every AM session is a search for that shape ([2024 #2 · 1:01:38](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3698)). People call this noise on a 1-minute or 5-minute chart. ICT says it is the same thing on a smaller interval ([2022 Ep 37 · 9:29](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=569)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is about who is on the other side of the trade. On a bearish day, smart money sells short into the buy stops that rest above highs. It then covers those shorts into sell stops below relative equal lows ([2022 Ep 22 · 12:51](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=771)). Stops, equal highs and equal lows are Chapter 3's subject.

Daily relative equal lows attract long-term trend-following and breakout traders, who sell at market when the level breaks. For smart money buying back shorts, they are the ideal counterparty ([2022 Ep 22 · 13:40](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=820)). That is why the covering point can be forecast: daily relative equal lows, old daily lows or Fibonacci projections, in agreement with the time of day ([2022 Ep 21 · 24:48](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1488)).

The fake move does the harmful work. It stops out traders who were right or traps them in the wrong direction, and then price goes the other way. Retail looks for support and resistance, while ICT looks for liquidity and inefficiencies ([2024 #2 · 1:02:25](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3745)).

### How to spot it

1. Put the chart on New York time. ICT says other time zones break his calibrations ([2022 Ep 5 · 8:23](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=503)).
2. Settle the bias first. Ask whether the day's range is more likely to expand above or below the open. Use daily structure, a daily fair value gap (FVG, Chapter 5), premium and discount (Chapter 4) and the draw on liquidity (Chapter 3) ([2022 Ep 10 · 17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064)).
3. Draw the opening price and extend it forward in time. ICT likes the 8:30 AM ET open ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)). In another lecture he uses the New York midnight opening price ([2022 Ep 37 · 7:54](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=474)).
4. Watch for the false move away from the bias side: below the open on a bullish day, above it on a bearish day.
5. Run his 8:30 checklist for a bullish day. Did price go below the 8:30 opening price? Did it trade into an imbalance? Did it take a short-term low? Did it hit an order block (Chapter 9)? Was it an optimal trade entry (OTE, Chapter 4)? Each yes adds confluence ([2022 Ep 9 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=394)).
6. Read ambiguous candles through the template. A daily candle with a big upper wick and a small body looks indecisive. Seen as Power of 3, it is accumulation around the 8:30 open and a rally above it (manipulation). Then come an FVG, a market structure shift and displacement (Chapter 2), and distribution down into a discount array ([2022 Ep 25 · 32:36](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1956)).

### How to trade it

- **Bullish day.** Buy below the opening price during the manipulation leg. Aim toward the day's draw, such as the high end of the daily FVG or relative equal highs. If you miss the dip, get long as close to the opening price as you can ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)).
- **Bearish day.** Smart money enters near the high above the opening price, exits at an important low, and the market closes near the low ([2022 Ep 22 · 14:26](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=866)).
- **Expect it daily.** Each day, expect a Power of 3 candle in the direction of your bias. Losses on counter days are expected, and you keep the bias until the target liquidity is taken ([2022 Ep 10 · 6:31](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=391)).
- **Entry model.** The daily template supplies the narrative. For entry criteria you can use his scalping model with daily-range logic, on the 15-minute and 5-minute charts. The aim is the lion's share of the range, not the exact high or low ([2022 Ep 38 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1041)). Entries are Chapter 18 and management is Chapter 19.
- **Holding.** To capture a bigger run, submit to the time the daily candle needs to form and close. See "The daily clock" below ([2024 #12 · 38:21](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2301)).

### Worked example: NQ, Monday 14 February 2022

ICT's example for the bullish profile. The notes give the sequence but no prices, so the diagram is illustrative.

1. Mark the 8:30 opening price and draw it forward.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Predicting the close.** ICT says the close is the advanced skill. The daily question is only which side of the open the range expands toward.
- **Expecting every day to conform.** In a sustained higher-timeframe swing there are few opposite candles, and one counter day should not knock you out of the bias ([2022 Ep 10 · 6:31](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=391), [17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064)).
- **Mistaking the manipulation for a breakout.** On a bearish day the rally above the open is the trap. Buying it makes you the breakout trader the template is built to catch.
- **Using the template as a trigger on its own.** The checklist is confluence. The order block, imbalance, low and optimal trade entry still have to be there.
- **Support and resistance thinking.** ICT contrasts it with liquidity and inefficiency ([2024 #2 · 1:02:25](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3745)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the template is taught as open-relative candle shapes, tied to the 8:30 opening price or the New York midnight open, with the mentorship's FVG and structure-shift pattern inside it. In 2024 he frames the AM session as a miniature day and stresses what the fake move does to participants ([2024 #2 · 1:02:25](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3745)). In the 2026 notes the emphasis shifts to forecasting the day's extremes and to weekly profiles, both covered below.

The notes use two opening anchors: 8:30 AM ET ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)) and the New York midnight open ([2022 Ep 37 · 7:54](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=474)). In 2024 he says to buy at or below the open on a bullish day without naming which ([2024 #12 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1723)). Chapter 12 covers the key opens.

### Does it apply to gold or CFDs?

ICT taught the daily template on ES and NQ. The notes give no rule restating it for gold. The concept needs only an opening price, and his anchors are New York clock times, so a CFD chart set to New York time lets you draw the same lines. Whether gold respects them is not tested in the notes, so check it on your own charts before leaning on it. The one rule in these notes that names gold is the reference-levels rule in the daily range section, which ICT says applies to any market.

## The Judas swing

### What it is

The Judas swing is the manipulation leg of Power of 3: the opening move that runs against where the candle is really going. On a bearish day it is the rally above the open, and ICT names it directly as the Judas swing ([2022 Ep 10 · 16:09](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=969)). On a bullish day it is the small false move lower after the open that you hunt for a long ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)).

Inside a single AM session it looks the same: a fake rally, a drop, and a close near the low ([2024 #2 · 1:01:38](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3698)). Its size varies. It can be a small rally or a large wick on the daily candle ([2022 Ep 10 · 17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064)).

### Why it works

The logic is the one above: the fake move takes stops and traps traders, and smart money uses that flow to enter at a better price. ICT adds that liquidity is scheduled to be taken at specific times of day, and the algorithm only has to run the level, not know how many contracts rest there ([2024 #40 · 1:44:18](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6258)).

### How to spot it

1. Know your bias side first. The Judas swing is the move to the other side of the open.
2. Look for it where something is waiting to be taken: buy stops above a short-term or relative equal high on a bearish day.
3. Expect it to end at a reference, not in empty space. In the NQ bullish example it went into an imbalance, a short-term low and an order block ([2022 Ep 9 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=394)). In another ICT example the drop went below the New York midnight open into the daily FVG's low ([2022 Ep 37 · 8:42](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=522)).
4. If you have projected a high, give the rally room. In ICT's July 2026 example, the minimum expectation before 9:30 was a run to the relative equal highs and perhaps a bump of that high before the fall ([2026 #42 · 25:31](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1531)).

### How to trade it

- Trade with the bias, into the swing: buy the dip below the open on a bullish day, or sell the rally above it on a bearish day, where confluence stacks up. If the swing has already run, wait for the retracement, and on a bullish day get long as close to the opening price as you can ([2022 Ep 9 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=346)).
- Take a partial early on the entry leg, as in the July 2026 example below.
- Do not buy a rally just because it is rallying. On a bearish day the rally is the trap.

### Worked example: NQ, Friday 31 July 2026

ICT's claim is that this short was called ahead of time and time-stamped on X. He says the screen shows no replay and that he does not give copy-trade-style setups ([2026 #42 · 5:33](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=333)).

1. At 8:14 AM ET he posted the idea. He had seen relative equal lows below and a BISI (a bullish fair value gap, Chapter 5), and he pre-announced a target of 28,400, the sell-side level at the equal lows.
2. He named the zone for the high: the -0.5 projection at 28,723, plus the CE of last Friday's new day opening gap. That CE sits at the same volume imbalance on the daily chart. He had told viewers the previous Sunday that price would trade up into that area and sell off.
3. Before 9:30 price drifted lower, with a minimum expectation of a run to the relative equal highs. Critics who laughed when price rallied first missed that. The rally was the Judas swing.
4. He shorted near the projection and took a partial. Price fell to the short-term low of the 7 to 9 AM range, broke below it and reached 28,400. The whole move took about 1 hour 45 minutes from the post, and nothing along the way stopped it ([2026 #42 · 25:31](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1531), [31:54](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1914), [37:32](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=2252)).

This was a Friday after a big rally, and he did short it. Chapter 11 covers the gap, and Chapter 19 covers targets and partials.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Not every first move is a Judas swing.** On a Friday in October 2024 he said the algorithm rushes at the open toward liquidity. The 9:30 rip was manual intervention, not a fake-out ([2024 #48 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1222)). In news weeks price can also run straight to buy-side stops without leaving usable entries ([2024 #40 · 1:41:55](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6115)). Chapter 14 covers manual intervention.
- **Judging it by shape alone.** A rally above the open is a Judas swing only if the bias is down and there is a reference to sell into.
- **Looking for a clock window here.** These notes give no time window for the swing. Chapter 12 holds the session and macro timing.

## The daily range: bias, clock and extremes

### Daily bias in one question

The most common question over the years is how to find daily bias. ICT calls the answer essentially all he teaches ([2022 Ep 10 · 3:18](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=198)). You need to know only whether the daily range is more likely to expand above or below the opening price ([2022 Ep 10 · 17:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1064)).

Once the bias is set, he treats opposite moves as opportunities. After a daily market structure shift and a retrace into a FVG, any rally or up-close candle becomes a potential shorting candidate ([2022 Ep 10 · 6:31](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=391)). You stay with the bias until the draw is reached, such as relative equal highs (buy-side liquidity), and only then study whether it wants to continue.

His bullish daily sequence, in order ([2022 Ep 10 · 20:57](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1257)):

1. A three-bar swing low forms.
2. The next day washes out below the open and closes on its high.
3. The following day is expected to rally into the FVG above.
4. Price retraces below the swing's equilibrium into discount.
5. A series of days follows that open near the low and close near the high.
6. Price keeps reaching for relative equal highs, and the bias stays bullish until it trades above them.

The bearish sequence is the mirror: a rally into a daily FVG, then days that open, rally and close on the low toward sell-side lows, and later a possible key reversal and chop. Chapter 15 covers how to build the bias itself.

### The daily clock

ICT gave the day a frame in his Episode 5 lesson. Set the chart to New York time and draw vertical lines at 8:30 AM, noon and 1:00 PM. That gives a morning session, a one-hour New York lunch, and an equal block of time after lunch ([2022 Ep 5 · 8:23](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=503)).

[[DIAGRAM ch13_d3 | caption: The daily frame in New York time, with the clock marks ICT names across the notes | The morning window comes first, and the late-day marks are for holding, not for new entries.]]

- **Morning.** 8:30 (when news releases come out) to noon. He prefers to be positioned before 11:00 and ride into lunch, and he generally avoids new trades after 11:00 ([2022 Ep 5 · 10:50](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=650)).
- **Lunch.** If holding through lunch toward the close, square up or take some off.
- **The lunch raid.** In 2024 he said to expect a raid against your move around 11:30, so do not trail a stop tightly. Then hold at least until 3:15, ideally through the last hour ([2024 #12 · 9:30](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=570)).
- **The late low.** A day that has been beaten down all day often prints its low around 3:30 to 3:45 PM ET ([2022 Ep 10 · 16:56](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1016)).

The 2024 example was an ES long from the opening drill, bought at or below the open on a bullish day. He held through the 11:30 raid into the afternoon run. He says he took roughly two handles of drawdown for a run of over 200 handles. He also says not to strangle a winner by moving the stop up and up ([2024 #12 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=1723)). His size advice is to leave one contract as a runner under these rules ([2024 #12 · 35:10](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2110)). He adds that holding like this is a skill built over months of drills, and you cannot force it.

### Where the extremes print

ICT gives several tools for estimating where a day's high or low sits. They overlap, and you use them together.

- **Reference levels.** Keep on the chart the previous day's high and low, the last three days' highs and lows, last week's high and low, and the upper quadrant, midpoint and lower quadrant of that range. ICT calls these the references the algorithm goes back to in any market, including forex, bonds, gold and indices, and says this is not classic support and resistance ([2024 #7 · 2:40:28](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9628)). The first daily PD array above or below (a level or zone price is drawn to, Chapters 4 to 10) is the usual first target ([2024 #7 · 2:42:53](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9773)).
- **The daily midpoint.** On a range-bound day, split the day's low and high. After a late-day excursion outside the range, price wants to gravitate back to that midpoint ([2022 Ep 32 · 17:06](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=1026)).
- **Programmed boundaries.** He compares the daily range to a game map, such as Pac-Man's maze. It has programmed edges until manual intervention removes them, and the algorithm does not need to know how much volume sits beyond them ([2022 Ep 39 · 44:11](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2651), [45:48](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=2748)).
- **Higher-timeframe magnets.** Levels on the daily chart (a mean threshold, a rejection block, buy-side liquidity, a candle's low, a volume imbalance) pull order flow. Keep them on the chart and on a notepad, and check whether price is marching to them. Intraday retracements against that bias are just retracements ([2024 #12 · 45:32](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=2732)).
- **Forecasting the extreme (2026).** ICT says the algorithm delivers daily highs and lows to hit liquidity. You can forecast them from mapped levels: RTH (regular trading hours) gaps, the previous day's high and low, event horizons, pre-session FVGs and standard deviations. His proof is a call he says he made at 11:40 AM the day before, on a livestream with a timestamp checked by someone else. He named 26,562.75 as the next daily high while price traded around 26,700 or higher, and says price later hit that level to the tick ([2026 #98 · 4:43](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=283), [24:40](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1480)). That is his claim, backed by a timestamp rather than a statistic.

The notes summarise the method. Map the candidate levels in the morning. Ask which one, aligned with the larger bias, would be the least obvious. Treat a hit as proof you are reading the algorithm correctly, and recalibrate if you miss. Do not force predictions ([2026 #98 · 9:31](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=571)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Mixing entries and holds.** The habit of avoiding new trades after 11:00 is about entries. The hold-to-3:15 advice is about a trade already working.
- **Treating the midpoint as a signal.** The 2022 rule describes where price gravitates after an excursion on a range-bound day, not a level to fade by itself.
- **Reading reference levels as support and resistance.** ICT says the algorithm returns to them, which is a different claim from "price bounces here".
- **Chasing the extreme.** The 2026 forecasts depend on a stack of mapped levels and on the larger bias. They are not a call to pick tops and bottoms.
- **Thinking the template replaces a model.** The daily-range view gives narrative, and the entry still comes from a model ([2022 Ep 38 · 17:21](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1041)).

### How the teaching changed

The 2022 lessons treat the day as a clock. ICT had said in Episode 3 that the afternoon was out of scope, then decided to teach it because of nonsense on YouTube ([2022 Ep 5 · 10:50](https://youtu.be/N29ZJ-o31xs?t=650)). The 2024 lectures add hold-by-time rules and the reference-level list ([2024 #12 · 9:30](https://youtu.be/EQfTD6ntB4A?t=570), [2024 #7 · 2:40:28](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=9628)). The 2026 lectures use the same map to forecast the extreme of the day ([2026 #98 · 4:43](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=283)).

## The weekly template: bias, profile and objective

### What it is

ICT applies the same logic one level up. Before the week opens, preferably on the weekend, decide whether the next weekly candle is more likely to expand higher or lower. You are not predicting the weekly close, only which side the range expands toward. That sets your initial bias for the week ([2022 Ep 2 · 12:22](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=742), [16:20](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=980)).

The inputs are the weekly chart, the prior weekly candle and where price is drawing, such as an old low or high not yet broken. The context is seasonality, macro news, earnings season and the tone on the daily chart ([2022 Ep 2 · 13:11](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=791)). His 2022 NQ weekly example expected lower prices on seasonal weakness, expected Fed rate hikes, earnings-season volatility and a heavy tone, with price drawing to an unbroken old low.

If you expect lower, look only for setups that sell. The move may come in the first half of the week or in a single day, and if you get your setup in that move, you are done for the week. He did not expect the whole range to be delivered in one week ([2022 Ep 2 · 15:32](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=932)).

### The weekly profile

By 2026 he drew weekly profiles: schematics of how a bullish or bearish week delivers across the days. He builds them before price prints, then maps the week's high-impact news onto the days ([2026 #32 · 25:16](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1516)). The one bullish profile he names is **consolidation midweek rally**: the week starts with consolidation, makes an important low on Wednesday, and rallies into the end of the week.

He says there are only so many profiles to choose from when you are bullish, so the job is to match the model to the economic calendar. He tells viewers to categorize each completed week, every week. He dates the profile teaching to the 2017 mentorship (hedging between month 7 and month 6) and says it is not "market profile".

[[DIAGRAM ch13_d4 | caption: The bullish "consolidation midweek rally" profile (schematic) and the news calendar of the week he mapped it on, August 2026 | The profile says where the low should form, and the calendar says which days are tradable.]]

His mapped week had CPI (consumer prices) on Wednesday at 8:30 ET, PPI (producer prices) on Thursday at 8:30 and medium-impact consumer sentiment on Friday. His read was that Monday and Tuesday have reasonable swings, the wild ride starts at 8:30 on Wednesday and runs into Thursday, and Friday is a nothing burger.

On Thursday at 8:14 AM ET he posted that it would be a large-range day after four small-range days, with a drop and then a rally taking out at least Wednesday's high. His reasons: the big news (PPI) was ending, it was near the last day, and price had not closed above the rejection block. These points come from [2026 #32 · 47:47](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=2867), [49:21](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=2961) and [53:20](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3200).

### How to spot it

1. On the weekend, ask the weekly question and note the unbroken high or low price is drawing toward.
2. Build the calendar. ICT does the weekly prep on Saturday evening or Sunday afternoon, and it takes little time. He uses the Forex Factory calendar, toggles only the currency you trade (USD for NQ and other US futures) and notes the day and time of every orange (medium) and red (high) event. Yellow and gray items are not a concern. He ignores the numbers themselves because the data is later revised. What matters is that the 8:30 report creates volatility ([2026 #19 · 0:02](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=2), [4:48](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=288)).
3. Pick the profile that fits the bias and the calendar, decide which days you will trade and which you will skip, and at the end of the week file it under the profile it followed.

### How to trade it

- **Define the week's range early.** After he published a draw (Monday London session highs) and the opposite side (relative equal lows below), taking both made "the weekly range fulfilled" for him. He then did not care what PPI or Friday's consumer sentiment did ([2026 #34 · 0:47](https://youtu.be/La2fTewydjw?t=47)). He adds that he still did a short demonstration afterwards, which is not a recommendation to keep trading.
- **Weekly objective, one big target.** His sell-side target (relative equal lows plus the weekly BISI high area) was his Terminus for the week. If a short traded into it, his weekly "one shot one kill" was satisfied and any further trading that week was scalps. His heaviest leverage is reserved for that short ([2024 #37 · 1:02:55](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3775), [1:01:22](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3682)). Chapter 17 covers one shot one kill and Chapter 20 covers size.
- **Keep the target modest.** In 2022 he framed a weekly goal as one good setup a week and about 25 handles (index points) out of ES, enough to cover bills over time ([2022 Ep 1 · 27:21](https://youtu.be/kt6V4ai60fI?t=1641)).
- **First week of the month.** He likes to have trading finished by Wednesday, and if the daily has no clear objective he sits on his hands ([2022 Ep 36 · 7:17](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=437)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Predicting the weekly close, or expecting the whole range in one week.** He does neither. The question is which side the range expands toward.
- **Forcing trades after the objective.** Once both sides are taken, the weekly range is done for you.
- **Treating the profile as a prediction.** It is a menu of shapes to match against the calendar. The notes record one profile name and the instruction to classify completed weeks.
- **Running into stops with no entry.** In news weeks price sometimes goes straight to buy-side stops with no usable PD arrays. ICT calls that characteristic of manual intervention, and the market gives nobody a chance to get on ([2024 #40 · 1:41:55](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=6115)). Do not chase it.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the weekly lesson is one question plus modest goals. In 2024 it becomes a named objective, the Terminus, with a size rule attached. He uses "one shot one kill" at weekly scale here, not in its 2022 intraday meaning ([2024 #37 · 1:02:08](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=3728)). In 2026 profiles are drawn against the economic calendar, and he calls the week fulfilled once both sides are taken.

### Does it apply to gold or CFDs?

All the weekly examples are NQ or ES, and the notes give no weekly rule for gold or for a CFD's weekend open. The calendar step is currency-specific, and the notes name only USD, for NQ and futures.

## Day-of-week tendencies and NFP week

### What it is

ICT does not treat all days alike, but he is firm that his day rules are guardrails, not bans. He denies the rumour that he never trades Mondays ([2024 #49 · 9:27](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=567), [2026 #19 · 7:12](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=432)). He calls the "don't trade Mondays and Fridays" line a running joke of naysayers and says he uses his model and a few factors rather than blanket rules ([2026 #13 · 4:05](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=245)). Experienced students can trade Mondays. The rule is aimed at newer traders ([2026 #64 · 33:04](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1984)).

NFP means non-farm payroll, the jobs report released on the first Friday of the month at 8:30 AM ET ([2026 #19 · 3:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=191)). Most of ICT's weekday rules are written around it.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation for NFP week is that the report is highly manipulated. The data is already baked into price, but it shifts sentiment, so the 8:30 release is a wild ride. He likens the volatility to a flame and new traders to moths ([2024 #41 · 1:07](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=67), [2026 #19 · 3:11](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=191)). Because the days after Wednesday morning are event-driven, the cleanest action tends to be Monday's ([2026 #64 · 33:04](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=1984)). Chapter 14 covers news days.

### How to trade it

1. Check the calendar before Monday, noting the first Friday of the month.
2. In NFP week, be in your seat on Monday expecting a setup. Trade Tuesday if Monday did not pay. Finish by Wednesday morning. In other weeks, do not demand a Monday setup.
3. On Thursday and Friday study rather than engage. Before the 8:30 NFP release, work out which side of liquidity price will reach for, and watch the 1-minute and 5-minute charts when volatility hits ([2022 Ep 37 · 3:58](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=238)).
4. Event weeks outside NFP get a milder version. For the Jackson Hole symposium he said it is not like FOMC, CPI or PPI but odd things happen, so do not over-leverage that week ([2026 #26 · 22:38](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=1358)).
5. An experienced trader with a model and sane sizing can still trade. As a mentor, ICT says it would be irresponsible to teach students to chase these moves. He can do it with 30 years of experience, and they should not copy that ([2024 #41 · 1:57](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=117)).

### Worked example: NFP Friday, 2022

On an NFP Friday in 2022 ICT's advice was to study the day, not speculate on it ([2022 Ep 37 · 1:35](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=95)). His own expectation was conditional. The short-term high might be run overnight. If not, and price at 8:30 sat above the daily FVG, he expected an attempt toward that high, though not necessarily through it ([2022 Ep 37 · 3:11](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=191)). In another 2022 lecture he expected NFP to matter less because FOMC (the Fed's rate decision) the day before had already done its work ([2022 Ep 24 · 19:06](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1146)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading the rule as "never".** He rejects it explicitly.
- **Applying NFP-week rules every week.** NFP is the first Friday of the month. In other weeks the calendar decides.
- **Treating the Wednesday cutoff as one rule.** The notes give four versions (see the table). The common core is that the first half of the week is the safe window.
- **Confusing a quiet Monday with a safe Monday.** A Monday with no news but big news on Tuesday or Wednesday is where traders lose most.

### How the teaching changed

The core is stable from 2022 to 2026: trade early in NFP week and stand aside late. What moves is the Wednesday cutoff and who the rule is for. By 2026 it is explicitly guidance for brand-new students, with the habit-forming argument that early rushing and reacting badly to losses can lead beginners to talk themselves out of trading ([2026 #19 · 6:25](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=385)).

## TGIF: the Friday filter

### What it is

ICT uses TGIF as shorthand for Friday behaviour. The notes never lay it out as an entry model, and they do not describe a Friday retracement trade. What they hold is three connected ideas about the last day of the week. One 2026 entry spells the acronym out as "Thursday Is FUN", but everything under it is about Friday, so treat that caption as unclear.

1. **Friday is the last bit of the candle.** Friday closes out the daily and weekly candle. In a bullish week the candle opens near its low and closes near its high. So Friday usually does not make a much higher high or a deep retracement, and it mostly works inside Thursday's range ([2024 #21 · 49:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2967)).
2. **Friday carries weekend gap risk.** Traders may not want to hold positions over the weekend, so the market may keep price rangebound on Friday and gap radically lower at the Sunday 6:00 PM ET open. That makes Friday high-risk for shorts ([2026 #114 · 23:02](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1382)).
3. **Friday can be rushed.** With little time left, price may rush at the open to the liquidity, because the algorithm does not want to offer stops a chance to stay ([2024 #48 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1222)).

### How to trade it

- **Have a target.** He trades such a Friday only if he can identify a daily, 4-hour or 1-hour pool of liquidity or inefficiency to aim at. Otherwise he does nothing ([2024 #21 · 48:41](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2921)).
- **Be slow to trust the short.** If in doubt on a Friday, sit out. ICT had a bearish bias and a setup in March 2026 and still declined to trust it ([2026 #114 · 20:42](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1242)).
- **Skip the gap-fill statistic.** On that October 2024 Friday he threw out his usual 70% expectation that the opening gap is half repriced in the first 30 minutes ("not today"). He expected little or no retracement into the opening range gap and waited for price to run to unfinished business instead ([2024 #48 · 29:59](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1799)). Chapter 11 covers the gap. The notes do not say which gap the 70% referred to, so treat that detail as unclear.
- **Do not push the edge.** On Fridays and days like them, he says not to dull a sharpened, tested edge by pushing ([2024 #21 · 56:46](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=3406)).
- **It is a filter, not a ban.** High-conviction setups can still work, with tighter risk, according to the notes' summary of the March 2026 lecture.

### Worked example: Friday 25 October 2024

ICT's Friday example: core durable goods at 8:30 and revised consumer sentiment at 10:00, both medium impact. Between 9:00 and 9:30 price went to just short of the new week opening gap's CE, dropped, rallied to the gap's high and dropped again to random levels and the CE. At 9:30 he saw manual intervention: a rip to the buy stops, not an algorithmic retracement. Price then hit the 20,495 target he had named the day before ([2024 #48 · 1:41:41](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=6101)). He also pointed out that split news (8:30 and 10:00) can whipsaw price, because the algorithm uses the first release as the fake run and the later one as the real run ([2024 #48 · 28:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1703)). Chapter 14 covers that.

For a Friday where he did trade, see the July 2026 short in the Judas swing section. It had a pre-announced target and a stack of confluence at the projected high.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Assuming TGIF means "short the weekly rally".** The notes say nearly the opposite: be reluctant to trust shorts on Friday.
- **Assuming TGIF means a gentle Friday.** On the Friday of Jackson Hole week in 2026 he told viewers to expect the unexpected and rejected a TGIF mindset. The notes do not spell out what the mindset was ([2026 #21 · 58:37](https://youtu.be/MD1Ts09dkYs?t=3517)).
- **Treating a long weekend as a normal weekend.** Holiday Fridays are thin, and his advice is not to trade Fridays going into holiday weekends ([2024 #21 · 28:01](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1681)).
- **Importing the Sunday-gap idea to a CFD without checking.** The 6:00 PM ET Sunday reopen is the futures clock. The notes give no gold rule, so check your broker's weekend close and open.

### How the teaching changed

The notes hold two descriptions of Friday that point different ways. In August 2024, on the Friday before Labor Day weekend, he said that in a bullish week Friday mostly works inside Thursday's range ([2024 #21 · 49:27](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=2967)). In October 2024 Friday rushes at the open to liquidity with a manual-intervention rip ([2024 #48 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=1222)). ICT does not reconcile them in these notes. Read them as two Friday shapes, both with the same lesson: do not trade Friday on the same assumptions as Tuesday. In 2026 the emphasis moves to gap risk for shorts and the label TGIF itself.

## Holidays and thin-participation days

### What it is

On days when large participants are away, ICT says the market behaves differently. In the notes this covers a holiday Friday, the first trading day after a holiday, and a Monday after a holiday that falls on a weekend. His mechanism is participation, not any pattern.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that volume is light because people are away (in summer, on vacation, hungover). There is then no need for the market maker to make the ranges big, and the market cannot engineer participation in a vacuum of interest. He says this contradicts the buying-and-selling-pressure myth, because a big range would not be justified without participation ([2026 #69 · 26:37](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1597)). It is participation, not nationality, so a US holiday matters to foreign traders too.

On a holiday Friday, deep-pocketed institutions are largely absent, so there is no clean institutional order flow. They roll stops to a manageable level and leave. Light volume means fewer participants for the algorithm to mark against, and efficient trading needs participants and clean runs ([2024 #21 · 28:01](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1681)).

### How to spot it

1. Check the US holiday calendar. Mark the Friday before a holiday weekend, and the first trading day after any holiday.
2. If the holiday falls on a weekend, mark the following Monday as a no-touch day ([2026 #68 · 4:56](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=296)).
3. Read the tape: price middling inside a 15-minute range, jagged upside moves, repeated failures to launch in both directions, and news at 10:00 that only nudges price ([2026 #69 · 21:04](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1264)).

### How to trade it

ICT gives two levels of caution, and the notes do not say what separates them.

- **First day back, size down.** After a holiday, go in light, like one contract, even on paper. It helps you see whether you are onside or offside with your expectation. Let the morning session go, get a pulse, and scale only after price confirms ([2026 #12 · 3:31](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=211), [15:30](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=930)).
- **Take the low-hanging fruit.** New traders reduce size, do not chase the best exit, and aim for the nearest objective. After Labor Day he traded one contract instead of six and exited at the first easy target, even though the bigger move lower then played out ([2026 #11 · 29:00](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1740), [17:41](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=1061)).
- **Weekend holiday, stand aside.** When a holiday falls on a weekend, he calls the following Monday a no-touch day. Participation is low, so the bid-ask is wider, runs are shorter and setups are lower probability. Tape-read, demo trade or step away ([2026 #68 · 4:56](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=296)). He says he has lost more money after holidays like this than in anything else, and not trading at all is the right answer ([2026 #69 · 5:55](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=355), [27:24](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1644)).
- **The information does not change.** The gap and FVG logic stays valid after a holiday. Only participation, and so your size, changes ([2026 #12 · 6:58](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=418)).

### Worked example: the day after the July 2026 holiday

The notes give the date as July 7, 2026, the trading day after the July 4 holiday that fell on a Saturday. ICT warned on X ahead of time not to trade it, then tested it anyway to show the odds. His precision was off and he was stopped out ([2026 #68 · 5:46](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=346)). In the companion lecture the NQ morning showed a 15-minute middle-of-range, sharp upside jaggedness and failures to launch both ways ([2026 #69 · 21:04](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1264)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating it as a hard law.** The notes frame it as a probability rule that saves capital and reduces drawdown variance, not as a claim that targets will not trade.
- **Confusing thin days with low-resistance days.** On a low-resistance liquidity-run day, price moves freely and technicals are clean. A holiday day does the opposite. Missing a move on a low-probability day is better than taking a loss ([2026 #68 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=99)).

### How the teaching changed

In August 2024 he said to be wary of the first day after a holiday while volume irons out ([2024 #21 · 28:01](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=1681)). In July 2026, for a holiday that fell on a weekend, he said not to trade live at all ([2026 #69 · 27:24](https://youtu.be/cDjxtT4-O-Q?t=1644)). After Labor Day 2026 the advice was lighter: one contract, even on paper ([2026 #12 · 3:31](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=211)). The notes draw no clean line between "light" and "none", so the safe reading is that the weekend-holiday Monday gets the stricter treatment.

## Seasonal tendencies

### What it is

ICT defines a seasonal tendency as a time of year when a market or asset class has historically swung in a certain direction, usually but not always. His analogy: in Maryland you expect heat, not snow, on July 4th. By itself a tendency means little. Combined with his model it is a road map to consistency, and he says to study it every year in old data and then forward-test it ([2022 Ep 25 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=672)).

### Why it works

For index futures ICT gives reasons of behaviour, not of supply and demand. In the autumn, holiday and end-of-year spending makes delivery clean ([2026 #19 · 4:48](https://youtu.be/BWEPtHZ1Y2Y?t=288)). In January, traders arrive from the holidays with opinions, the most energy, and impatience and greed, and market makers know big bets are coming ([2026 #136 · 1:15:49](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=4549)). In election years the market is pumped to flatter the incumbent, and shorting every wobble gets you chopped up, which is why his models require certain things to trade first before a short ([2024 #9 · 28:54](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1734)). The election-year point is opinion-level.

Crude oil differs. It has a real supply-and-demand factor: fuel suppliers must build stockpiles before winter and buy at the lower summer prices. ICT says stocks and futures have no such factor ([2026 #55 · 10:48](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=648)).

### How to use it

1. **As a filter, not a trigger.** A seasonal tendency is a weak signal alone ([2026 #115 · 15:12](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=912)). In May 2022 his bias came from three things together: a bullish dollar, the ES daily bearish order block and the May seasonal ([2022 Ep 21 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=389)).
2. **Pair it with a liquidity target and the clock.** A bearish seasonal plus a buy-side run at highs is a framework for shorts ([2022 Ep 25 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=672)). In August 2026 he said the pull toward lower prices, combined with a consolidating 7 to 9 AM window, sets up one-directional movement at 9:30 ([2026 #26 · 17:03](https://youtu.be/grcLMbjs-zs?t=1023)).
3. **Read conflicts.** If the tendency and your setup agree, conviction rises. If they conflict, sit out or demand more confluence. ICT's GBPUSD example: price falling during a seasonally bullish period would be extremely bearish for the pair and very bullish for the dollar index ([2026 #115 · 15:12](https://youtu.be/UQJW9obxt34?t=912)).
4. **Read failure.** In crude, if price should seasonally rise and does not, ICT says the weakness is a strong sign it will keep falling ([2026 #55 · 13:14](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=794)).
5. **Let events override.** Wars, sanctions and supply shocks can break a seasonal, and he cites crude going negative in 2020 as the extreme case ([2026 #55 · 12:25](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=745), [19:33](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=1173)).
6. **Build your own record.** For a market of your own, look at 5 to 10 years of charts, mark each occurrence of the start date and count how often the pattern held ([2026 #55 · 11:37](https://youtu.be/xbtW_0ipuos?t=697)).

### Does it apply to gold or CFDs?

The notes give no seasonal tendency for gold. The index tendencies apply to the markets he named (ES, NQ, Dow, Russell), and nothing says they carry over to a gold CFD. If you want a gold seasonal, ICT's own method is the 5 to 10 year count in step 6.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Treating it as a must.** He says they can twist this thing around and go against the grain ([2026 #17 · 12:33](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=753)).
- **Confusing direction with quality.** A weak September (lower prices) and clean delivery from September into year-end appear in different 2026 lectures. The notes flag a tension, and the likeliest reading is that one is about direction and the other about how cleanly price moves.
- **Using a seasonal or a presidential-year bias to pick tops.** In 2024 he says shorts can be taken intraday but are hard to hold, and that picking tops is the mistake to avoid ([2024 #46 · 7:21](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=441), [23:38](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1418)).

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 seasonality is one input to the weekly and May bias. In 2024 it appears as the election-year filter. In 2026 it widens into a calendar: September to early February for indices, January as a personal sit-out, a July to October energy cycle and currency pairs. Both the 2022 and 2026 lectures say to test the pattern yourself.

## Inside days: compression before expansion

### What it is

An inside day is a day whose high is lower than the previous day's high and whose low is higher than the previous day's low. ICT calls it a precursor to a large-range day, crediting Larry Williams and the Crabel and Raschke material, and he is clear that it does not give direction ([2024 #48 · 11:27](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=687)).

In 2026 he gave a second version. A day also counts as inside when its candle body sits inside the previous day's range, even if the wicks poke out. He says this body-inside day is in and of itself a PD array that signals compression ([2026 #129 · 5:32](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=332), [10:17](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=617)). He also describes back-to-back inside days: two days (his example is a Monday and a Tuesday) whose highs and lows stay contained within each other's band ([2026 #9 · 2:48](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=168)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is compression and release. Compression that builds over days, in smaller ranges and inside days, releases like a spring, either higher or lower, and the release gives a day trader sustained runs ([2026 #129 · 7:55](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=475)). A small-range day is also a high-resistance, fuzzy day: expect a choppy, overlapping day now and a larger one next.

### How to spot it

1. Mark yesterday's high and low.
2. Classic test: today's high is below yesterday's high and today's low is above yesterday's low.
3. His 2026 test: compare today's candle body with yesterday's high-to-low range. Wicks may exceed it.
4. For back-to-back days, mark the highs and lows of both. If all four points stay clustered inside one band, the range is contained.

### How to trade it

- **It is a context, not an entry.** Expect the next trading day, or the one after, to expand, and take direction from the draw on liquidity and your bias ([2024 #48 · 11:27](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=687)).
- **Use the edges.** The inside-day highs and lows are the breakout boundaries ([2026 #9 · 2:48](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=168)).
- **State the invalidation.** In February 2026 he held a bearish premise and expected big down-close candles and a move to the relative equal lows. He said he would reconsider if the swing high was taken out ([2026 #129 · 10:17](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=617)).
- **Do not trade the fuzzy day itself.** It is a high-resistance environment.

### Worked example: MNQ (micro NQ), Monday 23 February 2026

[[DIAGRAM ch13_d5 | caption: A body-inside day: the wick undercuts Friday's low, the body stays inside (MNQ, 23 Feb 2026; candle sizes illustrative, the two lows are real) | The classic test fails on the low, but ICT's body test passes and counts the day as compression.]]

Friday's low was 24,677.00. Monday traded to 24,667.75, which is 9.25 points lower, but Monday's high stayed below Friday's high and Monday's body stayed inside Friday's range. The low was only wick damage. Under the classic rule this is not an inside day. Under his rule it is, and he expected protraction the next day, with the relative equal lows as the draw ([2026 #129 · 7:08](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=428)).

In the 2026 FOMC example, Monday and Tuesday stayed contained until Wednesday's FOMC, when price broke down through Monday's low ([2026 #9 · 2:48](https://youtu.be/50VbES10ULs?t=168)). The 2024 example was Thursday 24 October: its high was below Wednesday's high and its low above Wednesday's low, and the intraday action was choppy and overlapping ([2024 #48 · 9:01](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=541)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading it as direction.** ICT warns that a clipped sound bite makes it seem as if he predicts direction. His February 2026 talk opened with a bearish premise.
- **Treating it as a guarantee.** Catalysts such as FOMC trigger the breakout. Without one, compression can drag on.
- **Trusting a number of days.** The 2026 notes record the timing two ways: usually three or more days of consolidation before expansion, and expansion usually within one to three days. The 2024 rule is the next trading day or the one after. Do not rely on any count.

### How the teaching changed

2024 gives the classic high-low definition and the precursor idea. 2026 adds the body-inside version, calls it a PD array, and extends the idea to back-to-back inside days with FOMC as the catalyst.
