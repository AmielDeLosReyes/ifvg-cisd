Liquidity, in ICT's use of the word, is the stop orders and breakout orders that rest above old highs and below old lows. His central claim is that price is delivered to those pools, runs them to find the other side of large orders, and then moves on to the opposite pool or to an imbalance. After this chapter you should be able to mark the pools on your chart, rank them, judge which side is more likely to be run next, and tell a raid that reverses from a run that keeps going.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch03_d1 | caption: stops rest beyond obvious highs and lows, and price raids one pool before the other (illustrative) | Notice the order: the sell-side pool is raided first, the buy-side pool second, and each raid hands smart money the orders it needs.]]

- Buy-side liquidity (BSL) is buy stops resting above a high: the protective stops of short sellers and the entries of breakout buyers. Sell-side liquidity (SSL) is sell stops below a low. ICT names a pool after the orders in it.
- Relative equal highs and lows (REH, REL) are the cleanest pools. Retail reads them as resistance and support. ICT reads them as stops waiting to be run, and from 2024 he wants the right-hand extreme to sit slightly inside the left one.
- Smooth beats jagged. Flat, untouched levels attract price. Levels that have already been run, leaving choppy swings, are finished business. His rule of thumb: where it is smooth, price is going; where it is jagged, price moves away.
- Order matters. The side taken first is usually the trap and the other side is the draw. A shallow poke that returns is a sweep (the root of turtle soup). A move over the level that does not look back is a run.
- The draw on liquidity is an expectation, not a promise. Name the nearest easy pool and a farther best case. Take partials at internal range liquidity and treat external range liquidity as the final target.
- A level is an observation point, not an entry. The entry still needs a model, a time and a reaction (Chapters 17 and 18).

## Buy-side and sell-side liquidity

### What it is
A stop order waits until price reaches a level and then becomes a market order. A short seller protects a trade with a buy stop above a recent high. A long protects with a sell stop below a low. Breakout traders enter the same way: a buy stop above a high, a sell stop below a low. ICT calls the total of these resting orders liquidity, "the lifeblood of any market" ([2026 #132 · 19:32](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1172)).

His two labels follow the order type:

- **Buy-side liquidity (BSL):** buy stops above old highs and above relative equal highs.
- **Sell-side liquidity (SSL):** sell stops below old lows and below relative equal lows.

In 2022 the rule was short: an old low means sell stops below, relative equal highs mean buy stops above ([2022 Ep 3 · 1:04](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=64)). In 2024 he names the owners. Above a high sit either breakout longs' entries or the stops that protect shorts. Below a low sit breakout shorts or the stops that protect longs ([2024 #24 · 1:29:59](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5399)).

Large traders use the same places as small ones. ICT says funds put stops where retail does and the difference is only size ([2022 Ep 25 · 15:06](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=906)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is about counterparties, not about buying or selling pressure.

1. **A run stop becomes a market order.** When price trades above a pool of buy stops, those stops turn into buy orders at market. A large seller who wants to sell high suddenly has willing buyers ([2022 Ep 2 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1321), [2022 Ep 32 · 16:20](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=980)).
2. **The cycle continues downward.** Having sold into buy stops, smart money is net short and must buy back cheaper. The sell stops below old lows are the natural place to do it ([2022 Ep 25 · 14:19](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=859)). In 2026 he restates this as finding the counterparty: the buy side is taken, they short into it, then they need a lower price, and old lows are where longs' stops become market sells ([2026 #132 · 27:45](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1665), [28:33](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1713)).
3. **Volume is not the cause.** The algorithm goes to the liquidity whether or not there is sufficient volume ([2022 Ep 25 · 49:58](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2998)).
4. **A line on a chart is not the reason.** Classical support and resistance only shows what retail will do. If an old high is "resistance", he asks, why does price keep returning to it? To go above it, where the shorts' stops are ([2024 #4 · 24:43](https://youtu.be/hdSscxZgE58?t=1483)).

He reduces every move to two reasons: price goes up to take buy-side liquidity or to fill an inefficiency above, and down to take sell stops or to fill an inefficiency below ([2024 #24 · 1:29:59](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5399)). Chapter 1 sets out that pairing; this chapter teaches the liquidity half.

### How to spot it
1. Mark the old highs and lows on the chart you trade and on the one above it.
2. Mark the pool at the extreme. If a swing point sits inside another candle's range, use the candle that holds the outermost price, because that is where the stops rest ([2022 Ep 7 · 11:24](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=684)).
3. Ask who is trapped. Shorts trail their buy stops just above the recent high. Longs trail their sell stops under the latest consolidation, gap or low ([2024 #9 · 11:22](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=682)).
4. Note which pools have already been taken. A pool that was just run is no longer the target; the opposite side usually is.
5. Rank what is left with the tools in the next sections: reference levels, equal highs and lows, smooth versus jagged, minor versus primary.

### How to trade it
Liquidity is the objective and the precondition for a setup. It is not an entry by itself.

- **As a target.** For a long, the buy stops above a high are where the willing buyers are. ICT places the exit at that pool, not at an arbitrary distance above entry ([2022 Ep 13 · 20:49](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1249)). His MNQ example put the objective at 14,110, just above the relative equal highs ([2022 Ep 13 · 12:51](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=771)).
- **As a precondition.** Before a significant move lower, expect a stop hunt on buy stops: a short-term high taken out. Before a rally, expect a short-term low and its sell stops to go first. He says this happens almost daily ([2022 Ep 2 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1321)). The run into the pool is what sets up the market structure shift of Chapter 2 ([2022 Ep 24 · 31:41](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1901)).
- **Not a sell signal.** In a bearish week, wait for the run above the old high and then look for the entry signature on a lower timeframe. Do not sell simply because highs were taken ([2022 Ep 2 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1321)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Delivery is not liquidity.** Sell-side delivery means the algorithm is delivering lower prices, a sell program. Sell-side liquidity is the resting stops below a low. In 2024 and again in 2026 he stresses that the run lower is delivery and the stops under the low are the liquidity ([2024 #8 · 42:17](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=2537), [2026 #127 · 58:44](https://youtu.be/TFyk2jbQzdc?t=3524)).

### How the teaching changed
A few 2026 note entries give the labels backwards, putting "sell-side" above highs ([2026 #93](https://youtu.be/6VaEdP4TYe8?t=48), [#106](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=487), [#114](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=432)), or describe SSL as resting sell orders above price ([2026 #53](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=280)). They conflict with his wording in the same year ([2026 #132](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=1172)) and with every 2022 and 2024 source, so this chapter keeps the meaning above. When in doubt, ask which orders he means: buy stops above highs, sell stops below lows.

### Watch it
- [2022 Ep 2 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1321): buy stops as the counterparty for smart money.
- [2024 #24 · 1:29:59](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5399): the two mechanisms, with nothing else moving price.

## Old highs, old lows and the reference list

### What it is
An old high or old low is a swing extreme that price has left behind. ICT calls old highs and lows, together with relative equal highs and lows, the easiest and most visual way to pick a target. The logic never changes, he says, because stops always rest above old highs and below old lows ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:49:37](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6577)).

He works from a short written list rather than a screen full of lines:

- The previous day's high and low.
- The highest high and lowest low of the last three trading days, plus each of those days' own high and low. Sundays do not count. The only thing taken from Sunday is the 6:00 PM ET opening, used for the new week opening gap (Chapter 11; [2024 #18 · 3:08:27](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11307), [3:10:03](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11403)).
- A later version adds the last five days and the previous week's high and low ([2024 #44 · 1:09:59](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4199)), and in 2026 the previous month's ([2026 #32 · 7:41](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=461)).
- Session ranges: Asian, London, NY AM, the NY pre-market range from 7:00 to 9:00, the lunch range from 11:30 to 1:30, and the previous day's PM session ([2024 #47 · 15:00](https://youtu.be/CQk-5lAirn0?t=900), [2024 #48 · 1:34:23](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=5663)). The window from midnight to 7:00 ET gives an overnight high and low ([2026 #141 · 17:40](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1060)).
- The midpoint of each range ([2026 #32 · 26:57](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1617)).

He marks them by hand with a horizontal ray, "high touch over high tech", and distrusts leaning on indicators for it ([2024 #18 · 3:08:27](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11307)). The higher the timeframe of the level, the more likely price is drawn to it, because a daily old high carries a lot of institutional interest between price and the level ([2026 #32 · 28:35](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1715)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is that the algorithm refers to a time-based reference: the highest high and lowest low of a daily interval. It does not know how many orders rest there and does not need to. Even after a later day has traded through the stops above or below one of these levels, it still refers back to that price ([2024 #19 · 8:21](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=501), [12:19](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=739)).

He calls the previous day's low a honey pot, available every day. If you sit down and price is in the lower half of the previous day's range, always look for a return to that low ([2024 #18 · 3:19:35](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11975)).

### How to spot it
1. Draw the list above on your chart, each as a ray to the right.
2. Note where price opened inside yesterday's range. The lower half points to the previous day's low; the upper half points to its high ([2024 #18 · 3:19:35](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=11975)).
3. On a bearish day, expect the open to rally just above the previous day's high, a Judas swing (the first move, which traps one side; Chapter 13), into a premium array (above the range's midpoint; Chapter 4), and then sell toward the previous day's low ([2022 Ep 18 · 18:20](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1100)).
4. In a bearish market he wants a retracement to stay in the lower half of the previous day's range. If it reaches the half, expect continuation only later in the day or the next day. He states only the bearish case ([2026 #73 · 1:05:27](https://youtu.be/hxdAOfcp6gE?t=3927)).

### How to trade it
- **As a target, not an entry.** The daily draw is not your intraday target, since it may not be reached in one day. Frame each day with the previous day's high as the premium level and its low as the target ([2022 Ep 18 · 17:32](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1052)).
- **As a turtle soup catalyst.** A break above a previous high while you are bearish is a place to look for a short, even on a daily chart for a position trader ([2024 #19 · 13:56](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=836)). See the turtle soup section.
- **Do not take the partial on the first pierce.** When reaching down for a previous day's low, let price make a short-term low below it, retrace and dive again. Take the partial beneath that new low ([2024 #19 · 17:59](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1079)). The simpler beginner stage: measure the day's high down to the low and project one quarter of that range below it. Getting out near there is a reasonable first partial ([2024 #19 · 26:09](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1569), [31:03](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=1863)).
- **A broken old high can become support.** Once price trades above an old high, that high becomes a discount array, though not every time, and the narrative decides whether it holds ([2022 Ep 16 · 35:09](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2109)).
- **The afternoon routine.** Around 1:15 to 1:30 PM ET, mark the day's high and low on the 15-minute chart, then the next lower high and next higher low on the 5-minute. That gives two layers of buy side and two of sell side. Write down, with the time, which side you expect before 1:30. Chances are price takes one, not both ([2024 #27 · 1:21:22](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=4882), [1:27:06](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=5226)).

### Worked example
EURUSD, hourly and 15-minute, April 7, 2022 ([2022 Ep 18](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1484)). He taught it on forex; the same previous-day logic appears on ES and NQ in 2024.

[[DIAGRAM ch03_d2 | caption: the shape of the April 7, 2022 EURUSD day against the previous day's high and low (schematic, no prices) | Notice that the previous day's low was the target, and the rally into premium at 9:30 came before the drop.]]

1. He marked the previous day's high (April 6) as the premium level, which coincided with a daily FVG, and the April 6 low as the target. An hourly relative equal high sat at the FVG ([2022 Ep 18 · 24:44](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1484)).
2. At about 5:25 a.m. New York, London swept the April 6 low. He ignored it because it did not give the rally into his level ([2022 Ep 18 · 45:21](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2721)).
3. At 9:30 a.m. price rallied above the relative equal highs into the premium level, then displaced down (an energetic move; Chapter 2) through a swing low and left an FVG ([2022 Ep 18 · 30:20](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1820)).
4. His limit sell filled and price reached the April 6 low at 2:45 in the afternoon, about 40 pips. Price went a little lower afterwards, which he dismissed as irrelevant ([2022 Ep 18 · 49:16](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2956)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Reading the level as a bounce line.** He is not a support and resistance trader. Price returning to an old high does not mean it bounces from the line ([2024 #44 · 1:11:35](https://youtu.be/HMn46vLjKvY?t=4295)).
- **Calling it a pool when price stopped short.** In an index example the old daily low was 3855 and the day's low was 3856, one handle (index point) short. After a down-close outside day (a day that trades above the previous high and below the previous low), he expected that safe-feeling double bottom to produce chop ([2022 Ep 32 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=100), [3:17](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=197)).

### Watch it

- [2026 #32 · 26:57](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1617): the full list of pools and the static versus dynamic split.

## Relative equal highs and lows

### What it is
Relative equal highs (REH) are two or more highs at about the same price. Relative equal lows (REL) are the mirror. Retail sees a double top as resistance and a double bottom as support. ICT sees stops sitting just beyond them, and says most of the time the market runs those levels before a meaningful move, though they may hold briefly first ([2022 Ep 2 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1419)).

From 2024 he adds a validity rule. A high is a good relative equal high when the high to its right is slightly lower: the market failed to get back to the first one, and the algorithm will reprice to it to disrupt the pool ([2024 #2 · 1:12:51](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=4371)). The lows mirror it: the right-hand low is slightly higher ([2024 #3 · 1:28:40](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=5320), [2026 #141 · 23:59](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1439)). Three refinements follow from his examples:

- If a higher high sits to the right but only wicks above while bodies stay below, the earlier high is still valid and price will go into the wick. You then need to see price trade above and close with speed and magnitude ([2024 #11 · 2:56:01](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10561)).
- If the right-hand low is lower than the left, it is not relative equal. A raid is less likely at once. Price may first retrace to an inefficiency above or to a short-term high ([2026 #141 · 24:46](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1486)).
- The reference is the extreme of the cluster. With 12,547 and 12,553.25 as equal highs, he used 12,553.25 ([2022 Ep 27 · 0:17](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=17)). For equal lows the sweep goes to the lowest of the cluster ([2024 #11 · 2:56:01](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=10561)).

He gives no fixed tolerance. His NQ example from March 17, 2026 was lows at 24,962.00 and 24,964.25, within a handle or two ([2026 #118 · 12:54](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=774)). A single high or low counts too when there is no pair; equal ones are just what new students spot easily ([2024 #48 · 1:40:54](https://youtu.be/svYZKOrWPRo?t=6054)).

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is the retail script. Two level highs look like a ceiling. Traders short there with stops above, and the sideways drift looks like a bear flag with a measured move lower. When the picture is "too perfect, too pristine, too classic", he expects it to be run ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:27:20](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=5240), [1:48:49](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6529)).

In 2026 he explains the lower right-hand high. New shorts enter at the failed second high and others tighten their stops down to it. More buy stops stack up just under the first high, so the pool is larger and the draw stronger. He says that is how he knows where the bigger pools sit without a depth-of-market tool ([2026 #138 · 1:05:41](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3941)).

### Where and when to look
ICT ties the search to a clock, then looks left.

- **2022, the 8:30 crosshairs.** Draw a vertical line at 8:30 New York time. Mark the first low and the first high to its left, then look further left for relative equal highs and lows. Time first, then price ([2022 Ep 7 · 11:24](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=684), [28:20](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1700)).
- **2024, three morning windows.** Sit down at 7:00-7:30, 8:00-8:30 or 9:00-9:30 ET. Scan the 15-minute, then 5-minute, then 1-minute chart for pairs that formed after 7:00. At 8:00 you may look back to 7:00 but never earlier. At 9:00 anything back to 7:00 is fair. The morning session runs 7:00 to 11:00 and he likes to be done by 10:15-10:30 ([2024 #1 · 1:43:12](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6192), [2024 #2 · 1:38:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5919)).
- **Never lead with a pool that has not formed.** Do not predict the setup; wait for it. Hold a mental note of a potential pair only. ICT can anticipate such things; beginners cannot ([2024 #2 · 55:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3313)).
- **2026, the overnight range.** Mark midnight to 7:00 ET. If its lowest low is a relative equal low, he treats it as a loaded setup for the day. It does not always trade that day ([2026 #141 · 17:40](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1060)).
- **Chart clock.** The chart must show New York time ([2024 #6 · 9:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=595)). The 7:00 windows were taught on NQ; for gold the notes give no separate rule.

### How to spot it
1. Pick your window. Scan the 15-minute, 5-minute and 1-minute charts for a pair formed since 7:00.
2. Apply the right-hand rule. For highs, the right one is slightly lower.
3. Check smoothness: has price already run through the cluster and left it jagged? (Next section.)
4. If a pair sits on both sides, mark both and wait for the first to be disrupted. It usually then attacks the other ([2024 #2 · 1:38:39](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5919)).

### How to trade it
- **Bias and target.** Whichever side has the pair is your short-term bias, because price draws to it. Once set, stick with it during observation even if it turns against you ([2024 #2 · 1:56:38](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6998)). Each window that passes without the run makes the run more likely, not less ([2024 #1 · 2:05:29](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7529)). Use PD arrays as stepping stones to it.
- **Through, not back.** If price sits just under relative equal highs after sell-side lows were already taken, the longs' stops have been trailed and enough damage is done. The move through should accelerate. A shallow peek above and back inside is a false breakout and points to an intraday reversal or a deeper retracement ([2026 #101 · 1:25:37](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5137), [1:26:24](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5184)).
- **Do not short them blindly.** Many students short into relative equal highs and get run. He wants evidence the buy side is being guarded: bodies failing, an inversion FVG, a reaction in the 9:50-10:10 macro (a 20-minute window each hour; Chapter 12). If unsure, wait one more candle ([2026 #32 · 29:23](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=1763), [1:04:37](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=3877)).
- **Exit before the exact level.** On a short, price took a 10:29 low by one tick (29,595.25 against 29,595.50), so an order at the low never filled. He now exits a tick or two earlier ([2026 #86 · 20:53](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1253), [21:40](https://youtu.be/wprXVW1pW9Y?t=1300)). Beginners are told to exit one tick below the sell-side pool ([2026 #132 · 38:10](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2290)).

### Worked example
NQ, January 11-12, 2026 ([2026 #138 · 25:42](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=1542)).

1. Smooth highs formed on Sunday around 10:00 PM ET.
2. Price then only fell overnight. A later, lower high failed to reach the first. That failed second high is the retail "strong resistance" trap ([2026 #138 · 57:34](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3454)).
3. At 7:00 AM price displaced up and ran to the pair.

He does not trade at the pair when it forms. He looks back across the session for it afterwards, because that is the trap.

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Believing every sweep reverses.** He pushes back on teachers who say price always hits relative equal lows and turns. His 2022 case: with the daily relative equal lows below, price that went under them did not go down only to reverse up. It kept drawing into the daily FVG below ([2022 Ep 39 · 2:35](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=155), [5:45](https://youtu.be/yFpHbBnsK_c?t=345)).
- **Marking the 7:00 candle's high at once.** He waits for the initial run to take it, then a short-term high to form and price to trade lower and take the low. Only then does he mark both ([2024 #6 · 9:55](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=595), [35:27](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=2127)).

### How the teaching changed
- **2024:** the right-hand rule, the 7:00 windows and a filter on quality. The 7:00 start is worded two ways in Lecture #1: look for pairs formed "prior to" 7:00 (1:43:12), then start looking at 7:00 "not before" (2:05:29). Later lectures use 7:00 as the start and treat pre-7:00 London levels as weaker ([2024 #11 · 1:46:27](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=6387)).
- **2026:** he says he has taught the right-hand rule for years ([2026 #141 · 23:59](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1439)). Some 2026 note entries phrase the pair as resistance that price must bust through, or as a breakout level ([2026 #33](https://youtu.be/RVHX56v-gUo?t=582), [#109](https://youtu.be/zpLCZ1Y46Yk?t=1870)). Read those as the run-versus-sweep test in the next sections, not as a new rule.

## Smooth versus jagged price

### What it is
Smooth price has two swing points close together that make a flat, obvious level nobody has touched. Jagged price has choppy swings where the stops have already been run, so the work has been done. ICT's pictures are still water, which invites rocks, and a shark with no teeth. Jagged price is where the shark attack already happened. The wounds show, and the feeding frenzy is over ([2024 #1 · 1:09:15](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4155)).

His closing rule: "Where it's smooth it's going; where it's jagged it's going to move away from" ([2024 #1 · 2:14:17](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=8057)).

A smooth level that price trades through and leaves jagged was an engagement for liquidity. Expect the opposite direction next, especially when a breaker (Chapter 9) or an inversion FVG supports it ([2024 #2 · 58:25](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3505)).

### Why it works
The smooth side holds unspent orders. The jagged side holds orders that were already taken. In his words, retail sees relative equal lows as support and expects buyers there, while the sell stops just under them make them a draw ([2024 #36 · 17:41](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=1061)).

### How to spot it
1. Compare both sides of the current range. Flat, clean highs or lows are smooth. Choppy swings with stop runs are jagged.
2. Zoom out or compress the chart. Jaggedness that is invisible up close becomes obvious.
3. Count the lows in a leg down. If each is lower than the last and none matches another, that side is jagged and offers nothing smooth to draw to ([2026 #136 · 26:52](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=1612)).
4. Check that the answer agrees across the 15-minute, 5-minute and 1-minute charts.

### How to trade it
- **Go toward the smooth side.** Use breakers, FVGs and inversion FVGs as stepping stones to it ([2024 #1 · 1:53:31](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6811)).
- **Do not trade into a finished frenzy.** On August 6, 2024 the relative equal highs formed after 7:00 were run, so he did not trade up into that area and looked lower. Later the lows were made jagged, and he looked for the buy side ([2024 #2 · 1:00:50](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3650), [2:02:12](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7332)).
- **When one side is already disturbed, lean to the other.** Before the September 6, 2024 payrolls release, one low had been undercut a little below its neighbour and price had spent time below a wick. He read that sell side as acted on and the blocky equal highs above as the likelier draw. Price ran up at the release. He calls it a lean, not a certainty: it could as easily have wiped the other pool and then gone up ([2024 #24 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=923), [40:11](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2411)).

### Worked example
NQ, Monday August 5, 2024, 5-minute chart ([2024 #1 · 1:47:59](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6479)).

[[DIAGRAM ch03_d3 | caption: NQ August 5, 2024, shape of the morning: smooth equal highs, jagged lows (schematic candles; the 17,820.75 level is from the notes) | Notice that the lows were already worked and jagged, so price went to the smooth highs.]]

1. After a multi-thousand-handle selloff there was a large new week opening gap overhead, and price had dropped early, around 7:00.
2. On the 5-minute chart each high was slightly above the next one to its right. The first line he drew was at 17,820.75. The lows below were jagged, so he judged they had already been raided.
3. Price formed a bullish breaker and made a 9:30 Judas-swing drop into it. It then ran up through intermediate FVGs and took the 17,820.75 highs.
4. For the entry area he drew the Fibonacci from just above those highs to the relative equal lows, not to the lowest low. An FVG sat near the midpoint. He called out that FVG live, and price took off for the highs ([2024 #1 · 2:00:43](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7243), [2:01:30](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=7290)).

He used only the 15-, 5- and 1-minute charts and the time. A higher-timeframe draw was not necessary that day.

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Treating smooth as a guarantee.** In 2024 #36 he uses the words loosely and gives no formal definition ([2024 #36 · 50:30](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=3030)). It is a way to rank two pools, nothing more.

## Sweeps, runs and the order of the raids

### What it is
ICT picks two words on purpose ([2022 Ep 23 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=290)).

- **Sweep:** price goes just a little above a high, or below a low, and reverses back into the range. It is a shallow run.
- **Run:** price goes right over the top of an old high or relative equal highs and carries on without looking back. That is expansion.

His example: before FOMC price made a shallow move above Tuesday's high and came down, a sweep. After the drop it ran Tuesday's high far more meaningfully, straight through and higher ([2022 Ep 23 · 4:03](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=243), [5:38](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=338)). "Stop hunt", "stop run" and "raid" name the act. Sweep and run name what price does afterwards.

[[DIAGRAM ch03_d4 | caption: a sweep pokes the pool and returns; a run goes over it and expands (illustrative) | Notice that the first candles are identical; the next few candles decide which one you are looking at.]]

### Why it works
A raid is useful to smart money for three reasons.

1. **Counterparty.** The stops become market orders (see the first section).
2. **It traps people.** A run above highs squeezes shorts and induces breakout longs, whose stops build sell-side liquidity below for later ([2022 Ep 32 · 7:26](https://youtu.be/hzoMJQlEv5M?t=446)). A drop below lows first stops out profitable longs, traps breakout shorts and gives smart money cheap fills ([2024 #40 · 29:20](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1760)).
3. **Time at the pool.** When a large unfilled range sits above, price often drops below relative equal lows first. Smart money accumulates the sell orders resting there as its buy orders. The longer price spends below the pool, the more protracted the later run ([2024 #18 · 46:43](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2803)). Smart money buys sell-side delivery at discounted prices ([2024 #18 · 1:02:50](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3770)).

### How to spot it
1. Mark the nearest clean pools on both sides.
2. Watch which side price takes first. The first side taken is the trap and the second is the draw ([2024 #11 · 2:08:13](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=7693)).
3. Read the order. Running the relative equal highs at 9:30 without first taking the previous low is bearish. Had price first taken the previous low and then rallied, he said it would not be bearish ([2022 Ep 25 · 27:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1621), [28:38](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1718)).
4. Require displacement after the run. Find the nearest short-term low formed during the run-up. Price must break it with an energetic move, not just drift back under the highs. Without that there is no setup ([2022 Ep 24 · 33:16](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1996), [34:03](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2043)). Chapters 2 and 17 build the rest.
5. Judge the size. Runs of 30 to 50-plus handles, not 10 to 20, usually follow a pool being taken first ([2024 #40 · 19:29](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1169)).

### How to trade it
- **Do not short the initial poke.** ICT expects one more push above the high that is big enough to engage stops and trap: not a tick or two but 10, 15, maybe 20 handles on NQ, which then peters out. The range is his rough preference, not a rule ([2024 #9 · 58:23](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3503), [1:01:43](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3703)).
- **Want speed through the level.** A fast, aggressive taking of the day's low, what he calls maximum carnage, is different from a drift through a well-traded area ([2024 #10 · 1:52:25](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=6745)).
- **A sell-side clearing with no model entry is a tell.** Price sank through relative equal lows around the open, bounced, took another low, and offered no short setup. He read that as a rush to clear sell-side in a bullish context and looked for the long afterwards ([2022 Ep 36 · 5:42](https://youtu.be/pWnMpdN_g98?t=342)).
- **Watch for the snare.** A grind of small pushes through highs, three drives into liquidity, that fails to run away is more likely a trap for breakout buyers. Expect a retracement to engage the sell side below ([2024 #9 · 16:14](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=974), [22:00](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=1320)).

### Worked example
NQ, a Friday at the end of a bearish week ([2022 Ep 2 · 20:19](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1219)). The notes give the sequence but no prices.

1. The week was bearish, with a sell-off on Thursday and an overnight consolidation.
2. On Friday price first traded below the consolidation low. Those sell stops were taken, which induced breakout shorts, a *sucker play*.
3. Price then ran up through the relative equal highs and old highs, taking the buy stops where those shorts had trailed their protection ([2022 Ep 2 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1419)).
4. That run was the setup context. The entry still needed the signature on a lower timeframe ([2022 Ep 2 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1321)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Calling the first poke a reversal.** Until price falls back inside and breaks structure, you do not know if it is a sweep or a run. In 2026 he wants a move through relative equal highs to expand aggressively and not stall ([2026 #101 · 1:25:37](https://youtu.be/KASpfAd1MnI?t=5137)).

### Watch it
- [2022 Ep 23 · 4:50](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=290): sweep versus run.
- [2022 Ep 25 · 27:01](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=1621): the order of the raids sets the bias.

## Turtle soup

### What it is
Turtle soup is a fake break of an old level. ICT's 2022 definition: a false break below an old low that then rallies, or a false break above an old high that then drops ([2022 Ep 7 · 25:09](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1509)). He says it is his own simplified version, not the pattern in Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' book *Street Smarts*, which he still recommends.

The name snubs Richard Dennis's turtle traders. The book fades a failed 20-day breakout ([2024 #5 · 6:09](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=369), [7:49](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=469)). ICT took only the idea of looking at stop hunts and standing opposite the stopped-out traders. He counts candles and structure, not 20 days ([2024 #37 · 34:11](https://youtu.be/l7eu9yvUUtg?t=2051)).

In 2024 he says there are only two forms ([2024 #8 · 3:33:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=12807)):

1. **A reversal that changes the direction of delivery.** Buy stops above old highs are taken, bodies as well as wicks. Price reaches an objective such as a new week or new day opening gap (Chapter 11), then shifts delivery (a CISD, Chapter 9), and the new direction lasts for a session, a day or longer.
2. **An entry technique inside a program already running.** Every short taken in a bearish FVG is a sale above an old high, the candle high that marks the gap's edge. Every long in a bullish FVG is a purchase below an old low. The reason is not support and resistance but the absence of buyers who were ever offered a chance above that candle's high ([2024 #8 · 2:46:05](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=9965)). The limit order goes at the old high plus one tick.

### Why it works
ICT's explanation is that it flips retail logic. After a one-sided first hour, breakout sellers are enticed to chase a break of support. The algorithm uses that: a shallow run below the low, then back inside the range. In his words, "we're flipping retail logic upside down and attacking them with it" ([2026 #113 · 53:06](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3186), [51:31](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3091)). It is the same trap described in the earlier sections, applied at a fresh high or low.

### How to spot it
1. A clear level: an old high or low, relative equal highs, the previous day's high or low, or the edge of an FVG.
2. The right time of day. A short-term high must form at the intended time and then be pierced ([2024 #24 · 2:35:27](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9327)). In 2026 he says turtle soups inside macro time windows have the highest probability ([2026 #100 · 3:26](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=206)).
3. A higher-timeframe draw the other way, such as a daily FVG or sell-side liquidity below ([2024 #23 · 0:01](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=1)).
4. Price pierces and fails to hold.
5. Two stages, in his 2026 version: the obvious pool first, then a second, less obvious one. Do not short after the first; wait for both ([2026 #100 · 5:06](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=306)).

### How to trade it
- **Frame the zone, then wait.** Define the higher-timeframe FVG as the entry window and mark the target. Wait for a short-term high to form at the right time. Enter on the piercing and retrace, then sit still ([2024 #24 · 2:35:27](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9327)). An FVG can be the entry mechanism.
- **Do not fade a strong trend blindly.** In 2026 he notes that in a very strong bull market turtle soup can fail and price runs through, so identify the bias first ([2026 #100](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=206)).

### Worked example
NQ, Thursday September 5, 2024 ([2024 #24 · 2:35:27](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9327), [3:05:06](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11106)). The notes show the logic but the price captions are unclear.

1. Wednesday's daily high was the lower edge of a daily bearish FVG. The target was the low of the August 13 daily FVG.
2. His rule: enter above Wednesday's daily high and below the FVG's upper edge, and only after a short-term high forms at the intended time and is pierced.
3. After the 9:30-10:00 opening range, price rose into the 1-hour and 15-minute FVG and wicked above the high. He sold, and added on the wick.
4. He had planned to close at the daily FVG's consequent encroachment (its midpoint; Chapter 6) on Thursday. Price reached only that, so he held for the low ([2024 #24 · 3:20:22](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=12022)).

He treats the whole position as one trade, one shot one kill (his phrase), entered at the high of the day and aimed at the sell side below ([2024 #23 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/N9RW9v2kOoY?t=438)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes
- **Importing the 20-day rule.** That is the book, not ICT.
- **Calling every failed break a turtle soup.** His version wants a level, a time of day and a higher-timeframe draw. He dismisses candle range theory, the social-media version, as worthless ([2024 #24 · 2:33:05](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9185)).
- **Confusing the two forms.** The reversal needs the objective and the shift in delivery. The entry technique does not predict a day-long change.

### How the teaching changed
Lecture #8 then narrowed the term to the two forms, and Lecture #24 paired it with an FVG entry and a higher-timeframe target. In 2026 he adds the two-stage pools, the macro windows and the first-hour dealing range ([2026 #100](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=306), [#102 · 16:15](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=975), [#113](https://youtu.be/6DuByzKLDsc?t=3091)).

### Watch it
- [2024 #8 · 3:33:27](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=12807): only two forms.
- [2024 #24 · 2:35:27](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9327): a run above the Wednesday high.

## Engineered liquidity

### What it is
Engineered liquidity is price shaped, or at least used, so that stops pile up where ICT expects them to be run. He gives many pictures of it.

- **Lows built under a rally.** Back-and-forth morning action leaves suspect relative equal lows. Anyone long overnight jams a stop right under them. Price runs up, consolidates, makes a low, starts rallying, and retail moves stops under that low ([2022 Ep 9 · 14:33](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=873), [17:45](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1065)).
- **Fake flags.** A retracement or consolidation holding above relative equal highs looks like a bull flag. In a market predisposed lower, it is a stop hunt, time spent there, and then displacement down ([2024 #19 · 35:02](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2102), [36:40](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2200)).
- **Time distortion.** In the last hour of a Thursday, price wicked up to unseat shorts' trailed stops, marked time, and dripped down, leaving fake bull flags so buyers would place entries with sell stops beneath.
- **Bait wicks and phantom trendlines.** Large down-wicks look like bullish accumulation and protection for shorts. He treats them as bait: the stops clustered around them are the pool, and the wick is a raid zone, not support ([2026 #91 · 15:59](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=959)). An ascending trendline that retail reads as support is likewise a marker of the stops below it ([2026 #137 · 22:11](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1331)).

### Why it works
The raw material is predictable retail behaviour: stops beyond support and resistance, breakout entries, flags and measured moves. Smart money needs counterparties in size, and an obvious picture supplies them. In 2024 he describes engineering as predicting where relative equal highs and lows will be built before they form, once a magnet such as a new day opening gap sits nearby ([2024 #3 · 50:57](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=3057)).

His example rule: with an opening gap far below and relative equal highs forming after 7:00, never short the first drop. Let price run those highs first and do not chase ([2024 #3 · 52:31](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=3151)). This is the one place where he predicts a pool before it exists. He says beginners should not ([2024 #2 · 55:13](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=3313)).

### How to spot it
1. The picture looks too perfect, too pristine, textbook support or resistance ([2022 Ep 19 · 1:48:49](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=6529), [2024 #38 · 41:28](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=2488)).
2. Ask what a retail trader would do: buy the break of the short-term high, put the stop under a smooth low or a wick ([2024 #38 · 43:02](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=2582), [44:37](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=2677)).
3. Watch the bodies. In 2026 he points to wicks touching a level while bodies cannot, and treats it as engineered redistribution ([2026 #54 · 12:17](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=737), [17:57](https://youtu.be/2L88yH3LKcs?t=1077)). The 2024 version is the same idea: wicks tag a level, bodies decline to accept it ([2024 #46 · 32:48](https://youtu.be/z2t62hgjLXo?t=1968)).

### How to trade it
- Ask whether you are being set up to buy and get stopped out. In April 2026 he read a rally that took relative equal highs, with bodies parked in the upper half of an inversion FVG and no continuation, as a trap and looked for the reversal ([2026 #94 · 29:19](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=1759)). Chapter 7 covers the inversion FVG.
- Treat the engineered level as the target or the place to fade after the run. The entry still comes from the model.
- Your own stops are liquidity. He asks students not to put theirs where he said his would be, since tens of thousands follow his streams and an easy target gets run ([2024 #10 · 2:38:00](https://youtu.be/qoNQfVzVXZQ?t=9480)).

### How the teaching changed
In 2024 he already allows that manual intervention, such as news or geopolitics, can break the pattern ([2024 #1 · 1:53:31](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=6811)). In 2026 he personifies the market maker as PHIL, and repeatedly asks what PHIL would do next, for example run back up to the relative equal highs ([2026 #53 · 29:17](https://youtu.be/tp0qkiWCctw?t=1757)). Use PHIL as a thought device, not as a rule.

### Watch it
- [2024 #24 · 3:18:44](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=11924): the late-day wick and the fake flags.
- [2026 #91 · 15:59](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=959): wicks as bait.

## Minor and primary pools, internal and external range liquidity

### What it is
ICT sorts pools by their distance from price and by where they sit against a range. Three vocabularies say the same thing, nearest first, and the notes never equate them exactly.

- **Minor and primary (2024).** On the 15-minute chart at about 8:15 ET, the nearest high and low are the minor pools. The next logical level, often the relative equal highs or lows beyond a small consolidation, is the primary pool ([2024 #42 · 18:12](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=1092), [20:54](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=1254)). Before 9:00 he annotates minor pools. At 9:00 he re-annotates, and the clean highs and lows nearest price become primary ([2024 #40 · 22:46](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=1366)). The 15-minute chart is his bellwether: its pairs are the primary pools, and the 5-minute chart shows minor pools inside that range ([2024 #24 · 17:53](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=1073), [36:58](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=2218)).
- **Internal and external range liquidity (2022).** Take a range. Internal range liquidity (IRL) is the short-term highs and lows, and the imbalances, inside the leg that price is retracing into ([2022 Ep 3 · 34:28](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2068)). External range liquidity (ERL) is the stops beyond the range, on old highs or lows. His rule is to take partials at internal range liquidity and treat external range liquidity as the trade ([2022 Ep 6 · 29:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1769)).
- **Dealing range and external range (2026).** The dealing range runs from the high of the current move down to a significant low, such as the 10:00 low. External range liquidity is the sell-side outside it, in lows formed yesterday or earlier in the week. The dealing-range low is the first target and the external pool the bigger one ([2026 #102 · 20:11](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1211), [29:57](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1797)).

### How to trade it
1. Mark the range and list the pools inside and beyond it.
2. Take the first partial at the nearest pool, internal or minor.
3. Hold the rest for the farther pool, external or primary, if the path stays clean. Speed through the nearer pool is the cue that the farther one is the target ([2026 #141 · 41:36](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2496)).
4. Place exits a tick or two short of the pool.
5. To size the farther target after a high is broken, he projects one quarter of the previous range above it. It is a rule of thumb from experience, it need not be hit, and he says being exact about it is not important to him ([2024 #51 · 1:40](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=100), [2:27](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=147)).

### Worked example
NQ, February 3, 2022, a trade running down through the range ([2022 Ep 6 · 45:00](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2700)).

[[DIAGRAM ch03_d5 | caption: partials at internal range liquidity, the final exit at external range liquidity (schematic candles; the two prices are from the notes) | Notice that the first target sits inside the range, in discount, and the last one sits outside it, under the equal lows.]]

1. Put a Fibonacci on the price run. The 50% level is equilibrium, and below it is discount.
2. A bullish FVG in that discount, inside the range, was the internal pool and the partial: 14,675.
3. The sell stops under the equal lows, outside the range, were the external pool and the exit: 14,647 ([2022 Ep 6 · 45:49](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=2749)).

A second, December 2024 case shows the minor-then-primary order. His posted sequence was to take the 8:00 AM high (the minor buy side), then trade down to 21,490, built on relative equal lows and the London lows, then toward 21,580 ([2024 #51 · 6:28](https://youtu.be/403Dzt_Vl5Y?t=388)).

### Watch it
- [2022 Ep 6 · 29:29](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1769): partials internal, external is your trade.
- [2026 #102 · 20:11](https://youtu.be/DJ67ft6XENo?t=1211): dealing range versus external range liquidity.

## The draw on liquidity

### What it is
The draw on liquidity (DOL) is where price is being pulled next. In 2022 his picture was a paperclip and a magnet: price is the clip, and levels and seasonality are the magnet. Price draws either to stops or to an imbalance, and most of the draw is found on the daily chart ([2022 Ep 2 · 14:45](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=885), [17:08](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1028)). In 2026 he counts four possible draws: sell-side liquidity, buy-side liquidity, a premium inefficiency above, and a discount inefficiency below ([2026 #141 · 40:00](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=2400)).

In 2024 he plays it down on purpose. The draw is an initial reasonable assumption about a directional move that could unfold. It is not the end point, and for a short-term bias he takes the low-hanging fruit, such as nearby relative equal highs ([2024 #1 · 49:08](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=2948), [1:06:50](https://youtu.be/GKeLVR3dPuI?t=4010)).

### Why it matters
Without a draw, he says, everything else puts you offside ([2024 #7 · 3:09:24](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=11364)). Knowing it tells you where the stop belongs and lets you judge each candle against an expectation ([2026 #136](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=585)).

### How to choose it
1. **Start high.** Build from the weekly chart to the daily, then the intraday charts. If the market moves, it moves to something on the daily ([2024 #7 · 7:34](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=454)).
2. **Lean to the nearer side.** After the weekly close, compare the open with the previous week's and month's highs and lows. The nearer side is the path of least resistance ([2026 #32 · 8:27](https://youtu.be/dmHSwlmS9iY?t=507)).
3. **Sort the pools** into smooth and jagged, taken and untaken, minor and primary.
4. **Pick the smallest easy objective first.** A short-term high is easier to find than an inefficiency, because shorts trail protection just above it. Take a partial there and hold the rest for the inefficiency above ([2026 #136 · 9:45](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=585)). Many setups have a near-term draw and a best case ([2026 #75 · 3:13](https://youtu.be/bUN_f8U_1Eg?t=193)).
5. **Stack factors.** He wants at least three, one of them time. If you can justify the trade on both the buy and the sell side, it is not high probability ([2024 #36 · 13:44](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=824), [16:07](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=967)).
6. **Make sure there is room.** He wants at least 20 handles of simple, timeable run on NQ, or 20 pips in forex ([2026 #132 · 40:34](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2434)).
7. **If nothing is clear, do nothing.** Veterans ask what price is likely to do now, not what the next three runs will be ([2026 #141 · 5:37](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=337)).

### How to trade it
- **It need not be reached.** The draw does not have to be traded to for the framework to pay ([2022 Ep 27 · 1:54](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=114)). A long that never reached the 12,553 equal highs still worked.
- **Keep the vision until proven wrong.** Hold it on the chart and do not abandon it until price has made it obvious ([2026 #141 · 20:02](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1202)). If price fails before reaching the pool, flip to the other side ([2026 #36 · 0:54](https://youtu.be/0Bu4g-2aMA4?t=54)).
- **A draw is not an entry.** The narrative alone is not a buy signal. In 2022 he waited for displacement, measured premium and discount, and bought only in discount ([2022 Ep 38 · 29:09](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1749)). In 2026 he adds that reaching a level needs confirmation in the bodies before an entry ([2026 #95 · 25:44](https://youtu.be/5PpnT-l-zSk?t=1544)).
- **Levels he names are not trades.** He points at liquidity as a scout while students do the sniping ([2022 Ep 31 · 3:19](https://youtu.be/OYNpIeu9czw?t=199)).

### Worked example
NQ, before and after the 8:30 news, January 29, 2026 ([2026 #136 · 9:45](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=585)).

1. Price had sold off overnight and left the lows jagged, with no equal lows to draw to.
2. A minor buy-side pool sat at the lower of two relative equal highs. Above it was an upper-quadrant inefficiency made of a volume imbalance and a SIBI (Chapters 5 and 6).
3. After the 8:30 news, price ran to the short-term high, then up to the bottom of the inefficiency, then sold hard.

## Check yourself

1. It is 7:20 AM ET on NQ. The 5-minute chart shows two highs at nearly the same price, the right one slightly lower. Below them, the last leg down made three lower lows in a row. Which side is the draw, and why?
2. A student marks two lows as relative equal lows, but the right-hand low is 2 handles lower than the left. What does ICT expect, and what should the student do instead of waiting for a raid?
3. Price pokes 3 handles above a clean high, closes back inside the range, and the next candle is bearish. Is it a sweep or a run? What would have made it a run?
4. In a bearish week at 9:30 price first takes the previous low, then rallies through the relative equal highs. Does ICT call that run bearish, and which side does he expect to be the draw?
5. It is 9:00 AM ET. You find relative equal highs that formed at 6:10 AM in London and others that formed at 8:15. Which does he want you to use, and what does he say about the London ones?
6. You are short, reaching down for yesterday's low. Price pierces it for the first time. Why does ICT not want your partial there, and what is the beginner alternative?
7. In a short from premium, a bullish FVG sits in discount inside the range, and sell stops sit under equal lows outside it. Which is internal and which external range liquidity, and what do you do at each?
8. A friend says "sell-side liquidity is the run lower" after a big down leg. What term has he mixed up, and what is the difference?

## Answers

1. The highs are the draw. They are smooth, untouched and fit the right-hand rule, while the jagged lows have already been run. See "Smooth versus jagged price".
2. It is not relative equal, since the right low undercuts the left. A raid is less likely at once. Price may first retrace to an inefficiency above or a short-term high. See "Relative equal highs and lows".
3. A sweep: a shallow poke that returned to the range. A run would have gone over the high and carried on expanding, with bodies beyond it. See "Sweeps, runs and the order of the raids".
4. No. Running the buy side without first taking the previous low is the bearish order; here the sell side was taken first, so the run higher is not bearish and the buy side is the draw. See "Sweeps, runs and the order of the raids".
5. Use the 8:15 pair. At 9:00 he looks back only to 7:00, and pre-7:00 London levels are the weaker filter. See "Relative equal highs and lows".
6. A first pierce is often followed by a pull back and a deeper second leg, and exiting just beneath the low can leave a larger run unpaid. A beginner can project one quarter of the range below the low and take a partial there. See "Old highs, old lows and the reference list".
7. The FVG in discount, inside the range, is internal: take the first partial there. The stops under the equal lows, outside the range, are external: the final exit, placed a tick or two short. See "Minor and primary pools, internal and external range liquidity".
8. Delivery versus liquidity. Sell-side delivery is the algorithm delivering lower prices. Sell-side liquidity is the resting sell stops under a low. See "Buy-side and sell-side liquidity".
