A fair value gap (FVG, the three-candle inefficiency from Chapter 5) comes in every size and at every hour, and ICT's most repeated answer to "which one do I use?" is the first one the market prints after the 9:30 ET open. This chapter teaches how to find that first presented FVG, how its rules tightened between 2024 and 2026, how long to keep it on the chart and how to trade from it. It then covers the other named gaps and gap-like candles that tell you what a move is doing next: breakaway, measuring and exhaustion gaps, the immediate rebalance, the vacuum block and the calendar-anchored Reaper FVG.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch08_d1 | caption: Where the first presented FVG can start: the 9:30 candle is only ever candle 1 (illustrative) | The earliest valid gap has its middle candle at 9:31 and is confirmed when the 9:32 candle closes; a gap built around the 9:30 candle is skipped.]]

- **Definition.** The first presented FVG (FPFVG) is the first three-candle gap on the 1-minute chart whose middle candle is 9:31 ET or later and which completes inside the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range. A gap built around the 9:30 candle does not count ([2024 #16 · 38:16](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=2296)).
- **A frame, not a trigger.** You also need a draw on liquidity, a bias and a time. Bodies should respect the gap's halves: a bearish gap should not take bodies into its upper half ([2026 #141 · 27:57](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1677)).
- **Carry it forward.** Keep it until 3:45 ET the same day. In 2024 its life was about 3 days; in 2026 he tracks Monday and Friday gaps for two weeks, and a gap after a turtle soup for 20 days.
- **2026 tightening.** The gap must come from displacement, so a tiny "common gap" does not count. A first presented FVG with displacement outranks a plain one, and the first opposite gap after an up-close one is the reflection FVG.
- **Special gaps.** A breakaway gap is left when price leaves a consolidation after a stop hunt and stays open: expect continuation. A measuring gap sits mid-move and sizes the second leg. An exhaustion gap is closed by bodies and is spent.
- **Immediate rebalance.** After a big displacement candle, the next candle (or the one after) returns to the prior candle's edge and the move then continues. ICT reads it as a continuation tell, not a reversal.

## The first presented FVG

### What it is

A fair value gap is the space between candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick that candle 2's range leaves untraded (Chapter 5). Its midpoint is the consequent encroachment (CE), and it divides into quadrants (Chapter 6). The first presented FVG is the first such gap the 1-minute chart prints once the regular session has opened.

ICT's precise definition is the first FVG that forms inside the opening range, 9:30 to 10:00 ET. The candle that creates the imbalance, the middle candle, cannot be earlier than 9:31 ([2024 #16 · 38:16](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=2296)). So the 9:30 candle can only be candle 1. Wait for the 9:31 candle to close; at 9:32, if the 9:30, 9:31 and 9:32 candles leave a gap around the 9:31 candle, that is the first FVG of the day ([2024 #31 · 1:09:31](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4171)).

He also calls it the first presentation, FPFVG, or a gap that cannot hide ([2024 #31 · 12:26](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=746)). The window is firm: the opening range is exactly 30 minutes, and he calls any other definition a "gimmick" ([2024 #22 · 11:31](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=691)). In 2026 he repeats that a 15-minute opening range is wrong ([2026 #126 · 55:06](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3306)).

Three details keep students from drawing the wrong gap.

- **The 9:30 candle.** A gap that includes it is still an FVG and worth seeing, but it is not the first presented one ([2024 #35 · 13:07](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=787)). In 2026 he says the window is technically 9:30 to 9:59 but tells students to start at minute 31, because the 9:30 candle goes wrong for people who do not fully know what they are doing ([2026 #129 · 52:37](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3157)).
- **ETH and RTH.** ETH is the full electronic futures session; RTH is the regular session that opens at 9:30. Toggle TradingView from ETH to RTH and a gap created at 9:30 disappears. A genuine first presentation must exist in both views ([2024 #16 · 20:29](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=1229)).
- **The 15-second option.** A trader comfortable below one minute, with a bias, can use the first 15-second FVG outside the 9:30 one-minute candle, counting from the 9:31 candle ([2024 #35 · 5:49](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=349)).

**Which markets.** ICT taught this on NQ and ES at the 9:30 ET cash open, and the RTH and ETH views are futures features. In 2026 he says he has used the logic across stocks, forex, bonds and commodities ([2026 #23 · 28:15](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1695)). The notes give no gold rule and no alternative clock time for XAUUSD, and a CFD chart has no RTH toggle. Any gold version is your own testing, not something he taught.

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is that the algorithm treats the 9:30 to 10:00 opening range as a preliminary range. It establishes the first imbalance and the initial highs and lows, and you build the day's narrative from them ([2024 #36 · 5:25](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=325)). He says the first FVG in that window gives an enormous edge on timing, meaning when the market will roll ([2024 #38 · 1:13:46](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4426)).

He insists the reaction is coded, not caused by buyers and sellers. On August 27, 2024 price traded down into the first FVG and stopped exactly at its top, with the candle low at 19555.25; nothing in buying and selling pressure, he says, explains stopping to the tick ([2024 #18 · 59:38](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=3578)). On August 23 the day's low formed at 3:45 right at the bottom of the day's first FVG, which he calls logic coded in time and price ([2024 #16 · 43:05](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=2585)). In 2026 he adds that it is the turning point because it is the first presented imbalance, not support and resistance ([2026 #141 · 27:07](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1627)).

There is a practical reason too: the first gap removes the "which FVG?" choice that beginners find trial and error ([2024 #28 · 14:37](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=877)) and gives every student the same reference point ([2024 #36 · 7:14](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=434)). He is candid that it is not the best PD array he has; he leans on it because it is obvious to new students ([2024 #22 · 18:15](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=1095)). Treat his claims that these gaps are "Easter eggs" repeating with high probability ([2026 #23 · 13:38](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=818)) as hypotheses for your own journal.

### How to spot it

1. Chart the 1-minute in New York time. Use the RTH view and confirm the gap also shows in ETH.
2. Step candle by candle from 9:30. The middle candle must be 9:31 or later. If a later candle's high touches an earlier candle's low there is no gap, and he says he cannot use that one ([2024 #31 · 12:26](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=746)).
3. Take the first true three-candle gap that completes before 10:00. Some mornings it forms late: on August 28, 2024 there was no FVG until 9:35 ([2024 #19 · 1:03:20](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=3800)).
4. Draw the rectangle from candle 1's wick extreme to candle 3's. When bodies do not meet, include the volume imbalance, the body-to-body space, so the quadrants come out right ([2024 #42 · 1:57:47](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=7067)). Mark the CE and extend the box to the right.
5. Shade it grey at first so no bias is baked in ([2024 #31 · 1:07:08](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4028)).

The 2026 displacement and size filter, in the next section, applies to step 3. Use it before you trust the gap.

### How to trade it

The gap is a frame, not an entry. ICT says the first FVG alone is not a trade; wait for the conditions around it ([2024 #36 · 38:34](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=2314)). In 2026 he repeats that something in the narrative must make you want to be short, because you are blending ideas into one story ([2026 #10 · 32:41](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=1961)).

**Reading the gap.**

- The market is bullish above it and bearish below it. Let the first 30 minutes of volatility trade through it, and weight it more when it lines up with the 9:50 to 10:10 macro window ([2024 #36 · 40:11](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=2411)).
- Judge it by bodies. Wicks outside the gap are normal; ICT calls this a "Mohawk", coloring outside the lines, and what counts is where the bodies close ([2024 #42 · 1:52:57](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=6777)).
- A bearish gap should keep bodies out of its upper half. If price cannot even reach the CE, he calls it exceedingly weak and the sell-off stands ([2026 #141 · 27:57](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1677)).
- On a trending day you get one chance at the gap. On a non-trending day price returns to it several times ([2024 #22 · 12:17](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=737)).

**Entry models as he gave them.**

- **The recipe (September 2024).** For a short from a premium gap, enter anywhere inside it. Candle 3's high is the most direct level, or one tick below it if you are aggressive; the CE and lower quadrant are other choices. The target is mid gap, the midpoint of the opening range gap ([2024 #31 · 1:07:08](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=4028)). Longs mirror it. Stay in until stopped or at target, and do not modify or rename the recipe. He lists the failure modes as over-leveraging, trading before the gap appears, skipping it because you have not desensitized, and adding your own extras ([2024 #29 · 29:50](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1790)).
- **Rejection entry.** Price returns into the gap and does not close above the upper half of a bearish one. That is a high-quality entry. If you miss it, you can short a return into the inversion FVG with the same stop idea ([2024 #19 · 1:12:07](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=4327)).
- **Displacement, retest, then 15 seconds.** Do not short merely because price is inside the gap. Wait for price to displace below it and leave a body away. When price comes back up and kisses the gap's low, that is the short. Then drop to 15 seconds and use the first 15-second FVG that price trades back into ([2024 #36 · 25:38](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=1538), [27:16](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=1636)). The live session did not follow this cleanly; it was a high-resistance day.
- **Short at the CE (February 2026).** Sell as price trades up to the CE. A weak gap should not trade above its CE, and in his example the wick failed to reach it twice. If stopped out, wait for price to trade back below the CE, then short the next run up: an FVG, or the up-close candle he calls a rejection block ([2026 #129 · 53:25](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3205), [54:12](https://youtu.be/NxwfXE6Zyns?t=3252)).
- **Lower half and partials (2026).** He calls a short anywhere in the lower half of a bearish gap the most realistic entry for you. The first partial is the 10:00-hour low or the relative equal lows, with a longer hold toward the 7 to 9 AM pre-market equal lows ([2026 #10 · 39:53](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2393)).
- **As a target.** If you are bearish and price sits above the gap, expect a drop into it. On March 17, 2026 he called the move into a 9:32 gap about 25 handles of bread and butter ([2026 #118 · 26:01](https://youtu.be/AxLXI00L8zM?t=1561)).

**Where the stop goes.** He gave several rules, so pick one and journal it.

**The 2026 checklist** (January 13, 2026, [2026 #137 · 1:22:19](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=4939)).

1. Pick a market whose higher-timeframe premise points to a specific level: an old high or low, equal highs or lows, or an imbalance.
2. Check what just happened. After a sell-side raid, look for the premium array price must climb through, and decide whether the draw is a single high or equal highs ([1:23:08](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=4988)).
3. On the lower timeframe, wait for the first presented FVG in your window. It must displace and be significant; enter there, with the stop from candle 1 ([1:23:56](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5036)).
4. Pre-plan a partial, and if you trade one contract, pre-plan where the stop moves.

He warns against piling on every model in a "kitchen sink". One candle is not a premise either: you need a tool for bias, one for timing and one to confirm ([1:26:18](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=5178)).

### Worked example

NQ, 1-minute, September 17, 2024. The first presented FVG formed at 9:33 after a run up to about 19,794 to 19,795, a bearish gap with candle 3's high near 19,795. ICT's referenced entry was a limit sell near 19,794.75, one tick under candle 3's high, with the stop above candle 2's high ([2024 #29 · 29:50](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1790), [36:17](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=2177)).

[[DIAGRAM ch08_d2 | caption: NQ 1-minute, Sept 17, 2024: first presented FVG short, schematic candles with the lecture's levels | Entry sits at the gap's lower edge, the stop above candle 2, and the target is the midpoint of the opening range gap.]]

Price spiked slightly above the gap, a Mohawk, without taking out candle 2's high, so the stop held. It then fell to mid gap at 19,728.25 by 10:00, 66.5 points from the entry. The sequence is the point: a time (9:31 to 10:00), a bias toward mid gap, a defined gap, stop and target ([2024 #29 · 1:11:46](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4306)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Building the gap on the 9:30 candle.** He slipped on this himself on September 16, 2024 and corrected it the next day ([2024 #28 · 1:14:56](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=4496), [2024 #29 · 55:41](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=3341)). Do not confuse yesterday's carried-over box with today's gap either ([2024 #35 · 4:56](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=296)).
- **Trading the first gap on its own.** On February 27, 2026 many traders read the 9:32 gap as a structure shift and went long. He says that was wrong: clean relative equal lows sat below, the long-term bias was bearish, it was a TGIF Friday, and price was still inside the first 30 minutes ([2026 #126 · 36:05](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=2165), [53:32](https://youtu.be/FBxxYUvNaY4?t=3212)).
- **Selling every rejection wick.** On September 16, 2024 NQ gapped lower, then fell in a straight line at 9:30 (a Judas swing, Chapter 13). A lone bearish gap sat in the lower half of that drop. Retail logic says wicks inside a gap mean rejection, so short. ICT calls it bait: the bodies respected the gap, which is accumulation, and he did not chase a move already deep in discount ([2024 #28 · 1:01:19](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3679), [1:03:42](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=3822)).
- **Confusing it with the opening range gap.** That gap runs from the prior settlement to the 9:30 open (Chapter 11). The first presented FVG is a three-candle gap in the first minutes, and the two can share the same price area ([2024 #45 · 18:42](https://youtu.be/xUWonBnfYOk?t=1122)). He dropped his new day opening gap studies because they told him nothing the first presented FVG did not ([2024 #35 · 21:03](https://youtu.be/NDwNIqUkASc?t=1263)).
- **Validating with volume profile.** He rejects it as the way to find real FVGs ([2026 #71 · 35:23](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2123)).
- **Calling it an inversion too early.** A gap keeps its original role until price trades through it; then it inverts (Chapter 7). He once misspoke and called a reclaimed bullish FVG an inversion, and says a down-close candle price rallies into is a bearish FVG ([2024 #36 · 38:34](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=2314)).
- **Ignoring the wick.** In April 2026 he says the visible gap between bodies is not the real gap; the wick to its left is the true inefficiency ([2026 #106 · 12:06](https://youtu.be/Hiq47zliJ_Y?t=726)). The notes do not say how to redraw the box, so see Chapters 6 and 10.

## Which gap counts: displacement, context and raids

### What it is

In 2024 the rule was mechanical: the first gap after 9:31. By 2026 ICT adds filters, and he keeps two tiers. The purist form, his original, is simply the very first gap after 9:30 ([2026 #17 · 43:12](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2592)). The stronger form is the first gap that comes from displacement, a move that takes out a short-term high or low, and that fits the story price is telling.

The filters, in the order he gives them:

- **Displacement.** The qualifying gap is made by a run through a short-term high or low at the specific time, and it shows direction and size. A tiny gap between two candles that does not displace is not it, and neither is an engulfing candle. He says an engulfing candle is not a market structure shift ([2026 #138 · 51:16](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3076), [2026 #137 · 18:06](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1086)).
- **The "common gap".** The 7:00, 7:01 and 7:02 candles often leave a tiny gap. He calls it a common gap, not the first presented FVG, because it causes no displacement ([2026 #137 · 16:30](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=990)).
- **Size.** As a rule of thumb, a gap under 10 handles on NQ is about 80% likely not to be the one; wait for another. He gives it as general guidance, not strict ([2026 #138 · 1:07:19](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=4039)).
- **Not always 9:31.** If a later candle makes a larger displacement, for example a big drop below a swing low, anchor on that candle's gap instead of a small gap at the open. It takes visual judgment ([2026 #52 · 10:21](https://youtu.be/e6XwTK4hVqI?t=621)).
- **Context and narrative.** Many FVGs can sit on a chart, but the one that matters forms as price breaks structure: it takes buy-side liquidity, displaces through swing lows, and the gap forms in that sequence. In his June 3 example the earlier gap did not act inversely and the second one was the fpFVG ([2026 #79 · 3:59](https://youtu.be/7M-AO01cAZ8?t=239)).
- **Aligned with the draw.** Only the gap in the direction of the plot counts. With a sell-side draw, that is the first sell-side imbalance (SIBI) after 9:30. Without the plot, he warns, you cannot use it ([2026 #141 · 16:52](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=1012)).
- **First presented or anchored.** An FVG is valid in one of two ways: it is the first presented, or it is anchored to an octant or quadrant level of a higher-timeframe range (Chapter 6). He does not discard other gaps; they just are not treated as valid ([2026 #71 · 35:23](https://youtu.be/pq9WuZ9q4Bg?t=2123)).

On a three-candle gap, candle 2 should touch a graded level, ideally two. A candle touching an upper quadrant and a lower octant signals a strong contraction in the trend direction ([2026 #47 · 8:50](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=530), [11:14](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=674)). He also notes it is a probability tilt: price retraced more than he expected that day.

- **Turtle soup version.** Price runs a swing high, fails, and snaps back through the prior run. - **A new move.** In other 2026 lectures he describes the first presented FVG as the first gap when price enters a new move, to be tracked as a target or an inversion candidate ([2026 #67 · 7:07](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=427), [2026 #68 · 8:55](https://youtu.be/2bke4YH6ZuE?t=535)).

### First presented FVG with displacement

In the August and September 2026 lectures ICT ranks the gaps. A first presented FVG whose candle, or the run it belongs to, also breaks the prior swing high or low is a first presented FVG with displacement (FPFVG-D). It gets much more emphasis. If a plain gap and an FPFVG-D both exist in the same session, extend the FPFVG-D for targeting ([2026 #11 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=99), [3:15](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=195)). He says it takes greater command over price delivery than a simple initial formation and "trumps" the first one ([2026 #10 · 36:42](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2202)).

His example: the first FVG at 10:01 was a down-close gap (SIBI). Price rallied, and a later up-close candle (BISI) took out highs, leaving a displaced range. Price returned to the first gap, closed below its midpoint without closing outside it, and sold off, which he says was over 175 handles ([2026 #10 · 39:53](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2393)). The notes warn that which candle is which is garbled in the captions, so read it as the concept.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Labeling a gap before there is displacement.** On March 10, 2026 he says not to call a gap first presented until displacement is confirmed, meaning a move outside the previous day's or session's range in your direction. No displacement means wait for more data; do not form-fit or cherry-pick ([2026 #122 · 27:40](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1660)). His example: at 9:44 AM, after upward displacement, the first presented FVG was identified and later held as resistance.
- **Treating a first gap as a shift in structure.** The February 27 case above: the gap was real, the context was not.

### How the teaching changed

- **August to October 2024.** The rule is time and sequence: first gap, middle candle 9:31 or later, inside 9:30 to 10:00. He says he first taught it in Twitter Spaces in 2023 ([2024 #18 · 37:47](https://youtu.be/yOZHawAE8QI?t=2267)). The 2024 notes contain no size or displacement filter.
- **January 2026.** Displacement, the common gap, the 10-handle guide and the 7:00 pre-market window appear. He calls it consistent with earlier teaching, and says in the December review that he has no intention of changing the algorithm or the teaching ([2026 #137 · 18:06](https://youtu.be/Rwao8f6oD0Q?t=1086), [2026 #141 · 53:42](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=3222)).
- **March to July 2026.** The idea spreads to any first gap after a raid or a range break, and to context: first presented or anchored.
- **August and September 2026.** Two tiers (plain and with displacement) and the reflection FVG (next section). On September 29 he gives the simple definition again: the first gap that forms after the open, which in his example formed at 9:33 ([2026 #5 · 4:15](https://youtu.be/I7fIf1eEz0w?t=255)).
- **A tension.** One lecture says the plain gap does not need displacement to be valid ([2026 #11 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=99)); others say it must displace ([2026 #122 · 27:40](https://youtu.be/54kzPj03Rkg?t=1660), [2026 #138 · 51:16](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3076)). The two-tier reading reconciles them, but he does not say so, so keep both versions in your journal and see which you can trade.

## Carrying it forward: life cycle and prior-day gaps

### What it is

ICT does not delete the first presented FVG when the morning ends. He extends the box to the right and keeps it as a level. Its edges are specific prices (the high, upper quadrant, CE, lower quadrant and low), and he stresses that they are "not a zone" ([2024 #32 · 47:33](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2853)). That precision is why he insists on drawing the rectangle carefully, with the volume imbalance included when there is one.

### Why it works

His explanation is that the algorithm refers back to these gaps unless the day is a trending one ([2024 #32 · 17:48](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1068)). In 2026 he says they act as magnets, like new week and new day opening gaps, and that price gravitates back to the one he chose before the week closes ([2026 #136 · 1:20:32](https://youtu.be/8wHewi6N6dg?t=4832)). Knowing the level in advance, he says, puts you ahead of price: you wait for time to agree with the level instead of discovering it afterwards ([2026 #141 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=815)). These are his claims; the way to test them is a journal.

### How to do it

1. **Same day: carry it to 3:45 ET.** The final-hour algorithm can reference it, and the 3:15 to 3:45 macro ends there ([2024 #16 · 42:14](https://youtu.be/kuURDD1ajdQ?t=2534)).
2. **Across days, set "extend right".** He explains where the setting lives and why you must use it ([2024 #32 · 21:46](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1306), [23:21](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1401)).
3. **Journal the last three days.** For each, ask whether that day's first FVG influenced price today ([2024 #32 · 17:48](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1068)).
4. **Check where the day opened.** If the market opens inside a prior day's larger gap, it uses that gap first. Today's small gap may only matter "next week instead" ([2024 #32 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=672)).
5. **Colour by role.** Grey while you have no bias ([2026 #67 · 11:19](https://youtu.be/N3qz13Hl-gg?t=679)), orange for a gap you are watching as a possible inversion ([2026 #18 · 17:35](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1055)).
6. **Keep separate layouts.** One for first presented FVGs only, one each for NWOG, NDOG and RTH opening range gaps, read against premium, discount and equilibrium ([2026 #13 · 14:28](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=868), [2026 #17 · 40:47](https://youtu.be/MB7clEZvF7s?t=2447)).
7. **Log each one.** His 2026 study rule, taught for the first FVG of the 10:00 hour, is to record the time it formed, how many minutes passed before price came back into it, and the range in handles from the return high to the low taken. For the first four to six weeks, do not predict; log it after the fact ([2026 #14 · 23:09](https://youtu.be/L81eMQhmXmc?t=1389)).

### How to trade it

- **Sell the rise into yesterday's gap.** With a bearish bias and yesterday's gap overhead, wait for price to make a lower low and then come back up into the box, and short at its high or CE. His reason is that large funds and high-frequency algorithms cannot short on a down-tick and must wait for an uptick. That is his claim and the notes do not verify it ([2024 #32 · 50:01](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3001)).
- **Buy a prior day's gap as a discount array.** The condition is no candle bodies in its lower half; a wick into it is acceptable, but preferably there is none. With that, he says, price can start "ripping higher" ([2026 #18 · 17:35](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=1055)).
- **Use the gaps as targets and partials.** In the notes, Thursday's gap low is a target on the way down, a limit order sits near the high of Thursday's gap, and Wednesday's gap low is the final partial ([2026 #15 · 15:37](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=937), [27:13](https://youtu.be/Khj1veL49r8?t=1633), [2026 #18 · 1:12:51](https://youtu.be/djabvhihIM8?t=4371)). On July 22 price traded overnight into Tuesday's gap, which he had called in advance ([2026 #51 · 7:20](https://youtu.be/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=440)).
- **Read clusters.** When several daily levels sit close together, such as the CEs of Monday's, Tuesday's and Thursday's opening range gaps, treat them as one decision zone: above it the market is more likely to sell off, below it to rally ([2026 #49 · 18:37](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=1117)).

### Worked example

NQ (micro), 1-minute, Friday, September 20, 2024. Thursday's first FVG was carried as an orange box, and Friday opened inside it. Today's own first FVG formed at 9:34. ICT removed its volume imbalance so it showed candle low to candle high only, calling the body-to-body part the least impactful ([2024 #32 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=672), [47:33](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=2853)).

Price dropped through today's gap at 9:50, then rallied into Thursday's box and touched its CE. He shorted at the top of that gap, which looks random but is the high of the reference gap, and covered just under the relative equal lows ([2024 #32 · 51:37](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3097), [55:37](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3337)). The notes do not give clear prices. He says he used a market order only because it was replay and paper trading; with limit orders you can get tight entries and meaningful stops.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Stacking every old gap.** In 2026 he says to keep only the last two weeks of Monday and Friday gaps and not clutter the chart; use separate workspaces ([2026 #23 · 28:15](https://youtu.be/P8Pn8DHFSW8?t=1695)).
- **Assuming every gap will matter.** On a trending day the algorithm may not come back, and a larger prior gap can overshadow today's. The edges are exact prices, not a zone.
- **Calling a muddy day a failure.** After a large-range day, candle bodies spill outside the gap and the setup looks messy. On September 4, 2024 critics said the gap had failed; he expected it to be reclaimed, and price returned to it later ([2024 #22 · 13:06](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=786), [1:01:28](https://youtu.be/Ipjk9S8HTbA?t=3688)).

### How the teaching changed

- **Life cycle.** The length depends on the lecture. In August and September 2024 it is about 3 days: after the third day its importance wanes but it can still be used, and a gap not yet traded back into can last weeks ([2024 #21 · 6:33](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=393), [2024 #32 · 17:48](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=1068)). In 2026 he tracks Monday and Friday gaps for the last two weeks ([2026 #13 · 13:41](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=821)). He carries Monday's gap through the whole week ([2026 #49 · 11:29](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=689)). After a turtle soup he keeps the gap for 20 days, though the notes flag that wording as unclear ([2026 #141 · 13:35](https://youtu.be/rXgEMIJ1fQg?t=815)). These are different settings; he never gives one rule, and he asks students to keep their own records rather than take his word ([2026 #13 · 13:41](https://youtu.be/-Qag-99u_BM?t=821)).
- **Drawing the volume imbalance.** On September 20, 2024 he drew today's gap wick to wick, calling the volume imbalance the least impactful part. By October 2024 and throughout 2026 the rule is to include the volume imbalance in the rectangle ([2024 #42 · 1:57:47](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=7067), [2026 #42 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1245)).

## The reflection FVG, first utilization and first instance

### What it is

Three 2026 ideas hang off the first presented FVG. They are easy to mix up, so take them one at a time. Chapter 7 teaches the inversion FVG they lean on.

**The reflection FVG.** After you mark an up-close first presented FVG (a BISI, buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency), scan forward for the first down-close gap that forms after it, a SIBI. That SIBI is the first presented reflection FVG: the mirror image of the first gap ([2026 #11 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389)). In that lecture, Friday's first gap was up-close and the first SIBI after it, at 10:01 AM, became the reflection FVG. On Monday and Tuesday price traded into it several times, so ICT treats it as a secondary reference whose high, low and midpoint price tends to respect.

The notes add a restriction: if the first presented FVG is not up-close, the reflection idea does not apply ([2026 #11 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389)). Another lecture uses the word differently. There the first gap at 10:01 was a down-close SIBI, and the later up-close gap that displaced above highs was also called a reflection FVG ([2026 #10 · 37:29](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2249)). That lecture's captions are garbled, and the notes do not reconcile the two uses.

**First presentation versus first utilization.** ICT separates when a gap appears from what it does. "First presentation" is the time a gap forms, such as 9:31. "First utilization" is how the gap functions according to the candle that made it: up-close is a bullish FVG, down-close a bearish one. If the market is bearish, you apply the inversion reading instead. In his example a candle creates a BISI at 9:31, so its first utilization is bullish, but in a bearish market he treats it as a potential inversion from the start rather than waiting for price to break below and flip it ([2026 #51 · 8:54](https://youtu.be/2H_qpeIp2rY?t=534)).

**The principle of first utilization.** Every PD array has an original utilization, the direction it formed in and the move it facilitated. Once price trades through it, its characteristic reverses: it inverts, can be used again in the opposite sense, and is often a more formidable level than before. The practical question for any gap is whether it has been traded through yet. If it has, bodies that stay out of the upper half of the inverted gap (below its midpoint) mean strong bearish rejection, and bodies entering the upper half mean the rejection is lost ([2026 #94 · 7:02](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=422), [12:36](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=756)). He stresses that the gap does not fail when traded through; it changes character, and that is not the same as a failed setup. The notes' ES example from April 21, 2026 is muddled on direction, so use Chapter 7 for the inversion mechanics.

**First instance.** In several lectures the first gap in a series is called the first instance of a fair value gap, used almost interchangeably with first presented ([2026 #61 · 2:23](https://youtu.be/IF7dEkgWoO0?t=143), [2026 #121 · 4:53](https://youtu.be/C90xGr3kW8Y?t=293)). It is the first inefficiency price can use as a target or magnet. A close outside it is the first confirmation that price is going further that way. The notes' own example for this, a Tuesday suspension block, is partly garbled.

### How to spot it

1. Mark the first presented FVG and note whether its candle was up-close or down-close.
2. If it was up-close and you want the reflection, scan forward for the first down-close gap after it ([2026 #11 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389)).
3. Ask whether price has traded through the first gap. If not, it is still in its original utilization; if so, read it as inverted ([2026 #94 · 12:36](https://youtu.be/PCMv3J6_9Lg?t=756)).
4. On a higher timeframe, mark where price first trades through a gap, then drop to a lower timeframe for the retracement ([2026 #76 · 11:26](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=686)).

### How to trade it

Use these as filters on the first presented gap, not as separate entries.

- **Reflection FVG.** A secondary reference for support and resistance in later sessions: price retraces into it or respects its high, low or midpoint. The notes give no separate entry or stop rule for it, so apply the rules from the first presented FVG section.
- **First utilization.** It sets the initial bias. If price invalidates it by closing through, recalibrate: it now has a new utilization, and a bullish array pierced from above can act as a bearish inversion ([2026 #58 · 21:33](https://youtu.be/Dj0663u7VRc?t=1293)).
- **Higher-timeframe first utilization.** On an hourly or daily gap, the first time price trades through it is the green light to hunt for iFVG behavior on the lower timeframes. In his NQ hourly example, price first broke above the gap; on the 1-minute retrace, bodies failed to reach the CE, which validated the inversion ([2026 #76 · 11:26](https://youtu.be/18vX-vv2bsg?t=686)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

"First utilization" has three meanings in the notes. Know which one a lecture is using.

- **Reflection when the first gap is down-close.** One lecture says the idea does not apply ([2026 #11 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=389)); another calls the up-close gap after a down-close first gap a reflection ([2026 #10 · 37:29](https://youtu.be/xnbtp_j81lI?t=2249)). ## Immediate rebalance

### What it is

An immediate rebalance is a candle that leaves a would-be gap behind it, followed at once by a candle that comes straight back to the prior candle's edge. The move then continues in its original direction. ICT treats it as one of the best on-side tells there is.

His clearest definition is the bearish version from August 26, 2024. A single candle prints a large range lower and closes lower, clearly bigger than the candles around it. The next candle opens and trades all the way back up to touch the prior candle's low. That low is the level that would have bounded a bearish FVG. The down candle's whole range is re-delivered with buy side, so the gap never forms ([2024 #17 · 32:30](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1950)).

[[DIAGRAM ch08_d5 | caption: The bearish immediate rebalance as ICT describes it (illustrative) | Candle 3 reaches candle 1's low, so no gap is left, and the move then continues lower.]]

Two rules bound it. The rebalance must come on the candle immediately after the big one; if that candle does not do it, the next one still counts, and after that it is not valid ([2024 #17 · 33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998)). And it works on all timeframes. He describes only the bearish case and says the mirror image matters not (the caption is unclear).

### The versions in the notes

The term keeps its core but changes shape from lecture to lecture. The September 12, 2024 wick version is in the vacuum block section.

| Version | What it looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Aug 6, 2024 | An extended-range candle goes up. Price drops back and just touches that candle's high rather than filling a gap, then runs to an objective | [2024 #2 · 2:27:41](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=8861) |
| Aug 19, 2024 | A big down-close candle, then a candle that opens and goes right back up to the previous candle. One of the best on-side indicators, provided liquidity has not already been taken | [2024 #11 · 3:24:05](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12245) |
| Aug 30, 2024 ("immediate rebound") | After an expansion candle the very next candle trades back to the gap's edge, the high of the candle before the expansion, then runs, in the same candle or the next | [2024 #21 · 12:45](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=765) |
| Feb 10, 2026 | A candle opens leaving a gap and the very next candle closes it in one pass. Either that candle or the next one then goes your way | [2026 #132 · 48:41](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2921) |
| Mar 13, 2026 | A discount wick forms; price opens, trades down to the wick's CE, bounces hard and delivers to the previous day's high or another key level | [2026 #119 · 2:13](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=133) |

### Why it works

ICT's explanation for the candle version is re-delivery. The big candle's range is traded back through with the opposite side's orders, so no inefficiency is left behind, and the market has shown its hand. He compares it to a poker player flashing a strong hand ([2024 #17 · 27:38](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1658)). It promotes speed and large range, and it is a continuation signal, not a reversal.

For the daily wick version in 2026 he says the algorithm rushes price to the previous day's high or key level instead of waiting for a full gap correction. He calls it the strongest algorithmic delivery array he has discovered, claims it as his own, and says he taught it in his 2016 to 2017 paid mentorship ([2026 #119 · 2:13](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=133), [4:41](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=281)). Those are his claims.

### How to spot it

1. A low forms.
2. One candle prints a large down-close range, bigger than the recent candles.
3. The next candle, or at most the one after, opens and trades back up to the low of the candle before the big one.
4. Check that liquidity has not already been taken ([2024 #11 · 3:24:05](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12245)).

### How to trade it

- **Anticipate it.** If you are short from higher, or want to short or add (pyramid), expect it when the large down candle closes. Watch where the next candle opens, expect a run back to the prior low, then speed lower. He says it "might be your model" by itself ([2024 #17 · 33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998)).
- **Add on the retest.** If you are short, add on a retest of that candle's low, look for speed through it, and treat it as the first partial. The captions are ambiguous on direction ([2024 #11 · 3:24:05](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=12245)). On August 7, 2024 he used it after a big displacement candle as the area to add ([2024 #3 · 1:49:36](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=6576)).
- **Demand speed after a second dip.** If price returns again and closes a body into the area, it must show quick strength, otherwise he calls it problematic ([2024 #21 · 12:45](https://youtu.be/eYYyonGwTRs?t=765)).
- **Use it as a micro entry when nothing else is clean.** After a 9:30 consolidation, a candle closed down, the next opened and closed the gap at once, and price ran lower into sell-side delivery, ending at the RTH opening range gap high. He suggests finding runs that are not complete, spotting small gaps, and waiting for price to trade up into them ([2026 #132 · 48:41](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2921), [49:32](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2972)).
- **Watch day two for the wick version.** If the bounce fails the next day and price trades below the immediate rebalance level, he calls it a complex reversal: the market will not protect the upside, and it is a high-conviction sell signal ([2026 #119 · 13:30](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=810)).

### Worked example

NQ, August 6, 2024. After the morning selloff there was a disruption at the lows and relative equal highs above. ICT expected a poke above the day's high near 7:00. Price did an immediate rebalance, touching the candle, rallied, traded back down into a down-close candle (an order block), closed the inefficiency once more, then expanded up through the 7:00 high. That was a run of more than 40 handles, and after touching the high of the day he was done with no afternoon trading ([2024 #2 · 2:34:55](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9295), [2:35:43](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=9343)).

On the dollar index, March 12 to 13, 2026, a wick formed, price opened, traded down to the wick's CE, bounced and delivered to 104.54, which he called "to the tick". EUR and GBP pairs then dropped as the dollar firmed ([2026 #119 · 7:07](https://youtu.be/l7hRTBvK7to?t=427)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Reading it as a reversal.** Retail chat applies candlestick logic and gets the signal backwards. In a bearish context it is a continuation tell ([2024 #17 · 33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998)).
- **Waiting too long.** After the second candle it is not valid.
- **Treating it as a gap fill.** The point is that the rebalance happens instead of a gap. ICT also says the superior gap is the one that does not fill in ([2024 #17 · 28:25](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1705)).
- **A tension with "no immediate rebalance".** In a 2026 fallback entry in the lower half of a bearish order block, one of the signs he lists is no immediate rebalance, meaning price does not immediately retrace into the gap after displacement ([2026 #70 · 5:54](https://youtu.be/EZB4vBUFdHw?t=354)). That sounds like the opposite emphasis. One reading, my own and not his, is that both are about speed: a candle that merely touches the edge and keeps going, and a displacement that never comes back, both show strength. The notes do not reconcile the two.

### How the teaching changed

- **Window.** The candle count is stated slightly inconsistently within one lecture: valid on the next candle but not after, then the candle after the first is allowed ([2024 #17 · 31:40](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1900), [33:18](https://youtu.be/iLe_4m84_yw?t=1998)). ## Breakaway gaps

### What it is

A breakaway gap is a gap left behind at the start of a move, as price leaves a consolidation or a higher-timeframe PD array, that then stays open. ICT reads an unfilled one as a sign that a lot more move is coming ([2024 #38 · 1:21:45](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4905)). The label is applied to the same pattern in several settings.

- **After a stop hunt out of a consolidation.** Price leaves a multi-bar range, the stops beyond one side are taken, and it breaks out the other side, leaving an FVG. Such gaps tend to stay open, not always but most of the time ([2024 #7 · 2:08:10](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7690)).
- **At the start of a distribution or reaccumulation stage.** After a shift in structure, the run through the first low leaves a gap that stays open. It signals that price will go much lower and fast, so he says not to rush out. Unfilled is "a cherry on top", a hallmark and not a requirement, and a closed gap can still see the drop ([2024 #19 · 43:58](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2638)).
- **Out of a higher-timeframe array.** When price leaves a daily box, he wants an imbalance created on the way out and left open ([2024 #38 · 1:21:45](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=4905)).
- **Partly open after trading through a gap.** In 2026: price trades through an FVG, exits, and leaves the upper or lower portion unfilled. If price comes back and fills it, he reads that as a reversal signal ([2026 #100 · 9:10](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=550)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation is algorithmic delivery. The gap formed as price left the consolidation, so it acts as support, or resistance in the mirror case, even if price does not return for a long time. He says it is not support and resistance, because price never had to retest a wick, and not supply and demand, which he calls a myth outside commodities ([2024 #7 · 2:04:55](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7495), [2:50:54](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=10254)). In 2026 he calls a bullish gap left partly open one of the strongest signatures that price still wants to go higher ([2026 #38 · 9:43](https://youtu.be/O217xQsEUgI?t=583)). He says it is more meaningful intraday; on daily and weekly charts gaps are sometimes filled weeks later ([2026 #125 · 41:13](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2473)).

### How to spot it

1. A multi-bar consolidation.
2. A sweep of one side (for a long idea, the low).
3. Displacement out of the other side that leaves an FVG on the way.
4. A higher-timeframe target ahead ([2024 #7 · 2:08:10](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7690)).

Other settings where he uses the label:

- A gap above the CE of the opening range gap after a two-candle consolidation between two PD arrays, for a short. You want price not to trade back up into it ([2026 #41 · 16:20](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=980)).
- A gap that forms exactly at the CE of the RTH opening range gap in the direction of the draw, with price never trading back into it ([2026 #47 · 12:01](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=721)).
- After a bearish gap holds its upper quadrant, price leaves lower and makes another FVG. That new gap marks the start of the run, with the CE of the wick and then the half-gap as the next steps ([2024 #42 · 1:50:33](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=6633)).
- A BISI after the 9:00 close of a time-based range, at 9:01, with a volume imbalance; he wants it to rip higher and take the short-term high ([2026 #42 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/DRTvbkKmuAw?t=1245)).
- Stacked gaps: a third FVG further up shows heaviness and price does not need to go to it ([2024 #40 · 1:37:53](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=5873)).

### How to trade it

- **Use it to hold.** An open gap behind the move is a reason not to be in a hurry to exit ([2024 #19 · 43:58](https://youtu.be/i3olkndPmEE?t=2638)).
- **It sets up the entry.** ICT says it is the setup for the IOFED entry (Chapter 18) ([2024 #7 · 2:25:58](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=8758)).
- **Order block entry behind a breakaway BISI.** On August 8, 2024 a displacement above the NDOG left a BISI he did not want price to revisit. The entry was in the discount of the down-close candle, below its body, anywhere in the wick. Stop below the NDOG's CE; once price takes the short-term high, move it to break-even or at least to the top of the NDOG ([2024 #5 · 53:07](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3187), [55:33](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3333)).
- **Say which you expect.** Other times he wants a gap to close so it can invert. Decide before the trade whether the gap should stay open or fail ([2024 #7 · 2:04:55](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7495)).
- **Allow the second gap.** With two close FVGs, either put the stop beyond the second or wait for price to trade into it ([2024 #40 · 57:26](https://youtu.be/YxpHUwZhu80?t=3446)).
- **If it fills.** In 2026 a fill is a reversal signal ([2026 #100 · 9:10](https://youtu.be/3J8drYX2zHM?t=550)) and a gap filled in a later period is a closing gap, a weak breakout ([2026 #125 · 41:13](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2473)).

### Worked example

The break left an FVG whose candle 3 low, the gap's upper edge, was 18931.00 as captioned. At 10:52 price dropped back to the upper edge without filling the lower part, then rallied to the daily volume imbalance ([2024 #7 · 2:08:10](https://youtu.be/-WItxLARGpE?t=7690)).

On August 8, 2024 the order block entry behind the breakaway BISI was a down-close candle that closed at 18,560, bought at 18,559.75, with an exit at 18,607.5 by limit order ([2024 #5 · 53:57](https://youtu.be/ul6x6BwOg6M?t=3237)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Naming any gap breakaway.** The label needs context: a consolidation or array being left, a sweep, displacement. He says the definition needs enough context to set the stage ([2024 #42 · 1:50:33](https://youtu.be/qcYYm1iNCIM?t=6633)).
- **Expecting it to hold every time.** "Tend to stay open" is not "always".
- **Calling it support and resistance or supply and demand.** He rejects both.
- **Treating the term as fixed.** In 2026 he says it is his label for the premium-half case, and the notes call it a loose term in a 2024 lecture ([2026 #41 · 16:20](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=980), [2024 #32 · 50:01](https://youtu.be/Vo8WKYKtzIU?t=3001)).

## Measuring, exhaustion and closing gaps

### What it is

Where a gap sits in a move, and what price does to it, gives it a name.

**Measuring gap.** A gap in the middle of a move. The second leg should equal the first. Draw a Fibonacci from the start of the move; the gap's 50% area marks the halfway point, so the second leg mirrors the first ([2024 #38 · 1:23:21](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5001)).

[[DIAGRAM ch08_d4 | caption: One bullish run with a breakaway gap at the start and a measuring gap at the 50% point (illustrative) | The early gap stays open as a sign of more to come; the mid-move gap projects a second leg equal to the first.]]

In 2026 he applies it to an implied dealing range. A gap that forms exactly at the 50% of the range and never closes is a measuring gap. He says he did not invent the term but is the one telling you where it will form before it does ([2026 #49 · 41:46](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2506)). The implication is acceleration: price drops lower and speeds up, or consolidates and drops harder, without wild retracements ([2026 #49 · 42:33](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2553), [44:56](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2696)).

He also uses the label for a plain Fibonacci level. Measure the pre-market high to low; the 0.5 level is the halfway point, and once price is beyond it, the continuation to the opposite extreme is likely. The July 31, 2026 example had a pre-market 0.5 at 28,723. He says it is not a stop, since price may exceed it ([2026 #43 · 8:46](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=526), [9:35](https://youtu.be/uws9egBxPLI?t=575)). The notes record both that he expected price to reach it and that he shorted there, and do not reconcile the two, so treat the example loosely.

**Exhaustion gap.** If price trades up into a gap and moves away aggressively without leaving part of it open, the gap is exhausting: it has done its job and loses relevance as resistance. A gap counts as closed when bodies fill it, not just wicks; a tiny Mohawk outside the range is fine as long as the bodies stop inside ([2024 #9 · 54:08](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3248)). For the key gap he wants it traded into only partially. An open upper half means "really heavy", and he would have strong conviction for a return to the 9:30 opening price ([2024 #9 · 44:42](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=2682)). The notes flag that the audio is ambiguous about which of two gaps he calls the exhaustion at each moment.

**Closing gap.** A gap that a later period fills. In his three-way split, a breakaway gap stays open at the start, a measuring gap forms mid-trend, and a closing gap is the one that got filled, which marks a weak breakout ([2026 #125 · 41:13](https://youtu.be/JIs96_2rfkE?t=2473)).

### How to use it

- **Measuring gap.** If one forms at the 50% and is not traded back into, hold the move, do not expect deep retracements, and take partials at the next graded levels. In his Thursday NQ example the gap at the 50% line never filled, price dropped aggressively, and the next candle laid on the lower octant, the .375 level ([2026 #49 · 44:56](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2696)). Octants are in Chapter 6.
- **Exhaustion gap.** Once it is fully closed by bodies, it no longer supports a short from there. Look at the next higher inefficiency or stand aside. On August 15, 2024 at about 10:18 on NQ, an entire gap closed with bodies stopping and one small Mohawk outside. He called it an exhaustion and wanted to see price trade below the gap before taking interest ([2024 #9 · 54:08](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=3248)).
- **Opening range gap.** He applies the same word to a gap-up that sells off, rallies and then folds, which he says could be viewed as an exhaustion gap, after which the gap low can be treated like an FVG ([2024 #36 · 22:26](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=1346)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Mixing measuring and breakaway.** Location decides: start of the move, breakaway; middle, measuring. He himself called the gap at the CE of the opening range gap breakaway in one lecture and measuring in another ([2026 #47 · 12:01](https://youtu.be/V5crdCw0AsY?t=721), [2026 #49 · 42:33](https://youtu.be/dYQSUCSbYn8?t=2553)).
- **Using the 0.5 as a stop.** It is a projection point, not a stop.
- **Judging exhaustion by wicks.** He judges it by bodies.

## Vacuum blocks and RTH liquidity voids

The notes contain no stand-alone "vacuum gap". They hold two related ideas.

**The vacuum block.** In a September 12, 2024 lecture ICT defines it as a separation between one candle's close and the next candle's open, with no price between the two except the wick. The wick trades back down to the prior close. That is the immediate rebalance, because the wick is the gap ([2024 #27 · 1:08:23](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=4103)). The notes say this was the first time he named vacuum block and immediate rebalance together with order block theory, and that the candle count is not stated.

His NQ example ran about 10:35 to 10:45 ET. Price hit the lower quadrant of a 1-hour BISI and spiked slightly past it, then rallied. A candle opened with a gap above the prior close and wicked back down to that close. Price then delivered to the high of the down-closed series (the order block) and on to the opening range gap high ([2024 #27 · 1:05:55](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=3955), [1:09:11](https://youtu.be/DRuEMX4RhMw?t=4151)). He counted three confluences at the wick: the immediate rebalance, the discount wick's CE, and the nearness of the BISI's CE. The block family is Chapter 9.

**The RTH liquidity void.** On an RTH-only chart, a stretch between one candle's low and another's high with no prints in regular trading hours is a liquidity void, even if electronic hours traded through it. RTH has to balance ETH to be efficiently delivered, and RTH carries more weight. Overnight ETH has traded since Sunday 6:00 PM ET, so RTH tends to overlap and redeliver levels ETH has already seen ([2026 #64 · 35:25](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2125), [36:13](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2173)). Price could drop and never visit, but "more times than not" it follows this logic ([2026 #64 · 37:00](https://youtu.be/rtIcbG6twrI?t=2220)). In his Monday NQ example the void was the opening range gap, from Friday's settlement near 30,048.5 to the open near 29,714.5 (implied), and price headed up into it later.

How to use it: switch to the RTH chart, find ranges with no RTH prints, compare with ETH, and treat the RTH voids as draws, weighting them over ETH-only inefficiencies. This is a futures feature; a CFD chart shows a single continuous feed, and the notes do not say how to map it to gold.

## The Reaper FVG and the first presented for everybody gap

ICT also anchors a first gap to the calendar.

**The Reaper FVG.** It is the first FVG that forms after 9:30 ET on a Monday, the first day of the week, or on the first trading day of the month. Draw it through the whole week or month. The algorithm goes back to specific dates as frames of reference; he lists the first trading day of the month, the first day of the week, Wednesday and the previous Friday, though not all of them at once. He says "Reaper" is a scythe that cuts through the whole month and makes it tradable. Use it when it also matches the time of day the market should deliver ([2024 #11 · 3:50:03](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=13803), [3:51:38](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=13898)).

In a new month, go back to the first trading day, take the first pertinent PD array and carry it through the chart. In his August example, the Reaper FVG he had shown from August 1 was referenced repeatedly through the month. The notes flag that "August 1st first day of the week" is unclear, since August 1, 2024 was a Thursday. He also says every FVG is unique: if it shares space with another PD array it changes its character, so do not rename or re-teach it ([2024 #11 · 3:48:26](https://youtu.be/aONkyy-HTqE?t=13706)). My reading, not his: this is an early form of the Monday and Friday tracking he stresses in 2026 (see the carrying-forward section); the notes do not connect them.

**The first presented for everybody gap (FPEG).** On January 6, 2026 he calls an orange box "first presented for everybody gap from Friday". It is the gap from the previous Friday's close into Monday's open, a reference for whether price trades in premium or discount to last week ([2026 #139 · 3:03](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=183)). That description matches the new week opening gap in Chapter 11, and the name appears only once in the notes, so treat the label with caution. He calls it a structural reference, not a standalone trade signal ([2026 #139 · 3:50](https://youtu.be/Jxl5UJk_ylc?t=230)).

## Answers

1. No. The 9:30 candle can only be candle 1; the earliest valid middle candle is 9:31, confirmed when the 9:32 candle closes. Reread "What it is".
2. It is probably not the one: no displacement, and under the roughly 10 NQ handle guide. Wait for a gap that displaces and has some size. Reread "Which gap counts".
3. It was built around the 9:30 candle and does not count; a genuine first presentation exists in both views. Reread "What it is".
4. On a rise into the high or CE of yesterday's gap, after price has dropped through today's. His stated rationale is that large funds and algorithms cannot short on a down-tick and must wait for an uptick; that is his claim. Reread "Carrying it forward".
5. Examples: above candle 2's high (September 2024 recipe), above the whole gap plus its height (high-resistance day), beyond candle 1's extreme (2026). The wider ones suit high-resistance days. Reread "How to trade it".
6. It is an immediate rebalance, a continuation tell for lower prices, not a reversal. The rebalance must come on the next candle or the one after. Reread "Immediate rebalance".
7. A breakaway gap; expect continuation. In 2024 a fill does not rule out the move; in 2026 a fill is a reversal signal. Reread "Breakaway gaps".
8. A measuring gap; expect acceleration with little retracement and partials at the next graded levels. Reread "Measuring, exhaustion and closing gaps".
9. Look for the first down-close gap (SIBI) that forms after it. If the first gap was down-close, one 2026 lecture says no reflection applies, while another uses the word differently. Reread "The reflection FVG".
10. In the notes the life is 3 days in 2024, two weeks for Monday and Friday gaps in 2026, a week for Monday's gap, and about 20 days after a turtle soup. Keep your own records. Reread "Carrying it forward".
