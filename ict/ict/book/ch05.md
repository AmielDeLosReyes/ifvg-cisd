A fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle pattern in which the middle candle runs so far in one direction that the candles on either side never trade the same stretch of price. ICT calls the bullish version a BISI and the bearish version a SIBI, and treats it as the basic PD array (premium/discount array: a price he expects the market to be drawn to or to react from). After this chapter you should be able to draw a gap from the candles, judge whether it is worth trading, pick an entry and a stop from named candles, handle stacked gaps and gaps on several timeframes, and know when to leave one alone. Grading a gap with its midpoint and quadrants is Chapter 6, inversions are Chapter 7, and first presented FVGs are Chapter 8.

## At a glance

[[DIAGRAM ch05_d1 | caption: Bullish (BISI) and bearish (SIBI) fair value gaps drawn candle by candle (illustrative) | Both gaps sit in candle 2: the edges are candle 1's extreme and candle 3's extreme, and the wicks of candles 1 and 3 never touch the gap.]]

- A gap needs three candles. The gap belongs to candle 2 and is the space candles 1 and 3 never traded. Bullish: candle 1's high up to candle 3's low. Bearish: candle 1's low down to candle 3's high.
- It only matters if displacement made it, meaning an energetic move through a swing high or low (Chapter 2). A break of structure that leaves no gap is not a setup: no gap, no trade.
- Price is expected to return into the gap, not necessarily through it. Bodies should respect it; wicks may poke a little. He prefers a partial return that then reacts.
- Entry is a specific price at the near edge (candle 3's edge, give or take one tick), with optional adds at the midpoint. The stop sits beyond candle 2 (tight) or candle 1 (standard), and wider on high-resistance days.
- With two gaps stacked, enter the nearer one and size the stop for a stab into the farther one. With gaps on several timeframes, the higher timeframe gap is the parent.
- A gap only marks where a pattern might form. Liquidity, time of day and the premium/discount side decide whether it is a trade.

## The three-candle gap: anatomy and naming

### What it is

Take three candles in a row and call them candle 1, candle 2 and candle 3. Candle 2 is the energetic one. The gap is the stretch of price that candle 2 traded and neither neighbour touched. In 2024 ICT put it plainly: a fair value gap is always three candles, the gap belongs to candle 2, and it shares no price with candles 1 and 3 ([2024 #49 · 1:40:50](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6050)).

The bullish gap, in the 2022 mentorship: the bottom is candle 1's high and the top is candle 3's low ([2022 Ep 6 · 22:18](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1338)). He expects it after a run below an old low or lows (sell-side liquidity), followed by a break of a short-term high with energetic displacement.

The bearish gap: candle 2 trades below candle 1's low, and candle 3 makes a lower low than candle 2 but its high does not climb back to candle 1's low ([2022 Ep 6 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=672)). The top of the gap is candle 1's low and the bottom is candle 3's high ([2022 Ep 6 · 16:46](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1006)). He likes it best after a run above an old high, because the gap then gives smart money a second chance to sell if it missed the top ([2022 Ep 6 · 15:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=912)).

| | Bullish gap (BISI) | Bearish gap (SIBI) |
|---|---|---|
| Candle 2 | closes up | closes down |
| Gap bottom | candle 1's high | candle 3's high |
| Gap top | candle 3's low | candle 1's low |
| Price comes back from | above, into the top edge | below, into the bottom edge |
| Near edge (met first on the return) | candle 3's low | candle 3's high |
| Far edge | candle 1's high | candle 1's low |
| Long name | buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency | sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency |
| You use it to | buy dips | sell rallies |

"Near edge" and "far edge" are this book's terms, not ICT's. The near edge is where price arrives first on the return, so it is where the easiest entry sits.

The inefficiency is always candle 2, and the label follows its direction. A down-close candle 2 makes a SIBI, a sell-side imbalance with a buy-side inefficiency ([2026 #20 · 4:44](https://youtu.be/iSWZOe3aUFE?t=284)). An up-close candle 2 makes a BISI ([2024 #24 · 1:26:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5209)). ICT says he introduced the phrase "fair value gap" in 2016, having called it an imbalance before ([2022 Ep 2 · 30:04](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1804)), and in Episode 6 he insists it not be renamed or repackaged.

Two drawing rules. Shade only the real gap, from one candle's extreme to the other's, and extend the box forward in time ([2024 #24 · 2:30:41](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9041); [2022 Ep 10 · 41:45](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2505)). And the shading is only a visual: he trades specific prices on the gap, not a zone ([2024 #28 · 28:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1727)).

The classic gap is measured with wicks. The volume imbalance, the space between two candle bodies, is a second, smaller measurement of the same inefficiency (Chapter 6). In 2026 he sketched the history: first wicks only, then the volume imbalance added, using the close instead of the wick high ([2026 #72 · 12:15](https://youtu.be/rbNhlJSXDaA?t=735)).

### Why it works

ICT's explanation starts with efficient delivery. When one candle moves one way and its neighbours' wicks do not overlap it, price was offered to one side of the market only, and the algorithm must later deliver the same prices in the other direction to book them ([2022 Ep 2 · 17:56](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1076)). In 2024 he made it a rule: for every range that goes up, efficient delivery means price must be offered back down through it ([2024 #24 · 1:26:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5209)).

His picture is a paint roller. The chart is a wall and each candle is a pass of the roller. A long candle leaves porous patches, and there is a strong but not universal tendency for price to roll back over the area until it is evenly covered ([2022 Ep 6 · 12:47](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=767); [2022 Ep 10 · 27:22](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=1642)). Once a range is fully balanced there is no reason to linger ([2022 Ep 10 · 12:06](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=726)). The 2024 NFP lecture scales the roller with the chart: the daily is the roller, the 1m a fine brush.

Time is part of it. A one-day drop leaves too little time for buyers to take part, so the algorithm goes back up to offer those prices again, whether or not those buyers prove right. He calls this likely, not certain, and not necessarily immediate ([2022 Ep 12 · 1:07:43](https://youtu.be/8GkQfdAXZP0?t=4063)). It fits his summary of the algorithm: it reaches for liquidity and/or imbalances, on time and then price ([2022 Ep 16 · 30:28](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1828)). In 2024 he adds that an old gap still matters because the algorithm refers back to the date, day, week, month, quarter, price and time of old trading ([2024 #3 · 49:17](https://youtu.be/ZkLc4-adodg?t=2957)).

"Fair" is meant literally. A gap is a fair value to buy, if you are bullish, when price comes back into part of it. He does not teach that every liquidity void must fill completely ([2022 Ep 38 · 4:43](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=283); [2022 Ep 38 · 5:31](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=331)).

### How to spot it

1. Start from an energetic candle, one that runs through a swing high or low (Chapter 2). Gaps made without displacement do not interest him.
2. Label the candle before it 1, the energetic candle 2, and the candle after it 3.
3. Compare wicks. Bullish: is candle 1's high below candle 3's low? Bearish: is candle 1's low above candle 3's high? If the wicks overlap, there is no gap.
4. Wait for candle 3 to finish. In 2022 he waited for the candle to close to "complete the imbalance" and then marked the low end of the gap ([2022 Ep 15 · 1:45](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=105)). Price may still react to the area before that candle closes ([2024 #9 · 9:37](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=577)).
5. Shade only the space between the two edges and extend it to the right.
6. Note the context before you care about the gap: which liquidity was just run, whether the gap is in premium or discount, and what time it formed (next section).

### Worked example

NQ 2-minute, a Friday in the 2022 mentorship. A big displacement candle breaks a short-term low and leaves a bearish gap. The next candle opens, trades up and stops in the gap: the short ([2022 Ep 2 · 29:18](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1758)). He sold near 14,800 (he abbreviates it "798") and covered near 14,675, about 120 to 130 handles, at the top of a target imbalance ([2022 Ep 2 · 32:26](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1946)). That target was a bullish gap in discount, just below the 50% of the day's range; price traded down into it and closed it ([2022 Ep 2 · 38:04](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2284)). One gap was the entry and the other the objective.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- **Every small separation is not a tradeable gap.** In the NFP lecture the small intraday inefficiencies inside a larger bearish structure are not tradeable, and you need other PD-array context. An FVG has no single-purpose explanation ([2024 #24 · 2:30:41](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=9041)).
- **Common gap versus displacement gap.** The old open-outcry "common gap", which mostly never retraced, does not interest him. A gap made with displacement does ([2026 #138 · 52:05](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3125)).
- **Support and resistance.** After a run below an old low he shorts the retrace into the gap, not a retest of the broken low. With no gap, he does not trust the level as old support turned resistance ([2022 Ep 33 · 9:00](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=540)).
- **Order blocks.** Not every down-close candle after a run-up is a bullish order block; the real context is the imbalance ([2022 Ep 11 · 8:03](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=483)). Order blocks are Chapter 9.
- **A "single imbalance" SIBI.** One 2026 note glosses SIBI as a single small imbalance on the 15m ([2026 #66 · 5:40](https://youtu.be/zq4hz_XiTRE?t=340)). It does not match the three-candle definition anywhere else, so this chapter does not use it.

### How the teaching changed

The geometry did not change between 2022 and 2026; the judging did. In 2022 the gap was the required piece of a structure shift and the place to enter. In 2024 he added wick tolerances, a preference for gaps that do not fully close, and naming by candle 2. In 2026 he added the volume imbalance, the midpoint close test, macro-time delivery and the demand that a gap be anchored to a key level.

## What makes a gap valid: displacement, context and time

### What it is

A gap is a place. A trade needs a reason to be at that place. ICT's filters, roughly in the order he applies them, are displacement, the liquidity just run, which half of the range the gap sits in, a key level to anchor it to, and the clock.

### Displacement is the gate

After an old high is run, ICT waits for a break below a short-term swing low. If that break leaves no gap, it does not meet the criteria and there is no trade: "It's there or it's not," and waiting forces the market to show its hand ([2022 Ep 4 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/L-ReMHiavPM?t=394)). In the final 2022 episode, price fell after a stop-out but did not take the short-term low with displacement, so the gap that formed was one he would not have taken ([2022 Ep 41 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=719)).

Which low counts? The one the displacement itself breaks, not a nearer low, because the gap requires price to move energetically away from it. If the candle after the gap opens right at the low, there is no displacement yet. He places the limit only after price has moved away ([2022 Ep 20 · 12:39](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=759); [2022 Ep 20 · 13:27](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=807)).

In 2026 the test is stated for any gap. If the candles that form it are part of a run that also breaks the prior swing high or low, it has displacement, and such gaps are far more significant later ([2026 #11 · 1:39](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=99)). He adds the clock: a run through a high or low at a specific time, such as 7:00 a.m. ([2026 #138 · 52:05](https://youtu.be/dt9mk4n5g_o?t=3125)). Displacement itself is Chapter 2.

### Context: liquidity, side of the range and a key level

**Liquidity first.** The bearish template is a run above an old high or highs, then a break below a short-term low that leaves the gap ([2022 Ep 6 · 15:12](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=912)). At the 9:30 open on the 15m he asks four questions: did it take a high, did it break a low, was it displacement, did it trade back into the imbalance ([2022 Ep 22 · 9:38](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=578)). The bullish form: price trades below an old low, displaces up through a swing high, and you look back through the up-leg for the gap ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925)).

**Side of the range.** A short wants its gap in premium and a long wants it in discount (Chapter 4). In an ES step-down a 4m gap sat in discount and was invalid for the short ([2022 Ep 25 · 35:43](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2143)).

**A key level.** In 2026 he picked out one SIBI near the 0.875 level of a graded range and ignored the other gaps: they were not salient, and a gap must be anchored to a key level ([2026 #27 · 5:40](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=340); [2026 #27 · 6:27](https://youtu.be/4KW2acdPi-M?t=387)). Grading is Chapter 6.

### Time: the gap is the where, not the when

Of a gap he had flagged in advance, ICT said it is just price by itself, and the first element is time ([2022 Ep 24 · 21:27](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1287)). His plan: highs overhead as the draw, price dropping into discount before 9:30, and a small gap below. He expected a drop into that gap at 9:30, then displacement and a new gap to buy, running to 4303. It never formed ([2022 Ep 24 · 5:38](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=338); [2022 Ep 24 · 19:53](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=1193)). The gap said where to look, the clock said when, and price did not do the required thing.

In 2026 a gap that forms at a macro time (his examples: 1:00 a.m. and 10:50 to 11:10 a.m.) gains weight. It is a filter, not a signal ([2026 #11 · 4:52](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=292); [2026 #11 · 7:18](https://youtu.be/9W4YpyAg5RY?t=438)). Macro times are Chapter 12.

### Choosing the gap when delivery is one-sided

"Which FVG do I pick?" has a mechanical answer in 2024. Once delivery turns bearish, key off every SIBI and every short-term high, even single ones, and extend each SIBI to the right. Ignore BISIs unless price does something violent and starts to reverse, when a BISI becomes a potential inversion FVG (Chapter 7) ([2024 #2 · 1:25:42](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5142); [2024 #2 · 1:31:18](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5478)). When bullish, do the mirror. Expect manipulation: a return into an old SIBI may first take the stops of shorts above the energetic drop, then break down off the gap's midpoint ([2024 #2 · 1:39:26](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5966)).

### Worked example

An index chart quoted around 3915 in the 2022 mentorship (the notes do not name the instrument). After 3915.25 broke, a gap formed that was visible on the 5m and not on the 15m, and that gap was the short. After 3872.25 broke, another gap formed and gave another short. Price did not stop at 3872.25 on the way back, because it first went up into the imbalance above it, and then delivered lower to and through 3855 ([2022 Ep 33 · 9:00](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=540); [2022 Ep 33 · 9:48](https://youtu.be/8z6My18WZqA?t=588)). The gap, not the broken level, set where the retrace ended.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the gap was the required piece of a structure shift and the sweep before it. In 2024 the question became which gap to key off, and the answer was the same-side ones. In 2026 it is salience: displacement, a key level and a good clock time. The gap alone was never enough.

## How price should treat the gap: bodies, halves and time

### What it is

Once a gap exists, ICT watches how price behaves in it: bodies respect it, the far half stays quiet, and price leaves quickly. Treat these as quality filters, not entry rules. He says the half-gap behaviour does not make the trade more or less profitable; it gives emotional comfort ([2024 #8 · 2:51:48](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10308)).

### Partial return is the point

The gap only has to be touched. In 2022 a daily gap needed price to trade to its edge. You can be greedy and wait for the midpoint or a deep fill, but he does not need it ([2022 Ep 18 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=527)). Partly, halfway or completely closed are all acceptable, and the return typically lands near 50% of the parent swing ([2022 Ep 19 · 6:29](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=389); [2022 Ep 19 · 13:38](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=818)).

By 2024 the preference was sharper. You do not want a bearish gap to close fully; you want price to go in a little and reverse. Trading just because a gap has closed completely is, in his words, the lowest form of trading with imbalances ([2024 #24 · 2:12:08](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=7928)). A full fill is permissible and does not change direction. Better, and a sign of real weakness, is a fill to the midpoint or less that leaves the upper half open. A bullish gap mirrors this with the lower half ([2024 #8 · 2:51:48](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10308)).

### Bodies decide, wicks may poke

Bodies carry the respect. On the DXY hourly he showed bodies respecting a gap while wicks went slightly above it ([2022 Ep 19 · 10:27](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=627); [2022 Ep 19 · 15:14](https://youtu.be/IEa1N0rTtbc?t=914)). In 2024 bodies stopped exactly at the levels while wicks bumped the bottom of a SIBI: wicks do some damage, but not to random places ([2024 #2 · 1:39:26](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=5966)).

The NFP lecture gave a number. Wicks may colour outside a gap by about one "standard deviation" of that gap, meaning roughly its own width and generally less. Bodies should not close beyond it. Small stray pokes are "mohawks", and price will not go five times the width ([2024 #24 · 2:47:31](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=10051)). The notes record that he said the rule was supposed to be in the book.

### The halves and the midpoint

Every gap has a midpoint, the consequent encroachment or CE (Chapter 6). A bearish gap should not take bodies in its upper half, and a bullish gap should not take them in its lower half (Chapter 4).

In 2026, on a SIBI in NQ, bodies never closed at or above the CE and the upper half was not tested: bearish order flow. When price entered the lower half and looked heavy, he expected sells within a few candles. The upper half called for caution, and a body close above the CE made a return toward the 9:30 open reasonable ([2026 #12 · 8:53](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=533); [2026 #12 · 9:40](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=580); [2026 #12 · 15:30](https://youtu.be/VBgfJRMVCaI?t=930)). In an April 2026 lecture the midpoint was a reference for whether the structure is holding: bodies that cannot reach it confirm the bias, and bodies that touch it but stay in the lower half mean the move lower likely continues. It is a reference, not a hard stop or target ([2026 #91 · 8:46](https://youtu.be/2OVAvl4H3IY?t=526)). While short, you also want gaps above your target to stay open ([2024 #8 · 1:52:31](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=6751)).

### Speed, distance and time inside the gap

Lecture 8 says when large-range candles should appear. If a gap is left partly open, the market must accelerate away. If it fills entirely, price must immediately show speed and distance. Without either, do not stay ([2024 #8 · 3:06:20](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11180); [2024 #8 · 3:02:13](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10933)).

The 2026 version adds a clock. A strong gap is entered and left within one or two candles. More than two candles with bodies inside is weak. If you are short and price lingers three or more, consider taking something off and do not assume continuation ([2026 #114 · 31:08](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1868); [2026 #114 · 1:38:46](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=5926)). Repeated use of the same small area is read the same way ([2026 #114 · 16:48](https://youtu.be/z6AxPV2NQ9U?t=1008)). In 2024 he passed on a short because price had spent too long inside the gap ([2024 #29 · 1:38:31](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5911)).

Used gaps lose value: a SIBI already overlapped and respected twice is low probability for a new entry ([2024 #2 · 1:46:45](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6405)). Inside a trade he reads it the other way. Once a gap had held twice he raised his stop, because a return into it meant he was wrong ([2022 Ep 38 · 25:13](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1513)).

A wick alone does not invalidate a gap. In Episode 15 the 9:30 open swept sell stops just under a gap (13,612 on NQ), price re-entered it and he went long, though buying blind into the open is high risk ([2022 Ep 15 · 3:23](https://youtu.be/tGxuitjtO88?t=203)). In a stacked long he closed only if price overlapped the whole lower gap and kept going ([2022 Ep 3 · 45:36](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2736)).

### Worked example

Daily NQ, a 2026 lecture. A SIBI had its low at 30,157.75 (a candle's high) and its high at 30,337.50. Friday closed at 30,154.00, below the gap's low. ICT called that an early warning: if the market were truly bullish, why could it close below the inefficiency ([2026 #29 · 6:32](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=392))? Monday opened without a gap higher, below the midpoint of the gap and its wick. Price rallied to the SIBI high and went 5.5 handles above it (a high of 30,343.00), which he called permissible on a daily chart. It could not continue, so he probed for a run back to the new week opening gap ([2026 #29 · 7:24](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=444); [2026 #29 · 9:02](https://youtu.be/EC0UuarROyo?t=542)). The Monday figures are as captioned in the notes, with the leading "30" inferred.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- Waiting for a full closure before acting.
- Calling a gap broken because a wick pierced its far side.
- Treating the half-gap rule as a requirement rather than a conviction filter.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the gap was a place to act on first touch, with partial fills accepted. In 2024 he added the one-width wick tolerance, the upper-half-open preference and the speed-and-distance test. In 2026 came the CE close test and the one-or-two-candle limit.

One tension is unresolved. In 2022 Episode 3 bodies staying inside a gap were a possible sign of accumulation, and in Episode 14 price consolidating at a gap above was reaccumulation, like a bull flag, not a reason to exit ([2022 Ep 3 · 45:36](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2736); [2022 Ep 14 · 1:34](https://youtu.be/NUdu1n-ML98?t=94)). In 2026 many candles inside a gap are weakness. The notes do not show him reconciling the two.

## Entering at the gap

### What it is

A gap entry is a limit order at a specific price on the gap, placed after displacement has carried price away from it. ICT wants one entry, one stop and one target, not a zone. He asks which price inside an 8 to 20 handle zone you would use, and a zone cannot answer ([2024 #28 · 28:47](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1727)).

### Where to put the limit

All of these are for the retrace into a gap that already exists.

| Entry | Bearish gap (SIBI) | Bullish gap (BISI) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

The nearest-edge entry is his "lowest threshold" or "lowest probable resistance": the edge he is confident price will reach, so there is no fear of a limit that never fills ([2024 #6 · 51:56](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=3116)). It carries the most pips of risk, but money risk is set by position size ([2022 Ep 18 · 35:54](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2154)). Act as soon as price trades into the near edge, and do not wait for a deeper fill that may never come ([2022 Ep 10 · 41:45](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2505)). Any price inside the gap is allowed, but the greedier the price, the less likely the fill.

The offset is a judgment call. In 2022 and Lecture 8 it was one tick inside the gap. In Lecture 28 he often places the order a little ahead of the level so that he is filled ([2024 #28 · 20:32](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=1232)). The NQ tick is 0.25: his 2022 limit at 14,528.25 against a low of 14,528.5 is one tick.

Learners should start with the easy objective entry on one contract, and may practise with market orders first to learn timing. The more professional route is limit orders, with limit exits too; market orders are really for getting out ([2024 #28 · 35:16](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2116); [2024 #28 · 36:05](https://youtu.be/HhgWGduQZQY?t=2165)). He does market-buy when he has missed a limit; read that as capturing a level you meant to be filled at. A sell-stop inside the gap candle, with the stop above its high, is an alternative that may give too wide a stop ([2022 Ep 2 · 43:47](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2627)).

Rules of the road:

1. **Place the limit after displacement**, not while price is still in the gap area ([2022 Ep 20 · 13:27](https://youtu.be/Q6GFu8-Z4rY?t=807)).
2. **Sell the rise.** It feels scary to sell while price climbs into the gap, and it is what you want. Retail waits for confirmation and arrives late ([2022 Ep 2 · 29:18](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1758)).
3. **Do not chase.** Once the short-term low is taken out, a market order risks slippage and bigger risk ([2022 Ep 2 · 34:02](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=2042)). If price reaches the target before it retraces, nix the trade ([2022 Ep 3 · 43:12](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2592)).
4. **Do not hunt the exact top or bottom** ([2022 Ep 22 · 24:07](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1447)).

### Missed fills and scaling in

Perfection costs fills. On the morning of Lecture 8 price came within a tick of the gap and turned, so even a limit one tick inside was not filled. ICT called it the punishment for trying to be too perfect. His answer is fallback entries (a wick's midpoint, the next array, a lower-timeframe gap) and the reminder that a missed fill still means you read direction correctly ([2024 #8 · 11:16](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=676)). One caption says the order would have been filled, but his own comment points to not filled.

If price touches the top of a gap, rejects and you miss it, wait for displacement and a new gap on a lower timeframe (on a 1m chart, the 15- or 30-second) and enter there. He calls this the price delivery continuum (Chapter 18) ([2024 #6 · 1:18:58](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=4738)). A second chance can also come at the short-term low formed after the gap: in 2022 he used a limit at that low or a tick under, because sell-side liquidity rests below ([2022 Ep 9 · 20:59](https://youtu.be/iZLXnNiZm_s?t=1259)).

Scaling. His own sample was 6 contracts at the edge plus one tick and 4 more at the midpoint, ten in all, and he said a beginner should not trade ten and should only watch ([2024 #8 · 2:50:57](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=10257)). For longs: the largest portion at the gap's high or a tick below, an add at the CE, another if price leaves and retests the CE ([2024 #8 · 2:11:46](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=7906)). Adding does not mean rolling the stop up; wait until the market has rolled out of the area and proved itself ([2024 #6 · 1:42:53](https://youtu.be/woAQ1XJeD7Y?t=6173)). For a prop-firm trader, one remark matters: in the 2026 gap-short segment he warns that some prop firms do not allow adding to a losing position, and if yours does not, do not trade that way ([2026 #41 · 39:24](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=2364)).

### Entry models built on the gap

- **The 2022 model.** Sweep of liquidity, displacement through a swing point, a gap in the leg, a limit in the gap, and the opposing liquidity as the target ([2022 Ep 7 · 15:25](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=925); [2022 Ep 14 · 0:48](https://youtu.be/NUdu1n-ML98?t=48)). Chapter 17 collects the models.
- **Raid into an old gap.** The swept level can be an old opposing gap instead of equal highs. Trading anywhere between its low and high is enough. Treat its low like relative equal highs, wait for the nearest short-term low to break with displacement, then trade the new gap in that leg ([2022 Ep 24 · 35:37](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2137); [2022 Ep 24 · 36:24](https://youtu.be/nv0ey5UyOmY?t=2184)).
- **Buying the sell stops inside the gap.** He placed a buy limit just under a short-term low sitting inside a discount gap, because sell stops rest there and it fortifies the setup. If price rallies without the sweep, he misses the move ([2022 Ep 38 · 29:58](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1798)).
- **Other variations.** The OTE (Chapter 4), turtle soup, a discount gap bought as price trades to its top with a stop under the lower low, and, least preferred, a buy stop above the swing high that formed just before price entered the gap ([2022 Ep 38 · 42:46](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2566); [2022 Ep 38 · 43:33](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=2613)).
- **A tiny gap in a gap-up.** In 2026 he shorted a small gap at the 9:30 retest after a gap-up open, entering in the upper quadrant of a wick range with the stop just above that wick's CE (Chapters 6, 11 and 19) ([2026 #41 · 39:24](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=2364)).
- **A no-bias drill.** Map relative equal highs or lows on the 5m or 1m and enter runs to them using the 15-second gap. It works in any session, but you will be stopped out more than with anything else he teaches, because it ignores time of day and the higher-timeframe flow. Use it for non-monetised baseline drills, and filter by the draw if monetised ([2024 #29 · 16:58](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=1018); [2024 #29 · 1:19:02](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=4742)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- Demanding the best price. It costs fills, and a missed fill is still a correct read of direction.
- Treating the shaded box as the entry. The trade is a price on the gap.
- Entering while price still sits in the gap area, or chasing after the short-term low has gone.
- Copying his 10-contract sample as a beginner. He said not to.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the default was the nearest-edge limit with a wide stop, plus the stacked-gap rule (below). In 2024 the same entry was renamed the lowest threshold or lowest probable resistance, with stress on missed fills, fallback entries, the lower-timeframe continuation and scale-in sizes. In 2026 the entries moved onto tiny gaps and quadrants of a wick range (Chapter 6) and a scale-out ladder (Chapter 19).

## Stops, re-entry and managing the trade

### What it is

The stop comes from the three candles of the gap, not from a fixed number of points. ICT gives a ladder from tight to wide, and the day's conditions decide the rung.

| Stop (for a short; mirror for a long) | Where | When | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tightest, advanced | CE of candle 1's wick plus 2 ticks | candle 1 has a wick above the gap; its overlap with candle 2 is a balanced range price should not cross back through | [2024 #8 · 3:04:43](https://youtu.be/zU7iAqg4sSA?t=11083) |
| Low-risk | candle 2's high plus 1 tick | the low-risk case | [2024 #49 · 1:40:50](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6050) |
| Standard, "maximum" | candle 1's high plus 1 tick (long: candle 1's low minus 1 tick) | price close to the low of the SIBI, or a high-resistance day | [2024 #49 · 1:40:02](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6002); [2026 #132 · 44:36](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2676) |
| Learning | above candle 1's or candle 2's high, whichever you can afford; the wider while learning | if the risk is too much, skip the trade | [2022 Ep 6 · 16:46](https://youtu.be/Bkt8B3kLATQ?t=1006) |
| Swing | above the swing high that ran the stops | whichever fits your risk | [2022 Ep 2 · 32:26](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1946) |
| High resistance | the gap's height added above its top: at least about twice the gap range from entry; probably micro size | Chapter 16 | [2024 #36 · 1:47:45](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=6465) |

In 2022 the minimum was a stop above the candle that creates the gap, with higher as the conservative choice. A big stop reflects volatility, and a wide stop means a small position, so use micros or skip ([2022 Ep 22 · 17:46](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1066); [2022 Ep 2 · 32:26](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1946)).

On high-resistance days price can retrace deeply, even wick above the gap, and still go your way, so a tight stop gets run while the direction is right ([2024 #49 · 1:40:02](https://youtu.be/3YfetuXVhOE?t=6002)). With the 2x stop it takes a real move to stop you, and a couple of ticks over the gap is not enough ([2024 #36 · 1:46:56](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=6416)). The notes add that this is not a standard rule and applies only to that regime. On low-resistance days the stop sits a tick above the gap's CE, though that caption is somewhat unclear.

### Re-entry

If you are stopped out and price later returns to the lower half of the same gap (for a short), that is another entry, with the stop again at candle 1 plus one tick ([2026 #132 · 43:01](https://youtu.be/E8EtGkn-x5w?t=2581)).

### Managing the trade

- **Do not hurry the stop.** Let it sit early in the day; after expansion, move to breakeven and then a bit higher ([2022 Ep 26 · 4:52](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=292); [2022 Ep 26 · 11:07](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=667); [2024 #36 · 1:29:57](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=5397)). In the last 2022 episode he rolled his stop up on a 2m long, price came back for a complete closure of the gap, and he was stopped out ([2022 Ep 41 · 11:12](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=672)).
- **Raise it only when the gap has proved itself.** After a gap held once and twice he raised the stop, since a return into it meant he was wrong ([2022 Ep 38 · 25:13](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=1513)).
- **A 2026 trail for a short.** Once price leaves and takes out the low of the entry candle's body on a closing basis, tighten to the gap's CE, then to just above its high, where it stays until the target ([2026 #41 · 42:38](https://youtu.be/BD7kqmgdBU8?t=2558)).
- **Partials.** First partial at the first discount array, then the sell-side liquidity ([2022 Ep 40 · 13:42](https://youtu.be/koN1ge8bewI?t=822)). In Episode 38 he took half off at an old buy-side area and imbalance to the left ([2022 Ep 38 · 12:40](https://youtu.be/36184dDAqtM?t=760)). Chapter 19 covers targets.
- **Log the heat.** Note how long the move took and how much drawdown the entry gap took. Zero heat and about a minute to the target was a bread-and-butter example ([2024 #9 · 10:30](https://youtu.be/pg5XBNkpotM?t=630)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- A tight stop on a high-resistance day. It gets run even when you are right.
- Rolling the stop up before the market has proved the gap.
- Copying a stop in points from an index example onto another instrument. The stop is a candle rule.

The same year he gives a closing-basis trail (above), and the study notes record that a later February 2026 lecture teaches trailing a stop under displacement, so the remark is not literal.

## Timeframes: the top-down search and parent gaps

### What it is

A gap exists on a timeframe. A 15m gap, a 5m gap and a 1m gap can all be live inside one leg of price. ICT's procedure is to isolate the displacement leg, then search it from the top down for the gap you will trade.

### Why it works

For index futures, ICT says the 1m, 2m and 3m charts are best for finding imbalances, because high-frequency algorithms operate on timeframes up to about 3 minutes, often in 15-, 30-, 45- and 60-second intervals ([2022 Ep 2 · 17:56](https://youtu.be/tmeCWULSTHc?t=1076)). The paint-roller idea scales: a bigger candle is a bigger roller. A higher-timeframe gap is the parent and a lower-timeframe gap is subordinate to it. He rejected a viewer's claim that the small-timeframe gap must form first, saying it never works like that ([2022 Ep 18 · 34:17](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2057)).

### How to spot it

1. **Isolate the leg.** On the 5m inside the killzone, a swing low is taken and then price punches above a swing high. Study that leg on the 5m, 3m and 1m. If the lower timeframe also took a swing low and a swing high, that is a valid condition to look for a trade ([2022 Ep 8 · 5:41](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=341); [2022 Ep 8 · 6:27](https://youtu.be/7rbV8aWkcqY?t=387)).
2. **Step down 5m, 4m, 3m, 2m, 1m.** The first timeframe with a gap in the leg is your trading timeframe, and you stop looking once you find it. If even the 1m has none, there is no trade ([2022 Ep 10 · 37:44](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2264); [2022 Ep 10 · 38:32](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2312)).
3. **Check the gap is usable.** In an ES example the 4m had a gap but in discount (invalid for the short), the 3m had none, and the 2m had a small one right after a short-term low was taken: the entry ([2022 Ep 25 · 35:43](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2143); [2022 Ep 25 · 41:15](https://youtu.be/CnTXwAuDi9Y?t=2475)). In another, the 4m gap was too small to use and he bought the 2m gap that sat on down-close candles ([2022 Ep 27 · 8:58](https://youtu.be/gLK4Qm6jte8?t=538)). So the first usable gap is the one you trade.
4. **Prefer clean candles.** On the 4m the candles looked beefier and cleaner than on the 5m. You want price action where the break in even distribution stands out ([2022 Ep 10 · 38:32](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2312)).

### How to trade it

- **The 15m minimum-expectation entry.** After displacement, a limit sell at the 15m gap, or a pip above it, with the stop above the high. If the imbalance is very stretched you can use it for the stop, or go above the swing high if worried. Then split the 15m down-leg into the 5m and search it for a finer gap, down through the 1m ([2022 Ep 18 · 32:43](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=1963); [2022 Ep 18 · 33:30](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2010)).
- **Carry the parent box down.** The 5m return into a 15m gap at 9:30 may not look clean, yet the 15m gap gives the framework, and the actual setup on the 1m was a small gap just above a short-term high ([2022 Ep 16 · 17:41](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1061); [2022 Ep 16 · 19:17](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=1157)). In 2024 he says to project higher-timeframe gaps down as boxes ([2024 #24 · 1:26:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5209)).
- **A bigger gap beneath.** Price ran over a small 15m gap because a larger one sat below it. The top of a candle marked the low end of one gap and the high end of the other, so price was likely to go over the smaller one and into the larger ([2022 Ep 41 · 5:37](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=337)).
- **Time the entry on a correlated index.** In 2022 Episode 7, ES printed a bullish gap and the 10:36 candle traded down into it. ICT used that timing to buy NQ at 14,505.5, near a bullish order block, though NQ showed no gap at that moment. He exited at 14,622.75, about 117 handles on one NQ mini ([2022 Ep 7 · 18:34](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1114); [2022 Ep 7 · 20:07](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=1207); [2022 Ep 7 · 37:58](https://youtu.be/G8-z91acgG4?t=2278)). Comparing markets is Chapter 15.

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- Waiting for the lower-timeframe gap to form before honouring the parent.
- Dropping further down after a usable gap is found, or forcing a gap onto a timeframe that has none.

### How the teaching changed

In 2022 the focus was the 5-to-1-minute step-down and the 15m minimum entry. In 2024 the sub-minute chart entered the picture (15-second gaps in drills, and as a fallback when a limit misses), and higher-timeframe gaps were projected down as boxes.

## Stacked gaps: the two-gap rule

### What it is

A leg sometimes leaves two gaps near each other. He repeats this rule through the 2022 mentorship (Episodes 3, 10, 16, 18, 21, 22 and 23) and returns to it in 2024. In this book the nearer gap is the one price meets first on the retrace and the farther gap is the one beyond it.

[[DIAGRAM ch05_d3 | caption: Two stacked bearish gaps: limit in the nearer gap, stop above the farther one (illustrative) | Price may stab into the upper gap, so the stop must sit above it; the entry stays in the lower gap because the upper one may never be reached.]]

### How to trade it

- **One gap.** Place your entry there and do not expect a deeper retracement ([2022 Ep 22 · 23:20](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1400)).
- **Two gaps.** Enter the nearer gap and always imagine a stab into the farther one. Place the stop to weather that stab (deep pockets, or micros), so your risk includes the farther gap ([2022 Ep 18 · 42:15](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=2535); [2022 Ep 22 · 22:33](https://youtu.be/QKc_i90chVg?t=1353); [2022 Ep 10 · 36:58](https://youtu.be/S9ORTYmXwdE?t=2218)).
- **Do not assume the better price.** The farther gap can be traded to on a spike, but the order goes in the nearer gap, because the farther one may never be reached. In the London ES setup of Episode 21, price never went to the upper gap ([2022 Ep 21 · 19:56](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1196); [2022 Ep 21 · 20:45](https://youtu.be/kmVXVJE08eQ?t=1245)).
- **A dip into the farther gap is permissible.** On the long side, a dip back into the lower gap is fine. Only if price overlaps the entire lower gap and goes lower do you close ([2022 Ep 3 · 45:36](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2736)).
- **Mind the untraded parent.** With an initial imbalance and a second beyond it, he would rather see price drop into the target first, since a move too far back up would upset a higher-timeframe gap not yet traded into ([2022 Ep 23 · 2:27](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=147)).
- **2024 name.** He called the sequence, a dip into the lower gap and a stab into the upper one, the "model 2022" entry. His fill was even better because it was outside, below, the lower gap ([2024 #31 · 20:59](https://youtu.be/XiZHGOeVAOk?t=1259)).

### Two versions of the rule

In Episode 16 he says to anticipate price trading up into the small gap above your gap and to use that upper gap as the entry, targeting the order block ([2022 Ep 16 · 37:34](https://youtu.be/EpGQnhjXBq8?t=2254)). From Episode 18 on, the instruction reverses: put the limit in the lower gap, because the upper one might not be reached, and let the stop include the upper gap. The later wording is the one he repeats in Episodes 21, 22 and 23, and it is the one this chapter uses.

### Worked example

NQ 1m, 25 January 2022. A bullish market structure shift left a gap (the green box) with a smaller gap just below it. Price dipped into the lower gap, leaving wicks, came back up into the green gap, and he bought at market. Price stabbed down once more into the wicks. He read it as the move taking price down to accumulate more longs, not as selling pressure. The maximum drawdown was about $455. Price then rallied to the bottom of a pink rectangle (an imbalance in premium), where he flattened for +$1,190 ([2022 Ep 3 · 13:45](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=825); [2022 Ep 3 · 41:38](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2498)).

### Mistakes and look-alikes

- Sizing the stop for the nearer gap only.
- Waiting for the farther gap because it is the better price.
- Treating a dip into the lower gap as failure. It fails only when the entire lower gap is overlapped and price keeps going.

## Gaps as targets and reference levels

### What it is

A gap is also an objective. Price is drawn to a gap, to its edge or to its midpoint, and old gaps stay on the chart as references. The reasoning is the same efficient-delivery idea, read from the other side.

### How to use it

- **Daily gaps.** After five consecutive down days with price deep in the range, expect a bounce into the new daily gap. It only needs to reach the gap's edge ([2022 Ep 18 · 15:57](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=957); [2022 Ep 18 · 8:47](https://youtu.be/eai0nHhAC8w?t=527)). A daily retracement up into premium to fill a gap can lull traders into thinking higher while the target stays the sell-side liquidity below the old low ([2022 Ep 11 · 1:42](https://youtu.be/Sqw2bww93Zo?t=102)).
- **Near the objective, expect a shallow retrace.** When price is close to its target it generally dips only into the gap, not all the way back to the order block ([2022 Ep 13 · 23:12](https://youtu.be/tpPtItWqmlg?t=1392)).
- **A spotted draw.** At 11:37 a.m. ET on 2 June 2022 he asked followers to find the 5m gap above market price, and it was reached soon after. He framed himself as a spotter showing where price is heading, not as giving a signal ([2022 Ep 37 · 12:45](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=765); [2022 Ep 37 · 15:56](https://youtu.be/HuZurY0ghDI?t=956)).
- **Weekly and daily gaps.** In the NFP lecture a daily bullish gap (Monday's high to Wednesday's low, Tuesday the displacement candle) was his weekly downside objective, and its low at about 18,765.25 was hit Thursday into Friday ([2024 #24 · 1:26:49](https://youtu.be/MxaJD1WIzos?t=5209)). On 3 October 2024 price delivered to the CE of a weekly BISI and made the low of the day there ([2024 #41 · 7:37](https://youtu.be/Xyiw_IFE9yc?t=457)).
- **Gaps stay valid.** He used the high of Monday's first presented FVG as a target several days later ([2024 #38 · 1:28:12](https://youtu.be/DnuCB5Stk-0?t=5292)). In 2026 he says a pre-market gap is a level you can count on price to use at least once in the day: first as a draw, then as a zone traded through (inversion), then as a barrier ([2026 #98 · 19:03](https://youtu.be/X-xYcsOG9Yg?t=1143)). Chapters 7 and 8 cover these.
- **Stair-steps and turn points.** On the hourly, a bullish leg can climb by repeated retracements into discount gaps, so with a bullish bias do not pick tops ([2022 Ep 35 · 10:21](https://youtu.be/MdYrslpUezU?t=621)). A discount gap below the 50% of a range was his turn point for a drop, with bodies respecting it ([2022 Ep 23 · 9:37](https://youtu.be/TV9TfwHXuqs?t=577)).

## When to stand aside

- **No displacement, or no gap on the break.** Skip it ([2022 Ep 4 · 6:34](https://youtu.be/L-ReMHiavPM?t=394); [2022 Ep 41 · 11:59](https://youtu.be/2XhDi5GoNUI?t=719)).
- **Price has already run to the target, or you would be chasing.** Nix the trade ([2022 Ep 3 · 43:12](https://youtu.be/nQfHZ2DEJ8c?t=2592)).
- **Long consolidation with equal highs and lows on both sides.** He is not a breakout artist. Once one side is run, price usually goes for the other ([2024 #2 · 1:43:32](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6212); [2024 #2 · 1:45:56](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6356)). If the day is not obvious, "I don't know" is an acceptable answer ([2024 #2 · 2:12:30](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=7950)).
- **A gap already used twice.** Low probability for a new entry ([2024 #2 · 1:46:45](https://youtu.be/pkI4AgC4OPI?t=6405)).
- **Shallow, justifiable both ways, too long inside.** He passed on a short for those reasons, and because selling the low end of the range after a big drop sells a discount ([2024 #29 · 1:38:31](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5911); [2024 #29 · 1:39:21](https://youtu.be/fhBnnU44A6E?t=5961)).
- **The imbalance crowd.** After the 8:30 drop was rebalanced, traders who follow imbalances sold the drift lower. He went the other way and aimed at their buy-side stops ([2022 Ep 26 · 9:32](https://youtu.be/k26euOG6zAk?t=572)). A gap is not a signal by itself.
- **A stop you do not want.** On a high-resistance day, if you cannot accept the wide stop, do not trade that day ([2024 #36 · 1:47:45](https://youtu.be/nr6CJqNWChw?t=6465)).

## Check yourself

1. Candle 1's high is 18,420.00 and the candle 3 low after a strong up candle 2 is 18,418.00. Is there a BISI? What if candle 3's low were 18,430.00?
2. NQ bearish gap: candle 1 low 20,050.00, candle 1 high 20,058.00, candle 2 high 20,052.00, candle 3 high 20,038.00 (tick 0.25). Give the default limit, the ahead-of-edge limit, the low-risk stop and the standard stop.
3. On the same gap, it is a high-resistance day. Where is the stop at minimum, and what does that imply for size?
4. A swing low breaks on a weak candle, with a tiny separation and no real move afterwards. Do you trade the gap?
5. Delivery is bearish. The chart shows a fresh BISI and two SIBIs. Which do you key off, and what would change that?
6. A SIBI is 10 points tall. In one case a wick prints 7 points above its top while bodies stay in the lower half. In another a body closes 4 points above its top. Which fits his tolerance?
7. You short from a SIBI and price spends four candles with bodies inside it. What does the 2026 teaching say, and what do the 2022 notes say about candles staying in a gap?
8. Your limit one tick inside the edge was not filled and price turned. What are his fallbacks?
9. A gap forms at 2:00 p.m. in dull chop, with no sweep beforehand. Why is that weaker than the same gap after a raid at a key time?

## Answers

1. No gap in the first case: candle 3's low (18,418.00) sits below candle 1's high, so the wicks overlap. In the second case the BISI runs 18,420.00 to 18,430.00, and 18,430.00 is the near edge. Reread "The three-candle gap".
2. Default limit: 20,038.25. Ahead of the edge: 20,037.75. Low-risk stop: 20,052.25 (candle 2's high + 1 tick). Standard stop: 20,058.25 (candle 1's high + 1 tick). Reread "Where to put the limit" and "Stops, re-entry and managing the trade".
3. The gap is 12 points tall (20,038 to 20,050). Add that above the top: at least about 20,062, roughly 24 points from entry, so micro size or no trade. Reread "Stops, re-entry and managing the trade".
4. No. Without displacement through the low there is no valid gap. Reread "Displacement is the gate".
5. SIBIs only. The BISI becomes relevant only if price reverses violently, when it can act as an inversion FVG (Chapter 7). Reread "Choosing the gap when delivery is one-sided".
6. The first fits: a wick up to about one gap width above the top is tolerated. The second breaks the rule that bodies must not close beyond. Reread "Bodies decide, wicks may poke".
7. In 2026, more than two candles inside is weakness, so consider taking something off and do not assume continuation. In 2022 he read bodies staying in a gap as possible accumulation, and the notes do not reconcile the two. Reread "Speed, distance and time inside the gap".
8. Fallback entries: the wick's midpoint, the next array, or a new gap on a lower timeframe (15s or 30s), and the thought that a missed fill still means you read direction correctly. Reread "Missed fills and scaling in".
9. A gap is only where a pattern might form; time and liquidity decide. A raid at a key or macro time followed by displacement gives it context; dull chop does not. Reread "Time: the gap is the where, not the when".
